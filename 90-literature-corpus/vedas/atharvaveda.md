# Atharvaveda — Per-Text Profile (Charms, Healing & Speculation)

[Vedas](./README.md) · [Literature Corpus](../README.md) · [Master Index](../../README.md)
The fourth and most heterodox of the Vedas: ~730 hymns / ~6,000 mantras in 20 kandas blending battlefield magic, healing charms, domestic ritual and some of the boldest cosmological speculation in shruti. Overseen by the **Brahman** priest. See companions [rigveda.md](./rigveda.md), [yajurveda.md](./yajurveda.md), the broader shruti map [shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md](../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md), [vedic-deities-and-suktas.md](../vedic-deities-and-suktas.md), [principal-upanishads-detail.md](../principal-upanishads-detail.md), and [upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md) for the Ayurveda link.

## Overview and Scale

- **Definition** - The Samhita of *atharvan* (fire-priest's formulas) and *angiras* (Angirasa charms); a collection of metrical and prose mantras governing everyday life — healing, protection, prosperity, sorcery, statecraft, marriage, funeral — together with high cosmological hymns. (Atharvaveda Samhita)
- **Scale** - 20 kandas ("books"); the Shaunaka vulgate counts **~730–731 suktas (hymns)** and **~6,000 stanzas** (commonly cited 6,015 mantras across 728 suktas). About one-sixth of the material overlaps the Rigveda, mostly in the late Kanda 20. (Shaunaka Samhita)
- **Oldest self-name** - The text calls itself **Atharvangirasah** (AV 10.7.20), a compound of *Atharvan* (auspicious, healing, "white" magic) and *Angiras* (aggressive, hostile, abhicara magic) — the two complementary streams of formula. Later names: *Atharvaveda*, *Brahmaveda* (the Veda of the Brahman priest), *Bhrigvangirasah*. (Atharvaveda nomenclature)
- **The Brahman priest** - The Atharvaveda is the Veda of the **Brahman** ("overseer"), the silent fourth chief priest who supervises the whole sacrifice, watches for and remedies error, and heals ritual mishaps — complementing the Hotr (Rigveda), Adhvaryu (Yajurveda) and Udgatr (Samaveda). See [yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md](../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md). (Vedic priesthood)
  - *Variant (academic):* The Brahman's exclusive Atharvavedic affiliation is partly a later claim by the Atharvan school to secure a place in the four-priest scheme; in the oldest stratum the Brahman could be drawn from any Veda. [scholarly]
- **Two clocks** - *Scripture clock:* apaurusheya shruti, "heard" by the rishis Atharvan and Angiras (and Bhrigu) at the cosmic dawn, of equal eternity with the other three Vedas. *Academic clock:* the bulk composed **c. 1200–900 BCE** (Witzel: at/after c. 1200/1000 BCE; Flood: c. 900 BCE), later than the Rigveda and roughly contemporary with the Yajurveda mantras; codified in the early-Iron-Age Kuru–Pancala realm. [scholarly]

## Structure: How the 20 Kandas Are Arranged

- **Ordering principle** - Books are arranged neither by deity nor by author but largely by **hymn length and formal type**, a different logic from the Rigveda's family-and-deity scheme. (Shaunaka arrangement)
- **Kandas 1–7 (short-hymn books)** - Collections of short hymns (roughly 2–8 stanzas each), the heartland of charms: healing (bhaishajyani), prosperity (paushtikani), sorcery and counter-sorcery (abhicarikani / krityapratiharanani), love-magic and women's rites (strikarmani). [folk-rooted]
- **Kandas 8–12 (long-hymn books)** - Longer hymns mixing extended charms with the great **speculative/cosmological suktas** (Skambha, Kala, Prithvi, Vratya, Uchchhishta, Prana). The philosophical core of the Veda. [late text]
- **Kandas 13–18 (thematic long books)** - Organized by theme/life-cycle: 13 (Rohita / the ruddy sun), 14 (the **marriage / vivaha** hymns, parallel to RV 10.85), 15 (the prose **Vratya** book), 16–17 (prose paryaya formulas), 18 (the **funeral / pitrmedha** hymns, parallel to RV 10.14–18). (Thematic kandas)
- **Kandas 19–20 (appendices)** - 19 is a supplementary gleaning of charms and the famous Kala/Time hymns; **Kanda 20** is almost wholly Rigvedic verses for the Soma sacrifice (incl. the **Kuntapa** hymns, 20.127ff.), widely judged a late liturgical appendix grafting the Atharvan into the soma cult. [disputed — latest stratum]

## The Two Recensions (Shakhas)

- **Shaunaka (Saunakiya)** - The surviving **vulgate**, used in modern study and recitation; 20 kandas, ~731 hymns, ~6,000 stanzas; named for the Shaunaka school. Its ancillary apparatus includes the **Kaushika Sutra** (grhya/magical-ritual sutra — the practical handbook for deploying the charms), the **Vaitana Sutra** (shrauta), the Atharva-Pratishakhyas, the Atharvan Prayashchittas, and the Parishishtas (supplements). (Shaunakiya Shakha)
- **Paippalada (Paippalada)** - A long-lost rival recension preserving substantially different order and many unique hymns; said to have once been the more authoritative school. (Paippalada Shakha)
  - *Kashmir branch:* Known from a single birch-bark **Sharada manuscript** from Kashmir (the basis of Roth's and Barret's editions), textually corrupt and hard to read.
  - *Odisha branch:* A living oral and manuscript tradition; a well-preserved set of palm-leaf manuscripts was discovered in **Odisha in 1957** (by Durgamohan Bhattacharyya), now the basis of the critical edition (Vishva Bandhu, then the Leiden / Witzel–Griffiths critical edition). The Paippalada is still recited by a handful of Odia families. [living tradition]
- **Lost shakhas** - The Charanavyuha lists nine Atharvan schools (Paippalada, Shaunaka, plus Stauda, Mauda, Jajala, Jalada, Brahmavada, Devadarsha, Charanavaidya); only the two survive in text. [most lost]

## Bhaishajyani — Healing Charms (Proto-Medicine)

- **Fracture and wound cure (Paippalada 4.15 / Shaunaka 4.12)** - The *rohani* ("the healer") plant knits broken bone: "let marrow be joined with marrow, joint with joint, let what is severed of thy flesh and bone grow together again." A direct ancestor of orthopaedic charm-medicine. (Bhaishajya hymn)
- **Takman / fever charms (e.g. AV 5.22, 1.25, 5.21)** - Spells to banish **takman** (fever, often malarial), personified and dispatched to distant peoples (the Mahavrishas, Mujavants, Gandharis, Balhikas), and to seasonal cold/heat/autumn fevers; among the earliest references to recurrent fever in world literature. (Takman hymns)
- **Herb hymns (AV 8.7, 6.21, etc.)** - Praise of the **oshadhayah** (medicinal plants/herbs) as a collective healing power: "the conquering strength, the power and might which ye victorious plants possess, therewith deliver this man from consumption (yakshma)." (Plant hymns)
- **Specific ailments** - Charms against **yakshma** (consumption/wasting), **jaundice/heart-disease** (AV 1.22, "let thy heart-burning and thy yellowness go to the sun"), **dropsy / Varuna's water-disease** (AV 1.10), **leprosy / kilasa** (AV 1.23–24, the dark herb against white spots), **kshetriya** (inherited/constitutional disease), worms (krimi, AV 2.31–32, 5.23), poison, snakebite, and difficult childbirth. [proto-clinical]
- **Anatomical hymn (AV 10.2)** - The *Purusha* body-enumeration hymn cataloguing limbs, organs and faculties — an early anatomical/physiological reckoning. (Body hymn)

## Abhicara, Protection & Domestic Magic

- **Abhicara (hostile/sorcery)** - Spells to harm enemies, drive off rivals, curse co-wives, deflect or return witchcraft (**krityapratiharana**, "repelling sorcery"); the Angirasa stream. Includes lead-amulet and demon-binding charms against **rakshasas, pishachas** and the demons of disease. [folk]
- **Paushtika (prosperity)** - Charms for cattle increase, abundant rain, success in trade, gambling luck (AV 7.50, 4.38, the apsaras of the dice), safe travel, and protective amulets (mani) of various woods and metals (e.g. the **jangida** amulet, the **darbha** grass, the **sraktya / khadira** amulet). [folk]
- **Strikarmani (women's/love rites)** - Love-attraction and retention charms, charms to secure a husband, to procure a son (pumsavana), to ensure conjugal harmony or sow discord between rivals. (Strikarmani hymns)
- **Sava / ritual gifts** - Hymns accompanying the **brahmaudana** (priest's cooked-rice fee) and dakshina, exalting the cow and the gift as cosmic forces (AV 10.10, the **Vasha / sacred cow** hymn; AV 4.34, 11.1 the odana/rice-mess hymns). [late text]

## Rajakarmani — Royal Rites & Statecraft

- **Consecration and re-establishment** - Charms for a king's **rashtra** (sovereignty): the consecration / *abhisheka*-adjacent hymns, restoring an exiled king (AV 3.3–4), securing the realm, the *parna* (palasha-wood) amulet for royal splendour, and hymns for the people's choice of a king (AV 3.4, "thee the people choose unto kingship"). (Rajakarmani hymns)
- **Victory and army** - War-drum hymns to terrify the foe (AV 5.20–21, the **dundubhi / battle-drum**), charms for victory in battle, and protective spells over a marching army. (Sangrama hymns)
- **Purohita's craft** - These royal charms underpin the **purohita** (chaplain) role and mark the Atharvan priest's bid to be the king's indispensable household priest. [scholarly]

## Speculative & Cosmological Hymns (the Philosophical Core)

- **Skambha Sukta (AV 10.7, with 10.8)** - The "cosmic pillar/support" (*skambha*) into which all gods, worlds, breath, the Vedas and being itself are set; an early monistic vision identifying Skambha with the ground of all existence — a clear precursor to the Brahman of the Upanishads. (Skambha hymn)
- **Kala / Time hymns (AV 19.53–54)** - **Kala** (Time) deified as the first cosmic principle: Time generated the sky and earth, drives the sun, is the lord of all, "the first-born among the gods" — one of the earliest hymns making Time itself the supreme creative power. (Kala-sukta)
- **Prithvi / Bhumi Sukta (AV 12.1)** - The 63-verse **Hymn to the Earth**, the longest sustained address to Bhumi/Prithivi in the Vedas, used in the **Agrayana** rite; celebrated as an early ecological/devotional vision of Earth as mother and sustainer; verse 12.1.26 (atoms → stones → rock → earth) is read as a seed of **Vaisheshika** atomism. (Bhumi-sukta)
- **Vratya hymns (AV 15, the Vratya-kanda; also 11.5 the Brahmacharin)** - The mysterious **Vratya** (the wandering, non-orthodox ascetic-warrior brotherhoods outside settled Vedic society) mystically exalted as a cosmic figure identified with the lone god (eka Vratya) — a key document for heterodox/proto-ascetic currents and the prehistory of Rudra-Shiva. [disputed interpretation]
- **Prana hymn (AV 11.4)** - Hymn to **Prana** (Breath/Life) as the all-pervading lord on whom all beings depend — an early "breath = cosmic principle" speculation feeding the Upanishadic prana doctrine. (Prana-sukta)
- **Uchchhishta hymn (AV 11.7)** - The sacrificial **remnant** (uchchhishta) exalted as containing the whole cosmos, gods and rita — a striking sacrificial-monist hymn. [late text]
- **Other speculative hymns** - The **Brahmacharin** (AV 11.5, the Vedic student cosmicized), **Rohita** (AV 13, the ruddy Sun as cosmogonic), **Kama** (AV 9.2, Desire as world-creating force), and the **Anadvan / sacred ox** (AV 4.11). (Speculative kandas)

## Gopatha Brahmana

- **The sole Atharvan Brahmana** - The **Gopatha Brahmana** is the only Brahmana attached to the Atharvaveda, comparatively late and partly compiled from other Vedas to give the Atharvan a Brahmana of its own. (Gopatha Brahmana)
- **Structure** - Two khandas: **Purvabhaga** (5 prapathakas, 135 kandikas) and **Uttarabhaga** (6 prapathakas, 123 kandikas); it expounds the Brahman priest's pre-eminence, the syllable **Om**, the Gayatri, the great vyahritis and the cosmic primacy of the Atharvaveda. [late text]

## Attached Upanishads

- **Mundaka Upanishad** - A verse Upanishad in three **mundakas**; famous for the **para/apara vidya** (higher vs lower knowledge) distinction, the **two birds** on one tree (the eating self and the watching Self), the bridge to Brahman, and the motto **"satyam eva jayate"** ("truth alone triumphs," the Indian national motto). See [principal-upanishads-detail.md](../principal-upanishads-detail.md). (Mundaka Upanishad)
- **Prashna Upanishad** - Prose; six pupils put **six questions (prashna)** to the sage Pippalada — on the origin of creatures, prana, the syllable Om, sleep and waking, the sixteen kalas of the Purusha; affiliated with the Paippalada school. (Prashna Upanishad)
- **Mandukya Upanishad** - The **shortest** principal Upanishad (12 verses); analyzes **Om** and the **four states of consciousness** (waking/vaishvanara, dream/taijasa, deep sleep/prajna, and the fourth, **turiya**); the seedbed of Gaudapada's *Mandukya Karika* and of Advaita Vedanta. (Mandukya Upanishad)
- **Minor Atharvan Upanishads** - A large body of late/sectarian Upanishads is conventionally classed under the Atharvaveda (e.g. the Nrisimha-tapaniya, Rama-tapaniya, Atharvashiras, Ganapati, Mahanarayana-adjacent, and various Yoga and Sannyasa Upanishads). [late text / sectarian]

## Trayi vs. Fourth Veda — The Canon-Status Debate

- **The "trayi" (threefold knowledge)** - Classical orthodoxy long spoke of the **trayi vidya** — Rik, Yajus, Saman — as "the three Vedas," frequently omitting the Atharvaveda from the canonical count. (Trayi)
- **Late acceptance** - Early Buddhist (and some Brahmanical) texts reference only three Vedas; full acceptance of the Atharvaveda as the **fourth Veda** is generally placed in the **second half of the 1st millennium BCE**, though its hymns existed by the time of the Chandogya Upanishad (~700 BCE). [scholarly]
- **Status of its priests** - Atharvan priests were sometimes regarded as a **lower tier** of Brahmins (its magic deemed less "pure" than soma liturgy); a residual stigma is documented among Atharvan/Paippalada Brahmins in Odisha into modern times. [scholarly]
- **Counter-claim from within** - The tradition itself elevates the Atharvaveda as **Brahmaveda**, the Veda of the supervising Brahman who must know all three others — a rhetorical move to outrank the trayi rather than be excluded by it. (Gopatha Brahmana apologetic)

## Ayurveda and the Upaveda Link

- **The named source-Veda of medicine** - Tradition assigns **Ayurveda** as the *upaveda* of the Atharvaveda; the Caraka Samhita (Sutrasthana 30.21) instructs the physician to give **devotion to the Atharvaveda among the four Vedas**, and Sushruta likewise credits it as foundational. See [upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md). (Ayurveda upaveda)
  - *Variant (academic):* The Atharvaveda contains charm-and-herb folk medicine, not systematic clinical science; the actual scholarly medical corpus (Caraka, Sushruta Samhitas) is later — the link is genealogical/legitimizing, not a continuous textual derivation. [scholarly]
- **Dhanurveda / Gandharvaveda note** - Some lists also slot martial (Dhanurveda) and other upavedas variously; the **Ayurveda = Atharvaveda** pairing is the stable and universally cited one. (Upaveda schemes)
