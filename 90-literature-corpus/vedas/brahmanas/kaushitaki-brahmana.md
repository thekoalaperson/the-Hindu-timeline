# Kauṣītaki (Śāṅkhāyana) Brāhmaṇa

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/vedas/brahmanas/`](../README.md) → [Vedas section](../README.md) → [Master Index](../../../README.md). The **second Brāhmaṇa of the Ṛgveda** — the prose ritual manual of the **Bāṣkala / Kauṣītaki śākhā**, sibling and rival to the [Aitareya Brāhmaṇa](aitareya-brahmana.md). Parent Veda: [`../rigveda.md`](../rigveda.md). For the four-layer (Saṃhitā → Brāhmaṇa → Āraṇyaka → Upaniṣad) scheme and śruti/yuga framing: [`../README.md`](../README.md).

Two clocks throughout: **scripture clock** — apauruṣeya Veda "re-heard" and re-divided by Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa at the Dvāpara–Kali junction of the current 28th Mahāyuga (c. **3102 BCE**); the Brāhmaṇa prose is the ritual exegesis carried in the same eternal transmission. **Academic clock** — orally composed Brāhmaṇa prose, c. **800–600 BCE**, Iron-Age Kuru-Pañcāla heartland, broadly contemporary with (some scholars say marginally later than) the Aitareya. [scholarly]

---

## 1. What the Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇa Is

- **The Ṛgveda's second Brāhmaṇa** — The Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇa (KB), also and equally called the **Śāṅkhāyana Brāhmaṇa**, is one of the two surviving prose Brāhmaṇas attached to the **Ṛgveda**; like the Aitareya it expounds the duties of the **Hotṛ** priest in the Soma sacrifice, supplying the *when, why, and ritual sense* of the ṛc that the Saṃhitā merely supplies. (Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇa)
- **Two names, one text** — "Kauṣītaki" names the teacher-line of **Kauṣītaki / Kuṣītaka**; "Śāṅkhāyana" names the ritual school of **Śāṅkhāyana** that preserved and arranged it. The names are used interchangeably for the same Brāhmaṇa, though strictly Śāṅkhāyana is the redactor-school and Kauṣītaki the older authority quoted within. (Śāṅkhāyana / Kauṣītaki school tradition)
- **Affiliation** — Belongs to the **Bāṣkala / Kauṣītaki śākhā** of the Ṛgveda, the second Ṛgvedic recension beside the Śākala/Aitareya line; this makes the KB the direct counterpart of the [Aitareya Brāhmaṇa](aitareya-brahmana.md) within the same Veda. (Śākhā tradition; cf. [`../rigveda.md`](../rigveda.md))
- **Scale and structure** — **30 *adhyāyas*** (chapters), a tighter and more unified compass than the Aitareya's 40. Chapters 1–6 treat the **haviryajña** (offerings of milk, grain, ghee); chapters 7–30 treat the **soma** sacrifices — so the whole sacrificial system is laid out in a single continuous sweep rather than the Aitareya's pañcikā blocks. [scholarly]

## 2. The Ritual Programme (Haviryajña and Soma)

- **The fire-establishment rites** — Opens with the foundational fire rituals: the **Agnyādheya** (setting up the three sacred fires) and the **Agnihotra** (the twice-daily milk oblation), the entry-gate of the whole śrauta system. (KB 1–3) — mechanics: [`../../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md`](../../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md).
- **The haviryajña series** — Systematically expounds the **new- and full-moon offerings (Darśapūrṇamāsa)**, the **four-monthly seasonal rites (Cāturmāsya)**, and the **Agrayaṇa** (first-fruits) — the cooked/ghee oblations that form the lower tier of śrauta practice, before any Soma is pressed. (KB 4–6)
- **The Soma sacrifice (Agniṣṭoma core)** — The bulk of the text (chs. 7–30) treats the model Soma rite, the **Agniṣṭoma**, and the wider **jyotiṣṭoma** family: consecration (dīkṣā), the buying and pressing of Soma, the three daily pressings (savana), and the Hotṛ's recitations (śastra) answering the Udgātṛ's chants (stotra). (KB 7–30) — ritual frame: [`../../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md`](../../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md).
- **Multi-day Soma rites** — Treats the **ekāha** (one-day), **ahīna** (several-day), and **sattra** (long session) Soma sacrifices, including the year-long **Gavām-ayana** and its **Mahāvrata** day, on broadly the same plan as the Aitareya but with its own ordering. (KB, later chapters)
- **The Hotṛ's recitation-forms** — Codifies the same great śastras the Hotṛ recites — **Ājya, Praüga, Marutvatīya, Niṣkevalya, Vaiśvadeva, Āgnimāruta** — with the Kauṣītaki school's distinctive choices of verse and insertion (nivid, viniyoga), the points on which it most visibly diverges from the Aitareya. (KB, throughout)

## 3. How It Differs from the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa

- **More systematic, more unified** — Scholars (notably Keith) read the KB as the **more orderly and consistent** of the two: it runs the *haviryajña → soma* progression in one continuous exposition, where the Aitareya is a layered compilation whose last ten chapters are a recognised later addition. The KB's 30 chapters read as a single planned manual. [scholarly]
- **Fuller on the lower sacrifices** — The KB gives **proper, sustained treatment to the haviryajña** (Agnyādheya, Agnihotra, Darśapūrṇamāsa, Cāturmāsya) up front; the Aitareya, by contrast, plunges almost at once into Soma and largely presupposes the simpler offerings. This is the single largest scope difference. [scholarly]
- **No Rājasūya set-piece, no Śunaḥśepa** — The KB does **not** carry the Aitareya's celebrated royal-consecration block (Rājasūya, the Mahābhiṣeka cakravartin king-list) nor the **Śunaḥśepa legend** (AB 7.13–18). Its value is liturgical-systematic rather than narrative; for those stories see [`aitareya-brahmana.md`](aitareya-brahmana.md). [disputed — some hold these to be Aitareya-specific accretions]
- **Different recitational emphasis** — Because it belongs to the **Kauṣītaki** rather than the **Śākala** śākhā, the KB sometimes prescribes different verses, sequences, or numbers of recitations for the same rite — the technical fingerprint by which the two Ṛgvedic schools are told apart. (KB vs. AB, comparative) [scholarly]
- **A second prose witness** — Where the two agree, they corroborate the antiquity of a usage; where they diverge, they preserve **two independent Hotṛ traditions**, making the KB indispensable as a control on the Aitareya rather than a mere duplicate. [scholarly]

## 4. The Attached Āraṇyaka and Upaniṣad

- **The Śāṅkhāyana (Kauṣītaki) Āraṇyaka** — The KB's "forest" companion is the **Śāṅkhāyana Āraṇyaka**, also called the **Kauṣītaki Āraṇyaka** — the transitional stratum between outward ritual and inner knowledge, treating the **Mahāvrata** day and the **Uktha** contemplatively (paralleling the Aitareya Āraṇyaka's role for its school). (Śāṅkhāyana / Kauṣītaki Āraṇyaka)
- **Embeds the Kauṣītaki Upaniṣad** — Within that Āraṇyaka sits the **Kauṣītaki Upaniṣad**, one of the older prose Upaniṣads and a principal (mukhya) Upaniṣad of the Ṛgveda alongside the Aitareya. Its set-pieces — the post-death paths and **Paryaṅka-vidyā**, the **Pratardana–Indra** dialogue on prāṇa as prajñā, and the **Bālāki–Ajātaśatru** dialogue — grow out of this same Brāhmaṇa stratum. Dedicated page: [`../../upanishads/kaushitaki-upanishad.md`](../../upanishads/kaushitaki-upanishad.md).
- **One continuous transmission** — Brāhmaṇa → Āraṇyaka → Upaniṣad of the Kauṣītaki line form a single school-corpus, exactly mirroring the Aitareya line; the symmetry of the two Ṛgvedic schools is what makes the pairing scholarly bedrock. (Kauṣītaki school)

## 5. Why This Text Matters to the Timeline

- **It completes the major-Brāhmaṇa roster** — Every peer first-tier Brāhmaṇa already has a file ([Aitareya](aitareya-brahmana.md), [Śatapatha](shatapatha-brahmana.md), [Pañcaviṃśa](panchavimsha-brahmana.md), [Taittirīya](taittiriya-brahmana.md)); the Kauṣītaki was the missing sibling. This page resolves that asymmetry.
- **A primary prose source for Ṛgvedic ritual** — As one of only two Brāhmaṇas of the Ṛgveda, the KB is a foundational witness for how the Hotṛ's recitations were understood, second only to (and the essential check upon) the Aitareya.
- **The systematic counterweight** — Its tidy, single-sweep exposition of *haviryajña + soma* makes it the cleaner reference for the architecture of the śrauta system, even where the Aitareya is richer in story and royal ritual.
- **No independent narrative anchor** — Unlike the Aitareya (Śunaḥśepa, Hariścandra, the Mahābhiṣeka king-list), the KB feeds the *ritual* and *philosophical* corpus rather than the *story* corpus; its narrative legacy is carried downstream through the Kauṣītaki Upaniṣad.
