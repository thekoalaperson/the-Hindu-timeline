# Panchavimsha (Tandya Maha-) Brahmana and the Samavedic Brahmana corpus

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/vedas/brahmanas/`](../../README.md) → [Vedas section](../README.md) → [Master Index](../../../README.md). The principal **Brāhmaṇa of the Sāmaveda** — the great prose manual of the **Udgātṛ** singing-priest's soma-chant. Parent Veda: [`../samaveda.md`](../samaveda.md). Forest companion stratum: [`../aranyakas.md`](../aranyakas.md). Śruti/yuga framing and the four-layer (Saṃhitā → Brāhmaṇa → Āraṇyaka → Upaniṣad) scheme: [`../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md).

Two clocks throughout: **scripture clock** — apauruṣeya Veda "re-heard" and re-divided by Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa at the Dvāpara–Kali junction of the current 28th Mahāyuga (c. **3102 BCE**); the Sāmavedic Brāhmaṇa prose is the chant-exegesis carried in the same eternal transmission, taught by Vyāsa's pupil **Jaimini** and his line. **Academic clock** — orally composed Brāhmaṇa prose, the Pañcaviṃśa among the **older** Brāhmaṇa-stratum texts (alongside the older Śatapatha layers), c. **900–700 BCE**, with the Jaiminīya reckoned roughly contemporary and the Ṣaḍviṃśa/Adbhuta a later appendix. [scholarly]

---

## 1. What the Panchavimsha Brahmana Is

- **The Udgātṛ's great Brāhmaṇa** — The **Pañcaviṃśa Brāhmaṇa** (PB) is the karma-kāṇḍa prose manual of the **Kauthuma–Rāṇāyanīya** śākhās of the [Sāmaveda](../samaveda.md), expounding the **stotra** (sung praise) and the chants of the soma sacrifice as performed by the **Udgātṛ** and his fellow singers (Prastotṛ, Pratihartṛ, Subrahmaṇya). Where the Sāmaveda Saṃhitā supplies the sāman-melodies, the PB explains *which chant goes where, why, and to what ritual effect.* (Pañcaviṃśa Brāhmaṇa)
- **"Of twenty-five" (pañca-viṃśa)** — Named for its **25 *adhyāyas*** (prapāṭhakas); hence also **Pañcaviṃśa**. (PB title)
- **Tāṇḍya Mahābrahmana** — Also called the **Tāṇḍya Mahābrāhmaṇa** ("the Great Brāhmaṇa of the Tāṇḍins"), after the school of **Tāṇḍin**; *mahā* ("great") marks it the largest and most important of the Sāmavedic Brāhmaṇas. (Tāṇḍya Mahābrāhmaṇa)
- **Shared by two śākhās** — The PB is common to both the **Kauthuma** and **Rāṇāyanīya** recensions, which differ mainly in melodic detail; it is the standard Brāhmaṇa behind the ~1,875-verse Kauthuma Saṃhitā. (Sāmaveda śākhās; cf. [`../samaveda.md`](../samaveda.md))
- **The longest soma-chant manual** — Among Vedic prose it is the fullest systematic treatment of the **stoma** (chant-structures) and the soma rites, indispensable for reconstructing the lost performance-practice of Vedic chant. [scholarly]

## 2. The Ritual Programme (Soma Chant and the Stomas)

- **The Jyotiṣṭoma core** — The opening chapters treat the model soma rite, the **Agniṣṭoma** and the wider **jyotiṣṭoma** family, fixing the chants of the three daily pressings (savana) and how the Udgātṛ's **stotra** answers the Hotṛ's recitations (śastra). (PB 1–8) — ritual mechanics: see Soma sacrifice cross-references in [`../samaveda.md`](../samaveda.md).
- **Theory of the stomas** — Systematizes the **stoma** — the numerical templates (15-fold, 17-fold, 21-fold, etc.) by which a verse-triplet is multiplied and rearranged into a full sung chant — the technical heart of Sāmavedic liturgy. (PB, throughout)
- **The one-day, multi-day, and session rites** — Codifies the **ekāha** (one-day), **ahīna** (several-day), and **sattra** (long sacrificial session) soma sacrifices, with their distinctive chants. (PB)
- **The Gavām-ayana year-sattra** — Lays out the **Gavām-ayana** ("the cows' going"), the great **year-long sattra** modelled on the solar year, with its turning-point days; the **Viṣuvant** (central "equinox" day) and the **Mahāvrata** day that crowns the session. (PB) — the year-sattra is the ritual mirror of the calendar.
- **The Dvādaśāha** — Expounds the **twelve-day** soma rite (dvādaśāha), a compendium rite folding together ekāha and ahīna forms. (PB)
- **Praiseworthy patrons (dānastuti echoes)** — Embeds **gāthās** and praise-verses naming legendary sacrificers and the cattle-and-gold largesse (dakṣiṇā) they bestowed — a charter-memory of patron kings and ṛṣis. (PB) [dynastic/regional charter]

## 3. The Vrātya-stomas (PB 17.1–4)

The PB's most discussed and socially singular passages: the chants that bring **outsiders into the fold**.

- **Who the Vrātyas were** — The **Vrātyas** were roving bands living outside settled brāhmaṇical orthopraxy — itinerant, marked by distinctive dress, a turban, a black garment, a silver ornament, a special chariot, and a **bow without an arrow**; ambiguous figures, neither plainly inside nor outside the Ārya order. (PB 17.1) [disputed — Vrātya identity]
- **The Vrātya-stoma chants** — The PB prescribes **four Vrātya-stomas**, the soma-chants by which a Vrātya band could **be consecrated and absorbed** into the regular sacrificial community, shedding their outsider status. (PB 17.1–4)
- **Redistribution of the gear** — On consecration the Vrātyas' characteristic equipment (the chariot, the turban, the whip, the black garment) is given away to specified persons — a Māgadha bard, a brāhmaṇa — ritually dismantling the outsider identity. (PB 17.1)
- **The "degraded" and the "honoured" Vrātya** — The texts distinguish grades of Vrātyas, including those who "do not study, do not plough, do not trade"; some passages treat them with disdain, others as bearers of a special, even consecrated, sanctity. (PB 17.1–4) [disputed]
  - *Variant (Atharvaveda 15, the Vrātya-kāṇḍa):* the AV's celebrated **fifteenth book** glorifies the **Vrātya** as a cosmic, quasi-divine wanderer identified with the Supreme (Mahādeva / the lone roaming god) — a strikingly elevated theology absent from the PB's more managerial framing. [regional/late within AV]
  - *Variant (scholarly):* modern readings range from the Vrātyas as a pre-/non-orthodox Indo-Aryan brotherhood, to a proto-Śaiva or ecstatic warrior sodality, to simply lapsed Āryas readmitted by rite — no consensus. [scholarly/disputed]

## 4. Narrative and Cosmogonic Lore in the PB

- **Prajāpati and the year** — Like other Brāhmaṇas, the PB equates **Prajāpati** with the sacrifice and with the **year** (saṃvatsara); the year-sattra is Prajāpati's body re-assembled in time, the chants restoring him. (PB) [creation framing]
- **Soma's purchase and the Gāyatrī's flight** — Carries the recurring Brāhmaṇa myth that **Soma** dwelt in heaven and was fetched to earth; the metre **Gāyatrī**, taking bird-form, flew up and seized Soma for the gods. (PB; cf. parallel tellings) [creation]
- **Etiologies of the sāmans** — Many chapters give the **origin-stories of individual sāmans** (named melodies): which ṛṣi "saw" a given sāman, in what crisis, and what cosmic power it secured — micro-myths attached to the chant-names. (PB, throughout)
- **Daśarātra and the contest-chants** — Preserves the framework of competitive and turning-point chants (the **Daśarātra**, ten-night core of the year-sattra), with the lore explaining their sequence. (PB)

## 5. The Shadvimsha (Adbhuta) Brahmana — the Appendix

- **"The Twenty-Sixth"** — The **Ṣaḍviṃśa Brāhmaṇa** is reckoned a **supplement/appendix to the Pañcaviṃśa**, its title ("twenty-sixth") continuing the PB's count of 25 chapters; it belongs to the same Kauthuma–Rāṇāyanīya line. (Ṣaḍviṃśa Brāhmaṇa)
- **The Subrahmaṇyā and minor chants** — Its earlier portion handles ritual matters left over from the PB — the **Subrahmaṇyā** invocation (summoning Indra), expiations (prāyaścitta), and chant-supplements. (Ṣaḍviṃśa)
- **The Adbhuta Brāhmaṇa (book of portents)** — Its **final (fifth) chapter** is the famous **Adbhuta Brāhmaṇa** ("Brāhmaṇa of marvels/portents") — a systematic treatise on **omens, prodigies, and the expiations (śānti) for them**: eclipses, earthquakes, unnatural births, weeping or sweating images, aberrant animal and celestial signs, and the rites that avert their evil. (Adbhuta Brāhmaṇa) — one of the earliest Indian texts of portent-lore (utpāta/adbhuta), ancestral to later **śānti** and astrological literature. [scholarly]

## 6. The Jaiminiya Brahmana — the Narrative-Rich Cousin

- **The Brāhmaṇa of the Jaiminīya (Talavakāra) śākhā** — The **Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa** (JB) is the principal Brāhmaṇa of the distinct **Jaiminīya / Talavakāra** recension of the Sāmaveda, preserved in the South (Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka). It covers much the same soma-chant ground as the PB but in a strikingly **more discursive, story-laden style**. (Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa; cf. [`../samaveda.md`](../samaveda.md))
- **Three books** — Transmitted in three sections (often cited as JB I–III), the first centred on the Agnihotra and the daily rites, the rest on soma and the sattras. Long known only in fragments; modern editions remain partial. [scholarly]
- **A treasury of Vedic myth** — The JB is, with the Śatapatha, the **richest Brāhmaṇa source of narrative myth and folktale**, often preserving older or wilder versions than the parallel PB. (Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa)
- **Bhṛgu's journey to the other world** — Embeds the celebrated **Bhṛgu and Varuṇa** legend: the proud young **Bhṛgu**, son of **Varuṇa**, is sent by his father to the next world, where he witnesses **men being devoured by the very men, animals, and plants they had eaten or felled in this life** — a foundational early statement of cosmic reciprocity and proto-karmic retribution; he returns chastened, and Varuṇa teaches him the inner meaning of the Agnihotra. (JB I.42–44) [creation/eschatology]
  - *Variant (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 11.6.1):* the same Bhṛgu-vision is told with its own emphases; the JB version is generally the fuller and more vivid. [parallel]
- **Kutsa, Indra, and the gods' quarrels** — Preserves numerous divine contest-myths — Indra's exploits, the gods' rivalries, the seizing of soma — many tied to the origin of particular sāmans. (JB)
- **Embeds the Kena Upanishad's home text** — The Jaiminīya school's **esoteric** Brāhmaṇa, the **Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa (Talavakāra)**, is its forest/secret stratum and the matrix of the **Kena (Talavakāra) Upaniṣad**. See [`../aranyakas.md`](../aranyakas.md) and [`../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md). (Talavakāra Brāhmaṇa)

## 7. The Wider Samavedic Brahmana Stratum (briefly)

- **The "minor" Kauthuma Brāhmaṇas** — Beyond the PB and Ṣaḍviṃśa, the Kauthuma line counts several short technical Brāhmaṇas: **Ārṣeya** (an index of sāman-names and their seers), **Devatādhyāya** (the deities of the chants), **Saṃhitopaniṣad** (theory of chant-construction), **Vaṃśa** (the teacher-lineage roll), and **Sāmavidhāna** (the magical/ritual application of sāmans). (Sāmavedic Brāhmaṇa corpus; cf. [`../samaveda.md`](../samaveda.md))
- **The Chāndogya Brāhmaṇa** — The Kauthuma (Tāṇḍya) school's larger Brāhmaṇa whose closing books form the great **Chāndogya Upaniṣad** — kept apart here as it belongs to the Upaniṣad stratum. See [`../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md).

## 8. Why These Texts Matter to the Timeline

- **The chant-half of the ritual world** — Where the [Aitareya](./aitareya-brahmana.md) and [Śatapatha](./shatapatha-brahmana.md) Brāhmaṇas profile the Hotṛ's and Adhvaryu's worlds, the PB and JB profile the **Udgātṛ's** — completing the four-priest picture of the soma sacrifice.
- **The Vrātya question** — The PB's Vrātya-stomas are a key datum for early Indian social history: the rite of **assimilating outsiders**, and the puzzle of who the Vrātyas were.
- **Portent-lore's seed** — The **Adbhuta Brāhmaṇa** is an ancestral source for the whole later genre of omen-and-expiation (utpāta-śānti) literature feeding into the Purāṇic and astrological corpus.
- **A narrative reservoir** — The **Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa** preserves myths (above all the Bhṛgu-vision of the eaten and the eaters) that anchor early ideas of cosmic reciprocity and the afterworld.
