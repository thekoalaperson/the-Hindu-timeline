# Aitareya Brahmana (and Aitareya Aranyaka)

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/vedas/brahmanas/`](../../README.md) → [Vedas section](../README.md) → [Master Index](../../../README.md). The principal **Brāhmaṇa of the Ṛgveda** — the prose ritual manual of the **Hotṛ** priest. Parent Veda: [`../rigveda.md`](../rigveda.md). Śruti/yuga framing and the four-layer (Saṃhitā → Brāhmaṇa → Āraṇyaka → Upaniṣad) scheme: [`../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md).

Two clocks throughout: **scripture clock** — apauruṣeya Veda "re-heard" and re-divided by Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa at the Dvāpara–Kali junction of the current 28th Mahāyuga (c. **3102 BCE**); the Brāhmaṇa prose is the ritual exegesis carried in the same eternal transmission. **Academic clock** — orally composed Brāhmaṇa prose, c. **800–600 BCE**, Iron-Age Kuru-Pañcāla heartland, among the later Brāhmaṇa-stratum texts. [scholarly]

## 1. What the Aitareya Brahmana Is

- **The Hotṛ's Brāhmaṇa** — The Aitareya Brāhmaṇa (AB) is the karma-kāṇḍa ("action portion") prose manual attached to the **Ṛgveda**, expounding the duties of the **Hotṛ** ("invoker") and his three assistants (Maitrāvaruṇa, Acchāvāka, Grāvastut) in the Soma sacrifice. Where the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā supplies the ṛc to be recited, the AB explains *when, why, and to what ritual effect* they are recited. (Aitareya Brāhmaṇa)
- **Ascribed to Mahidāsa Aitareya** — Tradition credits the redaction to the sage **Mahidāsa Aitareya** ("son of Itarā"); a legend holds that Itarā was a slighted co-wife, and the boy, favoured by the Earth-goddess Mahī, was granted supreme Vedic knowledge — hence *Mahi-dāsa*. (Aitareya school tradition) [folk etiology]
- **Affiliation** — Belongs to the **Śākala / Aitareya śākhā** of the Ṛgveda, the only fully extant Ṛgvedic recension; its sibling Brāhmaṇa in the Bāṣkala/Kauṣītaki line is the **Kauṣītaki (Śāṅkhāyana) Brāhmaṇa**, which covers similar ground with its own emphases. (Śākhā tradition; cf. [`../rigveda.md`](../rigveda.md))
- **Scale and structure** — 40 *adhyāyas* (chapters) grouped into **8 *pañcikās*** of five chapters each. The first 30 chapters (six pañcikās) are held the older core; the last 10 (the **Mahāvrata** and Soma-sacrifice supplements) are reckoned a later addition. [scholarly]

## 2. The Ritual Programme (Soma and Royal Rites)

- **The Soma sacrifice (Agniṣṭoma core)** — The opening pañcikās treat the model Soma rite, the **Agniṣṭoma** ("praise of Agni"), and the wider *jyotiṣṭoma* family: the consecration (dīkṣā), the buying and pressing of Soma, the three daily pressings (savana), and the Hotṛ's recitations (śastra) that answer the Udgātṛ's chants (stotra). (AB, pañcikās 1–3) — ritual mechanics: [`../../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md`](../../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md).
- **Multi-day Soma rites** — Expounds the **ekāha** (one-day), **ahīna** (several-day), and **sattra** (long session) Soma sacrifices, including the **Dvādaśāha** (twelve-day) and the great **Gavām-ayana** year-long sattra. (AB, later pañcikās)
- **The Rājasūya (royal consecration)** — Pañcikā 8 (chs. 36–40) is devoted to the **Rājasūya**, the consecration of a king, and to the higher imperial inaugurations. It details the *abhiṣeka* (aspersion/anointing) by which the king is sprinkled with sanctified waters and elevated above his peers. (AB 8) — see royal ritual under [`../../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md`](../../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md).
- **The Punarabhiṣeka and Aindra-mahābhiṣeka** — Records the "great anointing of Indra" (Aindra Mahābhiṣeka) and lists legendary world-emperors said to have performed it and won universal dominion — a charter-list of cakravartin kings (e.g. Janamejaya, Bharata Dauḥṣanti, Śatānīka, Ambāṣṭhya). (AB 8.21–23) [regional/dynastic charter]
- **Mahāvrata day** — The closing chapters treat the **Mahāvrata** ("great vow") day of the Gavām-ayana, with its archaic and exuberant rites; flagged by scholars as an appended, somewhat heterogeneous final layer. [late text within AB]
- **The Hotṛ's recitation-forms** — Codifies the technical units the Hotṛ recites — single verse (ṛc), strophe (trca / pragātha), and whole hymn (sūkta) — and the great recitations: **Ājya, Praüga, Marutvatīya, Niṣkevalya, Vaiśvadeva, Āgnimāruta** śastras. (AB, throughout)

## 3. The Śunaḥśepa Legend (AB 7.13–18)

The AB's most celebrated narrative, recited within the Rājasūya frame as part of the royal consecration liturgy; one of the oldest sustained legends in Sanskrit prose.

- **Hariścandra's vow** — The childless Ikṣvāku king **Hariścandra**, on the counsel of the sage **Nārada**, prays to **Varuṇa** for a son, vowing to sacrifice the boy back to Varuṇa. A son, **Rohita**, is born. (AB 7.13)
- **The deferred sacrifice** — Hariścandra repeatedly postpones the promised offering ("wait till he is ten days old… till his teeth come… till he can bear arms"); Rohita, grown, refuses to be sacrificed and flees to the forest, wandering for years while Varuṇa's anger afflicts the king with dropsy. (AB 7.14)
- **The bought victim** — Rohita meets the impoverished ṛṣi **Ajīgarta Sauyavasi**, and buys his middle son **Śunaḥśepa** ("dog's tail") for **a hundred cows** to serve as substitute victim; the father sells, the eldest will not be sold, the youngest is kept. (AB 7.15)
- **Ajīgarta's degradation** — Varuṇa accepts the brāhmaṇa boy as a worthier substitute. At the sacrifice **no one will bind or slay the victim**; Ajīgarta, the father, offers to bind his own son for another hundred cows, then to slay him for a third hundred — the depth of a father's avarice. (AB 7.16)
- **Śunaḥśepa's deliverance** — Bound to the stake, Śunaḥśepa praises the gods with ṛc addressed to **Prajāpati, Agni, Savitṛ, Varuṇa, and the Viśvedevāḥ**; verse by verse his bonds loosen and the king is cured. (AB 7.17) — the hymns are identified with Ṛgvedic sūktas (notably RV 1.24–1.30 ascribed to Śunaḥśepa). (cf. [`../rigveda.md`](../rigveda.md))
- **Adoption by Viśvāmitra** — The seer **Viśvāmitra**, the officiating Hotṛ, adopts the rescued boy as his eldest son, renaming him **Devarāta** ("god-given"); when Viśvāmitra's hundred sons resist, the elder fifty are cursed to outcaste descent (the Andhras, Puṇḍras, Śabaras, etc.), the younger fifty accept him. (AB 7.18) [dynastic/regional etiology]
  - *Variant (Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa 1.61–62):* the tale is retold with King **Ambarīṣa** as the sacrificer whose victim escapes, Śunaḥśepa sold by his parents, and **Viśvāmitra** teaching him secret mantras to **Indra and Viṣṇu** that save him at Puṣkara — a markedly different framing from the AB. [regional]
  - *Variant (Purāṇic / later):* genealogical handbooks fold Śunaḥśepa-Devarāta into the Gāthin–Viśvāmitra lineage as a Kauśika; details of the curse-list vary by recension. [late text]

## 4. The Aitareya Aranyaka (the Forest Companion)

- **The forest treatise** — The **Aitareya Āraṇyaka** (AĀ) is the "forest text" (āraṇyaka) of the same Aitareya school: the transitional stratum between outward ritual (Brāhmaṇa) and inner knowledge (Upaniṣad), intended for the forest-dwelling student (vānaprastha) for whom physical sacrifice yields to symbolic meditation. (Aitareya Āraṇyaka)
- **Five books (āraṇyakas)** — Composed of five *āraṇyakas*; not all are of one age. Book I treats the **Mahāvrata** day contemplatively; Book II and III turn philosophical; Books IV–V are appendices (the **Mahānāmnī** verses and the **Nirbhuja / Pratṛṇṇa / Ubhayamantarā** recitation-modes), regarded as later additions, parts ascribed to **Āśvalāyana** and **Śaunaka**. [scholarly]
- **The uktha = prāṇa = Brahman equation** — Its speculative core internalizes the great **uktha** recitation as the breath of life (prāṇa) and ultimately Brahman, the move that turns ritual into metaphysics. (AĀ II)
- **Embeds the Aitareya Upaniṣad** — Within the **second āraṇyaka** (chapters 4–6 of book II) sits the **Aitareya Upaniṣad**, one of the ten **principal (mukhya) Upaniṣads** commented on by Śaṅkara. Cross-link: [`../../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../../principal-upanishads-detail.md).
  - **Creation by the Self** — Opens *"ātmā vā idam eka evāgra āsīt"* ("the Self/Ātman alone was this, one only, in the beginning"); the Self creates the worlds (ambhas, marīci, mara, āpas), then the cosmic Person (Puruṣa) and the deities, and finally enters his own creation through the crown of the head. (Aitareya Upaniṣad 1)
  - **Three births of the self** — Teaches the doctrine of a man's **three births** — as seed in the father, in the womb of the mother, and at death/rebirth — cited by the sage **Vāmadeva** who "knew Brahman even in the womb and flew up." (Aitareya Upaniṣad 2)
  - **Prajñānam Brahma** — Climaxes in the **mahāvākya** ("great saying") *"prajñānaṃ brahma"* ("Consciousness is Brahman"), the canonical Ṛgvedic mahāvākya among the four. (Aitareya Upaniṣad 3)
- **Kauṣītaki parallel** — The Bāṣkala/Kauṣītaki school's counterpart is the **Kauṣītaki Āraṇyaka**, which embeds the **Kauṣītaki Upaniṣad**. (Kauṣītaki school)

## 5. Why This Text Matters to the Timeline

- **A "first-tier" Brāhmaṇa the corpus only cites** — Across this knowledge base the AB is referenced as the authority for the Hotṛ's ritual, the Rājasūya, and the Śunaḥśepa story but rarely profiled in its own right; this file is that dedicated profile.
- **Cosmographic remark** — The AB supplies the oft-cited proto-astronomical line that **"the sun neither rises nor sets"** (it only turns its bright/dark side, AB 3.44).
- **Narrative anchor** — The Śunaḥśepa legend is the AB's chief contribution to the *story* corpus (the Hariścandra cycle, Viśvāmitra's adoption, the origin of the Andhra/Puṇḍra peoples), feeding into later Rāmāyaṇa and Purāṇic retellings.
