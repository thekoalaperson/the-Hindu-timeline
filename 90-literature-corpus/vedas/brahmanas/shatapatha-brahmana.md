# Shatapatha Brahmana

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/vedas/`](../README.md) → [Section README](../../README.md) → [Master Index](../../../README.md). Dedicated profile of the **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa** ("Brāhmaṇa of the Hundred Paths"), the vast prose theology of the **Śukla (White) Yajurveda** — the largest, most complete, and most studied of all Brāhmaṇas. Parent Veda: [`../yajurveda.md`](../yajurveda.md); its closing books are the [`../../upanishads/brihadaranyaka-upanishad.md`](../../upanishads/brihadaranyaka-upanishad.md). For the four-layer śruti scheme see [`../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) and the forest-stratum hinge [`../aranyakas.md`](../aranyakas.md).

Two clocks throughout: **scripture clock** (apauruṣeya, eternal Veda re-divided by Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa at the Dvāpara–Kali junction of the current 28th Mahāyuga, c. **3102 BCE**) vs **academic clock** (orally composed ritual prose, c. **900–700 BCE**, Iron-Age Kuru-Pañcāla heartland; among the later Brāhmaṇas). [scholarly]

---

## 1. What the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa Is

- **The Brāhmaṇa of the Śukla Yajurveda.** It is the explanatory prose layer attached to the **Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā** (VS), the pure-mantra collection of the "separated" White Yajurveda. In the Śukla arrangement, mantra (VS) and commentary (ŚB) are kept apart — the Śatapatha *is* that hived-off commentary. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa; cf. [`../yajurveda.md`](../yajurveda.md))
- **"Hundred Paths."** *Śata-patha* = "hundred paths / hundred chapters," from the **100 adhyāyas** of the dominant Mādhyandina recension. The name is referenced repeatedly across the later corpus as shorthand for the entire Śukla prose theology. (Eggeling, *SBE* introduction)
- **The largest Brāhmaṇa.** Its sheer bulk, systematic ritual coverage, and embedded Upaniṣad make it the single most important Brāhmaṇa for both ritual reconstruction and the history of Indian thought.
- **Ascribed to Yājñavalkya.** Tradition credits the sage **Yājñavalkya Vājasaneya** — recipient of the fresh solar revelation that founds the Śukla line — with the bulk of the text; the closing dialogues are explicitly his. [traditional ascription]

## 2. The Two Recensions (śākhās)

- **Mādhyandina recension** — the standard northern text; **14 kāṇḍas, 100 adhyāyas**, ~68 prapāṭhakas, ~7,624 kaṇḍikās. The Eggeling translation (*Sacred Books of the East*, 5 vols) renders this recension; the "hundred paths" count derives from it. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa, Mādhyandina)
- **Kāṇva recension** — the parallel southern/Deccan text; **17 kāṇḍas, 104 adhyāyas**, differently numbered and partly reordered, with some additional and reorganized material. Equally titled Śatapatha despite the higher chapter count. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa, Kāṇva)
- *Variant (book-numbering):* the Mādhyandina and Kāṇva divisions do **not** align one-to-one — the same ritual matter sits in differently numbered kāṇḍas, so citations must specify the recension. Never silently equate the two numbering systems. [disputed cross-reference]
- *Note:* the famous "Śatapatha" name fits the Mādhyandina 100-adhyāya count exactly; the 104-adhyāya Kāṇva inherits the title rather than generating it.

## 3. Prajāpati Cosmogony — the Theological Spine

- **Prajāpati as the creator-sacrifice.** The ŚB's governing myth is that **Prajāpati** ("Lord of Creatures"), the supreme generative principle, **emits the cosmos out of himself** and is thereby exhausted, dismembered, and "fallen apart" (*visrasta*). Ritual — above all the fire-altar — exists to **reintegrate Prajāpati**, putting the scattered creator back together brick by brick. (ŚB, Agnicayana kāṇḍas)
- **"The sacrifice is Prajāpati."** The recurring equation *yajño vai prajāpatiḥ* makes every rite a re-enactment of, and repair to, the cosmogonic act; the sacrificer (yajamāna) participates in re-creating the year, the gods, and himself. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa)
- **Year, Agni, and the year-altar.** Prajāpati is identified with the **year (saṃvatsara)** and with **Agni**; the fire-altar's 360 enclosing stones and 10,800 bricks encode the days and *muhūrtas* of the year, so building the altar literally rebuilds Time. (ŚB Agnicayana)
- **Speech and waters.** Subsidiary cosmogonies derive creation from **Vāc (Speech)**, from the cosmic **waters** depositing a golden egg (Hiraṇyagarbha-type motif), and from Prajāpati's *tapas* (heat/austerity). The ŚB layers these without merging them. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa)

## 4. The Matsya Flood — Manu and the Fish (ŚB 1.8.1)

- **Earliest extant Hindu flood narrative.** Located at **ŚB 1.8.1.1–6** (Mādhyandina), this is the oldest surviving Indian deluge story, centuries before its Purāṇic and epic expansions. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 1.8.1) [scholarly]
- **The fish's bargain.** **Manu** (here **Manu Vaivasvata**, son of Vivasvant), washing his hands, finds a tiny **fish (matsya)** in the water. The fish begs protection, promising in return to save Manu from a coming flood that will "carry away all creatures." (ŚB 1.8.1.1–4)
- **Rearing and warning.** Manu keeps the fish — moving it from jar to ditch to the sea as it grows huge — and the fish instructs him to **build a ship** and embark when the flood rises. (ŚB 1.8.1.4–5)
- **The towing and the mountain.** When the deluge comes, the fish tows Manu's ship by a rope tied to its **horn** to the **northern mountain**, where Manu fastens the ship and waits out the receding waters. (ŚB 1.8.1.5–6)
- **Manu the sole survivor and re-progenitor.** Manu alone survives; he performs a sacrifice (the **pāka-yajña**), pours an oblation into the waters, and from it after a year arises a woman, **Iḍā (Iḍā/Iḷā)**, with whom he generates the renewed human race — Manu as the second progenitor of mankind. (ŚB 1.8.1.6–10)
- *Variant (later layer):* in the ŚB the rescuing fish is **not yet** identified as Viṣṇu; the explicit **Matsya-avatāra of Viṣṇu** identification and the rescue of the Vedas/seven sages are Purāṇic-epic developments (Matsya, Bhāgavata, Mahābhārata). Keep the ŚB version distinct. [late text — for the avatāra reading]

## 5. The Great Ritual Complexes

- **Agnihotra & the model iṣṭis.** Early kāṇḍas expound the daily fire-oblation and the new-/full-moon sacrifices (darśapūrṇamāsa) as the paradigm haviryajñas. (ŚB kāṇḍas 1–2)
- **Pravargya.** The intensely esoteric **hot-milk rite**: the **Mahāvīra** clay pot is heated red-hot and milk-and-ghee poured in, the glowing vessel identified with the **Sun, with Agni, and with the head of the sacrifice (Makha/Viṣṇu's severed head)**. The decapitation-of-the-sacrifice myth (the bow-string severing Viṣṇu's head, which becomes the sun) anchors the rite. (ŚB kāṇḍa 14, Pravargya section) [esoteric]
- **Agnicayana — the fire-altar.** The ŚB's centerpiece: the months-long construction of the **uttara-vedi** from **10,800 fired bricks** laid in **five layers** in the shape of a **bird (śyena / falcon)**, rebuilding the dismembered Prajāpati and Agni. Includes the **placement of the golden man (puruṣa) and the live tortoise, the heads of five victims, and the year-symbolism** of the bricks. The single most elaborate śrauta rite. (ŚB kāṇḍas 6–10; cf. VS adhy. 11–18)
- **Aśvamedha — the horse sacrifice.** The royal **horse-sacrifice** of universal sovereignty: a consecrated stallion roams free for a year guarded by warriors, then is sacrificed amid the queen's symbolic union with the dead horse, asserting the king's cosmic dominion. (ŚB kāṇḍa 13; cf. VS adhy. 22–25)
- **Rājasūya, Vājapeya, Soma.** The ŚB also details the royal consecration (rājasūya), the "draught of strength" (vājapeya), and the soma-sacrifice machinery underpinning all the above. (ŚB; cf. VS adhy. 9–10)

## 6. The Closing Books — Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad

- **The Āraṇyaka–Upaniṣad telescope.** The Śukla line runs Saṃhitā → Brāhmaṇa → Āraṇyaka → Upaniṣad as one continuous text; the **final kāṇḍa of the Śatapatha** *is* its Āraṇyaka-plus-Upaniṣad stratum. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa, closing kāṇḍa)
- **Location.** The **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad** forms **Mādhyandina kāṇḍa 14 (final adhyāyas) / Kāṇva book 17** — the oldest and longest principal Upaniṣad, dominated by **Yājñavalkya** (the dialogues with Maitreyī, Gārgī, and King Janaka; *neti neti*; early Ātman = Brahman). The Pravargya material (§5) also sits in this last kāṇḍa, just before the Upaniṣadic chapters. — [`../../upanishads/brihadaranyaka-upanishad.md`](../../upanishads/brihadaranyaka-upanishad.md). [scholarly]

---

## 7. Dating — Two Clocks

- **Scripture clock.** Apauruṣeya prose "heard" by ṛṣis and arranged in the Dvāpara compilation era around **3102 BCE**; the Śukla branch is the fresh solar revelation to **Yājñavalkya** (the Sūrya/horse myth — see [`../yajurveda.md`](../yajurveda.md)).
- **Academic clock.** Composed orally c. **900–700 BCE**, among the **later** Brāhmaṇas, in the Kuru-Pañcāla / eastward (Videha-Kosala) Iron-Age zone; the Bṛhadāraṇyaka stratum is early-to-mid Upaniṣadic (c. 700–500 BCE). The Kāṇva and Mādhyandina recensions reflect distinct regional transmission. [scholarly]
