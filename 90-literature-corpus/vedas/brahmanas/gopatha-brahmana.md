# Gopatha Brāhmaṇa

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/vedas/`](../README.md) → [Section README](../../README.md) → [Master Index](../../../README.md). The **sole surviving Brāhmaṇa of the Atharvaveda** — the prose ritual-and-speculation manual attached to the fourth Veda, expounding the supremacy of the **Brahman** priest in the sacrifice. Parent Veda: [`../atharvaveda.md`](../atharvaveda.md). For the four-layer śruti scheme (Saṃhitā → Brāhmaṇa → Āraṇyaka → Upaniṣad) see [`../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) and the forest-stratum hinge [`../aranyakas.md`](../aranyakas.md).

Two clocks throughout: **scripture clock** — apauruṣeya, eternal Veda re-divided by Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa at the Dvāpara–Kali junction of the current 28th Mahāyuga (c. **3102 BCE**); the Brāhmaṇa prose is the ritual exegesis carried in the same transmission. **Academic clock** — orally composed and compiled prose, reckoned the **latest of the surviving Brāhmaṇas**, c. **600–300 BCE** (some place it later still), heavily dependent on earlier Brāhmaṇas it excerpts. [scholarly]

---

## 1. What the Gopatha Brāhmaṇa Is

- **The Atharvaveda's only Brāhmaṇa** — The Gopatha Brāhmaṇa (GB) is the lone extant Brāhmaṇa attached to the **Atharvaveda**; where the Ṛgveda has the Aitareya/Kauṣītaki, the Sāmaveda the Pañcaviṃśa/Ṣaḍviṃśa, and the Yajurveda the Śatapatha/Taittirīya, the Atharvan school is served by this single late text. (Gopatha Brāhmaṇa) — parent Veda: [`../atharvaveda.md`](../atharvaveda.md).
- **The Brahman-priest's manual** — Its governing concern is the office of the **Brahman** (the silent fourth chief priest / *purohita*), the overseer who supervises the whole sacrifice and heals its errors; the GB argues this priest's Veda — the Atharvaveda — is therefore the highest. (GB Pūrvabhāga)
- **Affiliation** — Transmitted in the **Śaunaka** recension of the Atharvaveda (the Śaunakīya śākhā), and associated by tradition with the **Paippalāda** material as well; the name *Gopatha* ("lord/protector of cattle/paths") is variously explained, none decisively. (Śaunaka tradition) [disputed etymology]
- **Two parts** — Divided into two unequal sections: the **Pūrvabhāga** ("former part," 5 prapāṭhakas) and the **Uttarabhāga** ("latter part," 6 prapāṭhakas). The Pūrvabhāga is the more speculative and original; the Uttarabhāga is largely **ritual matter excerpted from older Brāhmaṇas**. (GB, 11 prapāṭhakas total) [scholarly]

## 2. The Pūrvabhāga — Brahmaveda and Praṇava Speculation

- **The Atharvaveda as Brahmaveda** — The Pūrvabhāga opens by elevating the Atharvaveda as the **Brahmaveda**, the "Veda of the Brahman/Brahman-knowledge," declaring it the source and crown of the other three Vedas rather than a mere fourth appendix. (GB 1.1) [self-elevation of the Atharvan school]
- **Cosmogonic frame** — Recounts a creation sequence in which **Brahman** (the absolute) and the cosmic waters generate the worlds and the Vedas; the Atharvan corpus is woven into the origin of knowledge itself, grounding the school's primacy claim in cosmogony. (GB Pūrvabhāga 1)
- **The supremacy of the Brahman priest** — Argues at length that the sacrifice has **four chief priests** — Hotṛ (Ṛgveda), Udgātṛ (Sāmaveda), Adhvaryu (Yajurveda), and **Brahman** — and that the Brahman, who must "know all three Vedas and the fourth," presides over and corrects the rite; a sacrifice without a true Brahman is "one-winged" and fails. (GB Pūrvabhāga) — ritual office context: [`../../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md`](../../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md).
- **Om / praṇava speculation** — Contains sustained speculation on the syllable **Om** (the *praṇava*), analysing its mystical structure and identifying it with Brahman and with the totality of the Vedas — material that prefigures the Atharvan Upaniṣads' Om-meditation. (GB Pūrvabhāga)
- **Gāyatrī and the vyāhṛtis** — Expounds the **Gāyatrī** mantra and the **vyāhṛtis** (the ritual utterances **bhūḥ, bhuvaḥ, svaḥ**), correlating them with the worlds, the Vedas, and the breaths; treats their recitation as the verbal skeleton of the cosmos. (GB Pūrvabhāga)
- **Praise of brahmacarya and the brāhmaṇa** — Includes passages exalting Vedic studentship (brahmacarya), the duties and dignity of the brāhmaṇa, and the dīkṣā (consecration), framing the Atharvan priest as guardian of the sacrifice's totality. (GB Pūrvabhāga)

## 3. The Uttarabhāga — Borrowed Ritual Matter

- **A digest of Soma ritual** — The Uttarabhāga turns to the mechanics of the **Soma sacrifice** — the dīkṣā, the pravargya, the savanas (pressings), the graha (Soma-draughts), and the priests' recitations — but does so largely by **drawing on the earlier Brāhmaṇas** rather than original composition. (GB Uttarabhāga) — cf. the fuller Soma programmes in [`./aitareya-brahmana.md`](./aitareya-brahmana.md) and [`./shatapatha-brahmana.md`](./shatapatha-brahmana.md).
- **Acknowledged dependence / borrowing** — Scholars have shown the Uttarabhāga **excerpts and paraphrases** passages traceable to the **Aitareya, Kauṣītaki, and Śatapatha** Brāhmaṇas (and Sāmavedic material), making the GB a compiler's text that presupposes the others — a chief argument for its late date. (GB Uttarabhāga) [scholarly]
- **The Brahman's corrective role in ritual** — Re-states, within concrete ritual settings, the Pūrvabhāga's thesis: the Brahman priest watches over the sacrifice and applies **prāyaścitta** (expiatory correction) when error occurs, so that the rite is made whole. (GB Uttarabhāga)
- **Heterogeneous appendices** — The latter prapāṭhakas read as a somewhat loosely assembled collection of ritual notes and explanations, reinforcing the picture of the GB as a late, secondary compilation rather than a unified school-composition. [late text]

## 4. Dating, Recension, and Disputes

- **The latest Brāhmaṇa** — On linguistic grounds and because of its borrowings, the GB is widely judged the **youngest of the extant Brāhmaṇas**, post-dating even the Śatapatha; estimates run from c. **600 BCE down to the early centuries BCE / CE**. [scholarly] [disputed dating]
- **Relation to the Atharvan recensions** — Belongs to the **Śaunaka** transmission; its relationship to the **Paippalāda** recension and to the lost Atharvan ritual literature (the *Vaitāna* and *Kauśika* Sūtras, with which it is closely connected) is debated. (Atharvaveda / Śaunaka recension) — see [`../atharvaveda.md`](../atharvaveda.md). [disputed]
- **Original vs. excerpted** — The standard view (since the 19th-century editions) splits the text: **Pūrvabhāga = the school's own speculative theology; Uttarabhāga = borrowed ritualism** — though the exact boundaries and the priority of some shared passages remain contested. [scholarly] [disputed]

## 5. Why This Text Matters to the Timeline

- **Completes the Brāhmaṇa shelf** — With the Aitareya/Kauṣītaki (Ṛgveda), Pañcaviṃśa (Sāmaveda), and Śatapatha/Taittirīya (Yajurveda) profiled, the GB is the **Atharvaveda's representative on the Brāhmaṇa shelf**, finishing the four-Veda set of ritual-prose layers.
- **Charter for the fourth Veda** — Its lasting historical contribution is doctrinal, not narrative: the **argument that the Atharvaveda is the Brahmaveda and its Brahman priest the supreme overseer of sacrifice** — the textual self-elevation by which the fourth Veda secured full canonical standing.
- **Matrix of the Atharvan Upaniṣads** — Its Om/praṇava, Gāyatrī, and Brahman speculation is the **prose matrix behind the great Atharvan Upaniṣads** — the [`../../upanishads/mundaka-upanishad.md`](../../upanishads/mundaka-upanishad.md), the **Praśna**, and the **Māṇḍūkya** (the supreme Om-treatise) — which carry the same school's meditation on the syllable and Brahman into the Vedānta. [scholarly]
- **A text the corpus cites but rarely profiles** — Elsewhere in this knowledge base the GB is invoked only as "the Atharvaveda's Brāhmaṇa"; this file is its dedicated profile.
