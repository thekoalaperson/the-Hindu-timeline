# Taittiriya Brahmana (and Taittiriya Aranyaka)

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/vedas/brahmanas/`](../README.md) → [Vedas section](../README.md) → [Master Index](../../../README.md). The principal **Brāhmaṇa of the Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda**, attached to the **Taittirīya śākhā** — the prose ritual manual of the **Adhvaryu** priest, and the only Veda whose Brāhmaṇa stratum survives as a continuous, separately-titled book. Parent Veda: [`../yajurveda.md`](../yajurveda.md). Forest-companion stratum: [`../aranyakas.md`](../aranyakas.md). Śruti/yuga framing and the four-layer (Saṃhitā → Brāhmaṇa → Āraṇyaka → Upaniṣad) scheme: [`../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md).

Two clocks throughout: **scripture clock** — apauruṣeya Veda "re-heard" and re-divided by Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa at the Dvāpara–Kali junction of the current 28th Mahāyuga (c. **3102 BCE**); the Taittirīya prose is ritual exegesis carried in the same eternal transmission. **Academic clock** — orally composed Brāhmaṇa prose, c. **800–600 BCE**, late-Brāhmaṇa stratum of the Iron-Age Kuru-Pañcāla / later South-Indian Adhvaryu tradition. [scholarly]

---

## 1. What the Taittiriya Brahmana Is

- **The Adhvaryu's Brāhmaṇa** — The Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa (TB) is the karma-kāṇḍa ("action portion") prose manual of the **Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda**, expounding the *yajus* (sacrificial formulae) handled by the **Adhvaryu** ("performer"), the priest who physically does the rite. Where the Śukla (White) Yajurveda separates mantra (Saṃhitā) from prose (Brāhmaṇa), the **Black** Yajurveda characteristically **mixes mantra and exegetical prose together**; the TB continues that very prose where the Taittirīya Saṃhitā (Taittirīya Saṃhitā / "Apastamba" recension) leaves off. (Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa) — see the kṛṣṇa/śukla split at [`../yajurveda.md`](../yajurveda.md).
- **The "partridge" school** — *Taittirīya* derives from the sage **Tittiri** (lit. "partridge"); a celebrated legend (Viṣṇu Purāṇa) holds that the pupils of **Vaiśampāyana**, made to disgorge the Yajus, took the form of partridges (*tittiri*) and picked up the scattered texts — hence the "Black/mixed" Yajurveda and the partridge name. (Taittirīya school tradition) [folk etiology]
- **Affiliation** — The TB belongs to the **Taittirīya śākhā**, the dominant living recension of the Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda in **South India** (Āndhra, Drāviḍa, Karṇāṭa lines). Its sibling prose in the rival Black-Yajurvedic schools (Maitrāyaṇī, Kāṭhaka, Kapiṣṭhala) is fused into their Saṃhitās rather than standing as a separate Brāhmaṇa. (Śākhā tradition)
- **Scale and structure — 3 *kāṇḍas* (aṣṭakas)**, subdivided into *prapāṭhakas* (a.k.a. *praśna*) and *anuvākas*:
  - **Kāṇḍa 1** — the **Agnihotra**, the **Agnyādhāna** (setting up the sacred fires), the **Vājapeya** and the royal **Rājasūya** consecration. (TB 1)
  - **Kāṇḍa 2** — the **Soma** sacrifices, the **Agnihotra** continued, the **Sautrāmaṇī**, and assorted iṣṭis (offerings). (TB 2)
  - **Kāṇḍa 3** — the **Nakṣatra-iṣṭi** (constellation offerings), the **Puruṣamedha**, the **Aśvamedha** supplements, and the **Nāciketa** fire. (TB 3) [richest narrative kāṇḍa]

## 2. The Prajāpati Cosmogony (TB 2.2–2.3)

- **Prajāpati as the sacrifice** — The TB's recurrent cosmogonic refrain is that **Prajāpati**, the Lord of Creatures, is himself the primal sacrificer and the sacrifice; he emits the worlds, the gods, and the year (saṃvatsara) out of his own being through tapas (heat/austerity). (TB 2.2–2.3)
- **Creation from the waters** — In one account the universe **"was water, a flood"** (āpo vā idam agre salilam āsīt); Prajāpati arises and, desiring offspring, generates the gods and the metres (chandas) — the move that grounds the ritual year in cosmogony. (TB, cosmogonic anuvākas)
- **The exhausted creator** — A signature Brāhmaṇa motif: having emitted creation Prajāpati is **drained, "disjointed," near collapse**, and must be **restored and re-membered by the sacrifice** (the Agnicayana / fire-altar logic) — sacrifice repairs the cosmos by reconstituting Prajāpati. (TB 2; cf. Śatapatha parallels) [scholarly]
- **The year and the metres** — Prajāpati is equated with the **year** and with the **fire-altar of 360 bricks**; the cosmogony is mapped onto the calendar, so that performing the rite re-enacts and sustains the original creation. (TB 2–3)
- *Variant (Śukla Yajurveda / Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa):* the fullest "Prajāpati creates, is exhausted, is restored by Agni/the altar" cycle is developed at length in the **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa**; the TB carries the same theology in more compressed Kṛṣṇa-Yajurvedic form. [parallel recension]

## 3. The Nāciketa Frame (TB 3.11.8) — Root of the Kaṭha Upaniṣad

The single most consequential narrative seed in the TB: the **Nāciketa(s)** episode, the ritual germ later expanded into the **Kaṭha Upaniṣad's** dialogue of the boy and Death.

- **The Nāciketa fire** — TB 3.11.8 expounds the **Nāciketa Agni**, a special fire-altar/rite, framed by the story of a boy, **Naciketas**, given over to **Yama** (Death). The Brāhmaṇa's interest is liturgical — the building and merit of the Nāciketa fire — not yet the metaphysics of the Upaniṣad. (TB 3.11.8)
- **The gift of the boy to Death** — The father **Vājaśravasa** (Uddālaka Āruṇi / "Auddālaki Āruṇi" in the later Upaniṣad), performing a sacrifice of all his goods, in anger consigns his son Naciketas to **Yama**. The boy goes to the house of Death. (TB 3.11.8)
- **The three nights and the boon** — Naciketas waits **three nights** in Yama's house unfed; Yama, to make amends for the unhonoured guest, grants him **boons** — in the Brāhmaṇa the central boon is the **knowledge of the Nāciketa fire** that conquers re-death (punar-mṛtyu). (TB 3.11.8)
- *Variant — expansion in the Kaṭha Upaniṣad (Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda, Kaṭha śākhā):* the same frame is enlarged into **three boons** — (1) the father's peace, (2) the Nāciketa fire, and (3) the famous request to know **what lies beyond death** — and into Yama's discourse on the Self (Ātman), the chariot-of-the-body simile, and *"uttiṣṭhata jāgrata"* ("arise, awake"). The Upaniṣad supplies the philosophy the Brāhmaṇa only gestures at. [later text — direct outgrowth] (Kaṭha Upaniṣad 1)
- *Variant (Mahābhārata / Anuśāsana-parva, Nāciketa-upākhyāna):* a heaven-and-hell vision retelling in which Naciketas, sent to Yama, is shown the rewards of the gift of a cow — a distinct, more folkloric branch of the same name. [regional/epic]

## 4. The Nakṣatra Deity-Lists (TB 1.5; 3.1)

- **The constellation offerings (Nakṣatra-iṣṭi)** — The TB is the **canonical Vedic source for the system of the lunar mansions** (nakṣatra): it lists the **nakṣatras with their presiding deities** and prescribes offerings to each, the textual backbone of later Hindu calendar and astrology. (TB 1.5.1; 3.1.1–2)
- **The deity-roster** — Pairs each asterism with its devatā, e.g. **Kṛttikā → Agni**, **Rohiṇī → Prajāpati**, **Mṛgaśiras → Soma**, **Ārdrā → Rudra**, **Punarvasu → Aditi**, **Puṣya → Bṛhaspati**, **Maghā → the Pitṛs**, **Citrā → Tvaṣṭṛ**, **Anurādhā → Mitra**, **Jyeṣṭhā → Indra**, **Mūla → Nirṛti**, **Śravaṇa → Viṣṇu**, **Revatī → Pūṣan**, etc. (TB 3.1) [open-ended roster — full 27/28-fold list varies by edition]
- *Variant (count 27 vs 28):* the TB's list is given with **Abhijit** included (28 nakṣatras) in some recitations and omitted (27) in others; the deity assignments are largely stable across recensions but the head of the list (Kṛttikā- vs Aśvinī-initial) reflects the older Vedic Kṛttikā-start. [scholarly/calendrical]
- **Why it matters** — These lists are the **earliest systematic nakṣatra theology** and feed directly into Jyotiṣa, muhūrta astrology, and the festival calendar. (TB) — a prose text the wider corpus cites for the lunar-mansion deities but rarely profiles.

## 5. The Taittiriya Aranyaka (the Forest Companion)

- **The forest treatise** — The **Taittirīya Āraṇyaka** (TĀ) is the "forest text" of the same Taittirīya school: the largest and ritually richest surviving Āraṇyaka, continuing the TB's prose into the esoteric and meditative register. Full profile: [`../aranyakas.md`](../aranyakas.md) §4. (Taittirīya Āraṇyaka)
- **Ten prapāṭhakas** — Mixed mantra-and-prose, characteristically Kṛṣṇa-Yajurvedic:
  - **Prapāṭhaka 1 — Āruṇa-praśna ("Aruṇam") / Sūrya-namaskāra** — a celebrated solar-cosmological recitation. (TĀ 1)
  - **Prapāṭhakas 2–3** — investiture (upanayana), svādhyāya, and ritual prose. (TĀ 2–3)
  - **Prapāṭhakas 4–5 — the Pravargya** — the hot-milk **Gharma** (Mahāvīra) rite, the Āraṇyaka's esoteric ritual core. (TĀ 4–5) [esoteric]
  - **Prapāṭhaka 6 — Pitṛmedha** — funerary rites for the ancestors. (TĀ 6)
  - **Prapāṭhakas 7–9 — the Taittirīya Upaniṣad** — the three vallīs (Śikṣā-, Brahmānanda-/Ānanda-, Bhṛgu-vallī); home of the *"satyaṃ jñānam anantaṃ brahma"* and the **pañcakośa** (five-sheath) and **ānanda-mīmāṃsā** (calculus of bliss) teachings. (TĀ 7–9)
  - **Prapāṭhaka 10 — the Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad** — see below. (TĀ 10)
- **Embeds the Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad** — TĀ prapāṭhaka 10 **is**, in its entirety, the **Mahānārāyaṇa (Yājñikī) Upaniṣad**, the concluding and most theistic stratum — hymns to **Nārāyaṇa** as supreme Brahman / Antaryāmin, and the source of the standard **sandhyā-vandana**, **prāṇāyāma**, and **sannyāsa** mantras. Full profile: [`../../upanishads/mahanarayana-upanishad.md`](../../upanishads/mahanarayana-upanishad.md). (Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad)
  - *Variant (recension/length):* survives in an **Āndhra (~64-anuvāka)** and a shorter **Drāviḍa** recension; anuvāka totals differ across editions. [regional]
- **Puruṣa-sūkta variants** — The TĀ and its appended praśnas house a **Yajurvedic recension of the Puruṣa-sūkta** (the Cosmic-Man / sacrificial-dismemberment hymn, cf. Ṛgveda 10.90), expanded with additional verses (the **Uttara-nārāyaṇa** continuation) beyond the Ṛgvedic original — the form recited liturgically in the Vaiṣṇava South. (TĀ 3 / 10) [liturgical core]
- **Other embedded sūktas** — **Nārāyaṇa-sūkta**, **Bhū-/Nīla-sūkta**, **Durgā-sūkta**, **Medhā-sūktas**, and the **Mahāmṛtyuñjaya / Tryambaka** mantra are all drawn from or housed in the TĀ. (Taittirīya Āraṇyaka)

## 6. Why This Text Matters to the Timeline

- **A "first-tier" prose text the corpus only cites** — Across this knowledge base the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa/Āraṇyaka is referenced for the Adhvaryu's ritual, the nakṣatra deities, the Prajāpati cosmogony, and the Nāciketa frame, but rarely profiled in its own right; this file is that dedicated profile.
- **Narrative anchor** — The **Nāciketa episode (TB 3.11.8)** is the demonstrable scriptural root of the **Kaṭha Upaniṣad**, one of the clearest Brāhmaṇa-to-Upaniṣad expansions in the corpus.
- **Calendrical anchor** — The **nakṣatra deity-lists** are the earliest systematic lunar-mansion theology, the seedbed of Jyotiṣa and the Hindu festival calendar.
- **Theological hinge** — Through its Āraṇyaka (TĀ 10 = Mahānārāyaṇa) the Taittirīya line carries the **emergence of Nārāyaṇa/Vaiṣṇava theism** out of Vedic ritualism. See [`../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md).
