# Jaiminīya (Talavakāra) Brāhmaṇa

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/vedas/brahmanas/`](../README.md) → [Vedas section](../../README.md) → [Master Index](../../../README.md). Dedicated profile of the **Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa** (JB), the principal Brāhmaṇa of the southern **Jaiminīya / Talavakāra** recension of the [Sāmaveda](../samaveda.md) — with the [Śatapatha](./shatapatha-brahmana.md) the **richest narrative-myth Brāhmaṇa** in the whole corpus. Sister-manual and its Kauthuma–Rāṇāyanīya counterpart: the [Pañcaviṃśa (Tāṇḍya) Brāhmaṇa](./panchavimsha-brahmana.md). Forest/secret stratum: [`../aranyakas.md`](../aranyakas.md).

Two clocks throughout: **scripture clock** — apauruṣeya Veda "re-heard" and re-divided by Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa at the Dvāpara–Kali junction of the current 28th Mahāyuga (c. **3102 BCE**); the Sāmavedic chant-exegesis is carried in the same eternal transmission, taught by Vyāsa's pupil **Jaimini** and his line (whence *Jaiminīya*). **Academic clock** — orally composed Brāhmaṇa prose, reckoned among the **older** Brāhmaṇa stratum (roughly contemporary with the older Śatapatha layers and the Pañcaviṃśa), c. **900–700 BCE**. [scholarly]

---

## 1. What the Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa Is

- **The Brāhmaṇa of the Talavakāra śākhā** — The JB is the great prose ritual-manual of the **Jaiminīya** (also called **Talavakāra**) recension of the [Sāmaveda](../samaveda.md), the third of the Sāmaveda's principal schools alongside the Kauthuma and Rāṇāyanīya. It expounds the **Udgātṛ** singing-priest's soma-chant — the same liturgical ground covered by the [Pañcaviṃśa Brāhmaṇa](./panchavimsha-brahmana.md). (Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa)
- **A southern recension** — Where the Kauthuma–Rāṇāyanīya Pañcaviṃśa is the broadly "northern/standard" Sāmavedic Brāhmaṇa, the Jaiminīya was preserved chiefly in the **South** — among small surviving communities in **Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Karnataka** — which is why it long remained little known and incompletely transmitted. [regional]
- **"Jaiminīya" and "Talavakāra"** — *Jaiminīya* names the school after **Jaimini**, traditionally the Sāmaveda-pupil of Vyāsa; *Talavakāra* is the older Vedic name for the same śākhā (whence the Talavakāra Āraṇyaka and the **Talavakāra = Kena Upaniṣad**). The two labels denote one and the same branch. (Sāmaveda Jaiminīya śākhā)
- **The discursive, story-laden Brāhmaṇa** — Its hallmark is **style**: where the Pañcaviṃśa is terse and technical, the JB is **expansive and narrative**, repeatedly breaking off ritual exposition to tell a legend, an etiological tale, or a contest-myth explaining a sāman. (Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa) [scholarly]

## 2. Structure — Three Books and a Disputed Numbering

- **Three books (JB I–III)** — The text is transmitted in **three sections**, conventionally cited as **JB I, II, III**. Book **I** centres on the **Agnihotra** and the daily/foundational rites; books **II–III** treat the **soma** sacrifices and the long sacrificial sessions (**sattras**, the **Gavām-ayana** year-rite, the **Dvādaśāha**, and the chant-contests). (Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa I–III)
- **Partial / incomplete transmission** — The JB survived only in **fragments and imperfect manuscripts**; no single complete critical edition of the whole exists, and substantial portions are reconstructed from defective copies. Long known to Western scholarship only through extracts before fuller manuscripts surfaced. [scholarly]
- **Disputed section/paragraph numbering** — Because the editions are **partial and built from different manuscripts**, the **book-, chapter-, and paragraph-numbering of the JB varies across the printed texts**; the same legend may be cited under different numbers in different editions. *Citations must name the edition.* Never silently equate one edition's numbering with another's. [disputed]
  - *Variant (editions):* the JB is drawn on through several layered publications — early selections, the Raghu Vira–Lokesh Chandra text, and the studies/translations of W. Caland and (for the myths) H. W. Bodewitz and W. D. O'Flaherty — which do not all divide and number the material identically. [scholarly]

## 3. A Treasury of Vedic Myth — JB and Śatapatha

- **The richest narrative Brāhmaṇa, with the Śatapatha** — The JB is, alongside the [Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa](./shatapatha-brahmana.md), the **fullest Brāhmaṇa reservoir of myth and folktale**, frequently preserving **older or wilder versions** of legends than the parallel Pañcaviṃśa or other Brāhmaṇas give. (Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa) [scholarly]
- **Bhṛgu's vision of the eaters and the eaten** — Its most celebrated narrative: the proud young **Bhṛgu**, son of **Varuṇa**, is humbled and sent by his father into the **other world**, where he witnesses **men being cut up and devoured, screaming, by the very men, animals, trees, and plants they had themselves eaten, felled, or used in this life** — a vision of the cosmos as mutual consumption. He returns terrified, and **Varuṇa** teaches him the **Agnihotra** as the rite that frees the sacrificer from being "food" in the world to come. This is a foundational early statement of **cosmic reciprocity / proto-karmic retribution** and of an **afterworld of requital**. (Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa I.42–44) [creation/eschatology]
  - *Variant ([Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa](./shatapatha-brahmana.md) 11.6.1):* the same **Bhṛgu-and-Varuṇa** other-world journey is told with its own emphases; the JB telling is generally reckoned the **fuller and more vivid**, and is often treated as the locus classicus of the "eaten in the next world" motif. [parallel]
- **Proto-karma reciprocity** — The Bhṛgu legend, with related JB passages on the dead being weighed and requited, supplies one of the **earliest pre-Upaniṣadic intimations of retributive reciprocity** between this world and the next — a conceptual seed of the later doctrine of karma. (Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa) [scholarly]
- **Divine contest-myths** — The JB preserves numerous **contest- and rivalry-myths of the gods** — Indra's exploits, the gods' quarrels with the Asuras, races and wagers, the seizing and purchase of **soma** — many of them **etiologies attached to particular sāmans**, explaining which chant secured which cosmic victory. (Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa)

## 4. The Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa (Talavakāra Āraṇyaka)

- **The school's esoteric stratum** — The Jaiminīya line also transmits a separate, more **secret/mystical** text, the **Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa (JUB)**, also called the **Talavakāra Āraṇyaka** — its forest/Āraṇyaka-level stratum, concerned with the **hidden meaning of the chant, the syllable Om (the Udgītha), breath, and the Self**. (Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa; cf. [`../aranyakas.md`](../aranyakas.md))
- **Matrix of the Kena Upaniṣad** — Embedded within the JUB / Talavakāra Āraṇyaka is the **Kena Upaniṣad**, accordingly also titled the **Talavakāra Upaniṣad** — the short, celebrated Upaniṣad ("By whom? — *kena*") on Brahman as the unknowable power behind mind, speech, and the senses, and the Umā-Haimavatī parable. See [`../../upanishads/kena-upanishad.md`](../../upanishads/kena-upanishad.md). (Kena / Talavakāra Upaniṣad)
- **Brāhmaṇa → Āraṇyaka → Upaniṣad in one school** — The Jaiminīya thus offers a near-complete Sāmavedic ladder: **Saṃhitā (Jaiminīya Sāmaveda) → Brāhmaṇa (JB) → Upaniṣad-Brāhmaṇa / Āraṇyaka (JUB) → Upaniṣad (Kena)** — paralleling the way the Kauthuma line runs to the Chāndogya. [scholarly]

## 5. Why It Matters to the Timeline

- **The Udgātṛ's myth-half** — With the [Pañcaviṃśa](./panchavimsha-brahmana.md) it completes the chant-priest's literature, but it is the JB that supplies the **stories**: where the Pañcaviṃśa gives the system, the JB gives the **legends behind the system**.
- **An afterworld and a karmic seed** — The **Bhṛgu vision** is among the earliest Indian depictions of a **moralized afterworld of requital**, a key data-point for the prehistory of **karma and rebirth** before their Upaniṣadic crystallization.
- **A bridge to the Kena** — Through the Talavakāra Āraṇyaka the JB school is the **direct textual home of the [Kena Upaniṣad](../../upanishads/kena-upanishad.md)**, tying this Brāhmaṇa to the mainstream of Vedānta.
- **A cautionary citation case** — Its **partial transmission and unstable numbering** make the JB a standing reminder that Brāhmaṇa references must be edition-specific. [disputed]
