# The Pratishakhyas (Veda-specific phonetic treatises)

*[Vedas](./README.md) · [Literature Corpus](../README.md) · [Master Index](../../README.md)*
The **Prātiśākhyas** (prati-śākhā, "[treatise] for each branch") are the oldest extant works of Indian linguistic science: Veda-specific phonetic-grammatical manuals that fixed the precise oral text of each [śākhā](rigveda.md#shakha-recensions--branches). They are the technical engine behind the [padapāṭha and the permutation recitations (krama/jaṭā/ghana)](rigveda.md#recitation-patha-styles-and-oral-transmission) — the apparatus of zero-corruption Vedic transmission and the direct ancestors of Pāṇini's grammar. They sit within the **Śikṣā** vedānga (phonetics); see [Vedāngas](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md#2-the-six-vedangas--limbs-of-the-veda).

## What a Prātiśākhya Is and Does

- **Core function** — A Prātiśākhya is a rule-book that derives the continuous *saṃhitāpāṭha* (text with euphonic combination / sandhi) from the analytic *padapāṭha* (the word-by-word text), and vice versa. It legislates exactly how words join, how accents shift at junctures, and how sounds are pronounced — so that any reciter reproduces the *same* sound-stream. (Prātiśākhya tradition)
- **One per branch, not one for all** — Each Prātiśākhya is tied to a specific śākhā (recension) of a specific Veda; the rules describe *that* branch's actual recited text, which is why no single Prātiśākhya serves the whole Veda. (Prātiśākhya = "belonging to a [particular] branch")
- **Subject matter** — Treats *varṇa* (phonemes), *svara* (the three Vedic accents: udātta, anudātta, svarita), *mātrā* (vowel duration), *sandhi* (euphonic combination), *krama* (the step-recitation that mechanically tests word order), gemination, nasalization, and the conversion rules between pada and saṃhitā. (Vedic phonetics)
- **The padapāṭha link** — Each Prātiśākhya presupposes an authoritative padapāṭha (e.g. **Śākalya's** padapāṭha of the Ṛgveda) as its analytic base, then states the rules that re-combine those isolated words into the flowing chant. The pada/saṃhitā pair plus the Prātiśākhya is the closed system that froze the text. (Padapāṭha; Śākalya)
- **Engine of the vikṛti-pāṭhas** — The mechanical permutation recitations the repo praises — *krama, jaṭā, ghana* — are codified and presupposed by the Prātiśākhyas (the Ṛk- and Atharva-Prātiśākhyas in particular describe the krama). These layered methods are why the Vedas survived millennia of oral transmission near letter-perfect. Cross-link [recitation styles](rigveda.md#recitation-patha-styles-and-oral-transmission). (Vedic chant)
- **Two clocks** — *Scripture clock:* the phonetic discipline (śikṣā) is treated as coeval with the eternal, authorless [Veda](../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) itself, transmitted from the ṛṣis to preserve the "heard" sound exactly. *Academic clock:* the surviving Prātiśākhyas are late-Vedic prose works, broadly **c. 600–300 BCE**, mostly **predating or contemporary with Pāṇini** (~4th c. BCE), built on still-older oral phonetic teaching. [scholarly]

## The Four Major Prātiśākhyas

- **Ṛk-Prātiśākhya (Ṛgveda)** — Attached to the **Śākala** śākhā of the [Ṛgveda](rigveda.md); ascribed to **Śaunaka** (the same lineage-name behind the *Charaṇavyūha* and the Bṛhaddevatā). Composed in ślokas (verse), uniquely among the major Prātiśākhyas. It states the rules deriving the saṃhitā text from Śākalya's padapāṭha and is the principal phonetic authority for Ṛgvedic recitation. (Ṛk-Prātiśākhya, Śaunaka)
  - *Variant (school):* sometimes located more precisely in the **Śaiśirīya upaśākhā of the Śākala** branch. [scholarly]
- **Taittirīya-Prātiśākhya (Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda)** — Attached to the **Taittirīya** śākhā of the **Black (Kṛṣṇa) [Yajurveda](yajurveda.md)**; arranged in two *praśnas* / 24 chapters. Famous in modern scholarship through **W. D. Whitney's** edition and translation (*Journal of the American Oriental Society*, 1871). Governs the recitation of the Taittirīya Saṃhitā, the most widely chanted Yajurveda text of South India. (Taittirīya-Prātiśākhya)
- **Vājasaneyi-Prātiśākhya (Śukla Yajurveda)** — Attached to the **Vājasaneyi (Mādhyandina/Kāṇva)** śākhā of the **White (Śukla) [Yajurveda](yajurveda.md)**; ascribed to **Kātyāyana**. The most systematic and "grammatical" of the four — closest in method to Pāṇini, and traditionally linked to the same Kātyāyana who wrote the *Vārttikas* on Pāṇini and Śrauta/Śulba-sūtras. Regulates the recitation of the Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā. (Vājasaneyi-Prātiśākhya, Kātyāyana)
  - *Variant (authorship):* the identification of this Kātyāyana with the *Vārttika*-author and the Kalpa-sūtra Kātyāyana is traditional but **disputed**; "Kātyāyana" may be a school-name. [disputed]
- **Atharva-Prātiśākhya / Śaunakīya Caturādhyāyikā (Atharvaveda)** — Attached to the **Śaunaka** śākhā of the [Atharvaveda](atharvaveda.md); ascribed to **Śaunaka**. Its name *Caturādhyāyikā* means "the [book] in four adhyāyas (chapters)." Edited and translated by **Whitney** (1862). It is the phonetic charter of the Atharvaveda's recitation, including its distinctive sandhi and accent rules. (Śaunakīya Caturādhyāyikā)
  - *Variant (second Atharvan text):* a separate **Atharvaveda-Prātiśākhya** (the *Śaunakīyā Caturādhyāyikā* being one of two Atharvan phonetic works) is sometimes distinguished; the Caturādhyāyikā is the standard surviving one. [scholarly]

> *Note — Sāmaveda:* the Sāmaveda has no Prātiśākhya of the same class; its analogous phonetic-recitation manuals are the **Sāmaveda Prātiśākhya-like texts (Puṣpasūtra, Ṛktantra, Sāmatantra)** governing its melodic chant. The "four Prātiśākhyas" canon therefore covers Ṛk, two Yajur recensions, and Atharva. [scholarly]

## Why They Matter: Predecessors of Pāṇini

- **Oldest extant Indian linguistics** — The Prātiśākhyas are the earliest surviving descriptive treatments of Sanskrit phonology and sandhi — a science of speech-sounds older than any formal grammar. (History of Sanskrit grammar)
- **The bridge to Vyākaraṇa** — They supply the technical vocabulary (sandhi, savarṇa, anunāsika, the accent terms) and the analytic instinct (pada-analysis) that **Pāṇini's *Aṣṭādhyāyī*** inherits and generalizes from text-specific rules into a universal grammar. Pāṇini explicitly assumes and improves upon Prātiśākhya doctrine. See [Vyākaraṇa](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md#2-the-six-vedangas--limbs-of-the-veda). [scholarly]
- **Śikṣā vedānga in concrete form** — Where the **Śikṣā** vedānga is the discipline of phonetics in the abstract, the Prātiśākhyas are its branch-specific working manuals; the later **Pāṇinīya Śikṣā** generalizes them. Body-image mnemonic: Śikṣā = the "nose" of the Veda. Cross-link [Śikṣā](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md#2-the-six-vedangas--limbs-of-the-veda). (Śikṣā)
- **Guarantor of fidelity** — Because the Prātiśākhya rules are *generative and reversible*, a reciter and a checker can independently regenerate the exact saṃhitā from the pada and detect any deviation — the technical reason the [śruti](../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) corpus reached print near-unchanged. [scholarly]
