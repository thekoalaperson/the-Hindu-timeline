# Rigveda — Per-Text Profile (Mandala-by-Mandala)

[Vedas](./README.md) · [Literature Corpus](../README.md) · [Master Index](../../README.md)
The oldest of the four Vedas and the oldest extant text of the Indo-Aryan tradition: 10 mandalas, 1,028 suktas, ~10,600 ric. Recited by the **Hotr** priest. See companion deity-and-hymn survey [vedic-deities-and-suktas.md](../vedic-deities-and-suktas.md) and the broader shruti map [shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md](../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md).

## Overview and Scale

- **Definition** - *Rig* (ṛc, "verse of praise") + *veda* ("knowledge"); a Samhita ("collection") of metrical hymns (sukta) addressed to deities, recited at sacrifice by the Hotr priest who invites and praises the gods. (Rigveda Samhita)
- **Scale** - 10 mandalas ("circles/books"), 1,028 suktas (hymns), ~10,600 ric (verses) in the standard Shakala count; subdivided alternately into 8 ashtakas → adhyayas → vargas for recitation. (Shakala Shakha)
- **Two clocks** - *Scripture clock:* apaurusheya (authorless), "heard" (shruti) by rishis at the dawn of the present cosmic order; transmitted orally without textual corruption. *Academic clock:* core composed **c. 1500–1200 BCE** in the greater Punjab/Sapta-Sindhu, with Mandalas 1 and 10 added later; codified under the early Kuru kingdom. [scholarly]
- **The Hotr priest** - The Rigveda is the priestly manual of the *Hotr* ("invoker"), one of the four chief sacrificial priests; he recites ric to summon deities, complementing the Adhvaryu (Yajurveda), Udgatr (Samaveda), and Brahman (Atharvaveda/overseer). See [yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md](../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md). (Vedic priesthood)
- **Invocation units** - The Hotr's recitations consist of a single verse (ric), strophes (a triplet = *trca*, or a pair = *pragatha*), or whole hymns (sukta). (Aitareya ritual usage)

## Structure: How the 10 Mandalas Are Arranged

- **The family-book core (2–7)** - The six "family books" (gotra/vamsha mandalas) are the oldest stratum, each attributed to a single seer-lineage and largely internally homogeneous. [scholarly]
- **Mixed/anthology books (1, 8)** - Younger collections gathering hymns of many poets; Mandala 8 in particular pairs with Mandala 1 (the Kanva poets span both). [scholarly]
- **Soma book (9)** - Wholly thematic: every hymn addresses Soma Pavamana. (Rigveda Samhita)
- **Late book (10)** - Latest layer, linguistically and philosophically distinct; home of the great cosmogonic and speculative suktas. [late text]
- **Family-book internal order** - Within each family book hymns are arranged first by deity (Agni hymns first, then Indra, then other gods), and within a deity-group by descending number of verses. This "Agni-then-Indra" template is a diagnostic of the old core. [scholarly]

## The Six Family Books (Mandalas 2–7)

- **Mandala 2 — Gritsamada** - Attributed to the Gritsamada (Bhargava-Shaunaka) lineage; ~43 hymns, smallest family book; prominent **Brahmanaspati / Brihaspati** hymns alongside the Agni–Indra core. (Family-book attribution)
- **Mandala 3 — Vishvamitra** - The Vishvamitra (Kushika) lineage; ~62 hymns; contains **RV 3.62.10, the Gayatri / Savitri mantra** to Savitr, the most recited verse in Hinduism, ascribed to Vishvamitra. Cross-link [vedic-deities-and-suktas.md](../vedic-deities-and-suktas.md). (Vishvamitra Mandala)
- **Mandala 4 — Vamadeva** - The Vamadeva Gautama lineage; ~58 hymns; notable for hymns to the **Ribhus** (deified artisan-craftsmen) and to Agni and Indra; a few hymns reflect Vamadeva's legendary prenatal wisdom. (Vamadeva Mandala)
- **Mandala 5 — Atri** - The Atri (Atreya) lineage; ~87 hymns; rich in **Maruts, Mitra-Varuna, Ushas, and Vishvadevas** hymns alongside Agni–Indra; associated with the Atri legend of rescuing Surya. (Atri Mandala)
- **Mandala 6 — Bharadvaja** - The Bharadvaja (Angirasa) lineage; ~75 hymns; especially strong **Agni** and **Pushan** hymns; the Bharadvajas are the purohita-line of the Bharata kings. (Bharadvaja Mandala)
- **Mandala 7 — Vasishtha** - The Vasishtha (Maitravaruni) lineage; ~104 hymns; contains the celebrated **Varuna** hymns (7.86–7.89, penitential), the **frog hymn** (7.103), and references to the **Battle of the Ten Kings (Dasarajna, 7.18, 7.33, 7.83)** where Vasishtha serves king Sudas. (Vasishtha Mandala)
- **Relative ages** - Vasishtha (7) and Bharadvaja (6) books are often judged among the oldest by metre and grammar; the family books collectively predate 1, 8, 9, 10. [scholarly]

## Mandala 1 and Mandala 8 (Mixed Books)

- **Mandala 1** - 191 hymns; a large later anthology by many seers (Madhuchchhandas, Gotama, Kutsa, Parashara, Dirghatamas, etc.); opens the whole Samhita with **RV 1.1, the Agni hymn** ("agnim ile purohitam"). Includes the Dirghatamas riddle hymn (1.164, "asya vamasya") and major Ushas, Ashvins, and Indra hymns. (Rigveda Mandala 1)
- **Mandala 8** - 103 hymns; dominated by the **Kanva** and **Angirasa** poets, sharing material with Mandala 1; many *pragatha* (strophic) hymns and danastutis (praise of patrons' gifts). (Rigveda Mandala 8)
- **Valakhilya hymns** - The **Valakhilya** appendix, RV 8.49–8.59 (11 hymns), is an interpolated supplement inside Mandala 8; counted within the 1,028 in the Shakala arrangement. [disputed boundary]

## Mandala 9 — The Soma Pavamana Book

- **Wholly thematic** - 114 hymns, every one addressed to **Soma Pavamana** ("Soma self-purifying"), the deified sacred draught as it is pressed and flows through the woolen filter into the vats. Unique among mandalas for a single-deity focus. (Ninth Mandala)
- **Compilation principle** - Assembled by extracting Soma-pressing hymns from across the poet-families; thus poets of Mandala 9 overlap with seers of books 2–8. The hymns are arranged largely by metre (gayatri group first, then others). [scholarly]
- **Ritual setting** - Belongs to the Soma sacrifice (Agnishtoma etc.); the Pavamana laudations accompany the physical purification of the pressed juice. Cross-link [yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md](../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md). (Soma in Vedic ritual)

## Mandala 10 — The Late Philosophical Book

- **Character** - 191 hymns; latest stratum, linguistically distinct; ranges from popular charms and wedding/funeral hymns to the loftiest cosmogonic speculation. [late text]
- **Nasadiya Sukta (10.129)** - The "Hymn of Creation"; begins "nasad asin no sad asit tadanim" ("then was neither non-existence nor existence"); agnostic climax — even the overseeing One "perhaps knows not." The high-water mark of Vedic skeptical cosmogony. (Mandala 10)
- **Purusha Sukta (10.90)** - The Cosmic Man sacrificed by the gods; from his dismembered body arise the cosmos and the **four varnas** (Brahmana from mouth, Rajanya from arms, Vaishya from thighs, Shudra from feet). The locus classicus for varna theory; widely regarded as a late insertion. [late text]
- **Hiranyagarbha Sukta (10.121)** - The "Golden Womb/Embryo"; each verse asks "kasmai devaya havisha vidhema" ("to which god shall we offer oblation?"), seeking the one creator-principle prior to the gods. (Mandala 10)
- **Devi Sukta / Vagambhrini Sukta (10.125)** - Spoken in the first person by the goddess **Vak** (Speech), seer-daughter of rishi **Ambhrna**; one of very few hymns where rishika (seeress) and devata are identical. The Devi proclaims herself the sustaining power pervading all gods and worlds — a foundational text for later Shakta theology. [scholarly]
- **Vak / Speech hymns** - Beyond 10.125, hymns deifying Vac/Speech (cf. 10.71) mark the rising importance of the sacred word; thematically continuous with the Devi Sukta. (Mandala 10)
- **Other notable 10 hymns** - **Yama–Yami dialogue (10.10)**, the **funeral hymns (10.14–10.18)**, the **wedding hymn (10.85, Surya's marriage)**, the **gambler's lament (10.34)**, and the **Vishvakarman** creator hymns (10.81–82). (Mandala 10)

## Khilas (Apocryphal Supplements)

- **Khila ("appendix")** - Supplementary hymns outside the canonical 1,028; the **Khilani** number ~98 hymns, preserved chiefly in the Bashkala tradition and a Kashmir (Sharada-script) manuscript now at Pune, not in the Shakala body proper. [disputed]
- **Sri Sukta** - The most famous khila, a 15–16 verse hymn to **Shri/Lakshmi** ("hiranyavarnam"); used liturgically though strictly an appendix, not part of the core Samhita. [folk/liturgical]

## Shakha (Recensions / Branches)

- **Five traditional shakhas** - Shaunaka's *Charanavyuha* lists five Rigvedic branches: **Shakala, Bashkala, Ashvalayana, Shankhayana (Kaushitaki), Mandukayana**; only Shakala (and traces of Bashkala/Ashvalayana) survive. (Shakha tradition)
- **Shakala shakha** - The only fully extant recension; defines the standard 1,028-hymn text used universally today; attached **Aitareya Brahmana** and **Aitareya Aranyaka**. [scholarly]
- **Bashkala shakha** - Differs by including the **Khilani**, by a slightly different hymn ordering, and by adding hymns; ~1,025 regular hymns + ~98 khilas; attached **Kaushitaki (Shankhayana) Brahmana**. [disputed/partial survival]

## Attached Brahmanas, Aranyakas, Upanishads

- **Aitareya Brahmana** - Brahmana of the Shakala/Aitareya school; details the Soma and royal (rajasuya) rituals from the Hotr's standpoint; ascribed to Mahidasa Aitareya. (Aitareya school)
- **Kaushitaki (Shankhayana) Brahmana** - Brahmana of the Bashkala/Kaushitaki school; parallel ritual exposition with its own emphases. (Kaushitaki school)
- **Aitareya Aranyaka** - Forest treatise of the Aitareya school; embeds the **Aitareya Upanishad** ("atma va idam eka evagra asit"), a principal Upanishad. Cross-link [principal-upanishads-detail.md](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).
- **Kaushitaki Aranyaka** - Forest treatise of the Kaushitaki school; embeds the **Kaushitaki Upanishad**. (Kaushitaki school)

## Recitation (Patha) Styles and Oral Transmission

- **Samhitapatha** - The continuous text with euphonic combination (sandhi) intact — the natural flowing recitation. (Vedic chant)
- **Padapatha** - The text broken into separate, sandhi-resolved words (pada); the analytic base form preserving each word's pure shape. (Padapatha)
- **Kramapatha** - "Step" recitation pairing consecutive words: 1-2, 2-3, 3-4, 4-5… so every word is chanted with both neighbours, fixing word order. (Krama)
- **Jatapatha** - "Woven" recitation: each pair forward-back-forward (1-2-2-1-1-2, 2-3-3-2-2-3…), a complex permutation that cements sequence. (Jata)
- **Ghanapatha** - "Bell/dense" recitation, the most intricate (…1-2-2-1-1-2-3-3-2-1-1-2-3…); the gold standard of mnemonic fidelity, its master titled *Ghanapathin*. These layered methods are why the Rigveda survived ~3 millennia of oral transmission near-letter-perfect. (Ghana; Vedic chant)
- **Eleven vikrti pathas** - Tradition counts elaborated forms beyond Samhita/Pada: Krama, Jata, Mala, Shikha, Rekha, Dhvaja, Danda, Ratha, Ghana (Ghana hardest). (Methods of chanting)

## Commentaries (Bhashya)

- **Sayana's bhashya** - The most authoritative classical commentary on the entire Rigveda, by **Sayanacharya** (14th c. CE), minister-scholar at the **Vijayanagara** court (under kings Bukka I / Harihara II), composed with his brother Madhava (Vidyaranya); gives ritual-grammatical glosses and word meanings still foundational to translation. [scholarly]
- **Other commentators** - Earlier glossators include **Yaska** (*Nirukta*, etymology of obscure Vedic words) and **Skandasvamin**, **Venkatamadhava**, and **Udgitha**; modern scholarly translations (Griffith, Geldner, Jamison–Brereton) build on and contest Sayana. [scholarly]
