# Yajurveda — Per-Text Profile (Shukla & Krishna Recensions)

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/vedas/`](./README.md) → [Section README](../README.md) → [Master Index](../../README.md). Dedicated profile of the **Yajurveda** ("knowledge of the *yajus*" — the muttered sacrificial-formula Veda), the operating manual of the **Adhvaryu** priest who physically performs the rite. For the four-Veda overview and śruti/yuga framing see [`../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md); sibling per-text profiles: [`./rigveda.md`](./rigveda.md), [`./samaveda.md`](./samaveda.md), [`./atharvaveda.md`](./atharvaveda.md). Ritual mechanics: [`../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md`](../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md).

Two clocks throughout: **scripture clock** (apauruṣeya, eternal Veda re-divided by Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa at the Dvāpara–Kali junction of the current 28th Mahāyuga, c. **3102 BCE**) vs **academic clock** (orally composed mantra-prose layers, c. **1200–800 BCE**, Iron-Age Kuru-Pañcāla heartland).

---

## 1. What the Yajurveda Is

- **The Veda of ritual action.** Where the Ṛgveda supplies hymns (ṛc) and the Sāmaveda supplies melody (sāman), the Yajurveda supplies the **yajus** — the precise prose formulas an officiant utters *while doing* each physical act of the sacrifice (pouring ghee, laying a brick, leading the victim). It is the karma-kāṇḍa Veda par excellence. (Yajurveda Saṃhitā tradition)
- **The Adhvaryu's Veda.** Each of the four Vedas is "owned" by one of the four chief ṛtvij. The **Adhvaryu** — the priest of manual operation who measures the altar, kindles fire, prepares offerings — recites the Yajurveda in a low murmur (upāṃśu). The Hotṛ (Ṛg) invites the gods, the Udgātṛ (Sāma) chants, the Brahman (Atharva) supervises. (Brāhmaṇa literature; see [`../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md`](../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md))
- **Prose + verse mixed.** Unlike the all-metrical Ṛgveda, the Yajurveda is largely **rhythmic prose** interspersed with borrowed and original ṛc, making it the earliest large body of Sanskrit prose.
- **The great fork.** The Yajurveda is uniquely split into two complete, parallel traditions — **Śukla (White/"clear")** and **Kṛṣṇa (Black/"unarranged")** — distinguished by *how* mantra and explanatory Brāhmaṇa material are arranged (see §2). This bifurcation is the single most important fact about the text and is the subject of a famous origin myth (§3).

---

## 2. The Śukla vs Kṛṣṇa Distinction (the structural fork)

- **Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda — "intermixed."** Mantra (saṃhitā) and prose Brāhmaṇa commentary are **woven together in a single running text**: a formula, then immediately its ritual explanation, then the next formula. "Black/unarranged" = the un-separated, jumbled-looking corpus. Adhvaryu schools of the older, southern/Deccan and Kashmiri lineages. Core text: **Taittirīya Saṃhitā** (§5).
- **Śukla (White) Yajurveda — "separated."** Pure mantras are collected **on their own** (the **Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā**), with all Brāhmaṇa explanation hived off into a separate, later work (the **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa**). "White/clear/orderly" = the cleanly arranged recension. (§4)
- *Variant (semantic gloss):* "śukla/kṛṣṇa" is also glossed devotionally — the White as the *purified/solar* revelation (received from Sūrya), the Black as the *regurgitated/older* layer; this maps onto the myth rather than describing a neutral editorial fact. [folk etymology layered on the editorial one]
- **Net:** same liturgical purpose, two packaging systems. The Kṛṣṇa is generally regarded as preserving the **older arrangement**; the Śukla as the **reorganized** (and partly later-expanded) recension.

## 3. The Yājñavalkya–Vaiśampāyana Origin Myth

- **Core legend.** **Vaiśampāyana** (pupil of Vyāsa, custodian of the Yajurveda) quarrels with his brilliant pupil **Yājñavalkya**. In anger he orders Yājñavalkya to **disgorge ("vomit") all the Yajus he had been taught**. The other disciples, told to gather the rejected knowledge, take the form of **partridges (tittiri)** and peck it up — hence the **Taittirīya** ("of the partridges") = the Kṛṣṇa/Black Yajurveda, "darkened" because cast off. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.5; later commentarial tradition) [legendary etiology]
- **The fresh revelation.** Stripped of his learning, Yājñavalkya worships **Sūrya** (the Sun). The deity — in **horse form (vājin / vāji-rūpa)**, in some tellings as **Nārāyaṇa** — teaches him a brand-new, untransmitted **Śukla (White)** Yajus directly. From *vājin* comes **Vājasaneyi** (the horse-revealed), patronymic of Yājñavalkya and name of his Saṃhitā. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.5; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 12.6)
  - *Variant (revealer):* the teacher is **Sūrya** in most Purāṇic accounts; some later devotional retellings make it **Nārāyaṇa/Viṣṇu** assuming the equine solar form.
- **Function of the myth.** It is an **etiological charter**: it ranks the Śukla as the divinely-renewed, "clean" recension while accounting for the Kṛṣṇa's older, intermixed, "cast-off" character — and ties the whole Śukla tradition to the historical sage **Yājñavalkya**, the towering figure of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad. [disputed as history; the two recensions are real, the personal quarrel is myth]
- **Yājñavalkya the man.** Independently the dominant philosopher of the Śukla line — his dialogues (with Maitreyī, Gārgī, King Janaka) in the Bṛhadāraṇyaka articulate **neti neti** ("not this, not this") and early Ātman=Brahman doctrine. See [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).

## 4. Śukla (White) Yajurveda — Texts

- **Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā (VS).** The core mantra collection: **40 adhyāyas (chapters)**, ~**1,975 mantras** (Mādhyandina count). Pure formulas, separated from explanation. (Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā)
  - **Two surviving śākhās:**
    - *Mādhyandina* — northern (orig. Mithilā/Bihar; spread to MP, Gujarat, UP); 40 adhyāyas, 303 anuvākas, 1,975 mantras. The standard printed Śukla text.
    - *Kāṇva* — southern/Deccan and Maharashtra; 40 adhyāyas, 328 anuvākas, **2,086 mantras** (111 more than Mādhyandina), with minor ordering and sandhi differences.
  - *Note:* the **Caraṇavyūha** assigns the Śukla many more nominal śākhās (traditionally **15/17**), but only Mādhyandina and Kāṇva survive intact. [most rosters lost]
- **Ritual map of the 40 adhyāyas** (Mādhyandina ordering):
  - **Adhy. 1–2 — Darśapūrṇamāsa**: the new- and full-moon iṣṭi, the model haviryajña.
  - **Adhy. 3 — Agnihotra, Cāturmāsya** (seasonal rites), Piṇḍapitṛyajña.
  - **Adhy. 4–8 — Somayāga**: the Soma sacrifice complex (Agniṣṭoma etc.).
  - **Adhy. 9–10 — Vājapeya & Rājasūya**: the "drink of strength" and royal consecration.
  - **Adhy. 11–18 — Agnicayana**: the months-long building of the great fire-altar from 10,800 bricks in the shape of a bird (often Falcon/Śyena), the most elaborate śrauta rite. Houses cosmological brick-laying formulas.
  - **Adhy. 16 — Śatarudriya / Rudram**: the famed litany to **Rudra** (66 kaṇḍikās in VS), "the hundred (forms/names) of Rudra," the seed of Śaiva worship and the **Namaka** of the Rudra-praśna; contains the **Pañcākṣara "namaḥ śivāya."** (Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā 16) — see [`../vedic-deities-and-suktas.md`](../vedic-deities-and-suktas.md).
  - **Adhy. 22–25 — Aśvamedha**: the horse sacrifice.
  - **Adhy. 30–31 — Puruṣamedha & Puruṣa-sūkta**: the (largely symbolic) human-sacrifice rite; adhy. 31 restates the cosmic-Puruṣa hymn (cf. Ṛgveda 10.90).
  - **Adhy. 39 — Pravargya / antyeṣṭi** material.
  - **Adhy. 40 — Īśā (Īśāvāsya) Upaniṣad**: the *only* Upaniṣad embedded directly inside a Saṃhitā. 18 verses; "**īśā vāsyam idaṁ sarvam**" (all this is pervaded by the Lord); reconciles action (karma) with renunciation (jñāna). See [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).
- **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa (ŚB) — "Brāhmaṇa of the Hundred Paths."** The vast prose commentary of the Śukla line, one of the largest and most important Brāhmaṇas.
  - *Mādhyandina recension*: 14 kāṇḍas, **100 adhyāyas** (Eggeling: the name "Śatapatha" = "hundred paths/chapters" from this count); ~7,624 kaṇḍikās.
  - *Kāṇva recension*: 17 kāṇḍas, 104 adhyāyas (also called Śatapatha).
  - **Notable contents:** the **Manu + flood / Matsya (fish-avatāra)** deluge narrative (ŚB 1.8.1) — the earliest extant Hindu flood story; Prajāpati cosmogony; extensive Agnicayana theology. Flood cross-links: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/) (Viṣṇu avatāras) and [Satya-Yuga of the current Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/01-satya-yuga.md).
- **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad** — the Śukla Āraṇyaka+Upaniṣad, forming the **closing kāṇḍa of the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa** (Mādhyandina kāṇḍa 14 / Kāṇva book 17). Two recensions (Mādhyandina, Kāṇva), 6 adhyāyas; the oldest and longest principal Upaniṣad, dominated by Yājñavalkya. — [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).
  - *Note on chain:* in the Śukla line Saṃhitā → Brāhmaṇa → Āraṇyaka → Upaniṣad telescope, with the Bṛhadāraṇyaka serving as both Āraṇyaka and Upaniṣad strata.

## 5. Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda — Texts

- **Four surviving Saṃhitās** (each a self-standing school with mantra+Brāhmaṇa intermixed):
  - **Taittirīya Saṃhitā (TS)** — the dominant, mostly **South Indian** Kṛṣṇa school; **7 kāṇḍas**, **44 prapāṭhakas** (~42–44 by count), 651 anuvākas. Living tradition (Andhra, Tamil country, Karnataka, Maharashtra). The reference Kṛṣṇa text.
  - **Maitrāyaṇī Saṃhitā (MS)** — often judged the **oldest-surviving** Kṛṣṇa recension; 4 kāṇḍas; western India (Gujarat/Maharashtra); archaic language.
  - **Kāṭhaka / Caraka Saṃhitā (KS)** — of the **Kaṭha (Caraka)** school; Kashmir and the northwest; partially preserved.
  - **Kapiṣṭhala-Kaṭha Saṃhitā** — a Kaṭha sub-branch; **fragmentary / largely extinct**, surviving only in incomplete manuscripts. [largely lost]
  - *Note:* the Caraṇavyūha lists far more Kṛṣṇa śākhās (Taittirīya, Kaṭha, Kapiṣṭhala, Caraka, Maitrāyaṇīya groupings, traditionally dozens of sub-schools); the vast majority are lost. [open-ended roster; only 4 partial saṃhitās extant]
- **Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa (TB)** — 3 kāṇḍas; supplements the TS with further ritual prose (Nakṣatra rites, Puruṣamedha material, etc.). Because mantra+Brāhmaṇa are intermixed in the Saṃhitā itself, the separate Brāhmaṇa is comparatively short.
- **Taittirīya Āraṇyaka (TĀ)** — 10 prapāṭhakas; the forest layer; contains the **Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad** (prapāṭhaka 10) and important mantras (e.g., the **Mṛtyuñjaya / Tryambakam**, the Medha-sūktas).
- **Taittirīya Upaniṣad (TU)** — within the Āraṇyaka (prapāṭhakas 7–9); three vallīs: **Śikṣā-vallī** (phonetics, the teacher's blessing), **Brahmānanda-vallī** (the five sheaths / pañcakośa, the *ānandamaya* analysis), **Bhṛgu-vallī** (Bhṛgu's progressive realization of Brahman as anna→prāṇa→manas→vijñāna→ānanda). — [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).
- **Kaṭha Upaniṣad** — attached to the **Kāṭhaka** (Kaṭha) school of the Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda: the Naciketas–Yama dialogue on death and the Self ("the razor's edge"), the chariot-of-the-body metaphor. — [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).
- **Śvetāśvatara & Maitrāyaṇīya Upaniṣads** — also affiliated to Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda lineages; the Śvetāśvatara is a pivotal early theistic (Rudra-Śiva) Upaniṣad. [some scholars class Śvetāśvatara as later/sectarian]

## 6. Dating — Two Clocks

- **Scripture clock.** Apauruṣeya and eternal; "heard" by ṛṣis and re-divided into four by **Vyāsa** at the close of the current Dvāpara Yuga (junction c. **3102 BCE**, onset of Kali Yuga). The Śukla branch is, within the tradition, a **fresh solar revelation to Yājñavalkya** (§3) — placed in the same Dvāpara compilation era.
- **Academic clock.** Mantra core composed orally c. **1200–1000 BCE**; the bulk of the Yajus prose and the Brāhmaṇas c. **1000–800/600 BCE**, in the **Kuru-Pañcāla** Iron-Age heartland — the Yajurveda is the literary signature of the consolidated śrauta sacrificial system. The Śukla recension and the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa are generally placed **later** than the Kṛṣṇa core; the Bṛhadāraṇyaka and Īśā Upaniṣads are early-to-mid Upaniṣadic (c. 700–500 BCE). [scholarly]
- *Relative-dating consensus:* Kṛṣṇa (intermixed) arrangement is older than the Śukla (separated) reorganization — flatly inverting the myth's value-ranking but agreeing the two are genuinely distinct traditions.

## 7. Quick Comparison Table

| | **Śukla (White)** | **Kṛṣṇa (Black)** |
|---|---|---|
| Arrangement | mantra & Brāhmaṇa **separated** | mantra & Brāhmaṇa **intermixed** |
| Core Saṃhitā | Vājasaneyi (40 adhyāyas) | Taittirīya (7 kāṇḍas) + Maitrāyaṇī, Kāṭhaka, Kapiṣṭhala |
| Surviving śākhās | Mādhyandina, Kāṇva | Taittirīya, Maitrāyaṇī, Kāṭhaka(-Caraka), Kapiṣṭhala (frag.) |
| Brāhmaṇa | Śatapatha (100/104 adhyāyas) | Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa |
| Āraṇyaka/Upaniṣad | Bṛhadāraṇyaka; **Īśā** (VS adhy. 40) | Taittirīya Āraṇyaka/Upaniṣad; **Kaṭha**, Śvetāśvatara, Maitrāyaṇīya |
| Signature content | Śatarudriya (adhy.16), Agnicayana, Matsya flood (ŚB) | Mahānārāyaṇa, Mṛtyuñjaya, Naciketas–Yama |
| Myth status | Sūrya/horse revelation to Yājñavalkya | "vomited" → partridges (tittiri) |
