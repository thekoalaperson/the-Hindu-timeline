# Āgamas, Tantras & Dharmaśāstras

> Literature-corpus node. These are post-Vedic, smṛti-class texts that **record and regulate** the events and practices of [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md). The Āgama/Tantra streams govern temple worship, initiation and esoteric practice; the Dharmaśāstra/Sūtra streams govern law, ritual and social order. By traditional reckoning all crystallise within the present Kali Yuga (post-3102 BCE); academic dating places most c. 600 BCE – 1200 CE. See sibling nodes [../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md](shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) and [../smriti-puranas.md](smriti-puranas.md).

---

## Orientation: where these texts sit

- **Śruti vs. smṛti.** Vedas = śruti ("heard," eternal revelation). Everything on this page is **smṛti** ("remembered," authored), the human-transmitted layer that operationalises the Veda for the current age. (Dharmaśāstra Wikipedia; ISKCON Heart of Hinduism)
- **Āgama vs. Nigama.** Āgama = "that which has come down" — revelation framed as a **dialogue of Śiva/Viṣṇu/Devī** with consort or disciple; Nigama = the Vedic corpus. Tantra/Āgama traditions claim *equal or supplementary* authority to śruti for Kali Yuga, when Vedic sacrifice is held to be impractical. (Agama (Hinduism) Wikipedia; Saivism.net)
- **The "Kali Yuga argument."** Many of these traditions justify themselves by the doctrine that the elaborate Vedic śrauta path is too demanding for the short-lived, low-dharma humans of [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md); hence Tantra/Āgama (image-worship, mantra, initiation) is the **yuga-appropriate** path. [late-text rationale]

## I. The Kalpa-Sūtras (Vedāṅga ritual manuals — the oldest layer)

Kalpa = the sixth **Vedāṅga** ("limb of the Veda"), the prose **sūtra** manuals that systematise ritual. Each Vedic śākhā (school) had its own set. Four sub-genres: (Kalpa (Vedanga) Wikipedia; Vedic Heritage Portal)

- **Śrauta-sūtras** — the great public/solemn fire sacrifices (agnihotra, soma rites, aśvamedha). Roughly contemporary with the Gṛhya corpus; late-Vedic Sanskrit, **mid-1st millennium BCE**. Examples: Āpastamba, Baudhāyana, Kātyāyana, Āśvalāyana, Lāṭyāyana Śrautasūtras. (Kalpa Vedanga Wikipedia)
- **Gṛhya-sūtras** — domestic rites (the **saṃskāras**/life-cycle rites: birth, upanayana, marriage, cremation), daily offerings. Examples: Āśvalāyana, Śāṅkhāyana, Gobhila, Pāraskara Gṛhyasūtras.
- **Dharma-sūtras** — religious/social/legal norms; the **seedbed of the later Dharmaśāstra** (see §IV). (Dharmaśāstra Wikipedia)
- **Śulba-sūtras** — geometry/measurement for constructing fire-altars (vedi); earliest Indian mathematics. Examples: Baudhāyana, Āpastamba, Kātyāyana, Mānava Śulbasūtras.

- **The four canonical Dharma-sūtras** *(prose, the ancestors of Manu)*: **Āpastamba, Gautama, Baudhāyana, Vasiṣṭha**. Traditional order of antiquity disputed; Wisdomlib/scholars often give **Āpastamba** as oldest, others **Gautama**. (Apastamba Dharmasutra Wikipedia; Mahavidya)
  - *Dating (academic):* oldest core **c. 500–300 BCE**; Baudhāyana c. 500–200 BCE; Vasiṣṭha c. 300–100 BCE.
  - *Variant (transmission):* Āpastamba & Baudhāyana Dharma-sūtras are **embedded in their Kalpa-sūtras** (one continuous school-corpus); **Vasiṣṭha's is a free-standing treatise** lacking accompanying Śrauta/Gṛhya parts. (search synthesis)
  - *Variant (authorship):* "Gautama" and "Vasiṣṭha" are **ṛṣi names attached to Vedic schools**, not securely historical authors — attribution is traditional, not biographical. [disputed]

## II. The Dharmaśāstra (the metrical law-codes — smṛti proper)

Verse reworkings and expansions of the Dharma-sūtra material into comprehensive treatises on **dharma** (duty/law/righteousness): the **varṇāśrama** system, kingship/governance (rājadharma), civil & criminal procedure (vyavahāra), penance (prāyaścitta), inheritance, ritual. (Dharmaśāstra Wikipedia; ISKCON Heart of Hinduism)

- **Manusmṛti / Mānava-Dharmaśāstra ("Laws of Manu")** — the most famous and influential. Attributed to **Manu Svāyambhuva / Vaivasvata Manu** (see [../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/) and the [14 Manus](../01-manvantaras/)), narrated to sages; ~2,690 verses, 12 chapters. Covers creation, the four varṇas, the four āśramas, women's duties, rājadharma, karma/rebirth.
  - *Dating (traditional):* a smṛti of the current Manvantara, "Vaivasvata Manu's code."
  - *Dating (academic):* compiled **c. 200 BCE – 200 CE** (Patrick Olivelle: 2nd–3rd c. CE); some popular sources give a wider 300 BCE–600 CE range. (Manusmriti Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (recensions):* multiple textual recensions exist; the **vulgate of commentator Kullūka Bhaṭṭa** (c. 12th–13th c.) became standard, but Olivelle's critical edition shows substantial earlier-layer differences. [disputed text history]
- **Yājñavalkya-smṛti** — second most authoritative; **more concise, systematic and "progressive"** than Manu (better legal procedure, somewhat improved position for women & property). Attributed to sage **Yājñavalkya** (of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad). Three sections: ācāra, vyavahāra, prāyaścitta.
  - *Dating (academic):* **c. 3rd–5th century CE**, composed after Manu. (Yājñavalkya Smṛti Wikipedia)
  - *Key commentary:* the **Mitākṣarā** of Vijñāneśvara (11th–12th c.) became the dominant school of Hindu inheritance law across most of India. (Yājñavalkya Smṛti Wikipedia)
- **Nārada-smṛti** — the **"juridical text" par excellence**: focused almost entirely on **vyavahāra** (legal procedure, the 18 titles of law), with little ritual/penance. Reflects a mature court system.
  - *Dating (academic):* **c. 100 BCE – 400 CE**; some place its juristic form 1st–4th c. CE. (search synthesis; Dharmaśāstra Wikipedia)
- **Other major smṛtis:** **Parāśara-smṛti** (explicitly the **"Kali-age code,"** declaring different dharma valid for each yuga — Manu for Kṛta/Satya, Gautama for Tretā, Śaṅkha-Likhita for Dvāpara, **Parāśara for Kali**); **Bṛhaspati, Kātyāyana, Viṣṇu, Hārīta, Devala** smṛtis. (Dharmaśāstra Wikipedia; ISKCON)
  - *Variant (yuga-dharma doctrine):* Parāśara's claim that **the operative law-code changes per yuga** is itself a canonical statement mapping Dharmaśāstra onto the cosmic clock. [scriptural]
- **Nibandhas & commentaries (medieval digests):** later jurists wove the smṛtis into topical digests — **Mitākṣarā**, **Dāyabhāga** (Jīmūtavāhana, ~12th c., the rival inheritance school of Bengal), **Smṛticandrikā**, **Vīramitrodaya**, **Kṛtyakalpataru**. These, not the root smṛtis, were the actual operative "Hindu law" by the colonial era. (Dharmaśāstra Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (two inheritance schools):* **Mitākṣarā** (joint-family coparcenary, son acquires birthright) vs. **Dāyabhāga** (Bengal; ownership only on the father's death) — the central doctrinal split of pre-modern Hindu property law.

## III. The Āgama/Tantra corpus (revelatory ritual-yoga scriptures)

A **parallel scriptural stream** to the Veda, structured around four pādas: **Jñāna** (knowledge/metaphysics), **Yoga**, **Kriyā** (temple construction, iconography, ritual), **Caryā** (conduct, festivals). They are the **operating manuals of Hindu temple religion**. (Agama Wikipedia; Saivism.net)

- **Threefold division (standard count):** **Śaiva Āgamas, Vaiṣṇava Āgamas (Pāñcarātra/Vaikhānasa), Śākta Āgamas (Tantras)** — traditionally **28 Śaiva, 108 Vaiṣṇava (Pāñcarātra Saṃhitās), 77 Śākta**, plus numerous Upa-Āgamas. (Agama Wikipedia)
- **Dating (academic):** principal texts mostly **post-Gupta, c. 500 CE onward**; many core Tantras not before the **8th century CE**. By tradition, eternal revelation re-spoken for Kali Yuga. (Mohan Ananda; Agama Wikipedia)

### A. Vaiṣṇava Āgamas

- **Pāñcarātra** — "Five Nights": doctrine the Lord (Nārāyaṇa) revealed over five nights to five sages. Core teaching = the **Vyūha doctrine**: the Absolute (Para/Vāsudeva) manifests as four **vyūhas** — **Vāsudeva, Saṃkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna, Aniruddha** — and Ramanuja's fivefold scheme **Para, Vyūha, Vibhava (avatāras), Antaryāmin (indweller), Arcā (the consecrated temple image)**. (Pancharatra Wikipedia; Vaishnavism Wikipedia)
  - **Defended by Rāmānuja** in the *Śrī-Bhāṣya* (Brahma-Sūtra II.2.39–42) as Nārāyaṇa-revealed and Veda-concordant; became the ritual backbone of **Śrī Vaiṣṇavism**. (search synthesis)
  - Texts are **Saṃhitās** (e.g., **Sātvata, Pauṣkara, Jayākhya, Ahirbudhnya, Lakṣmī, Pārameśvara Saṃhitās**); traditionally 108, far more extant.
#### Vaikhānasa — the Veda-rooted temple liturgy

The **second great Vaiṣṇava temple-worship system** alongside Pāñcarātra, but its self-understanding is the inverse: where Pāñcarātra is **Āgamic** (revealed by Nārāyaṇa over the five nights, qualifying its priests by **dīkṣā/initiation**), Vaikhānasa claims a **Vedic, sūtra-borne descent** and qualifies its priests by **birth/saṃskāra** rather than tantric initiation. It is the living liturgy of **Tirumala-Tirupati** (Veṅkaṭeśvara) and a large share of South Indian Viṣṇu temples. (Gold Coast Hindu; Vaikhanasa Wikipedia; TTD)

- **Vaikhānasa** — the temple system claiming descent from the sage **Vikhanas** (said to be born of / commissioned by Brahmā), transmitting Viṣṇu-worship through a **Vedic (not Tantric) lineage**.
  - **The Vedic-descent claim.** Vaikhānasa anchors itself in the **Vikhanas Kalpa-sūtra tradition** of the **Taittirīya (Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda)** school — i.e. it presents itself as one more **Kalpa-sūtra school-corpus** (see §I) extended into image-worship, *not* as a post-Vedic Āgama. Its four disciple-sages **Atri, Bhṛgu, Marīci, Kāśyapa** are credited with the foundational **saṃhitās**. By this genealogy the tradition asserts it never broke from the Veda, so its worship needs **no separate dīkṣā** to legitimise it. (Vaikhanasa Wikipedia; Vaikhanasa Agama) [tradition-internal]
  - **The Vaikhānasa Āgama / saṃhitās.** The corpus is built from the saṃhitās of the four sages — the largest being **Marīci** (e.g. *Vimānārcanakalpa, Jayasaṃhitā, Ānanda-saṃhitā*), **Bhṛgu** (*Khilādhikāra, Kriyādhikāra, Niruktādhikāra*, etc.), **Kāśyapa** (*Satyakāṇḍa* / *Jñānakāṇḍa*) and **Atri** (*Pūrva-, Viṣṇu-, Uttara-, Atri-tantra*) — covering temple construction, **vimāna**/iconography, consecration (pratiṣṭhā), daily and festival worship. Though grouped under "Vaiṣṇava Āgamas" in the standard threefold count (§III.A), Vaikhānasa reckons these as **sūtra-derived**, not śiva/devī-dialogue revelation. (Vaikhanasa Wikipedia; Vaikhanasa Agama)
  - **The Vaikhānasa archaka priesthood.** Worship is conducted by hereditary **archaka**s (**Vaikhānasa Brahmins**) for whom officiation is a **birthright conferred by saṃskāra** performed *in utero* / in childhood — explicitly contrasted with the Pāñcarātrins, who become eligible through **pāñcarātra-dīkṣā**. This birth-qualification is the practical crux of the two systems' rivalry over who may serve a given temple. (Vaikhanasa Wikipedia; TTD)
  - *Variant (Pāñcarātra vs. Vaikhānasa):* the two dispute precedence and purity of line — Vaikhānasa asserts **direct Vedic/sūtra descent** and **birth-qualification** of priests and tends to a **single-icon (one mūrti)** sanctum; Pāñcarātra admits **initiation (dīkṣā)** as qualifying and elaborates the **vyūha** multiplicity. A temple is generally served by **one system exclusively**, and conversions of a shrine from one to the other are historically contentious. (Gold Coast Hindu; TTD)
  - *Variant (which is older?):* both claim priority; Vaikhānasa's **Vedic-sūtra framing** is sometimes read as the **older, more conservative** stratum of Vaiṣṇava temple practice, while Pāñcarātra's vyūha theology won the **doctrinal mainstream** of Śrī Vaiṣṇavism via Rāmānuja. Dating of the extant Vaikhānasa saṃhitās is itself **post-Gupta**, contemporaneous with the Āgama corpus. [disputed]

### B. Śaiva Āgamas & the Śaiva schools

- **The 28 Śaiva-Siddhānta Āgamas** (10 **Śiva-bheda** + 18 **Rudra-bheda**) — dualistic/pluralistic theology; the canon of **Śaiva Siddhānta**, dominant in Tamil South India (Meykaṇḍār lineage, *Śivajñānabodha*). Pati–paśu–pāśa (Lord–soul–bond) metaphysics; liberation by grace + ritual. (Saivism.net; Shaivism Wikipedia)
- **The 64 Bhairava Āgamas / Tantras** — the **non-dual, Śākta-inflected** wing feeding Kashmir Śaivism (the Trika canon). (Kashmir Shaivism Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (the threefold Āgama hierarchy of Kashmir Śaivism):* the tradition reads its scriptures as a **progression** — **10 Śiva (dualist) → 18 Rudra (dualist-cum-nondualist) → 64 Bhairava (purely non-dualist)** — integrating Śākta Tantras at the apex. (search synthesis)
- **Lākulīśa-Pāśupata** — the ascetic, ash-smeared Pāśupata order (founder **Lakulīśa**, ~2nd c. CE), among the earliest organised Śaiva sects; draws on a separate body of Pāśupata-sūtras.
- **Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual Trika)** — the exegetical synthesis dominant in 9th–11th c. Kashmir. Threefold internal division: **Āgama Śāstra** (revealed Tantras), **Spanda Śāstra** (vibration/dynamism doctrine), **Pratyabhijñā Śāstra** (recognition philosophy). (Kashmir Shaivism Wikipedia; IEP)
  - Sub-systems: **Trika** (triads: Śiva–Śakti–Nara; Parā–Parāparā–Aparā), **Krama** (Śakti/Kālī-centred, sequential), **Kaula** (Ardhatraiyambaka line), **Spanda**, **Pratyabhijñā** (Śiva-centred). (search synthesis)
  - **Key revealed Tantras:** *Mālinīvijayottara, Svacchanda, Vijñānabhairava, Netra, Mṛgendra, Rudrayāmala.* **Key thinkers:** **Vasugupta** (*Śiva-Sūtras*, *Spanda-Kārikā*, 9th c.), **Somānanda** (*Śivadṛṣṭi*), **Utpaladeva** (*Īśvara-pratyabhijñā-kārikā*), **Abhinavagupta** (*Tantrāloka, Tantrasāra, Mālinīślokavārttika*, c. 950–1020 CE), **Kṣemarāja** (*Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya*). (Kashmir Shaivism Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (origin of Trika):* a Kaula sub-tradition probably arising **outside Kashmir before 800 CE**; the **Pratyabhijñā** recognition-philosophy is the genuinely Kashmiri development. [scholarly]

### C. Śākta Āgamas / Tantras

- **The 77 Śākta Āgamas (Tantras)** — Devī/Śakti as supreme. Divided into streams by region/orientation: **Kaula, Miśra, Samaya** classes. (Agama Wikipedia; Saivism.net)
  - *Variant (geo-classification):* Tantras grouped by **āmnāya** (transmission-face) — e.g. **Pūrva, Dakṣiṇa, Paścima, Uttara āmnāyas**; and by region into **Kerala, Gauḍa (Bengal), Kāśmīra** schools. [tradition-internal]
  - *Variant (right- vs. left-hand path):* **Dakṣiṇācāra** ("right-hand," symbolic/orthoprax) vs. **Vāmācāra** ("left-hand," antinomian **pañca-makāra** ritual). The split is the central Tantric controversy. [doctrinal divide]
- **Major texts:** *Mahānirvāṇa Tantra* [late, 18th c.], *Kulārṇava Tantra*, *Tantrarāja Tantra*, *Rudrayāmala*, *Śāradā-tilaka*; the **Śrī Vidyā** tradition (worship of Lalitā Tripurasundarī via the **Śrīcakra/Śrīyantra**), expounded in *Saundaryalaharī* (attrib. Śaṅkara) and *Yoginīhṛdaya*.
- **Buddhist & Jain Tantra** [related, non-Hindu]: the same era produced Vajrayāna Buddhist Tantras — noted only to mark the shared pan-Indic Tantric milieu, not Hindu canon.

## IV. Cross-cutting variants & disputes (catalogue)

- **Authority status:** orthodox **Mīmāṃsā/Smārta** view treats Āgamas/Tantras as **valid only where they don't contradict śruti**; Tantric traditions treat them as **independent revelation** (some claim a "fifth Veda" / Veda-equivalent for Kali Yuga). [doctrinal dispute]
- **Veda-mūlatva debate:** whether Pāñcarātra/Āgama is **rooted in the Veda** (Rāmānuja: yes) or **non-Vedic/heterodox** (some Mīmāṃsakas and Śaṅkara's school: rejected Pāñcarātra in part). (search synthesis) [disputed]
- **Number disputes:** the "28 Śaiva / 108 Vaiṣṇava / 77 Śākta" counts are **idealised**; actual surviving texts and Upa-Āgamas vary by list and sect. [count varies]
- **Yuga-mapping:** Parāśara-smṛti's per-yuga law-code doctrine and the Tantric "Kali Yuga sādhana" rationale both explicitly **locate these corpora within the cosmic time scheme** as Kali-appropriate revelations. [scriptural framing]
