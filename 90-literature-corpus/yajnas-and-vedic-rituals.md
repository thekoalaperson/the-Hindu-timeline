# Yajñas & Vedic Rituals

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/`](README.md) — the texts that record the cosmic chronology. This node catalogs the **ritual praxis** of the Śruti: the great Vedic sacrifices (yajña) the Saṃhitās and Brāhmaṇas exist to enact, the priestly offices that perform them, and the **śrauta/gṛhya** division of rite. For the gods invoked see [Vedic Deities & Sūktas](vedic-deities-and-suktas.md); for the parent text-tier see [Śruti — Vedas & Upanishads](shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md); the ritual sciences (Kalpa, Jyotiṣa) are limbed under [Upavedas, Vedāṅgas & Darśanas](upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md). The most famous epic enactment — Yudhiṣṭhira's horse sacrifice — is detailed in [Āśvamedhika Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/14-ashvamedhika-parva.md).

A **yajña** is the ritual offering of oblations (havis) into the consecrated **fire (Agni)**, the divine messenger who carries the offering to the gods; the worshipper (yajamāna) gains heaven, progeny, cattle, sovereignty, or rain in return. The Brāhmaṇa layer of each Veda is essentially a vast manual of yajña. *Note:* the canonical roster of śrauta rites is **finite and well-defined** (the Kalpa-sūtras enumerate them), but folk and royal one-off rites (e.g. specialized iṣṭis) are **open-ended** — flagged below.

---

## 0. The Master Division: Śrauta vs. Gṛhya

Vedic ritual splits by **how many fires** it uses and **which texts govern it**. (Kalpa-sūtra division; Wikipedia "Srauta")

- **Śrauta ("of the Śruti / solemn")** — the great public sacrifices requiring **three (or five) consecrated fires** and **multiple professional priests** (up to 16). Governed by the **Śrauta-sūtras** (a sub-class of the Kalpa Vedāṅga). The Agnihotra, Soma rites, Aśvamedha, Rājasūya etc. all belong here. (Śrauta-sūtras; Āpastamba, Baudhāyana, Kātyāyana)
- **Gṛhya ("domestic")** — the household rites performed by the householder (or a single purohita) over the **single domestic fire (gṛhyāgni / aupāsana)**. Governed by the **Gṛhya-sūtras**. Covers the **saṃskāras** (life-cycle sacraments — birth, upanayana, marriage, funeral) and the daily **Pañcamahāyajña**. (Gṛhya-sūtras; Āśvalāyana, Pāraskara)
  - *Variant (third stream):* a **Smārta** category (from Smṛti) is sometimes distinguished for rites grounded in tradition rather than direct Śruti — overlapping with gṛhya. [scholarly]

### The Three Śrauta Fires (Tretāgni)

- **Gārhapatya** - the round, perpetual "householder's fire" in the west, from which the others are drawn; symbol of earth/domestic continuity.
- **Āhavanīya** - the square "offering fire" in the east, into which oblations to the gods are poured; symbol of heaven.
- **Dakṣiṇāgni (Anvāhāryapacana)** - the crescent "southern fire," for offerings to the ancestors (pitṛs) and warding evil. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa)
  - *Variant (five-fire Soma rites):* large Soma sacrifices add the **Sabhya** and **Āvasathya** fires, giving the **Pañcāgni**. [late text]

## I. The Five Great Daily Sacrifices (Pañcamahāyajña)

The **five debts** every twice-born householder discharges **daily** — the gṛhya minimum of ritual life. (Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 2.10; Manusmṛti 3.69–71; Śatapatha Br.)

- **Brahma-yajña (Veda-/Ṛṣi-yajña)** - the debt to the **sages**: daily *svādhyāya* (recitation/study of the Veda) and its teaching. (Manusmṛti 3.70)
- **Deva-yajña** - the debt to the **gods**: oblations (homa) into the fire, esp. the morning/evening Agnihotra. (Manusmṛti 3.70)
- **Pitṛ-yajña** - the debt to the **ancestors**: *tarpaṇa* (water libation) and *śrāddha* (food offering) to the pitṛs. (Manusmṛti 3.70)
- **Bhūta-yajña** - the debt to **all beings**: *bali*, scattering food for animals, birds, insects, and spirits. (Manusmṛti 3.70)
- **Manuṣya-yajña (Atithi-yajña, Nṛ-yajña)** - the debt to **humanity**: hospitality (atithi-pūjā) to the unexpected guest. (Manusmṛti 3.70)
  - *Variant (naming):* lists alternately label these **Veda-/Deva-/Pitṛ-/Bhūta-/Nṛ-yajña**; substance identical. [late text]
  - *Note:* unlike the śrauta rites, the Pañcamahāyajña require **no special priests, season, or wealth** — the householder performs them himself, which is why they survive into modern practice.

## II. The Officiants (Ṛtvij) — The Priestly Offices

A full śrauta Soma sacrifice deploys **16 priests (ṣoḍaśa-ṛtvij)**: **4 chief priests**, each with **3 assistants**, plus the **Sadasya** observer. Each chief draws on one Veda. (Wikipedia "Vedic priesthood"; Aitareya Br.)

- **Hotṛ** - the **invoker**, reciter of the *śastra* invocations and litanies drawn from the **Ṛgveda**; the presiding speaker. (Ṛgveda priest)
  - *Assistants:* **Maitrāvaruṇa**, **Acchāvāka**, **Grāvastut**.
- **Adhvaryu** - the **executor**, in charge of the **physical performance** — measuring ground, building altars, pressing Soma, pouring oblations; mutters the formulae of the **Yajurveda**. The busiest priest. (Yajurveda priest)
  - *Assistants:* **Pratiprasthātṛ**, **Neṣṭṛ**, **Unnetṛ**.
- **Udgātṛ** - the **chanter**, who sings the *sāman* melodies of the **Sāmaveda** over the pressed Soma. (Sāmaveda priest)
  - *Assistants:* **Prastotṛ**, **Pratihartṛ**, **Subrahmaṇya**.
- **Brahman** - the **superintendent / silent overseer**, who watches the whole rite, corrects errors with expiatory verses (prāyaścitta), and is associated with the **Atharvaveda**; the "physician of the sacrifice." (Atharvaveda priest)
  - *Assistants:* **Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin**, **Agnīdh** (the fire-kindler), **Potṛ**.
- **Sadasya** - the 16th, a learned witness seated in the *sadas* (assembly hall) to validate correctness. (Aitareya Br.)
- **Supporting roles (not among the 16):**
  - **Yajamāna** - the **sacrificer/sponsor** for whom the rite is done and who reaps its fruit (with his wife, the **patnī**, required for many rites). The priests act on his behalf.
  - **Purohita** - the king's/family **chaplain**, often filling the Brahman or Hotṛ seat in royal rites (e.g. Vasiṣṭha, Viśvāmitra, Bṛhaspati as deva-purohita).
  - **Dakṣiṇā** - the priestly **fee** (cows, gold, land); its lavishness is itself a measure of the rite's grandeur.
  - *Variant (smaller rites):* the Agnihotra and simple iṣṭis need only the **Adhvaryu** (and yajamāna); the full 16 are required only for elaborate Soma/Aśvamedha rites. [scholarly]

## III. The Haviryajñas (Oblation Sacrifices) — the "seven havis" class

Rites offering **vegetable/animal oblations (havis)** rather than Soma; the foundational śrauta tier. The Kalpa-sūtras canonically count **seven Haviryajñas**. (HinduWebsite "Srauta Yajnas"; Dharmawiki "Yajna")

- **Agnyādhāna (Agnyādheya)** - the **establishment of the three sacred fires**; the prerequisite initiation that makes a man an *āhitāgni* qualified for all further śrauta rites.
- **Agnihotra** - the **twice-daily fire oblation** (dawn & dusk) of milk into the Āhavanīya; the simplest, most fundamental, lifelong śrauta duty of the consecrated householder. The morning offering is to **Sūrya/Agni**, the evening to **Agni/Prajāpati**. (Śatapatha Br. 2.3)
- **Darśapūrṇamāsa** - the **new-moon and full-moon sacrifices**, the paradigmatic iṣṭi (model for all iṣṭi rites), offered fortnightly.
- **Āgrayaṇa (Āgrayaṇeṣṭi)** - the **first-fruits** offering of new grain (harvest rite) before the householder may eat the new crop.
- **Cāturmāsya** - the **four-monthly / seasonal sacrifices** marking the three seasons (Vaiśvadeva, Varuṇapraghāsa, Sākamedha).
- **Nirūḍhapaśubandha** - the **independent animal sacrifice** (a he-goat to Indra-Agni), offered yearly or half-yearly.
- **Sautrāmaṇī** - a rite offering **surā (liquor)** alongside milk to the Aśvins, Sarasvatī, and Indra; expiatory, restoring one purged by Soma. (Śatapatha Br. 12)
  - *Variant (count/membership):* lists sometimes substitute **Piṇḍapitṛyajña** (ancestral rice-ball offering, appended to Darśa) into the seven, or count **Agnihotra + Darśapūrṇamāsa + Āgrayaṇa + Cāturmāsya + Nirūḍhapaśubandha + Sautrāmaṇī + Piṇḍapitṛyajña**. [scholarly] The roster of minor iṣṭis (kāmya, desire-specific) is **open-ended**.

## IV. The Somayajñas (Soma Sacrifices) — the "seven soma" class

Rites pressing and offering the sacred **Soma** juice; the grandest and most prestigious śrauta tier, sung over by the Udgātṛ. The Kalpa-sūtras count **seven Somasaṃsthās** ("forms of Soma rite"), graded by number of days and Soma-pressings. (HinduWebsite; Dharmawiki)

- **Agniṣṭoma** - the **model Soma sacrifice** ("praise of Agni"), a **one-day** rite with the Jyotiṣṭoma framework; archetype of the class. (Often the whole genus is called **Jyotiṣṭoma**.)
- **Atyagniṣṭoma** - an **extended** Agniṣṭoma adding a Soḍaśin cup.
- **Ukthya** - adds extra *uktha* recitations to the Agniṣṭoma.
- **Ṣoḍaśin (Soḍaśin)** - adds a **sixteenth** Soma-stotra (the "sixteenth" cup).
- **Vājapeya** - the **"drink of strength/vigor"**: a prestige rite with a ritual **chariot-race**, the yajamāna's symbolic ascent of a sacrificial post to "the sun," and consecration as **samrāṭ** (overlord); ranked even above Rājasūya by some Brāhmaṇas. Confers supremacy. (Śatapatha Br. 5.1)
- **Atirātra** - the **"overnight"** Soma rite, performed through the night with continuous chanting.
- **Aptoryāma** - the **longest/most elaborate** Soma rite, extending the Atirātra with extra stotras and śastras.
  - *Variant (Jyotiṣṭoma as umbrella):* many sources treat **Jyotiṣṭoma** as the parent term and list these seven as its *saṃsthās* (modes), Agniṣṭoma being the basic form. [scholarly]
  - *Note:* multi-day Soma rites stretching **a year or more** are **Sattras** ("sessions") — performed by a collective of consecrated yajamānas who are themselves the priests (e.g. the Gavāmayana year-rite, and the mythic Sarpa-sattra, §VI).

## V. The Royal & Great Public Sacrifices

The most spectacular śrauta rites — royal consecrations and "all-conquering" sacrifices, mostly Soma-based and only available to kings. (HinduWebsite; Wikipedia)

- **Rājasūya** - the **royal consecration** ("king's Soma"); a months-long rite transforming a king into a **samrāṭ** (emperor) over rival kings, with ritual dice, anointing (abhiṣeka), and tribute. In the Mahābhārata, **Yudhiṣṭhira** performs it at Indraprastha after the Pāṇḍava brothers conquer the four quarters and Jarāsandha is slain — culminating in the killing of **Śiśupāla** (Sabhā Parva). (Mahābhārata Sabhā Parva; Śatapatha Br. 5.2–5.4) → see [Sabhā Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md)
- **Vājapeya** - (also a Somayajña, §IV) the "drink of vigor" rite of overlordship, with chariot-race and sun-post ascent. (Śatapatha Br. 5.1)
- **Aśvamedha** - the **horse sacrifice**, the supreme imperial rite. A consecrated stallion roams free for a **year**, guarded by warriors; every land it enters must submit or fight. On its return it is sacrificed, the chief queen (**mahiṣī**) performing a fertility rite over the carcass; confers **universal sovereignty (sārvabhauma)** and expiates sin. Mythic performers: **Yudhiṣṭhira** (post-war atonement, the wandering horse, Babhruvāhana, the golden mongoose) — see [Āśvamedhika Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/14-ashvamedhika-parva.md); **Daśaratha** (preceding the Putrakāmeṣṭi, Rāmāyaṇa Bāla Kāṇḍa); **Sagara** (whose horse Indra steals, leading to the 60,000 sons' destruction and the Gaṅgā's descent); the Ṛgvedic **Aśvamedha hymns** (RV 1.162–163). **A hundred Aśvamedhas** entitle one to **Indra's throne** — hence Indra's habitual sabotage of the 100th. (Śatapatha Br. 13; Aśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra; Mahābhārata; Rāmāyaṇa)
  - *Variant (interpretation) [disputed]:* the queen-stallion episode is read literally (symbolic union) by some, allegorically/sanitized by others; reformers (Dayānanda Sarasvatī) argue *aśvamedha* meant only "proper governance of the realm," not a literal horse-killing. [scholarly]/[folk]
- **Puruṣamedha** - the **"human sacrifice"** rite, modeled on the Aśvamedha with a man as nominal victim. The Śatapatha and Vājasaneyi texts describe it, but the tradition holds the victim is **released unharmed** (the gods forbid completion) — making it largely **symbolic/disputed** as a literal practice. (Śatapatha Br. 13.6; Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā 30–31) **[disputed]**
  - *= Naramedha:* "Naramedha" is the same human sacrifice under another name; cited in lists of great sacrifices but almost universally read as **symbolic, prohibited in Kali Yuga, or never literally performed** in mainstream tradition. **[disputed]**/[folk]
- **Sarvamedha** - the **"all-sacrifice,"** a ten-day rite in which the yajamāna offers **everything he owns** (all his wealth, and symbolically himself), after which he renounces the world; the culminating, total sacrifice. (Śatapatha Br. 13.7)
  - *Note:* the standard "great four" beyond the Soma-saṃsthās are often listed as **Rājasūya, Aśvamedha, Puruṣamedha (Naramedha), Sarvamedha**. (HinduWebsite)

## VI. Narrative & One-Off Sacrifices (Kāmya / Iṣṭi rites in myth)

Desire-specific (kāmya) sacrifices and special satras that drive major mythic narratives. This class is **open-ended** — epics and Purāṇas invent rites for plot purposes.

- **Putrakāmeṣṭi** - the **"son-desiring sacrifice,"** a kāmya iṣṭi for obtaining male offspring. Famously, **Ṛṣyaśṛṅga** (son of Vibhāṇḍaka) performs it for the childless **Daśaratha** of Ayodhyā; **Agni** rises from the fire bearing a bowl of *pāyasa* (kheer), which Daśaratha divides among queens Kausalyā, Kaikeyī, and Sumitrā — begetting **Rāma, Bharata, Lakṣmaṇa, and Śatrughna** (the four avatāra-portions of Viṣṇu). (Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa 14–18) → see [Itihāsa — Epics](smriti-itihasa-epics.md)
- **Sarpa-Sattra (Sarpa-Yajña)** - the **snake sacrifice**, a year-long satra performed by **Janamejaya** (son of Parikṣit) to annihilate all serpents in vengeance for his father's death by the Nāga **Takṣaka**'s bite. Brahmins' mantras drag snakes into the fire until the sage **Āstīka** halts it, sparing Takṣaka and the Nāgas. This rite is the **frame-story of the Mahābhārata** — Vaiśampāyana narrates the epic *during* the sattra. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva) → see [Ādi Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md)
- **Daśaratha's Aśvamedha** - precedes the Putrakāmeṣṭi as the great consecratory horse sacrifice opening the Rāmāyaṇa. (Rāmāyaṇa Bāla Kāṇḍa)
- **Sagara's Aśvamedha** - whose stolen horse and the digging 60,000 sons frame the **Gaṅgāvataraṇa** (descent of the Gaṅgā). (Rāmāyaṇa; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9)
- **Dakṣa-yajña** - the great sacrifice of **Dakṣa Prajāpati** to which Śiva is pointedly not invited; **Satī** self-immolates and **Vīrabhadra** destroys the rite — the archetypal "sacrifice gone wrong." (Purāṇas; Mahābhārata) → see [Śiva myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)
- **Viśvajit** - the "all-conquering" Soma rite in which the yajamāna gives away **all his possessions** (drives a famous Upaniṣadic episode). [scholarly]
- *Open-ended note:* royal and ṛṣi-sponsored kāmya sacrifices (for rain, victory, cattle, sons, heaven) are **not a closed list**; the above are the narratively load-bearing ones.

## VII. The Pravargya & Other Embedded Rites

- **Pravargya** - the **"hot-milk" oblation**, a fierce preliminary rite to Soma sacrifices in which milk is boiled in a special clay pot (**mahāvīra / gharma**) heated red-hot and offered to the Aśvins; esoteric, with the pot identified with the sun and the head of the sacrifice. (Śatapatha Br. 14; Āraṇyaka layer)
- **Piṇḍapitṛyajña** - the **ancestral rice-ball offering** appended to the new-moon Darśa rite; feeds the pitṛs. (Śrauta-sūtras)
- **Upanayana / saṃskāras** - the **gṛhya life-cycle sacraments** (sacred-thread initiation, marriage homa, antyeṣṭi funeral) — the domestic counterpart to the śrauta system. (Gṛhya-sūtras) → see [Saṃskāras / Dharmaśāstra](agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md)
- *Note:* the **post-Vedic shift** — from public, animal-and-Soma śrauta sacrifice toward **interiorized, ahiṃsā-influenced, and devotional (pūjā/homa)** worship — is itself a major theme: the Upaniṣads already allegorize the yajña (e.g. **Aśvamedha = the cosmos itself**, Bṛhadāraṇyaka Up. 1.1), and Kali-Yuga injunctions formally suspend animal and human medhas. [scholarly]
