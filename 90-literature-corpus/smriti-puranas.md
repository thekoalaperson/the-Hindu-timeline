# Smṛti — The Purāṇas

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/` — the **recording texts**, mapped back onto the cosmic clock. The Purāṇas are *smṛti* ("that which is remembered," human-authored, in contrast to *śruti* / the Vedas), and they are the single largest narrative source for nearly every event in this timeline: creation (Brahmā's day), the Manvantaras, the Yuga cycle, the avatāra descents, and the royal genealogies (vaṃśa) that carry us into recorded history.
> See also `../01-manvantaras/` (the Manvantara framework the Purāṇas narrate) and `./` siblings for śruti and Itihāsa.

The Purāṇas are traditionally **18 Mahāpurāṇas** ("great Purāṇas") plus **18 Upapurāṇas** ("secondary Purāṇas"), conventionally said to total **400,000 ślokas** (verses), all attributed to **Vyāsa** (Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana) and recited in the frame-story by the *sūta* (bard) **Ugraśravas / Romaharṣaṇa** to the sages of the **Naimiṣa forest** during this Kali Yuga (see `../01-manvantaras/.../mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md`).

---

## The Pañca-Lakṣaṇa — the "Five Marks" of a Purāṇa

- **Pañca-lakṣaṇa (five defining topics)** — the classical definition of a Purāṇa, given in the **Matsya, Vāyu, and Viṣṇu Purāṇas** and in **Amarakośa**:
  1. **Sarga** — primary creation / cosmogony (the unfolding of the universe at the start of Brahmā's day).
  2. **Pratisarga** — secondary creation / dissolution-and-recreation (cycles of pralaya).
  3. **Vaṃśa** — genealogy of gods and ṛṣis (sages).
  4. **Manvantara** — the ages of the 14 Manus (we are in the 7th, Vaivasvata).
  5. **Vaṃśānucarita** — histories of the royal dynasties: the **Sūryavaṃśa** (Solar) and **Candravaṃśa** (Lunar) lines (Matsya P. 53.65; Viṣṇu P. 3.6).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 2.10 & 12.7):* the Bhāgavata expands the definition to **daśa-lakṣaṇa (ten marks)** — adding *sthāna* (cosmic maintenance), *poṣaṇa* (divine grace/sustenance), *ūti* (impulse/karmic motive), *īśānukathā* (tales of the Lord and devotees), *nirodha* (withdrawal), *mukti* (liberation), and *āśraya* (the ultimate substratum, Brahman). It calls the classical five the marks of a mere *alpa* (lesser) Purāṇa.
  - *Note:* In practice most Mahāpurāṇas are encyclopedic and far exceed the five marks — covering vrata (vows), tīrtha (pilgrimage), dāna (gift-giving), pratiṣṭhā (image consecration), dharmaśāstra, jyotiṣa, āyurveda, vāstu, and stotra.

## The Guṇa Classification — Sāttvic / Rājasic / Tāmasic

- **Tri-guṇa grouping of the 18 Mahāpurāṇas** — six Purāṇas to each of the three *guṇas* (qualities), correlated with a presiding deity who grants the corresponding rebirth/destination. Stated by **Śiva to Pārvatī in the Pādma Purāṇa (Uttara-khaṇḍa 236.18–21)**.
  - **Sāttvic (purity/light → Viṣṇu; leads to mokṣa):** Viṣṇu, Bhāgavata, Nāradīya, Garuḍa, Padma, Varāha.
  - **Rājasic (passion/activity → Brahmā; leads to svarga/heaven):** Brahma, Brahmāṇḍa, Brahmavaivarta, Mārkaṇḍeya, Bhaviṣya, Vāmana.
  - **Tāmasic (darkness/inertia → Śiva/Agni; leads to lower realms):** Matsya, Kūrma, Liṅga, Śiva, Skanda, Agni.
  - *Variant (assignment of Vāmana / Kūrma / Skanda):* lists disagree on which exact six fall in each guṇa. Some popular lists place **Śiva, Liṅga, Skanda, Agni, Kūrma, Vāmana** in tāmasic and shift **Matsya** to rājasic, etc. The core principle (Vaiṣṇava = sāttvic, Brāhma = rājasic, Śaiva = tāmasic) is stable; the boundary cases are not.
  - *[Disputed / sectarian]:* The scheme is itself a **Vaiṣṇava-leaning polemic** — labeling Śaiva texts "tāmasic" is a value judgment, not a neutral taxonomy. Śaiva traditions reject the rating. Treat the grouping as a *traditional mnemonic*, not an objective measure.

## The 18 Mahāpurāṇas

Conventional order and approximate verse counts; the one-line note records what each text *primarily covers in this timeline* (creation, which avatāra/yuga, which dynasty).

- **Brahma Purāṇa** (~10,000 v.) — "Ādi (first) Purāṇa." Cosmogony, sun/Sūrya worship, and tīrthas of Odisha (Puruṣottama/Jagannātha, Konark). Records *sarga* and early Sūrya/Candra genealogy.
- **Padma Purāṇa** (~55,000 v.) — Vast Vaiṣṇava encyclopedia in 5 khaṇḍas; cosmology, the lotus-creation of Brahmā, Viṣṇu's avatāras, vratas, and the guṇa-classification itself. Heavy on Treta/Dvāpara avatāra lore.
- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** (~23,000 v.) — The "model" Purāṇa, tightest fit to the pañca-lakṣaṇa. Sarga, Manvantaras, the **full Sūryavaṃśa and Candravaṃśa genealogies**, Kṛṣṇa's life, and a famed **Kali Yuga prophecy** (Book 4) ending the dynastic king-lists. A spine source for `../03-lineages-vamsha/`.
- **Śiva Purāṇa** (~24,000 v.) — Central Śaiva text; the liṅga, Śiva–Pārvatī marriage, Gaṇeśa and Kārttikeya's births, Dakṣa's sacrifice, the Tripura and Andhaka slayings. Primarily Satya/Treta-era cosmic Śaiva events.
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (Śrīmad Bhāgavatam, ~18,000 v.) — The most influential Purāṇa; 12 skandhas. The 10 (or 22/24) avatāras, the **full Kṛṣṇa carita (Dvāpara Yuga)** in Book 10, the Kali-doṣa and **Kalki prophecy** (Book 12), and the Manvantara scheme (Book 8). Core source for the Dvāpara node.
- **Nārada (Nāradīya) Purāṇa** (~25,000 v.) — Bhakti, vratas, tīrtha-māhātmya, and an index/summary of the other 17 Purāṇas. Largely ritual rather than narrative.
- **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa** (~9,000 v.) — Oldest core; frame of the birds (Jaimini's questions). Contains the **Devī Māhātmya / Durgā Saptaśatī** (Chaṇḍī) — Devī's slaying of Mahiṣāsura, Śumbha–Niśumbha. Primary Śākta cosmic-battle source.
- **Agni Purāṇa** (~15,400 v.) — Encyclopedic "Veda of all sciences": cosmology, avatāras, ritual, jyotiṣa, āyurveda, dhanurveda, vāstu, grammar. Narrated by Agni.
- **Bhaviṣya Purāṇa** (~14,500 v.) — "Purāṇa of the future"; prophecy + sun (Sūrya/Maga) worship, vratas, and notoriously interpolated "future king" sections [late text / heavily interpolated]. Records the *pratisarga* and Kali-future material.
- **Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa** (~18,000 v.) — Krṣṇa-Rādhā theology; Gaṇeśa- and Prakṛti-khaṇḍas. Cosmology centered on Kṛṣṇa as supreme. Dvāpara/devotional focus, late composition.
- **Liṅga Purāṇa** (~11,000 v.) — Śaiva; the meaning and origin of the liṅga, the 28 Śiva-avatāras (yogeśvaras), cosmogony, and Lakulīśa lore.
- **Varāha Purāṇa** (~24,000 v.) — Vaiṣṇava; framed by the **Varāha avatāra** (Viṣṇu lifting the Earth — a Satya-Yuga / Manvantara-boundary event), Mathurā tīrthas, vratas.
- **Skanda Purāṇa** (~81,100 v.) — The **largest** Purāṇa; vast collection of khaṇḍas (Kāśī, Veṅkaṭācala, Reva, etc.) and sthala-māhātmyas; Skanda/Kārttikeya's birth and the slaying of Tārakāsura. Mostly pilgrimage geography.
- **Vāmana Purāṇa** (~10,000 v.) — Framed by the **Vāmana (dwarf) avatāra** subduing Bali (Treta Yuga); also Śaiva/Śākta material, Kurukṣetra-region tīrthas.
- **Kūrma Purāṇa** (~17,000 v.) — Narrated by Viṣṇu as the **Kūrma (tortoise)** during the **Samudra Manthana** (churning of the ocean — a Satya-Yuga event); blends Vaiṣṇava and Śaiva, contains the **Īśvara Gītā**.
- **Matsya Purāṇa** (~14,000 v.) — One of the oldest; framed by the **Matsya (fish) avatāra** saving Manu and the seeds of life from the pralaya-flood (the Manvantara-transition par excellence). Gives the classic pañca-lakṣaṇa and king-lists.
- **Garuḍa Purāṇa** (~19,000 v.) — Narrated by Viṣṇu to Garuḍa; cosmology, but famed for the **Preta-khaṇḍa** — death, the afterlife journey, Yama, rebirth, and antyeṣṭi (funeral) rites.
- **Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa** (~12,000 v.) — Among the earliest; cosmology of the "cosmic egg" (brahmāṇḍa), detailed time-reckoning and geography; preserves the **Lalitā Sahasranāma** and **Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa** (in some recensions).

### Canonical-list variants (the contested 18th seats)
- ***Variant — Vāyu vs. Śiva Purāṇa:*** The **Vāyu Purāṇa** (~24,000 v., a very old Śaiva text) is counted among the 18 by the **Matsya, Vāyu, Liṅga, Kūrma, Agni, and Bhāgavata** lists — there it occupies the seat that other lists give to the **Śiva Purāṇa**. Lists that include **Śiva** (e.g. Padma, Viṣṇu) typically **drop Vāyu** to keep the count at 18. Many scholars regard *Vāyu* as the older, genuinely "great" Purāṇa and *Śiva* as a later substitute.
- ***Variant — Bhāgavata vs. Devī-Bhāgavata:*** The identity of "the Bhāgavata" is disputed between the Vaiṣṇava **Śrīmad Bhāgavata** (Kṛṣṇa-centered) and the Śākta **Devī-Bhāgavata** (Goddess-centered). Śākta tradition argues the *Devī-Bhāgavata* is the true Mahāpurāṇa; mainstream lists keep the *Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa Bhāgavata* and demote the Devī-Bhāgavata to an **Upapurāṇa**.
- ***Variant — Brahma vs. others:*** A few enumerations differ on whether **Brahma** or **Vāyu/Nṛsiṃha** fills a slot; the mnemonic verse "ma-dvayam bha-dvayam..." (two starting in *ma*: Matsya, Mārkaṇḍeya; two in *bha*: Bhaviṣya, Bhāgavata; etc.) was devised precisely to fix the canonical 18 against such drift.

## The 18 Upapurāṇas ("secondary" Purāṇas)

- **Note:** Unlike the Mahāpurāṇas, the lists of 18 Upapurāṇas **rarely agree**. Enumerations appear in the Kūrma, Garuḍa, Skanda, Padma, Sanatkumāra, Ekāmra, Vāruṇa, and Pārāśara Purāṇas, and in medieval digests (Hemādri's *Caturvargacintāmaṇi*, Ballāla Sena's *Dānasāgara*, the *Auśanasa*). Counting all texts ever called Upapurāṇas yields **nearly 100**.

A commonly cited list of 18 (per Kūrma & Garuḍa enumerations):

- **Sanatkumāra** — bhakti, dharma; ascribed to the sage Sanatkumāra.
- **Nṛsiṃha (Narasiṃha)** — glorification of the **Narasiṃha avatāra** (Satya-Yuga slaying of Hiraṇyakaśipu, protecting Prahlāda).
- **Bṛhan-Nāradīya** — Vaiṣṇava bhakti and vratas.
- **Śiva-rahasya** / **Śaiva** — Śaiva esoterica (some lists read this slot as "Śiva").
- **Durvāsā** — ascribed to sage Durvāsā.
- **Kapila** — Sāṃkhya philosophy, ascribed to Kapila.
- **Vāmana** — Vaiṣṇava (distinct from the Mahāpurāṇa).
- **Bhārgava (Bhārgava/Uśanas)** — ascribed to Śukra/Bhṛgu line.
- **Vāruṇa** — ascribed to Varuṇa.
- **Kālikā (Kālī)** — Śākta; Goddess worship, Kāmākhyā tīrtha.
- **Sāmba** — **Sūrya (sun) worship**, the founding of the Maga/Sāmba sun-cult (Kṛṣṇa's son Sāmba).
- **Nandi (Nandikeśvara)** — Śaiva, ascribed to Nandin.
- **Sūrya (Saura)** — solar deity worship.
- **Pārāśara** — ascribed to sage Parāśara.
- **Vāsiṣṭha (Vāsiṣṭha-Liṅga)** — Śaiva, ascribed to Vasiṣṭha.
- **Gaṇeśa** — Gāṇapatya; glorification of Gaṇeśa.
- **Mudgala** — Gāṇapatya esoterica (the eight Gaṇeśa avatāras).
- **Hamsa / Devī-Bhāgavata** — closing slot; many lists put the **Devī-Bhāgavata** here (see Mahāpurāṇa variant above).

### Upapurāṇa list variants
- ***Variant:*** Different source-lists swap in **Ādi, Maheśvara, Bhāgavata (Devī), Āditya, Vaiṣṇava, Manu, Auśanasa, Brahmāṇḍa, Varuṇa, Mārīca, Bhārgava, Saura, Pārāśara** in place of items above — no two enumerations (Kūrma vs. Garuḍa vs. Padma vs. Skanda) fully match.
- ***Variant (Devī-Bhāgavata):*** counted as a **Mahāpurāṇa** by the Vāyu, Matsya, and some Śākta lists; as an **Upapurāṇa** by the Padma, Garuḍa, and Kūrma lists. (See above.)
- ***Variant (Kalki Purāṇa):*** the **Kalki Purāṇa** (narrating the future Kalki avatāra ending Kali Yuga — see `../01-manvantaras/.../mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md`) is a **late Upapurāṇa** [late text], not in the classical 18.
- ***Variant (Sthala / Skanda fragments):*** countless regional **sthala-purāṇas** (temple/site legends) circulate as detached Skanda-Purāṇa khaṇḍas; these are *[folk / regional tradition]*, not canonical Upapurāṇas.

## Dating note (scripture vs. scholarship)

- **Traditional:** all Purāṇas are the work of **Vyāsa at the start of Kali Yuga (~3102 BCE)**, divided and disseminated by his pupil Romaharṣaṇa/Lomaharṣaṇa and the latter's son Ugraśravas.
- **Academic:** the Purāṇas are **composite, multi-layered texts** redacted over roughly **c. 300 BCE – 1500 CE**, with older cores (Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa, Mārkaṇḍeya, Matsya, Viṣṇu — early/mid 1st millennium CE) and very late strata (Bhaviṣya's "future kings," Brahmavaivarta, much of Skanda — medieval). The dynastic king-lists (vaṃśānucarita) are the most historically useful layer for `../03-lineages-vamsha/`. Keep scriptural attribution and historical dating clearly separate.
