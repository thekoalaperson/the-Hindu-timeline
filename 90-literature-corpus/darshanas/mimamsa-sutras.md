# Pūrva Mīmāṃsā Sūtras of Jaimini — Per-Text Profile

> Part of the **[Literature Corpus](../README.md)** → the six **[Darśanas](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md)**. Dedicated profile of **Jaimini's Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā-Sūtras** (a.k.a. *Mīmāṃsā Sūtra*, *Karma-Mīmāṃsā*) — the root text (*mūla*) of the **Mīmāṃsā** ("reflection / critical investigation") darśana, the school of **Vedic ritual hermeneutics and exegesis**. At **~2,700 sūtras in 12 adhyāyas** it is the **largest of the six darśana root-texts** (cf. the much shorter [Nyāya](./nyaya-sutras.md), [Vaiśeṣika](./vaisheshika-sutras.md), [Sāṃkhya-Kārikā](./samkhya-karika.md) and even the [Brahma Sūtras](./brahma-sutras.md)).
> "Pūrva" ("prior") = inquiry into the **earlier, action-portion (karma-kāṇḍa)** of the Veda; its sequel/sibling is **Uttara-Mīmāṃsā = Vedānta**, the [Brahma Sūtras](./brahma-sutras.md)' inquiry into the **knowledge-portion (jñāna-kāṇḍa)**. The two share the same **adhikaraṇa method** (§7) and were once a single *śāstra*.
>
> **Two clocks:** by tradition **Jaimini** is a r̥ṣi-pupil of [Veda Vyāsa](../../05-rishis-and-sages/README.md) (to whom Vyāsa is said to have taught the Sāma-Veda), the Mīmāṃsā being a timeless science of the eternal Veda; academically the surviving sūtra-text is **c. 300–200 BCE** (a likely multi-layered redaction), with the school developed by Śabara (~5th c. CE) and the great rivals **Kumārila Bhaṭṭa** and **Prabhākara** (~7th c. CE).

---

## 1. What the text is

- **Name & genre** — *Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā-Sūtra*; **mīmāṃsā** = "profound reflection / critical investigation" (from √man, "to think"), here the disciplined inquiry into the **meaning and ritual force of Vedic injunctions**. Also **Karma-Mīmāṃsā** ("inquiry into [ritual] action") and **Dharma-Mīmāṃsā**. The text is the *foundational exegetical grammar* of Vedic sacrifice — less a metaphysics than a **science of how to read the Veda as a system of commands**. (Wikipedia "Purva Mimamsa Sutras"; Britannica "Purva Mimamsa")
- **Author** — **Jaimini** (*Jaimini Maharṣi*), traditionally a disciple of **[Vyāsa](../smriti-itihasa-epics.md)** and the r̥ṣi to whom the Sāma-Veda was transmitted; also credited (by tradition) with a *Mīmāṃsā* on the deities and an astrological *Jaimini Sūtra*. As with all sūtra-r̥ṣis, "Jaimini" likely names a school/redactor for the surviving stratum. (Wikipedia "Jaimini")
- **The object of inquiry — *dharma*** — the entire text is launched by its first sūtra: **"athāto dharma-jijñāsā"** — *"Now, therefore, [begins] the inquiry into dharma"* (MS 1.1.1) — and immediately defines its object: **"codanā-lakṣaṇo 'rtho dharmaḥ"** — *"Dharma is that beneficial purpose (artha) characterized by an injunction (codanā)"* (MS 1.1.2). Crucially, **dharma is known ONLY from Vedic command, never from perception or reason** — it is the supremely *adr̥ṣṭa* (unperceivable) good. (Britannica; Grokipedia "Purva Mimamsa Sutras")
  - *Parallel openings:* this **"athāto … jijñāsā"** formula is mirrored verbatim by the [Brahma Sūtras](./brahma-sutras.md) — *"athāto brahma-jijñāsā"* ("now the inquiry into Brahman", BS 1.1.1) — marking Vedānta as the deliberate **Uttara** ("later") sequel to Jaimini's **Pūrva** ("prior") inquiry. [the two-Mīmāṃsā frame]
- **Sūtra brevity** — like every [darśana sūtra](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md), the aphorisms are maximally compressed and **unreadable without the bhāṣya**; **Śabara's *Śabara-Bhāṣya*** (§8) is the indispensable lens through which the whole text is read.

## 2. Architecture — 12 / 60 / ~907 / ~2,700

> The **largest** darśana root-text by far, and the most rigorously *systematic* in layout.

- **The standard count** — **12 adhyāyas** (chapters) → subdivided into **60 padas** (sections; most adhyāyas have 4 padas, but **3, 6, and 10 have 8 padas**) → organized into **~907 adhikaraṇas** (topical units, §7) → totalling roughly **~2,700–2,745 sūtras**. (Wikipedia "Purva Mimamsa Sutras"; Grokipedia)
  - *Variant (sūtra count):* commonly cited as **~2,700**, with **2,745** also reported; as with all sūtra-texts the exact figure is **edition/commentator-dependent** (split-vs-merge), but **12 adhyāyas / 60 padas is fixed**. [disputed count]
- **The Saṃkarṣa-kāṇḍa ("Devatā-kāṇḍa") — a 13th-book question** — a supplementary 4-adhyāya text (the *Madhyama-Mīmāṃsā*), traditionally ascribed to Jaimini and treated as adhyāyas 13–16, is held by some as a continuation; most reckon the canonical Sūtra as the **12** only. [disputed]
- **Thematic sweep (12 books)** — the work builds from **general hermeneutic principles → specific ritual mechanics**:
  - **Adhyāyas 1–2 — *pramāṇa & vākya* (authority and sentence-analysis):** Book 1 establishes the **validity of the Veda as the sole source of dharma** (the famous *pramāṇa-pāda*, MS 1.1) and classifies Vedic sentences into **vidhi / arthavāda / mantra / nāmadheya** (§4); Book 2 treats the **differentiation of rites (bheda)** and verbal distinctions. (Wikipedia; exoticindiaart)
  - **Adhyāyas 3–4 — *śeṣa* (subsidiary–principal relations) & *prayojana*:** which acts are **principal (pradhāna)** vs **auxiliary (śeṣa/aṅga)**, the order of performance, and the **purpose (apūrva-)** for which rites are done. (Britannica)
  - **Adhyāyas 5–6 — *krama* (sequence) & *adhikāra* (eligibility):** the **order** of ritual operations and the **qualification of the sacrificer (adhikārin)** — who is entitled to perform which sacrifice. (Wikipedia)
  - **Adhyāyas 7–8 — *atideśa* (transference/extension):** how the procedures of an **archetype rite (prakr̥ti)** are transferred to a **derived rite (vikr̥ti)** — the analogical machinery of the ritual system. (Wikipedia; exoticindiaart)
  - **Adhyāyas 9–10 — *ūha* (adaptation/modification) & *bādha* (exclusion):** how mantras and procedures are **modified** when transferred, and when an element is **cancelled** as inapplicable. (Britannica)
  - **Adhyāyas 11–12 — *tantra & prasaṅga* (concurrent performance / economy):** when one act serves **several sacrifices at once** (tantra), and incidental performance (prasaṅga) — the "efficiency rules" of ritual. (Wikipedia)

## 3. The five foundational doctrines

- **1. Apauruṣeyatva — the authorless, eternal Veda** — the Veda is **apauruṣeya** ("not made by any person," human *or* divine); it is **eternal (nitya), beginningless, and uncreated**. Jaimini grounds this on the **eternality of the word-meaning relation**: *"the relation of word and meaning is primordial / inborn (autpattika)"* (MS 1.1.5) — words and their referents are connected since beginningless time, so the Veda has **no author to err or to be doubted**. This is Mīmāṃsā's most distinctive thesis and the keystone of Vedic authority. (Wikipedia "Mīmāṃsā"; Wikipedia "Apauruṣeyā")
- **2. Śabda-nityatva — the eternality of sound/word** — corollary to apauruṣeyatva: **speech-sounds (śabda) are eternal substances**, not produced but only *manifested* by utterance; against the Nyāya/Vaiśeṣika view that sound is a produced, perishable quality. The Veda is therefore an **eternal verbal structure**, merely *handed down* (not composed) by the r̥ṣis. (Britannica; Mīmāṃsā sources)
- **3. Svataḥ-prāmāṇya — intrinsic validity of cognition** — **every cognition is self-validating**: it arises *already* carrying its own truth, and is to be **accepted as true unless and until contradicted** (by sublation or by discovery of a defective cause). Truth is **intrinsic (svataḥ)**, falsity **extrinsic (parataḥ)** — the reverse of Nyāya, which makes both validity and invalidity extrinsic. This is what makes the Veda **self-evidently authoritative**: needing no external warrant (no God, no inference) to certify it. (Wikipedia "Mīmāṃsā"; Britannica)
- **4. The injunction (codanā/vidhi) as the heart of the Veda** — the Veda's **essential, defining content is its COMMANDS**: a sentence that is not (directly or indirectly) in the service of an injunction to act is, for Mīmāṃsā, **not the Veda's true purport**. Dharma = "what the Veda enjoins"; the Veda is fundamentally **prescriptive, not descriptive**. (Britannica; Grokipedia)
- **5. Apūrva — the unseen ritual potency** (§6) — the mechanism by which a present sacrifice yields a **future, unseen result** (heaven, rain, a son) long after the rite is over.
- **Rejection of Īśvara (God) as Veda-source** — Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā is **non-theistic in its core logic**: it **denies any creator-God as author or validator of the Veda**. Kumārila and Prabhākara argue there is **"no need to postulate a maker for the world, just as there is no need for an author to compose the Vedas or a God to validate the rituals."** The eternal Veda + svataḥ-prāmāṇya makes a divine guarantor **redundant**. The Vedic gods (Indra, Agni…) exist as **words/datives in mantras** — they are the *deities to whom oblation is offered*, but **not** an Īśvara who created or authored scripture. **[non-theistic — contrast Nyāya's inferred Īśvara]** (Wikipedia "Mīmāṃsā"; cf. [Nyāya Sūtras §9](./nyaya-sutras.md))
  - *Note:* this is the central **theist (Nyāya/Vaiśeṣika) vs. ritualist-non-theist (Mīmāṃsā)** fault-line over the Veda. Both schools hold the Veda supremely authoritative; they disagree only on *why* — **divinely authored & rationally inferred** (Nyāya) vs. **authorless, eternal & self-evident** (Mīmāṃsā). [contrast]

## 4. Ritual hermeneutics — the four classes of Vedic sentence

> Mīmāṃsā's signature analytic move: **every Vedic utterance is sorted by its function**, and all classes are ultimately subordinated to the **injunction**.

- **1. Vidhi (injunction)** — the **prescriptive command** that enjoins an action not knowable by any other means, e.g. *"agnihotraṃ juhuyāt svarga-kāmaḥ"* — "one desiring heaven should offer the agnihotra." **This is the only truly self-sufficient class** — the *real* Veda; everything else exists to serve it. Subtypes: **apūrva-vidhi** (enjoins something wholly new), **niyama-vidhi** (restricts among options), **parisaṃkhyā-vidhi** (excludes/limits). (Wikipedia; scribd "Purva Mimamsa Key Doctrines")
- **2. Arthavāda (explanatory / eulogistic passage)** — sentences of **praise (stuti) or blame (nindā)**, narratives and "reasons," that **do not command but motivate** obedience to a vidhi (e.g. "the wind is the swiftest deity"). On their own they assert no independent fact; their authority is **borrowed** — they are valid *only* as they prop up an injunction. (Wikipedia; Grokipedia)
- **3. Mantra** — the **liturgical formula uttered during the rite**, whose function is to **bring to mind (smāraka)** the deity, object, and procedure at the moment of performance; mantras *accompany and enable* the act enjoined by the vidhi. (Britannica; Mīmāṃsā sources)
- **4. Nāmadheya (naming sentence)** — sentences that **merely name a sacrifice** (e.g. "[the rite is] called *udbhid*"); they look declarative but, by Mīmāṃsā's rule that **no Vedic sentence is idle**, are construed as **subservient to the injunction** that uses the name. (Wikipedia; scribd)
- **The governing maxim — *every Vedic word has a purpose*** — Mīmāṃsā holds the Veda contains **nothing meaningless or merely descriptive**; even apparent statements of fact (arthavāda, nāmadheya) must be **bent into the service of some vidhi** (*vidhinā tv ekavākyatvāt*…) — the great interpretive principle that **the Veda is, in its essence, a unified system of commands**. (Britannica)

## 5. Codanā — the injunctive force

- **Codanā** = the **specifically injunctive/impelling power** of a Vedic sentence — the "ought," the prompting to act, carried by the optative/imperative verb-endings (*-yāt*, *liṅ*). Jaimini's MS 1.1.2 makes **codanā the very mark (lakṣaṇa) of dharma**: dharma is whatever codanā enjoins. (Wikipedia "Mīmāṃsā"; Britannica)
- **The sub-schools split on what codanā/vidhi signifies** (see §9):
  - **Bhāṭṭa (Kumārila):** the verb conveys a **bhāvanā** — a "bringing-into-being," an *efficient force* impelling action toward a desired end (productive causation). Action is undertaken **for the sake of the desired fruit (phala)**.
  - **Prabhākara:** the verb conveys a **niyoga / kārya** — a pure **"ought-to-be-done" (duty)** that is *its own* motive; one acts because the Veda commands, **for duty's sake**, independent of desire. This is a famously **proto-Kantian "categorical" reading** of Vedic obligation. **[scholarly]**

## 6. Apūrva — the unseen ritual potency

> Mīmāṃsā's solution to the **time-gap problem**: a sacrifice performed *now* (e.g. the *jyotiṣṭoma*) is said to yield **heaven** only *after death* — so what carries the causal link across the interval, after the rite has ceased to exist?

- **Apūrva ("the not-before / the novel")** — a **supersensible (adr̥ṣṭa) potency or after-force** generated by the correct performance of a rite, which **persists** and later **ripens into the promised fruit** (svarga, rain, a son). It is the **invisible bond between the transient act and its remote result** — Mīmāṃsā's purely *ritual* substitute for a fruit-dispensing God. (CGPCS "Theory of Apurva"; indianphilosophyblog "Apūrva")
- **Bhāṭṭa apūrva** — a **metaphysical efficacy**: a real supersensible *capacity* (potential, *śakti*) lodged by the act in the agent/self, that matures into the result. A causal **link** between deed and recompense. (CGPCS; Britannica)
- **Prabhākara apūrva** — read more **deontically**: apūrva ≈ the **kārya / "what-ought-to-be-done"** itself — the very content of the duty (niyoga), not a separate metaphysical fluid. The "novelty" is the *new obligation* the injunction creates. (indianphilosophyblog "The Apūrva in Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā")
- **Why it matters** — apūrva lets Mīmāṃsā keep its **non-theism**: the moral economy (good rite → good fruit) runs **automatically and impersonally**, with **no Īśvara needed** to bookkeep karma. It is the ritual cousin of the **adr̥ṣṭa/karma** mechanism used across Indian philosophy. [contrast Nyāya, where Īśvara supervises adr̥ṣṭa] (cf. [Nyāya §9](./nyaya-sutras.md))

## 7. The adhikaraṇa method — Mīmāṃsā's five-part exegetical engine

> The **shared procedural backbone** that Mīmāṃsā gave to the whole of Sanskrit scholasticism — and which the **[Brahma Sūtras](./brahma-sutras.md) adopt wholesale**.

- **Adhikaraṇa** ("topic / case") — the **unit of exegesis**: each of the ~907 adhikaraṇas resolves **one interpretive doubt** about a Vedic passage, proceeding through a fixed **five-membered dialectic**:
  - **1. Viṣaya** — the **passage/topic** under examination (the text to be interpreted).
  - **2. Saṃśaya** — the **doubt**: what does it mean? which of two readings holds?
  - **3. Pūrvapakṣa** — the **prima-facie / opponent's view** (the wrong-but-plausible answer), stated at full strength.
  - **4. Uttarapakṣa (siddhānta)** — the **refutation and the established conclusion** (the correct reading).
  - **5. Saṅgati** — the **connection / relevance** of the resolution to the surrounding discussion and the larger system.
  - *(Source: Grokipedia "Purva Mimamsa Sutras"; the adhikaraṇa schema is the standard Mīmāṃsā format.)*
- **The Mīmāṃsā-nyāyas (maxims)** — out of this method came a stock of **interpretive maxims (nyāyas)** — e.g. the *adhikaraṇa-nyāya* itself, the principle that an injunction governs its eulogy (*ekavākyatā*), and the rule that an explicit text overrides an inferred one — which became the **standard tools of Dharmaśāstra, law, and Vedānta** for reading authoritative texts. **[Mīmāṃsā as India's "science of interpretation"]** (Britannica)
- **Inherited by Vedānta** — the [Brahma Sūtras](./brahma-sutras.md) are organized into the *same* adhikaraṇa units with the *same* viṣaya→saṃśaya→pūrvapakṣa→siddhānta→saṅgati flow; **Pūrva and Uttara Mīmāṃsā are two halves of one hermeneutic enterprise.** (Wikipedia "Brahma Sutras")

## 8. The commentarial lineage

- **Śabara (Śabarasvāmin) — *Śabara-Bhāṣya*** (**c. 4th–5th c. CE**, dated variously) — the **only extant commentary on all 12 adhyāyas**, and the **foundational** prose exposition through which the entire Sūtra is read; effectively *constitutes* classical Mīmāṃsā. All later Mīmāṃsā is commentary on Śabara. (Wikipedia "Purva Mimamsa Sutras")
  - *Note:* Śabara preserves fragments of a still-earlier commentator, **Upavarṣa** (the "Vr̥ttikāra"), cited also by Vedāntins — a lost layer behind both Mīmāṃsā and Vedānta. [scholarly]
- **Kumārila Bhaṭṭa** (**c. 7th c. CE**) — sub-commentary on Śabara in **three works: *Ślokavārttika* (verse), *Tantravārttika*, *Ṭupṭīkā***; founder of the **Bhāṭṭa** sub-school (§9); a towering defender of Vedic ritualism against Buddhism, famed for vindicating the Veda's authority by reason. (Wikipedia "Mīmāṃsā"; Britannica)
- **Prabhākara Miśra** (**c. 7th c. CE**) — commentaries *Br̥hatī* and *Laghvī* on Śabara; founder of the rival **Prābhākara** sub-school (§9). Nicknamed **"Guru"**; his system is the **Guru-mata**. (Wikipedia "Prabhākara"; philopedia)
- **Maṇḍana Miśra** (**c. 8th c.**) — Mīmāṃsaka (*Vidhiviveka*, *Bhāvanāviveka*) who, by tradition, was converted by Śaṅkara to Advaita — a hinge between Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā and Vedānta. (Britannica)
- **Śālikanātha** (*Prakaraṇa-pañcikā*) and **Pārthasārathi Miśra** (*Śāstra-dīpikā*) — the great systematizers of the **Prābhākara** and **Bhāṭṭa** schools respectively. (exoticindiaart "Prakaraṇapañcikā")
- **Khaṇḍadeva, Āpadeva, Laugākṣi Bhāskara** (16th–17th c.) — late manuals (*Bhāṭṭa-dīpikā*, *Mīmāṃsā-nyāya-prakāśa*, *Artha-saṃgraha*) that codified Mīmāṃsā for pedagogy. [late texts]

## 9. The two sub-schools — Bhāṭṭa vs. Prābhākara

> The school cleaves into two great rivals named for **Kumārila Bhaṭṭa** and **Prabhākara**, differing on pramāṇas, error, language, and the nature of duty.

- **Pramāṇas accepted** —
  - **Prābhākara: five (5)** — pratyakṣa (perception), anumāna (inference), upamāna (comparison), **arthāpatti** (postulation), śabda (testimony). (Wikipedia "Mīmāṃsā")
  - **Bhāṭṭa: six (6)** — the same five **plus anupalabdhi** (**non-apprehension** — knowledge of an *absence*, e.g. "there is no pot here" by the non-perceiving of one). The Bhāṭṭa six-pramāṇa list is the **most expansive of any darśana** (cf. Nyāya's 4, Sāṃkhya's 3, Vaiśeṣika's 2). (Wikipedia "Mīmāṃsā"; "Pramana"; cf. [Nyāya §4](./nyaya-sutras.md))
  - *Both* schools (unlike Nyāya) accept **arthāpatti** (postulation) as irreducible — e.g. "fat Devadatta does not eat by day" *postulates* that he eats by night.
- **Theory of error** —
  - **Prābhākara — *akhyāti-vāda* (a.k.a. *vivekākhyāti*, "non-discrimination"):** there is **no positive false cognition**; "error" is a **failure to distinguish** two separately-valid cognitions (a perceived *this* + a remembered *silver*), so that the difference between them is *not apprehended*. All cognition as such is true; we merely fail to keep two truths apart. (philosophy.institute "Mīmāṃsā Error"; Wikipedia "Prabhākara")
  - **Bhāṭṭa — *viparīta-khyāti* (a.k.a. anyathā-khyāti, "misapprehension"):** error is a **positive misplacement** — the *this* is actually cognized *as* silver (the object is apprehended "otherwise" than it is). [contrast]
- **Theory of meaning / sentence-construction** —
  - **Prābhākara — *anvitābhidhāna* ("designation of the connected"):** words **directly signify their referents already-construed-with-the-others**; sentence-meaning is grasped *together with* word-meaning. Tied to their view that **children learn language through injunctions** (commands acted upon).
  - **Bhāṭṭa — *abhihitānvaya* ("connection of the designated"):** words **first signify their isolated meanings**, which are *then* secondarily combined into a sentence-meaning. A compositional semantics. **[the central Mīmāṃsā debate in philosophy of language]** (Wikipedia "Mīmāṃsā"; "Prabhākara")
- **Nature of the injunction / motive to act** (cf. §5) —
  - **Bhāṭṭa:** the verb = **bhāvanā** (productive impulse toward a *desired result*); one acts **for the fruit**. Apūrva = a real supersensible efficacy.
  - **Prabhākara:** the verb = **niyoga / kārya** (a categorical **"ought," duty for its own sake**); one acts **because commanded**, the fruit being secondary. **A deontological ("Kantian") ethics of pure obligation.** [scholarly]
- **The self & liberation** — both schools posit a **plurality of permanent selves**; classical Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā's goal is **svarga (heaven) via ritual**, and it is famously **reticent or unconcerned about mokṣa** — liberation enters Mīmāṃsā only late (under Vedāntic influence), reconceived as the self's freedom from rebirth once one **ceases desire-driven rites and exhausts karma**. [late development]

## 10. Place in the system — Mīmāṃsā among the darśanas

- **The ritualist pole of Vedic orthodoxy** — Mīmāṃsā is the most **Veda-centric, Brāhmaṇa-rooted** of the six āstika schools: where Sāṃkhya/Yoga turn inward and Nyāya/Vaiśeṣika turn to logic and atoms, Mīmāṃsā makes the **performance and interpretation of yajña** the whole of dharma. (See [yajñas & Vedic rituals](../yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md) and [Vedas & Upaniṣads](../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md).)
- **Paired with Vedānta** — Pūrva (karma-kāṇḍa, action) + Uttara (jñāna-kāṇḍa, knowledge) = the two **Mīmāṃsās**; some hold (esp. earlier) that the **full Veda enjoins ritual** and even read the Upaniṣads' Brahman-knowledge as subordinate to action — a thesis Śaṅkara's Advaita ([Brahma Sūtras](./brahma-sutras.md)) was written to **overturn**. (Wikipedia "Brahma Sutras")
- **Gift to all of Sanskrit learning** — the **adhikaraṇa method and the Mīmāṃsā maxims (nyāyas)** became the **shared interpretive toolkit** of Dharmaśāstra/Hindu law, Vedānta, and Sanskrit scholasticism generally — Mīmāṃsā is the **discipline that taught India how to read an authoritative text**. (Britannica)
- **Engagement with grammar & philosophy of language** — through the word-eternality and anvitābhidhāna/abhihitānvaya debates, Mīmāṃsā is, with the grammarians (Bhartr̥hari), one of the two great Indian theaters of **philosophy of language**. [scholarly]

## 11. Dating — the two clocks

- **Traditional / scripture clock** — **Jaimini**, pupil of [Vyāsa](../smriti-itihasa-epics.md) and r̥ṣi of the Sāma-Veda, transmits (does not "compose") an eternal science of the **apauruṣeya** Veda; no historical date is assigned in tradition. [scripture]
- **Academic clock** — the surviving Sūtra-text is dated **c. 300–200 BCE** (Wikipedia), with broader scholarly ranges spanning **c. 500–200 BCE** for a likely **multi-layered** redaction; **Śabara's Bhāṣya c. 4th–5th c. CE**; the sub-schools (**Kumārila, Prabhākara**) **c. 7th c. CE**. [scholarly] (Wikipedia "Purva Mimamsa Sutras"; Grokipedia)
  - *Variant (composite authorship):* "Jaimini" is widely taken to name a **tradition/redactor**, not a single historical author, given the text's systematic but layered character. [disputed]
