# Sāṃkhya-kārikā of Īśvarakṛṣṇa — Per-Text Profile

> Part of the **[Literature Corpus](../README.md)** · Master index: **[The Hindu Timeline](../../README.md)** · Parent survey: **[Upavedas, Vedāngas & the Six Darśanas](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md)**. This node is the **dedicated deep profile** of the *Sāṃkhya-kārikā* (~4th–5th c. CE) — the authoritative classical text of the **Sāṃkhya darśana**, the oldest-surviving systematic Sāṃkhya work (the rival "root" *Sāṃkhya-sūtra* being a late medieval reconstruction). Composed within the current [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/), early [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) by traditional reckoning; academically Gupta-era (c. 350–500 CE).

---

## Two clocks — dating

- **Scriptural / traditional clock.** Sāṃkhya is held to be primordial revelation: the sage **Kapila** (a manifestation/avatāra of Viṣṇu) taught it at the dawn of the present cosmic order, transmitting it through **Āsuri → Pañcaśikha** to a long disciple-lineage. By this clock the doctrine is timeless; Īśvarakṛṣṇa is only its late versifier. (*Sāṃkhya-kārikā* 69–71; *Bhagavata Purāṇa* 3.24–33.)
- **Academic clock.** The *Kārikā* itself is dated to the **Gupta period, c. 350–500 CE**; its *terminus ante quem* is **569 CE**, fixed by **Paramārtha's Chinese translation** (with commentary) — the oldest datable witness to the text. Gerald Larson and others place Īśvarakṛṣṇa floruit c. 350 CE. [scholarly]
- **Why this text and not the "Sūtras."** The *Sāṃkhya-sūtra* traditionally ascribed to Kapila **cannot be dated earlier than c. 1380–1450 CE** and is a late reconstruction; therefore the *Kārikā* is treated as the **authoritative classical statement** of the school, not its (lost or notional) root-sūtra. [scholarly] See the school overview in [Upavedas, Vedāngas & the Six Darśanas §3](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md).

## Form & scope

- **~72 verses (kārikās)** in the **Āryā metre** — terse, mnemonic, doctrine-dense; among the most compact darśana root-texts.
- **The "70 verses" puzzle** - kārikā 72 states the work contained only **seventy** ślokas (omitting illustrative/colophon verses); the received text runs to ~72. Gaudapāda's commentary covers the first **69**; the *Yuktidīpikā* analyzes 66 of 72. The "missing" verses (sometimes including a so-called 63rd/abhūtādi verse) are a known **transmission crux**. [scholarly]
- **Self-placement (kārikā 69–71).** Īśvarakṛṣṇa names himself a successor in the line **Kapila → Āsuri → Pañcaśikha**, presenting the *Kārikā* as a faithful epitome (*saptati*, "the seventy") of that older oral tradition.

## The 25 tattvas (principles of reality)

The *Kārikā* (verse 3) frames all reality under a **fourfold productivity scheme**, totaling **25 tattvas**:

- **Mūla-prakṛti** — the **uncreated creator**: primordial unmanifest matter; produces but is not produced. (*SK* 3.)
- **Seven evolutes** — **created creators** (productive products): **mahat/buddhi, ahaṃkāra,** and the **five tanmātras** (subtle elements). (*SK* 3.)
- **Sixteen evolutes** — **created non-creators** (products only): the **eleven indriyas** (manas + 5 sense-organs + 5 action-organs) and the **five mahābhūtas** (gross elements). (*SK* 3.)
- **Puruṣa** — **neither created nor creating**: pure witnessing consciousness, outside the productive chain. (*SK* 3.)

### The dualism: Puruṣa and Prakṛti

- **Puruṣa (पुरुष) — consciousness.** Eternal, inactive, attributeless, a passive **witness (sākṣin)**; **plural** (many puruṣas, one per being — *SK* 18, argued from the staggered birth/death and varied fortunes of beings); never an agent, never bound in truth — only *apparently* bound through misidentification.
- **Prakṛti (प्रकृति) — primordial matter/nature.** Single, unconscious, ever-active, the **material cause** of everything manifest; exists as **avyakta** (unmanifest equilibrium) and **vyakta** (manifest, evolved). It is the sole **doer**; Puruṣa only seems to act.
- **The blind-and-lame allegory (*SK* 21).** Their union is purposive and symbiotic - **blind Prakṛti carries lame Puruṣa**: matter is active but unconscious, consciousness is aware but inert; their proximity (*saṃyoga*) sets evolution going **for the sake of Puruṣa** (experience *bhoga* and ultimately release *apavarga*). Then they separate.
- **Teleology without a God.** Prakṛti evolves "unconsciously yet purposively," like **milk flowing for the calf** (*SK* 57) — directed toward Puruṣa's experience and liberation, requiring **no divine designer**. See [non-theism](#non-theism-nirīśvara) below.

### The three guṇas (constituents of Prakṛti)

- **Sattva** — illumination, lightness, pleasure (*sukha*), buoyancy. (*SK* 12–13.)
- **Rajas** — activity, motion, restlessness, pain (*duḥkha*); the kinetic principle that **drives evolution**. (*SK* 12–13.)
- **Tamas** — inertia, heaviness, darkness, delusion (*moha*). (*SK* 12–13.)
- **Mechanics.** The guṇas are **substantive constituents**, not mere qualities; mutually dominating-and-subordinating, ever in flux. **Equilibrium = unmanifest Prakṛti; disturbance (by Puruṣa's proximity) = manifestation.** Their cooperation is likened to **lamp, wick, and oil acting jointly to give light** (*SK* 13). All three pervade every evolute, in varying ratio — the world's diversity is guṇa-proportion.

## The order of evolution (sarga / pariṇāma)

From Prakṛti, disturbed, the tattvas emerge in a fixed sequence (*SK* 22–38):

- **Mahat / Buddhi** — the **"Great Principle," intellect**; first evolute; seat of determination (*adhyavasāya*), the faculty by which liberating discrimination finally arises. (*SK* 23.)
- **Ahaṃkāra** — the **ego / I-maker**; from it the stream forks by guṇa-predominance (*SK* 24–25):
  - *Sāttvika (vaikārika) ahaṃkāra* → the **eleven indriyas**.
  - *Tāmasa (bhūtādi) ahaṃkāra* → the **five tanmātras**.
  - *Rājasa (taijasa) ahaṃkāra* → energizes both branches.
- **Manas (mind)** — the **eleventh indriya**; coordinator/synthesizer of sense-data, both a *buddhīndriya* and *karmendriya* function. (*SK* 27.)
- **Five jñānendriyas (sense-organs)** — ear, skin, eye, tongue, nose (faculties of hearing, touch, sight, taste, smell).
- **Five karmendriyas (action-organs)** — voice, hands, feet, anus, generative organ.
- **Five tanmātras (subtle elements)** — *śabda, sparśa, rūpa, rasa, gandha* (sound, touch, form/color, taste, smell-essence). (*SK* 38.)
- **Five mahābhūtas (gross elements)** — **ākāśa, vāyu, tejas/agni, ap, pṛthivī** (ether, air, fire, water, earth), each arising from its tanmātra. (*SK* 38–39.)
- **The liṅga-śarīra (subtle body).** The transmigrating "subtle body" of **mahat + ahaṃkāra + 11 indriyas + 5 tanmātras (18 components)** persists across rebirths until liberation. (*SK* 40–42.)
  - *Variant (count):* the subtle body is enumerated as **17 or 18 tattvas** depending on whether manas is counted separately; commentaries differ. [scholarly]

## Satkāryavāda — the pre-existent effect

- **Core thesis (*SK* 9).** The **effect pre-exists in its cause** before manifestation; production = *manifestation* of the latent, not creation *ex nihilo*. "That which is not (*asat*) cannot be brought into being."
- **Five proofs (*SK* 9).** (1) *asadakaraṇāt* — the non-existent cannot be produced; (2) *upādāna-grahaṇāt* — a specific material is needed for a specific effect (oil only from oilseeds); (3) *sarva-sambhava-abhāvāt* — not everything can arise from everything; (4) *śaktasya śakya-karaṇāt* — a capable cause produces only what it is capable of; (5) *kāraṇa-bhāvāt* — the effect is of the nature/essence of its cause.
- **Cosmological payoff.** Because the effect is latent in the cause, the **entire manifest world pre-exists in Prakṛti** and merely *unfolds* (*pariṇāma*, real transformation) — the metaphysical engine behind every Puranic emergence-from-the-unmanifest. Contrast with Advaita's **vivarta-vāda** (apparent, illusory transformation) and Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika's **asatkāryavāda** (the new effect). See [Vedānta sub-schools](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md).

## Epistemology — the three pramāṇas

- **Three valid means of knowledge (*SK* 4–6)** — **pratyakṣa** (perception), **anumāna** (inference), **āpta-vacana / śabda** (reliable testimony). All other purported pramāṇas reduce to these three.
- **Inference reaches the unseen.** Subtle realities (Prakṛti, Puruṣa, the tanmātras) are established by **inference**, since they exceed perception — Sāṃkhya's licence for its whole invisible ontology. (*SK* 6–8: non-perception has seven causes, e.g. excessive subtlety, distance.)

## Non-theism (nirīśvara)

- **No creator God required.** Classical Sāṃkhya is **nirīśvara** — Prakṛti's unconscious teleology plus plural Puruṣas suffices to explain the world; an **Īśvara is neither posited nor needed.** This is Sāṃkhya's signature divergence from **[Yoga](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md)** ("Seśvara Sāṃkhya," Sāṃkhya-with-God). [doctrinal variant]
- **The *Kārikā*'s own stance.** The text is **functionally atheistic**: it grounds cosmic process in matter and consciousness alone and never invokes a God as cause. (Bronkhorst notes the *Kārikā* is technically *silent* on God — neither affirming nor explicitly denying — but the school's classical commentators read it as nirīśvara.) [scholarly nuance]
- **Vācaspati Miśra's argument.** In the *Tattvakaumudī*, a creator-God is rejected as incoherent - a perfect God **has no unfulfilled desire** and **no need to act**, so cannot be motivated to create; the world's purpose is instead the liberation of bound souls.

## Liberation — viveka, kaivalya

- **Bondage is misidentification.** Puruṣa, ever-free, *appears* bound by wrongly identifying with Prakṛti's evolutes (buddhi, ego, body). Suffering (the *triple suffering* opening the text, *SK* 1) motivates the whole inquiry. (*SK* 20, 62.)
- **Knowledge alone liberates (*SK* 63–64).** Release comes by **viveka / viveka-khyāti** — the **discriminative discernment that Puruṣa is utterly distinct from Prakṛti** ("I am not, nothing is mine, not-I" — *nāsmi, na me, nāham*, *SK* 64). No ritual, no grace.
- **Kaivalya (isolation).** On discrimination, Prakṛti **ceases to perform for that Puruṣa** ("like a dancer who withdraws after the audience has seen her," *SK* 59) and the two separate; Puruṣa abides in **absolute aloneness/isolation (kaivalya)** — the same liberation-goal Yoga adopts. (*SK* 65–68.)
- **Jīvanmukti via residual momentum (*SK* 67).** Even after liberating knowledge dawns, the body persists by **lingering momentum** — like a **potter's wheel still spinning after the potter's hand lifts** — until prārabdha karma exhausts; then final isolation. [a Sāṃkhya warrant for "living liberation"]

## Kapila — the mythic founder

- **Founder-sage (mythic).** Sāṃkhya is ascribed to the primordial sage **Kapila**, source of the lineage **Kapila → Āsuri → Pañcaśikha** that the *Kārikā* claims to transmit. (*SK* 69–71.) Note the **two-clock gap**: the founder is mythic-primordial; the surviving classical text is Gupta-era. [scholarly]
- **Avatāra of Viṣṇu.** Multiple Purāṇas — **Bhāgavata, Brahmāṇḍa, Viṣṇu, Padma, Skanda, Nārada** — and the *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* identify **Kapila as an incarnation of Viṣṇu**. (See the avatāra rosters across the [Purāṇa corpus](../puranas/README.md).)
- **Son of Kardama and Devahūti (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.24–33).** Kapila is born to the **Prajāpati Kardama** and **Devahūti** to impart self-knowledge to his mother. The **Kapila–Devahūti dialogue** (the *Kapilopadeśa*, BhP 3.25–33) is the **theistic / bhakti-inflected Sāṃkhya** of the Purāṇas — pointedly *seśvara* and devotional, in contrast to the *Kārikā*'s nirīśvara classical system. [doctrinal contrast]
  - *Variant (which Kapila):* tradition multiplies Kapilas — the Sāṃkhya founder, the Viṣṇu-avatāra of the Bhāgavata, and the wrathful sage whose glance **incinerates the 60,000 sons of Sagara** in the Gaṅgā-descent story (*Rāmāyaṇa*, *Bhāgavata* 9.8) are variously identified or distinguished. Catalog all; the identification is **tradition-dependent**. [disputed identity]

## Why Sāṃkhya cosmology underlies the Puranic *sarga*

- **The *sarga* template.** Purāṇic **primary creation (*sarga*)** narratives — the unfolding from unmanifest *avyakta/pradhāna* through **mahat → ahaṃkāra → tanmātras → mahābhūtas** — are **structurally Sāṃkhya**. The evolute-sequence above is the skeleton of the cosmogony in the *Viṣṇu*, *Mārkaṇḍeya*, *Kūrma*, *Liṅga*, and especially the *Bhāgavata* Purāṇas. See [Bhāgavata Purāṇa skandha map](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md), [Viṣṇu Purāṇa](../puranas/vishnu-purana.md), [Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa](../puranas/markandeya-purana.md), [Kūrma Purāṇa](../puranas/kurma-purana.md), [Liṅga Purāṇa](../puranas/linga-purana.md).
- **Theistic retrofit.** The Purāṇas **borrow the 24 tattvas of evolution wholesale** but **re-theize** the scheme - Prakṛti is subordinated to a **supreme deity** (Viṣṇu / Brahman / Devī) who **agitates the guṇas** and presides over the unfolding, converting nirīśvara Sāṃkhya into a **theistic creation account**. Thus Sāṃkhya supplies the *mechanism* of manifestation while the Purāṇas supply the *agent*. [doctrinal synthesis]
- **Devī as Prakṛti.** Śākta texts (e.g. the *Devī-Bhāgavata*, *Devī-Māhātmya*) identify the **Goddess with Mūla-prakṛti** itself — the Sāṃkhya unmanifest personified as supreme Śakti. See [Devī-Bhāgavata Upapurāṇa](../puranas/devi-bhagavata-upapurana.md).

## Major commentaries (the *Kārikā* commentarial tradition)

- **Gaudapāda-bhāṣya** — earliest extant Sanskrit commentary; covers *SK* 1–69. (Author often distinguished from the Advaita *Māṇḍūkya-kārikā* Gauḍapāda. [disputed identity])
- **Paramārtha's Chinese version (c. 557–569 CE)** — translation **with commentary**; the **oldest datable witness** to the text, anchoring its *terminus ante quem*. [scholarly]
- **Yuktidīpikā** — anonymous; the **richest and most philosophically detailed** commentary, preserving lost Sāṃkhya debate; analyzes 66 of 72 kārikās. Rediscovered in manuscript in the mid-20th c. [scholarly]
- **Tattvakaumudī of Vācaspati Miśra (~9th–10th c. CE)** — the **standard, most-studied** commentary; supplies the classic anti-theistic argument (above) and fixes much of the school's later interpretation.
- **Māṭhara-vṛtti** and **Jayamaṅgalā** — earlier strata (likely pre-9th c.), closely paralleling the Chinese commentary; important for textual history. [scholarly]
- *Variant (relation to later Sāṃkhya):* the late **Sāṃkhya-sūtra** (c. 14th–15th c.) with **Vijñānabhikṣu's *Sāṃkhya-pravacana-bhāṣya*** revives Sāṃkhya in a **more theistic, Vedānta-leaning** key — a *later* layer distinct from the classical *Kārikā* system. [late text]
