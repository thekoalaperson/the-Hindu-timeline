# Vaiśeṣika Sūtras of Kaṇāda — Per-Text Profile

> Part of the **[Literature Corpus](../README.md)** · Master index: **[The Hindu Timeline](../../README.md)** · Parent survey: **[Upavedas, Vedāngas & the Six Darśanas](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md)**. This node is the **dedicated deep profile** of the *Vaiśeṣika Sūtra* attributed to the sage **Kaṇāda (Ulūka / Kāśyapa)** — the root-text of the **Vaiśeṣika darśana**, India's classical **atomist + categorial (padārtha) realism**. Sister-school of [Nyāya](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md); later fused into the combined **Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika**. Compare the dualist evolutionism of the [Sāṃkhya-kārikā](./samkhya-karika.md) and the exegesis of the [Brahma-Sūtras](./brahma-sutras.md).

---

## Two clocks — dating

- **Scriptural / traditional clock.** The system is held to be revealed knowledge taught by the ṛṣi **Kaṇāda**, also named **Ulūka** ("owl") and patronymically **Kāśyapa**; in the orthodox reckoning he is a primordial sage of the present [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/), and the sūtra is timeless darśana ("vision"), not an authored novelty. The nickname *Kaṇāda* ("atom-eater / grain-eater") is itself doctrinally legendary — one who "feeds on the smallest particle." By this clock the atomic doctrine is ancient revelation, not discovery.
- **Academic clock.** Composition is placed broadly **c. 6th–2nd century BCE** (late Vedic to early classical), with the received text crystallizing **before the Common Era**. Some scholars push the core older on the ground that the sūtra **makes no mention of Buddhism** (suggesting a pre-/proto-Buddhist horizon for its earliest layer); the text as transmitted is composite, with later accretions. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Two stages of the school.** A practical "second clock" runs *inside* the tradition: (1) the **sūtra stratum** (Kaṇāda, terse aphorisms); (2) the **bhāṣya/saṃgraha stratum** — above all **Praśastapāda (c. 4th–6th c. CE)**, whose *Padārtha-dharma-saṃgraha* effectively re-founds and systematizes the school. Most "classical Vaiśeṣika" doctrine known today is read **through Praśastapāda**, not directly off the sūtras. [scholarly]

## Form & structure

- **Ten adhyāyas (books)**, each divided into **two āhnikas** (lessons / "day's-readings") — twenty sections total; aphoristic *sūtra* style throughout.
- **Opening sūtra (VS 1.1.1–2)** - frames the whole work under **dharma**: "*athāto dharmaṃ vyākhyāsyāmaḥ*" ("Now, therefore, an explanation of dharma") — dharma being "that from which results *abhyudaya* (prosperity / worldly good) and *niḥśreyasa* (the highest good / liberation)." Striking for an "atomist" text: the metaphysics of substances and atoms is presented **in service of dharma and mokṣa**, not as bare natural science.
- **Authority of the Veda** - the validity of dharma is grounded in Vedic authority (VS 1.1.3; cf. 10.2.9), tying the system to the *āstika* (orthodox) fold even as it argues largely from reason and perception.

## The padārthas (categories of the real)

Vaiśeṣika is built on an exhaustive **inventory of what there is** — *padārthas*, "namable / knowable categories." Kaṇāda's root system has **six**; later masters add a seventh.

- **The six original padārthas (VS 1.1.4).** **dravya** (substance), **guṇa** (quality), **karma** (action/motion), **sāmānya** (universal/generality), **viśeṣa** (particular/ultimate-differentiator), **samavāya** (inherence). Liberation comes from true knowledge (*tattva-jñāna*) of these. (*Vaiśeṣika Sūtra* 1.1.4.)
  - **First three are *artha* (real, perceptible objects)** that have independent objective existence and can bear causal relations; **last three are *buddhy-apekṣam*** — relational/logical categories grasped by the discriminating intellect. [scholarly]
- **Dravya — substance.** That which possesses qualities and actions and is the **inherence-locus (material cause)** of effects. **Nine** in number (see below). (*VS* 1.1.5, 1.1.15.)
- **Guṇa — quality.** Resides *in* substance, itself qualityless and actionless. Kaṇāda lists **17** qualities (VS 1.1.6: color, taste, smell, touch, number, dimension, separateness, conjunction, disjunction, priority, posteriority, knowledge, pleasure, pain, desire, aversion, effort); **Praśastapāda raises the canonical count to 24** by adding gravity (*gurutva*), fluidity (*dravatva*), viscidity (*sneha*), faculty/disposition (*saṃskāra*), merit (*dharma*), demerit (*adharma*), and sound (*śabda*).
  - *Variant (count of guṇas):* **17 (Kaṇāda) → 24 (Praśastapāda)**; the "24 qualities" became the school standard. [scholarly]
- **Karma — action / motion.** Five kinds (*VS* 1.1.7): throwing-up (*utkṣepaṇa*), throwing-down (*apakṣepaṇa*), contraction (*ākuñcana*), expansion (*prasāraṇa*), and going/motion (*gamana*). Resides in substance; momentary; cause of conjunction and disjunction.
- **Sāmānya — universal / generality.** The common property by which a class is one (e.g., "cow-ness," "substance-ness"); grounds inductive recognition across many particulars. Higher (*para*, e.g. **being / sattā**, the highest universal) vs. lower (*apara*) generality. (*VS* 1.2.)
- **Viśeṣa — ultimate particular.** The **school's namesake** (*viśeṣa* → *vaiśeṣika*). The final individuating principle that distinguishes one **eternal atom (or soul/manas)** from another otherwise-identical one; without it two paramāṇus of earth would be indiscernible. (*VS* 1.2.)
- **Samavāya — inherence.** The **single, eternal, all-pervading relation** binding inseparables: whole-and-parts, substance-and-quality, substance-and-action, universal-and-instance, particular-and-eternal-substance. Distinguished from **saṃyoga** (conjunction), which is a temporary, separable contact. (*VS* 7.2.)

### The seventh: abhāva (absence / non-existence)

- **Abhāva — absence**, added by **later Vaiśeṣikas** (notably **Śrīdhara**, **Udayana**, **Śivāditya**) to make **seven** padārthas. Kinds: prior absence (*prāg-abhāva*), posterior/destructional absence (*dhvaṃsa*), absolute absence (*atyanta-abhāva*), and mutual absence/difference (*anyonya-abhāva*). [late text]
  - *Variant (number of categories):* **6 (Kaṇāda's sūtras)** vs. **7 (later Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika, with abhāva)**. Some sources read abhāva as already implicit in the sūtra's treatment of non-existence (VS 9.1). [disputed]

## The nine dravyas (substances)

The fundamental furniture of the cosmos — **nava-dravya** (*VS* 1.1.5):

- **Pṛthivī (earth)** - atomic; bearer of smell (its distinctive quality); eternal as atoms, non-eternal as compounds.
- **Ap / jala (water)** - atomic; distinctive quality cool touch + taste; fluid.
- **Tejas / agni (fire)** - atomic; distinctive quality color + hot touch; source of light and heat.
- **Vāyu (air)** - atomic; distinctive quality touch (no color); the only one of the four "elemental" atomic substances imperceptible to sight yet inferable.
- **Ākāśa (ether / space-as-medium)** - **one, eternal, infinite, non-atomic**; the unique substrate of **sound (śabda)**, which is its inferential mark.
- **Kāla (time)** - **one, eternal, all-pervading**; inferred as the cause of the notions "prior/posterior, simultaneous/successive, fast/slow"; the universal occasioning-cause of all production and change.
- **Dik (space / direction)** - **one, eternal, all-pervading**; inferred from directional cognitions ("east of," "ten yojanas from here").
- **Ātman (self / soul)** - eternal, all-pervading, **plural** (one per living being, plus *Paramātman / Īśvara* in theistic later forms); substrate of consciousness, desire, effort, merit/demerit; known by inner perception (*mānasa-pratyakṣa*) and inference.
- **Manas (mind)** - **atomic (aṇu) and eternal**, the internal organ; **one per soul**; its atomic size and singularity explain why cognitions arise **serially**, never two at once. Mediates between ātman and the senses.

- **Grouping note.** The first **four (earth, water, fire, air)** are **atomic and have specific qualities**; **manas** is also atomic; **ākāśa, kāla, dik, ātman** are **vibhu** (all-pervading, partless, eternal). Only the four elemental atomics + manas come in atoms; the all-pervading four do not aggregate.

## Paramāṇu (atom) theory and aggregation

The signature doctrine: **eternal, indivisible, point-like atoms** as the imperishable substratum of the material world.

- **Paramāṇu — the ultimate atom.** Defined by **reductive argument**: divisibility cannot regress infinitely (else a mustard-seed and a mountain would contain equal infinite parts); therefore matter terminates in a **partless, dimensionless, eternal, indestructible** unit — the *paramāṇu*. (*VS* 4.1; 7.1.) Four kinds, one per elemental substance (earth-, water-, fire-, air-atoms), differing by their inherent specific qualities and individuated by *viśeṣa*.
- **Atoms are imperceptible.** The single atom and the **dyad** are sub-perceptual; only from the **triad upward** does matter become visible — making the *tryaṇuka* the smallest perceptible quantum. [scholarly]
- **Dvyaṇuka (dyad).** Two paramāṇus conjoin (via *saṃyoga*) to form a **dyad** — the first compound; minute, still imperceptible.
- **Tryaṇuka / trasareṇu / truṭi (triad).** **Three dyads** combine into a triad (also called *trasareṇu*, the "mote in a sunbeam," or *truṭi*) — the **smallest perceptible mote**, the threshold of visibility; macroscopic bodies build up from these.
  - *Variant (aggregation rule):* mainstream classical Vaiśeṣika holds **2 atoms → 1 dyad, 3 dyads → 1 triad**; some expositions describe the triad as **3 atoms**. The "3 dyads" reading (Praśastapāda's school) is standard. [disputed]
- **Why atoms move — adṛṣṭa.** Atoms are inert in themselves; their initial motion (*parispanda*) at the start of a creation-cycle, and their combination into the right bodies, is driven by **adṛṣṭa** ("the unseen") — the **invisible moral force / accumulated karmic residue (dharma–adharma) of souls** awaiting fruition. Adṛṣṭa is the lawful, non-arbitrary principle steering atomic aggregation toward a world fit for souls' experience. (*VS* 5.2.) This supplies a **moral-cosmological motor** in place of (or, in theistic forms, alongside) a creator.
- **Cosmic cycle.** At dissolution (*pralaya*) compounds break down to dyads and finally to **uncombined eternal atoms**; at the next creation, adṛṣṭa (later: + Īśvara's will) restarts conjunction. Atoms persist across cycles — matter is conserved, only its configurations perish.

## Epistemology & non-theism (sūtra stratum)

- **Two pramāṇas only.** Vaiśeṣika admits just **pratyakṣa (perception)** and **anumāna (inference)** as valid means of knowledge — narrower than [Nyāya](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md), which adds *upamāna* (comparison) and *śabda* (testimony). Vaiśeṣika subsumes verbal testimony under inference. [scholarly]
- **Complementarity with Nyāya.** Vaiśeṣika supplies the **ontology** (categories, substances, atoms); Nyāya supplies the **logic/epistemology** (16 categories of debate, theory of inference). The pair are often glossed as **inductive (Vaiśeṣika) + deductive (Nyāya)** halves of one realist program.
- **Original non-theism.** Kaṇāda's sūtra **does not name God (Īśvara)**; the early system is naturalistic, running on **atoms + adṛṣṭa**. Vedic authority is invoked for dharma, but cosmogony needs no creator-deity. [scholarly]
- **Liberation (mokṣa / niḥśreyasa).** Attained through **complete, true knowledge of the padārthas** — discerning the self as distinct from body, senses, and mind; cessation of merit-demerit ends rebirth, and the liberated ātman abides without consciousness or qualities (a "stone-like" release, in later critics' phrasing). (*VS* 1.1.4; 5.2; 10.)

## Praśastapāda and the classical re-founding

- **Praśastapāda — *Padārtha-dharma-saṃgraha* (c. 4th–6th c. CE).** Though styled a *bhāṣya* (commentary) on the *Vaiśeṣika Sūtra*, it is "**basically an independent work**" — a systematic re-statement that organizes the categories under "**properties of the padārthas**," fixes the **24 guṇas**, elaborates atomic combination (dyad/triad), and standardizes the cosmology. It, not the terse sūtra, is the **operative classical text** of the school; nearly all later commentary is on *it*. [scholarly]
- **Introduction of Īśvara.** Praśastapāda's stratum brings in a **creator-god (Īśvara)** who, at the start of each world-cycle, directs adṛṣṭa-laden atoms into combination — converting the originally non-theistic system into a **theistic atomism**. (Contrast Kaṇāda's silence on God.) [late text]
- **Major commentary lineage (on Praśastapāda):**
  - **Vyomaśiva — *Vyomavatī*** (8th c.), earliest surviving commentary on Praśastapāda.
  - **Śrīdhara — *Nyāya-kandalī*** (991 CE), a key authority; added/championed **abhāva**.
  - **Udayana — *Kiraṇāvalī*** (10th c.), who also wrote landmark proofs of Īśvara (e.g. *Nyāya-kusumāñjali*).
  - **Śrīvatsa — *Līlāvatī*** (11th c.).
  - **Śivāditya — *Saptapadārthī***, expressly presenting **Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika as one whole** (note the title: "the seven padārthas").
- **Commentary on the Sūtra itself.** **Śaṃkara Miśra's *Upaskāra*** (c. 15th c.) is the principal surviving running commentary directly on Kaṇāda's text; earlier sūtra-commentaries (e.g. an attributed *Rāvaṇa-bhāṣya*, *Bhāradvāja-vṛtti*) are lost or fragmentary. [late text]

## Merger into Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika

- **From sister-schools to one school.** Vaiśeṣika and **Nyāya** arose independently but "**became similar and are often studied together**," fusing by the early-medieval period into the combined **Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika** tradition, with Vaiśeṣika ontology slotted into the Nyāya logical-epistemological frame.
- **Syncretic manuals.** Texts like Śivāditya's *Saptapadārthī*, and later **Navya-Nyāya** ("New Logic," from **Gaṅgeśa**, 13th–14th c. CE, *Tattva-cintāmaṇi*) and primers such as the *Tarka-saṃgraha* (Annaṃbhaṭṭa, 17th c.), teach the **two as a single system** — Nyāya's pramāṇa-theory + Vaiśeṣika's 7 padārthas / 9 dravyas / 24 guṇas.
- **Outcome.** Vaiśeṣika **survives mainly as the metaphysics-half of Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika**; few worked purely in "Vaiśeṣika" after Udayana. Its atomism and category-realism remained the standard Hindu **physics/ontology** down to the colonial encounter. [scholarly]

## Significance & cross-links

- **India's atomism.** The **paramāṇu / dvyaṇuka / tryaṇuka** scheme is the most developed **pre-modern Indian atomic theory**, frequently (and anachronistically) compared with Greek and modern atomism — though Vaiśeṣika atoms are **qualitatively distinct (4 kinds, with smell/taste/color/touch)** and morally driven by **adṛṣṭa**, unlike inert Democritean atoms. [scholarly]
- **Relation to other darśanas.** Realist-pluralist counterpart to dualist **[Sāṃkhya](./samkhya-karika.md)** (which derives the world from one *prakṛti*, not many eternal atoms) and to the non-dual reading of the **[Brahma-Sūtras](./brahma-sutras.md)** (which rejects atomism, esp. *Brahma-Sūtra* 2.2.11–17, the *paramāṇu-jagad-akāraṇatva* refutation). See the school survey in **[Upavedas, Vedāngas & the Six Darśanas](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md)**.
- **Open rosters flagged.** The exact pre-Praśastapāda sūtra-commentary list (Rāvaṇa-bhāṣya etc.) is **lost / uncertain**; the earliest dating of the sūtra layer is **disputed**; and the count of categories (6 vs. 7) and guṇas (17 vs. 24) is **stratum-dependent**.
