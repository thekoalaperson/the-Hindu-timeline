# Brahma Sutras (Vedanta Sutras) — Per-Text Profile

> Part of the **[Literature Corpus](../README.md)** → the six **[Darśanas](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md)**. Dedicated profile of **Bādarāyaṇa's Brahma Sūtras**, the root text (*mūla*) of the **Vedānta (Uttara Mīmāṃsā)** darśana — the third pillar of the **prasthāna-trayī**, the aphoristic distillation of the [Upanishads](../principal-upanishads-detail.md), and the single text that *every* Vedānta school commented on to ground its theology. See the founder-school readers under [Acharyas & Sampradayas](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md): [Adi Shankara](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/adi-shankara.md), [Ramanuja & Madhva](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/ramanuja-and-madhva.md), [Nimbarka, Vallabha, Chaitanya](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md).
>
> **Two clocks:** by tradition Bādarāyaṇa = **Vyāsa**, composing in [Dvāpara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)/early [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) (after [Kali begins 3102 BCE](../../00-time-and-cosmology/)); academically the surviving form is c. 200 BCE – 450 CE.

---

## 1. What the text is

- **Name & genre** — *Brahma-sūtra* ("aphorisms on Brahman"), also called **Vedānta-sūtra** (the sūtras concluding/completing the Veda), **Śārīraka-sūtra** ("on the embodied self"), **Uttara-mīmāṃsā-sūtra** ("later/higher inquiry," vs. Jaimini's *Pūrva*-mīmāṃsā on ritual), and **Bhikṣu-sūtra** in some lists. A **sūtra** is a maximally terse aphorism — by the classical ideal "fewer syllables, unambiguous, pithy, comprehensive" — so the text is almost unreadable without a *bhāṣya* (commentary). (Wikipedia "Brahma Sutras"; *upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md*)
- **Function** — systematizes and *harmonizes* the scattered, sometimes apparently contradictory teachings of the [Upanishads](../principal-upanishads-detail.md) into one coherent doctrine of **Brahman** (ultimate reality), **Ātman** (self), their relation, and **mokṣa** (liberation). It is the "string" (sūtra) threading the Upanishadic gems. (Wikipedia; Grokipedia "Brahma Sutras")
- **The prasthāna-trayī ("three points of departure")** — the canonical triple foundation that *defines* a Vedānta ācārya: (1) **Upaniṣads** = *śruti-prasthāna* (revealed), (2) **Bhagavad Gītā** = *smṛti-prasthāna* (remembered), (3) **Brahma Sūtras** = *nyāya-prasthāna* (the reasoned/logical pillar). To found a school you must write a *bhāṣya* on all three. The Brahma Sūtras are the **reasoning** member — they argue the Upanishads into a system. (Wikipedia; *upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md*)

## 2. Architecture — 4 / 16 / 223 / 555

- **The standard count** — **4 adhyāyas** (chapters) → each of **4 pādas** (quarters/sections) = **16 pādas** → subdivided into **223 adhikaraṇas** (topical "case studies") → totalling **555 sūtras**. (Wikipedia "Brahma Sutras"; Divine Life Society edition)
  - *Variant (sūtra count by recension):* the **555** total reflects Shankara's division; because commentators split or merge sūtras differently, counts vary — figures of **545, 549, 555, 564** appear across editions, and the **adhikaraṇa count** ranges c. **191–223** depending on the commentator. Catalog: treat 4/16 as fixed, but **sūtra and adhikaraṇa totals as commentator-dependent.** [disputed count]
- **Adhyāya I — Samanvaya ("harmony / reconciliation")** — establishes that *all* Upanishadic passages, even those seemingly about something else, **converge on Brahman** as their unified purport. Brahman is shown to be both the *material* and *efficient* cause of the world; resolves which words/passages refer to Brahman. The chapter that "reads everything as being about Brahman." (Divine Life Society; Wikipedia)
- **Adhyāya II — Avirodha ("non-contradiction")** — defends the Vedānta doctrine against objections: (a) **smṛti & reasoning** (Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Vaiśeṣika, atomism), (b) rival philosophies (**Buddhist, Jaina, Pāśupata, Pāñcarātra**), then (c) treats internal consistency — the elements (*pañcabhūta*), the soul (*jīva*), and its faculties as emanating from Brahman without contradiction. The polemical/defensive chapter. (Divine Life Society; Wikipedia)
- **Adhyāya III — Sādhana ("the means")** — the path to Brahman-knowledge: transmigration (*saṃsāra*) and the soul's journey, the nature of Brahman as known via the *vidyās* (meditative knowledges of the Upanishads), the requirement of renunciation, and the means (*sādhana*) of liberating knowledge. The longest, most practice-oriented chapter. (Divine Life Society; Wikipedia)
- **Adhyāya IV — Phala ("the fruit")** — the *result* of realization: the dissolution of karma, the soul's departure at death, the **path of the gods (devayāna)**, attainment of Brahman, and the nature of the liberated state (whether *krama-mukti* — gradual liberation via Brahmaloka — or immediate). Closes the text on **mokṣa**. (Divine Life Society; Wikipedia)
- **Mnemonic** — the four chapters answer four questions in sequence: *Is it really all about Brahman?* (Samanvaya) → *Does the doctrine survive objections?* (Avirodha) → *How do I reach it?* (Sādhana) → *What do I get?* (Phala).

## 3. The famous opening sūtras

- **1.1.1 — *athāto brahma-jijñāsā*** ("now, therefore, the inquiry into Brahman"). The single most-quoted sūtra. Every word is contested in the *bhāṣyas*: **atha** ("now") — *after what* prerequisite? Shankara: after acquiring the *sādhana-catuṣṭaya* (discrimination, dispassion, the six virtues, longing for liberation); the Mīmāṃsakas read it as *after* the study of *karma-kāṇḍa* (ritual). **ataḥ** ("therefore") = because the *fruit* of ritual is perishable, only Brahman-knowledge is sought. **jijñāsā** = *desire to know* Brahman. (pkrishnan.net; Wikipedia; Vaniquotes)
- **1.1.2 — *janmādy asya yataḥ*** ("[Brahman is] that from which the origin, etc. [sustenance and dissolution] of this [world proceed]"). The famous **definition (lakṣaṇa) of Brahman** as the source/cause of cosmic creation, maintenance, and dissolution — directly echoing *Taittirīya Upaniṣad* 3.1 ("that from which these beings are born..."). Establishes Brahman as the *jagat-kāraṇa* (world-cause). (Vaniquotes; Wikipedia; *principal-upanishads-detail.md*)
- **1.1.3 — *śāstra-yonitvāt*** ("because [Brahman is knowable only] through scripture / because scripture is its source") — Brahman is established by **śruti**, not bare inference. *Variant reading:* "Brahman is the source (yoni) of scripture itself."
- **1.1.4 — *tat tu samanvayāt*** ("but that [Brahman] is [the purport], because of harmony/convergence") — names the **Samanvaya** principle that titles the chapter: all Vedānta passages harmoniously denote Brahman. The pivot sūtra of Adhyāya I. (Wikipedia)
- **Closing — 4.4.22 — *anāvṛttiḥ śabdāt, anāvṛttiḥ śabdāt*** ("there is no return [for the liberated soul], because scripture says so" — repeated to mark the *end of the text*). Doubling a phrase is the sūtra-convention for "the work is finished."

## 4. The adhikaraṇa method (the hermeneutic engine)

- **What an adhikaraṇa is** — a "topical unit" / **case study** (Francis Clooney's term): a self-contained argumentative cell built to settle the meaning of one disputed Upanishadic passage. The 223 adhikaraṇas are the *real* structural unit; the 4/16 chapter scheme is the container. (Wikipedia; Clooney)
- **The five-limbed (pañcāvayava) anatomy of an adhikaraṇa** (inherited from Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā exegesis): **(1) viṣaya** — the topic / the Upanishadic text quoted; **(2) saṃśaya** — the doubt (what could it mean?); **(3) pūrvapakṣa** — the *prima facie* / opponent's view, stated fairly and at full strength; **(4) siddhānta** (a.k.a. *uttarapakṣa*) — the established/refuting conclusion; **(5) saṅgati** — the *connection*, showing how this adhikaraṇa coheres with the chapter's flow.
- **Pūrvapakṣa vs. siddhānta** — the dialectical heart: the author first voices the **misconception/rival** (pūrvapakṣa) — often Sāṃkhya, Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika atomism, Mīmāṃsā ritualism, or Buddhist idealism — then **refutes** it and lands the **siddhānta** (Vedānta conclusion). Shankara's *bhāṣya* runs a tripartite *pūrvapakṣa → khaṇḍana (rebuttal) → siddhānta*. The method *forces* steel-manning of opponents before refutation. (atmashrama.org; Grokipedia "Purva paksha"; Wikipedia)
- **Why the same text yields opposite schools** — the sūtras are so compressed that the **assignment** of a sūtra to pūrvapakṣa vs. siddhānta is itself a commentarial choice. Famous fault-line: **2.3.19–28** (is the *jīva* atomic (*aṇu*) or all-pervading (*vibhu*)?) — **Shankara reads these as pūrvapakṣa** (the soul only *seems* atomic; truly it is Brahman = all-pervading); **Ramanuja reads the same sūtras as siddhānta** (the soul really *is* atomic). One word's parsing flips the entire metaphysics. (Wikipedia; swami-krishnananda.org)
  - *Variant (chaining):* some commentators read the **last word of a sūtra as the first word of the next**, re-segmenting the stream — yielding different sūtra boundaries and counts (see §2). [textual variant]

## 5. One text, many schools — divergent bhāṣyas

The defining feature: **every Vedānta founder commented on the identical 555 sūtras and reached an incompatible theology.** The Brahma Sūtras are the shared battleground of Hindu metaphysics. (Wikipedia "Brahma Sutras"; *ramanuja-and-madhva.md*; *nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md*)

- **Adi Śaṅkara (c. 8th c. CE) — *Brahma-sūtra-bhāṣya*** → **Advaita** (non-dualism). Brahman alone is real (*sat*); the world and individual selves are appearance (*vyāvahārika*) due to **māyā/avidyā**; mokṣa = *recognition of identity*, "I am Brahman" (*ahaṃ brahmāsmi*). The oldest *surviving* complete bhāṣya and the benchmark all others answer. See [Adi Shankara](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/adi-shankara.md). [reads jīva-Brahman *identity*]
- **Rāmānuja (11th–12th c. CE) — *Śrī Bhāṣya*** → **Viśiṣṭādvaita** (qualified non-dualism). Brahman is **one but internally differentiated**; selves (*cit*) and matter (*acit*) are *real modes/body (śarīra)* of Brahman (= Viṣṇu/Nārāyaṇa); mokṣa = eternal loving participation, *not* identity. Explicitly written to **overturn Shankara's māyā-reading** of the same sūtras. See [Ramanuja & Madhva](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/ramanuja-and-madhva.md). [theistic; selves real]
- **Madhva / Ānandatīrtha (13th c. CE) — *Brahma-sūtra-bhāṣya* + *Aṇu-bhāṣya*** → **Dvaita** (dualism). Brahman (Viṣṇu) and *jīvas* are **eternally and absolutely distinct** (*pañca-bheda* — five real differences); mokṣa = *nearness* to, never merger with, the Lord; introduces a gradation of souls. Reads many sūtras Shankara takes as identity-statements as *difference*-statements. See [Ramanuja & Madhva](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/ramanuja-and-madhva.md). [hard dualism]
- **Nimbārka (13th c. CE, trad. earlier) — *Vedānta-pārijāta-saurabha*** → **Dvaitādvaita / Bhedābheda** (dual-non-dual). Brahman (Kṛṣṇa-Rādhā) and souls/matter are **simultaneously different and non-different** — like waves and sea. See [Nimbarka, Vallabha, Chaitanya](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md). [difference-and-identity]
- **Vallabha (15th–16th c. CE) — *Aṇu-bhāṣya* (a.k.a. *Brahma-sūtra-aṇubhāṣya*)** → **Śuddhādvaita** (pure non-dualism). Brahman (Kṛṣṇa) is real and the world is its **real, non-illusory** transformation — rejects Shankara's *māyā* as illusion; the world is *līlā*. See [Nimbarka, Vallabha, Chaitanya](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md). [no illusion-māyā]
- **Bhāskara (8th–9th c. CE) — bhāṣya** → **Bhedābheda** ("upādhi"-conditioned difference-and-identity); an early rival to Shankara, often cited as a target of Ramanuja's via media. [early bhedābheda]
- **Śrīkaṇṭha (Śaiva, c. 14th c.) — *Śaiva-bhāṣya*** → **Śiva-Viśiṣṭādvaita**: reads Brahman as **Śiva** rather than Viṣṇu, paralleling Ramanuja's structure. (Wikipedia notes "Shiva Advaita" sub-school.) See [Shaiva schools](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/shaiva-schools.md). [Śaiva reading]
- **Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa (18th c.) — *Govinda-bhāṣya*** → supplies the **Gauḍīya / Chaitanya** school its required Brahma-sūtra commentary (**Acintya-bhedābheda** — inconceivable difference-and-non-difference). Written so the Gauḍīya sampradāya could claim a legitimate Vedānta seat. See [Nimbarka, Vallabha, Chaitanya](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md). [late; Gauḍīya]
  - *Concrete divergence example (2.3.15, the word **antarā**):* Shankara, Ramanuja, and Nimbarka read it "**in the midst**"; **Madhva reads it "without/excluding"** — opposite senses from one word. (Wikipedia)

## 6. Author — Bādarāyaṇa and the Vyāsa identification

- **Bādarāyaṇa** — the attributed author, named in the *third person inside the sūtras themselves* (e.g. citing the views of "Bādarāyaṇa" alongside other teachers), which suggests a **school/redactor tradition** rather than a single hand. Other sages quoted within the text — **Jaimini, Auḍulomi, Āśmarathya, Kāśakṛtsna, Bādari, Kārṣṇājini, Ātreya** — show the sūtras crystallize *prior* Vedānta debate. (Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com "Bādarāyaṇa"; Grokipedia)
- **Bādarāyaṇa = Vyāsa** — later tradition identifies Bādarāyaṇa with **Vyāsa (Veda-Vyāsa, Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana)** — the legendary arranger of the [Vedas](../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md), compiler of the [Mahābhārata](../smriti-itihasa-epics.md), and a [Manvantara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)-spanning *cira-jīvin*. This makes the *same* sage the author of the **Gītā** (in the Mahābhārata) and the **Sūtras** — i.e. two of the three prasthāna-trayī pillars. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (academic view):* the Vyāsa equation is a **post-compositional, devotional development**, not the original attribution; scholars generally keep "Bādarāyaṇa" (possibly a Bādari-clan lineage) distinct from the mythic Vyāsa. [disputed identity]
- **Bādarāyaṇa & Jaimini** — traditionally **Jaimini** (author of the *Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā Sūtras*) is Bādarāyaṇa's **pupil**; the two texts cross-cite each other, Bādarāyaṇa privileging **jñāna** (knowledge/Brahman) and Jaimini **karma** (ritual). The Brahma Sūtras are the *Uttara* (later) Mīmāṃsā answering the *Pūrva* (prior) Mīmāṃsā — see the [Mīmāṃsā/Vedānta pair](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md). (Wikipedia; Grokipedia)

## 7. Dating (the academic clock)

- **Core vs. final form** — the *core* material may reach back to **c. 500–200 BCE** (Lochtefeld: 500–200 BCE; Radhakrishnan & Dasgupta: ~2nd c. BCE most likely); the text in its **surviving final form** was likely complete **c. 400–450 CE** (Hajime Nakamura). So: *old kernel, late redaction.* (Wikipedia; Grokipedia)
  - *Variant (range):* individual scholars place initial composition anywhere from **c. 500 BCE to 200 CE**, with up to **c. 400 CE** for final shaping; no consensus single date. Catalog as a **range, not a point.** [disputed dating]
- **Relative chronology** — *after* the principal [Upaniṣads](../principal-upanishads-detail.md) (which it harmonizes) and roughly contemporary with / responding to the early **Mīmāṃsā Sūtras**, **Sāṃkhya**, early **Buddhist** schools, and the **Bhagavad Gītā** (which it presupposes as smṛti). Internal references to Buddhist positions in Adhyāya II are a key dating anchor. [scholarly]
- **Traditional clock** — by tradition, authored by **Vyāsa** at the **[Dvāpara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)–[Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) junction** (the same era as the Mahābhārata war and the onset of [Kali Yuga, 3102 BCE](../../00-time-and-cosmology/)), in the current **28th Mahāyuga** of the [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md). [traditional]

## 8. Significance & open rosters

- **Why it matters** — the Brahma Sūtras are the *constitutional document* of Vedānta: no figure counts as a Vedānta ācārya without a Brahma-sūtra-bhāṣya. The history of the school *is* the history of its commentaries. (Wikipedia; *upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md*)
- **Open-ended roster — commentaries:** the list of *bhāṣyas* and sub-commentaries (vārttikas, ṭīkās) is **large and not exhaustively cataloged here** — beyond the founders above there are Vijñānabhikṣu (*Vijñānāmṛta-bhāṣya*, a bhedābheda-Sāṃkhya synthesis), Śrīpati (Vīraśaiva), the Advaita sub-commentaries on Shankara (Vācaspati Miśra's *Bhāmatī*, Padmapāda's *Pañcapādikā*), and many more. [open-ended roster]
- **Open-ended roster — sub-sages cited within:** the internal teachers (Āśmarathya, Auḍulomi, Kāśakṛtsna, Bādari, Kārṣṇājini, Jaimini, Ātreya) represent **lost pre-Bādarāyaṇa Vedānta schools** whose full doctrines do not survive. [open-ended roster]
