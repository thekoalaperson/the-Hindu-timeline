# Vedānta Prakaraṇa Granthas — The Advaita Teaching Manuals

> Part of the **[Literature Corpus](../README.md)** → the **[Darśana root-texts](./README.md)**. Where the **[Brahma Sūtras](./brahma-sutras.md)** and their *bhāṣyas* argue Vedānta into a system, the **prakaraṇa granthas** ("topical manuals") *teach* it — free-standing primers from [Śaṅkara's](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/adi-shankara.md) *Upadeśa Sāhasrī* to Vidyāraṇya's *Pañcadaśī* that walk a student from qualification to realization without presupposing the sūtra apparatus.
>
> **Two clocks:** all of these are **Kali Yuga** compositions (Kali began 3102 BCE) of the [documented-history era](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md); academically they span **c. 8th–16th c. CE** [scholarly], though the traditional maṭha chronologies push Śaṅkara — and hence every text bearing his name — back to **509–477 BCE** [disputed].

---

## 1. What a prakaraṇa grantha is

- **Definition** — a *prakaraṇa* is an independent didactic treatise that takes up **one part of a śāstra's subject-matter** and serves the śāstra's aim, as the traditional definition quoted by Vedānta commentators puts it: a work "connected with one topic of a śāstra and serving its purpose." It is the third genre of Vedānta writing after **śruti/mūla texts** ([Upaniṣads](../principal-upanishads-detail.md), [Brahma Sūtras](./brahma-sutras.md), Gītā) and **bhāṣya** (commentary). (Traditional definition cited in *Vedāntasāra* commentaries)
- **Function — the teaching ladder** — the manuals presuppose no prior study: they define the qualified student (*adhikārī*), lay out the **sādhana-catuṣṭaya** (fourfold qualification: *viveka*, *vairāgya*, the six virtues, *mumukṣutva*), then run the standard analyses — [five kośas](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/pancha-koshas.md), three bodies, three states, [māyā/avidyā](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/maya-and-avidya.md), the [mahāvākyas](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md) — and end at *jīvanmukti* (liberation while living). (*Tattvabodha*; *Vedāntasāra*; *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi*)
- **Modern curriculum** — contemporary traditional Vedānta courses (Daśanāmī, Chinmaya, Arsha Vidya lineages) still teach the manuals as a graded sequence — typically *Tattvabodha* → *Ātmabodha* → *Vedāntasāra* / *Dṛg-Dṛśya-Viveka* → *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi* / *Pañcadaśī* → *Upadeśa Sāhasrī* — before admitting a student to the *bhāṣyas*. [oral tradition — living pedagogy, sequence varies by lineage]
- **Boundary of this file** — Śaṅkara's devotional **stotras** are a different genre with their own profiles: see [Bhaja Govindam](../../04-deep-dives/stotras/bhaja-govindam.md) and [Nirvāṇa Ṣaṭkam](../../04-deep-dives/stotras/nirvana-shatkam.md). For his life, digvijaya, and the four maṭhas, see [Adi Shankara](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/adi-shankara.md).

## 2. Upadeśa Sāhasrī — "A Thousand Teachings" (Śaṅkara)

- **Status** — the **one non-commentarial work whose Śaṅkara authorship is broadly accepted by modern scholarship** [scholarly]: Sengaku Mayeda's critical study authenticated it by testing its terminology and doctrine against the undisputed *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya* and by its citation in the later Advaita tradition. Everything else under Śaṅkara's name in this file carries a question mark; this one does not. (Mayeda, *A Thousand Teachings*; Wikipedia, "Upadesasahasri")
- **Structure — two parts** — a **metrical part** (*padya-bandha*, 19 chapters) and a **prose part** (*gadya-bandha*, 3 chapters); the metrical part's chapter 18, an extended exegesis of **"tat tvam asi"**, is the longest and most celebrated. (Mayeda; Wikipedia)
- **Prose ch. 1 — how to teach** — a manual *for the teacher*: how a knower of Brahman should examine a renunciate pupil who approaches "in the prescribed manner," and instruct him through śruti passages and the mahāvākya until the pupil's identity-knowledge is firm. It is the clearest surviving picture of Śaṅkara's actual classroom method. (*Upadeśa Sāhasrī*, prose ch. 1)
- **Prose ch. 2 — the dialogue of awakening** — a student convinced "I am a transmigrator, subject to birth and death" is led by stages of question and answer to see that the Self is the changeless witness, never the sufferer — a live demonstration of the method of ch. 1. (*Upadeśa Sāhasrī*, prose ch. 2)
- **Prose ch. 3 — parisaṃkhyāna** — a contemplative exercise (*parisaṃkhyāna*, "recounting/disidentification") for the seeker whose knowledge has not yet steadied, systematically withdrawing self-identification from body, senses, and mind. (*Upadeśa Sāhasrī*, prose ch. 3)
- **Doctrinal signature** — uncompromising **jñāna-only** soteriology: liberation comes from knowledge alone, and the text explicitly rejects **jñāna-karma-samuccaya** (combining knowledge with ritual action) — the same polemic that runs through the authenticated *bhāṣyas*. (*Upadeśa Sāhasrī*; Mayeda)

## 3. Vivekacūḍāmaṇi — "Crest-Jewel of Discrimination" [disputed]

- **The text** — the most famous of all Advaita manuals: c. **580 verses** (editions count 576–581) cast as a **guru–disciple dialogue**, opening with a salutation to **Govinda** — read by tradition as honoring both the Lord and Śaṅkara's own guru Govinda Bhagavatpāda. (*Vivekacūḍāmaṇi* 1; Madhavananda trans.)
- **The rare three** — early verses declare three things attainable only by the gods' grace: **human birth, the longing for liberation (mumukṣutva), and the shelter of a great teacher** — the classic statement of the human birth's preciousness. (*Vivekacūḍāmaṇi* 3)
- **Viveka defined** — the work's title-discipline is defined in the famous formula: **"brahma satyaṃ jagan mithyā"** — "Brahman is real, the world is appearance"; the firm conviction of this is *nitya-anitya-vastu-viveka*, discrimination of the eternal from the transient. (*Vivekacūḍāmaṇi* 20)
- **Course of the dialogue** — qualification of the seeker (sādhana-catuṣṭaya) → the disciple's plea → analysis of the **[five kośas](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/pancha-koshas.md)** (the Self mistaken for its sheaths like light for the lamps enclosing it, vv. ~149–210) → **māyā** and superimposition → the mahāvākya *tat tvam asi* → the portrait of the **jīvanmukta**, and the disciple's closing hymn of realization. (*Vivekacūḍāmaṇi*; cross-profiled in [pancha-koshas.md](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/pancha-koshas.md) §4)
- **Authorship dispute [disputed]** — traditional Advaita (the maṭhas, and the whole devotional culture around Śaṅkara) holds it his; modern scholarship largely doubts it:
  - *Variant (academic — against authenticity) [scholarly]:* Paul Hacker's stylistic criteria and subsequent scholars (Mayeda; Michael Comans) find the vocabulary and the strong emphasis on **nirvikalpa samādhi** as a means to knowledge uncharacteristic of the bhāṣya-Śaṅkara and closer to later, Yoga-inflected Advaita; the work is not cited by Śaṅkara's early disciples, and some scholars assign it to a later Śaṅkarācārya of one of the maṭhas.
  - *Variant (defense of possibility):* John Grimes and others argue the case against is not decisive — a teacher may write differently for a beginner's manual than for a polemical bhāṣya — and the tradition's unanimous attribution deserves weight. [disputed]
  - *Catalog rule:* this corpus writes "Śaṅkara (attrib.)" for the *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi* — the text is inseparable from his image either way. (See [adi-shankara.md](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/adi-shankara.md) "Key works.")

## 4. The short manuals — Ātmabodha, Tattvabodha, Aparokṣānubhūti [attributed]

All three circulate under Śaṅkara's name; all three are treated by modern scholarship as later products of his school [late text] [disputed], and by tradition as his own. Variants cataloged, not merged.

- **Ātmabodha ("Self-Knowledge")** — **68 verses**; a compressed statement of the whole path for one "purified by austerities." Knowledge alone directly destroys ignorance — as **fire alone cooks**, no other discipline substitutes; and like the **kataka nut** that clears muddy water and then settles out itself, knowledge removes ignorance and then no longer functions as a separate "means." Ends in the realization "I am that Brahman — witness, non-dual." (*Ātmabodha*, opening verses; Nikhilananda trans.)
- **Tattvabodha ("Knowledge of the Categories")** — a short **prose catechism** in question-and-answer form; the standard *first* text of the traditional curriculum. Defines in sequence: sādhana-catuṣṭaya → the three bodies (*śarīra-traya*) → three states (*avasthā-traya*) → **[five kośas](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/pancha-koshas.md)** → the 24 tattvas of creation and *pañcīkaraṇa* (quintuplication of the elements) → jīva vs Īśvara → the mahāvākya → jīvanmukti and the three karmas (*sañcita, āgāmī, prārabdha*). A syllabus in miniature. (*Tattvabodha*) [late text — attribution to Śaṅkara traditional]
- **Aparokṣānubhūti ("Direct, Unmediated Experience")** — c. **144 verses**; its signature move is re-defining the **fifteen limbs of yoga** in purely Advaitic terms — e.g. true *āsana* is "that in which contemplation of Brahman flows spontaneously," true *prāṇāyāma* the negation of the world-appearance — turning [Patañjali's ladder](../../04-deep-dives/yoga-sutras-of-patanjali.md) into stages of inquiry (*vicāra*). It also presses the radical line that for the knower even **prārabdha karma** is ultimately unreal — talk of prārabdha is a concession to the unenlightened. A commentary (*ṭīkā*) attributed to **Vidyāraṇya** attaches to it, linking it to §5. (*Aparokṣānubhūti*; Vimuktananda trans.) [disputed authorship]

## 5. Pañcadaśī of Vidyāraṇya — the medieval summa

- **Author & era** — **Vidyāraṇya** (14th c. CE [scholarly]), Jagadguru of the **Śṛṅgerī maṭha** (c. 1380–1386), traditionally identified with **Mādhava**, the brahmin counselor associated with **Harihara and Bukka** and the founding of the **Vijayanagara empire** (1336 CE) — an identification tradition asserts and scholars debate [disputed]. His wider corpus includes the *Jīvanmukti-viveka* (which argues liberation-while-living largely through the [Yoga Vāsiṣṭha](../yoga-vasishtha.md)) and *Anubhūtiprakāśa*. (Wikipedia, "Vidyaranya"; Swahananda trans., introduction)
- **Architecture — 15 chapters in three pentads**, the scheme that gives the work its name ("of the fifteen"):
  - **Viveka-pañcaka (chs. 1–5, discrimination):** *Tattva-viveka* (reality discriminated through the three states and five kośas), *Mahābhūta-viveka* (the five elements), *Pañcakośa-viveka* (the sheaths — the chapter cited throughout [pancha-koshas.md](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/pancha-koshas.md)), *Dvaita-viveka* (duality of Īśvara-made and jīva-made), *Mahāvākya-viveka* (the great sayings).
  - **Dīpa-pañcaka (chs. 6–10, the "lamps" of illumination):** *Citradīpa* (the picture), *Tṛptidīpa* (satisfaction), *Kūṭasthadīpa* (the immutable witness), *Dhyānadīpa* (meditation), *Nāṭakadīpa* (the theatre).
  - **Ānanda-pañcaka (chs. 11–15, bliss):** *Yogānanda*, *Ātmānanda*, *Advaitānanda*, *Vidyānanda*, *Viṣayānanda* — bliss analyzed from yogic absorption down to ordinary sense-pleasure as fractions of Brahman-bliss. (*Pañcadaśī*, chapter colophons; Swahananda trans.)
- **Signature analogies** — **Citradīpa's painted canvas**: as a cloth is successively washed, stiffened, outlined, and painted, so pure consciousness "wears" the stages of world-appearance while remaining the unchanged ground; **Nāṭakadīpa's stage-lamp**: the witness-consciousness (*kūṭastha/sākṣī*) is the lamp in a theatre — it illumines the dancer (buddhi), the audience, and the whole play impartially, and **keeps shining when the stage is empty** (deep sleep). (*Pañcadaśī* 6, 10)
- **Doctrinal note** — the *Pañcadaśī* consolidates the later-Advaita distinction wherein **māyā** conditions Īśvara and **avidyā** the jīva — see the variant cataloged in [maya-and-avidya.md](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/maya-and-avidya.md). (*Pañcadaśī* 1; later doxography)
- **Joint authorship variant** — *Variant (tradition/commentators):* a persistent tradition assigns part of the work — commonly the later chapters — to Vidyāraṇya's teacher and predecessor at Śṛṅgerī, **Bhāratītīrtha**; editions and commentators split on where the seam lies. Catalog as co-authored, boundary unsettled. [disputed]

## 6. Vedāntasāra of Sadānanda — the schoolroom digest

- **The text** — *Vedāntasāra* ("Essence of Vedānta") by **Sadānanda Yogīndra** (c. 15th c. CE [scholarly]) — the most widely used compact prose introduction to Advaita, prized for giving every technical term a crisp definition. (Nikhilananda trans., introduction)
- **Method** — opens with the **anubandha-catuṣṭaya** (the four preliminaries any śāstra must state: the qualified student, subject-matter, connection, purpose), then proceeds by **adhyāropa-apavāda** — deliberate superimposition of the world on Brahman followed by its systematic retraction, "the method of the Vedānta." (*Vedāntasāra*)
- **Contents** — collective vs individual ignorance (*samaṣṭi/vyaṣṭi*), the [kośas](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/pancha-koshas.md) and the **three bodies** (the mapping cited in pancha-koshas.md §1), *pañcīkaraṇa*, and a famous analysis of **"tat tvam asi"** by *bhāga-tyāga-lakṣaṇā* (partial-abandonment implication): as in "this is that Devadatta," the incompatible elements (remoteness/limitation of *tat* and *tvam*) are dropped and the one consciousness-substrate is kept. Ends with **śravaṇa–manana–nididhyāsana**, *savikalpa/nirvikalpa samādhi*, and the marks of the jīvanmukta. (*Vedāntasāra*; Nikhilananda trans.)
- **Commentaries** — carried by two standard ṭīkās, **Subodhinī** and **Vidvanmanorañjanī**, which fixed it as the *entry* text of the traditional syllabus. (Nikhilananda trans., introduction)

## 7. Dṛg-Dṛśya-Viveka — seer and seen [disputed]

- **The text** — a jewel of **46 verses** discriminating the **seer (dṛk)** from the **seen (dṛśya)**: forms are seen and the eye is their seer; the eye in turn is seen and the mind its seer; the mind's modifications are seen and the **witness (sākṣī)** their seer — and the witness alone is **never seen**. The whole of Advaita compressed into a regress that stops at consciousness. (*Dṛg-Dṛśya-Viveka* 1–2; Nikhilananda trans.)
- **Māyā's two powers** — the manual's other standard contribution: māyā operates by **āvaraṇa** (veiling the real) and **vikṣepa** (projecting the unreal) — the analytic pair used across later Advaita and cataloged in [maya-and-avidya.md](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/maya-and-avidya.md). (*Dṛg-Dṛśya-Viveka* 13–15)
- **Samādhi scheme** — closes by classifying **six samādhis** (savikalpa and nirvikalpa, each practiced internally and externally) as the ripening of the seer-seen discrimination. (*Dṛg-Dṛśya-Viveka* 22–30)
- **Authorship [disputed]** — attributed variously to **Śaṅkara** (popular tradition), to **Bhāratītīrtha**, or to **Vidyāraṇya**; editions print all three ascriptions. Catalog: a Śṛṅgerī-circle text of the Vidyāraṇya era under an inherited Śaṅkara label. [disputed]
- **Modern afterlife** — **Ramana Maharshi** translated the *Dṛg-Dṛśya-Viveka* into Tamil prose for devotees, giving the little manual a large 20th-century readership. (Nikhilananda trans., preface; Ramanasramam editions) [regional]

## 8. Dating — the two clocks kept apart

- **Scriptural/cyclic clock** — every text here belongs to **Kali Yuga** (begun 3102 BCE) of the 28th Mahāyuga, Vaivasvata Manvantara; tradition treats the manuals not as innovations but as restatements of timeless Upaniṣadic truth for a darkening age. The **maṭha chronologies** (Kāñcī, Dvārakā lists) date Śaṅkara himself to **509–477 BCE**, pulling the attributed manuals into the pre-Common Era [disputed — traditional chronology].
- **Academic clock [scholarly]** — *Upadeśa Sāhasrī*: c. **700–750 CE** (with Śaṅkara, often cited 788–820 CE; see [adi-shankara.md](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/adi-shankara.md)). *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi* and the short manuals: **post-Śaṅkara, date uncertain** — anywhere from soon after him to the second millennium [disputed]. *Pañcadaśī*: **14th c. CE** (Vidyāraṇya d. 1386). *Dṛg-Dṛśya-Viveka*: same Śṛṅgerī milieu, **c. 14th c.** if by Bhāratītīrtha/Vidyāraṇya. *Vedāntasāra*: **c. 15th c. CE**.
- **The arc** — read in academic order, the genre shows Advaita's history in miniature: Śaṅkara's austere jñāna-only instruction (*Upadeśa Sāhasrī*) → increasing accommodation of **yoga and samādhi** as means (*Aparokṣānubhūti*, *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi*, *Dṛg-Dṛśya-Viveka*) → full scholastic systematization with māyā/avidyā technicalities (*Pañcadaśī*, *Vedāntasāra*). [scholarly] For the post-Śaṅkara teachers who drove that development (Sureśvara, Padmapāda, Vācaspati Miśra, Bhāratītīrtha, Vidyāraṇya), see [Ācāryas & Sampradāyas](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md).
