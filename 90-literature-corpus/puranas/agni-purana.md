# Agni Purāṇa

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/puranas/` — one of the eighteen *Mahā-Purāṇas*.
> Up-link: [`../README.md`](../README.md) (puranas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (the literature corpus).
> See also the parent survey [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md) and the [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).

The **Agni Purāṇa** (also **Āgneya Purāṇa**) is the great **encyclopedia among the Mahā-Purāṇas** — less a story-collection than a *kośa* (compendium) of the whole of Sanskritic civilization. It is named for and framed as the teaching of **Agni**, the fire-god, who is said to have revealed it to sage **Vasiṣṭha**. Where most Purāṇas spend their bulk on myth, *māhātmya*, and bhakti, the Agni Purāṇa famously runs across roughly **382–383 chapters** whose subjects "succeed one another without the slightest connection or transition" [scholarly]: cosmology and the avatāras, but then **iconography (pratimā-lakṣaṇa), temple and city architecture (vāstu/sthāpatya), āyurveda (medicine, veterinary, botany), dhanurveda (archery/martial arts), vyākaraṇa (grammar), chandas and alaṅkāra (metrics and poetics), jyotiṣa (astronomy/astrology), ratna-śāstra (gemology), nīti and rāja-dharma (statecraft), pilgrimage, ritual, yoga, and lexicography**. It is the Purāṇa most often called a "**guide to civilization**" or an "encyclopedia of *Sanātana Dharma*." Its narrative spine is deliberately compressed — including a **condensed Rāmāyaṇa narrated by Agastya** and a **condensed Mahābhārata/Hari-vaṃśa (Kṛṣṇa) summary** — so that the bulk of the text can be given over to its applied-science material.

## Structure — chapters, frame, and the avatāra opening

- **Size & form:** the canonical lists assign it **15,400 ślokas**; surviving recensions hold roughly **12,000–15,000 verses in 382 or 383 chapters (adhyāyas)**, with no *khaṇḍa*/*aṃśa*/*saṃhitā* macro-division — just a long, loosely-ordered chapter sequence. This stratified, accreted character is the usual signature of a much-revised text [scholarly].
- **Frame of transmission:** the classic nested chain — **Agni → Vasiṣṭha → Vyāsa → Sūta (Lomaharṣaṇa/Ugraśravas) → the sages of Naimiṣāraṇya.** Agni as the *speaking* authority is unusual and gives the text its name; it self-presents as the knowledge "containing the essence of all sciences" (*sarva-vidyā*).
- **The avatāra opening (ch. 2–16, approx.):** begins with the **daśāvatāra**, devoting early chapters to **Matsya (ch. 2), Kūrma (ch. 3), and Varāha (ch. 4)**, then the condensed Rāma and Kṛṣṇa narratives. Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Pañca-lakṣaṇa coverage [scholarly]:** *weak and pro-forma.* The five marks (*sarga / pratisarga / vaṃśa / manvantara / vaṃśānucarita*) are present only in compressed token form near the front; the overwhelming bulk is **technical/śāstric encyclopedia** — a prime exhibit for the academic view that the *pañca-lakṣaṇa* definition fits almost no surviving Purāṇa. Cross-link: [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md).
- **Sectarian leaning:** notably **non-sectarian / Smārta-inclusive** — variously classed as Śaiva, Vaiṣṇava, Śākta, and Smārta because it teaches **Viṣṇu, Śiva, the Devī, Sūrya, and Gaṇeśa worship impartially**. Its heavy **Tāntric ritual** layer (mantras, dīkṣā, maṇḍala/yantra worship, even *Buddhist incantations* in some chapters) is one of its distinctive features. Cross-link: [`../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md`](../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md).
- **Dating:** *traditional:* an eternal revelation of Agni, of Vyāsa's compilation. *Academic [scholarly]:* a **late, layered** text — a core probably **post-7th c.**, a recognizable version by the **11th c.** (al-Bīrūnī, c. 1030, knew an Agni Purāṇa), with a youngest stratum possibly as late as the **17th c.** **R. C. Hazra** treated the extant text as essentially a **9th-c. Tāntric compilation** assembled with a ritual/sectarian motive, suggesting the "original" Āgneya may have survived under the title **Vahni Purāṇa** (Vahni = Agni). Often tagged **[late text]**.

## Notable / distinctive stories and sections

### The Matsya deluge frame and the avatāras (ch. 2–4)
- **Matsya, Manu, and the great flood** — Agni opens by recounting **Viṣṇu's fish-avatāra (Matsya)**: the fish rescued by **Vaivasvata Manu** grows enormous, warns of the *pralaya*, and tows Manu's boat (with the **seven ṛṣis** and the **seeds/Vedas**) to safety; the version recovers the **Vedas from the demon Hayagrīva** and is keyed to the **Kṛtamālā river**, close to the Bhagavata telling. The Agni Purāṇa also adds the meta-note that **Matsya preached the Matsya Purāṇa to Manu at the beginning of the kalpa** — making the deluge a frame for revelation itself. *(Chronology: the inter-kalpa / start-of-kalpa deluge and the dawn of the present **Vaivasvata manvantara**.)* Cross-link the avatāra and the flood: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md); the Manu/manvantara spine: [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/) and [`../../01-manvantaras/`](../../01-manvantaras/).
  - *Variant (seeds vs. living beings):* here Manu gathers **all seeds** (not pairs of living creatures), and assembles the **seven sages** — the Mahābhārata-type recension, distinct from the Bhāgavata/Matsya-Purāṇa elaborations. Catalog alongside other deluge versions.
- **Kūrma and Varāha** — the tortoise (churning-of-the-ocean support) and boar (earth-rescue) follow in ch. 3–4, in compact form. Cross-link the churning and *bhūmi-uddhāra*: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/).

### The condensed Rāmāyaṇa — *narrated by Agastya* (early chapters)
- **A Rāma-carita told by the sage Agastya** — the Agni Purāṇa carries a **highly condensed Rāmāyaṇa**, distinctively placed in the mouth of **Agastya** (the southern ṛṣi who, in Vālmīki, gives Rāma the *Āditya-hṛdaya* hymn before the final battle). It rapidly summarizes the seven *kāṇḍas* — birth, exile, **Sītā's abduction by Rāvaṇa**, the alliance with **Sugrīva/Hanumān**, the war at Laṅkā, and the return — as a *purāṇic* digest rather than an epic. *(Chronology: **Tretā-yuga** of the present mahāyuga.)* Cross-link the Rāma material: [`../../04-deep-dives/`](../../04-deep-dives/) and the Tretā events: [`../../01-manvantaras/`](../../01-manvantaras/).
  - *Variant (narrator):* the **Agastya framing** is the Agni Purāṇa's own; most Purāṇic Rāma-digests (e.g. in the Viṣṇu/Bhāgavata) are told within their general narration. The choice fits Agastya's canonical role as Rāma's preceptor. Cross-link: [`./vishnu-purana.md`](vishnu-purana.md).

### The condensed Mahābhārata and Kṛṣṇa (Hari-vaṃśa) summary (early chapters)
- **A Bhārata + Hari-vaṃśa précis** — following Rāma, the text gives a compressed **Mahābhārata** and **Kṛṣṇa-carita**: the Yādava birth, the Vṛndāvana/Mathurā exploits, the Pāṇḍava–Kaurava conflict, and the war — again as an index, not a retelling. *(Chronology: late **Dvāpara-yuga** into the **Dvāpara–Kali junction**.)* Cross-link Kṛṣṇa: [`../../04-deep-dives/`](../../04-deep-dives/) and the Bhāgavata: [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).

### Iconography and image-making — *pratimā-lakṣaṇa* (ch. ~39–67, 99–101)
- **The canon of divine images** — extensive *śilpa*/iconographic prescriptions: measurements, postures, *mudrās*, attributes, vāhanas, and consecration (*pratiṣṭhā*) of images of **Viṣṇu, Śiva (incl. *liṅga*), the Devī, Sūrya, Gaṇeśa, the avatāras, and minor deities** — one of the **most important early Sanskrit sources for Hindu iconography and the *pañcāyatana* image-program**. *(Chronology: framework/śāstra — timeless ritual technology.)* Cross-link: [`../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md`](../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md).

### Temple, city, and *vāstu* architecture (ch. ~25, 39–45, 55–67, 92, 106)
- **Sthāpatya-veda / vāstu-śāstra** — rules for **temple plans, mound/site selection, the *vāstu-puruṣa-maṇḍala*, the temple axis and perimeter, towns and royal cities** (ch. 106 on city *vāstu*), and house-construction — a key technical source for Indian sacred architecture, studied alongside the *Bṛhat-saṃhitā* and the *Śilpa-śāstras*. *(Chronology: framework/śāstra.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/README.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/README.md).

### Āyurveda, veterinary science, and botany (ch. ~279–286, 370)
- **Medicine for humans, elephants, horses, cattle — and trees** — chapters on **diseases and cures, materia medica, antidotes, *gaja*/*aśva*-āyurveda (elephant and horse medicine), cattle care, and arboriculture/agriculture**; a notable repository of the *upaveda* of medicine inside a Purāṇa. *(Chronology: framework/upaveda.)* Cross-link the upavedas: [`../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md`](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md).

### Dhanurveda — archery and martial arts (ch. ~249–252)
- **The science of weapons and war** — a compact **dhanurveda**: classes of weapons (*mukta*/*amukta*/*mukta-amukta*/*yantra-mukta*), the bow and archery, training, the warrior's stances, and martial discipline — among the **fullest dhanurveda passages preserved in any Purāṇa**, frequently cited in histories of Indian martial science. *(Chronology: framework/upaveda.)* Cross-link: [`../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md`](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md).

### Nīti, rāja-dharma, and statecraft (ch. ~218–242)
- **The conduct of kings** — duties of the king, ministers and the *saptāṅga* (seven limbs of the state), diplomacy (the *ṣāḍguṇya* — six measures of foreign policy), *daṇḍa-nīti*, taxation, fort and army organization, just causes for war, law (*vyavahāra*), and ordeals — a Purāṇic condensation of the **Arthaśāstra/Nīti** tradition. *(Chronology: framework/śāstra.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md).

### Vyākaraṇa, lexicography, metrics, and poetics (ch. ~328–347)
- **The language sciences** — chapters on **grammar (vyākaraṇa)**, a **synonym/lexicon (kośa)** section, **prosody (chandas)**, and **poetics/rhetoric (alaṅkāra-śāstra)** with figures of speech (*rasa*, *guṇa*, *doṣa*, *alaṅkāra*) — making the Agni Purāṇa a minor but real source for the history of **Sanskrit literary theory**. *(Chronology: framework/vedāṅga.)* Cross-link: [`../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md`](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md).

### Jyotiṣa, ratna-śāstra, and the auxiliary sciences
- **Astronomy/astrology and gemology** — sections on **jyotiṣa** (planetary lore, *muhūrta*, omens) and **ratna-parīkṣā** (testing and lore of gems), rounding out the encyclopedia of *vidyās*. *(Chronology: framework/vedāṅga.)* Cross-link: [`../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md`](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md).

### Ritual, Tāntra, pilgrimage, and yoga (ch. ~21–38, 109–120, 372–381)
- **The Smārta-Tāntric core** — *pūjā* manuals and *mantras* for **Viṣṇu, Śiva, Devī, Sūrya, Gaṇeśa**, *dīkṣā* (initiation), *maṇḍala*/*yantra* worship, *prāyaścitta* (expiation), *śrāddha*, **tīrtha-māhātmya** (incl. Mithilā/Bihar geography, ch. 114–116), and a closing **yoga + mokṣa** section (the *Gītā-sāra* and *Yama-gītā* digests). *(Chronology: framework — ritual and soteriology, timeless; recommended esp. for **Kali-yuga**.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md) and [`../../06-sacred-geography/README.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/README.md).
  - *Variant / caution (academic):* the presence of **Buddhist incantations** and a thick Tāntric ritual layer is Hazra's main evidence for dating the extant text to a **9th-c. Tāntric redaction** [scholarly] [interpolation].
