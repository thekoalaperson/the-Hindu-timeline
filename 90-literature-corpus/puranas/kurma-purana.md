# Kūrma Purāṇa

> Up-link: [`../README.md`](../README.md) (the Purāṇas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (the literature corpus).
> See also the parent survey [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md), the siblings [`linga-purana.md`](linga-purana.md), [`shiva-purana.md`](shiva-purana.md), [`varaha-purana.md`](varaha-purana.md), and the [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).

The **Kūrma Purāṇa** (*Kūrma Mahāpurāṇa*) is one of the eighteen *Mahāpurāṇas*, named for and framed by the **Kūrma (tortoise) avatāra of Viṣṇu**: the text is narrated *as* the discourse Viṣṇu-as-Kūrma delivered to the sage-king **Indradyumna** and the assembled ṛṣis **during the churning of the milk-ocean (Samudra-manthana)**, when the tortoise bore Mount Mandara on its back. It is the great Purāṇic curiosity in **sectarian balance**: classed by tradition (and by its title) as **Vaiṣṇava**, it is in substance one of the most **Śaiva** of the Purāṇas — it houses the **Īśvara Gītā** (Śiva's answer to the Bhagavad Gītā) and the **Vyāsa Gītā**, glorifies **Vārāṇasī/Kāśī** and **Prayāga**, and gives **equal enthusiasm** (so Ludo Rocher) to Viṣṇu, Śiva, and Śakti rather than privileging one. Ludo Rocher called it "the most interesting of all the Purāṇas in its discussion of religious ideas." Its frame is the standard one — **Sūta (Romaharṣaṇa)** reciting at **Naimiṣāraṇya**, repeating the Kūrma–Indradyumna dialogue.

- **Structure (four *saṃhitās*, only one surviving)** — the text declares itself originally cast in **four saṃhitās** — **Brāhmī, Bhāgavatī, Saurī, and Vaiṣṇavī** — conferring (respectively) *dharma, artha, kāma,* and *mokṣa*. **Only the Brāhmī Saṃhitā survives**; the Bhāgavatī, Saurī, and Vaiṣṇavī are **lost**. [late text] [scholarly]
  - The surviving **Brāhmī Saṃhitā** is split into a **Pūrva-vibhāga (Pūrvārddha)** and an **Uttara-vibhāga (Uparivibhāga / Uttarārddha)**:
    - **Pūrva-vibhāga (~51–53 chapters)** — cosmogony, Viṣṇu's avatāras, *bhuvana-kośa* (geography/cosmography), solar and lunar dynasties, *manvantara*/*yuga* time-reckoning, planetary motions, *varṇāśrama-dharma*, and the origin of Mahālakṣmī.
    - **Uttara-vibhāga (~44–46 chapters)** — the theological/soteriological heart: the **Īśvara Gītā** and the **Vyāsa Gītā**, plus the *tīrtha-māhātmyas* of **Kāśī** and **Prayāga**.
  - *Variant (verse count):* tradition cites **17,000 ślokas**; the extant text preserves **~6,000** (some report ~8,000). The Venkateshwara Press edition gives ~95 chapters total. [disputed] [scholarly]
- **Pañca-lakṣaṇa coverage** — the Kūrma Purāṇa **explicitly invokes the five marks** and is comparatively **strong** on them: *sarga* (creation, origin of the Prajāpatis), *pratisarga* (dissolution), *manvantara* (the 14 Manus), *vaṃśa* (solar/lunar dynasties, ṛṣi-lineages), and *vaṃśānucarita*. Much of the Pūrva-vibhāga **parallels the Viṣṇu Purāṇa**. Cross-link the framework: [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/) and [`../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/`](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/).
- **Sectarian leaning** — nominally **Vaiṣṇava** (Kūrma-avatāra frame, Pāñcarātra-flavored exaltation of **Śrī/Lakṣmī** as supreme Śakti) but **substantively Śaiva–Śākta**: hymns to **Maheśvara**, the **Īśvara Gītā**'s Śiva-as-supreme-Brahman, Pāśupata-yoga, the **Lakulīśa** prophecy, and Durgā/Devī worship. Wilson concluded it is "correctly classed with the **Śaiva Purāṇas**." Its idiom blends **Advaita Vedānta + Sāṃkhya-Yoga + Pāśupata** theism. [scholarly]
- **Dating** — *traditional:* eternal, recited by Kūrma-Viṣṇu and transmitted via Sūta, abridged by **Vyāsa**. *Academic [scholarly]:* the core is commonly placed **c. 7th–9th century CE** (Wilson noted it is "avowedly posterior to" the Tāntrika, Śākta, and Jain sects), a **stratified** text revised over centuries; the **Īśvara Gītā** is often dated to the same broad window as a relatively early layer. [late text]

## Notable / distinctive stories and sections

### The Kūrma-avatāra frame and the churning of the ocean
- **Kūrma bearing Mandara — the narration-frame** — during the **Samudra-manthana**, Viṣṇu descends as the **tortoise (Kūrma)** and supports the sinking **Mount Mandara** on his back so the gods and asuras can churn; *in that moment* he discourses to **Indradyumna** and the ṛṣis, and **that discourse is the Purāṇa itself**. The whole text is thus voiced from inside the churning episode. *(Chronology: the canonical Samudra-manthana belongs to the framework of cyclic deva–asura conflict; classically placed in the **Cākṣuṣa Manvantara** / early in the cosmic order — see the event file for the placement debate.)* Cross-link the full event and its variants: [`../../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md`](../../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md), and the avatāra survey [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
  - *Variant (the listener):* Wilson notes the opening dialogue is between **Viṣṇu-as-Kūrma and Indradyumna** at the churning; the outer frame remains **Sūta → ṛṣis at Naimiṣa**. (*Kūrma Purāṇa, opening; Wilson*)
- **Origin of Mahālakṣmī (Śrī)** — among the treasures churned from the ocean rises **Lakṣmī**, who chooses Viṣṇu; the Kūrma Purāṇa details her **glory and her mystic forms** with a **Pāñcarātra** exaltation of **Śrī as the supreme Śakti** and consort-energy of Viṣṇu. *(Chronology: framework — at the Samudra-manthana.)* Cross-link: [`../../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md`](../../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md).

### The Īśvara Gītā — Śiva's Bhagavad-Gītā
- **Īśvara Gītā ("Song of the Lord")** — an **11-chapter** philosophical gītā in the **Uttara-vibhāga** (over **~768 verses**), modeled on the Bhagavad Gītā but with **Śiva as the spokesman**: after Viṣṇu and Śiva embrace, Viṣṇu (or the ṛṣis) requests instruction, and Śiva expounds **Ātman, Brahman-Puruṣa, Prakṛti, Māyā, Yoga, and Mokṣa** through a **Śaiva non-dualist (Śiva-Advaita)** lens, identifying **Śiva with Brahman**. It teaches an **eightfold yoga of Brahman** (resembling Patañjali's aṣṭāṅga), **liṅga-worship**, and **Oṃ/praṇava** recitation, and carries a strong **Pāśupata** stratum. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — timeless soteriological teaching, set within the Kūrma-narration.)* Cross-link the parallel discourse and Śiva-cycle: [`../../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md`](../../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md), [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md), and [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/).
  - *Variant (themes beyond the Gītā):* the Īśvara Gītā introduces **new themes absent from the Bhagavad Gītā** — Śiva-liṅga worship and Śiva (not Kṛṣṇa) as supreme — and is treated as a distinct **Śaiva-Vedānta** text in its own right. (*Īśvara Gītā; cf. Vidyāraṇya / later Advaita reception*) [scholarly]

### The Vyāsa Gītā — dharma and devotion taught by Vyāsa
- **Vyāsa Gītā** — a teaching ascribed to **Vyāsa** (commonly identified with **chapters 12–33 of the Uttara-vibhāga**, sometimes ~12–46), expounding **knowledge of Śiva through devotion combined with prescribed works (karma + bhakti)**, the duties of the *āśramas*, and the path to liberation; it pairs with the jñāna-centric Īśvara Gītā as the more **dharma/observance**-centric discourse. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — timeless teaching.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/).
  - *Variant (extent):* some scholars treat the **entire Uttara-vibhāga** as "Vyāsa Gītā," with the Īśvara Gītā as an embedded section; others restrict it to chs. 12–33/12–46. [disputed] [scholarly]

### Varṇāśrama-dharma and the universality of liberation
- **Varṇāśrama-dharma** — the Pūrva-vibhāga sets out the **origin of the Prajāpatis**, the **duties and livelihoods of the four varṇas**, the four *āśramas*, and the characteristics of the **puruṣārthas (dharma, artha, kāma, mokṣa)** — the text's mapping of social-spiritual order onto its saṃhitā-scheme. *(Chronology: framework — normative dharma across the yugas, with degeneration toward Kali.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/).
  - *Variant (bhakti-egalitarianism):* the Kūrma Purāṇa distinctively asserts that **anyone of any varṇa can attain liberation through bhakti-yoga** — a notable universalizing claim. (*Kūrma Purāṇa*) [scholarly]

### Geography and cosmography — the bhuvana-kośa
- **Bhuvana-kośa (cosmography)** — a systematic **geography of the world**: the seven *dvīpas* and oceans, **Jambū-dvīpa** and **Bhārata-varṣa**, **Mount Meru**, the mountains and rivers, the *lokas*, and **planetary/celestial motions** with the time-reckoning of *kalpa/manvantara/yuga*. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — the geography of the cosmic clock.)* Cross-link: [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/), [`../../06-sacred-geography/meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md), and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).

### Śaiva deeds, the Goddess, and the Lakulīśa prophecy
- **Andhakāsura-vadha by Bhairava** — the blind demon **Andhaka** is defeated; in the Kūrma strand the slaying is associated with **Bhairava** and culminates (as in the Liṅga/Śiva strand) in Andhaka's **redemption as a gaṇa**. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine deed, timeless.)* Cross-link the full event and variants: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) §3 and [`linga-purana.md`](linga-purana.md) §Andhaka-vadha.
- **Origin of the four Śaktis from Śiva** — Wilson singles out a Kūrma-Purāṇa account of **four Śaktis (Goddess-energies) arising from Śiva** — a Śākta theophany folded into the Śaiva cosmology. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine theophany, timeless.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md).
- **The Lakulīśa prophecy (28th Yogeśvara)** — the Kūrma Purāṇa (traditionally cited around **ch. 53**) carries the prophecy of **Maheśvara descending as the wandering ascetic Lakulin/Lakulīśa**, last of the **28 Yogeśvaras**, with the **four disciples Kuśika, Garga, Mitra, and Kauruṣya** who found the historical **Pāśupata** sect — shared with the Vāyu (ch. 23) and Liṅga (ch. 24) Purāṇas. *(Chronology: the avatāra appears in the **Kali-Yuga** of the current mahāyuga, **Vaivasvata Manvantara**, **Śveta-Varāha Kalpa**.)* Cross-link: [`linga-purana.md`](linga-purana.md) §"The 28 Yoga-avatāras."
- **The Liṅgodbhava pillar** — the Kūrma Purāṇa is one of the texts that carries the **Liṅgodbhava** charter myth (the infinite pillar of fire between Brahmā and Viṣṇu). *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — primordial theophany.)* Cross-link: [`linga-purana.md`](linga-purana.md) §Liṅgodbhava and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).

### Tīrtha-māhātmyas — Kāśī and Prayāga
- **Kāśī-māhātmya (Vārāṇasī)** — extensive glorification of **Kāśī** as Śiva's city, the sanctity of **dying in Kāśī**, and the grace of **Bhagavān Viśvanātha** — a major Śaiva pilgrim's guide embedded in the Uttara-vibhāga. *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography active across yugas; founding legends in mythic antiquity.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md) and [`../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md).
- **Prayāga-māhātmya (Allahabad)** — glorification of **Prayāga** at the Triveṇī-saṅgam — the merit of bathing and dwelling there, a key witness for the Prayāga pilgrimage tradition. *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md) and [`../../06-sacred-geography/kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md).
