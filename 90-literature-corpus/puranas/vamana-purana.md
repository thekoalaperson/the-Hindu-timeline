# Vāmana Purāṇa

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/puranas/` — a per-Purāṇa source file in the literature corpus.
> Up-link: [`../README.md`](../README.md) (the Purāṇas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (the literature corpus).
> See also the parent survey [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md) and the companion [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).

The **Vāmana Purāṇa** is one of the eighteen Mahāpurāṇas, named for **Viṣṇu's Dwarf incarnation (Vāmana)** — the avatāra who, in three world-spanning strides as **Trivikrama**, reclaims the cosmos from the *dānava*-king **Bali**. The name and frame are **Vaiṣṇava**, and the text opens (ch. 1) with the sage **Nārada asking Pulastya** how and why Viṣṇu took the dwarf form. Yet the **surviving manuscripts are far more strongly Śaiva than Vaiṣṇava** [scholarly]: the bulk of the book glorifies **Śiva, Pārvatī/Umā, Skanda-Kārttikeya, Gaṇeśa, the Goddess, and a long roll of asura-slayings**, and it is now usually classed a **Śaiva (or impartially Hari-Hara, "tolerable impartiality" — Wilson) Purāṇa** built on a Vaiṣṇava skeleton. Its other great preoccupation is **sacred geography**: the appended **Saro-māhātmya** is a 28-chapter pilgrimage guide to **Kurukṣetra / Thānesar (Sthāṇvīśvara)** and the *tīrtha*s of the Sarasvatī country. A lost recension, the **Bṛhad-Vāmana** in four *saṃhitās*, is referenced but does not survive [scholarly].

- **Structure — two strata, *adhyāya*-based (no *khaṇḍa*/*aṃśa* division in the surviving core):**
  - **The Vāmana Purāṇa proper** — the **All-India Kashiraj Trust critical edition** (ed. **Anand Swarup Gupta**, Varanasi 1968) standardizes the core at **69 *adhyāyas***; older printed editions ran to **~95–96 chapters / ~6,000–10,000 ślokas**. Tradition (Matsya/Nāradīya lists) assigns it **10,000 ślokas**.
  - **The Saro-māhātmya ("Glory of the [Sarasvatī] Lake")** — a **28-chapter** appendix, treated by the critical edition as a separate supplement: a *tīrtha*-māhātmya of **Kurukṣetra, the Sarasvatī, and the forests/rivers of Haryana and eastern Punjab** [late text].
  - The lost **Bṛhad-Vāmana-Saṃhitā** (4 *saṃhitās*) is named in the text itself as the "greater" form [scholarly].
- **Pañca-lakṣaṇa coverage — present but uneven:** it carries genuine **cosmogony/*sarga*** (creation, the *aṇḍa*, Brahmā's day) and scattered **manvantara/*vaṃśa*** notes, but its real weight falls on **deity-mythology and *tīrtha*-māhātmya** rather than systematic dynastic chronicle — more story-anthology and pilgrim's manual than a Viṣṇu-Purāṇa-style *pañca-lakṣaṇa* text. Contrast the model in [`./vishnu-purana.md`](./vishnu-purana.md) and the survey [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md).
- **Sectarian leaning:** **labelled Vaiṣṇava, practiced Śaiva-Śākta.** Wilson found it distributes "homage between Śiva and Viṣṇu with tolerable impartiality" — a **Hari-Hara, broadly smārta-pilgrimage** outlook rather than a polemical Vaiṣṇava tract. Its Goddess chapters (Śatākṣī/Śākambharī, Koṭavī, Cāmuṇḍā) also give it real Śākta value.
- **Dating:** *traditional:* eternal, narrated by **Pulastya to Nārada**, recorded by Vyāsa. *Academic [scholarly]:* core manuscript range **~450–900 CE**, but **most scholars favour the 9th–11th c. CE** for the surviving recension, with the **Saro-māhātmya a later appendage** reflecting **medieval Kurukṣetra pilgrimage** [late text][interpolation]. Wilson, struck by its Benares/Sarasvatī geography, judged it **comparatively late**.

## Notable / distinctive stories and sections

### Vāmana–Bali–Trivikrama — the title myth (the eponymous core)
- **Vāmana measures the three worlds in three strides** — when **Bali** (grandson of Prahlāda) conquers heaven, the gods petition Viṣṇu, who is born as a **dwarf brāhmaṇa** to Aditi and Kaśyapa; at Bali's sacrifice he begs **"three paces of ground,"** then swells into the cosmic **Trivikrama**, covering earth and heaven in two strides and (with the third, on Bali's head) pressing the king to the netherworld (**Pātāla/Sutala**), restoring **Indra's** sovereignty. This is the **dramatic climax** Wilson identifies for the whole book. *(Chronology: traditionally a deva–asura episode of the early world; placed in **Satya-yuga** of the present kalpa in the avatāra-sequence.)* Cross-link the avatāra entry [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) and the Satya-yuga spine [`../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/01-satya-yuga.md`](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/01-satya-yuga.md).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata skandha 8 / Viṣṇu P. / Bhāgavata bali-tale):* the standard telling stresses **Bali's generosity and devotion** — Viṣṇu makes him a future Indra and guards his door; the Vāmana Purāṇa keeps the dwarf-deception frame Wilson calls "humiliation by stratagem." Cross-link [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).
  - *Variant (Vedic seed, Ṛgveda):* the three strides (*tri-vikrama*) of Viṣṇu predate the dwarf; the Purāṇa fuses the **Vedic stride-myth** with the **epic dwarf-and-Bali tale** [scholarly].

### Prahlāda — devotee, moral counsellor, and pilgrim
- **Prahlāda dissuades Andhaka; recites dharma** — the great Viṣṇu-devotee **Prahlāda** (son of Hiraṇyakaśipu, grandfather of Bali) appears not only in the Bali backstory but as a **moral teacher**: he counsels his demon-kinsman **Andhaka** against lust for Gaurī, citing the karmic consequences of adharma, and later undertakes an **extended *tīrtha*-circuit** (Mānasa, Yamunā, Kedāra, Badarikāśrama, Kurukṣetra), making him a vehicle for the text's pilgrimage doctrine. *(Chronology: deva–asura age, early world / Satya-yuga frame.)* Cross-link the Prahlāda–Narasiṃha material via [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).

### Śiva slays Andhaka — and Andhaka becomes Bhṛṅgin (signature Śaiva myth)
- **The destruction of Andhakāsura** — the blind/dark demon **Andhaka** (son of Hiraṇyākṣa, cousin of Prahlāda) lusts after **Pārvatī/Gaurī**; Śiva assumes a terrifying **Bhairava** form and impales him on the **triśūla**. *(Chronology: deva–asura conflict, framework/cosmology — timeless/prior-age.)* Cross-link [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md).
  - *Distinctive Vāmana detail:* the streams of Andhaka's blood spawn **multiple demons** that the Goddess **Cāmuṇḍā/Cārcikā must drink**, and from his pierced body emerge the **Aṣṭa-Bhairavas** (Vidyā-rāj, Kāla-rāj, Vighna-rāj, etc.) along with the planet **Maṅgala (Mars)**; the now-purified Andhaka becomes a leading Śiva-*gaṇa*, renamed **Bhṛṅgin/Bhṛṅgi**. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine origins.)*
  - *Variant (Matsya / Kūrma / Liṅga P.):* the **blood-born-demons-multiply** motif parallels the **Raktabīja** episode of the Devī-Māhātmya; cross-link [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md). *Variant (Skanda P.):* Andhaka pursues Pārvatī to Viṣṇu's abode, where she shows the **Ardhanārīśvara** form to repel him — absent in the Vāmana telling.

### Sati, Dakṣa's sacrifice, and Śiva–Pārvatī
- **Dakṣa's sacrifice destroyed; Śiva weds Umā** — the Purāṇa narrates the classic Śaiva cycle: **Satī's self-immolation** at her father **Dakṣa's** insulting sacrifice, Śiva's wrath (the *yajña*-wrecking), and his later marriage to **Pārvatī/Umā** (Satī reborn) — the marriage that frames the births of Skanda and Gaṇeśa. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine/prior-age events.)* Cross-link the dedicated event file [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md).

### Birth of Skanda-Kārttikeya and the slaying of Tāraka
- **Skanda's birth and the death of Tārakāsura** — to produce the war-god who alone can kill the boon-protected **Tāraka**, Śiva's seed is borne by **Agni/Gaṅgā** and the **Kṛttikā** stars, generating six-faced **Skanda/Kārttikeya**, who is consecrated commander of the gods and slays Tāraka (the Vāmana devotes a full birth-and-consecration sequence to this, ~ch. 31). *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — deva–asura war of the divine age.)* Cross-link [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md).
- **Origin of Gaṇeśa** — the elephant-headed son of Pārvatī, made **lord of obstacles** and chief of the *gaṇa*s, is given an origin-account alongside Skanda. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine origins.)* Cross-link [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md).

### The Goddess: Śatākṣī / Śākambharī and the slaying of Durgama
- **Śatākṣī ("Hundred-Eyed") and Śākambharī end the demon Durgama** — when the asura **Durgama** seizes the Vedas and causes a hundred-year drought, the Goddess manifests with **a hundred eyes** (weeping to make rivers flow), nourishes the world from her body as **Śākambharī ("Herb-Bearer")**, and destroys Durgama — a key **Śākta** myth and a named **etymology of "Durgā."** *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — deva–asura conflict, timeless.)* Cross-link [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md).
  - *Variant (Devī-Bhāgavata / Devī-Māhātmya):* the **Śatākṣī–Śākambharī–Durgama** episode also appears (with variations) in the Devī-Bhāgavata; the Vāmana is one of its **principal Mahāpurāṇic witnesses** [scholarly].

### Jalodbhava, Gajendra, and other deliverances
- **Śiva and Viṣṇu jointly slay Jalodbhava** — the water-born demon **Jalodbhava**, hiding in waters and mountains, is destroyed by the two great gods together; in the episode **Śiva gifts the Sudarśana-cakra to Viṣṇu**, the gods materialize sacred rivers, and a triśūla is forged — a strongly **Hari-Hara** narrative. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — deva–asura age.)* Cross-link [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).
- **Gajendra-mokṣa** — the elephant-king, seized for a thousand years by a crocodile, wins release by a heartfelt Viṣṇu-*stotra*; Viṣṇu beheads the crocodile and liberates both the elephant and the cursed **gandharva** within it. *(Chronology: legendary antiquity / framework.)* Cross-link the dedicated deep-dive [`../../04-deep-dives/gajendra-moksha.md`](../../04-deep-dives/gajendra-moksha.md).

### Sukeśi(n) the virtuous rākṣasa
- **King Sukeśi and the city in the sky** — **Sukeśi**, son of the rākṣasa-king **Vidyutkeśi**, is so devoted to **Śiva** that Śiva grants him invincibility and a **flying city**; enlightened by sages, Sukeśi teaches dharma to all the demons, who become **virtuous** — an unusual "redeemed-rākṣasa" tale carrying the text's *dharma*-instruction. *(Chronology: legendary antiquity / framework.)* Cross-link [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).

### Cosmogony and the divine age
- **Creation, the cosmic egg, and Brahmā's day** — the text supplies a *sarga* account (the *aṇḍa*, the unfolding of the elements and the gods) and scattered manvantara/*vaṃśa* notices, the *pañca-lakṣaṇa* residue beneath its myth-and-pilgrimage bulk. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — start of the kalpa.)* Cross-link [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md) (if present) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).

### The Saro-māhātmya — Kurukṣetra and the Sarasvatī *tīrtha*s (the appendix)
- **Kurukṣetra / Sthāṇvīśvara (Thānesar) as supreme pilgrimage zone** — the 28-chapter appendix maps the **holy land between the Yamunā and Sutlej**, exalting **Kurukṣetra** and the **Sarasvatī**; at **Sthāṇu/Sthāṇvīśvara** a great **banyan** is flanked by abodes of **Śukra (north), Soma (east), Dakṣa (south), and Skanda (west)**. *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography of the present kalpa; some sites tied to legendary/Mahābhārata events.)* Cross-link [`../../06-sacred-geography/README.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/README.md), [`../../06-sacred-geography/sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md), and the 12-jyotirlinga roll [`../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md).
- **Pṛthūdaka — holiest of the holy** — the text's famous gradation: *"Kurukṣetra is holy; holier is the Sarasvatī; holier than the Sarasvatī are all the tīrthas together; and holiest of all is **Pṛthūdaka**"* (modern Pehowa), celebrated as **Kārttikeya's tīrtha**. Other named sites include **Kanakhala, Cakra-tīrtha, Soma-tīrtha, Indra-tīrtha, Nāga-tīrtha, Śveta-tīrtha, Sapta-Sārasvata, Prabhāsa, Prayāga, Badarikāśrama, Kedāra, and Rudramahālaya**. *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography of the present age.)* Cross-link [`../../06-sacred-geography/kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md).
- **Devikā-tīrtha and Vāmana's residence** — geography is fused with myth: **Vāmana** is said to abide at **Devikā-tīrtha**, and many sites are keyed to Śiva's manifestations and Vāmana's exploits — the appendix's signature **myth-as-map** method [late text]. *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography.)*
