# Matsya Purāṇa

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/puranas/` — a per-Purāṇa source file in the literature corpus.
> Up-link: [`../README.md`](../README.md) (the Purāṇas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (the literature corpus).
> See also the parent survey [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md) and the companion [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).

The **Matsya Purāṇa** ("the Fish Purāṇa") is widely held to be **among the oldest of the Mahāpurāṇas** and is repeatedly singled out as the text that gives **one of the earliest explicit definitions of the Purāṇa genre itself** (the *pañca-lakṣaṇa*). It takes its name and frame from **Matsya**, Viṣṇu's first (fish) *avatāra*, who narrates the entire work to **Manu Vaivasvata** while towing his ark through the cosmic deluge — making the Purāṇa formally a teaching delivered *during* the *pralaya*. Despite the Vaiṣṇava frame, it is a markedly **syncretic, encyclopaedic** text: the *Pādma Purāṇa*'s *tri-guṇa* scheme files it among the **tāmasic** group (Śiva/Agni-presiding), and in practice it praises Śiva, Viṣṇu, the Goddess, Sūrya and the Pitṛs alike, while devoting huge blocks to **temple architecture (*vāstu*), iconography (*pratimā-lakṣaṇa*), *dāna* (charity), *vrata*, *tīrtha* (esp. the Narmadā and Prayāga), and statecraft (*rāja-dharma*)**. Many scholars treat it (with the *Vāyu* and *Brahmāṇḍa*) as preserving an old common Purāṇic core, and it is one of the **oldest surviving Indian texts with detailed sections on temple and image-making**.

- **Structure — a single continuous run of *adhyāyas* (no *khaṇḍa*/*aṃśa*/*saṃhitā* division):** most printed editions (e.g. Ānandāśrama) have **291 chapters**; the South-Indian/Tamil Grantha recension has **172 (≈176) chapters**. Tradition assigns it **14,000–20,000 ślokas**; extant manuscripts run **~13,000–15,000 verses**. It is **narrated by Matsya to Manu**, then re-told in the Naimiṣa forest by **Sūta to the ṛṣis** — the standard double frame.
  - **Cosmogony & framework** — creation, dissolution, the four yugas and *manvantaras*, the cosmic egg, the lineage of gods and sages.
  - **Genealogies (*vaṃśa*)** — solar and lunar dynasties; the **Tārā/Soma–Budha origin of the lunar line**; the **Āndhra (Sātavāhana) king-list** (chs. ~271–273), historically mined for chronology [scholarly].
  - **Dharma, vrata & dāna** — festivals and *saṃskāras* (chs. ~54–102); the **sixteen great gifts (*ṣoḍaśa-mahādāna*)**, including the *tulāpuruṣa* and *hiraṇyagarbha* gifts; the **catalogue of the 18 Mahāpurāṇas and the merit of donating each** (chs. ~53/290).
  - **Tīrtha & māhātmya** — Prayāga / Kumbh (chs. ~103–112), Vārāṇasī (chs. ~180–185), and the **Narmadā** pilgrimage (chs. ~186–194).
  - **Rāja-dharma & nīti** — kingship, omens, and good government (chs. ~215–243).
  - **Vāstu-śāstra & śilpa** — **temple, image, palace and house architecture** in roughly **chs. 252–270** (the core 252–257, plus 269–270), incl. the 64-/81-pada *vāstu-maṇḍala*, pillar and doorway proportions, and *pratimā-lakṣaṇa*.
- **Pañca-lakṣaṇa coverage:** covers all five marks — *sarga* (creation), *pratisarga* (dissolution/re-creation), *vaṃśa* (genealogy), *manvantara* (Manu-cycles), *vaṃśānucarita* (dynastic history) — and is, indeed, a **locus classicus for the *definition* of those five marks**; but it overlays vast *upabṛṃhaṇa* (supplementary) material (*vāstu*, *dāna*, *tīrtha*) atop the old core.
- **Sectarian leaning:** nominally **Vaiṣṇava** (Matsya frame) but functionally **smārta/syncretic with a strong Śaiva tilt** — classed **tāmasic** in the *Pādma* scheme (see [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md)); it gives the **Tripura-dahana / Tāraka-maya war** and other Śaiva matter major space alongside Vaiṣṇava avatāra lore.
- **Dating:** *traditional:* spoken by **Matsya-Viṣṇu** to Manu during the deluge, eternal and authorless-but-for-Vyāsa's redaction. *Academic [scholarly]:* the **earliest core is among the oldest of any Purāṇa**, with a first version by **c. 200–300 CE**; continuous revision/expansion through the **mid-1st to 2nd millennium CE**. Doniger: **c. 250–500 CE**; the *vāstu* and *tīrtha* strata are later accretions [late text]. The Āndhra king-list anchors part of the dynastic material to the **3rd–4th c. CE** [scholarly].

## Notable / distinctive stories and sections

### The Matsya–Manu deluge (the narrative frame) — *signature episode*
- **Matsya saves Manu, the seeds, the Vedas and the ṛṣis from the *pralaya* flood** — King **Manu Vaivasvata**, performing tapas, finds a tiny fish in his water-cupped hands begging protection; it grows monstrously (pot → tank → Gaṅgā → ocean), revealing itself as **Viṣṇu**. It warns of the coming deluge, has Manu build a ship loaded with **the seeds of all beings, the seven ṛṣis, and (in many tellings) the Vedas**, and tows the ark by a rope tied to its horn to safety on the northern mountain — after which it narrates the Purāṇa to Manu. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — at a **pralaya / kalpa boundary**, traditionally tied to the start of the present Vaivasvata manvantara.)* This is the Purāṇa's defining myth and the **dialogic frame** for the whole text. Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) (Matsya as the first avatāra), [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/kalpa-and-pralaya.md`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/kalpa-and-pralaya.md), and [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md).
  - *Variant (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa, the oldest layer):* the fish is **not yet identified with Viṣṇu**; it simply rescues Manu, the lone survivor, who then re-creates humankind through the sacrificial maiden **Iḍā** — the archetypal Indian flood myth [scholarly].
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata, Vana-parva):* the fish (Brahmā/Viṣṇu) saves Manu and the **seven ṛṣis**; closely parallels the Matsya version.
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata P., skandha 8):* here Matsya additionally **rescues the Vedas stolen by the asura Hayagrīva** and slays him; Manu is **Satyavrata** (the future Vaivasvata Manu). Cross-link: [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).

### Samudra Manthana — the churning of the ocean
- **The gods and asuras churn the Milk-Ocean for *amṛta*** — using **Mount Mandara** as churning-rod and **Vāsuki** as rope, with **Kūrma (Viṣṇu's tortoise)** as the pivot beneath the mountain; out come the **fourteen ratnas** (Lakṣmī, Dhanvantari with the *amṛta*, the *kālakūṭa* poison swallowed by Śiva, the Apsarases, Airāvata, etc.), and Mohinī tricks the asuras out of the nectar. The Matsya Purāṇa carries a **full, much-cited recension** of the myth. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — a primordial deva–asura event, "before" the ordered yugas.)* Cross-link the dedicated deep-dive: [`../../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md`](../../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md), and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).

### Tārā, Soma and the birth of Budha — *origin of the Lunar dynasty*
- **The abduction of Tārā and the Tārakāmaya war** — **Soma (Candra)**, swollen with pride after his *Rājasūya* sacrifice, **carries off Tārā, the wife of Bṛhaspati** (guru of the gods). The gods (with Bṛhaspati) and the asuras (with **Śukra**, who backs Soma) split into two armies — the **Tārakāmaya / "war for Tārā"** — until **Brahmā** intervenes and forces Tārā's return. Tārā is found pregnant; questioned, she names **Soma** as the father, and she bears **Budha (Mercury)**, from whom — through Purūravas — the entire **Candravaṃśa (Lunar dynasty)** descends. (The birth of Budha is given around **adhyāya 24**.) *(Chronology: framework / primordial divine prehistory, seeding the lineages that run through the yugas.)* Cross-link: [`../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md`](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md).

### Maya the asura-architect and the Tripura war — *Śaiva centrepiece*
- **Maya, the asura builder, and the three flying cities (Tripura)** — the great asura craftsman **Maya** raises strategy and defences for the asuras: the Matsya Purāṇa describes his **life-restoring *vāpī* (a nectar step-well)** that revives slain demons, the doctrine that **Kāla (Time) overrules even invincible fortresses**, and how **Janārdana (Viṣṇu)** neutralizes the asuras' resurrection by **draining the well's reserve**. The narrative climaxes in **Śiva's destruction of Tripura** by his Pramatha hosts and a single fiery arrow. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — a deva–asura cosmic war; Maya recurs across the yugas as the demon-world's architect.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
  - *Variant (Śiva/Liṅga P.):* the Tripura-dahana is told as a primarily **Śaiva glorification** (the gods build Śiva's chariot, Mandara as bow, Vāsuki as string); the Matsya version foregrounds **Maya's engineering and the *kāla*/nectar-well motif**. Cross-link: [`shiva-purana.md`](shiva-purana.md), [`linga-purana.md`](linga-purana.md).

### The 18 Mahāpurāṇas and the merit of donating them (the *dāna* of the Purāṇas)
- **Enumeration and *māhātmya* of the eighteen Mahāpurāṇas** — the Matsya Purāṇa contains a **classic list of all 18 Mahāpurāṇas**, with their **verse-counts and the religious merit (and ritual procedure) of gifting a copy of each** (e.g. donating the Matsya P. together with a golden fish-and-elephant). It is a **primary source for the canonical count and naming of the eighteen**, and for the genre's self-description. *(Chronology: framework — doctrinal/meta-textual, timeless.)* Cross-link: [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md) (the 18-Purāṇa canon and *tri-guṇa* classification).
- **The sixteen great gifts (*ṣoḍaśa-mahādāna*)** — the *tulāpuruṣa* (weighing oneself in gold), *hiraṇyagarbha*, *go-sahasra*, etc.; the Matsya Purāṇa is a **foundational text for royal mahādāna ritual** later quoted in the dharma-nibandhas. *(Chronology: framework — *dāna-dharma*, age-independent.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md).

### Vāstu, temple and image-making (chs. ~252–270) — *art-historically pivotal*
- **The earliest systematic Purāṇic *vāstu-śāstra*** — eight chapters on architecture and sculpture (the core **252–257**, plus **269–270**): the **64-/81-square *vāstu-puruṣa-maṇḍala*** and the myth of the *vāstu-puruṣa* pinned down by the gods; site selection and orientation; **proportions of pillars, doorways and the *garbhagṛha*****; and ***pratimā-lakṣaṇa*** (iconometry of divine images). Together with the *Bṛhat-Saṃhitā*, this is **among the oldest surviving Indian texts codifying temple and icon design**, with lasting influence on medieval building practice. *(Chronology: framework — technical/ritual canon, timeless.)*

### Tīrthas: Prayāga, Vārāṇasī and the Narmadā
- **Pilgrimage *māhātmyas*** — the **Prayāga-māhātmya** (chs. ~103–112; the Triveṇī-saṅgam and the Kumbh), the **Avimukta/Kāśī (Vārāṇasī) māhātmya** (chs. ~180–185), and the long **Narmadā-māhātmya** (chs. ~186–194) cataloguing the river's *tīrthas*. These are early, much-quoted *sthala-purāṇa* sources. *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography, timeless.)*

### Other distinctive matter
- **Genealogies and the Āndhra (Sātavāhana) king-list** — solar and lunar dynasties down into the Kali age, including the **Āndhra-bhṛtya** dynasty (chs. ~271–273), a **near-historical king-list** parallel to (and often differing from) those of the Vāyu/Brahmāṇḍa/Viṣṇu Purāṇas — quarried for early Indian chronology [scholarly]. *(Chronology: Dvāpara → **Kali-yuga** of the current 28th mahāyuga.)* Cross-link: [`../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md`](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) and the Kali-yuga recorded-history file [`../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md`](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).
  - *Variant (across the "Vāyu group"):* dynasty counts and regnal years differ from purana to purana; the Matsya's figures are among the most cited for the Sātavāhanas [scholarly].
- **Vāmana / Bali and the asura tales** — the dwarf avatāra's three strides reclaiming the worlds from **Bali**, embedded in the deva–asura material (cross-link [`vamana-purana.md`](vamana-purana.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)). *(Chronology: framework — a prior-age avatāra event.)*
- **Sāvitrī / Pitṛ and śrāddha lore, *yoga*, and *rāja-dharma***  — ancestral-rite (*śrāddha*) chapters, varieties of yoga, royal duties, *nīti* and omens; the broad smārta encyclopaedism that marks the text. *(Chronology: framework — applicable across yugas.)* Cross-link the Satya-yuga and manvantara spine: [`../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/01-satya-yuga.md`](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/01-satya-yuga.md).
