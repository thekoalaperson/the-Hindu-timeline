# Skanda Purāṇa

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/puranas/` — a per-Purāṇa source file in the literature corpus.
> Up-link: [`../README.md`](../README.md) (the Purāṇas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (the literature corpus).
> See also the parent survey [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md), the siblings [`shiva-purana.md`](shiva-purana.md) and [`linga-purana.md`](linga-purana.md), and the [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).

The **Skanda Purāṇa** (*Skanda Mahāpurāṇa*) is the **largest of the eighteen Mahāpurāṇas** — traditionally **~81,000 (eighty-one thousand) ślokas**, dwarfing every other Purāṇa — and is named for **Skanda / Kārtikeya** (Murugan), the war-god son of Śiva and Pārvatī whose **conception, six-fold birth, and slaying of Tārakāsura** form its mythic charter. But the text is far more than a Skanda-biography: it is the **single greatest Purāṇic repository of *sthala-purāṇas* and *tīrtha-māhātmyas*** — temple- and pilgrimage-origin legends — functioning, in scholarly description, as an **encyclopedic pilgrimage handbook** that sanctifies the sacred geography of the whole subcontinent (Kāśī, Kedāra, the Narmadā/Revā, Setu/Rāmeśvaram, Veṅkaṭācala/Tirupati, Somanātha/Prabhāsa, Ujjain/Avantī, and dozens more). Its frame is the standard one — the sage **Sūta (Lomaharṣaṇa/Romaharṣaṇa)** reciting what **Vyāsa** transmitted, set within dialogues of Śiva, Skanda, Nārada, and the ṛṣis. It is best understood not as one book but as an **open, accreting "library"** of regional māhātmyas that grew over centuries.

- **Structure (two rival arrangements)** — the text survives in **two organizational systems** that do not fully match, a hallmark of its composite nature:
  - **Khaṇḍa-recension (the standard printed/Vulgate version)** — **seven khaṇḍas** named for pilgrimage regions: **(1) Māheśvara, (2) Vaiṣṇava (Viṣṇu), (3) Brahma, (4) Kāśī, (5) Āvantya, (6) Nāgara, (7) Prabhāsa**. Each subdivides into sub-khaṇḍas / *māhātmyas*:
    - **Māheśvara-khaṇḍa** — Kedāra, Kaumārikā, and Aruṇācala (Tiruvaṇṇāmalai) sections; carries the central **Skanda/Tārakāsura** cycle and Śaiva myth.
    - **Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa** — nine sections including **Veṅkaṭācala-māhātmya (Tirupati-Tirumala)**, **Puruṣottama-kṣetra (Jagannātha Purī)**, **Ayodhyā**, **Mathurā**, and **Bhāgavata**/Mārgaśīrṣa māhātmyas — a strongly Vaiṣṇava layer inside a Śaiva text.
    - **Brahma-khaṇḍa** — Setu-māhātmya (the Rāma-Setu / Rāmeśvaram region), **Dharmāraṇya** (Modhera), and an Uttara-khaṇḍa.
    - **Kāśī-khaṇḍa** — ~100 chapters glorifying **Vārāṇasī/Kāśī** and the Vindhya; reached its present form ~mid-13th c. CE. [late text]
    - **Āvantya-khaṇḍa** — **Avantī-kṣetra (Ujjain, with the Mahākāleśvara jyotirliṅga)**, **Caturaśīti-liṅga**, and the vast **Revā-khaṇḍa (Narmadā-māhātmya)** — the longest single māhātmya, on the Narmadā (Revā) river. [scholarly]
    - **Nāgara-khaṇḍa** — ~279 chapters on the **Hāṭakeśvara-kṣetra (Vaḍnagar)** region of Gujarat.
    - **Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa** — the largest khaṇḍa (~491 chapters, four sections): **Prabhāsa/Somanātha**, **Girnar (Vastrāpatha)**, the **Arbuda (Ābū)** region, and **Dvārakā**.
  - **Saṃhitā-recension** — an alternate division into **six saṃhitās**: **Sanatkumāra, Sūta, Śaṃkarī, Vaiṣṇavī, Brāhmī, and Saura** — oriented to cosmogony, theology, and questions of virtue/vice rather than geography. The khaṇḍa- and saṃhitā-versions are largely **non-overlapping bodies of material** circulating under one title. [scholarly]
- **Pañca-lakṣaṇa coverage** — **weak/uneven** by the classical five-mark standard: *sarga*, *pratisarga*, *vaṃśa*, *manvantara*, and *vaṃśānucarita* appear (chiefly in the Saṃhitā/Brahma material) but are heavily outweighed by ***tīrtha-māhātmya*, *vrata*, *dāna*, and *upadeśa*** — the genre that the Skanda Purāṇa most fully embodies. It is the prime exhibit for the academic point that **later Mahāpurāṇas drifted from cosmology toward pilgrimage-encyclopedia**. Cross-link the framework: [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/) and [`../../01-manvantaras/`](../../01-manvantaras/).
- **Sectarian leaning** — predominantly **Śaiva / Kaumāra** (Skanda-centered), composed (per scholars) largely in **Pāśupata** circles around **Vārāṇasī**, yet remarkably **integrative**: it absorbs major **Vaiṣṇava** māhātmyas (Veṅkaṭācala, Purī, Setu, Bhāgavata-māhātmya) and **Śākta** and **Saura** matter — reflecting the period's Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava competition and the drive to claim regional sacred sites. [scholarly]
- **Dating** — *traditional:* timeless, narrated by Skanda/Śiva and transmitted via Vyāsa and Sūta. *Academic [scholarly]:* the **oldest known manuscript** is a **Nepalese palm-leaf codex (NAK 2-229)** in transitional-Gupta script dated **~810 CE**, proving a Skanda Purāṇa existed by then; the earliest titled text likely existed **by the 6th–8th c. CE** (some place a Pāśupata core in late-6th/early-7th c. Benares), while individual khaṇḍas/māhātmyas accreted **as late as the 13th–15th c. CE** (e.g. the Kāśī-khaṇḍa ~13th c.). Thus there is **no single "Skanda Purāṇa"** but a centuries-long, ever-growing compilation. [late text] [interpolation]

## Notable / distinctive stories and sections

### The charter myth — Skanda / Kārtikeya and the slaying of Tārakāsura
- **The need for a war-god** — the asura **Tāraka(sura)**, empowered by Brahmā's boon that **only a son of Śiva** could kill him, oppresses the devas; but the ascetic, recently-widowed (post-Satī) Śiva is unmarried, so the gods engineer **Pārvatī's tapasya and remarriage to Śiva** — and the burning of **Kāma** — to produce the destined son. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine biography, set in mythic antiquity at the dawn of the war between devas and asuras.)* Cross-link the full deity treatment and variants: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md) §"The need for him & the conception" and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md).
- **The six-fold birth (Ṣaṇmukha)** — Śiva's fiery seed, too potent to be borne, is carried by **Agni** then **Gaṅgā** and deposited in a **reed-thicket (śara-vana)**; it becomes **six infants** nursed by the **six Kṛttikās (the Pleiades)** and fused by Pārvatī into one child with **six faces** — hence **Kārtikeya** ("son of the Kṛttikās"), **Śaravaṇabhava**, **Ṣaṇmukha/Subrahmaṇya**. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine birth, mythic antiquity.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md) §"The six-fold birth."
  - *Variant (carriers of the seed):* the relay **Śiva → Agni → Gaṅgā → reed-bed** is the Skanda/Śiva-Purāṇa norm; the **Mahābhārata (Vana-parvan)** and Kālidāsa's *Kumārasambhava* give differing chains (Agni alone, or Svāhā impersonating the Kṛttikās). (*Skanda P. / Śiva P.; cf. Mahābhārata, Kumārasambhava*) [disputed]
  - *Variant (one child vs six):* whether the six bodies are **merged into one six-headed god** or remain distinct varies by recension. (*Purāṇic tradition*)
- **Tāraka-vadha and the generalship of Skanda** — anointed **Devasenāpati** (commander of the divine host), Skanda leads the devas and **slays Tārakāsura** (and, in expanded cycles, the brothers **Sūrapadma / Siṃhamukha** and the demon **Pralamba/Pralambāsura**), restoring cosmic order. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — the deva–asura war, mythic antiquity.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md) §"The slaying of the demon."
  - *Variant (Northern vs Southern/Tamil recension):* the Sanskrit Skanda Purāṇa centers **Tāraka**; the **Tamil/Southern Murugan tradition** (e.g. the *Kanda Purāṇam* of Kacciyappa Civācāriyār, a Tamil reworking) centers **Sūrapadman**, the six abodes (*Aṟupaḍai Vīḍu*), and the marriage to **Vaḷḷi**. (*Skanda P. vs Tamil Kanda Purāṇam*) [scholarly]

### Kāśī-khaṇḍa — the glorification of Vārāṇasī
- **Kāśī-māhātmya** — an exhaustive *sthala-purāṇa* of **Vārāṇasī/Kāśī** as the supreme *mokṣa-kṣetra* on Śiva's trident: the **Viśveśvara (Viśvanātha) jyotirliṅga**, the **Maṇikarṇikā** cremation-ghāṭ (where Śiva whispers the *tāraka-mantra* to the dying), **Dhuṇḍhirāja-Gaṇeśa**, **Daṇḍapāṇi/Kāla-Bhairava** as the city's guardian, and the legend of **King Divodāsa** and Śiva's reclaiming of the city. *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography active across all yugas; founding legends in mythic antiquity; the khaṇḍa's text-form ~13th c. CE.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md), [`../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md). [late text]

### Āvantya-khaṇḍa — Ujjain and the Narmadā (Revā)
- **Avantī-kṣetra-māhātmya** — the sanctity of **Ujjain (Avantī/Avantikā)**, seat of the **Mahākāleśvara jyotirliṅga**, with origin-legends of its tīrthas and the Caturaśīti-liṅga (84 liṅgas) circuit. *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography; legends in mythic antiquity.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md).
- **Revā-khaṇḍa (Narmadā-māhātmya)** — the great river *sthala-purāṇa*: the **origin of the Narmadā (Revā)** from Śiva (in some tellings from his sweat/tapas on Maikala), her supreme purifying power (mere **sight** of the Narmadā equals bathing in the Gaṅgā), and a chapter-by-chapter tour of her bank-tīrthas (Oṃkāreśvara/Amareśvara, Maheśvar, Bharuch). *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography across yugas; the river's descent in mythic antiquity.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/README.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/README.md).
  - *Variant (Revā-khaṇḍa attribution):* scholars note the Revā-khaṇḍa **also circulates attached to the Vāyu Purāṇa**, and the two recensions differ — its placement is genuinely disputed. (*Skanda P. Āvantya-khaṇḍa / Vāyu P.*) [disputed] [scholarly]

### Brahma-khaṇḍa — Setu (Rāmeśvaram) and Dharmāraṇya
- **Setu-māhātmya** — a ~52-chapter glorification of the **Rāma-Setu / Setubandha (Rāmeśvaram)** region: how **Rāma** installed the **Rāmanātha(svāmin) jyotirliṅga** and the immense merit of the **Dhanuṣkoṭi** tīrtha and the Setu pilgrimage. *(Chronology: the Setu's building is set in the **Treta-Yuga** Rāma narrative; the māhātmya frames a tīrtha active across yugas.)* Cross-link the Rāma narrative and variants: [`../../04-deep-dives/ramayana/`](../../04-deep-dives/ramayana/), and the jyotirliṅga: [`../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md).
- **Dharmāraṇya-māhātmya** — the legend of **Dharmāraṇya (Modhera, Gujarat)** and its Brahmin community — a regional caste/community charter as much as a temple-origin tale. *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography, mythic antiquity.)* [scholarly]

### Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa — the Vaiṣṇava māhātmyas inside a Śaiva text
- **Veṅkaṭācala-māhātmya** — the *sthala-purāṇa* of **Tirumala–Tirupati**: the self-manifest hill of **Veṅkaṭeśvara (Bālājī/Śrīnivāsa)**, the marriage of Viṣṇu (as Śrīnivāsa) to **Padmāvatī**, and his loan from **Kubera** — origin-myths of one of the world's most-visited shrines. *(Chronology: set in the Kali-Yuga in many tellings; the hill itself a timeless tīrtha.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md), [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Puruṣottama-kṣetra-māhātmya** — the origin-legend of **Jagannātha at Purī** (the *dāru-brahman*/log-deity, King Indradyumna). *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography; legend in mythic antiquity.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md).
- **Bhāgavata-māhātmya & Mārgaśīrṣa-māhātmya** — devotional appendices praising recitation of the *Bhāgavata* and the merit of the month Mārgaśīrṣa — Vaiṣṇava *vrata*/*dāna* material. *(Chronology: framework — living ritual across yugas.)* Cross-link: [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md). [late text]

### Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa & Nāgara-khaṇḍa — the Gujarat/Saurāṣṭra māhātmyas
- **Prabhāsa / Somanātha-māhātmya** — the *sthala-purāṇa* of **Prabhāsa-kṣetra** and the **Somanātha jyotirliṅga**: the **Moon-god (Soma/Candra)**, cursed by **Dakṣa** to wane, regains his light by worshiping Śiva here — the etiology of the "**Lord of Soma**." Also **Girnar/Vastrāpatha**, the **Arbuda (Mt. Ābū)** region, and **Dvārakā**. *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography; the Soma-curse a timeless divine event; Dvārakā tied to the **Kṛṣṇa/Dvāpara-Yuga** cycle.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md), [`../../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md`](../../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md).
- **Nāgara-khaṇḍa — Hāṭakeśvara/Vaḍnagar** — ~279 chapters on the **Hāṭakeśvara-kṣetra** and the **Nāgara Brahmins'** sacred town (Vaḍnagar) — again a fused temple-origin and community charter. *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography, mythic antiquity.)* [scholarly]

### Cosmology, philosophy, and famous embedded texts (Saṃhitā material)
- **Sūta-saṃhitā & Sanatkumāra-saṃhitā** — the more **doctrinal** strata: cosmogony, Advaita-leaning theology, dharma, and *jñāna* — material later quoted as authoritative by Vedāntins. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — timeless teaching.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/).
- **The "Skanda" Bhagavad-Gītā commentaries and stotras** — the corpus is the traditional locus of widely-recited devotional texts attributed to it, e.g. forms of the **Satyanārāyaṇa-vrata-kathā** (Revā/Reva-khaṇḍa) and various **māhātmyas of months, vratas, and tīrthas**. *(Chronology: framework — living ritual across yugas.)* [disputed]
  - *Variant (Satyanārāyaṇa-kathā):* the immensely popular **Satyanārāyaṇa Pūjā** narrative is traditionally cited to the **Revā-khaṇḍa of the Skanda Purāṇa**, though it is widely regarded by scholars as a **late accretion** not in older manuscripts. (*Skanda P., Revā-khaṇḍa*) [late text] [interpolation]
