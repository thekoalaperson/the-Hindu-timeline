# Garuḍa Purāṇa

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/puranas/` — one of the eighteen *Mahā-Purāṇas* (a **Vaiṣṇava / *sāttvika*** Purāṇa).
> Up-link: [`../README.md`](../README.md) (puranas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (the literature corpus).
> See also the parent survey [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md) and [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).

The **Garuḍa Purāṇa** is the Vaiṣṇava *Mahā-Purāṇa* framed as a **dialogue spoken by Viṣṇu to Garuḍa** — his eagle *vāhana* — who then relayed it to the sage **Kaśyapa**, whence it passed (via the Naimiṣāraṇya sages and Vyāsa) into the world. Though it is, like the Agni Purāṇa, a sprawling **encyclopedia** (Viṣṇu-*bhakti*, cosmology, iconography, *vāstu*, *ratna-parīkṣā* gemology, *āyurveda*, *nīti*/statecraft, *vyākaraṇa*, *vrata* and *dāna*, yoga, lexicography), it is by far **best known to ordinary Hindus for one thing**: its **Preta-khaṇḍa** — the most detailed Purāṇic account of **death, the soul's post-mortem journey, the hells (*naraka*), the realm of Yama, and the *preta*/*śrāddha* funerary rites**. It is the text whose readings/recitation accompany the **thirteen days of mourning** in many North-Indian households, so much so that hearing "Garuḍa Purāṇa" in colloquial use almost always means "the book read after a death." Its cosmology centres on **Viṣṇu and Lakṣmī**, whose union generates the cosmos.

## Structure — khaṇḍas, the Viṣṇu→Garuḍa frame, and dating

- **Macro-division (khaṇḍas):** two core sections in the standard recensions, with a third in one printed edition —
  - **Pūrva-khaṇḍa** (a.k.a. **Ācāra-khaṇḍa**) — the great bulk, **~229 chapters** (some recensions 240–243), covering "*ācāra*" = the whole conduct of life: cosmology, Viṣṇu worship, *vrata*/*dāna*, iconography, *vāstu*, gemology, medicine, *nīti*, grammar, yoga.
  - **Preta-khaṇḍa** (a.k.a. **Uttara-khaṇḍa**, "**Dharma-khaṇḍa**") — the famous **death/afterlife** section, **~34–49 chapters** depending on recension.
  - **Brahma-khaṇḍa** — **only in the Veṅkaṭeśvara (Bombay) printed edition**, **~29 chapters**, a later add-on (Kṛṣṇa→Garuḍa dialogue on Viṣṇu's supremacy and the Tirupati shrine) [late text] [interpolation].
- **Size:** the canonical lists assign it **19,000 ślokas**; surviving manuscripts preserve roughly **8,000** — a strong signal of a much-revised, partly-lost text whose "varying numbers of chapters and considerably different content" across recensions are the usual academic flag [scholarly].
- **Frame of transmission:** **Viṣṇu → Garuḍa → Kaśyapa → … → Vyāsa → Sūta (Lomaharṣaṇa/Ugraśravas) → the Naimiṣāraṇya sages.** The Garuḍa-as-listener device is distinctive: the eagle who asks Viṣṇu about death gives the text its name and its question-and-answer texture.
- **The Saroddhāra problem [scholarly]:** the most-circulated English "Garuḍa Purāṇa" in the West — the **Garuḍa-Purāṇa-Saroddhāra** (tr. Ernest Wood & S. V. Subrahmanyam, 1911) — is **not the Purāṇa itself** but a later **digest/commentary by Naunidhirāma (Navanidhi-rāma)** that quotes a now-lost recension. Much popular "Garuḍa Purāṇa afterlife" lore actually derives from the Saroddhāra, not the Mahā-Purāṇa proper. Tag accordingly.
- **Pañca-lakṣaṇa coverage [scholarly]:** *weak/pro-forma.* The five marks (*sarga / pratisarga / vaṃśa / manvantara / vaṃśānucarita*) appear only in token form near the front; the overwhelming bulk is **applied śāstra + eschatology** — another exhibit for the academic view that the classical definition fits almost no surviving Purāṇa. Cross-link: [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md).
- **Sectarian leaning:** firmly **Vaiṣṇava** and self-classed *sāttvika*, but **Smārta-inclusive** — it folds in **Śaiva, Śākta, Sūrya and Gaṇeśa** worship and the *pañcāyatana* program, so its Viṣṇu-centrism is irenic rather than polemical. Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).
- **Dating:** *traditional:* an eternal teaching of Viṣṇu, compiled by Vyāsa. *Academic [scholarly]:* a **layered, accreted** text — a core variously placed in the **first millennium CE** (estimates spanning the **4th–11th c.**, with **R. C. Hazra** and others favoring a developed form by **~900–1000 CE**), with material added through the second millennium and the Brahma-khaṇḍa latest of all. Often tagged **[late text]**.

## Notable / distinctive stories and sections

### The Preta-khaṇḍa — death, the soul's journey, and Yama-loka *(its signature section)*
- **Viṣṇu answers Garuḍa on what happens after death** — the section opens with Garuḍa asking Viṣṇu about the fate of the dying and the dead; Viṣṇu describes the **departure of the *jīva* (thumb-sized *aṅguṣṭha-mātra* soul)**, the seizure by the **Yamadūtas** (Yama's messengers), and the terrifying year-long road to **Yama-loka**, ~**86,000 *yojanas*** distant. *(Chronology: framework/eschatology — the timeless map of dying, applied especially to **Kali-yuga** mortals.)* Cross-link the death-and-karma doctrine: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md); Yama as a deity: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
- **Crossing the Vaitaraṇī river** — between earth and Yama-loka lies the dreadful **Vaitaraṇī**, a river of blood, pus and fearsome creatures; the *preta* crosses it only on the strength of merit and of a **cow gifted (*vaitaraṇī-dāna*) by the family**, which becomes the boat/bridge. *(Chronology: framework/eschatology.)*
- **The bhoga-deha built by piṇḍa-dāna** — the disembodied soul cannot make the journey in its subtle form; the **ten days of *piṇḍa-dāna* (rice-ball offerings)** by descendants knit a new **"travel body" (*bhoga-/yātana-deha*)**, limb by limb, in which the *preta* then walks to judgment — making the funerary rite *cosmologically necessary*, not merely pious. *(Chronology: framework/ritual.)* Cross-link *saṃskāra*/*śrāddha*: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md).
- **Judgment by Yama and Citragupta** — at Yama's city the scribe **Citragupta** reads the **complete ledger of deeds**, and Yama assigns *svarga* (heaven), one of the *narakas*, or rebirth. A milder counter-image of a **beautiful City of Yama** for the righteous balances the hell-vision. *(Chronology: framework.)*
- **The catalogue of *narakas* (hells)** — a graphic enumeration of hells and the **sins that send souls to each** (e.g. boiling oil, beds of spikes, devouring worms, the **Kumbhīpāka / Raurava / Tāmisra** type-hells), and the **diseases, poverty, and birth-defects** said to mark those *reborn* after hell as the karmic residue of specific sins — a doctrine of bodily affliction as fossilized karma. *(Chronology: framework/eschatology.)* Compare the older hell-and-judgment template in [`../../04-deep-dives/nachiketa-katha-upanishad.md`](../../04-deep-dives/nachiketa-katha-upanishad.md) (Naciketas before Yama) and the *Ajāmila* salvation case [`../../04-deep-dives/ajamila.md`](../../04-deep-dives/ajamila.md).
  - *Variant (Saroddhāra vs. Mahā-Purāṇa):* the most vivid, widely-quoted hell descriptions and the **"signs of a sinful death / symptoms in the dying"** lists circulate from the **Garuḍa-Purāṇa-Saroddhāra** (Naunidhirāma's digest), and differ in detail from the Preta-khaṇḍa of the printed Mahā-Purāṇa [scholarly] [late text]. Catalog both as "Garuḍa-Purāṇa tradition."
  - *Variant (hell-counts):* the number and names of *narakas* differ across Purāṇas (cf. the Bhāgavata's 28 hells, the Viṣṇu/Manu lists); the Garuḍa account is one node in that variant family. Cross-link: [`./vishnu-purana.md`](vishnu-purana.md) and [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).
- **Funeral procedure, *sapiṇḍīkaraṇa*, and the ghost-state** — concrete rules for **cremation, the eleven/twelve-day rites, *ekoddiṣṭa* and *sapiṇḍīkaraṇa*** (the rite merging the new *preta* into the body of the ancestors/*pitṛs*), the astrological timing of observances, and *dāna* (gifts of cow, bed, sesame, gold) — i.e. the liturgy that converts a wandering **preta into a settled *pitṛ***. *(Chronology: framework/ritual; performed in every age, emphasized for **Kali-yuga**.)*
  - *Variant (regional practice):* which chapters are recited and for how many days is governed by **regional and sampradāya custom**, not a single canonical prescription — the "thirteen-day reading" is convention, not text-internal command [disputed].

### Viṣṇu-bhakti, cosmology, and the Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa cosmos (Pūrva-khaṇḍa)
- **Creation from Viṣṇu and Lakṣmī** — the cosmogony makes the manifest world the fruit of **Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa's** union; with the standard *daśāvatāra*, *manvantara*, and *kalpa* scaffolding stated in compressed form. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — anchors the whole time-spine.)* Cross-link: [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/), [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md), and the manvantara spine [`../../01-manvantaras/README.md`](../../01-manvantaras/README.md).
- **Viṣṇu worship, vratas, stotras, and tīrthas** — *pūjā* manuals, the **Viṣṇu-/Garuḍa-/Gāyatrī** mantras, fasts (*ekādaśī* etc.), gift-rites (*dāna*), and pilgrimage *māhātmyas* — the everyday devotional apparatus of medieval Vaiṣṇavism. *(Chronology: framework/ritual.)* Cross-link sacred geography: [`../../06-sacred-geography/README.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/README.md).
- **Iconography and *vāstu*** — *pratimā-lakṣaṇa* (image canons for Viṣṇu, Śiva, Devī, Sūrya, the avatāras) and temple/house *vāstu*, on the same encyclopedic axis as the Agni Purāṇa. *(Chronology: framework/śāstra.)* Cross-link: [`./agni-purana.md`](agni-purana.md) and [`../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md`](../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md).

### Āyurveda — the *Dhanvantari-samhitā* layer (Pūrva-khaṇḍa, ~ch. 146–218)
- **A medical handbook inside the Purāṇa** — a substantial **āyurvedic stratum** (associated with **Dhanvantari**, the physician-avatāra): disease *nidāna* (pathology) and symptoms, **materia medica of 450+ plants/herbs**, formulary, and treatments — including **human, equine (*aśva*) and elephant (*gaja*) medicine** and even therapeutic uses of gems. *(Chronology: framework/upaveda — *āyurveda*.)* Cross-link: [`../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md`](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md); Dhanvantari's emergence at the churning: [`../../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md`](../../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md).

### Gemology — *ratna-parīkṣā* and the origin of the nine gems (Pūrva-khaṇḍa, ch. ~68–70)
- **Where gems come from, and how to test them** — one of the **classic Sanskrit sources on gemology**: the **origin of precious stones**, the testing of authenticity (color, water-immersion, fire/abrasion trials), grading, flaws, and the **astrological/medicinal virtues** of each stone. *(Chronology: framework/śāstra — *ratna-śāstra*.)* Cross-link the planetary correspondences: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md), and the parallel Agni-Purāṇa gem chapters [`./agni-purana.md`](agni-purana.md).
  - *Variant (the demon Bala/Vala myth):* the **origin-of-the-navaratna** story — the great Asura **Bala (Vala)**, sacrificed by the gods, whose **scattered body-parts became the gems** (blood→ruby, teeth→pearl, bile→emerald, eyes→sapphire, flesh→coral, skin→topaz, marrow→chrysoberyl, nails→cat's-eye, fat→rock-crystal) — is the celebrated *ratna* myth; it is best attested in **Buddha-bhaṭṭa's *Ratna-parīkṣā*** and the **Agni Purāṇa**, and runs in parallel with the Garuḍa gem section [scholarly]. Cross-link the *navaratna* and grahas: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md).

### Nīti, yoga, grammar, and lexicon (Pūrva-khaṇḍa)
- **Nīti-sāra (statecraft & ethics)** — a condensation of *rāja-dharma* and worldly wisdom (duties of the king, conduct, gnomic maxims) on the model of the *nīti* tradition. *(Chronology: framework/śāstra.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md).
- **Yoga / the "Brahma-gītā" and Dattātreya** — closing yoga material (*āsana*, *prāṇāyāma*, meditation, *mokṣa*), with **Dattātreya** invoked as a teacher of eight-limbed yoga — a soteriological coda to the encyclopedia. *(Chronology: framework/soteriology.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md) and [`../../04-deep-dives/dattatreya.md`](../../04-deep-dives/dattatreya.md).
- **Vyākaraṇa & kośa** — sections on **grammar** and a **synonym-lexicon**, plus *chandas*/poetics in some recensions — completing its claim to encyclopedic *sarva-vidyā* coverage. *(Chronology: framework/vedāṅga.)* Cross-link: [`../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md`](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md).
