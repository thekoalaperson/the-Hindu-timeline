# Bhaviṣya Purāṇa

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/puranas/` — a per-Purāṇa source file in the literature corpus.
> Up-link: [`../README.md`](../README.md) (the Purāṇas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (the literature corpus).
> See also the parent survey [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md) and the companion [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).

The **Bhaviṣya Purāṇa** ("Purāṇa of *what is to be*," from *bhaviṣya*, "the future") is the eighteen-Mahāpurāṇa whose self-advertised speciality is **prophecy** — Brahmā narrating *future* dynasties and events as though already history. In practice the extant text is a sprawling, loosely-stitched **ritual and festival handbook** (a *vrata*- and *dāna*-encyclopaedia) wrapped around two genuinely distinctive cores: (1) the **most comprehensive surviving treatment of Sun-worship (Saura) in Sanskrit literature**, including the legend of **Sāmba** and the immigrant **Maga / Bhojaka brāhmaṇas** from **Śākadvīpa**; and (2) a notorious **Pratisarga Parva** that "predicts" Adam and Eve, Noah, Moses, Jesus, Muhammad, Akbar, the Mughals, Timur, the British, and even **Queen Victoria** — a section virtually all scholars treat as **18th–19th-century fabrication** [interpolation][disputed]. It is, in Ludo Rocher's and Maurice Winternitz's view, the **most heavily and most recently interpolated of the Mahāpurāṇas**: its name made it a magnet for forgers who could insert any contemporary "prophecy" and claim ancient authority. Treat almost any specific named-historical-figure passage in this text as **suspect by default**.

- **Structure — four *parvans* (Bombay/Veṅkaṭeśvara recension, ~14,000 ślokas claimed; the *Matsya/Nārada* index promises far more verses than survive, itself a sign of loss/reconstruction):**
  - **Brahma Parva (~215 chapters):** the ritual heart — caste/āśrama duties, *saṃskāras*, festival *tithis*, worship of Brahmā, Gaṇeśa, Skanda, the **Nāgas**, and a **massive Sūrya / Sun-worship section** (the Sāmba and Maga-brāhmaṇa material), much of it lifted from the older **Sāmba Purāṇa** (an Upapurāṇa, c. 7th–8th c.).
  - **Madhyama Parva (~62 chapters, 3 sub-sections):** strongly **Tāntric** in flavour — generally judged a **late, separately-circulating accretion** [late text].
  - **Pratisarga Parva (~100 chapters, 4 *khaṇḍas* of ~7, 35, 32, 26):** the "future-history" section — cyclic re-telling of past yugas and dynasties bleeding into the **fabricated prophecies** of Mughal, British, and Biblical/Islamic figures [interpolation].
  - **Uttara Parva (~208 chapters):** an enormous catalogue of **vratas, festivals, dānas, and pilgrimage** keyed to lunar dates; circulates independently as the **"Bhaviṣyottara Purāṇa,"** quoted heavily by the medieval *nibandha* (dharma-digest) compilers (e.g. Hemādri, Ballālasena).
- **Pañca-lakṣaṇa coverage:** **poor and lopsided** — despite being a "Purāṇa," it largely abandons the classic five marks (*sarga, pratisarga, vaṃśa, manvantara, vaṃśānucarita*) in favour of *vrata*/*utsava*/*dāna* prescription; its *pratisarga* ("re-creation/recurring history") mark is hypertrophied into the prophetic Pratisarga Parva, while genuine cosmogony and genealogy are thin. It reads more as a **dharmaśāstra-adjacent ritual digest** than a narrative Purāṇa.
- **Sectarian leaning:** nominally classed among the **rājasa / Brahmā-associated** Purāṇas (a scheme scholars call "fanciful"), but its real centre of gravity is **Saura (Sun-worship)** in the Brahma Parva, with substantial **smārta** ritual material and a Tāntric layer in the Madhyama Parva. No sustained Vaiṣṇava/Śaiva polemic; its theology is diffuse.
- **Dating:** *traditional:* eternal, spoken by Brahmā, foretelling all ages. *Academic [scholarly]:* a **multi-layered text of radically different dates**. Pargiter (1912) used land-grant quotations to argue some Brahma-Parva material is early-CE; **Winternitz** doubted the method. The **Saura/Sāmba core** descends from the **Sāmba Purāṇa (c. 7th–8th c. CE)**. The **Pratisarga Parva is an 18th–19th-c. creation**: **Alf Hiltebeitel** sets its *terminus a quo* at the **mid-18th c.** (references to **Nādir Shāh** and **Muhammad Shāh**) and its *terminus ad quem* at the **mid/late-19th c.** (mentions of **Queen Victoria's palaces** and Calcutta). **R. C. Hazra** and **Ludo Rocher** likewise stress its late, accreted, and forged character. There is **no critical edition**; the printed *vulgate* is itself partly a colonial-era reconstruction.

## Notable / distinctive stories and sections

### Sun-worship core: Sāmba and the Maga / Bhojaka brāhmaṇas (Brahma Parva) — *the text's genuine treasure*
- **Sāmba's leprosy and his penance at the Sun-temple (Mitravana / Arka-kṣetra)** — **Sāmba**, son of **Kṛṣṇa** (by Jāmbavatī), is afflicted with **leprosy** — variously from a **curse of the sage Durvāsā** (or of **Nārada**, who tricked Kṛṣṇa into cursing his own son after a staged seduction-scandal among Kṛṣṇa's wives). Advised that only **Sūrya (Mitra)** can cure him, Sāmba performs a **twelve-year penance**, recovers an image of the Sun from the **Candrabhāgā (Chenab)** river, installs it, and is healed. *(Chronology: end of the **Dvāpara Yuga**, just after the Mahābhārata war, in the Yādava era — the Vaivasvata manvantara, current 28th mahāyuga.)* Cross-link: [`../../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md`](../../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md), the Sun among the planets [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md), and Sun-temple geography [`../../06-sacred-geography/README.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/README.md).
  - *Variant (Sāmba Purāṇa & Varāha Purāṇa):* the same cure-by-Sūrya legend appears in the older **Sāmba Purāṇa** (the Bhaviṣya borrows it) and in the **Varāha Purāṇa**; details of curser and locale differ.
  - *Variant (regional / Konārk tradition):* later Odia tradition localises Sāmba's penance and image at **Konārk (Arka-kṣetra)** and ties it to the **Sāmba Daśamī** festival; the Bhaviṣya's own setting is the **Multān / Mitravana** Sun-temple on the Candrabhāgā in the northwest [scholarly].
- **The importing of the eighteen Maga families from Śākadvīpa** — finding that **no local brāhmaṇa would touch (and thereby "pollute") the Sun-image** for ritual, Sāmba brings — via **Garuḍa** — **eighteen families of Maga (Magha) priests** from **Śākadvīpa** (understood as the land of the **Śakas/Scythians**, i.e. Iranian solar priests). These become the **Bhojaka / Sākaladvīpīya brāhmaṇas**, the hereditary Sun-temple officiants of north India. *(Chronology: end of the **Dvāpara Yuga**, Yādava era.)*
  - *Variant (historical reading [scholarly]):* scholars read this as a **myth encoding the actual immigration of Iranian (Zoroastrian-influenced) Magi/Mithra priests** into India in the early centuries CE — the Magas described as wearing the sacred girdle **avyaṅga (Avestan *aiwiiåŋhana* / kusti)** and reciting in a "mysterious" tongue; corroborated by **Varāhamihira's Bṛhat-Saṃhitā**, which names the Magas as the proper installers of Sun-images.

### The Pratisarga Parva — *the notorious "prophecies"* [interpolation][disputed]
> Flag: this entire parvan is the textbook example of Purāṇic forgery. Named-historical-figure passages here are **late insertions** designed to retroject modern history as ancient prophecy; do **not** treat any of the following as authentic early tradition.
- **The Biblical "Ādama" and "Havyavatī" (Adam and Eve)** — the Pratisarga Parva narrates a Genesis-derived cycle: **Ādhama and Havyavatī** in a paradise, **Nyūha (Noah)** and the flood, **Mūsā (Moses)**, etc., transparently lifted from the **Bible / Christian missionary material** and Sanskritised. *(Chronology: framework — pseudo-prophetic; **textually 18th–19th-c. fabrication**.)* [interpolation]
- **The Śālivāhana–Īśā (Jesus) meeting** — King **Śālivāhana** is said to encounter, in the Himalayas, a fair-skinned teacher who calls himself **Īśā-putra** ("son of God"), born of a virgin, preaching among the *mleccha* in the land of the **Huns** — a clear **Christian interpolation** (likely connected to 18th–19th-c. missionary activity). *(Chronology: framework — pseudo-prophetic; **late fabrication**.)* [interpolation][disputed]
- **"Mahāmada" (Muhammad)** — a passage casts **Muhammad** as a *piśāca*/demon-incarnation whose followers are called *musalmān*; polemical and unmistakably **post-Islamic late insertion**. *(Chronology: framework — pseudo-prophetic; **late fabrication**.)* [interpolation][disputed]
- **Mughal, Timurid, and British "prophecies"** — the parvan "foretells" the **Mughals (Mukula)**, **Timur (Timiralinga)**, **Akbar**, **Nādir Shāh**, **Muhammad Shāh**, the coming of the **English (the *Aṅgarāja* / "land of Viśva-bhū")**, **Calcutta**, and **Queen Victoria's palaces** — the very details Hiltebeitel uses to date the layer to the **mid-18th to late-19th c.** *(Chronology: framework — pseudo-prophetic; **18th–19th-c. fabrication**.)* Cross-link the boundary into documented time [`../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md`](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md). [interpolation]

### Vratas, festivals, and dāna (Uttara Parva = "Bhaviṣyottara," and Brahma Parva) — *the most-quoted layer in practice*
- **The great vrata / utsava calendar** — exhaustive prescriptions for **fasts and observances keyed to lunar tithis**, including **Nāga Pañcamī** (serpent-worship), **Bhīṣma Pañcaka**, sun- and Gaṇeśa-vratas, the **Saptamī Sun-vows** (e.g. *Ṣaṣṭhī/Saptamī* solar observances), and numerous *dāna* (gifting) rites. This material made the Bhaviṣya/Bhaviṣyottara a **primary quarry for the medieval dharma-nibandhas** (Hemādri's *Caturvarga-cintāmaṇi*, Ballālasena's *Dāna-sāgara*). *(Chronology: framework — recurring ritual time, applicable across the Kali Yuga.)* Cross-link festival/pilgrimage cycles [`../../06-sacred-geography/kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md).
  - *Variant (independent circulation):* the Uttara Parva travels separately as the **Bhaviṣyottara Purāṇa**, and many "Bhaviṣya" vrata-quotations in digests cannot be matched to the printed vulgate — evidence of **fluid, recension-specific contents** [scholarly].
- **Worship manuals for Brahmā, Sūrya, Gaṇeśa, Skanda, and the Nāgas** — image-installation (*pratiṣṭhā*), *pūjā* sequences, and festival days for these deities; the Sun-worship manual is the densest. *(Chronology: framework — ritual cosmology.)* Cross-link [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md).

### Madhyama Parva (Tāntric layer) [late text]
- **Tāntric rites and goddess material** — a self-contained block of **Tāntric worship**, *mantra*, and ritual judged a **late, loosely-attached accretion** with little narrative; minimal connection to the rest of the text. *(Chronology: framework — ritual; **late textual layer**.)* Cross-link the broader Tantra survey [`../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md`](../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md).
