# Nārada Purāṇa

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/puranas/` — one of the eighteen *Mahā-Purāṇas*.
> Up-link: [`../README.md`](../README.md) (puranas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (the literature corpus).
> See also the parent survey [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md) and the [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).

The **Nārada Purāṇa** (also **Nāradīya Purāṇa**) is a strongly **Vaiṣṇava** *Mahā-Purāṇa* named for and largely voiced by the divine sage **Nārada** — the wandering *bhakti*-propagator, *vīṇā*-player, and patron of music. It is, more than almost any other Purāṇa, a **devotional handbook and ritual encyclopedia**: a manual of **bhakti** to Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa, of **vrata** (vows, above all *Ekādaśī*), of **tīrtha** (pilgrimage, especially along the Gaṅgā), and of **saṅgīta** (music — *svara*, *rāga*, *tāla*). Its single most-cited distinction is that it **summarizes the contents of all eighteen Mahā-Purāṇas** (Pūrvabhāga ch. 92–109), earning it the nickname a **"Purāṇa of the Purāṇas"** / a Purāṇic *anukramaṇī* (table of contents). In the *tri-guṇa* classification (Pādma P. Uttara-khaṇḍa) it is counted among the **sāttvic** six (Viṣṇu-presiding, *mokṣa*-leading), though scholars regard that label as "entirely fanciful" for so heterogeneous a text [scholarly]. (See the guṇa scheme in [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md) and [`./padma-purana.md`](./padma-purana.md).)

## Structure — two *bhāgas*, with a famous embedded *carita*

- **Pūrvabhāga ("earlier part") — four *pādas*, 125 chapters total.** Opens with ~41 chapters on **bhakti to Viṣṇu** and Vaiṣṇava festivals/rites; then the *vrata*-and-*tīrtha* material, the **Vedāṅga survey (ch. 50–57)**, the philosophical/*adhyātma* sections, and the celebrated **Purāṇa-summaries (ch. 92–109)**.
- **Uttarabhāga ("later part") — 82 chapters.** More devotional and *māhātmya*-driven; it **embeds the *Rukmāṅgada-carita*** (the Ekādaśī-vrata legend, see below) and a long **Gaṅgā-tīrtha pilgrimage itinerary** (Haridvāra → Vārāṇasī → Bengal → Gayā → Nepāl).
- **Traditional size:** the canonical Mahā-Purāṇa lists (Matsya, etc.) assign the Nārada Purāṇa **25,000 ślokas** — a number larger than the surviving recensions, the usual signature of a stratified, accreted text [scholarly].
- **The Nārada / Bṛhan-nāradīya distinction [scholarly]:** there are effectively **two related texts** — the larger **Nārada (Nāradīya) Mahā-Purāṇa** (the two-*bhāga* text above) and a shorter **Bṛhan-nāradīya Purāṇa** (~38 *adhyāyas*, ~3,500 verses) almost wholly devoted to Viṣṇu-worship; scholars debate which is "primary" and treat them as a closely-linked recensional family.
- **Pañca-lakṣaṇa coverage [scholarly]:** very weak as a "five-mark" Purāṇa — *sarga/pratisarga/vaṃśa/manvantara/vaṃśānucarita* are thin or absent; the text is overwhelmingly a **māhātmya + vrata + bhakti + nibandha (digest)** compilation. This makes it a prime exhibit for the academic view that the *pañca-lakṣaṇa* definition fits few actual Purāṇas. Cross-link: [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md).
- **Sectarian leaning:** firmly **Vaiṣṇava** (Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa supreme; **Rādhā** named as *mūla-prakṛti*), yet broadly inclusive — it carries **Pāśupata-Śaiva** worship instructions and even **verses praising the Buddha** (Pūrvabhāga 1.2), unlike the Buddha-hostile tone of some Purāṇas [disputed].
- **Frame / narrators:** told in the standard **Sūta → Naimiṣa-sages** outer frame, with **Nārada** as the great interior authority; dialogues with **Sanaka/Sanandana** and other Kumāras, and the Rukmāṅgada material as a father–son (Rukmāṅgada–Dharmāṅgada) dialogue.
- **Dating:** *traditional:* an eternal Purāṇa revealed through Nārada, of Vyāsa's compilation. *Academic [scholarly]:* a **late and stratified** text — **H. H. Wilson** put it as late as the **16th–17th c.** (reading references he took as alluding to Islamic invasions); **R. C. Hazra** dated a **9th-c. core** for the Bṛhan-nāradīya with expansions before the **11th c.**; most modern scholars judge the precise date "fundamentally unclear," the work having "grown by numerous accretions." Often tagged **[late text]**.

## Notable / distinctive stories and sections

### The Purāṇa-summaries (Pūrvabhāga ch. 92–109) — *the signature feature*
- **Synopsis of all eighteen Mahā-Purāṇas** — eighteen successive chapters, one per Mahā-Purāṇa, summarizing each one's contents — the basis of the "Purāṇa of the Purāṇas" epithet and a key source for **how the Purāṇas were understood and ordered** in the medieval period. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — a meta-textual index, timeless.)* Cross-link the per-Purāṇa files in this folder: [`./vishnu-purana.md`](./vishnu-purana.md), [`./brahma-purana.md`](./brahma-purana.md), [`./padma-purana.md`](./padma-purana.md), and the survey [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md).
  - *Variant / caution (academic):* the summaries **differ markedly from the extant manuscripts** of the Purāṇas they describe — evidence that either the source-Purāṇas were later revised, or the summaries are themselves a later stratum, or both [scholarly]. They are therefore valuable but **not** a reliable mirror of today's printed texts.

### Rukmāṅgada, Mohinī, and Dharmāṅgada (Uttarabhāga — *Rukmāṅgada-carita*) — *the Ekādaśī legend*
- **King Rukmāṅgada's Ekādaśī-vrata and the test by Mohinī** — the pious Vaiṣṇava king **Rukmāṅgada** (of Vidiśā) so successfully enforces fasting on **Ekādaśī** across his realm that the sinful stop dying and **Yama's underworld empties**. Yama complains to **Brahmā**, who fashions the bewitching **Mohinī** to break the king's vow. Rukmāṅgada, smitten, marries her on her terms; for years he keeps both the marriage and his fast — until Mohinī forces the dilemma: abandon the Ekādaśī fast, or behead his own devoted son **Dharmāṅgada**. The boy consents to die for his father's *dharma*; as the sword falls, **Viṣṇu intervenes**, reveals the whole affair as a test, and bears Rukmāṅgada, queen **Sandhyāvalī**, and Dharmāṅgada to **Vaikuṇṭha**. The premier Purāṇic charter for the **supreme merit of the Ekādaśī vow** and the **bhakti-over-everything** ideal. *(Chronology: legendary antiquity — a *dharma-rāja* exemplar tale, not pinned to the spine's yuga sequence; framework/devotional.)* Cross-link the vrata/dharma material: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md); the Mohinī motif: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
  - *Variant (Mohinī figure):* here Mohinī is **Brahmā's enchantress-agent** sent to break a vow — distinct from the famous **Mohinī = Viṣṇu's female form** at the *samudra-manthana* (who distributes the *amṛta* and beguiles the asuras / Śiva). Catalog both as the "Mohinī" of different episodes. Cross-link the churning: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/).
  - *Variant (Rukmāṅgada elsewhere):* the name also attaches to other minor figures across the epics/Purāṇas; the Nārada Purāṇa's Vidiśā-king Ekādaśī hero is the canonical "Rukmāṅgada."

### Vrata and the cult of Ekādaśī (Pūrvabhāga + Uttarabhāga)
- **The vrata-encyclopedia** — extensive prescriptions for **fasts, vows, and observances**, with **Ekādaśī** as the crown vrata (each fortnight's eleventh tithi sacred to Viṣṇu), plus the named monthly/seasonal Ekādaśīs, **Janmāṣṭamī**, and other Vaiṣṇava festivals; functions as a practical *nibandha* (ritual digest). *(Chronology: framework — calendar-bound observances applicable in every yuga, esp. recommended for **Kali-yuga**.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md).

### Tīrtha and the Gaṅgā pilgrimage (Uttarabhāga)
- **The Gaṅgā-tīrtha māhātmya / itinerary** — a structured pilgrimage account running **Haridvāra → Prayāga/Vārāṇasī → Bengal → Gayā → Nepāl**, with the merits of bathing-sites and temples; a major source of medieval sacred geography. *(Chronology: framework — the sacred map of the present kalpa.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/README.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/README.md).

### Music and the arts (Pūrvabhāga, the Vedāṅga/Śikṣā section, ch. 50–57)
- **Saṅgīta-śāstra: svara, rāga, and tāla** — woven into its **Śikṣā (phonetics)** coverage, the text treats the **seven notes (sapta-svara)** of the **Sāma-veda**, classifies pitch as **nīca / madhya / ucca** (low/middle/high), and discusses **rāga, tāla, dance, and the recitation of sacred sound** — fittingly placed in a Purāṇa named for Nārada, mythic master of the *vīṇā*. One of the notable **early Purāṇic statements on Indian classical music**. *(Chronology: framework — śāstra, timeless.)*
- **The six Vedāṅgas surveyed (ch. 50–57)** — Śikṣā, Kalpa, Vyākaraṇa, Nirukta, Chandas, Jyotiṣa — a compact auxiliary-sciences digest. Cross-link: [`../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md`](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md).

### Philosophy, theology, and the goddess (Pūrvabhāga)
- **Bhakti, *adhyātma-jñāna*, and *mokṣa*** — discourses on devotion to Viṣṇu as the supreme path, renunciate/monastic philosophy, and liberation; the doctrinal core that earns the Vaiṣṇava label. *(Chronology: framework — soteriology, timeless.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md).
- **Rādhā as *mūla-prakṛti*** — the elevation of **Rādhā** to primordial creative essence alongside Kṛṣṇa, a marker of the text's **late, Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa-devotional stratum** [late text]. *(Chronology: framework/theology.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/).
- **Inclusive worship instructions** — *vidhis* for worshipping Viṣṇu, Śiva (incl. **Pāśupata** practice), and the Devī; the **Buddha-praising** verses (Pūrvabhāga 1.2) make it unusually accommodating of heterodoxy among the Purāṇas [disputed].
