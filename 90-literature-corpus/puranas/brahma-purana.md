# Brahma Purāṇa

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/puranas/` — a per-Purāṇa source file in the literature corpus.
> Up-link: [`../README.md`](../README.md) (the Purāṇas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (the literature corpus).
> See also the parent survey [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md) and the [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).

The **Brahma Purāṇa** is conventionally listed **first** among the eighteen *Mahāpurāṇas* in nearly every traditional anthology (Matsya, Bhāgavata, Viṣṇu lists), which earns it the honorific **Ādi Purāṇa** ("the First Purāṇa"). Despite the title and the framing — narrated by **Brahmā** himself (the *vaktā*, traditionally to **Marīci** and the sages / to **Dakṣa**) — it is **not** a Brahmā-sectarian text and is only loosely about Brahmā. It is also called the **Saura Purāṇa** ("Sun Purāṇa") for its heavy Sūrya/solar material. In the *tri-guṇa* scheme (Pādma P. Uttara-khaṇḍa) it is the lead text of the **rājasic** six, presided over by Brahmā and said to lead to *svarga* (see [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md)).

- **Structure** — the extant text divides into a **Pūrva-bhāga** (former part) and an **Uttara-bhāga** (latter part), with **~245–246 adhyāyas**. Traditional count is **10,000 ślokas**; surviving manuscripts hold roughly **7,000–8,000**, with an additional **~2,000–3,000** verses circulating as the appended **Brahmottara Purāṇa** supplement. There are no *khaṇḍas/saṃhitās/amśas* of the Viṣṇu-Purāṇa type; instead the bulk of the book is organized as **tīrtha-māhātmyas** (place-glorifications), the largest of which, the **Gautamī-māhātmya** (Godavari glory), runs ~105 chapters.
- **Pañca-lakṣaṇa coverage** — *technically* meets the five marks (*sarga, pratisarga, vaṃśa, manvantara, vaṃśānucarita*), but **thinly**: the cosmogony, Manvantara, and dynastic sections are short, largely **lifted verbatim** from other texts, and the genuine center of gravity is geography, pilgrimage merit (*tīrtha*), *vrata*, and *dāna*. Over **60%** of the chapters are devoted to holy sites — making it functionally a **tīrtha-guide** more than a *pañca-lakṣaṇa* narrative Purāṇa. (Cross-link the framework: [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/) and [`../../01-manvantaras/`](../../01-manvantaras/).)
- **Borrows heavily / compilatory character** — a defining feature: the extant Brahma Purāṇa is a **patchwork that quotes and paraphrases earlier texts** — large blocks from the **Mahābhārata**, **Viṣṇu Purāṇa**, **Vāyu Purāṇa**, **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa**, **Sāmba Purāṇa** (for the Sūrya material), and **Harivaṃśa** (for the Kṛṣṇa-līlā). Scholars (R. C. Hazra) hold that the surviving "Brahma Purāṇa" only **shares the name** of the lost original.
  - **Sectarian leaning** — *non-sectarian on the surface* (its tīrtha sections honor Viṣṇu, Śiva, Devī and Sūrya impartially), but the **Jagannātha/Puruṣottama (Vaiṣṇava) material is given disproportionate weight**, leading scholars to infer **Vaiṣṇava redactors** for the extant recension. One stratum also carries **anti-Dvaita / Śaiva-leaning polemic** ("Śiva is the Ātman") [disputed].
  - **Dating** — *traditional:* eternal, spoken by Brahmā at the dawn of creation. *Academic [scholarly]:* the kernel may be old, but the **extant compilation is late** — generally placed **c. 10th–13th century CE and later**, since it **describes the Koṇārka Sun Temple** (built **1241 CE**) and the **Jagannātha temple cult at Puri** (12th–13th c.), giving a firm *terminus post quem* for those layers; some sections reworked as late as the **13th–16th c.** [late text].

## Notable / distinctive stories and sections

### Cosmogony, Brahmā, and the framework
- **Creation by Brahmā (sarga)** — opens with Brahmā emerging from the cosmic egg / from Viṣṇu's navel-lotus and bringing forth the *Prajāpatis*, the Manus, and the worlds; a **condensed retelling** drawn largely from the Viṣṇu/Vāyu accounts. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — start of Brahmā's day.)* Cross-link: [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).
- **Manvantaras and the dynasties (vaṃśa / vaṃśānucarita)** — brief survey of the **14 Manus** and of the **Solar (Sūryavaṃśa)** and **Lunar (Candravaṃśa)** royal lines. *(Chronology: spans the manvantaras; the dynastic kings fall in the current **Vaivasvata manvantara**.)* Cross-link: [`../../03-lineages-vamsha/`](../../03-lineages-vamsha/).

### Daksha and the goddess
- **Dakṣa-prajāpati and his daughters** — Dakṣa's progeny and the marriage of his daughters to Kaśyapa, Soma, etc., as the seed of gods, demons, and creatures; standard Prajāpati genealogy. *(Chronology: framework — early secondary creation.)*
- **Pārvatī's tapas, the Śiva–Pārvatī marriage, and the Dakṣa-yajña** — the cycle of Satī, Dakṣa's insulting sacrifice, Satī's self-immolation, and the rupture between Dakṣa and Śiva, with Pārvatī's later austerity to win Śiva. The Brahma Purāṇa carries its own retelling. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine biography, timeless.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md).

### Markandeya
- **Mārkaṇḍeya and the vision of the pralaya (the cosmic flood)** — the deathless sage **Mārkaṇḍeya** survives the dissolution and beholds the infant Viṣṇu/Nārāyaṇa floating on the banyan leaf upon the cosmic waters, then is swallowed and sees the universe inside the Lord's body. The Brahma Purāṇa includes this *deluge-vision*. *(Chronology: at a **pralaya / mahāpralaya** — boundary of a kalpa or Brahmā's life; timeless cosmology.)* Cross-link: [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/) and the parallel in the [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md) (skandha 12).

### Sūrya / solar worship (the "Saura Purāṇa" core)
- **Sūrya-māhātmya and the origin of sun-worship** — extensive chapters on the Sun's glory, the **Sāmba legend** (Kṛṣṇa's son Sāmba, cursed with leprosy, cured by worshipping Sūrya and installing his image), and the importation of the **Maga/Bhojaka** sun-priests — material shared with the **Sāmba Purāṇa**. *(Chronology: the Sāmba episode falls in the **Dvāpara Yuga**, late in Kṛṣṇa's life; the worship-prescriptions are timeless.)*
  - *Variant (Sāmba Purāṇa / Bhaviṣya Purāṇa):* the same Sāmba-leprosy-and-Sun-cure legend grounds the founding of the **Multan / Mūlasthāna** and other Sūrya shrines; the Brahma Purāṇa localizes the solar cult especially to **Koṇārka (Konarak)**.
- **Koṇārka / Konark Sun Temple (Arka-kṣetra)** — describes the great **Sun Temple of Koṇārka** in Odisha and its pilgrimage merit. Because the temple was **built 1241 CE**, this section is a key **dating anchor** [late text]. *(Chronology: framework — present-Kali-Yuga sacred geography.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/`](../../06-sacred-geography/).

### Puruṣottama / Jagannātha (Puri) māhātmya
- **Puruṣottama-kṣetra / Jagannātha-māhātmya** — the **largest and most lavishly treated** sthala-section: the glory of **Puruṣottama-kṣetra (Puri)**, the legend of King **Indradyumna** who recovers the divine image, the **dāru-brahman** (the wood-form deity carved by Viśvakarmā/Anantamāyin), and the triad **Jagannātha–Balabhadra–Subhadrā**, plus the **Ratha-yātrā** and *snāna* rites. This Vaiṣṇava emphasis is the chief evidence of **Vaiṣṇava redaction**. *(Chronology: framework — present-Kali-Yuga tīrtha; the Indradyumna frame is set in legendary antiquity.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) (Puri = a Char-Dhām) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) (Jagannātha as Kṛṣṇa/Viṣṇu).
  - *Variant (Skanda Purāṇa, Utkala-khaṇḍa & Niḷādri-māhātmya):* the Indradyumna / dāru-vigraha origin of Jagannātha is told at greater length in the Skanda Purāṇa; details of the unfinished carving by the disguised artisan and the deities' "unfinished" eyes/limbs differ between recensions.

### Godavari / Gautamī tirtha-mahatmya (the largest block)
- **Gautamī-māhātmya (Godavari glory)** — the single biggest portion (~105 chapters): the descent of the **Gautamī Gaṅgā (Godavari)** through the sage **Gautama** (who, after the contrived "cow-killing" sin engineered by jealous ṛṣis, brings Gaṅgā down to the Deccan to expiate it), and a chapter-by-chapter catalog of the **tīrthas along the Godavari** with their merits, vows, and origin-legends. *(Chronology: the Gautama frame is set in a prior age; the geography is present-world.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/README.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/README.md).
  - *Note:* this section is **non-sectarian** — it celebrates Viṣṇu, Śiva, Devī, and Sūrya sites side by side, and is prized by scholars as a near-**"travel-guide"** snapshot of Deccan/Odisha sacred geography.

### Kṛṣṇa-līlā summary
- **Condensed Kṛṣṇa biography** — a compact retelling of the **Kṛṣṇa-carita**: birth at Mathurā, Gokula childhood and *bāla-līlā*, slaying of Kaṃsa, the Dvārakā years, and his role in the Mahābhārata era — drawn substantially from the **Harivaṃśa / Viṣṇu Purāṇa** rather than original to this text. *(Chronology: **Dvāpara Yuga**, 28th mahāyuga of the Vaivasvata manvantara — Kṛṣṇa's avatāra.)* Cross-link the fuller account: [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md) (skandhas 10–11) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata vs Brahma/Harivaṃśa):* the Brahma Purāṇa's Kṛṣṇa is closer to the **Harivaṃśa/Viṣṇu-Purāṇa** narrative skeleton and **lacks the elaborated *bhakti*-devotional and Rāsa-līlā expansion** that defines the Bhāgavata's Book 10.

### Eschatology and dharma
- **Naraka, karma, and the description of hells** — chapters on the **hells (narakas)**, sins and their punishments, and yamic judgment, used to motivate *dāna* and *vrata*. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — the *naraka-lokas*, timeless.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
- **Varṇāśrama-dharma, śrāddha, and vratas** — prescriptive sections on caste/āśrama duty, ancestral rites (*śrāddha*), and observances — typical encyclopedic Purāṇic dharma material. *(Chronology: framework — applicable across yugas.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md).
