# Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/puranas/` — a per-Purāṇa source file in the literature corpus.
> Up-link: [`../README.md`](../README.md) (the Purāṇas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (the literature corpus).
> See also the parent survey [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md) and the [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).

The **Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa** ("the Purāṇa of the *transformation/modification* — *vivarta* — of Brahman") is a thoroughly **Vaiṣṇava-Kṛṣṇaite** Mahāpurāṇa whose single governing thesis is the **absolute supremacy of Kṛṣṇa as Para-Brahman** and of his *śakti* **Rādhā** as the **mūla-prakṛti** (root nature). Despite the name invoking Brahmā, Brahmā here is a **derivative manifestation of Kṛṣṇa** acting under his guidance, and *all* gods — Viṣṇu, Śiva, Brahmā, Gaṇeśa, the goddesses — are folded back into Kṛṣṇa/Rādhā as their one source. It is narrated by **Sāvarṇi (Sūta)** to the sages at Naimiṣāraṇya, transmitting the teaching from **Nārāyaṇa → Nārada → Vyāsa**. In the *tri-guṇa* scheme (Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa) it is counted among the **rājasic** group presided over by Brahmā — a classification at odds with its overwhelmingly *sāttvic* Vaiṣṇava-bhakti content [disputed].

- **Structure — four *khaṇḍas*** (not *amśas/saṃhitās*), traditional total **~18,000 ślokas** across **~274–276 adhyāyas**:
  - **Brahma-khaṇḍa** (~30 chapters) — cosmogony with Kṛṣṇa as the supreme cause; Brahmā, the elements, and the worlds emanate from / under Kṛṣṇa.
  - **Prakṛti-khaṇḍa** (~67 chapters) — the goddess as *Prakṛti* and her fivefold and manifold manifestations (the *Pañca-devī-rūpa Prakṛti*).
  - **Gaṇeśa-khaṇḍa** (also *Gaṇapati-khaṇḍa*, ~46 chapters) — birth, beheading, broken-tusk, and exploits of Gaṇeśa.
  - **Kṛṣṇa-janma-khaṇḍa** (the largest, ~131–133 chapters) — Goloka, Rādhā, Kṛṣṇa's birth and *līlā*, painted with frank *śṛṅgāra/*erotic devotion.
- **Pañca-lakṣaṇa coverage** — *formally* touches the five marks (*sarga, pratisarga, vaṃśa, manvantara, vaṃśānucarita*) but **thinly and idiosyncratically**: the cosmogony (*sarga*) is recast as Kṛṣṇa-emanation, the genealogies are largely **divine/theological** (the descent of goddesses and *aṃśas* of Rādhā) rather than the standard solar/lunar *vaṃśānucarita*. Functionally it is a **bhakti-theology and goddess-origins compendium**, not a *pañca-lakṣaṇa* chronicle. (Cross-link the framework: [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).)
- **Sectarian leaning** — **Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa (Kṛṣṇaite Vaiṣṇava)**, with a strongly **Śākta-inflected** Prakṛti-khaṇḍa; it asserts an aggressively **non-dual hierarchy in which Kṛṣṇa/Rādhā subsume even Viṣṇu's Vaikuṇṭha** beneath their **Goloka**. Notable for a current of **honoring the feminine** — "every insult to a woman is an offence against the divine Rādhā," and all females are emanations of the one Goddess.
- **Dating** — *traditional:* eternal, Vyāsa-compiled. *Academic [scholarly]:* a kernel may date to **c. 700–1000 CE**, but the **extant text is a late Bengal composition/redaction of the 15th–16th century CE** [late text]; the rewrite was so thorough that **only ~30 of ~1,500 verses** quoted from a "Brahmavaivarta" by medieval *nibandha* scholars survive in today's manuscripts, evidence of **massive interpolation/recomposition** [interpolation]. Its mature Rādhā-Goloka theology is contemporaneous with, and influential upon, **Gauḍīya (Bengal) Vaiṣṇavism**.

## Notable / distinctive stories and sections

### Brahma-khaṇḍa — Kṛṣṇa as the supreme cause
- **Kṛṣṇa as Para-Brahman; creation from Goloka** - the universe issues from **Kṛṣṇa**, the one self-existent Lord, who first divides into male (Kṛṣṇa) and female (**Rādhā/Prakṛti**); Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and Śiva are produced as functionaries. The *vivarta* of the title = Brahman "appearing as" the world. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — pre-cosmic, timeless.)* Cross-link: [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md).
- **Goloka above Vaikuṇṭha** - the eternal **cow-realm Goloka**, Kṛṣṇa-Rādhā's abode, is placed **above Viṣṇu's Vaikuṇṭha**, inverting the usual Vaiṣṇava hierarchy. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — eternal *dhāman*.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
- **Origin of Surabhi and the sanctity of the cow** - the wish-cow **Surabhi/Kāmadhenu** emanates from Kṛṣṇa in Goloka, grounding cow-worship and the cowherd cosmology. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology.)*

### Prakṛti-khaṇḍa — the goddess as Prakṛti and her origins
- **The Pañca-devī-rūpa Prakṛti (fivefold Goddess)** - on Kṛṣṇa's will, **Prakṛti** divides into **five primary goddesses — Rādhā, Durgā, Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī, and Sāvitrī** (with Rādhā the highest), each assigned a cosmic office (Durgā = power/protection, Lakṣmī = wealth, Sarasvatī = knowledge, Sāvitrī = Veda/dharma, Rādhā = supreme love). All other goddesses are *aṃśas* of these. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine emanation.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/).
- **Origin of "minor" goddesses** - distinctive aetiologies for **Ṣaṣṭhī** (protectress of children), **Maṅgala-Caṇḍī**, **Manasā**, **Svāhā, Svadhā, Dakṣiṇā**, the river-goddesses, and others as named portions of Prakṛti — a key Śākta source for Bengali folk-goddess cults [late text]. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology.)*
- **Tulasī (Tulsī) and Śaṅkhacūḍa** - the celebrated **Tulasī legend**: the chaste **Tulasī** (an emanation of Rādhā / former gopī) is wife of the *asura* **Śaṅkhacūḍa** (a portion of the Goloka attendant Sudāman, cursed to demonhood). Viṣṇu, to enable Śaṅkhacūḍa's slaying by Śiva, **deceives Tulasī by assuming her husband's form**, breaking her *pati-vrata* shield; the enraged Tulasī curses Viṣṇu into the **śālagrāma stone**, and is herself transformed into the sacred **tulasī plant** and the river **Gaṇḍakī**, thereafter eternally united with Viṣṇu/śālagrāma. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine biography; the demon-war is "timeless" celestial history.)*
  - *Variant (Padma Purāṇa / Devī-Bhāgavata):* the Tulasī–Śaṅkhacūḍa–śālagrāma cycle is also told there with shifted detail (Tulasī as Vṛndā, wife of the *asura* Jalandhara, deceived by Viṣṇu); the Brahmavaivarta names the demon **Śaṅkhacūḍa** and frames the players as fallen Goloka residents.
- **Sāvitrī, Satyavān, and the dialogue with Yama** - an expanded retelling of **Sāvitrī**, who follows **Yama** as he carries off her husband **Satyavān's** soul and wins him back by her wit and virtue; the Brahmavaivarta embeds a long **Sāvitrī–Yama discourse** on *dharma*, hells (*naraka*), *karma*, and the fate of the soul. *(Chronology: legendary antiquity — set in a prior age; the eschatological discourse is timeless.)* Cross-link the Mahābhārata source: [`../smriti-itihasa-epics.md`](../smriti-itihasa-epics.md); and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) (Yama / narakas).
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata, Vana-parva):* the classical Sāvitrī-upākhyāna is shorter and lacks the Brahmavaivarta's elaborate naraka-catalogue, which is a **late devotional expansion** [late text].
- **Origins of Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī, Gaṅgā** and the **Sarasvatī–Gaṅgā–Lakṣmī co-wife quarrel** - the three consorts of Viṣṇu quarrel and curse one another, explaining why Gaṅgā descends to earth and Sarasvatī becomes a river — a distinctive river-goddess aetiology. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/`](../../06-sacred-geography/).

### Gaṇeśa-khaṇḍa — birth, beheading, and the broken tusk
- **Birth and beheading of Gaṇeśa** - Pārvatī obtains a son through the **Puṇyaka-vrata**; at the infant's naming-feast the gods come to admire him. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine biography, timeless; one of the few Mahāpurāṇas with a whole *khaṇḍa* for Gaṇeśa.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/).
  - *Variant — the Śani (Saturn) gaze [distinctive to this text]:* **Śani**, warned that his glance is destructive, is pressed by Pārvatī to look at the child; his **inauspicious gaze severs/burns the infant's head**. **Viṣṇu** then mounts Garuḍa, finds and brings the head of a **young elephant** (in some tellings the calf of **Airāvata**, born of the ocean-churning), and fixes it on, restoring the boy with the elephant head. This **Śani-causes-the-beheading** version is the signature Brahmavaivarta account, contrasting the more familiar **Śiva-decapitation** story.
  - *Variant (Śiva Purāṇa):* Pārvatī fashions Gaṇeśa from her own bodily unguent to guard her door; **Śiva**, refused entry, beheads him, then replaces the head with an elephant's. (Cross-link [`shiva-purana.md`](shiva-purana.md).)
- **The broken tusk and the name Ekadanta** - the loss of Gaṇeśa's **left tusk**. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine biography.)*
  - *Variant — Paraśurāma fight [Brahmavaivarta]:* **Paraśurāma**, come to see Śiva, is barred at the gate by Gaṇeśa; they fight, and Paraśurāma hurls **Śiva's own *paraśu* (axe)**; Gaṇeśa, refusing to nullify a weapon of his father, takes the blow and **loses his left tusk**, becoming **Ekadanta**.
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata / scribal tradition):* Gaṇeśa **snaps off his own tusk to use as a stylus** to write the Mahābhārata at Vyāsa's dictation. (Cross-link [`../smriti-itihasa-epics.md`](../smriti-itihasa-epics.md).)
  - *Variant (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa & others):* the tusk is broken in a fight with the *asura* **Gajamukha**, or thrown at the **moon** for mocking him.
- **Gaṇeśa as a portion of Kṛṣṇa** - consistent with the text's thesis, Gaṇeśa is theologized as an *aṃśa*/manifestation of Kṛṣṇa, and given **two wives, Siddhi and Buddhi** (and sons Kṣema/Lābha). *(Chronology: framework/cosmology.)*

### Kṛṣṇa-janma-khaṇḍa — Rādhā, Goloka, and the līlā (the largest section)
- **Rādhā as supreme Goddess** - **Rādhā** is exalted as the **highest divinity**, inseparable from Kṛṣṇa "as body to soul," the *mūla-prakṛti* from whom all forms evolve; her worship and her thousand names are given. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — eternal in Goloka; manifest in the Dvāpara *līlā*.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) (Kṛṣṇa) and [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md) (skandha 10).
- **The eternal Rāsa and the descent to Vraja** - the **Rāsa-līlā** is presented first as an **eternal event in Goloka** between Kṛṣṇa and Rādhā/the gopīs, then re-enacted on earth at **Vṛndāvana**; the text's *śṛṅgāra* (erotic-devotional) treatment is more explicit than the Bhāgavata's. *(Chronology: eternal in Goloka; on earth in the **Dvāpara Yuga**, 28th mahāyuga of the **Vaivasvata manvantara**.)* Cross-link the earthly *līlā*: [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md) (skandha 10) and [`../../01-manvantaras/`](../../01-manvantaras/) for the Dvāpara placement.
  - *Variant (vs Bhāgavata):* the Bhāgavata's Book-10 Rāsa does **not name Rādhā**; the Brahmavaivarta makes **Rādhā the named, supreme partner and even Kṛṣṇa's eternal wedded consort in Goloka** — a hallmark of the later Rādhā theology [late text].
- **Kṛṣṇa's birth and Vraja *bāla-līlā*** - retelling of the Mathurā birth, the move to Gokula/Vṛndāvana, the childhood marvels, demon-slayings, and the Kaṃsa cycle, framed throughout by Rādhā-bhakti. *(Chronology: **Dvāpara Yuga**.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Curse-and-fall origin myths (Goloka exiles)** - many earthly figures are explained as **cursed/fallen Goloka attendants** (e.g., Sudāman → Śaṅkhacūḍa; gopīs and demons as descended *pārṣadas*), tying the whole cosmos back to the Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa court. *(Chronology: framework → working out in the Dvāpara *līlā*.)*
- **Theology of bhakti, japa, and the divine feminine** - hymns (*stotras*), *mantra*-instruction, and the doctrine that **all goddesses are Rādhā** and that honoring women is honoring the Goddess. *(Chronology: framework — applicable across yugas.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/).
