# The 18 Upapurāṇas — Overview & Per-Text Notes

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/puranas/` — survey of the **eighteen Upa-Purāṇas** ("secondary Purāṇas"), the lesser canon paralleling the 18 Mahāpurāṇas.
> Up-link: [`../README.md`](../README.md) (puranas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (literature corpus).
> Parent survey: [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md) (verse-counts, guṇa scheme, list variants). Sibling files: the **Gāṇapatya pair** [`ganesha-and-mudgala-upapuranas.md`](ganesha-and-mudgala-upapuranas.md); the **Devī-Bhāgavata** in [`devi-bhagavata-upapurana.md`](devi-bhagavata-upapurana.md) and the dispute in [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md); the **Viṣṇudharmottara** in [`vishnudharmottara-purana.md`](vishnudharmottara-purana.md); [`markandeya-purana.md`](markandeya-purana.md) (Devī Māhātmya).

The **Upapurāṇas** ("subsidiary / secondary Purāṇas") are a second canon of **eighteen** texts that mirrors the 18 Mahāpurāṇas in form (sage-to-sage dialogue frame, *pañca-lakṣaṇa* pretensions) but ranks below them in authority and is far more **sectarian, ritual-technical, and regional** in character. The Purāṇas do not uniformly credit them to **Vyāsa**: the Kūrma says the eighteen Purāṇas were told by Vyāsa and "the other, Upa-purāṇas" by his disciples (Kūrma 1.11.279–280), or by sages after hearing the eighteen in brief (Kūrma 1.1.16; Garuḍa 1.223.17); the Matsya calls them *upabhedas* ("sub-divisions") that "issued from" the eighteen (Matsya 53.60, 53.64); the Revā-khaṇḍa says they were "spoken by Vedhas (Brahmā)" (1.46). Unlike the Mahāpurāṇa lists — which are essentially stable — **the lists of 18 Upapurāṇas rarely agree**; Hazra calls them "greatly divergent" and collected the names of **"more than one hundred"** Upapurāṇas. They are the workhorses of **sect formation**: Śaiva ritual (Śivadharma), Śākta tantra (Kālikā), Saura sun-worship (Sāmba), Gāṇapatya theology (Gaṇeśa, Mudgala — own file), and Vaiṣṇava avatāra-lore (Nārasiṃha). Most are weak on the full *pañca-lakṣaṇa*; their academic dates run from the 5th century CE (Nārasiṃha, Āditya) to medieval additions as late as c. 1500 (parts of the Sāmba). The foundational academic study is **R. C. Hazra, *Studies in the Upapurāṇas*** (vol. I Saura & Vaiṣṇava, 1958; vol. II Śākta & non-sectarian, 1963) **[scholarly]**.

This file gives **per-text notes** on the eighteen and catalogs the **enumeration variants**. For the two **Gāṇapatya** upapurāṇas and the **Devī-Bhāgavata** (treated as Mahā- vs. Upa- in different traditions) see the cross-links above.

## The enumeration is not fixed — list variants

The "18" is a target number, not a closed set. Different sources name different texts; the same name can mean different works (Hazra treats a *Brahmāṇḍa-*, *Kūrma-*, *Garuḍa-*, *Vāmana-* and *Nāradīya-upapurāṇa* apart from the Mahāpurāṇas of those names); and several texts float between the Mahā- and Upa- canons. Hazra assembles some twenty-three lists (numbered I–XXIII); the main ones are below.

- ***Variant — the Kūrma list (Hazra's basic list):*** Kūrma 1.1.17–20: **Sanatkumāra** ("spoken by Sanatkumāra"), **Nārasiṃha**, **Skānda** ("spoken by Kumāra"), **Śivadharma** ("spoken by Nandīśa himself"), **Daurvāsasa**, **Nāradokta** ("spoken by Nārada"), **Kāpila, Mānava, Auśanasa, Brahmāṇḍa, Vāruṇa, Kālikā, Māheśvara, Sāmba, Saura, Pārāśara, Mārīca, Bhārgava.** (The eighth seat is *mānavaṃ* in the GRETIL text; Hazra's printed Kūrma editions, Veṅkaṭeśvara and Asiatic Society, read *vāmanaṃ*, while the Kūrma verses as quoted by Hemādri and Mitra Miśra read Mānava.) Hazra treats this as the standard group and dates the list "not later than 850 A.D."; he places the formation of the group of eighteen c. 650–800 CE. **[scholarly]**
  - *Variant (Garuḍa 1.223.17–20):* the same roster almost word for word (Nandīśvara for Nandīśa), with **Vāmana** in the eighth seat (as in Hazra's Kūrma editions), and the GRETIL text has no separate *Saura* (it reads *sāmbam evaṃ sarvārthasañcayam* where the Kūrma reads *sāmbaṃ sauraṃ sarvārthasaṃcayam*), which leaves seventeen; Hazra emends to *sauraṃ*.
- ***Variant — the Devī-Bhāgavata list:*** Devī-Bhāgavata 1.3.12–17 (Vijñānānanda's numbering): **Sanatkumāra, Nārasiṃha, Nāradīya, Śiva, Daurvāsasa, Kāpila, Mānava, Auśanasa, Vāruṇa, Kālikā, Sāmba, Nandikeśvara, Saura, Pārāśara, Āditya, Māheśvara, Bhāgavata, Vāsiṣṭha.** This is the list Wilson reports, and the per-text notes below follow it loosely. Against the Kūrma it drops Skānda, Brahmāṇḍa, Mārīca and Bhārgava and adds Nandikeśvara, Āditya, Bhāgavata and Vāsiṣṭha.
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata vs. Bhārgava):* **Wilson** held that "in any authentic list" the name Bhāgavata does not occur among the Upapurāṇas, and that the true reading is **Bhārgava** (the Purāṇa of Bhṛgu) — which the Kūrma and Garuḍa lists do in fact name. **Hazra** does not adopt the emendation: he reads the Devī-Bhāgavata's "Bhāgavata" as the Vaiṣṇava Bhāgavata (the Devī-Bhāgavata claiming that title for itself), and says the "Bhāgavata" of the Revā lists is a different, unidentified work. Catalog both. (Mahā- vs. Upa- dispute: [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md).)
- ***Variant — the Revā-khaṇḍa list:*** Revā-khaṇḍa 1.46–53 (GRETIL; printed in the Veṅkaṭeśvara *Skanda*, though GRETIL's contributor notes that this Revā-khaṇḍa belongs to the Vāyu and was misassigned) attaches the first six Upapurāṇas to Mahāpurāṇas: first the **Saura** "of the Brahma Purāṇa", in two *saṃhitās* and "known by Sanatkumāra's name" (1.47–48); **Nārasiṃha** "in the Padma"; **Śaukeya** "in the Viṣṇu"; **Bārhaspatya** "in the Vāyu"; **Daurvāsasa** "in the Bhāgavata"; **the one told by Nārada, in the Bhaviṣya**; then Kāpila, Mānava, Auśanasa, Brahmāṇḍa, Vāruṇa, **"the two Kālikās"** (*kālikādvayam*, where the Kūrma and Garuḍa read *kālikāhvayam*, "the one named Kālikā" — cf. Hazra's two-Kālikā thesis below), Māheśvara, Sāmba, Saura, Pārāśara, **Bhāgavata**, **Kaurma**. The passage introduces them as Upapurāṇas "spoken by Vedhas (Brahmā)" (1.46).
  - *Variant (reading of seats 3–4):* **Wilson** read these as **Nandā** and **Śivadharma**, and Hazra's Revā-māhātmya list also puts the Śivadharma under the Vāyu; the GRETIL text (and Hazra's Revā-khaṇḍa list) reads *śaukeyaṃ* and *bārhaspatyaṃ*. Hazra takes the Revā's "Śaukeya" to be a name of the Nandi-purāṇa (which also appears as "Vāyavīya" in Raghunandana's reading of the Kūrma's third seat), though he cannot say how it got these names. Wilson also took the sixth as "Bhaviṣya, related by Nārada"; the passage's pattern (Upa- text, then the Mahāpurāṇa it belongs to) reads it as the Nārada-told Upapurāṇa attached to the Bhaviṣya.
- ***Variant — the Matsya's four:*** Matsya 53.60–63 names only four *upabhedas*: the **Nārasiṃha** (the Narasiṃha narrative told in the Padma, 18,000 verses), the **Nandī-purāṇa** (where **Kārttikeya** tells the glory of **Nandā**), the **Sāmba**, and the **Āditya**. Hazra, who thinks these verses spurious but not very late, uses them to fix the earliest date for the formation of the group of eighteen, and as evidence that some Upapurāṇas are older than the group.
- ***Variant — other Purāṇic lists (per Hazra):*** the **Padma (Pātāla-khaṇḍa)** list (Ādya/Sanatkumāra, Nārasiṃha, "Anda" (Hazra queries "Skanda?"), Durvāsa, "another Nāradīya", Kāpila, Mānava, Auśanasa, "another Brahmāṇḍa", Vāruṇa, Kālikā, Maheśa, Sāmba, Saura, Pārāśara, Mārīca, Bhārgava, Kaumāra); the **Bṛhaddharma** (1.25.23–26), which counts itself and the Viṣṇudharmottara, Viṣṇudharma, Kriyāyogasāra, Ādi, Āditya, Bṛhannāradīya and two Nandīśvaras; the **Ekāmra** (1.20b–23), almost wholly different (Bṛhannarasiṃha, Devī, Kālikā, Ekāmra, Ekapāda, Mṛtyuñjaya, Laghu-bhāgavata…); and the **Vāruṇa-upapurāṇa** ch. 1, which opens with "the Ādya, told by Kumāra" and includes a **Vasiṣṭha-laiṅga**.
- ***Variant — the Garuḍa's guṇa-sorted list [late text]:*** the third part of the Garuḍa (the Brahma-kāṇḍa in GRETIL's heading, 3.1.57–63) sorts a different "eighteen" by guṇa: *sāttvika* — Viṣṇudharmottara, Bhāgavata-tantra (*tantraṃ bhāgavataṃ*), Tattvasāra, Nārasiṃha, Vāyuprokta, Haṃsa; *rājasa* — Bhaviṣyottara, Bṛhannārada, Yama-Nārada-saṃvāda, Laghunārada, Vināyaka, Bṛhadbrahmāṇḍa; *tāmasa* — *purāṇaṃ bhāgavataṃ śaivaṃ* (either two works, a "Bhāgavata" and a "Śaiva", or one "Śaiva Bhāgavata"; the text praises "the Bhāgavata" as best of all Purāṇas two verses later, 3.1.64), Nandiprokta, Pāśupata, Raiṇuka, Bhairava.
- ***Variant — digest lists:*** medieval *nibandhas* reproduce or adapt the lists — Hemādri's *Caturvargacintāmaṇi* and Raghunandana's *Malamāsa-tattva* quote variants of the Kūrma list; Ballālasena's *Dānasāgara* refers to the lists of the Kūrma and the Ādi-purāṇa — and the digests quote many Upapurāṇas now lost (Hazra reconstructs the Nandi-purāṇa wholly from such quotations).
- ***Variant — the modern "Major Upapurāṇas" table:*** the Wikipedia table (drawn from Hazra's 1962 essay in *The Cultural Heritage of India*) keeps Nāradīya but has **Mudgala, Gaṇeśa, Viṣṇudharmottara, Bhārgava** where the Devī-Bhāgavata list has Daurvāsasa, Āditya, Bhāgavata and Vāsiṣṭha.
- ***Variant — floaters:*** the **Devī-Bhāgavata** (claimed as the true Mahāpurāṇa "Bhāgavata" by Śākta tradition, which reads Matsya 53.20–21 — the Bhāgavata "based on the Gāyatrī" and containing "the slaying of Vṛtra" — as describing it; classed as a late Upapurāṇa by Hazra; it is *not* named in the Kūrma, Garuḍa or Padma lists), **Kalki**, **Viṣṇudharmottara**, **Bṛhaddharma**, **Gaṇeśa/Mudgala**, and **Sūrya/Saura** vs. **Sāmba** all migrate between rosters. **[disputed]**

## Per-text notes (the standard eighteen)

### Vaiṣṇava / avatāra-leaning
- **Sanatkumāra Purāṇa** - First in the Kūrma, Garuḍa, Devī-Bhāgavata, Revā, Padma and Vāruṇa lists ("spoken by Sanatkumāra", Kūrma 1.1.17; "the Ādya, told by Kumāra", Vāruṇa list), though not in the Bṛhaddharma or Ekāmra lists. The Revā-khaṇḍa (1.47–48) puts in this first seat "the excellent **Saura** of the Brahma Purāṇa", a **Śaiva** text in two *saṃhitās*, the first told by Sanatkumāra and the second by Sūrya, "known by Sanatkumāra's name" — the description the extant **Saura Purāṇa** gives of itself (see Saura below) — so its identity is uncertain. The Wikipedia table (after Hazra 1962) lists a published Sanatkumāra of 19 chapters. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — Sanatkumāra is a primordial, ageless ṛṣi, one of Brahmā's mind-born Kumāras.)* On the Kumāras: [`../../04-deep-dives/the-four-kumaras.md`](../../04-deep-dives/the-four-kumaras.md), [`../../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md`](../../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md). **[disputed identity]**
- **Nārasiṃha (Nṛsiṃha) Purāṇa** - **68 chapters** (Jena ed., GRETIL). Frame: sages gathered at **Prayāga** in the month of Māgha meet **Bharadvāja**, who asks the *sūta* **Lomaharṣaṇa**, Vyāsa's disciple, for the Nārasiṃha after having heard the Vārāha from him (1.4–16). Strongly Vaiṣṇava, but a full Purāṇa rather than a single-episode text: the **Narasiṃha avatāra, Hiraṇyakaśipu and Prahlāda** occupy chiefly chs. 40–44; it also carries a **Mārkaṇḍeya** cycle (chs. 7–11), royal genealogies, and a **Rāma** narrative (chs. 47–52). *(Chronology: the Narasiṃha episode is set in a primordial cycle, narratively early — cross-link the avatāra spine [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) and the story [`../../04-deep-dives/prahlada-narasimha.md`](../../04-deep-dives/prahlada-narasimha.md).)*
  - *Dating [scholarly]:* **Hazra** — "between 400 and 500 A.D.", probably the latter half of the 5th century; he calls it "one of the oldest and most important of the extant Vaiṣṇava Upapurāṇas". Manuscripts vary from 53 to 67 chapters.
  - *Variant (Matsya 53.60):* the Matsya's Nārasiṃha is "where the account of Narasiṃha is given in the Padma Purāṇa", 18,000 verses.
  - *Variant:* not to be confused with the **Nṛsiṃha-tāpanīya Upaniṣad** (same deity, different genre).
- **Bṛhannāradīya / Nāradīya (Upa-)** - The *minor* Nārada text (distinct from the Mahāpurāṇa **Nārada/Nāradīya**, which has its own file [`narada-purana.md`](narada-purana.md)); the Kūrma lists it simply as "the one spoken by Nārada" (1.1.18). The printed **Bṛhannāradīya** (Asiatic Society, Calcutta 1891, and Vaṅgavāsī editions) has **38 chapters**: the sages of the Naimiṣa forest go to the *sūta* at Siddhāśrama, and within that **Nārada** speaks to **Sanatkumāra** on Meru. Vaiṣṇava throughout (Viṣṇu-Nārāyaṇa, *vrata*, *varṇāśrama*, the glories of Hari), while stressing the unity of Śiva and Viṣṇu; it attacks Buddhists and Tantrikas as *pāṣaṇḍa*. *(Chronology: framework/devotional.)*
  - *Dating [scholarly]:* **Hazra** — c. **750–900 CE**, written by someone from the Narmadā or Vārāṇasī region who had migrated to the eastern part of Orissa or the western part of Bengal.
  - *Variant (Revā-khaṇḍa):* the Nārada-told Upapurāṇa is attached to the **Bhaviṣya** (1.51) — see the list variants above and [`bhavishya-purana.md`](bhavishya-purana.md).
- **Kāpila Purāṇa** - Ascribed to the sage **Kapila**. The printed text (critical ed. S. Tripathi, Varanasi 1981) has **21 chapters**, mostly glorifying the sacred sites of **Utkala (Odisha)** — Puruṣottama-kṣetra (Puri), Virajā-kṣetra, Maitreya-vana (identified with Konark) and Ekāmra (Bhubaneswar) — with Śiva, Durgā, Viṣṇu and Sūrya shrines, and a closing chapter on *jñāna-yoga*. Kapila narrates to king **Śalyajit** a dialogue between Bharadvāja and sages at Puṣkara (Rocher 1986, via Wikipedia). Wikipedia dates it c. 11th c. *(Chronology: framework — Kapila as avatāra of Viṣṇu and founder of Sāṃkhya; cross-link [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).)* It is a regional *tīrtha-māhātmya* rather than a Sāṃkhya treatise or a purely Vaiṣṇava work. **[regional]**
- **Mānava Purāṇa** - Ascribed to **Manu**; in the Kūrma (GRETIL text), Devī-Bhāgavata, Revā and Padma lists (the Garuḍa, and the Kūrma editions Hazra used, have Vāmana in this seat). No manuscript is reported (Wikipedia table after Hazra 1962), and Hazra's *Studies* gives it no separate study. *(Chronology: a Manu-narrated text — frame keyed to a manvantara; see [`../../01-manvantaras/README.md`](../../01-manvantaras/README.md).)* **[largely lost]**
- **Bhārgava Purāṇa** - Ascribed to the **Bhṛgu** lineage. It is not only Wilson's emendation of the disputed "Bhāgavata": the Kūrma (1.1.20), Garuḍa (1.223.20), Padma, Bṛhaddharma and Vāruṇa lists name a **Bhārgava** outright. The Wikipedia table (after Hazra 1962) lists a published Bhārgava of 40 chapters narrated by **Vasiṣṭha** (not checked against the text). *(Chronology: framework.)* **[disputed identity]**

### Śaiva
- **Śivadharma Purāṇa** - The Kūrma (1.1.18) and Garuḍa (1.223.18) list a **Śivadharma** "spoken by Nandīśa / Nandīśvara"; the Devī-Bhāgavata list has simply **Śiva**. The surviving **Śivadharmaśāstra** is a manual for **Śaiva laity** — *liṅga*-worship, vows, gifts, and the devotee's duties, rather than narrative myth — and one of the two earliest texts (with the Śivadharmottara) of a lay Śaiva corpus; these two also have an independent transmission in India, while the wider corpus survives as a fixed set in Nepalese multiple-text manuscripts (De Simini 2016, abstract). The content summary here has not been checked against the Śivadharmaśāstra itself. *(Chronology: framework/ritual — timeless.)* Cross-link the liṅga origin: [`linga-purana.md`](linga-purana.md), [`shiva-purana.md`](shiva-purana.md).
  - *Variant (extent):* the Wikipedia table gives **24 chapters**; De Simini (2016) describes the Śivadharmaśāstra and the Śivadharmottara as **twelve chapters each** — the 24 may count the pair. **[scholarly]**
  - *Variant (Wilson's "Śiva Upa-purāṇa"):* Wilson saw a Śiva Upa-purāṇa of about 6,000 stanzas in two parts, narrated by **Sanatkumāra** to Vyāsa and the sages at Naimiṣa, which he called "a Śaiva manual, not a Purāṇa"; whether it is the Śivadharma of the lists is not established.
- **Nandi / Nandā / Nandikeśvara Purāṇa** - The Matsya (53.61) describes the **Nandī-purāṇa** as the work "in which **Kārttikeya** recounts the glory of **Nandā**"; the Devī-Bhāgavata list names a **Nandikeśvara** Purāṇa, and the Kūrma/Garuḍa give the Śivadharma to **Nandīśa** (Śiva's gaṇa-chief). **Hazra** finds no manuscript and reconstructs the Nandi-purāṇa from quotations in the digests (*Kṛtyakalpataru*, *Dānasāgara*, *Caturvargacintāmaṇi*), mostly on gifts, *śrāddha* and the gift of learning; he identifies Nandā with **Gaurī**, takes the work to have been "originally a Śākta work dealing with the praise of Nandā" that later gained Śaiva and finally Vaiṣṇava material, and dates it to the **6th or 7th c. CE**. *(Chronology: framework.)* Cross-link Skanda/Kārttikeya: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md). **[largely lost]**
  - *Variant (Nandi vs. Nandikeśvara):* the lists use the names interchangeably, and the Bṛhaddharma and Ekāmra lists name a Nandīśvara/Nandikeśvara and a Bṛhan-nandīśvara/Bṛhan-nandi side by side; **Hazra** holds the Nandi-purāṇa and the Nandikeśvara-purāṇa to be **distinct** works, as separate digest citations show.
  - *Variant (sect):* placed here with the Śaiva texts by name; Hazra classes the Nandi-purāṇa among the **lost Śākta** Upapurāṇas.
- **Māheśvara Purāṇa** - In the Kūrma, Garuḍa, Devī-Bhāgavata and Revā lists. The Wikipedia table (after Hazra 1962) gives 12 chapters, unpublished, on Śiva, Pārvatī's family and the deities. **Hazra** distinguishes a *Maheśa-purāṇa* (named in only two lists; before 1200 CE) from the **Vāsiṣṭha-laiṅga**, which he thinks "was known as Vasiṣṭha also and perhaps as Maheśvara too". *(Chronology: framework.)* **[unpublished / obscure]**
- **Saura Purāṇa** - The printed text has **69 chapters**. It calls itself a **supplement (*khila*) to the Brahma Purāṇa**, in two *saṃhitās*, the first told by Sanatkumāra and the second by **Sūrya** to Vaivasvata Manu (Winternitz and Mohanty, via Wikipedia; cf. Revā-khaṇḍa 1.47–48, which gives the same two-*saṃhitā* description). Despite its **Saura (sun)** name the extant text is **strongly Śaiva** — Śiva and Pārvatī, *liṅga* theology, the sacred places and liṅgas of **Vārāṇasī** — and chs. 38–40 attack **Madhvācārya** in the form of a prophecy (Rocher). **Hazra:** "the present Saura-purāṇa … is a Śaiva work"; an **earlier Saura**, now lost, also existed **[scholarly]**. *(Chronology: framework.)* Cross-link sun-worship and the Saura sect: [`brahma-purana.md`](brahma-purana.md) (Sūrya), [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md).
- **Vāsiṣṭha / Vāsiṣṭha-Liṅga Purāṇa** - Ascribed to **Vasiṣṭha**; the Devī-Bhāgavata list has **Vāsiṣṭha**, the Vāruṇa-upapurāṇa list **Vasiṣṭha-laiṅga**. Hazra suggests it may also be the **Māheśvara** of other lists (see above). *(Chronology: framework — Vasiṣṭha as Saptarṣi of the present manvantara; cross-link [`../../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md`](../../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md).)*

### Śākta
- **Kālikā Purāṇa** - The major **Śākta tantra-Purāṇa**, centered on **Pārvatī/Kālī/Kāmākhyā** and the **Kāmarūpa (Assam)** cult. Editions differ in length: **93 chapters** (Veṅkaṭeśvara, Bombay), **90** (Vaṅgavāsī, Calcutta); Wilson's manuscript had **98** (about 9,000 stanzas). Notable **distinctive material**:
  - **The Rudhirādhyāya — *bali* / blood-sacrifice manual** [disputed]: detailed prescriptions for **animal (and, exceptionally, human) sacrifice** to the Goddess, with **Vāmācāra** tantric rites — the genre's most explicit such text. Wikipedia, citing Rocher (1986), places it in **chs. 67–78**; Hazra's (unproofread) chapter summary agrees, naming **ch. 71** the "rudhirādhyāya" (sacrifice of animals, praise of human sacrifice and of offering one's own blood, and rules for human sacrifice by kings); it also has the sacrifice of animals and men in the Vaiṣṇavī-tantra (chs. 54–58) and the offering of one's own blood in Durgā-worship (ch. 61). Chapter numbers depend on the edition. The text sets conditions on human sacrifice: only with a prince's consent, before a war or in imminent danger (Lawrence 1976, via Wikipedia). *(Chronology: framework/ritual.)*
  - **The Naraka myth & Kāmarūpa kings:** the demon-king **Naraka(sura)** — born of the Boar and the Earth, reared by Janaka, ruling at **Prāgjyotiṣa**, allied with Bāṇa, slain by Kṛṣṇa (Hazra's summary: chs. 36–41) — from whom the historical Kāmarūpa dynasties claimed descent. *(Chronology: narratively a primordial/Dvāpara-adjacent demon-king; the framing is regional-dynastic.)*
  - *Dating [scholarly]:* **Hazra** — the extant text was composed in **Kāmarūpa or the adjoining part of Bengal**, in "the tenth or the first half of the eleventh century A.D." (he rejects the arguments for placing it after Indrapāla of Kāmarūpa, c. 1030–1055, or in Dharmapāla's reign, c. 1090–1115); an **earlier, different Kālikā-purāṇa**, now lost, was written most probably in **Bengal** in the **7th c.** **[late text]**
  - *Variant [scholarly]:* **B. N. Shastri** (1994) rejects Hazra's earlier text and dates the extant work after the 10th c. using **Ratnapāla of Kāmarūpa (c. 920–960)** (Hazra uses the Brahmapāla–Ratnapāla capital at Durjaya the other way, to argue that the text was written earlier); others, from a mention of **Dharmapāla** of Kāmarūpa, argue for the 11th–12th c. (Rocher 1986) — both via Wikipedia. Cross-link the Goddess: [`markandeya-purana.md`](markandeya-purana.md) (Devī Māhātmya), [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).
- **Devī Purāṇa / Devī-Bhāgavata (Upa-)** - Two **different Śākta texts** share this seat in modern surveys; neither is named in the Kūrma, Garuḍa or Padma lists (the Ekāmra list names a **Devī**).
  - **Devī Purāṇa** — **128 chapters** (some manuscripts 138): the Goddess incarnates on the **Vindhyas** as a maiden riding a lion (**Vindhyavāsinī**) and slays the demon **Ghora**, who takes the buffalo form of Mahiṣa. **Hazra** dates the text as we have it to the **6th c. CE**, most probably its latter half. **[scholarly]**
  - **Devī-Bhāgavata** — 12 *skandhas*; glorifies the **Mahādevī** as supreme Brahman; contains the **Devī Gītā** (7.31–40). Claimed a **Mahāpurāṇa** by Śākta tradition (the Bhāgavata-vs-Devī-Bhāgavata dispute); **Hazra** classes it as a late Upapurāṇa (11th–12th c.). *(Chronology: framework/cosmology.)* Full treatment: [`devi-bhagavata-upapurana.md`](devi-bhagavata-upapurana.md); dispute: [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md). **[disputed canon-rank]**

### Saura (sun-worship) & Naimittika
- **Sāmba Purāṇa** - **84 chapters** (Veṅkaṭeśvara, Bombay 1899; manuscripts 70–83); the principal **Saura (sun-worship)** Purāṇa — for Hazra the only extant Purāṇa that "deals principally with the cult of the Sun". Frame: the *sūta* at Naimiṣa, and within it **Vasiṣṭha** telling king **Bṛhadbala** of the Raghu line about the Sun and **Sāmba** (Kṛṣṇa's son). **Nārada** tells Kṛṣṇa that his wives desire Sāmba; Kṛṣṇa (Vāsudeva) curses the wives and curses Sāmba to become a leper; on Nārada's counsel Sāmba worships the **Sun**, is cured, and installs the Sun's image at **Mitravana** on the **Candrabhāgā**, in a city named after him (Sāmbapura, which Hazra identifies with **Multan**); in the Varāha Purāṇa's retelling (ch. 177, as summarised by Hazra), **Mūlasthāna** is the third of three Sun-images Sāmba sets up, on the Astamana mountain, while Skanda VII.i.139 puts the Sun as Mūlasthāna on the bank of the Candrabhāgā. He brings **eighteen families of Magas** from **Śākadvīpa** as the Sun's priests — the **Maga Brāhmaṇas**, linked by scholars to the Iranian *Magi*. A later curse of **Durvāsas** leads to the destruction of Sāmba's family by the *musala*. *(Chronology: narratively **Dvāpara Yuga** — Sāmba is Kṛṣṇa's son; cross-link [`../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md`](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md).)*
  - *Variant (who curses Sāmba):* in the Sāmba it is **Kṛṣṇa**, after Nārada's provocation; Durvāsas's curse is a separate, later episode. (Hazra also discusses the parallel Maga material in the **Bhaviṣya** Purāṇa, 1.139.)
  - *Variant (site):* chs. 42–43 put the Sun's penance-forest on the shore of the salt ocean, which Hazra places in Orissa, at or very near **Konark** (the image there is named Muṇḍīra), instead of the Candrabhāgā in the Punjab.
  - *Dating [scholarly]:* **Hazra** — the core (chs. 1–16, 18–21, 24–32, 34–38, 46 and 84) "between 500 and 800 A.D."; ch. 33 added 700–950; chs. 17, 22–23 and 44–45 inserted after 950 (44–45 before 1050); the tantric chs. 39–43 and 47–83 "added between 1250 and 1500 A.D." The text is "mentioned by Alberuni in 1030 A.D." **[late text]** **[interpolation]**
- **Āditya Purāṇa** - Named by the Matsya (53.63) among its four *upabhedas* and in the Devī-Bhāgavata and Bṛhaddharma lists. Despite the solar name, **Hazra** calls it "a non-sectarian work dealing with the praise and worship of Sūrya, Viṣṇu, Śiva, Durgā and other deities" — *śrāddha*, impurity, marriage, gifts, public works, *vratas* and festivals (especially Sun-worship), cosmography and Kali-age duties; known from Alberuni and from hundreds of lines quoted by digest-writers (Lakṣmīdhara, Aparārka, Hemādri, Mādhava, Raghunandana…); "highly probable that it was written in the fifth century A.D."; not identical with the lost earlier Saura. *(Chronology: framework/ritual.)* **[largely lost]**
- **Saura** — see under Śaiva above (the surviving "Saura" is paradoxically Śaiva; the one extant Purāṇa centred on the Sun cult is the **Sāmba**, while the **Āditya** is mixed). *(Cross-listed.)*

### Miscellaneous narrators
- **Auśanasa / Auśanasa Purāṇa** - Ascribed to **Uśanas/Śukra** (Venus, *guru* of the asuras); in nearly all lists ("spoken by Uśanas", Kūrma 1.1.19; *Śaukra* in Brahmavaivarta quotations). **Hazra:** "not a single Smṛti or non-Smṛti work is found to draw upon or refer to it", so nothing is known of its contents; it "must have been written before 800 A.D." and either became extinct early or fell out of use from the 10th c. A *Vindhya-māhātmya* claims to belong to a separate **Bṛhad-auśanasa**. *(Chronology: framework — Śukra as asura-preceptor; cross-link [`../../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md`](../../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md).)* **[largely lost]**
- **Varuṇa Purāṇa** - Named for **Varuṇa** (god of waters/cosmic order). It survives: Hazra cites a manuscript of the **Vāruṇa-upapurāṇa** (Kuppuswami Sastri's Madras catalogue) whose ch. 1 gives its own list of Upapurāṇas; the Wikipedia table (after Hazra 1962) lists it as published, 12 chapters, narrated by Varuṇa. *(Chronology: framework.)*
- **Pārāśara Purāṇa** - Ascribed to **Parāśara** (father of Vyāsa); in the Kūrma, Garuḍa, Devī-Bhāgavata, Revā and Padma lists. A manuscript of the **Parāśara-upapurāṇa** is described by Eggeling (India Office catalogue), and the text itself carries a list of Upapurāṇas (Hazra); the Wikipedia table gives 18 chapters, published. Its contents have not been checked here. *(Chronology: framework — Parāśara as a Saptarṣi-line sage; cross-link [`../../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md`](../../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md).)*
- **Durvāsasa Purāṇa** - Ascribed to the irascible sage **Durvāsā** ("spoken by Durvāsas", Kūrma 1.1.18); in the standard lists. **Hazra:** no manuscript, no quotation by any commentator or digest-writer, so "it is not possible to say anything about its contents"; a **Vaiṣṇava** work (the Revā lists attach it to the Bhāgavata), "written before 800 A.D.", and extinct "even before the tenth century A.D." Durvāsas is *not* the narrator of the Sāmba, though he curses Sāmba's family in it. *(Chronology: framework.)* **[lost]**

### Other list members
- **Skānda (Upa-)** - "Spoken by Kumāra" (Kūrma 1.1.17; Garuḍa 1.223.18), third in both lists; distinct from the Skanda Mahāpurāṇa, which the same Kūrma passage lists among the eighteen (1.1.14) — see [`skanda-purana.md`](skanda-purana.md). **Hazra** identifies this "Skānda" with the **Nandi-purāṇa**, which Kārttikeya also tells: Hemādri and the *Vīramitrodaya* quote the Kūrma line as *tṛtīyaṃ nandam*, and other lists put the Nandā in this seat.
- **Brahmāṇḍa (Upa-) and Mārīca** - The Brahmāṇḍa is in the Kūrma, Garuḍa, Revā and Padma ("another Brahmāṇḍa") lists; Hazra studies a lost Brahmāṇḍa-upapurāṇa apart from the Mahāpurāṇa ([`brahmanda-purana.md`](brahmanda-purana.md)). The **Mārīca** (of Marīci) appears in the Kūrma, Garuḍa, Padma and Vāruṇa lists. **[largely lost]**

## Notable cross-canon points

- **Pañca-lakṣaṇa coverage [scholarly]:** as a class, the Upapurāṇas are **weak** on the five marks — they are **ritual manuals, sect-charters, and *māhātmyas*** far more than cosmogonies/genealogies; like the Gaṇeśa and Nāradīya, they are prime exhibits for the academic view that *pañca-lakṣaṇa* fits few real Purāṇas. Some style themselves supplements (*khila*) of a Mahāpurāṇa — the Saura of the Brahma — and the Revā list attaches its first six Upapurāṇas to Mahāpurāṇas. (Parent: [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md).)
- **Sectarian map:** **Śaiva** (Śivadharma, Saura, Māheśvara, Vāsiṣṭha-Liṅga), **Śākta** (Kālikā, Devī Purāṇa, Devī-Bhāgavata, and — for Hazra — the Nandi), **Saura** (Sāmba), **Vaiṣṇava** (Nārasiṃha, Bṛhannāradīya, and the lost Daurvāsasa), **Gāṇapatya** (Gaṇeśa, Mudgala — [`ganesha-and-mudgala-upapuranas.md`](ganesha-and-mudgala-upapuranas.md)); **mixed, non-sectarian or regional** (Āditya; the Odishan Kāpila).
- **Dating (traditional vs academic):** *Traditional* — told after the eighteen, by Vyāsa's disciples or by sages (Kūrma 1.11.280, 1.1.16), as "sub-divisions" issued from them (Matsya 53.60–64). *Academic* — **Hazra**: "the age of the Upapurāṇas began approximately from the Gupta period"; the group of eighteen took shape c. **650–800 CE**; individual dates range from the **5th c.** (Nārasiṃha 400–500, Āditya) and **6th c.** (Devī Purāṇa) through **500–800** (Sāmba core) and **750–900** (Bṛhannāradīya) to the **10th–11th c.** (extant Kālikā) and **11th–12th c.** (Devī-Bhāgavata), with additions to the Sāmba as late as c. 1500. Many show **[interpolation]**.
