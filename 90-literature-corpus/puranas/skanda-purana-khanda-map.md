# Skanda Purāṇa — Khaṇḍa-by-Khaṇḍa Story Map

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/puranas/` — a per-text *structural map* of the largest Mahāpurāṇa, on the model of the Bhāgavata skandha-map.
> Up-link: [`./README.md`](./README.md) (the Purāṇas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (timeline root).
> Companion: the story/overview file [`skanda-purana.md`](skanda-purana.md) (this map drills into the *sections* it summarizes). Sibling map: [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md). Parent survey: [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md).

The **Skanda Purāṇa** is the **largest of the eighteen Mahāpurāṇas** — traditionally **~81,000 ślokas** (printed vulgate ≈ **75,943 verses**), named for **Skanda / Kārtikeya**. Unlike the Bhāgavata's tight 12-skandha architecture, the Skanda Purāṇa has **no single canonical structure**: it survives in **two non-matching arrangements** — a **seven-khaṇḍa (Sapta-Khaṇḍa) recension** (the standard printed vulgate, organized by pilgrimage *region*) and a **six-saṃhitā recension** (organized by *doctrine*) — that circulate as **largely non-overlapping bodies of material under one title**. This map enumerates the **seven khaṇḍas**, their **upakhaṇḍas / sthala-māhātmyas**, their chapter-counts, and the manuscript-recension problem, then re-anchors the Skanda/Tārakāsura charter myth that gives the whole library its name. Chapter-counts below follow the **AITM / Motilal printed vulgate** and may differ in other editions — that variability is itself the point. [scholarly]

---

## The two clocks for this text

- **Scripture/traditional clock** — the Skanda Purāṇa is timeless: narrated by **Śiva and Skanda**, transmitted via **Vyāsa** and recited by the *sūta* **Sūta (Lomaharṣaṇa)** at the Naimiṣa forest in *this* Kali Yuga (begun **3102 BCE**). Its sthala-māhātmyas describe tīrthas active **across all yugas**; founding legends sit in **mythic antiquity**.
- **Academic clock [scholarly]** — there is **no one "Skanda Purāṇa."** The oldest titled manuscript is **Nepalese palm-leaf NAK 2-229**, transitional-Gupta script, dated **~810 CE** (copied possibly Monday 10 March 811 CE), proving a Pāśupata Śaiva *Skandapurāṇa* existed by then; scholars place its **core composition in late-6th / early-7th c. CE Vārāṇasī**. Individual khaṇḍas/māhātmyas of the *vulgate* accreted **as late as the 13th–15th c. CE** (Kāśī-khaṇḍa ~mid-13th c.).
- **The two-text trap** — the **original Śaiva *Skandapurāṇa*** (being critically edited at **Leiden/Groningen**, the *Skandapurāṇa Project*) and the **seven-khaṇḍa printed vulgate** are **substantially different texts that share a name**: the vulgate's seven khaṇḍas are largely *not* the Leiden archetype's content. Treat "Skanda Purāṇa" as a **library label**, not a book. [scholarly] [disputed]

## Arrangement A — the seven khaṇḍas (Sapta-Khaṇḍa vulgate)

The seven khaṇḍas are each named for a pilgrimage region; each subdivides into **upakhaṇḍas** and **māhātmyas** that read as standalone *sthala-purāṇas* / pilgrimage handbooks. Total **~26 sub-sections**.

### Khaṇḍa 1 — Māheśvara-khaṇḍa (the Śaiva core + the Skanda charter myth)
- **Kedāra-khaṇḍa** (~35 chapters) — the **Kedāranātha** region; carries the **central Skanda / Tārakāsura cycle**, Śiva–Pārvatī marriage, the burning of Kāma, and core Śaiva cosmogony. The mythic "engine room" of the whole text. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine biography, mythic antiquity.)* Cross-link: [`skanda-purana.md`](skanda-purana.md) §"charter myth", [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md).
- **Kaumārikā- (Kumārikā-) khaṇḍa** (~66 chapters) — the **Mahīsāgara-saṃgama-tīrtha (Cambay/Khambhāt)** region; Kumāra (Skanda) lore, vrata and dāna teaching, and origin-legends of Gujarat coastal tīrthas. *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography, mythic antiquity.)*
- **Aruṇācala-khaṇḍa** (~37 chapters; Pūrvārdha 13 + Uttarārdha 24) — the **Aruṇācala / Tiruvaṇṇāmalai** hill (the *teja-liṅga* / fire-liṅga of the *pañca-bhūta* set); Śiva manifesting as the infinite column of fire, the Kārttikai-Dīpam etiology. *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography across yugas.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md).

### Khaṇḍa 2 — Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa (the Vaiṣṇava library inside a Śaiva text — 9 sections, ~221 chapters)
- **Veṅkaṭācala-māhātmya** (~40 ch.) — *sthala-purāṇa* of **Tirumala–Tirupati**: self-manifest **Veṅkaṭeśvara (Bālājī/Śrīnivāsa)**, his marriage to **Padmāvatī**, the loan from **Kubera**. *(Chronology: Kali-Yuga tellings; hill a timeless tīrtha.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md), [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Puruṣottama-kṣetra-māhātmya** (~49 ch.) — origin of **Jagannātha at Purī**: the *dāru-brahman* (log-deity), **King Indradyumna**, and the unfinished images. *(Chronology: framework — mythic antiquity.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md).
- **Badarikāśrama-māhātmya** (~8 ch.) — **Badrīnāth** in the Himālaya; Nara-Nārāyaṇa's tapas. Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md).
- **Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya** (~36 ch.) — the merit of the month **Kārttika** (lamps, Tulasī, Kārttika-snāna) — a Vaiṣṇava *vrata* manual. *(Chronology: living ritual across yugas.)*
- **Mārgaśīrṣa-māsa-māhātmya** (~17 ch.) — the merit of the month **Mārgaśīrṣa** (Gītā-jayantī season).
- **Bhāgavata-māhātmya** (~4 ch.) — praise of reciting the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa**; a devotional appendix. Cross-link: [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md). [late text]
- **Vaiśākha-māsa-māhātmya** (~25 ch.) — the merit of the month **Vaiśākha** (river-bathing, water-gifts).
- **Ayodhyā-māhātmya** (~10 ch.) — sanctity of **Ayodhyā** and its Rāma-tīrthas (Sarayū, Svargadvāra). *(Chronology: tied to the Treta-Yuga Rāma narrative.)* Cross-link: [`../../04-deep-dives/ramayana/`](../../04-deep-dives/ramayana/).
- **Vāsudeva-māhātmya** (~32 ch.) — a doctrinal **Kṛṣṇa/Vāsudeva** bhakti and dharma section.

### Khaṇḍa 3 — Brahma-khaṇḍa (3–4 sections, ~114 chapters)
- **Setu-māhātmya** (~52 ch.) — glorification of the **Rāma-Setu / Setubandha (Rāmeśvaram)**: **Rāma** installs the **Rāmanātha jyotirliṅga**; merit of **Dhanuṣkoṭi** and the Setu pilgrimage. *(Chronology: the bridge built in the **Treta-Yuga**; the tīrtha active across yugas.)* Cross-link: [`../../04-deep-dives/ramayana/`](../../04-deep-dives/ramayana/), [`../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md).
- **Dharmāraṇya-khaṇḍa** (~40 ch.) — legend of **Dharmāraṇya (Modhera, Gujarat)** and the **Modha Brahmin/Baniā** community — a temple-origin *and* caste/community charter. *(Chronology: mythic antiquity.)* [scholarly]
- **Brahmottara- (Uttara-) khaṇḍa** (~22 ch.) — supplementary Śaiva *vrata*/devotional matter (Pradoṣa, Rudrākṣa, Bilva, Bhasma glorifications).

### Khaṇḍa 4 — Kāśī-khaṇḍa (~100 chapters; Pūrvārdha 50 + Uttarārdha 50) [late text]
- **Kāśī-māhātmya** — the exhaustive *sthala-purāṇa* of **Vārāṇasī/Kāśī** as supreme *mokṣa-kṣetra* on Śiva's trident: the **Viśveśvara (Viśvanātha) jyotirliṅga**, the **Maṇikarṇikā** cremation-ghāṭ (Śiva whispers the **tāraka-mantra** to the dying), **Dhuṇḍhirāja-Gaṇeśa**, **Kāla-Bhairava / Daṇḍapāṇi** as the city's magistrate-guardian, and the legend of **King Divodāsa** vs. Śiva's reclaiming of the city (via the 64 Yoginīs and Gaṇeśa). Also embeds the **Satī/Dakṣa** and **Agastya** narratives. *(Chronology: founding legends in mythic antiquity; the khaṇḍa's text-form ~mid-13th c. CE.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md), [`../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md). [late text]

### Khaṇḍa 5 — Āvantya- (Avanti-) khaṇḍa (3 sections)
- **Avantī-kṣetra-māhātmya** (~71 ch.) — sanctity of **Ujjain (Avantī/Avantikā)**, seat of the **Mahākāleśvara jyotirliṅga** and its tīrthas (Śiprā river, Kṣiprā-saṃgama). *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md).
- **Caturaśīti-liṅga-māhātmya** (~84 ch.) — the **84-liṅga circuit (Caturaśīti-liṅga yātrā)** of Ujjain, one liṅga per chapter.
- **Revā-khaṇḍa (Narmadā-māhātmya)** (~116–232 ch., edition-dependent) — the great river *sthala-purāṇa*: the **origin of the Narmadā (Revā)** from Śiva (her descent / from his tapas on Mt. Maikala), her supreme power (mere **sight** of the Narmadā equals bathing in the Gaṅgā), and a chapter-by-chapter tour of bank-tīrthas (**Oṃkāreśvara/Amareśvara**, Maheśvar, Bharuch). *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography; the river's descent in mythic antiquity.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/README.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/README.md).
  - *Variant (Revā-khaṇḍa attribution):* the Revā-khaṇḍa **also circulates attached to the Vāyu Purāṇa**, with differing recensions; its placement is genuinely disputed. (*Skanda P. Āvantya-khaṇḍa / Vāyu P.*) [disputed] [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Satyanārāyaṇa-vrata-kathā):* the immensely popular **Satyanārāyaṇa Pūjā** narrative is traditionally cited to the **Revā-khaṇḍa**, but is widely regarded as a **late accretion** absent from older manuscripts. (*Skanda P., Revā-khaṇḍa*) [late text] [interpolation]

### Khaṇḍa 6 — Nāgara-khaṇḍa (~279 chapters)
- **Hāṭakeśvara-kṣetra-māhātmya** — ~279 chapters on the **Hāṭakeśvara-kṣetra** and the **Nāgara Brahmins'** sacred town **Vaḍnagar (Ānarta, Gujarat)** — a fused temple-origin tale and **community/caste charter** of the Nāgara Brahmins. *(Chronology: framework — sacred geography, mythic antiquity.)* [scholarly]

### Khaṇḍa 7 — Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa (the largest khaṇḍa, ~491 chapters, 4 sections)
- **Prabhāsa-kṣetra-māhātmya** (~365 ch.) — the **Prabhāsa-kṣetra / Somanātha** *sthala-purāṇa*: the **Moon-god (Soma/Candra)**, cursed by his father-in-law **Dakṣa** to wane, regains his light by worshiping Śiva here — the etiology of "**Somanātha, Lord of Soma**" (1st jyotirliṅga). Also the death-place lore of **Kṛṣṇa** at Prabhāsa. *(Chronology: the Soma-curse a timeless divine event; the Prabhāsa/Kṛṣṇa material tied to the **Dvāpara-Yuga** Kṛṣṇa cycle.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md), [`../../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md`](../../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md).
- **Vastrāpatha-kṣetra-māhātmya** (~19 ch.) — **Girnar (Vastrāpatha)** in Saurāṣṭra; Dattātreya and Ambikā lore.
- **Arbuda-khaṇḍa** (~63 ch.) — the **Arbuda (Mt. Ābū)** region; Vasiṣṭha's fire-pit (*agni-kuṇḍa*) and the **Agnikula** Rajput-origin legend, Achaleśvara. [folk]
- **Dvārakā-māhātmya** (~44 ch.) — sanctity of **Dvārakā**, Kṛṣṇa's city. *(Chronology: Dvāpara-Yuga / end-of-Dvāpara submergence.)* Cross-link: [`../../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md`](../../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md), [`../../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md).

## Arrangement B — the six saṃhitās (Saṃhitā-recension)

- **An alternate, doctrine-oriented division** into **six saṃhitās** circulating under the same title; **largely non-overlapping** with the seven-khaṇḍa material above. Where the khaṇḍas are *geographic* (pilgrimage), the saṃhitās are *theological* (cosmogony, jñāna, the origin and cure of evil/vice). [scholarly]
  - **Sanatkumāra-saṃhitā** — cosmogony and *jñāna* taught by **Sanatkumāra**; a doctrinal stratum quoted by later Vedāntins.
  - **Sūta-saṃhitā** — the most famous and **most quoted** saṃhitā: **Advaita-leaning theology**, dharma, and *jñāna*; cited as authoritative by Vedāntins (a commentary, the *Tātparya-dīpikā*, is ascribed to Mādhava). *(Chronology: framework — timeless teaching.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/).
  - **Śaṃkarī-saṃhitā** — Śaiva theology centered on **Śaṃkara/Śiva**; one section is the source of the Tamil **Kandapurāṇam** tradition's frame.
  - **Vaiṣṇavī-saṃhitā** — Vaiṣṇava theology.
  - **Brāhmī-saṃhitā** — Brahmā-centered cosmogony.
  - **Saura-saṃhitā** — **Sūrya / sun-worship** doctrine and ritual.
- **The mismatch is the headline** — the khaṇḍa- and saṃhitā-versions "do not fully match," a hallmark of the text's composite, open-library nature: the title names a *genre of regional and doctrinal material* rather than a fixed work. [scholarly]

## The charter myth re-anchored — Skanda / Kārtikeya and Tārakāsura

(Carried chiefly in the **Māheśvara-khaṇḍa / Kedāra-khaṇḍa**; the mythic core that names the whole Purāṇa. Fuller treatment in [`skanda-purana.md`](skanda-purana.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md).)

- **The need for a war-god** — the asura **Tāraka(sura)**, boon-protected so that **only a son of Śiva** can kill him, oppresses the devas; the gods engineer **Pārvatī's tapasya, the burning of Kāma, and her marriage to the ascetic Śiva** to produce the destined son. *(Chronology: framework — the dawn of a deva–asura war, mythic antiquity.)*
- **The six-fold birth (Ṣaṇmukha)** — Śiva's too-potent seed, relayed **Śiva → Agni → Gaṅgā → reed-thicket (śara-vana)**, becomes **six infants** nursed by the **six Kṛttikās (Pleiades)** and fused by Pārvatī into one **six-faced** child — **Kārtikeya**, **Śaravaṇabhava**, **Ṣaṇmukha/Subrahmaṇya**. *(Chronology: framework — divine birth, mythic antiquity.)*
  - *Variant (carriers of the seed):* the Śiva→Agni→Gaṅgā→reed-bed relay is the Skanda/Śiva-Purāṇa norm; the **Mahābhārata (Vana-parvan)** and Kālidāsa's **Kumārasambhava** give differing chains. (*Skanda P. / Śiva P.; cf. Mahābhārata, Kumārasambhava*) [disputed]
- **Tāraka-vadha** — anointed **Devasenāpati**, Skanda leads the devas and **slays Tārakāsura** (and, in expanded cycles, **Sūrapadma / Siṃhamukha** and **Pralamba**), restoring cosmic order. *(Chronology: framework — the deva–asura war, mythic antiquity.)*
  - *Variant (Northern Sanskrit vs Southern/Tamil):* the Sanskrit text centers **Tāraka**; the Tamil **Kandapurāṇam** of **Kacciyappa Civācāriyār** (a reworking ascribed to the Śaṃkarī-saṃhitā) centers **Sūrapadman**, the six war-camps (*Aṟupaḍai Vīḍu*), and the marriage to **Vaḷḷi**. (*Skanda P. vs Tamil Kandapurāṇam*) [scholarly]

## The "no two manuscripts agree" problem

- **A living, ever-edited text** — the Skanda Purāṇa is the classic Purāṇic example of a **fluid, accreting text**: "widely edited over many centuries, creating numerous variants," with different parts added by different traditions in different periods. Chapter-counts, section-names, and even *which* khaṇḍas appear differ from edition to edition — hence the AITM counts above are indicative, not fixed. [scholarly]
- **Two recensions of the *original* Śaiva Skandapurāṇa** — the early text (in the ~810 CE Nepalese manuscript family) was later expanded into two recensions: the **Revākhaṇḍa recension (RKR)**, surviving in a **single 1682 manuscript**, and the **Ambikākhaṇḍa recension (AKR)**, in **four later manuscripts** "with much more alterations." Neither equals the printed vulgate. (*Skandapurāṇa Project, Leiden/Groningen*) [scholarly]
- **The critical edition** — the **Skandapurāṇa Project** (begun early 1990s, Groningen → Leiden; **Adriaensen, Bakker, Isaacson**, later **Bisschop, Yokochi**) collates **50+ manuscripts (9th–19th c.)** by **stemmatic** method to reconstruct the **archetype of the earliest Śaiva layer** — explicitly *not* the seven-khaṇḍa vulgate. Volume III edits the **Vindhyavāsinī cycle**, the **oldest full account** of the Vindhya-goddess and a major source of the **Devī-Māhātmya**. Cross-link: [`../markandeya-purana.md`](markandeya-purana.md). [scholarly]
- **Practical upshot** — when a story is cited "from the Skanda Purāṇa," ask **which recension and which khaṇḍa**: the original Śaiva *Skandapurāṇa* and the vulgate's seven khaṇḍas are, for many purposes, **different texts**. Rosters of upakhaṇḍas/māhātmyas here are **open-ended**, not exhaustive. [disputed]
