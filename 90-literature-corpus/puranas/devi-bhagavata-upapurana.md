# Devī Bhāgavata (Upapurāṇa)

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/puranas/` — a per-Purāṇa source file in the literature corpus.
> Up-link: [`../README.md`](../README.md) (the Purāṇas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (the literature corpus).
> See also the parent survey [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md), the companion [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md) (its Vaiṣṇava rival/namesake), and the Śākta core text [`markandeya-purana.md`](markandeya-purana.md) (the Devī Māhātmya).

The **Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (*Śrīmad Devī Bhāgavatam*, also *Devī Bhāgavatam* / *Durgā Bhāgavatam*) is the **great theological epic of Śāktism** — the systematic, full-length scripture that does for the **Goddess (Mahādevī / Bhuvaneśvarī / Ādi Parāśakti)** what the Vaiṣṇava Bhāgavata does for Kṛṣṇa: it absorbs and re-narrates the whole Purāṇic cosmos with the **Goddess as the one supreme, independent, ungendered ultimate reality (Brahman)** above the Trimūrti, who creates, sustains, and dissolves the worlds through her three primary Śaktis (Mahākālī, Mahālakṣmī, Mahāsarasvatī). Composed in **12 skandhas, 318 chapters, ~18,000 ślokas**, it deliberately mirrors the Vaiṣṇava Bhāgavata in scale, skandha-count, and frame so as to **claim the title of "the real Bhāgavata," the eighteenth Mahāpurāṇa**, against the Vishnu Bhāgavata (see the **dispute** section below). Its philosophical jewel is the **Devī Gītā** (7th skandha, chs. 31–40), and it is the principal Purāṇic source for the **108 Śakti Pīṭha list**. Tradition ascribes it (like all Purāṇas) to **Vyāsa**, narrated by his son **Śuka** / by Vyāsa himself to **Janamejaya**; academics treat it as an anonymous, composite **Śākta Upapurāṇa** of roughly the **9th–14th c. CE**.

- **Structure — 12 skandhas / 318 chapters / ~18,000 ślokas** (it copies the Vaiṣṇava Bhāgavata's twelve-book architecture, even matching its self-claimed verse-total of 18,000 — a pointed rivalry):
  - **Skandha I (20 chs):** frame and cosmogony — Devī as the primordial reality manifesting three Śaktis; the **Madhu–Kaiṭabha** episode; the curse-origin of the narration.
  - **Skandha II (12 chs):** Mahābhārata-linked narratives (birth of Vyāsa, the Pāṇḍava-cycle backstory) — woven so as not to contradict the epic.
  - **Skandha III (30 chs):** worship of **Bhuvaneśvarī**; the Trimūrti's vision of the Goddess and their dependence on her; the institution of **Navarātri**; Rāma's Navarātri-pūjā to win the Goddess's aid.
  - **Skandha IV (25 chs):** Kṛṣṇa/Vāsudeva material and avatāra-interactions recast in Śākta terms; tantric themes.
  - **Skandha V (35 chs):** the Śākta **Devī Māhātmya** proper — the war on **Mahiṣāsura** and on **Śumbha–Niśumbha** (and Kauśikī).
  - **Skandha VI (31 chs):** the supremacy of the Goddess argued at length; the **Vṛtra / Indra–Triśiras** cycle retold; *tīrthas* and sacred geography.
  - **Skandha VII (40 chs):** royal genealogies; **Sunaḥśepa/Hariścandra-type** material; **Satī & Dakṣa's yajña** (ch. 30); and the **Devī Gītā** (chs. 31–40, ~507 verses) — Pārvatī/Bhuvaneśvarī instructing **Himavat** (her father). The **108 Śakti Pīṭhas** are enumerated here.
  - **Skandha VIII (24 chs):** *bhuvana-koṣa* cosmography — the dvīpas, planetary motion, the calendar (closely paralleling Bhāgavata skandha V).
  - **Skandha IX (50 chs, the largest):** the Prakṛti-Devī manifestations (the **Pañca-prakṛti** — Durgā, Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī, Sāvitrī, Rādhā); much **Kṛṣṇa/Rādhā** theology (paralleling the **Brahmavaivarta** Prakṛti-khaṇḍa); Kali-yuga description.
  - **Skandha X (13 chs):** the **14 Manus** across the manvantaras; **Pañcāyatana-pūjā**; the **Bhrāmarī Devī** (bee-goddess) narrative.
  - **Skandha XI (24 chs):** dharma, sadācāra, **rudrākṣa** and *bhasma*, the integration of tantric worship with Vedic authority.
  - **Skandha XII (14 chs):** the Goddess as **mother of the Vedas / Gāyatrī**; the **1,008 names of Gāyatrī**; the great description of **Maṇidvīpa** (the Goddess's supreme abode, above all the lokas).
- **Pañca-lakṣaṇa coverage:** present but subordinated to *bhakti/māhātmya* — *sarga/pratisarga* (skandhas I, VIII), *vaṃśa/vaṃśānucarita* (skandha VII), *manvantara* (skandha X); the bulk is **Goddess-theology, stotra, and tantric/ritual instruction** far beyond the classical five marks.
- **Sectarian leaning:** emphatically **Śākta** — the Goddess is the unconditioned Brahman and the Trimūrti are her dependents; yet **Hazra** noted the author wrote "so as to be consistent with all the Hindu sects," and **Dowson** even read it as quasi-**Śaiva**. It synthesizes **Advaita-Vedānta nondualism with Śrīvidyā/Śākta tantra**.
- **Dating:** *traditional:* eternal, Vyāsa's composition, narrated to **Janamejaya** (placing the telling at the **Dvāpara–Kali junction** of the current 28th mahāyuga). *Academic [scholarly] [late text]:* a composite **Upapurāṇa** — **R. C. Hazra** ~**11th–12th c. CE**; **Tracy Pintchman** c. **1000–1200 CE**; broader range **9th–14th c.**, with skandha IX's *mleccha/yavana* references possibly **12th–15th c.** **Doniger** classes it as a late Śākta text.

## Notable / distinctive stories and sections

### The frame: why the Goddess's Bhāgavata is told
- **Vyāsa narrates to Janamejaya** — like the Vaiṣṇava Bhāgavata (told by Śuka to Parīkṣit), the Devī Bhāgavata is framed as **Vyāsa instructing King Janamejaya** (and via the Naimiṣa-forest assembly of Sūta and the ṛṣis), explicitly so the listener may attain *mukti* through Goddess-devotion. The deliberate mirroring of the rival's frame is itself part of the "real Bhāgavata" claim. *(Chronology: frame device — narrated at the **Dvāpara–Kali junction**; Janamejaya is the great-grandson of Arjuna.)* Cross-link the rival frame: [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).

### Cosmogony and the Goddess above the Trimūrti
- **Madhu and Kaiṭabha (skandha I)** — at the dawn of creation the demons **Madhu and Kaiṭabha** (born from Viṣṇu's body/ear during the cosmic sleep) menace **Brahmā** on the lotus; Brahmā hymns the **Goddess as Yoganidrā/Mahāmāyā**, who withdraws from Viṣṇu, wakes him, and enables the demons' slaying — the **Śākta reading makes the Goddess, not Viṣṇu, the decisive power** behind the deed. *(Chronology: cosmology — at a creation/pralaya boundary, before ordinary time.)* Cross-link the Vaiṣṇava/Devī-Māhātmya version of the same episode: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md).
  - *Variant (Devī Bhāgavata):* the **Trimūrti themselves are shown the Goddess's supreme form** and taught that they are her creations, dependent on her *śakti* — a polemical inversion of the Vaiṣṇava framing in which Devī is Viṣṇu's *yoganidrā*.
- **Maṇidvīpa, the supreme abode (skandha XII)** — beyond all the lokas (above even Vaikuṇṭha/Kailāsa/Brahmaloka) lies the **island of jewels, Maṇidvīpa**, the imperishable seat of the **Ādi Parāśakti** — the Śākta analogue of the highest heaven; the Goddess is also the **mother of the Gāyatrī and the Vedas**. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — timeless supreme realm.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).

### The goddess battles — the Śākta retelling (skandha V)
- **Mahiṣāsura-vadha (Śākta variant)** — the gods, defeated by the buffalo-demon **Mahiṣa**, pool their **tejas** into the form of the lion-riding **Durgā/Caṇḍikā**, who is armed by each deity and slays Mahiṣa — the **Mahiṣāsuramardinī** episode, here expanded to greater length than the Mārkaṇḍeya original and embedded in the Goddess-supreme theology. *(Chronology: cosmology — a deva–asura war outside ordinary historical time.)* Cross-link the canonical Devī Māhātmya treatment: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md).
  - *Variant (vs. Mārkaṇḍeya Devī Māhātmya):* the Devī Bhāgavata's account is a **downstream expansion** of the compact ~700-verse Saptaśatī — more elaborate dialogue and stotra, framed by the Goddess-as-Brahman doctrine [late text]. See [`markandeya-purana.md`](markandeya-purana.md).
- **Śumbha and Niśumbha (and Kauśikī)** — the Goddess emanates the fair **Kauśikī** from her sheath (leaving the dark **Kālikā** behind) and destroys the brothers **Śumbha–Niśumbha** with their generals **Caṇḍa, Muṇḍa** (→ **Cāmuṇḍā**) and **Raktabīja** (each blood-drop a clone, drunk by Kālī). *(Chronology: cosmology — deva–asura war.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md).
- **Bhrāmarī Devī and Aruṇāsura (skandha X)** — when the demon **Aruṇa**, immune to harm from any creature with more or fewer than two feet, threatens the gods, the Goddess takes the form of **Bhrāmarī**, attended by swarms of **bees, wasps, and stinging insects** (six-legged, thus outside the boon), who tear the demon's army apart — a distinctive Śākta variant absent from the Mārkaṇḍeya. *(Chronology: cosmology — deva–asura war.)*

### The Vṛtra cycle, retold (skandha VI)
- **Indra, Triśiras, and Vṛtra (Śākta reframing)** — the slaying of the three-headed **Triśiras (Viśvarūpa)** and the dragon **Vṛtra** by **Indra**, and the **brahmahatyā guilt** that follows — retold so that Indra's eventual purification and victory depend on **Goddess-worship**; a Śākta overlay on the ancient Vedic (Ṛgvedic) Indra–Vṛtra myth. *(Chronology: cosmology — a deva–asura conflict of cosmic antiquity; the Vedic nucleus is the oldest layer.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).

### The Devī Gītā (skandha VII, chs. 31–40) — *the philosophical crown* [scholarly]
- **The Devī Gītā ("Song of the Goddess"), ~507 verses** — modeled on the **Bhagavad Gītā**, it is the **Goddess (Bhuvaneśvarī) instructing her devotee Himavat** (the mountain-king, Pārvatī's father) and the assembled gods: the Goddess reveals her **Virāṭ/universal form**, expounds **nondual (Advaita) Brahman-identity**, **māyā**, **aṣṭāṅga-yoga**, the yogas of **jñāna, karma, and bhakti**, the **locations of her temples (pīṭhas)**, and the rites of her worship — climaxing in her cosmic theophany. It **circulates independently** like the Bhagavad Gītā. *(Chronology: framework — a timeless theological discourse set in the legendary mountain-court of Himavat.)* Cross-link the Vaiṣṇava analogue: [`../../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md`](../../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md), and the Goddess survey [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).
- **Satī, Dakṣa, and the yajña (ch. 30, lead-in to the Devī Gītā)** — Satī's birth as Dakṣa's daughter and her self-immolation at Dakṣa's sacrifice — the narrative hinge that, in the Śākta telling, scatters her body and creates the **pīṭhas**. *(Chronology: a primordial divine episode — placed in the **early divine age**, the origin-story of the Goddess's terrestrial seats.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md).

### The 108 Śakti Pīṭha list (skandha VII) — *the canonical enumeration*
- **The 108 Śakti Pīṭhas** — **Vyāsa recounts to Janamejaya 108 seats of the Goddess** with their **locations** and the **local name/form of the Devī** at each — the most-cited Purāṇic pīṭha-list (other texts give 51, 52, or 4). Sample entries: **Śaṅkarī** (Laṅkā), **Kāmākṣī** (Kāñcī), **Bhramarāmbikā** (Śrīśaila), **Mahālakṣmī** (Kolhāpura), **Mahākālī** (Ujjayinī), **Kāmarūpā** (Harikṣetra/Kāmākhyā), **Mādhaveśvarī** (Prayāga), **Jvālāmukhī**, **Viśālākṣī** (Vārāṇasī). *(Chronology: framework/sacred geography — the pīṭhas arise from the Satī-dismemberment myth and are mapped onto the present-kalpa landscape.)* Cross-link the dedicated geography file: [`../../06-sacred-geography/51-shakti-peethas.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/51-shakti-peethas.md).
  - *Variant (number of pīṭhas):* lists vary widely by text — **4 ādi-pīṭhas**, **18 mahā-pīṭhas**, **51/52** (the common Tantra-cūḍāmaṇi count), and the Devī Bhāgavata's **108**; the Devī Bhāgavata's is the longest and one of the most influential [disputed].

### The Pañca-Prakṛti and Kṛṣṇa–Rādhā theology (skandha IX)
- **The five forms of Prakṛti** — the Goddess as **Durgā, Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī, Sāvitrī, and Rādhā**, with much **Kṛṣṇa–Rādhā** narrative — material closely paralleling (and likely sharing a source-stratum with) the **Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa's** Prakṛti-khaṇḍa, here folded into the Goddess-supreme scheme. *(Chronology: framework — divine emanation theology; the Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa frame nominally in the **Dvāpara/Kali** transition.)* Cross-link: [`brahmavaivarta-purana.md`](brahmavaivarta-purana.md).

## The "real Bhāgavata" dispute — which is the eighteenth Mahāpurāṇa? [disputed] [scholarly]

- **The core problem** — the canonical list of 18 Mahāpurāṇas contains a "**Bhāgavata**." **Two texts claim that slot**: the **Vaiṣṇava (Viṣṇu) Bhāgavata** (the Kṛṣṇa-Bhāgavata, [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md)) and the **Devī (Śākta) Bhāgavata**. Both are 12-skandha texts of ~18,000 verses with parallel frames — by design.
- **The Śākta claim** — proponents argue the **Devī Bhāgavata is the *original* Bhāgavata** and that the Vaiṣṇava Bhāgavata is a **later substitute composed by the grammarian-poet Vopadeva (c. 1300 CE)** at the court of Hemādri; they cite the **Uma-Saṃhitā of the Śiva Purāṇa**, which lists "the fifth Mahāpurāṇa called Śrīmad Bhāgavata," and verses in the Devī Bhāgavata asserting its own equivalence to the eighteen.
- **The Vaiṣṇava counter-claim** — Vaiṣṇava authorities reject this: the jurist **Mitra Miśra** in his **Vīramitrodaya** argued for the **Viṣṇu-Bhāgavata** and dismissed the Devī-Bhāgavata as a **sectarian Śākta composition / interpolation**; the Vaiṣṇava Bhāgavata is held to be far older and is the universally recited text.
- **The academic verdict [scholarly]** — **R. C. Hazra** judged the "Devī-Bhāgavata is the real Bhāgavata" position (held by "a band of scholars, both European and Indian") **untenable**, classing the Devī Bhāgavata as a **late Upapurāṇa (11th–12th c.)** that **deliberately imitated** the already-famous Vaiṣṇava text to borrow its prestige. Most academics therefore count the **Viṣṇu Bhāgavata** as the Mahāpurāṇa and the **Devī Bhāgavata as the leading Śākta Upapurāṇa** — while noting that **Śāktas treat it as a full Mahāpurāṇa**, so the answer is fundamentally **sect-relative**.
