# Padma Purāṇa — Khaṇḍa-by-Khaṇḍa Story Map

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/puranas/` — a per-text *structural map* of the second-largest Mahāpurāṇa, on the model of the Skanda khaṇḍa-map.
> Up-link: [`./README.md`](./README.md) (the Purāṇas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (timeline root).
> Companion: the story/overview file [`padma-purana.md`](padma-purana.md) (this map drills into the khaṇḍas it summarizes). Sibling maps: [`skanda-purana-khanda-map.md`](skanda-purana-khanda-map.md), [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md). Parent survey: [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md).

The **Padma Purāṇa** ("the Lotus Purāṇa") claims **~55,000 ślokas** (surviving text ~50,000) and, like the Skanda, has **no single canonical shape**: an older **Bengal recension in five khaṇḍas** faces a **Western/Devanāgarī printed recension in six khaṇḍas**, to which the Veṅkaṭeśvara-press and AITM printings attach a **seventh, the Kriyāyogasāra-khaṇḍa**, wholly absent from the Bengal manuscripts. This map enumerates the khaṇḍas with per-khaṇḍa story inventories — including three Bhūmi-khaṇḍa cycles (Sukalā, Kuñjala the parrot, Śivaśarman's sons) and the Kriyāyogasāra found nowhere else in this corpus — and the recension problem itself. Chapter-counts follow the printed (Veṅkaṭeśvara/ĀnSS/AITM) editions and **vary by edition**; that variability is part of the record. [scholarly]

---

## The two clocks for this text

- **Scriptural/traditional clock** — timeless Vyāsa-revelation: narrated by **Pulastya to Bhīṣma**, and by **Sūta** to the sages of Naimiṣa in *this* Kali Yuga (begun **3102 BCE**), with embedded **Śiva–Pārvatī** and **Śeṣa–Vātsyāyana** dialogue frames. Its stories range from the pre-temporal lotus-creation through the Tretā-yuga Rāma cycle to vrata charters explicitly *for* the Kali age.
- **Academic clock [scholarly]** — a stratified compilation, roughly **4th–15th c. CE**, with core layers c. **750–1000 CE** and manuscripts revised into the **14th–16th c.**; the Uttara-khaṇḍa's *māhātmyas* and the Kriyāyogasāra-khaṇḍa are widely treated as the **latest strata**. [late text]

---

## The recension problem — Bengal 5 vs printed 6 (+1)

- **Bengal recension (older, largely unpublished)** — **five khaṇḍas**: **Sṛṣṭi, Bhūmi, Svarga, Pātāla, Uttara**. It lacks the Brahma-khaṇḍa and the Kriyāyogasāra entirely, lacks ~39 *Dharma-śāstra* chapters found in the West, and its Svarga-khaṇḍa differs substantially in content from the printed one — H. H. Wilson already flagged the two versions as seriously divergent in the 1830s. (*Padma P., manuscript tradition*) [scholarly]
- **Western/Devanāgarī printed recension** — **six khaṇḍas**: **Sṛṣṭi (~82 ch.), Bhūmi (~125 ch.), Svarga (~62 ch.), Brahma (~26 ch.), Pātāla (~117 ch.), Uttara (200+ ch., the largest)**. This is the text behind the AITM English translation and most modern citation.
  - *Variant (khaṇḍa-naming):* some editions and catalogs list an **Ādi-khaṇḍa** in place of (or overlapping with) the Svarga-khaṇḍa — e.g. the Puṣkara opening sits in "Ādi/Svarga" depending on the printing; the labels, order, and chapter-counts do not match across editions. [disputed] [scholarly]
- **The seventh khaṇḍa** — the **Kriyāyogasāra-khaṇḍa (26 ch.)** rides as a supplement to the printed text (sometimes numbered as part of the Uttara, sometimes separate). It is absent from the Bengal recension and reads as a self-contained late appendix. (*Padma P., Kriyāyogasāra-khaṇḍa*) [late text] [scholarly]
- **Practical upshot** — as with the Skanda (see [`skanda-purana-khanda-map.md`](skanda-purana-khanda-map.md)), "the Padma Purāṇa says" requires asking *which recension and which khaṇḍa*; the guṇa-classification, the Gītā-māhātmya, and the Ekādaśī kathās all live in the most edition-fluid strata. [scholarly]

## Khaṇḍa 1 — Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa (creation; ~82 chapters)

- **The Pulastya–Bhīṣma frame** — Bhīṣma, practicing austerities at Gaṅgādvāra, is taught cosmogony and dharma by the sage **Pulastya**; Sūta relays the whole to the Naimiṣa sages. (*Padma P., Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa 1*)
- **Padma-sṛṣṭi (the lotus creation)** — from Viṣṇu on the causal waters rises the **lotus** bearing **Brahmā**, who creates the worlds; the eponymous *padma*. (*Padma P., Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa*) Cross-link: [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md).
- **Puṣkara-māhātmya** — the khaṇḍa's centerpiece: **Puṣkara (Ajmer)** as Brahmā's foremost tīrtha; Brahmā's great **yajña at Puṣkara**, the delay of **Sāvitrī**, the substitute bride **Gāyatrī**, and Sāvitrī's curse limiting Brahmā's worship — the charter of the near-unique Brahmā shrine. (*Padma P., Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa, Puṣkara chs.*) Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md).
- **Genealogies and deva–asura matter** — solar/lunar vaṃśa summaries, pitṛ and śrāddha lore, and vrata/dāna prescriptions filling out the *pañca-lakṣaṇa* skeleton. (*Padma P., Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa*)

## Khaṇḍa 2 — Bhūmi-khaṇḍa (earth; ~125 chapters) — the story-rich khaṇḍa

The Bhūmi-khaṇḍa's signature teaching is that **the true tīrthas are living beings** — mother, father, guru, wife, truth itself outrank geographic fords — and it argues this through some of the Purāṇa's best sustained story-cycles. [scholarly]

- **Śivaśarman tests his five sons; the Somaśarman–Suvrata cycle** — the yogin-brahmin **Śivaśarman**, master of māyā, subjects his five sons to conjured ordeals (including the illusory corpse of their own mother, which they are commanded to dismember) to prove obedience and dispassion; sons who pass win exalted stations, and the cycle runs on into the piety of **Somaśarman** and his son **Suvrata**, whose steadfast devotion is rewarded by Viṣṇu. A charter of filial dharma as tīrtha. (*Padma P., Bhūmi-khaṇḍa 1–22*)
- **Sukalā the pativratā** — the epic of **Sukalā**, wife of a merchant (**Kṛkala**) who departs on tīrtha-yātrā leaving her behind; **Indra and Kāma** conspire to break her fidelity and are routed by the sheer power of her *pativrata* virtue, which she expounds in long discourses — the khaṇḍa's proof that the devoted wife needs no pilgrimage. Framed with the **Ikṣvāku boar-hunt** episode and the tale of **Sudeva and Padmāvatī** as sub-stories. (*Padma P., Bhūmi-khaṇḍa 41–60*)
- **Kuñjala the pious parrot** — the learned parrot **Kuñjala** instructs his four sons in dharma and jñāna; the sage **Cyavana**, touring the tīrthas, is sent to the bird for teaching, and further dialogues involve **Divyadevī** — beast-fable frame carrying past-birth narratives and the "inner tīrtha" doctrine. (*Padma P., Bhūmi-khaṇḍa 85–101, 123*) Cross-link (the sage's canonical file): [`../../04-deep-dives/cyavana-sukanya.md`](../../04-deep-dives/cyavana-sukanya.md).
- **King-cycles: Vena/Pṛthu, Nahuṣa, Yayāti** — the redemption of Vena and the milking of the Earth by **Pṛthu**; the birth-and-rise of **Nahuṣa**; an extended **Yayāti** cycle. (*Padma P., Bhūmi-khaṇḍa*) Cross-links: [`../../04-deep-dives/prithu-prithvi.md`](../../04-deep-dives/prithu-prithvi.md), [`../../04-deep-dives/nahusha.md`](../../04-deep-dives/nahusha.md), [`../../04-deep-dives/yayati.md`](../../04-deep-dives/yayati.md).
- **Aśokasundarī** — the Bhūmi-khaṇḍa is also the home of the cycle of **Aśokasundarī**, daughter of Śiva and Pārvatī born of the Kalpa-tree wish, menaced by the demon **Huṇḍa** and destined to wed **Nahuṣa** — a cycle treated as its own standalone story in this corpus (kept separate from this catalog). (*Padma P., Bhūmi-khaṇḍa*) [regional]

## Khaṇḍa 3 — Svarga-khaṇḍa (heaven; ~62 chapters)

- **Cosmography of the lokas** — **Vaikuṇṭha** and the heavens of Sūrya, Indra, Agni, Yama; gandharva/vidyādhara/apsaras realms, woven with illustrative myths. (*Padma P., Svarga-khaṇḍa*)
- **Bhārata-varṣa geography and tīrtha rosters** — mountains, rivers, and holy fords of the earth catalogued from heaven's vantage, plus **varṇāśrama** and **yoga** teaching chapters. (*Padma P., Svarga-khaṇḍa*) Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/bharatavarsha-and-puranic-geography.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/bharatavarsha-and-puranic-geography.md).
  - *Variant (Bengal recension):* the Bengal manuscripts' Svarga-khaṇḍa is substantially **different in content** from the printed one — this khaṇḍa is the recensions' sharpest divergence. [scholarly] [disputed]

## Khaṇḍa 4 — Brahma-khaṇḍa (~26 chapters; printed recension only) [late text]

- **A short Vaiṣṇava supplement absent from the Bengal recension** — Kṛṣṇa- and **Rādhā**-centered bhakti matter, vrata rules (Ekādaśī- and Kārttika-adjacent observances), and brahmin-dharma tales; its absence in Bengal marks it as a late Western addition. (*Padma P., Brahma-khaṇḍa*) [late text] [scholarly]

## Khaṇḍa 5 — Pātāla-khaṇḍa (~117 chapters)

- **The Śeṣa–Vātsyāyana frame** — the serpent **Śeṣa** narrates to the sage **Vātsyāyana**, opening from the netherworld upward into the Rāma narrative. (*Padma P., Pātāla-khaṇḍa*)
- **Rāma's Aśvamedha** — the khaṇḍa's centerpiece and the Padma's most distinctive narrative: after the return to Ayodhyā, **Rāma's horse-sacrifice** — the wandering horse, the campaigns of **Śatrughna**, the capture of the horse by **Lava and Kuśa** at Vālmīki's āśrama, the battle with Rāma's own army, recognition and reunion, and Sītā's earth-return — told with full bhakti framing (Rāma–Sītā explicitly as Viṣṇu–Lakṣmī). (*Padma P., Pātāla-khaṇḍa*) Cross-links: [`../../04-deep-dives/uttara-ramayana-lava-kusha.md`](../../04-deep-dives/uttara-ramayana-lava-kusha.md), [`../../04-deep-dives/ramayana/README.md`](../../04-deep-dives/ramayana/README.md), spine: [`../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md`](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md).
  - *Variant (vs Vālmīki):* Vālmīki's Uttara-kāṇḍa has no full aśvamedha-digvijaya of this shape; the Padma/Jaimini-style horse-sacrifice epic is a later devotional expansion. [late text] [scholarly]
- **Śiva–Pārvatī dialogue on Kṛṣṇa; Vṛndāvana-māhātmya** — a long Śaiva-framed glorification of **Kṛṣṇa** and the Vraja land, plus heavy Śiva glorification — the khaṇḍa's Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava braid. (*Padma P., Pātāla-khaṇḍa*) Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/mathura-vrindavan-braj.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/mathura-vrindavan-braj.md).

## Khaṇḍa 6 — Uttara-khaṇḍa (the largest, 200+ chapters; the vrata-and-māhātmya library) [late text]

- **Ekādaśī-māhātmya cycle** — the origin of **Ekādaśī** (the demon **Mura** slain by the śakti born of Viṣṇu's body, who becomes the fast-day goddess) and the year-round cycle of named Ekādaśī kathās that feed the living vrata calendar. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa*) Canonical catalog in this corpus: [`../../04-deep-dives/ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md`](../../04-deep-dives/ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md).
  - *Variant (attribution):* individual Ekādaśī kathās circulate attributed variously to the Padma, Brahmāṇḍa, Bhaviṣya and other Purāṇas; attribution is fluid in the pamphlet tradition. [disputed] [folk]
- **Kārttika-māhātmya — with Jalandhara, Vṛndā and Tulsī** — the merit of the month **Kārttika** (lamps, snāna, Tulsī service), carrying embedded within it the **Jalandhara–Vṛndā** epic: the asura born of Śiva's wrath rendered invincible by his wife's chastity, **Viṣṇu's deception of Vṛndā**, her curse, and her rebirth as the **tulsī plant** (charter of *Tulsī Vivāha*). (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa, Kārttika-māhātmya*) Cross-links: [`../../04-deep-dives/jalandhara-vrinda.md`](../../04-deep-dives/jalandhara-vrinda.md) (canonical home), [`../../04-deep-dives/masa-mahatmyas-kartika-magha-vaishakha.md`](../../04-deep-dives/masa-mahatmyas-kartika-magha-vaishakha.md).
  - *Variant (Śiva P. / Skanda P.):* the Śiva Purāṇa foregrounds Jalandhara's birth and death by Śiva's cakra; the Skanda adds further detail — see [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md). [disputed]
- **Māgha-māhātmya** — merit of Māgha bathing; with the Kārttika material, part of the month-glorification genre cataloged at [`../../04-deep-dives/masa-mahatmyas-kartika-magha-vaishakha.md`](../../04-deep-dives/masa-mahatmyas-kartika-magha-vaishakha.md). (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa*)
- **Gītā-māhātmya (18 chapters)** — one framing parable per *adhyāya* of the **Bhagavad-Gītā**, each a devotee liberated by recitation of that chapter. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa*) [late text] Cross-link: [`../../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md`](../../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md).
- **Bhāgavata-māhātmya** — **Bhakti** personified as a mother with fainting sons **Jñāna** and **Vairāgya**, revived by Bhāgavata recitation; with the **Gokarṇa–Dhundhukārī** ghost-liberation tale. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa*) [late text] Canonical home: [`../../04-deep-dives/bhagavata-mahatmya-gokarna-dhundhukari.md`](../../04-deep-dives/bhagavata-mahatmya-gokarna-dhundhukari.md).
  - *Variant (Skanda P.):* a closely parallel Bhāgavata-māhātmya circulates under the Skanda Purāṇa. [disputed]
- **Śiva-Gītā** — Śiva instructs **Rāma** in self-knowledge and grants the pāśupata; a Śaiva Gītā inside the Vaiṣṇava khaṇḍa. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa*) [interpolation] Canonical home: [`../../04-deep-dives/shiva-gita.md`](../../04-deep-dives/shiva-gita.md).
- **Guṇa-classification of the 18 Purāṇas** — the locus classicus (**Uttara 236**) sorting the Mahāpurāṇas into sāttvic/rājasic/tāmasic. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 236*) [late text] Covered in [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md).

## Khaṇḍa 7 — Kriyāyogasāra-khaṇḍa (26 chapters; the supplement the Bengal recension never knew) [late text]

- **The Jaimini–Vyāsa frame** — **Jaimini** asks **Vyāsa** how beings of the degenerate **Kali-yuga**, incapable of long meditation and Vedic rigor, can reach Viṣṇu; Vyāsa answers with **kriyā-yoga** — the *yoga of devotional action*: image-worship, tīrtha-bathing (with heavy praise of the **Gaṅgā**), vratas (Ekādaśī above all), tulasī and śālagrāma service. Sūta retells the dialogue at Naimiṣa. (*Padma P., Kriyāyogasāra-khaṇḍa 1–26*)
- **The exemplum story-chapters** — each teaching is driven home by a named tale: **Manobhadra**, the paired tale of **Mādhava and Sulocanā**, **Kālakalpa**, **Indra and Padmagandhā**, **Cakrikā**, and **Bhadratanu** — a self-contained story-anthology of Kali-yuga sinners and devotees saved by simple kriyā (a bath, a fast, a leaf of tulasī). (*Padma P., Kriyāyogasāra-khaṇḍa 1–26*)
- **Status** — wholly absent from the Bengal recension; printed as a seventh khaṇḍa (or an Uttara appendix) in the Veṅkaṭeśvara/AITM text; universally treated as among the Purāṇa's latest strata — its Kali-yuga-facing program dates it functionally as a medieval bhakti manual. [late text] [scholarly]
