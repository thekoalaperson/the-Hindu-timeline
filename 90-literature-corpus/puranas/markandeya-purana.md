# Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/puranas/` — a per-Purāṇa source file in the literature corpus.
> Up-link: [`../README.md`](../README.md) (the Purāṇas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (the literature corpus).
> See also the parent survey [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md), the companion [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md), and the deep-dive on the sage [`../../04-deep-dives/markandeya.md`](../../04-deep-dives/markandeya.md).

The **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa** is among the **oldest, shortest, and most distinctive** of the eighteen Mahāpurāṇas — celebrated above all as the **textual home of the Devī Māhātmya** (the *Durgā Saptaśatī* / *Caṇḍī*), the foundational scripture of **Śākta** Goddess-worship, embedded in its later chapters. It is named for the long-lived ṛṣi **Mārkaṇḍeya** (a *cirañjīvī*; see [`../../04-deep-dives/markandeya.md`](../../04-deep-dives/markandeya.md)) but uses an unusual **double frame**: the **Mīmāṃsā founder Jaimini** (Vyāsa's pupil) brings to Mārkaṇḍeya four moral puzzles left unresolved by the **Mahābhārata**, and Mārkaṇḍeya — pleading his own ongoing devotions — refers Jaimini instead to **four wise birds** (the *dharma-pakṣin*, sons of the sage Vipulāsva, reborn as birds by a curse) dwelling in the Vindhyas, who answer through interwoven stories. It is one of the **least sectarian** of the Purāṇas: it praises no single supreme deity as its banner, devotes major space to **Sūrya (the Sun)** and to the **Goddess**, and reads in places more as a treatise on dharma, karma, and yoga than as a partisan cult-text. Scholars (Pargiter, Hazra, Rocher, Doniger) consistently single it out for its **antiquity and relative freedom from interpolated *māhātmya* padding** in its core.

- **Structure — a single continuous text of ~137 chapters (*adhyāyas*), traditionally ~9,000 ślokas (~6,900 survive):** it is **not** divided into *khaṇḍas*, *aṃśas*, or *saṃhitās* like most large Purāṇas — a mark of its archaic, unitary character. Pargiter (1904, the standard English translation) groups it into functional blocks:
  - **The Jaimini frame & the four birds (chs. 1–9):** Jaimini's four Mahābhārata questions (Why was the impartial Viṣṇu born as a man? Why was Draupadī wife to five? Why did Balarāma expiate brahmanicide? Why were Draupadī's unmarried sons slain?); the curse-origin of the **four learned birds**; the birds begin to answer.
  - **Cosmology, ethics, yoga (chs. 10–44):** karma and rebirth, the torments of hell (*naraka*) and the nature of sin, the **duties of the householder**, *śrāddha*, the **bird-discourses** on dharma, and a compact treatise on **yoga and jñāna** (chs. ~39–43) with a notably **Advaita/jñāna-mārga** flavor — the **Madālasā–Alarka** cycle culminates here.
  - **The Mārkaṇḍeya core (chs. 45–80, 94–137):** at ch. 45 the nominal bird-speakers yield to the real teachers **Mārkaṇḍeya and his disciple Krauṣṭuki (Bhārgava)**: cosmogony and *pralaya*, the genealogies and royal lines, the geography of the *dvīpas* and **Bhārata-varṣa**, the **14 Manvantaras** (the most detailed of any Purāṇa), and a major **Sūrya** section (the births and exploits of the Sun, *vivasvat*, the Vaivasvata line).
  - **The Devī Māhātmya (chs. 81–93):** the self-contained "Greatness of the Goddess," 700 verses (*saptaśatī*), thirteen chapters — structurally a *digression* placed within the **Sāvarṇi-Manvantara** narrative (it is told to explain how the future 8th Manu, Sāvarṇi, won his rank by Goddess-worship).
- **Pañca-lakṣaṇa coverage:** covers all five marks but **unevenly** — strong on *manvantara* (its specialty) and *vaṃśa/vaṃśānucarita* (genealogies), with *sarga* and *pratisarga* present but compact; the bird-dialogues, the Madālasā ethics, the Sūrya glorification, and the Devī Māhātmya are **"surplus" material** beyond the classic five marks, typical of how real Purāṇas exceed the textbook definition.
- **Sectarian leaning:** **broadly non-sectarian / "smārta"** in its core — uniquely it lavishes attention on **Sūrya** (a near-Saura emphasis) — but the embedded **Devī Māhātmya makes it a, even *the*, primary Śākta scripture** [the Devī section is widely held to be a later insertion]. There is no anti-Śaiva or anti-Vaiṣṇava polemic. Cross-link the Goddess treatment: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md).
- **Dating:** *traditional:* spoken by Mārkaṇḍeya, eternal, narrated at the dawn of an age. *Academic [scholarly]:* its **core is among the earliest extant Purāṇa material** — **Pargiter, Hazra, Wilson, Rocher** placed the bulk to roughly the **3rd–7th c. CE**; **Doniger** dates the main text to **c. 250 CE** but the **Devī Māhātmya separately to c. 550 CE** [late text]. A **terminus ante quem** for the Devī Māhātmya is fixed by the **Dadhimati Mātā temple inscription of 608 CE**, which quotes it — confirming the Goddess-hymn existed by the early 7th century [scholarly]. Critical edition: K. M. Banerjea / later editions; standard translation **F. E. Pargiter, Asiatic Society of Bengal, 1904** (his historical conclusions are now widely disputed).

## Notable / distinctive stories and sections

### The Jaimini frame and the four questioning birds (chs. 1–9) — *the signature device*
- **Jaimini's four unanswered Mahābhārata questions** — the **Mīmāṃsā master Jaimini**, dissatisfied with riddles the epic left open, seeks out Mārkaṇḍeya, who deflects him to four birds; the birds' answers form the opening teachings, tying the Purāṇa explicitly to the **itihāsa** tradition. *(Chronology: frame device — the birds narrate from the timeless seat of Purāṇic instruction; the Mahābhārata events sit at the **Dvāpara–Kali junction** of the current 28th mahāyuga.)* Cross-link: [`../smriti-itihasa-epics.md`](../smriti-itihasa-epics.md) and [`../../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/README.md`](../../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/README.md).
- **The origin of the four learned birds (*dharma-pakṣin*: Piṅgākṣa, Vibodha, Suputra, Sumukha)** — in their bird-birth they are **sons of Droṇa and the apsaras Vapu** (whom Durvāsas had cursed to bird-form); in their prior human birth they were **sons of the brahmana Sukṛśa (grandsons of the sage Vipulāsva)**, cursed by their father to be reborn as birds yet retaining Vedic knowledge; nursed in the Vindhyas, they become enlightened expounders. A vivid sub-tale of curse, rebirth, and the persistence of *vidyā* across forms. *(Chronology: legendary antiquity — frame backstory; Pargiter tr., Cantos I–II.)*

### Madālasā and Alarka (chs. ~16–44) — *the jīvanmukta queen and her lullaby*
- **Queen Madālasā's Ātma-jñāna lullaby (Madālasā Upadeśa)** — the gandharva princess **Madālasā**, a liberated **brahmavādinī / jīvanmuktā** married to King **Ṛtadhvaja (Kuvalayāśva)**, rocks each crying infant son to sleep not with comfort but with **non-dual Vedānta**: *"śuddho'si buddho'si nirañjano'si…"* ("You are pure, awake, untainted; abandon the illusion of the body, this dream of *saṃsāra*"). Her first three sons (Vikrānta, Subāhu, Śatrumardana) renounce the world straightaway. *(Chronology: framework — a timeless dharma/jñāna teaching set in legendary royal antiquity.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md).
  - *Variant (within the text):* warned by her husband that an all-renouncing line will end the dynasty, Madālasā instructs her **fourth son Alarka** instead in **rāja-dharma and worldly duty**, giving him a ring with secret counsel to use when worldly life fails him — balancing *pravṛtti* (engagement) against *nivṛtti* (renunciation).
- **The instruction of King Alarka by Dattātreya** — when Alarka's kingdom is later threatened (by his own ascetic brother Subāhu, to provoke his awakening), he opens his mother's ring, follows her counsel to the avadhūta **Dattātreya**, and receives a full course in **yoga and the realization of the Self**, finally renouncing — the Purāṇa's principal **yoga/jñāna treatise** (chs. ~39–43). *(Chronology: framework — yoga doctrine embedded in legendary narrative.)* Cross-link: [`../../04-deep-dives/dattatreya.md`](../../04-deep-dives/dattatreya.md) and [`../../04-deep-dives/yoga-sutras-of-patanjali.md`](../../04-deep-dives/yoga-sutras-of-patanjali.md).

### Harishchandra (chs. ~7–8, in the bird-dialogues) — *the truthful king tested*
- **The trials of Satyavādī Hariścandra** — the **Sūryavaṃśa** (Ikṣvāku-line) king, by his unbending vow of truth, is stripped of kingdom, wife (Śaivyā), and son to honor a debt to the sage **Viśvāmitra**; sold into servitude, he becomes a keeper of the **cremation ground (caṇḍāla's slave)** and is forced to demand the funeral-fee even for his own dead son — yet never lies, and is finally restored and exalted to heaven. The Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa is the **principal Purāṇic source** for this beloved morality-tale (echoing an older Vedic/Aitareya-Brāhmaṇa Hariścandra–Śunaḥśepa nucleus). *(Chronology: legendary antiquity — **Tretā/early epoch**, Ikṣvāku/Solar dynasty.)* Cross-link: [`../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md`](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) and [`../../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md`](../../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md).
  - *Variant (Vedic — Aitareya Brāhmaṇa):* the older Hariścandra story centers on his vow to **Varuṇa** and the near-sacrifice of his son Rohita, resolved by the sage **Śunaḥśepa** — the truth-test/Viśvāmitra framing is the **Purāṇic/Mārkaṇḍeya** development [scholarly].
  - *Variant (Devī Bhāgavata & later folk/theatre):* later retellings (and the famous 1913 film *Raja Harishchandra*) amplify the cremation-ground pathos and the rivalry of Viśvāmitra and **Vasiṣṭha** over the king.

### The 14 Manvantaras (chs. ~45–80, 94–) — *the text's specialty*
- **The fullest Purāṇic Manvantara account** — Mārkaṇḍeya and Krauṣṭuki narrate all **fourteen Manus**, their Indras, sapta-ṛṣis, deva-classes, and the avatāra/aṃśa of Viṣṇu in each cycle — the **most detailed of any Mahāpurāṇa**, a base text for the manvantara framework. *(Chronology: spans the fourteen manvantaras of the current kalpa; the present is the **Vaivasvata, the 7th**.)* Cross-link: [`../../01-manvantaras/README.md`](../../01-manvantaras/README.md).
- **The birth of Vaivasvata Manu and the Sūrya cycle** — an extended **glorification of Sūrya** (the Sun): Vivasvat's marriage to Saṃjñā/Chāyā, the **trimming of the Sun's excess radiance** by Viśvakarman, and the descent of the **Solar dynasty** through Vaivasvata Manu — the Purāṇa's near-Saura highlight. *(Chronology: the **Vaivasvata manvantara**, opening the present age of humanity.)* Cross-link: [`../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md`](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md) and the Sun among the grahas [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md).
- **The future 8th Manu, Sāvarṇi, and the frame of the Devī Māhātmya** — the account of the coming **Sāvarṇi manvantara** is the very setting into which the Goddess-glory is inserted: King Suratha and the merchant Samādhi worship the Devī and Suratha is destined to be reborn as **Sāvarṇi Manu**. *(Chronology: the **future 8th (Sāvarṇi) manvantara**.)* Cross-link: [`../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-08-savarni.md`](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-08-savarni.md).

### The Devī Māhātmya / Durgā Saptaśatī (chs. 81–93) — *the crown jewel* [late text]
- **The Devī Māhātmya ("Glory of the Goddess"), 700 verses, 13 chapters** — the **oldest extant text proclaiming the Goddess (Mahādevī) as the supreme reality and creatrix**, recited daily and especially at **Navarātri/Durgā Pūjā**; the cornerstone of **Śākta** theology. Framed as the sage **Medhas** instructing the dethroned king **Suratha** and the merchant **Samādhi**, it narrates three great Goddess-victories across three *caritas*. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine combat outside ordinary time; narratively set in the lead-up to the **Sāvarṇi manvantara**.)* Cross-link the dedicated file: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md).
  - **Prathama-carita (ch. 1) — Mahākālī** — the Goddess as **Yoganidrā/Mahāmāyā** rouses Viṣṇu from cosmic sleep to slay the demons **Madhu and Kaiṭabha** (who arose from his ear-wax to threaten Brahmā). *(Cosmology — at a pralaya/creation boundary.)*
  - **Madhyama-carita (chs. 2–4) — Mahālakṣmī** — the gods' combined splendor coalesces into **Durgā**, who rides her lion and destroys the buffalo-demon **Mahiṣāsura** — the iconic **Mahiṣāsuramardinī** episode. *(Cosmology — deva–asura war.)*
  - **Uttama-carita (chs. 5–13) — Mahāsarasvatī** — the campaigns against **Śumbha and Niśumbha** and their generals **Dhūmralocana, Caṇḍa-Muṇḍa** (whence **Cāmuṇḍā**), **Raktabīja** (each blood-drop spawning a clone, drunk up by **Kālī**), capped by the **Devī's declaration of her own supremacy** and the promise to incarnate again. The hymns — **Tantroktaṃ Devīsūktam, Aparājitā-stuti ("Yā Devī sarvabhūteṣu…")** — are its devotional heart. *(Cosmology — deva–asura war.)* Cross-link the emanated goddesses: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md) and [`../../06-sacred-geography/51-shakti-peethas.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/51-shakti-peethas.md).
  - *Variant (ritual *aṅgas*):* in worship the 13 chapters are bracketed by added limbs — **Kavaca, Argalā, Kīlaka** prologues and the **Rātri-/Devī-sūkta** — and the **Vaikṛtika Rahasya, Prādhānika Rahasya, Mūrti Rahasya** appendices; these are **liturgical accretions** not part of the original Purāṇic text [interpolation].
  - *Variant (Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa):* the later **Devī-Bhāgavata** expands the same Śākta theology into a full 12-skandha Purāṇa, re-telling Mahiṣa and Śumbha-Niśumbha at greater length and asserting the Goddess above the Trimūrti — a downstream development of the Mārkaṇḍeya's compact original [late text].

### Cosmography, dharma, and the afterlife (chs. 10–44, 54–)
- **The geography of the *dvīpas* and Bhārata-varṣa** — the concentric continents and oceans, Mount Meru, and the praise of **Bhārata** as the land of *karma*; standard Purāṇic *bhuvana-koṣa*. *(Chronology: framework — cosmic geography of the present kalpa.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/README.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/README.md).
- **Karma, the hells (naraka), and śrāddha** — among the **earliest systematic Purāṇic descriptions of the hells**, the mechanics of sin and rebirth, and the rites for ancestors, delivered in the bird-dialogues; a much-cited source for Hindu eschatology. *(Chronology: framework — dharma applicable across yugas.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
- **Dattātreya and the Haihaya/Kārtavīrya connection** — the avadhūta **Dattātreya** appears both as Alarka's teacher and in the broader Haihaya lineage; the Purāṇa is an early witness to the Dattātreya cult. *(Chronology: legendary antiquity.)* Cross-link: [`../../04-deep-dives/dattatreya.md`](../../04-deep-dives/dattatreya.md).
