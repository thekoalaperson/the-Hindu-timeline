# Śiva Purāṇa

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/puranas/` — a per-Purāṇa source file in the literature corpus.
> Up-link: [`../README.md`](../README.md) (the Purāṇas index) · [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) (the literature corpus).
> See also the parent survey [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md) and the [`../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md`](../bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).

The **Śiva Purāṇa** (*Śiva Mahāpurāṇa*) is the **Śaiva master-text** of the Purāṇic corpus — a sprawling devotional encyclopedia centered wholly on **Śiva/Maheśvara/Rudra** as the supreme reality (*para-brahman*), with the **liṅga** as the prime object of worship and the **bhakti** of Śiva as the principal soteriology. Where the Bhāgavata is the great Vaiṣṇava book, the Śiva Purāṇa is its Śaiva counterpart: it gathers the liṅga-theology, the Sati–Pārvatī cycle, the births of Gaṇeśa and Kārttikeya, the great demon-slayings (Tripura, Andhaka, Jālandhara), the conquest of Death (Kālāntaka), and the catalog of the **twelve jyotirliṅgas** into one corpus. Its narration frame is the sage **Sūta Romaharṣaṇa / Lomaharṣaṇa** reciting it to the ṛṣis at Naimiṣāraṇya, the digest of an immense original taught by Śiva → Brahmā → the sages.

- **Structure (seven *saṃhitās*)** — the standard surviving (Kāśī / Veṅkaṭeśvara, 1906) recension is divided into **seven saṃhitās**, some with nested *khaṇḍas*, totaling **~457 adhyāyas** and (traditionally cited) **24,000 ślokas** in this abridged form:
  - **Vidyeśvara-saṃhitā** (~25 ch.) — the glory of Śiva and the liṅga; the **Liṅgodbhava** charter myth; merits of liṅga-worship, *rudrākṣa*, *bhasma* (ash), and the *Praṇava* (Oṃ); the frame-narrative.
  - **Rudra-saṃhitā** (~197 ch.) — the heart of the text, in **five khaṇḍas**: *Sṛṣṭi* (creation), *Satī* (Sati & the Dakṣa-yajña), *Pārvatī* (Pārvatī's rebirth, tapas, marriage), *Kumāra* (births of Gaṇeśa & Kārttikeya), and *Yuddha* (the demon-wars — Tripura, Jālandhara, Śaṅkhacūḍa, Andhaka).
  - **Śatarudra-saṃhitā** (~42 ch.) — the **manifestations/forms of Śiva** ("the hundred Rudras"): avatāras, Bhairava, Hanumān as a Rudra-aṃśa, Nandin, the Vīrabhadra material.
  - **Koṭirudra-saṃhitā** (~43 ch.) — the **twelve jyotirliṅgas** described in detail, plus countless liṅga-māhātmyas ("a crore of Rudras").
  - **Umā-saṃhitā** (~51 ch.) — Devī/Umā theology, Śākta material, cosmology, the hells (*naraka*) and karma.
  - **Kailāsa-saṃhitā** (~23 ch.) — esoteric/yogic: Mount Kailāsa, Śiva's celestial court, the *Praṇava*, *prāṇāyāma*, *sannyāsa*, and tantric-yogic teaching (a Śaiva *gītā* layer).
  - **Vāyavīya-saṃhitā** (~76 ch., *Pūrva-* + *Uttara-bhāga*) — narrated "by Vāyu"; Pāśupata/Śaiva-Siddhānta-flavored doctrine, cosmogony, and the **Śiva-gītā**-type philosophical core. (Its very name underlies the Vāyu-vs-Śiva confusion noted below.)
  - *Variant (the twelve-saṃhitā ur-text):* the text claims an original of **100,000 ślokas in twelve saṃhitās** (adding e.g. *Sanatkumāra, Īśāna, Mātṛ, Sāṃkhya/Devī, Sāhasra-koṭi-rudra* saṃhitās), abridged by **Vyāsa**; manuscript traditions also circulate a different seven-saṃhitā ordering and a separate **Jñāna-saṃhitā** recension. [late text]
- **Pañca-lakṣaṇa coverage** — present but *subordinated to bhakti and māhātmya*: *sarga/pratisarga* (creation/dissolution from Śiva) appear in the Rudra-saṃhitā Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa and the Vāyavīya/Umā cosmology; *manvantara* and *vaṃśa/vaṃśānucarita* are thin and largely conventional. The genuine center of gravity is **Śiva-māhātmya, liṅga/tīrtha glorification, vrata, and devotional narrative** rather than systematic chronicle. (Cross-link the framework: [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/) and [`../../01-manvantaras/`](../../01-manvantaras/).)
- **Sectarian leaning** — emphatically **Śaiva**, with strong **Śaiva-Advaita** philosophy (non-dual Śiva mixed with theistic *bhakti*) and **Pāśupata / Śaiva-Siddhānta** strata; it repeatedly asserts **Śiva-primacy** — Brahmā and Viṣṇu are shown searching for the limits of Śiva, Viṣṇu is the obedient devotee, and other gods become mere components of Śiva's weapon-system. Yet it is **integrative**, not hostile: Viṣṇu is honored (and his honesty rewarded), and the Umā-saṃhitā folds in Śākta Devī theology.
- **Dating** — *traditional:* eternal, taught by Śiva himself at the dawn of creation, abridged by Vyāsa. *Academic [scholarly]:* the **oldest surviving chapters are placed c. 10th–11th century CE**, with much material later (some after the 14th c.); Klostermaier and Hazra give the 10th–11th c.; Bibek Debroy places the **extant text c. 1000–1400 CE**, taking its current shape c. 1000–1100 CE. The text is thus a **late compilation** [late text] over older Śaiva kernels.

### The major canon variant — Vāyu Purāṇa as the 18th [disputed]

- The **single most contested seat** in the list of eighteen *Mahāpurāṇas* is the one filled by the Śiva Purāṇa. **Many traditional lists count the *Vāyu Purāṇa* (~24,000 v.) as the 18th instead of the Śiva Purāṇa**, while others count the Śiva Purāṇa and **drop Vāyu** to keep the total at eighteen:
  - Lists that name **Vāyu** (and omit Śiva): the **Matsya, Vāyu (self-naming), Liṅga, Kūrma, Agni**, and **Bhāgavata** enumerations.
  - Lists that name **Śiva** (and omit Vāyu): the **Padma** and **Viṣṇu** enumerations, among others.
- The 11th-c. Persian scholar **Al-Bīrūnī** recorded **two competing lists** he had learned, both beginning with the Brahma Purāṇa: one places **Śiva as no. 4 with no Vāyu**, the other places **Vāyu as no. 7 with no Śiva** — fixing this swap as old and well-attested. [scholarly]
- **Why the confusion runs deep:** the Śiva Purāṇa's own seventh section is the **Vāyavīya-saṃhitā** ("of Vāyu"), and in the 19th–20th c. the **Vāyu Purāṇa was sometimes titled "Śiva Purāṇa" or treated as a part of it**. With more manuscripts, modern scholarship treats them as **distinct texts**, with the **Vāyu Purāṇa the older** (a stratum likely before the 2nd c. CE) and the Śiva Purāṇa a **later substitute** for it in the canon. Many scholars therefore regard *Vāyu* as the genuinely ancient "great" Purāṇa and *Śiva* as the medieval replacement. (Cross-link the survey of this dispute: [`../smriti-puranas.md`](../smriti-puranas.md) → "Canonical-list variants (the contested 18th seats)".)
  - *Variant (mnemonic verse):* the standard mnemonic "*ma-dvayaṃ bha-dvayaṃ caiva...*" was devised to **lock the canonical 18** against exactly this drift; depending on regional recitation it may resolve the slot to Śiva or to Vāyu.

## Notable / distinctive stories and sections

### Liṅgodbhava — the origin of the liṅga and jyotirliṅga (the charter myth)
- **Liṅgodbhava ("the rising of the liṅga")** — **Brahmā** and **Viṣṇu** quarrel over who is supreme; to settle it, Śiva manifests as an **infinite pillar of fire/light (the jyotirliṅga)** piercing all three worlds, and challenges them to find its ends. **Viṣṇu** (as the boar **Varāha**) dives down, **Brahmā** (on his swan **haṃsa**) flies up; neither reaches an end. Viṣṇu honestly admits failure; **Brahmā lies, claiming to have found the top**, producing a fallen **ketakī (kewra) flower** as false witness. The enraged Śiva curses **Brahmā to receive (almost) no worship** and the **ketakī flower to be barred from his worship**, while **Viṣṇu, for his honesty, is to be eternally worshipped**. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — a primordial, timeless theophany at the start of creation.)* Cross-link the summary with these variants: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) §1.
  - *Variant (the false witnesses):* alongside the ketakī flower, Brahmā suborns the **cow (Kāmadhenu / Surabhi)** as a co-witness; she nods "yes" with her head but signals "no" with her tail, so she is half-cursed (her mouth-end deemed impure, her hind-end sacred). (*Śaiva tradition; cf. Liṅga & Skanda Purāṇas*)
  - *Variant (cross-text):* the Liṅgodbhava is shared by the **Liṅga, Kūrma, Vāmana, Skanda, Brahmāṇḍa, and Vāyu Purāṇas** with slight differences; the Śiva Purāṇa's telling (Vidyeśvara-saṃhitā) is the locus classicus that fixes it as the etiology of liṅga-worship and of **Mahāśivarātri** as the day the pillar appeared.

### The twelve jyotirliṅgas (Koṭirudra-saṃhitā)
- **The twelve jyotirliṅgas (*dvādaśa jyotirliṅga*)** — the Koṭirudra-saṃhitā enumerates and glorifies the **twelve "liṅgas of light"**, each a self-manifest (*svayambhū*) form of Śiva at a sacred site, each with its own origin-legend: **Somnāth** (Saurāṣṭra), **Mallikārjuna** (Śrīśailam), **Mahākāleśvara** (Ujjain), **Oṃkāreśvara** (Māndhātā), **Kedārnāth** (Himalaya), **Bhīmaśaṅkar**, **Viśveśvara/Kāśī Viśvanāth** (Vārāṇasī), **Tryambakeśvara** (Godavari source), **Vaidyanāth** (Deoghar), **Nāgeśvara**, **Rāmeśvaram**, and **Ghṛṣṇeśvara/Gṛṣṇeśvar** (Ellora). *(Chronology: framework — Kali-Yuga sacred geography, though the founding legends are set in mythic antiquity.)* Cross-link the dedicated catalog: [`../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md).
  - *Variant (originally 64):* the tradition holds there were originally **64 jyotirliṅgas**, of which these twelve are the supreme; lists of the twelve **differ on a few seats** (e.g. **Vaidyanāth** identified with Deoghar vs Parli; **Nāgeśvara** located variously in Dwarka, Aundha, or Almora). (*regional sthala-purāṇas*)
  - *Variant (Mahākāla origin):* the **Mahākāleśvara** liṅga at Ujjain arises when Śiva bursts forth to destroy the demon **Dūṣaṇa** threatening the devotee-boy, and remains as the only *svayambhū* south-facing (*dakṣiṇāmūrti*) jyotirliṅga. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā*)

### Sati, the Dakṣa-yajña, and the rebirth as Pārvatī (Rudra-saṃhitā, Satī- & Pārvatī-khaṇḍas)
- **Satī, the Dakṣa-yajña, and Vīrabhadra** — **Satī**, daughter of **Dakṣa**, marries Śiva against her father's contempt; humiliated at Dakṣa's grand sacrifice (to which Śiva is pointedly not invited), she **immolates herself in yogic fire**. The grief-maddened Śiva tears a lock of his hair to create the colossal **Vīrabhadra** (with **Bhadrakālī**), who destroys the *yajña* and **beheads Dakṣa** — later restored with a **goat's head**. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine biography, timeless; pre-Pārvatī.)* Cross-link the full event: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) §3.
  - *Variant (Śakti Pīṭhas):* in the version where Śiva carries the corpse and Viṣṇu's discus dismembers it, Satī's scattered body-parts become the **Śakti Pīṭhas** — the Śiva Purāṇa carries a Śaiva framing of this Śākta etiology. Cross-link [`../../06-sacred-geography/51-shakti-peethas.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/51-shakti-peethas.md).
- **Pārvatī's rebirth, Kāma-dahana, and the marriage** — Satī is reborn as **Pārvatī**, daughter of **Himavān**; the gods send **Kāmadeva** to rekindle desire in the ascetic Śiva, who **burns Kāma to ash with his third eye** (later restored bodiless as *Ananga*); Pārvatī wins Śiva through severe **tapas**, and the divine wedding follows. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine biography; the marriage is prerequisite to the birth of Kārttikeya, needed to slay Tārakāsura.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md) §§6–9.

### The births of the two sons (Rudra-saṃhitā, Kumāra-khaṇḍa)
- **Birth of Gaṇeśa and the elephant head** — Pārvatī fashions **Gaṇeśa** from the turmeric/paste of her own body to guard her door; he bars Śiva, who in fury **beheads** him; to console Pārvatī, Śiva replaces the head with that of an **elephant**, and Gaṇeśa becomes lord of the *gaṇas* and remover of obstacles (*Vighneśvara*). *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine biography, timeless.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md) (Birth & the elephant head).
- **Birth of Kārttikeya/Skanda and the slaying of Tārakāsura** — Śiva's seed, too fiery to be borne, passes through **Agni** and **Gaṅgā** to the **Kṛttikā** (Pleiades) and is born as the six-faced **Kārttikeya/Skanda**, war-general of the gods, who slays the otherwise-invincible demon **Tārakāsura**. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — the purpose of the whole Pārvatī cycle; divine biography.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md) (Part II — Kārttikeya / Skanda / Murugan).

### The great demon-slayings (Rudra-saṃhitā, Yuddha-khaṇḍa)
- **Tripura-dahana / Tripurāntaka — the burning of the three cities** — three asura brothers (**Tārakākṣa, Vidyunmālī, Kamalākṣa**, sons of Tāraka) win three indestructible flying cities (**Tripura**) of gold, silver, and iron, destructible only by a **single arrow at the instant of their alignment**. Śiva mounts a **cosmic chariot** (Earth = body, Sun & Moon = wheels, Meru = bow, Vāsuki = bowstring, Viṣṇu = arrow, Brahmā = charioteer) and incinerates all three. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — a Śaiva cosmic deed, timeless; festival link **Tripurī Pūrṇimā / Kārttika Pūrṇimā**.)* Cross-link the variants (the "burning smile," Viṣṇu-as-deluding-monk): [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) §3.
- **Andhaka-vadha — the blind/darkness demon** — **Andhaka** (born of Śiva's own sweat, raised by Hiraṇyākṣa) lusts after Pārvatī, unaware she is in effect his mother; Śiva impales him on the trident and holds him aloft until his pride burns away. Each falling blood-drop spawns a clone, so the **Mātṛkās / Saptamātṛkā** drink the blood before it lands; the chastened Andhaka becomes the gaṇa **Bhṛṅgi**. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine deed.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) §3.
  - *Variant (redemption vs slaying):* in the Śiva/Kūrma/Liṅga strand Andhaka is **converted into a devotee** and made a gaṇa-chief rather than destroyed — "salvation through impalement." (*Śiva Purāṇa; Kūrma/Liṅga Purāṇa*)
- **Jālandhara-vadha & the Vṛndā deception** — **Jālandhara**, an immensely powerful asura born of Śiva's fiery glance fallen into the ocean, is invincible through the **pativratā-chastity of his wife Vṛndā**. **Viṣṇu** assumes Jālandhara's form and tricks Vṛndā into breaking her vow; her power shattered, Śiva slays Jālandhara with a **discus spun from his own toe**. The betrayed Vṛndā curses Viṣṇu (→ the **śālagrāma**) and immolates herself, reborn as the sacred **Tulasī**. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — divine deed; folds a Vaiṣṇava Tulasī-etiology into a Śaiva victory.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) §3.
  - *Variant (Śaṅkhacūḍa):* the Yuddha-khaṇḍa pairs Jālandhara with the parallel **Śaṅkhacūḍa** episode — another asura protected by his wife **Tulasī's** chastity, broken by Viṣṇu in disguise, then slain by Śiva's trident; the two stories cross-pollinate across recensions. (*Rudra-saṃhitā*)

### Conquering Death — Mārkaṇḍeya & Kālāntaka
- **Mārkaṇḍeya and Kālāntaka ("Ender of Time/Death")** — the boy-sage **Mārkaṇḍeya**, son of **Mṛkaṇḍu**, is fated to die at **sixteen**; a perfect Śiva-devotee, on the appointed day he embraces the **liṅga**. When **Yama** flings his noose, it loops the liṅga itself; Śiva bursts forth as **Kālāntaka**, strikes down (in some tellings slays) Yama, and grants Mārkaṇḍeya **deathless eternal youth at sixteen**. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — the theological seat of the **Mahā-Mṛtyuñjaya / Tryambaka mantra**, Ṛgveda 7.59.12; Mārkaṇḍeya thereafter is the deathless sage who witnesses the pralaya.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) §3.
  - *Variant (Yama restored):* because Yama is indispensable to cosmic order, Śiva **revives** him after the lesson; "Kālāntaka" thus means *master* of death, not its abolition. (*Śiva Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (mantra-source):* the death-conquering mantra is taught to Mārkaṇḍeya beforehand (by Brahmā or by his father), which is what makes him invulnerable. (*Śaiva tradition*)

### Devotional, ritual, and esoteric layers
- **Liṅga, rudrākṣa, bhasma, and the Praṇava (Vidyeśvara- & Kailāsa-saṃhitās)** — extensive prescriptions on liṅga-worship, the merit of **rudrākṣa** beads and sacred **ash (vibhūti)**, the five-syllable **Pañcākṣara (Oṃ Namaḥ Śivāya)**, and the **Praṇava (Oṃ)** as Śiva's sound-body. *(Chronology: framework — applicable across yugas; living ritual.)* Cross-link: [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/).
- **Forms of Rudra and Hanumān as a Rudra-aṃśa (Śatarudra-saṃhitā)** — the "hundred Rudras": Śiva's many manifestations and avatāras, including the identification of **Hanumān** as an *aṃśa* of Rudra. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology; the Hanumān aspect resonates with the Tretā-Yuga Rāmāyaṇa.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md).
- **Naraka, karma, and Devī theology (Umā-saṃhitā)** — the **hells** and their punishments, karma and rebirth, and Goddess (Umā/Śakti) doctrine — motivating *dāna* and *vrata*. *(Chronology: framework/cosmology — the naraka-lokas, timeless.)* Cross-link: [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md).
- **Yoga, sannyāsa, and Kailāsa (Kailāsa- & Vāyavīya-saṃhitās)** — *prāṇāyāma*, *dhyāna*, renunciation, the description of **Mount Kailāsa** and Śiva-loka, and a Pāśupata/Śaiva-Siddhānta philosophical core (a *Śiva-gītā*-type teaching). *(Chronology: framework/cosmology.)* Cross-link: [`../../06-sacred-geography/meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md`](../../06-sacred-geography/meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md).
