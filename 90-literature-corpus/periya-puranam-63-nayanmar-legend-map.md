# The Periya Purāṇam — Legend Map of the 63 Nāyaṉmār (Tirumurai Book 12)

> Hierarchy location: `90-literature-corpus/` — a per-saint map of the Tamil Śaiva hagiographic epic. Its saints live in the present **Kali Yuga** (academically c. 6th–9th c. CE [scholarly]); its frame opens in timeless Kailāsa.
> Up-link: [`./README.md`](README.md) · Parent survey: [`./tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md`](tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md) · Saint roster: [`../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/alvars-and-nayanars.md`](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/alvars-and-nayanars.md)

The **Periya Purāṇam** (properly **Tiruttoṇṭar Purāṇam**, "Purāṇa of the Holy Servants") is Cēkkiḻār's (Sekkizhar's) epic of **4,286 Tamil verses**, composed **c. 1135 CE** under the Chola **Kulōttuṅga II (Anapāya)** [scholarly]. It expands **Sundarar's** 11-verse *Tiruttoṇṭattokai* (Tēvāram 7.39) and **Nambiyāṇḍār Nambi's** *Tiruttoṇṭar Tiruvandāti* into lives of the **63 individual Nāyaṉmār plus 9 collective bodies of devotees**, in **13 carukkams (cantos)** named from the *Tiruttoṇṭattokai* verse-openings. Canonized as **Book 12 of the Tirumurai**, it is the definitive legend-source of Tamil Śaivism.

## The composition legend — "Ulagelām"

- **Cēkkiḻār rebukes the king** — Cēkkiḻār (born Aruḷmoḻittēvar of Kuṉṟattūr, the king's chief minister) admonishes the king for delighting in the Jain epic *Cīvaka Cintāmaṇi* and points him to the lives of Śiva's servants; the king commissions the epic. (*Cēkkiḻār Purāṇam* of Umāpati Śivācārya, c. 1313 CE) [late text]
- **The grace-word "Ulagelām"** — at Chidambaram (Tillai) Śiva himself, a voice from the sanctum, gives Cēkkiḻār the opening word **"ulakelām"** ("all the world"); the epic begins and ends with it. (*Cēkkiḻār Purāṇam*) [late text]
- **The year-long arangetram** — Cēkkiḻār composes and premieres the work in the **Thousand-Pillared Hall** at Chidambaram; tradition holds the recital lasted a year, the king attending. Cross-link: [`../06-sacred-geography/pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md`](../06-sacred-geography/pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md). (*Cēkkiḻār Purāṇam*) [late text]
- **Cēkkiḻār venerated in turn** — later tradition gives him images and a guru-pūjā day. [folk]

## The frame: Sundarar's descent and return

- **Kailāsa prologue** — the epic opens on **Kailāsa**: the gana **Ālāla Sundarar**, distracted by love for two of Pārvatī's attendants, is sent to be born as **Sundarar (Nampi Ārūraṉ)**; the maidens become his wives **Paravai** and **Saṅgili**. (Periya Purāṇam, Tirumalai carukkam)
- **"Seized as a slave"** — at Sundarar's wedding an old brāhmaṇa halts the rite with a palm-leaf deed proving him his hereditary slave — Śiva of Tiruveṇṇainallūr in disguise, who bids him sing; the first hymn addresses Śiva as "madman" (*pittā*). (Periya Purāṇam, Taṭuttāṭkoṇṭa Purāṇam)
- **The Tiruttoṇṭattokai revealed** — at Tiruvārūr, prompted by **Viranmiṇḍar's** rebuke and Śiva's command beginning "Tillai vāḻ antaṇar," Sundarar sings the 11-verse hymn naming Śiva's servants — the seed-list the epic expands. (Periya Purāṇam; Tēvāram 7.39)
- **The white-elephant finale** — Sundarar returns to Kailāsa on a **white elephant**, while the Chera king **Cheramān Perumāḷ (Kaḻaṟiṟṟaṟivār)** whispers the pañcākṣara into his horse's ear and rides after him through the sky. (Periya Purāṇam, Vellāṉai carukkam)

## Saints of extreme giving

- **Iyarpagai** ("never-refuser") — merchant of Pukār who, asked by Śiva-in-disguise for his own wife, surrenders her, escorts the pair, and cuts down his outraged kinsmen before Śiva reveals himself. (Periya Purāṇam, Iyarpagai Purāṇam)
- **Amarnīti** — merchant holding a young ascetic's loincloth in trust; when it vanishes, he mounts the balance-scale with wealth, wife, son, and finally himself to equal it — the scale rises to heaven. (Periya Purāṇam, Amarnīti Purāṇam)
- **Ilaiyāṉkuḍi Māṟar** — farmer famed for feasting devotees; ruined, he scrapes up freshly-sown seed-paddy at midnight to feed one more guest — who is Śiva. (Periya Purāṇam, Ilaiyāṉkuḍi Māṟa Purāṇam)
- **Kuṅkiliya Kalayar** — sells his wife's marriage-necklace (tāli) for a last basket of *kuṅkiliyam* (frankincense) to burn before Śiva; the Lord fills his house with grain, and he later rights the tilted Tiruppanandāl liṅga with a rope round his neck where elephants failed. (Periya Purāṇam, Kuṅkiliya Kalaya Purāṇam)
- **Māṉakañcāṟar** — chieftain who, on his daughter's wedding day, cuts off her tresses for a Śaiva ascetic needing hair for a waist-cord; Śiva restores them. (Periya Purāṇam, Māṉakañcāṟa Purāṇam)
- **Kōṭpuli** — Chola commander who stores paddy for temple service; finding his kinsmen ate it in famine, he puts the whole clan to the sword — Śiva raises the slain to his world. Sundarar later accepts Kōṭpuli's daughters as his own. (Periya Purāṇam, Kōṭpuli Purāṇam)
- **Ātipattar** — fisherman who dedicates the first fish of every catch to Śiva; reduced to one fish a day, he releases it daily — even a fish of gold. (Periya Purāṇam, Ātipatta Purāṇam)
- **Neca** — weaver who ceaselessly recites the pañcākṣara and clothes every devotee. (Periya Purāṇam, Neca Purāṇam)
- **Kaṇampulla** — sells *kaṇampul* grass to fund temple lamps; destitute, he burns his own hair as the wick. (Periya Purāṇam, Kaṇampulla Purāṇam)
- **Kaliya** — ruined oil-monger who prepares to fill the lamps with his own blood; Śiva stays his hand. (Periya Purāṇam, Kaliya Purāṇam)

## Saints of fierce zeal

- **Meypporuḷ** — Śaiva king of Cēdi stabbed by his enemy Muttanāthaṉ, disguised in ascetic's ash; dying, he orders his guard to escort the assassin unharmed — "he wears the sacred ash." (Periya Purāṇam, Meypporuḷ Purāṇam)
- **Eṟipattar** — axe-bearing devotee of Karuvūr who kills the royal elephant and mahouts for snatching a devotee's flower-basket; king **Pugazh Chola** offers his own life in atonement, and Śiva restores the slain. (Periya Purāṇam, Eṟipatta Purāṇam)
- **Chaṇḍeśvara (Caṇṭēcura Nāyaṉār)** — the brāhmaṇa boy Vicāra Śarman bathes a sand-liṅga in the cows' surplus milk; when his father kicks the liṅga, the boy severs the father's leg with a stick that becomes an axe. Śiva crowns him with His own garland as **Caṇḍeśa**, steward of His household and food-offerings — hence the Caṇḍeśvara shrine in Śiva temples. (Periya Purāṇam, Caṇṭēcura Purāṇam)
- **Kaṇṇappar** — hunter of Kāḷatti (Kalahasti) who offers meat tasted on his tongue and, seeing the liṅga's eye bleed, gouges out one eye, then — marking the spot with his toe — begins on the second; Śiva's hand stops him. (Periya Purāṇam, Kaṇṇappa Purāṇam)
- **Kalikkampar** — merchant who washes devotees' feet; when his wife hesitates to serve a former servant turned devotee, he cuts off her hand. (Periya Purāṇam, Kalikkampa Purāṇam)
- **Catti** — cuts out the tongues of those who revile Śiva or His devotees. (Periya Purāṇam, Catti Purāṇam)
- **Ceṟuttuṇai and Kaḻaṟciṅkar** — the temple-servant Ceṟuttuṇai cuts off the nose of the queen who smelled a flower meant for Śiva; her husband, the Pallava king Kaḻaṟciṅkar (identified with Narasiṃhavarman II [scholarly] [disputed]), rules the offending hand should have gone first — and cuts it off. (Periya Purāṇam, respective Purāṇams)
- **Eṉātināthar** — toddy-tapper-caste fencing master cut down when he drops his guard rather than strike an opponent who has cunningly donned the sacred ash. (Periya Purāṇam, Eṉātinātha Purāṇam)

## Saints of ordeal and paradox

- **Tirunīlakaṇṭar the potter** — bound by his wife's oath "by Tirunīlakaṇṭam" never to touch her, the couple grow old in secret celibacy; Śiva entrusts a begging-bowl, makes it vanish, and forces them to swear innocence hand-in-hand in the temple tank — they emerge young. (Periya Purāṇam, Tirunīlakaṇṭa Purāṇam)
- **Ciṟuttoṇṭar (Siruthondar)** — Paranjōti, the general who took Vātāpi for Narasiṃhavarman I (642 CE [scholarly]), retires to Tirucceṅkāṭṭaṅkuḍi; Śiva as a Bhairava ascetic demands a curry of his only son **Cīrāḷaṉ**, cooked by the parents; commanded to call the boy to table, Ciṟuttoṇṭar calls — and the child comes running, alive. (Periya Purāṇam, Ciṟuttoṇṭa Purāṇam)
- **Tirunāḷaippōvār (Nandanar)** — the Pulaiyar (untouchable) laborer who ever says "I will go tomorrow" to Chidambaram; at Tiruppuṅkūr Nandi shifts aside for him; at Chidambaram he passes through fire at the Lord's command and emerges a sage, merging into the sanctum. (Periya Purāṇam, Tirunāḷaippōvār Purāṇam)
  - *Variant (Gopalakrishna Bharati, 19th c.):* the *Nandanar Charitram* opera adds the landlord and paddy-field miracles, not in Cēkkiḻār. [late text] [regional]
- **Tirukkuṟipputtoṇṭar** — washerman of Kāñcī; when unseasonal clouds (Śiva's trick) keep an ascetic's rag from drying by dusk as promised, he dashes his head on the washing-stone — Śiva's hand catches it. (Periya Purāṇam, Tirukkuṟipputtoṇṭa Purāṇam)
- **Cākkiyar (Sakkiya)** — a Buddhist inwardly won to Śiva, keeping his Buddhist garb; his daily rite is throwing a stone at the liṅga (begun by accident, kept as vow); the day he nearly eats without performing it he rushes to throw first, and Śiva appears. (Periya Purāṇam, Cākkiya Purāṇam)
- **Mūrkkar** — funds the feeding of devotees by his skill at dice, ferocious ("mūrkka") to defaulters. (Periya Purāṇam, Mūrkka Purāṇam)
- **Arivāṭṭāyar** — poor farmer whose offering of red rice, greens and mango spills in the mud; he sets his sickle (*arivāḷ*) to his throat before Śiva intervenes. (Periya Purāṇam, Arivāṭṭāya Purāṇam)
- **Pukaḻccōḻar (Pugazh Chola)** — the Karuvūr king who, finding among battle-trophies the matted-haired head of a Śaiva ascetic, enters the fire in horror, chanting the pañcākṣara. (Periya Purāṇam, Pukaḻccōḻa Purāṇam)
- **Kūṟṟuva** — chieftain whom the Tillai priests refuse to crown (fleeing to Kerala rather than serve a non-Chola); Śiva crowns him in dream with His own feet. (Periya Purāṇam, Kūṟṟuva Purāṇam)

## Saints of quiet service

- **Aṉāyar** — cowherd whose flute-song is nothing but the five syllables *namaḥ śivāya*; absorbed into the Lord as he plays. (Periya Purāṇam, Aṉāya Purāṇam)
- **Mūrtti** — Madurai merchant who grinds sandal-paste for Śiva; when a hostile king bans sandalwood he grinds his own elbow; the state elephant then garlands him king. (Periya Purāṇam, Mūrtti Purāṇam)
- **Rudra Paśupati** — recites the Śrī Rudram standing neck-deep in a lotus pond, dawn and dusk. (Periya Purāṇam, Rudra Paśupati Purāṇam)
- **Naminandi Aḍigaḷ** — taunted by Jain neighbors to light lamps "with water," he fills the Tiruvārūr temple lamps from the tank and they blaze. (Periya Purāṇam, Naminandi Aḍigaḷ Purāṇam)
- **Taṇṭi Aḍigaḷ (Dandi Adigal)** — blind devotee who extends the Tiruvārūr temple tank alone by stake and rope; the mocking Jains are confounded when Śiva grants him sight and the king expels them. (Periya Purāṇam, Taṇṭi Aḍigaḷ Purāṇam)
- **Pūcalār (Pusalar)** — poor brāhmaṇa of Tiruniṉṟavūr who builds Śiva a temple **in his mind**, brick by mental brick; Śiva tells the Pallava king (builder of the great Kāñcī temple) He must first attend Pūcalār's consecration — the king finds no building, only the saint. (Periya Purāṇam, Pūcalār Purāṇam)
- **Vāyilār** — of Mylapore, whose worship is likewise wholly interior: mind-temple, thought-lamps. (Periya Purāṇam, Vāyilār Purāṇam)
- **Appūti Aḍigaḷ** — names his sons, water-sheds and cattle after Tirunāvukkaracar sight-unseen; when a snake kills his son during Appar's own visit, he hides the corpse so the guest may eat first — Appar revives the boy. (Periya Purāṇam, Appūti Aḍigaḷ Purāṇam)
- **Tirunīlanakkar** — brāhmaṇa who repudiates his wife for blowing a spider off the liṅga; Śiva shows him in dream blisters everywhere *except* where she blew, and he takes her back. (Periya Purāṇam, Tirunīlanakka Purāṇam)
- **Pukaḻttuṇai** — famine-starved priest who faints while bathing the liṅga; Śiva thereafter leaves a gold coin on the pīṭha daily. (Periya Purāṇam, Pukaḻttuṇai Purāṇam)
- **Iṭaṅkaḻi** — chieftain who, catching a devotee stealing his paddy to feed Śiva's servants, throws open all his granaries. (Periya Purāṇam, Iṭaṅkaḻi Purāṇam)
- **Cōmāci Māṟar, Ciṟappuli, Kaṇanātar, Muruga, Kāri, Naraciṅka Muṉaiyaraiyar, Muṉaiyaḍuvār** — briefer purāṇams of soma-sacrifice dedicated to Śiva, brāhmaṇa largesse, organized devotee-service, flower-and-mantra worship, shrine-endowing poetry, chieftain-patronage of Sundarar, and mercenary wages spent on devotees. (Periya Purāṇam, respective Purāṇams)

## Royal, ministerial and bardic saints

- **Tirunāvukkaracar (Appar)** — the lapsed-Jain monk Dharmasēna, reconverted by his sister **Tilakavatī** after an agonizing illness; survives the Pallava king's lime-kiln, poison, elephant, and drowning (floating on his stone, singing), and converts the king (Mahendravarman I [scholarly]). Full profile: [`../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/the-nalvar-tamil-shaiva-saints.md`](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/the-nalvar-tamil-shaiva-saints.md). (Periya Purāṇam, Tirunāvukkaracar Purāṇam)
- **Tiruñāṉacampantar (Sambandar)** — the Sirkazhi child fed the milk of Umā at three; at Madurai he cures the fevered Pāṇḍya and defeats the Jains in ordeals of fire and flood (his palm-leaf swims *against* the Vaigai current) — per the epic 8,000 Jains are impaled (historicity doubted [disputed] [scholarly]); at his wedding the whole party enters the divine light. Cross-link: [`../06-sacred-geography/madurai-meenakshi-sundareshwara-kshetra.md`](../06-sacred-geography/madurai-meenakshi-sundareshwara-kshetra.md). (Periya Purāṇam, Tiruñāṉacampantar Purāṇam)
- **The Madurai triad** — the converted Pāṇḍya king **Ninṟacīr Neṭumāṟaṉ**, his queen **Maṅkaiyarkkaraci**, and his minister **Kulacciṟai** are each canonized separately for enabling Sambandar's mission. (Periya Purāṇam, respective Purāṇams)
- **Kāraikkāl Ammaiyār** — Punitavati, abandoned by her husband after her mango-miracle; she asks Śiva for a demoness's form and walks to Kailāsa on her hands; Śiva calls her "Ammai" (Mother). Canonical home: [`../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/karaikkal-ammaiyar.md`](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/karaikkal-ammaiyar.md). (Periya Purāṇam, Kāraikkāl Ammaiyār Purāṇam)
- **Tirumūlar** — the northern yogi who enters the corpse of the cowherd Mūlaṉ and composes the **Tirumandiram** (Tirumurai 10), by legend one verse a year for 3,000 years. Cross-link: [`../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/tamil-siddhars.md`](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/tamil-siddhars.md), [`../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/shaiva-schools.md`](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/shaiva-schools.md). (Periya Purāṇam, Tirumūla Purāṇam)
- **Eyarkōṉ Kalikkāmar** — general who despises Sundarar for making Śiva his go-between with Paravai; afflicted by Śiva with a colic only Sundarar can cure, he rips open his belly rather than owe the cure, and is restored when Sundarar arrives. (Periya Purāṇam, Eyarkōṉ Kalikkāma Purāṇam)
- **Kōcceṅkaṭ Cōḻaṉ (Kochengat Chola)** — in a former life the spider of Tiruvāṉaikkā that wove a web-canopy over the liṅga and died fighting the temple elephant; reborn red-eyed as a Chola king, he builds seventy-plus elevated *māṭakkōyils* no elephant can enter; he is praised also in Sangam poetry [scholarly]. (Periya Purāṇam, Kōcceṅkaṭ Cōḻa Purāṇam)
- **Aiyaḍigaḷ Kāḍavarkōṉ** — Pallava king who abdicates to wander the shrines composing veṇpā verses (in Tirumurai 11). (Periya Purāṇam, Aiyaḍigaḷ Kāḍavarkōṉ Purāṇam)
- **Tirunīlakaṇṭha Yāḻppāṇar** — the Pāṇar bard who accompanies Sambandar, setting his hymns to the yāḻ (lute). (Periya Purāṇam, Tirunīlakaṇṭha Yāḻppāṇa Purāṇam)
- **Perumiḻalai Kuṟumpar** — chieftain who, foreknowing Sundarar's ascension, precedes him by yogic power. (Periya Purāṇam, Perumiḻalai Kuṟumpa Purāṇam)
- **Caṭaiyaṉār and Icaiñāṉiyār** — Sundarar's father and mother, whose inclusion (with Sundarar) completes the canonical 63. (Periya Purāṇam, final Purāṇams; roster in [`../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/alvars-and-nayanars.md`](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/alvars-and-nayanars.md))

## The nine collective bodies (Tokai Aḍiyār)

Sundarar's hymn also honors nine *classes* of devotees, each given its own purāṇam: **Tillai vāḻ antaṇar** (the brahmins of Chidambaram), **Poyyaḍimai illāta pulavar** (poets without false service — traditionally the Sangam poets), **Pattarāyp paṇivār** (serving bhaktas), **Paramaṉaiyē pāḍuvār** (singers of the Supreme alone), **Cittattaic civaṉpālē vaittār** (minds resting on Śiva), **Tiruvārūrp piṟantār** (all born in Tiruvārūr), **Muppōtum tirumēṉi tīṇṭuvār** (those touching the Lord's form thrice daily), **Muḻunīṟu pūciya muṉivar** (ascetics smeared wholly in ash), and **Appālum aḍiccārntār** (devotees beyond all lands and ages). (Periya Purāṇam, Tokai Aḍiyār Purāṇams; Tēvāram 7.39)

## Two clocks & textual history

- **Scriptural/cyclic frame** — the action is bracketed by timeless Kailāsa; the saints live in the present **Kali Yuga** (see [`../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md`](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)); Kōcceṅkaṭ Cōḻaṉ's spider-birth and Sundarar's gana-birth are the only reincarnation frames.
- **Academic frame [scholarly]** — the saints cluster c. **6th–9th c. CE** (Appar/Sambandar 7th c., Sundarar c. 8th c.; Ciṟuttoṇṭar anchored by **642 CE Vātāpi**); Nambi's *Tiruvandāti* c. 10th–11th c. under Rājarāja I's canon-recovery; Cēkkiḻār's epic **c. 1135 CE**; Umāpati's *Cēkkiḻār Purāṇam* **c. 1313 CE**, when the epic is fixed as Book 12.
- **Status of the legends** — the kings and poets are historical [scholarly]; the miracles are hagiography, resting on Cēkkiḻār alone five centuries after the events; the Madurai mass-impalement is widely doubted as history [disputed] [scholarly]. Verse-count is conventionally 4,286, with edition variants.
- **Geography as structure** — Cēkkiḻār routes each life through named shrines; the epic doubles as a pilgrimage map of the [`../06-sacred-geography/275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md`](../06-sacred-geography/275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md) and [`../06-sacred-geography/temple-networks-and-circuits.md`](../06-sacred-geography/temple-networks-and-circuits.md); Śiva's trickster-interventions parallel [`../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-tiruvilaiyadal-64-lilas.md`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-tiruvilaiyadal-64-lilas.md).
