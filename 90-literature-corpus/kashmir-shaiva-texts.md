# Kashmir Śaiva Texts — Śiva-Sūtras to Vijñāna Bhairava

> Literature-corpus node — up to the [Literature Corpus overview](README.md). The textual canon of **non-dual Kashmir Śaivism (Trika)**: by the tradition's own clock these are **eternal Āgamic revelation re-spoken for Kali Yuga** (Yuga began 3102 BCE); on the academic clock the human-authored core crystallises in the Kashmir Valley **c. 850–1050 CE** [scholarly], within [recorded history](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md).
> This file is the **text-map**; the **lineage/acharya roster** lives at [`shaiva-schools.md`](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/shaiva-schools.md), the Āgama/Tantra genre-frame at [`agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md`](agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md), and the tradition's vernacular afterlife at [Lalleśvarī / Lal Ded](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/lalleshwari-lal-ded.md).

## Orientation: the three śāstras of the Trika canon

- **The threefold textual map** — the Kashmir lineage sorts its own literature into **Āgama-śāstra** (revealed Tantras, held to be Śiva's eternal word), **Spanda-śāstra** (the "vibration" doctrine descending from the Śiva-Sūtras), and **Pratyabhijñā-śāstra** (the "recognition" philosophy of Somānanda's line). (Kṣemarāja's exegetical tradition; IEP, *Kashmiri Shaiva Philosophy*) [scholarly framing of a tradition-internal division]
- **The Āgamic base** — the school reads the Śaiva scriptures as a hierarchy: **10 Śiva-bheda (dualist) → 18 Rudra-bheda (mixed) → 64 Bhairava Tantras (purely non-dual)**, with the Trika drawing chiefly on the Bhairava wing: *Mālinīvijayottara* (Abhinavagupta's declared root-text), *Svacchanda*, *Netra*, *Vijñānabhairava*, *Rudrayāmala*. (Tantrāloka 1, citing Mālinīvijayottara; Kashmir Shaivism — Wikipedia)
- **Two clocks, stated plainly** — *scriptural:* the Tantras are beginningless, periodically concealed and re-revealed as the yugas turn; *academic [scholarly]:* the surviving Bhairava Tantras are roughly 7th–9th c. CE compositions, and the exegetical canon below is 9th–11th c. Kashmir. The corpus keeps both frames distinct throughout.

## The revelation legend — how the canon (re)descends in Kali Yuga

- **Loss of the Śaiva teaching in Kali Yuga** — tradition recorded in the Trika's own histories holds that as **Kali Yuga** deepened the great Śaiva śāstras vanished from the earth, the sages who held them having withdrawn. (lineage account preserved with Tantrāloka 36 and Jayaratha's *Viveka*) [tradition-internal]
- **Śrīkaṇṭha commissions Durvāsas** — Śiva in his form as **Śrīkaṇṭha** commands the sage **Durvāsas** to revive the teaching; Durvāsas creates mind-born sons to carry it: **Tryambaka** for the non-dual stream, **Āmardaka** for the dual, **Śrīnātha** for the mixed — the non-dual **Tryambaka line (Traiyambaka-sampradāya)** being the claimed ancestry of Kashmir Śaivism. Somānanda's line traces itself to Tryambaka. (Śivadṛṣṭi ch. 7 lineage-account; Tantrāloka-viveka) [tradition-internal]
- **The Śiva-Sūtra descent to Vasugupta closes the circuit** — the 9th-century rock-revelation (next section) is framed by the tradition as Śiva *personally re-opening* the non-dual teaching for the age, against the then-dominant dualist exegesis. (Kṣemarāja, *Śiva-sūtra-vimarśinī*, opening narrative)

## Śiva-Sūtras of Vasugupta (the seed-text)

- **The text** — **77 terse aphorisms** revealed to **Vasugupta** (fl. c. 850–900 CE [scholarly], on Mahādeva mountain near Srinagar), arranged in **three sections (unmeṣas, "openings/upliftings")** corresponding to the three means (upāyas): **I. Śāmbhavopāya (22 sūtras)**, **II. Śāktopāya (10 sūtras)**, **III. Āṇavopāya (45 sūtras)**. Opening sūtra: **"caitanyam ātmā"** — "Consciousness is the Self." (Śiva-Sūtras; Kṣemarāja, *Śiva-sūtra-vimarśinī*; SUNY tr. *The Aphorisms of Śiva*)
- **The Śaṅkarpāl rock-revelation legend** — Śiva appears to Vasugupta **in a dream** and directs him to a great rock, the **Śaṅkarpāl / Śaṅkaropala ("Śiva's stone")**, on **Mahādeva mountain**: at his touch the boulder **turns over of itself**, revealing the sūtras engraved on its underside, placed there by Śiva to counter dualist teaching. The rock is still shown to pilgrims near the Harwan/Dachigam side of the mountain. (Kṣemarāja, *Śiva-sūtra-vimarśinī*, introductory account) [tradition-internal] [regional]
  - *Variant (Bhāskara, Śiva-sūtra-vārttika):* the sūtras were transmitted to Vasugupta **in a dream by a Siddha** (a perfected being), with no rock involved. [disputed transmission]
  - *Variant (Kallaṭa's line, Spanda tradition):* **Śiva himself taught** the sūtras to Vasugupta in the dream directly. The three transmission-stories are cataloged by the commentators themselves and never harmonised. [disputed transmission]
- **Commentary corpus** — **Kṣemarāja's *Śiva-sūtra-vimarśinī*** (the standard gloss, c. 11th c.), **Bhāskara's *Vārttika*** (a partly divergent line), and Varadarāja's later verse rendering. (IEP; SUNY tr.)

## Spanda-Kārikās (the vibration doctrine)

- **The text** — roughly **52 verses** (53 with the epilogue verse; count is recension-dependent [disputed]) unpacking the Śiva-Sūtras' vision as **spanda**: reality as the single divine **pulsation/vibration** of Śiva–Śakti, by whose mere opening and closing of the eyes (unmeṣa–nimeṣa) worlds arise and dissolve — the theme of the famous first kārikā saluting Śaṅkara. Kṣemarāja divides the verses into **three sections**: *Svarūpa-spanda* (spanda as one's own nature), *Sahaja-vidyodaya* (rise of innate wisdom), *Vibhūti-spanda* (spanda's powers). (Spanda-Kārikās; Kṣemarāja, *Spanda-nirṇaya*)
- **Authorship dispute** — **Kṣemarāja** attributes the kārikās to **Vasugupta** himself; the rival commentarial line attributes them to Vasugupta's disciple **Kallaṭa** (fl. c. 855–883 CE, under King Avantivarman [scholarly]), who at minimum wrote the earliest commentary, the *Spanda-vṛtti*. Never resolved within the tradition. [disputed]
- **Commentaries** — Kallaṭa's *Spanda-vṛtti*; Rāmakaṇṭha's *Vivṛti*; Utpalavaiṣṇava's *Spanda-pradīpikā*; Kṣemarāja's *Spanda-sandoha* (on the first verse alone) and *Spanda-nirṇaya* (full). (IEP; Kashmir Shaivism — Wikipedia)

## Śivadṛṣṭi of Somānanda (Pratyabhijñā founded)

- **The text** — **Somānanda** (c. 900–950 CE [scholarly]) composes the **Śivadṛṣṭi** ("The Vision of Śiva"), **seven chapters (āhnikas)** of tightly argued verse founding the **Pratyabhijñā ("recognition") school**: everything whatsoever — jar, cloth, cognizer, cognition — **is Śiva**, one consciousness knowing and acting freely; hence the school's radical claim that even so-called insentient things participate in Śiva-hood. (Śivadṛṣṭi; IEP, *Kashmiri Shaiva Philosophy*)
- **Polemical edge** — the Śivadṛṣṭi's second chapter attacks the **grammarians' (Bhartṛhari's) doctrine** that *paśyantī* speech is the ultimate — for Somānanda, consciousness-as-Śiva stands above every level of the Word. (Śivadṛṣṭi āhnika 2) [scholarly]
- **Lineage claim** — the work's closing account traces Somānanda's descent from **Tryambaka** of the Durvāsas revival-legend (above), binding the new philosophy to the Āgamic dispensation for Kali Yuga. (Śivadṛṣṭi ch. 7) [tradition-internal]
- **Transmission note** — **Utpaladeva**, his direct pupil, wrote a *Vṛtti* on it that survives only for the first four chapters. [scholarly]

## Īśvara-pratyabhijñā-kārikā of Utpaladeva

- **The text** — **Utpaladeva** (c. 925–975 CE [scholarly]) systematises his teacher's vision in the **Īśvara-pratyabhijñā-kārikā** ("Verses on the Recognition of the Lord"), about **190 kārikās in four books**: **Jñānādhikāra** (on knowledge), **Kriyādhikāra** (on action), **Āgamādhikāra** (scriptural synthesis of the 36 tattvas), **Tattvasaṅgrahādhikāra** (summation). Liberation requires no new attainment — only the **recognition (pratyabhijñā)** that one's own self *already is* Maheśvara, like a woman who realises the man before her is the beloved she had only heard praised. (Īśvara-pratyabhijñā-kārikā IV; Utpaladeva's own *Vṛtti*)
- **Philosophical front** — the Jñāna and Kriyā books conduct a sustained duel with the **Buddhist epistemologists (Dharmakīrti's school)** over memory, the unity of the knowing self, and whether awareness is self-luminous — the reason the Pratyabhijñā corpus is treated as a full *darśana* and not just sectarian theology. [scholarly]
- **Self-commentaries and after** — Utpaladeva wrote a short **Vṛtti** and a long **Vivṛti/Ṭīkā** (the latter mostly lost, fragments recovered in modern times [scholarly]); Abhinavagupta then wrote the two standard commentaries, the **Īśvara-pratyabhijñā-vimarśinī** and the massive **Īśvara-pratyabhijñā-vivṛti-vimarśinī**. His devotional verses survive separately as the **Śivastotrāvalī** (arranged by Kṣemarāja).

## Abhinavagupta — Tantrāloka & Tantrasāra (the great synthesis)

- **Tantrāloka ("Light on the Tantras")** — **Abhinavagupta** (c. 950–1016 CE [scholarly]) composes the encyclopaedic verse summa of the Trika: **37 chapters (āhnikas)**, on the order of **5,800 verses**, organising the whole tradition around the **means to liberation** — **anupāya** (the "no-means"), **śāmbhava-, śākta-** and **āṇava-upāyas** (āhnikas 1–5), then the paths of time and space (kālādhvan/deśādhvan), the 36 tattvas, **dīkṣā** (initiation rites), the **Kaula ritual** (āhnika 29), mantra, and scriptural theory — all anchored, by his own statement, on the **Mālinīvijayottara Tantra**. (Tantrāloka 1.17 ff.; Kashmir Shaivism — Wikipedia)
- **Tantrasāra ("Essence of the Tantras")** — his own **prose digest** of the Tantrāloka, written for those unable to traverse the full work; the standard entry-point used by the later tradition. (Tantrasāra, opening)
- **The one indispensable commentary** — the Tantrāloka is read through **Jayaratha's *Viveka*** (Kashmir, c. 12th–13th c. [scholarly]), which also preserves much of the school's lost history and the Durvāsas–Tryambaka lineage material. (Tantrāloka-viveka)
- **The author-legend** — tradition makes Abhinavagupta a **yoginī-bhū** ("born of a yoginī"), the child of a Kaula union of his parents Narasiṃhagupta and Vimalā, hence spiritually marked from birth; a Kashmiri legend adds that he ended his life by **walking into the Bhairava cave** with his disciples, never to emerge. (hagiographic tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **Beyond Tantra** — the same author's *Mālinīślokavārttika*, his Gītā commentary (*Gītārtha-saṃgraha*), and his aesthetics (*Abhinavabhāratī*, the *rasa-dhvani* synthesis) sit outside this file's scope but mark the canon's reach into poetics; see the roster in [`shaiva-schools.md`](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/shaiva-schools.md).

## Kṣemarāja — Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam and the popularising layer

- **Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam ("The Heart of Recognition")** — **Kṣemarāja** (Abhinavagupta's disciple, fl. c. 1000–1050 CE [scholarly]) distils the entire Pratyabhijñā system into **20 sūtras with his own gloss**, explicitly written, he says, for devotees **untrained in the rigorous logic** of the kārikā literature. Opening sūtra: **"citiḥ svatantrā viśva-siddhi-hetuḥ"** — "Consciousness, absolutely free, is the cause of the manifestation of the universe." (Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam, sūtra 1 and auto-commentary)
- **The commentator of record** — Kṣemarāja also supplies the standard glosses across the canon: *Śiva-sūtra-vimarśinī*, *Spanda-sandoha*, *Spanda-nirṇaya*, and **uddyota** commentaries on the *Svacchanda* and *Netra* Tantras — effectively fixing how the revealed layer is read. (IEP; Kashmir Shaivism — Wikipedia)

## Vijñāna Bhairava — the 112 dhāraṇās

- **The text** — the **Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra**, roughly **163 verses**, transmitted as part of the **Rudrayāmala** cycle (the text calls itself its essence [tradition-internal]); academically dated among the earlier Bhairava Tantras, **c. 7th–9th century CE** [scholarly]. Frame-story: **the Goddess (Bhairavī/Devī) questions Bhairava** — she has heard the Trika teachings but asks what Bhairava's *ultimate* nature truly is. Compare the Valley's other Śiva-tells-the-Goddess frame at [Amarnāth and the Amar Kathā](../06-sacred-geography/amarnath-ice-lingam-and-amar-katha.md). (Vijñāna Bhairava, opening dialogue)
- **Bhairava's answer** — the forms, visualisations and ritual described in the Tantras are declared **gross, external, provisional**; the real Bhairava is the state of undivided awareness (*bhairavī avasthā*), and the Devī is not different from him. (Vijñāna Bhairava, opening section)
- **The 112 dhāraṇās** — the body of the text gives **112 contemplative techniques (dhāraṇās)**, each a doorway into Bhairava-consciousness, famously including: resting in the **pause between the in-breath and out-breath**; fixing awareness at the meeting-point of prāṇa and apāna; the **junction between two thoughts**; gazing into **cloudless sky** or into darkness; awareness at the onset of **sneezing, terror, hunger**; dissolving into the **resonance of a plucked string or chanted mantra** as it fades; savouring the **pleasure of food and music** as the bliss of the Self; and the union of lovers as an emblem of the one energy — techniques ranging over breath, void, sound, body and emotion. Kuṇḍalinī-frame practices among them connect to [chakras & kuṇḍalinī](../05-concepts-and-dharma/chakras-and-kundalini.md); the mantra-dhāraṇās to [mantra-śāstra](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/mantra-shastra.md). (Vijñāna Bhairava, dhāraṇā verses)
- **Commentaries** — a partial commentary by **Kṣemarāja** survives (early verses only); the complete standard gloss is **Śivopādhyāya's *Vivṛti*** (Kashmir, **18th century** — evidence the Valley's exegesis continued long after the classical age [scholarly]); also Bhaṭṭa Ānanda's *Kaumudī*. (KSTS editions; Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra — Wikipedia)
- **Modern afterlife** — the 112 dhāraṇās reached global audiences through **Paul Reps' English rendering "Centering"** in *Zen Flesh, Zen Bones* (1957), **Swami Lakshman Joo's** oral teaching in Srinagar, and **Osho's** lecture-series *The Book of Secrets*. (publication history) [scholarly] — likely why searches for "112 dhāraṇās" now outnumber those for the parent canon.

## Afterlife & transmission of the canon

- **Vernacularisation** — after the Sultanate transition the Sanskrit exegesis thins, but the Trika voice re-sounds in Kashmiri in the 14th-century **vākhs of [Lalleśvarī / Lal Ded](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/lalleshwari-lal-ded.md)**. [regional]
- **Manuscript rescue** — the canon survived in Kashmiri paṇḍit families and was printed in the **Kashmir Series of Texts and Studies (KSTS, 1911 onward)**, the edition-series on which nearly all modern study rests. [scholarly]
- **The last traditional master** — **Swami Lakshman Joo (1907–1991)** taught the Śiva-Sūtras, Vijñāna Bhairava and Tantrāloka orally in Srinagar, becoming the bridge to modern academic and practitioner lineages. [scholarly] [oral tradition]
- **Sibling Kashmir corpus** — the Valley's other great medieval production, the *Mokṣopāya / Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha*, is mapped separately at [`yoga-vasishtha.md`](yoga-vasishtha.md); the deity whose myth-cycle all of this exegetes is mapped at [Śiva's myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md), and the Śākta interweaving at [Śākta & Tantra lineages](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/shakta-and-tantra-lineages.md).
