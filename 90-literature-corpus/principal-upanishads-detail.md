# The Principal Upaniṣads — Per-Text Detail

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/`](README.md) — companion to [`shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md), which frames the four Vedas, their four strata, and where the Upaniṣads sit on the cosmic clock. This node zooms into the **mukhya** ("principal") Upaniṣads one text at a time: parent Veda, key teaching / mahāvākya, famous passages, and dating with variants.

---

## 1. What Makes an Upaniṣad "Principal"

- **The mukhya (principal) set.** Of the 108 Upaniṣads listed in the **Muktikā** canon (the closing text of the Muktikā list itself), a small core is singled out as *mukhya* — those on which **Ādi Śaṅkara** (c. 8th c. CE) is traditionally said to have written or quoted commentary, anchoring the **Vedānta** school. (Muktikā Upaniṣad; Śaṅkara's bhāṣyas)
- **Count: 10, 11, 12, or 13.** The list is not fixed:
  - **10 — Daśopaniṣad:** Īśa, Kena, Kaṭha, Praśna, Muṇḍaka, Māṇḍūkya, Taittirīya, Aitareya, Chāndogya, Bṛhadāraṇyaka — the texts Śaṅkara is held to have formally commented on. Mnemonic verse: *"Īśa-Kena-Kaṭha-Praśna-Muṇḍa-Māṇḍūkya-Taittiri / Aitareyaṃ ca Chāndogyaṃ Bṛhadāraṇyakaṃ tathā."*
  - *Variant (+1 = 11):* add **Śvetāśvatara** — Śaṅkara cites it heavily; many lists treat it as principal.
  - *Variant (+1 = 12):* add **Kauṣītaki** — an old prose Upaniṣad, often counted with the early canon.
  - *Variant (+1 = 13):* add **Maitrī / Maitrāyaṇīya** — the standard "13 principal Upaniṣads" of modern scholarship (e.g. Hume's and Radhakrishnan's collections) rounds out at thirteen with these three additions. [count varies by source]
- **The four "great sayings" (mahāvākyas).** Vedānta tradition pairs one Veda with one summary aphorism (note: the Veda-assignment is itself a later schematization, not always matching where the verse physically sits):
  - **Prajñānaṃ Brahma** ("Consciousness is Brahman") — Aitareya 3.3 (Ṛgveda). *(Source: Aitareya Up.)*
  - **Ahaṃ Brahmāsmi** ("I am Brahman") — Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.4.10 (Yajurveda). *(Source: Bṛhadāraṇyaka Up.)*
  - **Tat tvam asi** ("That thou art") — Chāndogya 6.8.7 (Sāmaveda). *(Source: Chāndogya Up.)*
  - **Ayam ātmā Brahma** ("This Self is Brahman") — Māṇḍūkya 2 / Bṛhadāraṇyaka 2.5.19 (Atharvaveda). *(Source: Māṇḍūkya Up.)*

## 2. Relative Chronology (the scholarly frame)

Indology dates the principal Upaniṣads by language stratum (prose vs. verse, archaic vs. classical Sanskrit), not by the apauruṣeya / "re-heard each Dvāpara" tradition recorded in [`shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md). **Patrick Olivelle's** widely used ordering (1996/1998):

- **Earliest, early prose (c. 7th–6th c. BCE, pre-Buddhist):** **Bṛhadāraṇyaka**, then **Chāndogya** — the two oldest and longest.
- **Early prose (c. 6th–5th c. BCE, probably pre-Buddhist):** **Taittirīya**, **Aitareya**, **Kauṣītaki**.
- **Verse Upaniṣads (last few centuries BCE):** **Kena** (oldest of the verse group), then **Kaṭha**, **Īśa**, **Śvetāśvatara**, **Muṇḍaka**.
- **Late prose (around the start of the Common Era):** **Praśna**, then **Māṇḍūkya**; **Maitrī** is later still (synthesizes Sāṃkhya–Yoga).
  - *Variant (orthodox / traditional):* on the cosmic clock all Upaniṣads are eternal Śruti, "seen" by ṛṣis and re-compiled by **Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa** at the Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; see Kali-Yuga onset constant) — not authored in 1st-millennium-BCE strata. This file records both frames and corrects neither. [scripture vs. academic dating]
  - *Variant (relative order disputes):* scholars differ on fine ordering — e.g. Winternitz holds Śvetāśvatara pre-Buddhist but later than Kauṣītaki; Phillips puts Śvetāśvatara before Maitrī; some place Īśa very early because it caps the Śukla Yajus Saṃhitā. [disputed]

## 3. Per-Text Detail

### Ṛgveda Upaniṣads

- **Aitareya Upaniṣad** — embedded in the **Aitareya Āraṇyaka** (chs. 4–6) of the **Ṛgveda** (Śākala śākhā), attributed to the sage **Mahidāsa Aitareya**. Three chapters. *(Source: Aitareya Up.)*
  - **Key teaching / mahāvākya:** **Prajñānaṃ Brahma** — "Consciousness is Brahman" (3.3). Opens with a creation account: Ātman alone existed and brought forth the worlds (loka), the cosmic guardians, and the human person.
  - **Famous passage:** the threefold birth of the self (2.1) — conception, gestation, and rebirth in one's offspring; the ātman entering the body through the crown ("vidṛti").
  - **Dating:** early prose, c. 6th–5th c. BCE (Olivelle), pre-Buddhist.

- **Kauṣītaki (Kauṣītakī-Brāhmaṇa) Upaniṣad** — embedded in the **Kauṣītaki Āraṇyaka** of the **Ṛgveda**. Four chapters. Often counted principal (12th/13th of the set). *(Source: Kauṣītaki Up.)*
  - **Key teaching:** **prāṇa** (vital breath) as the conscious self / Brahman; doctrine of **rebirth and the two post-death paths** (devayāna / pitṛyāna) and the moon as gateway of returning souls.
  - **Famous passage:** the **Pratardana** dialogue with Indra — Indra declares himself prāṇa, "the conscious self, life, immortality" (ch. 3); the doctrine that life-breath and cognition are inseparable.
  - **Dating:** early prose, c. 6th–5th c. BCE; among the older Upaniṣads.

### Śukla (White) Yajurveda Upaniṣads

- **Īśa (Īśāvāsya) Upaniṣad** — uniquely sits *inside* a Saṃhitā: it is the **40th and final chapter of the Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā** (Śukla Yajurveda); hence also called the "Mantra Upaniṣad." Among the shortest — 18 verses (Kāṇva recension; 17 in Mādhyandina). *(Source: Īśa Up.)*
  - **Key teaching:** synthesis of action (karma) and renunciation — *"Īśāvāsyam idaṃ sarvam"* ("all this is pervaded by the Lord," v.1); do your work yet covet nothing.
  - **Famous passage:** the paradox verses on Brahman — *"It moves, it moves not; it is far, it is near"* (v.5); v.15's prayer to the Sun to unveil the golden disc hiding Truth (*hiraṇmayena pātreṇa...*), recited at cremations.
  - **Dating:** verse group, last few centuries BCE (Olivelle); but its placement closing the Saṃhitā has led some to treat it as very early. [position vs. language disputed]

### Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda Upaniṣads

- **Kaṭha (Kāṭhaka) Upaniṣad** — in the **Kaṭha (Caraka-Kaṭha) śākhā** of the Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda; two chapters (adhyāya) of three vallīs each. *(Source: Kaṭha Up.)*
  - **Key teaching:** the immortal Self learned through the **Naciketas–Yama** dialogue; the **chariot allegory** (1.3.3–9) — body = chariot, intellect (buddhi) = charioteer, mind = reins, senses = horses, Ātman = lord of the chariot.
  - **Famous passage:** **Naciketas's three boons** and Yama's reluctant teaching of what survives death; *"the Self is not born, nor does it die"* (1.2.18, echoing/echoed by Bhagavad-gītā 2.20). The "razor's edge" path (1.3.14, *kṣurasya dhārā*). See the full deep-dive: [`../04-deep-dives/nachiketa-katha-upanishad.md`](../04-deep-dives/nachiketa-katha-upanishad.md).
  - **Dating:** verse group, last few centuries BCE; shares vocabulary with early Sāṃkhya and the Gītā.

- **Taittirīya Upaniṣad** — embedded in the **Taittirīya Āraṇyaka** (chs. 7–9) of the Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda. Three sections: Śīkṣā-vallī, Brahmānanda-vallī (Ānanda-vallī), Bhṛgu-vallī. *(Source: Taittirīya Up.)*
  - **Key teaching:** the **pañca-kośa** (five sheaths) — annamaya (food), prāṇamaya (breath), manomaya (mind), vijñānamaya (intellect), ānandamaya (bliss) — wrapping the Self; **"Brahman is bliss" (ānanda)**.
  - **Famous passages:** the **Śīkṣā-vallī** graduation address — *"Satyaṃ vada, dharmaṃ cara"* ("Speak the truth, practice dharma," 1.11), the oldest Indian convocation charge; the definition **"Satyaṃ jñānam anantaṃ Brahma"** ("Brahman is truth, knowledge, infinity," 2.1); the **Bhṛgu–Varuṇa** inquiry deriving Brahman as food → bliss.
  - **Dating:** early prose, c. 6th–5th c. BCE, pre-Buddhist.

- **Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad** — attached to the Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda (Taittirīya school); named for the sage **Śvetāśvatara**. Six chapters. The most overtly **theistic** of the principal Upaniṣads. *(Source: Śvetāśvatara Up.)*
  - **Key teaching:** personal-God Vedānta — **Rudra / Śiva (also called Hara, Bhagavān, Deva, Īśa)** identified *as* Brahman; early **bhakti** ("devotion to God, and equally to one's guru," 6.23) and proto-Sāṃkhya/Yoga vocabulary (puruṣa, prakṛti, māyā — *"māyāṃ tu prakṛtiṃ vidyāt,"* 4.10).
  - **Famous passage:** *"Eko devaḥ"* — "One God hidden in all beings" (6.11); the cosmos as the play of the one Lord; one of the first texts to use **māyā** in its later Vedāntic sense. Core scripture for **Śaivism**.
  - **Dating:** verse group, c. 5th–4th c. BCE (Flood, Gorski; Muller-Ortega ~6th–5th c.), roughly contemporary with the Buddha. [scholarly] Counted principal as the 11th text.

### Sāmaveda Upaniṣads

- **Chāndogya Upaniṣad** — the latter part (chs. 3–10) of the **Chāndogya Brāhmaṇa** of the **Sāmaveda** (Tāṇḍya / Kauthuma school). Eight chapters (prapāṭhaka) — one of the two longest and oldest Upaniṣads. *(Source: Chāndogya Up.)*
  - **Key teaching / mahāvākya:** **Tat tvam asi** ("That thou art," 6.8.7) — the **Śvetaketu–Uddālaka Āruṇi** dialogue, repeated nine times, teaching the identity of the individual self with the universal Being (Sat).
  - **Famous passages:** the **Udgītha / Oṃ** meditations (ch.1); *"Sarvaṃ khalv idaṃ Brahma"* ("all this is verily Brahman," 3.14.1); the **Śāṇḍilya-vidyā**; the **honey doctrine** and **dahara-vidyā** (the space within the heart); the salt-in-water and banyan-seed parables (6.12–13); the **Sanatkumāra–Nārada** teaching on bhūman (the Infinite, ch.7).
  - **Dating:** earliest stratum, c. 8th–6th c. BCE; slightly younger than Bṛhadāraṇyaka.

- **Kena (Talavakāra) Upaniṣad** — in the **Jaiminīya / Talavakāra Brāhmaṇa** of the **Sāmaveda**. Four sections (two verse, two prose). *(Source: Kena Up.)*
  - **Key teaching:** Brahman as the unknowable power *behind* the senses and mind — *"That which the mind cannot think but by which the mind thinks"* (1.5–9); Brahman is the knower, never the known object.
  - **Famous passage:** the **parable of the yakṣa** — the gods (Agni, Vāyu, Indra) boast of victory until a mysterious spirit (Brahman, revealed via the goddess **Umā Haimavatī**) humbles them, showing their power is borrowed from Brahman (chs. 3–4).
  - **Dating:** oldest of the verse Upaniṣads (Olivelle).

### Atharvaveda Upaniṣads

- **Praśna Upaniṣad** — of the **Atharvaveda** (Piṕṕalāda school), attached to the Atharva tradition; named for its structure of six **praśnas** ("questions"). *(Source: Praśna Up.)*
  - **Key teaching:** six disciples question the sage **Pippalāda** on creation, prāṇa, sleep, **Oṃ (the syllable as Brahman)**, and the "person of sixteen parts" (ṣoḍaśa-kala).
  - **Famous passage:** the 5th question — meditation on **Oṃ** with one, two, or three mātrās leading to progressively higher realms (linking it closely to the Māṇḍūkya).
  - **Dating:** late prose, around the start of the Common Era (Olivelle). [late text]

- **Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad** — of the **Atharvaveda** (Śaunaka school). Three muṇḍakas of two khaṇḍas each. *(Source: Muṇḍaka Up.)*
  - **Key teaching:** the distinction of **parā vidyā** (higher knowledge — of the imperishable Brahman) vs. **aparā vidyā** (lower knowledge — the Vedas, rituals, sciences; 1.1.4–5); a strong note that ritual works ("leaky boats," 1.2.7) cannot grant liberation.
  - **Famous passages:** the **two birds on one tree** — one eating the fruit (the embodied self), one looking on serene (the Supreme Self; 3.1.1–2), shared verbatim with Śvetāśvatara 4.6; and **"Satyam eva jayate"** ("Truth alone triumphs," 3.1.6) — the **national motto of India**.
  - **Dating:** verse group, last few centuries BCE.

- **Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad** — of the **Atharvaveda**; the **shortest** principal Upaniṣad (12 prose verses). *(Source: Māṇḍūkya Up.)*
  - **Key teaching / mahāvākya:** **Ayam ātmā Brahma** ("This Self is Brahman," v.2); the analysis of **Oṃ (AUM)** as the four states of consciousness — **jāgrat** (waking), **svapna** (dream), **suṣupti** (deep sleep), and **turīya** (the "fourth," pure non-dual awareness; v.7).
  - **Famous association:** **Gauḍapāda's Māṇḍūkya-kārikā** (c. 6th–7th c. CE) — the foundational systematic statement of **Advaita Vedānta** (ajāta-vāda, "non-origination") — is appended to it; the Muktikā says this one text alone can grant liberation.
  - **Dating:** latest of the prose principal Upaniṣads, around the turn of the Common Era. [late text]

### Śukla Yajurveda — the great early prose text

- **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad** ("Great Forest Teaching") — the closing chapters of the **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa** (Śukla Yajurveda; Kāṇva and Mādhyandina recensions). Six chapters in three kāṇḍas (Madhu, Yājñavalkya/Muni, Khila). **The oldest and longest principal Upaniṣad.** *(Source: Bṛhadāraṇyaka Up.)*
  - **Key teaching / mahāvākya:** **Ahaṃ Brahmāsmi** ("I am Brahman," 1.4.10); the **neti neti** ("not this, not this," 2.3.6 & 3.9.26) apophatic method for pointing to Brahman.
  - **Famous passages:** the **Yājñavalkya–Maitreyī** dialogue — *"not for love of the husband is the husband dear, but for love of the Self"* (2.4 / 4.5); the **Yājñavalkya–Gārgī** debate at King Janaka's court (3.6, 3.8) with the formidable woman philosopher Gārgī Vācaknavī; the **Pavamāna / Asato mā** prayer *"Lead me from the unreal to the real, from darkness to light, from death to immortality"* (1.3.28); the cosmic horse-sacrifice opening (1.1).
  - **Dating:** earliest stratum, c. 7th c. BCE; generally judged marginally older than Chāndogya.

## 4. At-a-Glance Table

| Upaniṣad | Parent Veda | Form | Mahāvākya / key idea | Olivelle layer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bṛhadāraṇyaka | Śukla Yajurveda | prose | *Ahaṃ Brahmāsmi*; neti neti | earliest |
| Chāndogya | Sāmaveda | prose | *Tat tvam asi* | earliest |
| Taittirīya | Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda | prose | five sheaths; Brahman = bliss | early prose |
| Aitareya | Ṛgveda | prose | *Prajñānaṃ Brahma* | early prose |
| Kauṣītaki | Ṛgveda | prose | prāṇa; rebirth paths | early prose |
| Kena | Sāmaveda | verse+prose | Brahman behind mind/senses | early verse |
| Kaṭha | Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda | verse | Naciketas; chariot allegory | verse |
| Īśa | Śukla Yajurveda | verse | action + renunciation | verse |
| Śvetāśvatara | Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda | verse | theistic Rudra/Śiva = Brahman | verse |
| Muṇḍaka | Atharvaveda | verse | parā vs. aparā vidyā; *Satyam eva jayate* | verse |
| Praśna | Atharvaveda | prose | six questions to Pippalāda | late prose |
| Māṇḍūkya | Atharvaveda | prose | *Ayam ātmā Brahma*; AUM & four states | late prose |
| Maitrī | Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda | prose | Sāṃkhya–Yoga synthesis | latest |

- **Maitrī / Maitrāyaṇīya Upaniṣad** — embedded in the **Maitrāyaṇī (Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda)** tradition; seven prahāṭhakas. The **latest** of the principal set: it explicitly folds in **Sāṃkhya** (the three guṇas) and **Yoga** (a six-limbed ṣaḍaṅga-yoga), discusses the **distinction between the supreme and individual self**, and even names "deluded" heterodox doctrines — marking the transition toward the classical darśanas. *(Source: Maitrī Up.)* [late text]
