# Smṛti — Itihāsa (The Epics)

> Where this sits: `90-literature-corpus/` — the texts that RECORD events on the cosmic clock. **Itihāsa** ("thus it was") = the two great epics, traditionally Smṛti ("remembered," human-authored) as opposed to Śruti (Veda, "heard"). Both narrate events in the current 28th Mahāyuga: the **Rāmāyaṇa** in [Tretā Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) and the **Mahābhārata** at the [Dvāpara→Kali](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) junction (Kṛṣṇa's departure = start of Kali, 3102 BCE).

The two itihāsas are the cosmos's primary narrative records of the Vaivasvata Manvantara's 28th cycle: avatāra 7 (Rāma) and avatāra 8 (Kṛṣṇa) of Viṣṇu. This node catalogs their STRUCTURE, authorship tradition, and the full spread of regional/sectarian recensions and retellings. For the dharma-decline backdrop see [the four yugas](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current); for avatāra theology see [../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy).

## I. The Rāmāyaṇa (Vālmīki)

- **Core data:** Sanskrit; ~24,000 ślokas in **7 kāṇḍas** (books), ~500 sargas (chapters). Composed in the anuṣṭubh meter. Attributed to sage **Vālmīki**, the *ādi-kavi* ("first poet"); tradition holds the anuṣṭubh verse itself was born from his grief-cry (*śoka → śloka*) on seeing a hunter kill a krauñca bird. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*)
- **Cosmic placement:** narrates the deeds of **Rāma**, 7th avatāra of Viṣṇu, in [Tretā Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) (dharma on 3 legs). Vālmīki is depicted as a contemporary of Rāma — he shelters Sītā and teaches the epic to Rāma's sons Lava and Kuśa. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara Kāṇḍa)
- **Academic dating:** core text composed roughly **7th–4th c. BCE**, with the Bāla and Uttara kāṇḍas generally judged later additions; reached present form by ~early centuries CE. (scholarly consensus, e.g. R. Goldman, *The Rāmāyaṇa of Vālmīki*)

### The 7 Kāṇḍas (events, in narrative order)
- **Bāla Kāṇḍa** ("childhood") — *Birth & training of Rāma.* King Daśaratha of Ayodhyā, sonless, performs the *putrakāmeṣṭi* yajña; Viṣṇu incarnates as four sons (Rāma, Bharata, Lakṣmaṇa, Śatrughna). Sage Viśvāmitra takes Rāma to slay the demoness Tāṭakā and guard sacrifices; Rāma breaks Śiva's bow and wins **Sītā** (daughter of Janaka of Mithilā) at her svayaṃvara. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* 1)
  - *Variant (academic):* Bāla Kāṇḍa widely regarded a later prefix — divinity-of-Rāma framing and the *śloka*-origin legend belong to this stratum.
- **Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa** — *The exile.* On the eve of Rāma's coronation, queen Kaikeyī (prompted by maid Mantharā) invokes two boons: Bharata to be king, Rāma exiled 14 years. Rāma departs with Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa; Daśaratha dies of grief; Bharata refuses the throne and rules in Rāma's name, installing Rāma's sandals (*pādukā*). (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* 2)
- **Araṇya Kāṇḍa** ("forest") — *Abduction of Sītā.* Life in Daṇḍaka forest; Rāma slays demons; Śūrpaṇakhā (Rāvaṇa's sister) is mutilated by Lakṣmaṇa after her advances. Rāvaṇa sends Mārīca as a golden deer to lure Rāma away, then abducts Sītā to Laṅkā; the vulture **Jaṭāyu** dies trying to stop him. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* 3)
  - *Variant (Adbhuta & some Purāṇas):* a **Māyā-Sītā / Chāyā-Sītā** ("shadow Sītā") is abducted while the real Sītā hides in fire — used to preserve Sītā's purity; prominent later in Tulsīdās and Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa (see §III).
- **Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa** — *Alliance with the vānaras.* Rāma allies with **Sugrīva** (monkey-king), kills his usurping brother Vālī, and gains the army; **Hanumān** emerges as chief agent; the search for Sītā begins. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* 4)
  - *Variant:* the killing of Vālī from concealment is the epic's most-debated dharma question; later texts add Rāma's justifications and Vālī's protest.
- **Sundara Kāṇḍa** ("beautiful") — *Hanumān's leap.* Hanumān leaps the ocean to Laṅkā, finds Sītā in the Aśoka grove, gives Rāma's ring, burns Laṅkā with his tail, returns with news. The only kāṇḍa centered on Hanumān; recited devotionally as a self-contained unit. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* 5)
- **Yuddha / Laṅkā Kāṇḍa** ("war") — *The war & rescue.* Vānaras build the bridge (*Setu/Rāma Setu*) to Laṅkā; great battle; Rāvaṇa's brother **Vibhīṣaṇa** defects to Rāma; deaths of Kumbhakarṇa, Indrajit (Meghanāda), and finally **Rāvaṇa**. Sītā undergoes the fire-ordeal (*agni-parīkṣā*); Rāma returns to Ayodhyā and is crowned (**Rāma-rājya**). (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* 6)
- **Uttara Kāṇḍa** ("later") — *The epilogue.* Rāvaṇa's back-story and lineage; Rāma banishes the pregnant Sītā over a washerman's gossip; she lives in Vālmīki's hermitage and bears **Lava and Kuśa**, who later challenge Rāma's Aśvamedha horse; Sītā is swallowed by the Earth (her mother); Rāma departs into the Sarayū river, returning to Vaikuṇṭha. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* 7)
  - *Variant (academic):* Uttara Kāṇḍa is broadly judged a later appendix; many devotional traditions (and several regional epics, e.g. Kamban) **omit or downplay** the banishment of Sītā.

### Sanskrit recensions of Vālmīki
- **Three regional pāṭhas (recensions):** the **Dākṣiṇātya** (Southern), the **Gauḍīya** (Bengal/Eastern), and the **Vāyavya / Uttarīya** (North-western) — diverging in verse count and wording, the basis for the modern critical edition. (*Versions of the Ramayana*, Wikipedia; Baroda Critical Edition, M.S. University 1960–75)

## II. The Mahābhārata (Vyāsa)

- **Core data:** Sanskrit; the longest poem in the world — traditionally **100,000 ślokas** ("Śatasāhasrī Saṃhitā") in **18 parvas** (books) plus the **Harivaṃśa** appendix (the *khila*, ~16,000 verses). Subdivided into ~100 sub-parvas and ~2,000 chapters. (*Mahābhārata*; BORI)
- **Authorship tradition:** attributed to **Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa** ("the compiler," also Veda-Vyāsa), who is also a *character* and progenitor in the story (grandfather of the Kuru/Pāṇḍava lines). Tradition says Vyāsa dictated and **Gaṇeśa** wrote it down — on condition Vyāsa never pause, while Gaṇeśa must understand each verse before writing; Vyāsa wove in *kūṭa* (knotty) verses to buy time. (*Mahābhārata*, Ādi Parva)
- **Three-stage growth (tradition + scholarship):** the text names its own expansion — **Jaya** ("Victory," 8,800 verses) → **Bhārata** (24,000 verses) → **Mahābhārata** (100,000). Academic dating: composed ~**400 BCE–400 CE**, layered over centuries. (V.S. Sukthankar, BORI Prolegomena)
- **Critical Edition:** **Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute (BORI), Pune**, 1919–1966 (~47 years), under V.S. Sukthankar et al.; constituted text of ~75,000 verses from collation of **1,259 manuscripts** across all scripts/regions. The standard scholarly reference. (BORI)
- **Cosmic placement:** the **Kurukṣetra war** and **Kṛṣṇa** (8th avatāra) belong to the close of [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md); Kṛṣṇa's departure marks the onset of **[Kali Yuga, 3102 BCE](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** (see [the cosmic address](../00-time-and-cosmology/README.md#you-are-here-current-cosmic-address)). The epic thus straddles the yuga-junction.

### The 18 Parvas (events, in order)
- **1. Ādi Parva** ("beginning") — *Origins.* Frame narration by Ugraśravas at Naimiṣa forest; Vyāsa's compilation; lineage of Kuru; births of Bhīṣma, Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Pāṇḍu, and the **Pāṇḍavas** & **Kauravas**; the lac-house (Lākṣāgṛha) plot; Draupadī's svayaṃvara and marriage to the five Pāṇḍavas. (*Mahābhārata* 1)
- **2. Sabhā Parva** ("assembly hall") — *The dice game.* Yudhiṣṭhira's Rājasūya; the rigged dice match against Śakuni; loss of kingdom, brothers, self, and Draupadī; her attempted disrobing (Kṛṣṇa's miracle) and the **13-year exile** decree. (*Mahābhārata* 2)
- **3. Vana / Āraṇyaka Parva** ("forest") — *The exile.* 12 years in the forest; pilgrimages and sub-tales — **Nala & Damayantī**, the abridged **Rāmopākhyāna** (Rāma's story inside the epic), Sāvitrī & Satyavān, the Yakṣa-praśna; Arjuna's penance and divine weapons. The longest-tale-bearing parva. (*Mahābhārata* 3)
- **4. Virāṭa Parva** — *The year in disguise.* The 13th year incognito at King Virāṭa's court; the Pāṇḍavas in hidden roles; Bhīma kills Kīcaka; Arjuna (as Bṛhannalā) repels the Kaurava cattle-raid. (*Mahābhārata* 4)
- **5. Udyoga Parva** ("effort") — *The failed peace.* War preparations; **Kṛṣṇa's peace embassy** to Hastināpura fails (Duryodhana refuses even five villages); armies muster; Karṇa's parentage revealed to him. (*Mahābhārata* 5)
- **6. Bhīṣma Parva** — *War begins.* Contains the **Bhagavad Gītā** (700 verses, Kṛṣṇa's counsel to Arjuna before battle); first 10 days under commander Bhīṣma, who falls on the bed of arrows. (*Mahābhārata* 6)
- **7. Droṇa Parva** — *Days 11–15.* Droṇa as commander; the cakra-vyūha and death of **Abhimanyu**; Jayadratha slain; Droṇa killed via the "Aśvatthāmā is dead" ruse. (*Mahābhārata* 7)
- **8. Karṇa Parva** — *Days 16–17.* Karṇa commands; his chariot sinks; **Arjuna kills Karṇa**. (*Mahābhārata* 8)
- **9. Śalya Parva** — *Day 18.* Śalya commands and is killed; the mace-duel; Bhīma smashes **Duryodhana's** thigh, mortally wounding him. (*Mahābhārata* 9)
- **10. Sauptika Parva** ("of the sleeping") — *The night massacre.* Aśvatthāmā, Kṛpa, Kṛtavarmā slaughter the sleeping Pāṇḍava camp; the **Brahmāstra** duel; Aśvatthāmā cursed. (*Mahābhārata* 10)
- **11. Strī Parva** ("of the women") — *The lament.* Gāndhārī, Kuntī and the widows mourn the dead; Gāndhārī curses Kṛṣṇa (foreshadowing the Yādava ruin). (*Mahābhārata* 11)
- **12. Śānti Parva** ("peace") — *Yudhiṣṭhira's instruction.* Bhīṣma on his arrow-bed teaches *rāja-dharma*, *āpad-dharma*, and *mokṣa-dharma*; vast didactic/philosophical core. The largest parva. (*Mahābhārata* 12)
- **13. Anuśāsana Parva** ("instruction") — *Further teaching.* Continued discourse on dharma, gifts, vows; the Viṣṇu Sahasranāma; Bhīṣma's death (departs at the winter solstice). (*Mahābhārata* 13)
- **14. Aśvamedhika Parva** — *The horse sacrifice.* Yudhiṣṭhira's Aśvamedha to expiate the war; the **Anu-Gītā** (Kṛṣṇa's recap of the Gītā's teaching). (*Mahābhārata* 14)
- **15. Āśramavāsika Parva** — *Retirement.* Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Gāndhārī, Kuntī retire to the forest and perish in a wildfire. (*Mahābhārata* 15)
- **16. Mausala Parva** ("of the clubs") — *Fall of the Yādavas.* Gāndhārī's curse ripens: the Yādavas slay each other with iron clubs at Prabhāsa; **Kṛṣṇa is killed** by a hunter (Jarā) — marking the onset of [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md); Dvārakā sinks into the sea. (*Mahābhārata* 16)
- **17. Mahāprasthānika Parva** ("great departure") — *The final journey.* The Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī walk toward Mount Meru/heaven; all fall one by one; only Yudhiṣṭhira and a dog (Dharma in disguise) reach the gate. (*Mahābhārata* 17)
- **18. Svargārohaṇa Parva** ("ascent to heaven") — *The afterworld.* Yudhiṣṭhira's test in seeing his kin in apparent hell/heaven; final reconciliation; the heroes attain their celestial states. (*Mahābhārata* 18)

### Embedded texts & appendix
- **Bhagavad Gītā** — within Bhīṣma Parva (ch. 23–40); 18 chapters, 700 verses; Kṛṣṇa's synthesis of karma-, bhakti-, and jñāna-yoga. The single most influential Hindu philosophical text; arguably itself Smṛti/Upaniṣadic in standing. (see [../90-literature-corpus](.) and avatāra theology)
- **Harivaṃśa** ("genealogy of Hari") — the *khila* (appendix), ~16,000 verses in 3 parvas (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu, Bhaviṣya); supplies **Kṛṣṇa's birth and childhood** (absent from the main epic) and Yādava/Vṛṣṇi lineage; bridges itihāsa and the Kṛṣṇa-centric Purāṇas (esp. Bhāgavata). (*Harivaṃśa*)
- **Major embedded sub-tales** (upākhyānas): Nala–Damayantī, Sāvitrī–Satyavān, Śakuntalā (Ādi), Rāmopākhyāna (the Rāmāyaṇa-in-miniature), Yayāti, Ṛṣyaśṛṅga, Mārkaṇḍeya's discourses. (*Mahābhārata*)

## III. Recensions, Reworkings & Regional Retellings (CATALOG OF VARIANTS)

> The dictum (A.K. Ramanujan, *Three Hundred Rāmāyaṇas*) is that there is no single "the" Rāmāyaṇa — hundreds exist across languages, religions, and centuries. Below catalogs the notable ones; sectarian framing noted where it shifts theology.

### A. Sanskrit re-castings of the Rāmāyaṇa
- **Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa** — *Vedāntic/Rāma-as-Brahman retelling*, extracted from the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa (~14th–15th c.); Rāma is fully divine (Saguṇa Brahman), Sītā is Māyā; introduces/centralizes the **Māyā-Sītā** device. The primary devotional source-text behind Tulsīdās. (*Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa*)
- **Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa** — late text (~15th c.) focused on Rāma's later life, ritual, and peripheral legends. (*Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa*)
- **Adbhuta Rāmāyaṇa** — Śākta-leaning; **Sītā** as the active power who slays a thousand-headed Rāvaṇa (as Mahākālī) when Rāma faints — foregrounds the Goddess. (*Adbhuta Rāmāyaṇa*)
- **Yoga Vāsiṣṭha (Mahā-Rāmāyaṇa)** — vast Advaita dialogue between Vasiṣṭha and the young Rāma; philosophy, not narrative. (*Yoga Vāsiṣṭha*)
- **Hanumān-nāṭaka / Mahānāṭaka** — dramatic Sanskrit retelling foregrounding Hanumān. (late text)

### B. Indian vernacular Rāmāyaṇas
- **Irāmāvatāram / Kamba-rāmāyaṇam** (Tamil; **Kambar**, 12th c.) — ~10,000+ verses; a literary masterpiece, not a translation; Rāma fully divine; **omits the Uttara Kāṇḍa** (no banishment of Sītā). (*Versions of the Ramayana*)
- **Rāmacaritamānasa** (Awadhi Hindi; **Tulsīdās**, 1574 CE) — ~12,000 lines in **7 kāṇḍas** mirroring Vālmīki; the supreme North-Indian devotional Rāmāyaṇa; bhakti to Rāma as Supreme God; uses Māyā-Sītā so the real Sītā is never truly defiled; basis of the Rāmlīlā performance tradition. (*Rāmacaritamānasa*)
- **Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇ** (Bengali; **Kṛttibās Ojhā**, 15th c.) — the most popular Bengali version; adds folk/devotional episodes (e.g. Rāma's autumn worship of Durgā — *akāl-bodhan*). (*Versions of the Ramayana*)
- **Raṅganātha Rāmāyaṇam** (Telugu; **Gona Budda Reddy**, ~1300–10) and **Molla Rāmāyaṇam** (Telugu; **Ātukūri Molla**, a woman poet, 16th c.). (*Versions of the Ramayana*)
- **Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇam Kilippāṭṭu** (Malayalam; **Thunchaththu Ezhuthachan**, 16th c.) — foundational to Malayalam literature. (*Versions of the Ramayana*)
- **Saptakāṇḍa Rāmāyaṇa** (Assamese; **Mādhava Kandalī**, 14th c.) — among the earliest regional vernacular Rāmāyaṇas. (*Versions of the Ramayana*)
- **Kannada:** Jaina **Pampa/Nāgacandra Rāmacandra-carita-purāṇa** (12th c.) and **Kumudendu / Torave Rāmāyaṇa** (Kumara Valmiki). (*Versions of the Ramayana*)
- **Marathi:** **Bhāvārtha Rāmāyaṇa** of **Sant Eknāth** (16th c.). **Nepali:** **Bhānubhakta Rāmāyaṇa** (Bhanubhakta Acharya, 19th c.), first major Nepali epic. (*Versions of the Ramayana*)

### C. Jain Rāmāyaṇas (Rāma = noble human, NOT divine; non-violent reframing)
- **Paümacariya(ṃ)** (Prakrit; **Vimalasūri**, ~earliest Jain version) — Rāma (Padma) is a pious Jain layman/balabhadra; **Lakṣmaṇa kills Rāvaṇa**, not Rāma (Rāma is non-violent); Rāvaṇa is a learned vidyādhara, not a literal demon; rationalizes the "monkeys" as a vidyādhara clan. Rāma attains mokṣa. (*Versions of the Ramayana*)
- **Padma-purāṇa** (Sanskrit; **Raviṣeṇa**) and **Jain Rāmāyaṇa** within Hemacandra's **Triṣaṣṭiśalākāpuruṣacarita** (12th c.) continue the Jain reframing. (*Versions of the Ramayana*)

### D. Buddhist Rāmāyaṇa
- **Daśaratha Jātaka** (Pali) — Rāma (Rāma-paṇḍita) as a **Bodhisattva**; notably the exile is to spare him from a scheming queen, there is **no abduction of Sītā** in the oldest form, and **Sītā is Rāma's sister** as well as wife (a Jātaka peculiarity); teaches equanimity in the face of death. (*Versions of the Ramayana*; Dasaratha Jataka)

### E. Southeast Asian Rāmāyaṇas
- **Rāmakien** (Thailand) — the national epic; adapted via Khmer sources; Hanumān is a prominent, more roguish hero; Rāvaṇa (Thotsakan) is sympathetically drawn; basis of khon masked dance. Draws on the Jātaka (Dasaratha), Viṣṇu Purāṇa and Hanumān-nāṭaka. (*Versions of the Ramayana*; British Library)
- **Reamker** (Cambodia/Khmer) — older than and parent to the Thai version; Buddhist coloring; famous shadow-puppet and Apsara-dance episodes. (*Versions of the Ramayana*)
- **Phra Lak Phra Lām / Phra Lak Phra Ram** (Laos) — title names both brothers (Lakṣmaṇa & Rāma); treated as a **Jātaka** (a past life of the Buddha, the Dasaratha Jataka); Hindu association largely dropped. (*Phra Lak Phra Lam*, Wikipedia)
- **Kakawin Rāmāyaṇa** (Old Javanese, ~9th c.) — earliest and most faithful SE-Asian version, based on the Sanskrit *Bhaṭṭikāvya*; preserved in Bali; basis of wayang Rāmāyaṇa. (*Versions of the Ramayana*)
- **Hikayat Seri Rama** (Malay) — Islamicized framing (Allah referenced); Rāvaṇa given heroic dimension. (*Versions of the Ramayana*)
- **Yama Zatdaw** (Myanmar/Burma) — the unofficial national epic, via Thai sources; performed as yama pwe theatre. (*Versions of the Ramayana*)
- **Maharadia Lawana** (Philippines, Maranao/Moro) — an Islamized, localized fragment of the Rāma story. (*Versions of the Ramayana*)

### F. Mahābhārata recensions & retellings
- **Two main Sanskrit recensions:** the **Northern** and **Southern** (the latter fuller/more ornate); reconciled in the **BORI Critical Edition** (see §II). (BORI)
- **Regional/vernacular Mahābhāratas:** **Villiputtūrār Pāratam** (Tamil), **Pampa Bhārata / Vikramārjuna Vijaya** (Kannada, Jain-tinged, 10th c.), **Nannaya–Tikkana–Errapragada Andhra Mahābhāratam** (Telugu, the Kavitraya), **Sāraḷā Dāsa Mahābhārata** (Odia), **Kāśīrām Dās Mahābhārat** (Bengali), **Razmnāma** (Persian, commissioned by Akbar). (*Mahābhārata*, regional traditions)
- **Jain Mahābhārata tradition:** reworks the Kṛṣṇa/Pāṇḍava material around the 22nd Tīrthaṅkara Nemināṭha (a cousin of Kṛṣṇa); the Pāṇḍavas attain liberation as Jain monks (e.g. **Pāṇḍava-purāṇa**). (Jain tradition)
- **Bhagavad Gītā** has its own vast commentarial "recension" tradition (Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja, Madhva, Jñāneśvar's Marathi *Jñāneśvarī*, etc.) — see [../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy).
