# Maitrāyaṇīya (Maitrī) Upaniṣad

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/upanishads/`](./README.md) — a per-text deep-dive into the **Maitrāyaṇīya (Maitrī) Upaniṣad**, the **latest of the thirteen principal (*mukhya*) Upaniṣads** and the **transitional seam** at which Upaniṣadic Vedānta visibly absorbs the categories of **classical Sāṃkhya** and lays out a **six-limbed (ṣaḍaṅga) yoga** that prefigures Patañjali. Companion to the cross-text overview in [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md); for the yoga-technique stream it feeds see [`./yoga-upanishads-overview.md`](./yoga-upanishads-overview.md); for the Sāṃkhya categories it imports see [`../darshanas/samkhya-karika.md`](../darshanas/samkhya-karika.md). Master index: [`../../README.md`](../../README.md).

## 1. Identity, Placement, Dating

- **Names** — known both as the **Maitrāyaṇīya Upaniṣad** (after the **Maitrāyaṇī śākhā**, the school whose name it carries) and, in the shorter contracted form, the **Maitrī Upaniṣad**; occasionally **Maitrāyaṇa / Maitrāyaṇa-brāhmaṇa Upaniṣad**. *(Source: Maitrī Up.; Muktikā tradition)*
- **Parent Veda & school** — belongs to the **Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda**, transmitted in the **Maitrāyaṇī (Maitrāyaṇīya) caraṇa/śākhā** — a Black-Yajurveda school that also preserves its own Saṃhitā (the Maitrāyaṇī Saṃhitā). *(Source: Maitrī Up.; Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda tradition)*
- **Counted among the principal Upaniṣads** — included in the standard **thirteen-principal set** edited by **Hume** and **Radhakrishnan** (the ten Śaṅkara-commented *mukhya* plus Śvetāśvatara, Kauṣītaki and Maitrī); numbered variously in the **Muktikā** list of 108. *(Source: Hume, *The Thirteen Principal Upaniṣads*; Radhakrishnan, *The Principal Upaniṣads*)*
- **Scale & structure** — a **prose Upaniṣad in seven *prapāṭhakas* (chapters/lessons)**; the last two (and parts of others) are widely regarded as **later appendices/khilas (supplements)**. *(Source: Maitrī Up.)* [composite text]
- **Dating (two clocks)** —
  - *Academic:* the **latest of the thirteen principal Upaniṣads**, a **late, composite prose work** generally placed **c. 3rd–1st c. BCE or later**, after Sāṃkhya categories and proto-Yoga terminology had crystallised; it quotes and presupposes earlier Upaniṣads and even shows post-Buddhist features. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (orthodox / traditional):* eternal *apauruṣeya* Śruti "seen" by ṛṣis and fixed at the Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; see Kali-Yuga onset constant), not "authored" in a BCE stratum. Both frames are recorded here; neither is corrected. [scripture vs. academic dating]
- **Composite, layered character** — the text is **stratified and uneven**: a core dialogue swollen by inserted hymns, etymologies and doctrinal digressions, with a textual transmission so loose that **editions diverge** in chapter-and-verse numbering and in which passages count as original. Its **explicit Sāṃkhya guṇa-vocabulary and ṣaḍaṅga-yoga** mark it as standing **at the threshold of the classical darśanas** rather than within the older Upaniṣadic world. [late text] [disputed redaction]

## 2. The Frame — King Bṛhadratha and the Sage Śākāyanya

- **Bṛhadratha's renunciation (1.1–1.2)** — **King Bṛhadratha**, having established his son in the kingdom and judged the body **"impermanent (anitya)"** and the world **transient**, retires to the **forest** to practise austerity (tapas), standing with arms uplifted, gazing at the sun, for a long period. *(Source: Maitrī Up. 1.1)* — a classic **renouncer frame**, signalling the moment the Upaniṣadic quest is set inside a king's *saṃnyāsa*.
- **The boon and the question (1.2–1.4)** — the sage **Śākāyanya**, pleased by Bṛhadratha's austerities, appears and offers a boon; the king asks not for return to the body but for **knowledge of the Self (ātma-vidyā)** — declaring the **Self caught in the cycle of bodies** to be like a frog in a dry well. *(Source: Maitrī Up. 1.2–1.4)*
- **The catalogue of decay (1.4)** — Bṛhadratha pours out a famous **lament on bodily and cosmic transience**: the body is a stinking, pithless frame of bone, skin, sinew, marrow, flesh, bile and phlegm; great kings (a long *ubi sunt* list — Sudyumna, Bhūridyumna, Indradyumna and others) and even **seas, mountains, the pole-star, gods and the cosmos itself perish** — "in this kind of cycle of existence (saṃsāra), what is the good of the enjoyment of desires?" *(Source: Maitrī Up. 1.4)* — one of the **strongest vanitas / world-weariness passages in the Upaniṣads**.
- **Śākāyanya's reluctant teaching (2.1ff.)** — Śākāyanya warns this is **hard knowledge**, then (in some recensions deferring to **Maitri / the Vālakhilyas**) consents and expounds the **two-fold Self** and the path beyond it. *(Source: Maitrī Up. 2.1–2.3)* [transmission-name varies by edition]

## 3. The Two-Fold Brahman — Bhūtātman and the Supreme

- **The two forms of Brahman** — the Upaniṣad teaches that Brahman has **"two forms (dve vāva brahmaṇo rūpe)"**: the **embodied / elemental self (bhūtātman, śarīra-ātman)** entangled in the body and the guṇas, and the **supreme, tranquil, unmoving Self (the higher Brahman / paramātman)**. *(Source: Maitrī Up. 2.5; 6.3 "dve vāva brahmaṇo rūpe")* — the **mūrta/amūrta (formed/formless)** distinction here sharpened into the **bound vs. transcendent** Self.
- **The bhūtātman bound by the guṇas (3.2)** — the **elemental self (bhūtātman)** is **overcome by the qualities (guṇas) of prakṛti**; **bewildered by them, it does not perceive the blessed Lord dwelling within**, and so is **driven to and fro, sunk, deluded, full of desire** through countless births. *(Source: Maitrī Up. 3.2)* — explicitly attributes bondage to the *guṇas*, importing Sāṃkhya's diagnosis.
- **The inner ruler untouched (3.3)** — like the sun unstained by the eye's faults, or a charioteer differing from his chariot, the **inner Self (the higher Brahman)** remains **un-acting, un-bound, the witness**, while it is the **bhūtātman** that, **driven by the bright or dark fruits of works**, wanders. *(Source: Maitrī Up. 3.2–3.5)*
- **Liberation as recovery of the higher Self (4.2–4.4)** — release comes when the embodied self, by **knowledge, austerity and meditation**, **shakes off the guṇa-born qualities** and is **lifted up into union with the supreme, blissful Ātman** — described as **"the elemental self mounting upward to its own source."** *(Source: Maitrī Up. 4.2–4.4)*

## 4. The Absorption of Sāṃkhya — The Three Guṇas and Prakṛti

- **The three guṇas named outright (5.2)** — the Maitrī gives an **explicit, systematic statement of the three *guṇas* — sattva, rajas, tamas** — as the constituent strands of nature, mapped onto a **theological triad** (in one famous passage **tamas → Rudra, rajas → Brahmā, sattva → Viṣṇu**, the threefold unfolding of the One). *(Source: Maitrī Up. 5.2)* — among the **clearest pre-Gītā Upaniṣadic uses of the guṇa-triad as cosmological principles**; cf. [`../darshanas/samkhya-karika.md`](../darshanas/samkhya-karika.md).
- **Prakṛti as the field of bondage** — the **bhūtātman's bondage is precisely its identification with prakṛti's guṇas**; the language of **the qualities "binding," "colouring" and "bewildering"** the self is essentially the **Sāṃkhya account of *prakṛti* enmeshing *puruṣa***, here folded into a Vedāntic frame that **retains a supreme Self/Brahman above** the dualism. *(Source: Maitrī Up. 3.2; 6.10)*
- **Distinctive synthesis** — unlike classical **non-theistic (nirīśvara) Sāṃkhya** (prakṛti + plural puruṣas, no God), the Maitrī **keeps the supreme Brahman/Īśvara** over the guṇa-bound self — a **theistic-Vedāntic recasting of Sāṃkhya categories**, parallel to the move made by the [Śvetāśvatara](./svetashvatara-upanishad.md) and pointing toward the **Bhagavad-Gītā's** guṇa-doctrine. [Vedānta absorbing Sāṃkhya] [scholarly]
- **The food-and-eater, the cosmic Person (6.9–6.13)** — long passages on **Anna (food) and the eater**, the **Vaiśvānara fire**, and the **Person of the sun and within the eye** weave the older Upaniṣadic symbolism together with the new analytic vocabulary of constituents and faculties. *(Source: Maitrī Up. 6.9–6.13)*

## 5. The Six-Limbed Yoga — Ṣaḍaṅga-Yoga (6.18)

- **The definition (6.18)** — the Maitrī sets out the **"sixfold yoga (ṣaḍaṅga-yoga)"** as the means of union with the supreme Self — *"prāṇāyāmaḥ pratyāhāro dhyānaṃ dhāraṇā tarkaḥ samādhiḥ ṣaḍ-aṅgā ity ucyate yogaḥ"*: the **six limbs are (1) prāṇāyāma (breath-control), (2) pratyāhāra (withdrawal of the senses), (3) dhyāna (meditation), (4) dhāraṇā (concentration/fixing), (5) tarka (inquiry/reasoning), (6) samādhi (absorption).** *(Source: Maitrī Up. 6.18)* — the **earliest *ṣaḍaṅga* (six-limb) yoga scheme in the principal Upaniṣads**.
- **Relation to Patañjali's eight limbs** — this six-limb list **prefigures the classical aṣṭāṅga-yoga** of the **Yoga-Sūtra** but **omits the first three** (*yama, niyama, āsana*) and uniquely **includes *tarka* (reasoning/discrimination)** — a limb absent from Patañjali's eight. The Maitrī's scheme is thus a **distinct, older sibling** of Patañjali's, not a copy of it. *(Source: Maitrī Up. 6.18; cf. Yoga-Sūtra 2.29)* [pre-Pātañjala ṣaḍaṅga model]
- **The downstream lineage** — this **ṣaḍaṅga-yoga** is the **direct Śruti ancestor of the six-limbed yoga taught in the later Yoga Upaniṣads** (e.g. Amṛtanāda, Dhyānabindu) and, through them, of **Haṭha/Tantra** practice; the **Maitrī's verse is repeatedly echoed** in that corpus — see [`./yoga-upanishads-overview.md`](./yoga-upanishads-overview.md). [scriptural root of ṣaḍaṅga-yoga]
- **Practice of Oṃ and the inner sound (6.21–6.25)** — yoga is centred on **meditation on *Oṃ* (praṇava)**, on the **upward channel (suṣumnā)** by which the breath rises through the palate, and on the **"unstruck" inner sound** heard by closing the ears — early **nāda / praṇava** material later systematised by the Yoga Upaniṣads. *(Source: Maitrī Up. 6.21–6.25)*
- **The fruit (6.30; 6.34)** — by the sixfold yoga the yogī **beholds the golden, brilliant Person**, casts off good and evil, and **"having become tranquil, attains unity in the Self (ātman)"**; *"as fire without fuel goes out in its own source, so the thought (citta), its activity stilled, subsides in its source."* *(Source: Maitrī Up. 6.30, 6.34)* — a **citta-nirodha** image strikingly close to Yoga-Sūtra 1.2.

## 6. Other Notable Material

- **Vālakhilya frame & Kratu (2.3)** — in some recensions the teaching is mediated through the **diminutive Vālakhilya sages** and the **Prajāpati Kratu / the Person Kratu**, on the self that **"holds up the body"** like spokes in a hub. *(Source: Maitrī Up. 2.3–2.6)*
- **The vile body and renunciation (1.3–1.4)** — its **disgust-meditation on the body** (a litany of its impurities) is among the most graphic in Śruti and feeds the later **Saṃnyāsa-Upaniṣad** mood of world-renunciation. *(Source: Maitrī Up. 1.3–1.4)*
- **Time and the two forms of Brahman (6.14–6.16)** — a notable passage on **Kāla (Time)** as a form of Brahman: from time all beings flow, in time they grow and disappear; **"there are two forms of Brahman, Time and the Timeless (kāla and akāla)."** *(Source: Maitrī Up. 6.14–6.16)* — an early Upaniṣadic theology of **Time as cosmic principle**.
- **Appendix chapters (6–7)** — the later *prapāṭhakas* heap up **hymns, the Gāyatrī and the "Sāvitrī" knowledge, denunciations of false teachers and heretics**, and miscellaneous praises — the clearest evidence of the text's **accretional growth**. *(Source: Maitrī Up. 6–7)* [khila / appendix layer] [disputed authenticity]

## 7. Why the Maitrī Matters — The Transitional Text

- **The last principal Upaniṣad** — it stands at the **far edge of the *mukhya* corpus**, the point where the genre **opens directly onto the classical *darśanas***: its vocabulary is already that of **Sāṃkhya (guṇas, prakṛti, bhūtātman) and Yoga (ṣaḍaṅga, prāṇāyāma, samādhi)** rather than the older Brāhmaṇic Upaniṣadic idiom. [bridge to the darśanas]
- **Sāṃkhya inside Vedānta** — it shows the historical mechanism by which **Sāṃkhya's analysis of bondage (the guṇas binding the self)** was **absorbed into a Brahman-centred Vedānta** that keeps a supreme Self above the dualism — the same synthesis the **Bhagavad-Gītā** would canonise. See [`../darshanas/samkhya-karika.md`](../darshanas/samkhya-karika.md).
- **The seed of systematic yoga in Śruti** — the **6.18 ṣaḍaṅga-yoga** is the **scriptural taproot of the entire later Yoga-Upaniṣad and Haṭha tradition**, supplying a six-limb model that the Yoga Upaniṣads inherit and expand — see [`./yoga-upanishads-overview.md`](./yoga-upanishads-overview.md).
- **A window on a lost school** — as the surviving Upaniṣad of the **Maitrāyaṇī (Black-Yajurveda) school**, it preserves the doctrinal flavour of a *caraṇa* otherwise known mainly through its Saṃhitā. For its placement among the thirteen see [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).
