# Praśna Upaniṣad

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/`](../README.md) — a per-text deep-dive into the **Praśna Upaniṣad**, a prose *mukhya* Upaniṣad of the **Atharvaveda** (Pippalāda śākhā), structured as **six "questions" (praśna)** put by six seekers to the sage **Pippalāda**. ## 1. Identity, Placement, Dating

- **Parent Veda & school** — The Praśna belongs to the **Atharvaveda**, transmitted in the **Pippalāda (Paippalāda) śākhā**, and is named after its frame-sage **Pippalāda**. *(Source: Praśna Up.; Muktikā Up.)*
- **Form & scale** — A **prose** Upaniṣad in **six praśnas ("questions")**, each opening with a named seeker's question and closing with Pippalāda's answer; it reads as a catechism, not a continuous treatise. Listed **4th** in the **Daśopaniṣad** mnemonic *"Īśa-Kena-Kaṭha-**Praśna**-Muṇḍa-Māṇḍūkya-Taittiri…"*. *(Source: Daśopaniṣad mnemonic; Praśna Up.)*
- **The six seekers** — **Sukeśā Bhāradvāja, Śaibya Satyakāma, Sauryāyaṇī Gārgya, Kausalya Āśvalāyana, Bhārgava Vaidarbhi,** and **Kabandhī Kātyāyana** — described as devoted to Brahman, fuel in hand (*samit-pāṇi*), who approach Pippalāda and are told to dwell a year in austerity, celibacy and faith before asking. *(Source: Praśna Up. 1.1–1.2)*
- **Dating (two clocks)** —
  - *Academic:* **late prose stratum**, generally placed **around the start of the Common Era**; Olivelle and others group it with the Māṇḍūkya as among the latest of the principal Upaniṣads. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (orthodox / traditional):* eternal *apauruṣeya* Śruti, "seen" (not authored) by ṛṣis and re-compiled by **Vyāsa** at the Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; see the Kali-onset constant); not a "BCE-stratum composition." This file records both frames and corrects neither. [scripture vs. academic dating]
- **Commentary** — **Ādi Śaṅkara** (c. 8th c. CE) wrote a **bhāṣya** on the Praśna, counting it among his ten canonical Upaniṣad commentaries. In his arrangement the **Praśna is the prose exposition of the verse Muṇḍaka** — the two Atharvan texts are treated as complementary, the Praśna unpacking in detail what the Muṇḍaka states in mantra. *(Source: Śaṅkara's Praśna-bhāṣya; intro)* — see [`mundaka-upanishad.md`](./mundaka-upanishad.md).

## 2. The Six Questions (praśnas)

### Praśna 1 — Kabandhī Kātyāyana: origin of creatures

- **The question** — *Whence are these creatures born?* *(Source: Praśna Up. 1.3)*
- **Rayi and Prāṇa** — Pippalāda answers that **Prajāpati**, desiring offspring, performed *tapas* and produced a **pair (mithuna)**: **rayi** (matter, the female/moon principle) and **prāṇa** (life/breath, the male/sun principle); from their union all creatures arise. *(Source: Praśna Up. 1.4)*
- **Sun and moon, year and paths** — **Āditya (the sun) is prāṇa**, **Candramā (the moon) is rayi/matter**; the **year (saṃvatsara) is Prajāpati**, with its two courses — the **southern path (dakṣiṇāyana)** of works/offspring leading to the world of the fathers (recurring rebirth), and the **northern path (uttarāyaṇa)** of *tapas, brahmacarya, śraddhā and vidyā* leading to the world of the sun (immortality). *(Source: Praśna Up. 1.5–1.10)*
- **Result** — creation is fundamentally the interplay of **matter (rayi) and life (prāṇa)**; everything has these two as its constituents. *(Source: Praśna Up. 1.13–1.16)*

### Praśna 2 — Bhārgava Vaidarbhi: the powers, and the supremacy of prāṇa

- **The question** — *How many powers (devas) sustain the creature, which illumine it, and which is supreme?* *(Source: Praśna Up. 2.1)*
- **The deva-faculties** — the sustaining powers are named — **space (ākāśa), wind (vāyu), fire (agni), water (āpas), earth (pṛthivī)**, together with **speech, mind, eye, ear** — each declaring "I uphold the body." *(Source: Praśna Up. 2.2)*
- **The bee / king parable** — **Prāṇa** asserts its primacy; when prāṇa makes to depart, all the other faculties find they cannot stand without it — as bees rise when the king-bee rises and settle when it settles. Hence **prāṇa is the chief (jyeṣṭha)** among the powers. *(Source: Praśna Up. 2.3–2.4)*
- **Hymn to Prāṇa** — a praise-hymn follows: prāṇa is identified with **Agni, Sūrya, Parjanya, Vāyu, Indra**, the gods and the sustainer of all; "as spokes in a hub, all is fixed in prāṇa." *(Source: Praśna Up. 2.5–2.13)*

### Praśna 3 — Kausalya Āśvalāyana: origin, division and departure of prāṇa

- **The question** — *Whence is prāṇa born, how does it enter and divide in the body, how does it depart, how sustain the outer and inner?* *(Source: Praśna Up. 3.1–3.2)*
- **Birth of prāṇa** — prāṇa **arises from the Ātman (Self)**; as a shadow follows a person, so prāṇa is projected by the Self and enters the body by the action of the mind. *(Source: Praśna Up. 3.3)*
- **The five-fold prāṇa** — prāṇa divides itself into **five vital airs**, each posted like an officer over a region: **prāṇa** (in eye, ear, mouth, nostrils — outward breath), **apāna** (in the lower organs of excretion and generation), **samāna** (in the middle, distributing food evenly — "the seven flames"), **vyāna** (pervading the body, in the *nāḍīs*), and **udāna** (the upward breath that leads the soul out at death). *(Source: Praśna Up. 3.4–3.5)*
- **The nāḍīs** — the **heart** holds **101 chief channels (nāḍīs)**, each branching a hundredfold and again a thousandfold, in which **vyāna** moves. *(Source: Praśna Up. 3.6)*
- **Departure at death** — by the **upāna (udāna)** breath the soul ascends; according to merit and one's last thought it is led to the corresponding world; **the man who departs through the upward, solar nāḍī** wins the higher world. *(Source: Praśna Up. 3.7–3.10)* — compare the death-passage and "two paths" in [`chandogya-upanishad.md`](./chandogya-upanishad.md).

### Praśna 4 — Sauryāyaṇī Gārgya: sleep, dream and the resting of the senses

- **The question** — *What sleeps in a person, what stays awake, which power sees dreams, in whom do all rest when sleep brings happiness?* *(Source: Praśna Up. 4.1)*
- **Sun-rays parable & the senses' rest** — as the sun's rays are gathered into its orb at setting and scatter again at dawn, so in **sleep** the senses are gathered into the **mind**; the sleeper then "hears not, sees not, smells not." The **five fires (the prāṇas) remain awake** guarding the city of the body even as the senses rest. *(Source: Praśna Up. 4.2–4.4)*
- **Dream (svapna)** — in dream the **mind**, illumined by its own impressions (*vāsanā*), re-experiences what was seen, heard and done — projecting a world from memory. *(Source: Praśna Up. 4.5)*
- **Deep sleep (suṣupti)** — when the mind too is overcome by light/sleep, it sees no dreams and rests in **bliss**; all the faculties, the elements and their objects, **merge into the supreme imperishable Ātman**. *(Source: Praśna Up. 4.6–4.9)*
- **The knower of the resting-place** — he who knows that **imperishable** in whom all rest "becomes all-knowing and enters into all." *(Source: Praśna Up. 4.10–4.11)* — the waking/dream/deep-sleep schema is the prose cousin of the Māṇḍūkya's four states; see [`mandukya-upanishad.md`](./mandukya-upanishad.md) §3.

### Praśna 5 — Śaibya Satyakāma: meditation on Oṃ

- **The question** — *What world does one win who meditates on **Oṃ** until death?* *(Source: Praśna Up. 5.1)*
- **The one, two and three mātrās** — Pippalāda answers that **Oṃ is both the lower and the higher Brahman**, and the fruit depends on how far the meditation reaches:
  - *One mātrā (A only):* meditation on a single measure brings quick return to the **world of men** (a human rebirth, endowed with *tapas, brahmacarya, śraddhā*). *(Source: Praśna Up. 5.3)*
  - *Two mātrās (AU):* meditation on two measures leads to the **lunar world (Soma-loka / antarikṣa)**, whence one returns after enjoyment. *(Source: Praśna Up. 5.4)*
  - *Three mātrās (AUM):* meditation on the complete syllable, all three measures together with the **higher Puruṣa**, leads to the **Brahma-loka / the world of the sun** and to the vision of the supreme Person — release from all evil "as a snake from its slough." *(Source: Praśna Up. 5.5)*
- **Oṃ the bow/support** — the wise, taking Oṃ as support, attain "that which is tranquil, ageless, deathless, fearless, the Supreme." *(Source: Praśna Up. 5.6–5.7)* — the graded-mātrā meditation parallels the Māṇḍūkya's A–U–M analysis; see [`mandukya-upanishad.md`](./mandukya-upanishad.md) §2.

### Praśna 6 — Sukeśā Bhāradvāja: the Person of sixteen parts

- **The challenge** — Sukeśā reports that the prince **Hiraṇyanābha Kausalya** had once asked him about the **Puruṣa of sixteen parts (ṣoḍaśa-kala puruṣa)** and he could not answer; he now puts it to Pippalāda: *where is that Person?* *(Source: Praśna Up. 6.1–6.2)*
- **The Puruṣa in the body** — Pippalāda answers that **that very Person is here, within the body**; from him the sixteen **kalās** are produced. *(Source: Praśna Up. 6.2–6.3)*
- **The sixteen kalās** — the Puruṣa, reflecting, projects in order: **prāṇa; faith (śraddhā); the five elements (ākāśa, vāyu, agni, āpas, pṛthivī); the senses (indriya); mind (manas); food (anna); from food — vigour (vīrya), austerity (tapas), the mantras, action (karma), and the worlds (loka); and in the worlds, name (nāma)** — sixteen in all. *(Source: Praśna Up. 6.3–6.4)*
- **The dissolution (the river simile)** — at liberation the sixteen kalās **flow back into and dissolve in the Puruṣa**, just as **rivers flowing to the sea lose their name and form (nāma-rūpa) in the ocean** and are simply called "the sea"; so the parts of the knower merge in the Person, who is **partless (akala) and immortal**. *(Source: Praśna Up. 6.5)* — the same river-to-sea image appears in the Muṇḍaka; see [`mundaka-upanishad.md`](./mundaka-upanishad.md).
- **The closing teaching** — *"He is the Self to be known, in whom the kalās rest like spokes in the nave of a wheel."* Pippalāda declares this the highest object of knowledge, beyond which there is nothing, and dismisses the six, who honour him as their father across the shore of ignorance. *(Source: Praśna Up. 6.6–6.8)*

## 3. Reference — the Six Praśnas at a glance

| # | Questioner | Topic | Core answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kabandhī Kātyāyana | origin of creatures | Prajāpati produces the pair **rayi (matter) + prāṇa (life)** |
| 2 | Bhārgava Vaidarbhi | the powers; what is supreme | **prāṇa is chief** — the bee-king parable |
| 3 | Kausalya Āśvalāyana | source & movement of prāṇa | prāṇa from **Ātman**; its **five-fold** division; **nāḍīs**; **udāna** at death |
| 4 | Sauryāyaṇī Gārgya | sleep, dream, deep sleep | senses rest in mind; all merge in the **imperishable Ātman** |
| 5 | Śaibya Satyakāma | meditation on **Oṃ** | one / two / three **mātrās** → world of men / moon / Brahma-loka |
| 6 | Sukeśā Bhāradvāja | the **ṣoḍaśa-kala puruṣa** | sixteen **kalās** arise from and dissolve in the partless Person |

## 4. Significance & Cross-Links

- **Atharvan prose–verse pair** — Śaṅkara's framing makes the Praśna the **discursive complement of the Muṇḍaka**: the questions of creation, prāṇa, Oṃ and the imperishable Person elaborate in prose what the Muṇḍaka delivers in metre. See [`mundaka-upanishad.md`](./mundaka-upanishad.md). *(Source: Śaṅkara's bhāṣya)*
- **Prāṇa-vidyā** — Praśnas 2–3 are a primary scriptural locus for the **doctrine of the five prāṇas** (prāṇa, apāna, samāna, vyāna, udāna) and the **nāḍī** physiology later central to yoga and Āyurveda. [scholarly]
- **Oṃ / praṇava theology** — Praśna 5's graded meditation on the one/two/three mātrās is the closest sibling of the **Māṇḍūkya's** AUM analysis; both are Atharvan. See [`mandukya-upanishad.md`](./mandukya-upanishad.md) and [`principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).
- **Roster note** — the catechism's six named seekers and the prince **Hiraṇyanābha Kausalya** are frame-figures internal to the text; they are not independently developed elsewhere in the principal corpus. [late text]
