# Kaṭha (Kāṭhaka) Upaniṣad

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/upanishads/`](./README.md) — a per-text deep-dive into the **Kaṭha (Kāṭhaka) Upaniṣad**, a *mukhya* Upaniṣad of the **Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda** (Kaṭha śākhā) and one of the most celebrated and poetic of all the Upaniṣads: the dialogue in which the boy **Naciketas** wins teaching from **Yama (Death)** himself — source of the **chariot allegory**, the **razor's-edge** image, the **thumb-sized Person in the heart**, and a body of verses shared almost verbatim with the **Bhagavad-gītā**. For the parent Veda see [`../vedas/yajurveda.md`](../vedas/yajurveda.md); for the cross-text *mukhya* overview see [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md); for the schools of interpretation it seeds see [`../darshanas/README.md`](../darshanas/README.md). Master index: [`../../README.md`](../../README.md).

## 1. Identity, Placement, Dating

- **Parent Veda & school** — the Kaṭha belongs to the **Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda**, attached to the **Kaṭha (Cāraka-Kaṭha) śākhā**; the alternate name **Kāṭhaka** points to that recension. *(Source: Kaṭha Up.; Kaṭha śākhā)* — see [`../vedas/yajurveda.md`](../vedas/yajurveda.md).
- **The two names** — *Kaṭha* / *Kāṭhaka* derive from the **Kaṭha school**, not from an incipit; it is also styled the **Kāṭhakopaniṣad**. *(Source: Kaṭha Up.)*
- **Structure** — **two adhyāyas of three vallīs each** (six vallīs / "creepers" total), wholly **metrical** verse with a brief prose frame; the older first adhyāya carries the Naciketas–Yama dialogue, the second develops the yoga and Self-doctrine. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.1–III, II.1–III)*
- **Root in the Black Yajurveda** — the Naciketas legend is itself **older than the Upaniṣad**, appearing in the **Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa (3.11.8)** as the *Naciketas / Nāciketa fire* story; the Upaniṣad reworks that Brāhmaṇa seed into a Vedānta dialogue. *(Source: Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa 3.11.8; Kaṭha Up.)* [scholarly]
- **Canonical placement** — counted among the **principal (mukhya) Upaniṣads** and listed **third** in the standard Daśopaniṣad sequence (*Īśa–Kena–Kaṭha–Praśna–Muṇḍaka…*). *(Source: Daśopaniṣad mnemonic; Muktikā Up.)* — catalogued in [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).
- **Śaṅkara's bhāṣya** — **Ādi Śaṅkara** (c. 8th c. CE) wrote a full **Kāṭhaka-bhāṣya**, reading it as a core Advaita proof-text; it is among the ten Upaniṣads on which his commentary survives. *(Source: Śaṅkara, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya)* [scholarly]
- **Dating (two clocks)** —
  - *Academic:* a **middle-period verse Upaniṣad**, broadly **5th–4th c. BCE** or a little later, composed after the great prose Upaniṣads; its proto-Sāṃkhya/yoga vocabulary marks it as relatively developed. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (orthodox / traditional):* eternal *apauruṣeya* Śruti "seen" by the ṛṣis, not authored in any century, fixed in transmission at the **Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; Kali-Yuga onset constant)** by Vyāsa's redaction. Both clocks are recorded; neither is corrected. [scripture vs. academic dating]

## 2. The Frame Narrative — Naciketas Sent to Death (I.1)

- **Vājaśravasa's flawed sacrifice** — the father, **Vājaśravasa (Uddālaka Āruṇi's line / "son of Vājaśravas")**, performs the **Viśvajit** sacrifice, in which all one's possessions are given away — but gives only **old, barren, used-up cows** "that have drunk their last water," a defective dāna. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.1.1–3)*
- **Naciketas' challenge** — the young son **Naciketas**, watching, is seized by *śraddhā* (faith) and, knowing the gift is worthless, asks his father three times: **"To whom will you give me?"** *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.1.4)*
- **The angry vow** — provoked, the father snaps: **"Unto Death (Yama / Mṛtyu) I give you!"** Naciketas, taking the careless word as binding, resolves to go. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.1.4)*
- **The grass that withers** — Naciketas reflects on mortality in the famous line: **"Like corn a man ripens, like corn he is born again"** (*sasyam iva martyaḥ pacyate, sasyam iva ājāyate punaḥ*) — and departs willingly for Yama's house. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.1.6)*
- **Three nights unwelcomed** — Naciketas waits **three nights at Yama's abode** while Death is absent; an unhonoured Brahmin guest "burns up" a host's merit. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.1.7–9)*

## 3. The Three Boons (I.1)

- **Yama's amends** — returning, Yama grants Naciketas **three boons (vara)** to atone for the three unhosted nights. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.1.9)*
- **First boon — reconciliation** — that his **father's anger be appeased** and that he return home recognised and at peace. Yama grants it at once. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.1.10–11)*
- **Second boon — the Naciketas-fire** — knowledge of the **fire-sacrifice (Nāciketa agni) that leads to heaven** and conquers death-by-hunger; Yama teaches the altar's bricks and lays-up, and decrees the **fire shall bear Naciketas' own name**. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.1.12–19)* — the ritual-knowledge boon, the bridge from the Brāhmaṇa legend.
- **Third boon — the great doubt** — Naciketas asks **the secret of death**: *"When a man dies, this doubt remains — some say 'he is,' some say 'he is not'; teach me this."* *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.1.20)* — the pivotal question that opens the metaphysics.

---

## 4. Yama's Test — The Pleasant vs. the Good (I.1–I.2)

- **The temptation of wealth** — Yama tries to **deflect** the third boon: he offers instead **sons and grandsons of a hundred years, herds, horses, elephants, gold, vast lands, long life, and celestial maidens (apsarases) with chariots and music** — "ask anything but this." *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.1.21–25)*
- **Naciketas refuses** — the boy answers that **all such pleasures are fleeting** (*śvo-bhāvā*, "lasting but till tomorrow") and wear out the senses; "no man is made content by wealth." He holds Yama to the question of the Self. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.1.26–29)*
- **The two paths — *preyas* and *śreyas*** — Yama, satisfied by the test, declares the doctrine of the **Good (śreyas) versus the Pleasant (preyas)**: both present themselves to a man; **the wise choose the Good, fools choose the Pleasant** and miss the goal. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.2.1–2)* [the core ethical fork]
- **Praise of the seeker** — Yama lauds Naciketas as one who has **rejected the cord of desire** and is fit for the supreme teaching, distinguishing the *vidyā* (knowledge) and *avidyā* (ignorance) paths "wide apart, leading to opposite ends." *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.2.3–6)*
- **The teaching is subtle** — the Self is **"not to be gained by reasoning"**; it is taught by a knower and grasped by the **grace/choosing**: *"This Self cannot be attained by instruction, nor by intellect, nor by much learning; he whom the Self chooses, by him is it gained"* (*yam evaiṣa vṛṇute tena labhyaḥ*). *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.2.23 = Muṇḍaka 3.2.3)* [shared verse]

## 5. The Great Images of the Self (I.2–I.3, II.1–II.3)

- **Oṃ as the supreme support** — Yama names the **syllable Oṃ (akṣaram)** as the imperishable Brahman, "the highest support; knowing it, whatever one desires is his." *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.2.15–17)*
- **The unborn slayer-and-slain verse** — *"na jāyate mriyate vā vipaścit… na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre"* — **the wise Self is never born and never dies; though the body is slain, It is not slain.** *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.2.18–19)* — taken up almost word-for-word at **Bhagavad-gītā 2.19–20**. [proto-Gītā]
- **The thumb-sized Person (aṅguṣṭha-mātra puruṣa)** — **"the Person of the size of a thumb dwells in the heart (madhya ātmani)"**, the lord of past and future, like a smokeless flame; knowing Him, one fears no more. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.2.12; II.1.12–13; II.3.17)* [the heart-Self image]
- **The chariot allegory (ratha-kalpanā)** — the masterpiece simile of I.3:
  - **the Self (ātman) is the rider / lord of the chariot;**
  - **the body is the chariot;**
  - **the intellect (buddhi) is the charioteer (sārathi);**
  - **the mind (manas) is the reins;**
  - **the senses (indriyas) are the horses;**
  - **the sense-objects (viṣayas) are the roads they range over.** *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.3.3–4)* — when the charioteer-intellect is undiscerning and the mind-reins loose, the sense-horses run wild; when the intellect is wise and the mind controlled, the senses are like good horses and the rider reaches "the end of the road, the supreme abode of Viṣṇu." *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.3.5–9)* [the canonical psychology-of-the-Self image]
- **The ladder of subtlety** — above the senses are the objects, above them the mind, above mind the intellect (buddhi), above that the **Great Self (mahān ātmā)**, above that the **Unmanifest (avyakta)**, and above all the **Puruṣa**, beyond which there is nothing — the goal. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.3.10–11)* [proto-Sāṃkhya hierarchy] — see the schools that build on this in [`../darshanas/README.md`](../darshanas/README.md).
- **The razor's edge (kṣurasya dhārā)** — **"Arise! awake! having obtained your boons, understand them; the sharp edge of a razor is hard to cross — thus the wise say the path is hard"** (*kṣurasya dhārā niśitā duratyayā, durgaṃ pathas tat kavayo vadanti*). *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.3.14)* — the most-quoted line of the text: the spiritual path as a razor's edge.
- **The cosmic tree, root-up** — the world is an **eternal aśvattha (fig) tree with its root above and branches below** (*ūrdhva-mūlo'vāk-śākhaḥ*), whose root is the pure Brahman; That is the deathless. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. II.3.1)* — reworked at **Bhagavad-gītā 15.1**. [proto-Gītā]
- **The light by which all shines** — **"There the sun shines not, nor moon and stars, nor lightning, much less this fire; when That shines, everything shines after It; by Its light all this is lit"** (*tam eva bhāntam anubhāti sarvam*). *(Source: Kaṭha Up. II.2.15)* — echoed at Muṇḍaka 2.2.10, Śvetāśvatara 6.14, and **Bhagavad-gītā 15.6**. [shared verse]
- **The two in the heart-cave** — *"two enter the cave (guhā) and dwell in the highest place — the knowers call them light and shade (chāyā-tapau)"*: the individual self and the supreme Self seated together, image of the *jīva* and the *paramātman*. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.3.1)*
- **The city of eleven gates** — the body as the **"city of eleven gates" (pura-ekādaśa-dvāram)** of the unborn, untroubled Self; controlling it, one grieves no more. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. II.2.1)* — re-echoed at Bhagavad-gītā 5.13 ("nine-gated city"). [proto-Gītā]
- **The withdrawn senses = yoga** — the closing teaching: **"when the five senses together with the mind cease, and the intellect stirs not — that they call the highest state (paramā gati); this firm holding-back of the senses they call yoga (yogam)."** *(Source: Kaṭha Up. II.3.10–11)* — one of the earliest scriptural definitions of **yoga as sense-restraint**.

## 6. The Proto-Gītā Verses & Reception

- **Verses shared with the Bhagavad-gītā** — the Kaṭha is the Upaniṣad most densely echoed in the Gītā, supplying or paralleling:
  - *Variant (Kaṭha I.2.18–19 ≈ Gītā 2.19–20):* the **un-slain, unborn Self** "is not slain when the body is slain."
  - *Variant (Kaṭha II.3.1 ≈ Gītā 15.1–3):* the **inverted aśvattha tree**, root-above.
  - *Variant (Kaṭha II.2.15 ≈ Gītā 15.6):* the **self-luminous abode** "where sun, moon and fire shine not."
  - *Variant (Kaṭha II.2.1 ≈ Gītā 5.13):* the **body as a multi-gated city** of the indwelling Self. *(Source: Kaṭha Up.; Bhagavad-gītā)* [proto-Gītā parallels — never merged: scholars dispute direction of borrowing]
- **Shared with other Upaniṣads** — the *"whom the Self chooses"* verse (I.2.23) is identical to **Muṇḍaka 3.2.3**; the self-luminous verse recurs in **Śvetāśvatara** — marking a common stock of late-Vedic Self-poetry. *(Source: Muṇḍaka Up.; Śvetāśvatara Up.)*
- **Commentarial tradition** — beyond Śaṅkara's bhāṣya, the Kaṭha carries sub-commentaries (Ānandagiri's ṭīkā) and is read across **Advaita, Viśiṣṭādvaita and Dvaita** Vedānta as a *prasthāna*-supporting Śruti; rendered in **Hume's *Thirteen Principal Upanishads*** and **Radhakrishnan's *Principal Upaniṣads***. *(Source: Śaṅkara; principal set)* — see [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).

## 7. Why the Kaṭha Matters

- **The death-dialogue charter** — it is the great Upaniṣadic treatment of **what survives death**, answering Naciketas' "is he, or is he not?" with the doctrine of the deathless Self. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.1.20–29)*
- **The most-quoted images in Hindu thought** — the **chariot of body-and-soul**, the **razor's-edge path**, the **thumb-sized Person in the heart**, and "arise, awake" are among the most widely cited lines in the whole corpus. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.3)*
- **A bridge to Sāṃkhya-Yoga and the Gītā** — its ladder (senses–mind–buddhi–mahat–avyakta–puruṣa) and its sense-restraint definition of yoga feed directly into the **darśanas** and the **Bhagavad-gītā**. *(Source: Kaṭha Up. I.3, II.3)* — see [`../darshanas/README.md`](../darshanas/README.md).
- **Two clocks held open** — the **academic mid-millennium-BCE** date and the **eternal-Śruti / Vyāsa-redaction** frame are both recorded here without reconciliation. [scripture vs. academic dating]
