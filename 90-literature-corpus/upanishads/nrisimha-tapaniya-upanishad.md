# Nṛsiṃha-tāpanīya Upaniṣad (Pūrva & Uttara Tāpinī)

## 1. Identity, Placement, Dating

- **Parent Veda & class** — the Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī is a **sectarian (Vaiṣṇava) Upaniṣad of the Atharvaveda**, the senior member of the **tāpanīya / tāpinī** ("of the heating, of ascetic discipline") sub-class, paired in name and form with its junior the **Gopāla-tāpanī**. It is **not** one of the older *mukhya* (principal) Upaniṣads but a later **devotional / mantra-vidyā Upaniṣad**. *(Source: Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī Up.; Muktikā tradition)* — see [`../vedas/atharvaveda.md`](../vedas/atharvaveda.md).
- **Muktikā placement** — counted among the **108 Upaniṣads of the Muktikā** canon and grouped with the **Vaiṣṇava Upaniṣads** (alongside Gopāla-tāpanī, Rāma-tāpanī, Vāsudeva, Kṛṣṇa, Gopīcandana, etc.). *(Source: Muktikā Upaniṣad; Vaiṣṇava-Upaniṣad group)*
- **Structure** — two texts under one title: the **Nṛsiṃha-pūrva-tāpanīya** (former) in **five sections (upaniṣads / khaṇḍas)** and the **Nṛsiṃha-uttara-tāpanīya** (latter) in **nine sections**. The **Pūrva** expounds the **Nṛsiṃha-anuṣṭubh (Mantrarāja), the Sāvitrī, the Yajus**, and the protective **Nṛsiṃha-gāyatrī**; the **Uttara** is an **Oṃkāra / praṇava and turīya** treatise reading the syllable **AUM** as Narasiṃha-Brahman. *(Source: Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī Up., Pūrva & Uttara)*
- **Dating (two clocks)** —
  - *Academic:* a **late, sectarian Upaniṣad**; the Uttara especially is often judged **later than the Pūrva** and is sometimes placed among the **later compositions of the Atharva tāpanī corpus** (well after the *mukhya* Upaniṣads, as the Narasiṃha cult matured), though precise dates are uncertain. [scholarly] [late text]
  - *Variant (orthodox / traditional):* eternal *apauruṣeya* Śruti of the Atharvaveda, "seen" by ṛṣis and not "authored" in a datable stratum; the Narasiṃha it celebrates is the **man-lion avatāra of the Satya / Kṛta age** who slew Hiraṇyakaśipu — set on the cosmic clock long before the **Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; Kali-Yuga onset)**. Both frames are recorded here; neither is corrected. [scripture vs. academic dating]
- **Commentarial weight** — the text carries unusual prestige for a sectarian Upaniṣad: a **commentary in the Śaṅkara tradition** (ascribed to Śaṅkara / to **Gauḍapāda** by some) circulates, and **Vidyāraṇya / Sāyaṇa-school** glosses treat the Uttara's Oṃkāra-turīya doctrine as Advaita-compatible. *(Source: Advaita commentarial tradition; ascriptions traditional)* [disputed authorship of the bhāṣya]

## 2. Pūrva-tāpanīya — The Mantrarāja & the Birth of Narasiṃha

- **The Mantrarāja (Nṛsiṃha-anuṣṭubh) revealed** — the Pūrva's heart is the **Nṛsiṃha-anuṣṭubh mantra**, called the **"king of mantras" (mantrarāja)** — an anuṣṭubh verse of thirty-two syllables (*ugraṃ vīraṃ mahā-viṣṇuṃ jvalantaṃ sarvato-mukham / nṛsiṃhaṃ bhīṣaṇaṃ bhadraṃ mṛtyu-mṛtyuṃ namāmy aham*), invoking the **fierce, valiant, all-facing, blazing Man-Lion as the "death of death."** *(Source: Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī Up., Pūrva)* — the principal **Śruti basis of the Mantrarāja**. [foundational mantra-text]
- **Origin myth of the mantra** — the frame narrates that, after Narasiṃha tore apart **Hiraṇyakaśipu**, the gods and ṛṣis, terrified by his blazing wrath, composed and recited this **anuṣṭubh hymn** to pacify and praise him; the mantra is thus born from the avatāra-event itself. *(Source: Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī Up., Pūrva)* — links the text to the **Narasiṃha–Prahlāda–Hiraṇyakaśipu** narrative of the Purāṇas.
- **The Sāvitrī of Narasiṃha** — the Pūrva supplies a **Narasiṃha-Sāvitrī / gāyatrī** form (*"vajra-nakhāya vidmahe tīkṣṇa-daṃṣṭrāya dhīmahi, tan no nārasiṃhaḥ pracodayāt"* in received usage), placing Man-Lion meditation in the prestige register of the Vedic Gāyatrī. *(Source: Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī Up., Pūrva; sampradāya usage)* [as received in practice]
- **The Yajus and the Aṅga-mantras** — the text gives the accompanying **Yajus** formula and the **six aṅga-mantras (ṣaḍaṅga-nyāsa)** by which the worshipper installs the syllables of the Mantrarāja on the limbs of the body, a fully **tāntric nyāsa** apparatus. *(Source: Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī Up., Pūrva)* [mantra-vidyā / nyāsa]
- **The Sudarśana / cakra connection** — the Pūrva associates the Mantrarāja with the **Sudarśana-cakra** and a protective **maṇḍala (the Sudarśana-yantra / cakra-rāja)**, making Narasiṃha-with-the-disc the focus of an **apotropaic "armour" (kavaca)** worship. *(Source: Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī Up., Pūrva)* — root of the later **Sudarśana-Narasiṃha cult**. [foundational for the Sudarśana cult]
- **Phala-śruti of the Pūrva** — one who knows and recites the Mantrarāja is promised **protection from fear, demons, untimely death, and saṃsāra**, and is purified "as by all the Vedic sacrifices." *(Source: Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī Up., Pūrva)*

## 3. Uttara-tāpanīya — Oṃkāra, Turīya & Narasiṃha-Brahman

- **AUM as Narasiṃha-Brahman** — the Uttara is built around the **praṇava (Oṃ)**: it analyses **A-U-M and the fourth, soundless mātrā (the ardha-mātrā / turīya)** and identifies the whole syllable with **Narasiṃha as the supreme Self (Ātman = Brahman)**. *(Source: Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī Up., Uttara)* — fuses the **Māṇḍūkya-style AUM analysis** with Vaiṣṇava theism; compare [`mandukya-upanishad.md`](./mandukya-upanishad.md).
- **The four states of consciousness** — following the Māṇḍūkya pattern, the four mātrās are mapped to **jāgrat (waking / Viśva), svapna (dream / Taijasa), suṣupti (deep sleep / Prājña), and turīya (the fourth, pure non-dual awareness)** — turīya being **Narasiṃha** himself, the witness beyond the three. *(Source: Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī Up., Uttara)* [Advaita-compatible reading]
- **Identity of Mantrarāja and Praṇava** — the Uttara teaches that the **Mantrarāja of the Pūrva and the Oṃkāra of the Uttara are one**: meditation on the man-lion mantra is, esoterically, meditation on **AUM as Brahman**, uniting mantra-vidyā with Vedānta. *(Source: Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī Up., Uttara)* — the structural hinge between the two halves.
- **The cosmic Person** — Narasiṃha-Brahman is declared the **inner controller (antaryāmin)** and the **source and dissolution of all worlds**; the gods, the Vedas, and the cosmos arise from and rest in him. *(Source: Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī Up., Uttara)*
- **Phala-śruti of the Uttara** — knowing this Oṃkāra-Narasiṃha grants **liberation (mokṣa)**: the knower "crosses death," attains the **turīya**, and is freed from rebirth. *(Source: Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī Up., Uttara)*

## 4. Theology, Cult & Sampradāya Reception

- **Vaiṣṇava counterpart to the Śaiva turn** — the Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī performs for **Narasiṃha** what the Śvetāśvatara does for **Rudra-Śiva** and the Gopāla-tāpanī for **Kṛṣṇa**: it takes the impersonal Brahman of the older Upaniṣads and **identifies it with a named, fierce, worshipped avatāra**. Compare [`svetashvatara-upanishad.md`](./svetashvatara-upanishad.md) and [`gopala-tapaniya-upanishad.md`](./gopala-tapaniya-upanishad.md). [paired theistic Upaniṣads]
- **Charter of Narasiṃha worship** — it is the **principal Śruti warrant** for the cult of Narasiṃha across South India (Ahobilam, Sholingur, Mangalagiri, Simhachalam) and for **Narasiṃha-mantra dīkṣā** in the **Śrī Vaiṣṇava** and broader Pāñcarātra-influenced traditions. *(Source: Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī Up.; sampradāya initiation practice)* [living ritual use]
- **The Sudarśana cult** — through the Pūrva's cakra-association, the Upaniṣad underwrites the worship of **Sudarśana-Narasiṃha** and the protective **Narasiṃha-kavaca / Sudarśana-yantra** widely used against affliction and black magic. *(Source: Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī Up., Pūrva; tāntric kavaca tradition)* [foundational for the Sudarśana cult]
- **Advaita reception** — uniquely among sectarian Upaniṣads, its **Oṃkāra-turīya doctrine** drew a **bhāṣya in the Śaṅkara tradition**, so the text sits at the **meeting of tāntric mantra-worship and Advaita Vedānta** — read by Vaiṣṇavas as devotion to Narasiṃha and by Advaitins as a praṇava-vidyā. *(Source: Advaita commentarial tradition)* — see [`principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).
- **Two clocks, restated** — academically a **late sectarian Śruti** presupposing a developed Narasiṃha cult; traditionally **timeless Atharvavedic revelation** of the Man-Lion who descended in the **Satya age** to save Prahlāda. Both are kept side by side. [scripture vs. academic dating]
