# The Vaiṣṇava, Śaiva, and Śākta (Sectarian) Upaniṣads

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/upanishads/`](./README.md) — a cross-text overview/catalogue of the three largest **sectarian (sampradāya) sub-groups** of the **Muktikā** canon: the **~14 Vaiṣṇava**, **~14–15 Śaiva**, and **~8 Śākta Upaniṣads**. These are the minor (*gauṇa*) Śruti texts that re-read the impersonal **Brahman** of the older Upaniṣads as a **named sectarian deity** — Viṣṇu/Rāma/Kṛṣṇa/Nṛsiṃha, Śiva/Rudra/Gaṇapati, or the Goddess (Devī/Tripurā/Lakṣmī) — and so form the **Śruti charter of theistic and Tantric (Śrīvidyā) devotion**. Frame-files: [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md) (the *mukhya* core they grow out of); [`../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) (the Vedas, the four strata, the Muktikā 108-list, the cosmic clock); [`../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md`](../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md) (the Āgama/Tantra stream they feed). Practice-cousins: [`./yoga-upanishads-overview.md`](./yoga-upanishads-overview.md), [`./sannyasa-upanishads-overview.md`](./sannyasa-upanishads-overview.md). Master index: [`../../README.md`](../../README.md).

## 1. What the Sectarian Upaniṣads Are

- **Three deity-defined clusters of the 108.** Of the **108 Upaniṣads** listed in the **Muktikā Upaniṣad's** closing enumeration, modern editors group a large block by **deity / sampradāya** rather than by parent Veda — the **Vaiṣṇava**, **Śaiva**, and **Śākta** Upaniṣads. *(Source: Muktikā Up.; Adyar Library sectarian editions)*
- **The Adyar groupings.** The standard reference editions are the **Adyar Library's *Vaiṣṇava Upaniṣads*, *Śaiva Upaniṣads*, and *Śākta Upaniṣads***, each with the gloss of **Upaniṣad-brahmayogin** (Upaniṣad-brahma-yogin, the Kāñcī commentator who alone glossed all 108). These editions fix the canonical membership and content of each group. *(Source: Adyar editions, ed. C. Kunhan Raja / A. Mahadeva Sastri)* [scholarly]
- **The shared "deity-as-Brahman" move.** Every text in all three groups performs the same operation: it **identifies its chosen deity with the absolute Brahman** of the *mukhya* Upaniṣads — *"this Viṣṇu/Rudra/Devī is the One without a second, the ātman, Oṃ."* The metaphysics is borrowed Vedānta; the **personalisation** is the sectarian contribution. [the unifying thesis]
- **Counts and assignments differ by edition.** The exact membership is **not fixed**: editors disagree on which borderline texts (e.g. *Mahānārāyaṇa*, *Kaivalya*, *Dattātreya*) count as "sectarian," and some texts are claimed by two groups. Treat the numbers (~14 / ~14–15 / ~8) as **conventional, not canonical**. [count varies by edition] [disputed]
- **Mostly late and Atharvanic.** None belongs to the ten/twelve *mukhya* set; they are **minor, post-classical** texts, the bulk attached (in their colophons) to the **Atharvaveda** or the **Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda**, several composed (academically) in the **early-to-late medieval centuries**, the most Tantric of them late. [late text] [scholarly]
- **The "two clocks."** *Academic:* a **late layer** of the canon, post-dating the rise of Pāñcarātra/Bhāgavata, Pāśupata/Śaiva-siddhānta, and Śrīvidyā Tantra — most placed **c. 1st millennium CE into the 2nd**. [scholarly] *Variant (orthodox / traditional):* eternal *apauruṣeya* Śruti "seen" by ṛṣis and fixed at the **Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; see Kali-Yuga onset constant)**, not "authored" in a CE stratum. Both frames are recorded; neither is corrected. [scripture vs. academic dating]

## 2. The Vaiṣṇava Upaniṣads (~14)

The Viṣṇu-centred group, dominated by the **tāpanī ("heating / ascetic") genre** — mantra-expositions in which the deity's name-mantra is the path to liberation.

- **Rāma-tāpanīya (Rāma-pūrva- & Rāma-uttara-tāpanī)** — Atharvaveda. Exalts **Rāma as para-Brahman**; expounds the **ṣaḍakṣara Rāma-mantra ("rāṃ rāmāya namaḥ")**, the *tāraka* "Rāma" name said at death at Kāśī, and a **Rāma-yantra**; the chief Rāma-bhakti Śruti text. *(Source: Rāmatāpanī Up.)*
- **Rāma-rahasya** — Atharvaveda. The **"secret of Rāma"** — Hanumān expounds the Rāma mantras, *bīja*s and worship to the sages; a Tantric Rāma-vidyā text. *(Source: Rāmarahasya Up.)* [late text]
- **Nṛsiṃha-tāpanīya (pūrva- & uttara-)** — Atharvaveda. The major Vaiṣṇava-tāpanī: **Narasiṃha as Brahman** via the **Nṛsiṃha-anuṣṭubh mantra** and the **Sudarśana-cakra**; the *uttara* part is strongly **Advaitic (Oṃ / praṇava)**. See [`./nrisimha-tapaniya-upanishad.md`](./nrisimha-tapaniya-upanishad.md). *(Source: Nṛsiṃhatāpanī Up.)*
- **Gopāla-tāpanī (pūrva- & uttara-)** — Atharvaveda. **Kṛṣṇa as Gopāla = Brahman**; the **eighteen-syllable Gopāla mantra** and the Kṛṣṇa of Vṛndāvana as the supreme; a foundation text for **Gauḍīya/Caitanya** theology. See [`./gopala-tapaniya-upanishad.md`](./gopala-tapaniya-upanishad.md). *(Source: Gopālatāpanī Up.)*
- **Kṛṣṇa Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. Identifies **Kṛṣṇa with the supreme Self**, allegorising his attributes and companions (the *gopī*s as Vedic mantras, etc.). *(Source: Kṛṣṇa Up.)*
- **Vāsudeva Upaniṣad** — Sāmaveda (by attribution). On the **ūrdhva-puṇḍra (Vaiṣṇava forehead-mark)**, the **gopī-candana**, and the **twelve-syllable Vāsudeva mantra (Oṃ namo bhagavate vāsudevāya)**; a manual of Vaiṣṇava marking and mantra. *(Source: Vāsudeva Up.)*
- **Garuḍa Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. A **Garuḍa-vidyā** against snake-venom — mantras and charms invoking **Garuḍa** as poison-remover; an apotropaic/Tantric text. *(Source: Garuḍa Up.)* [regional]
- **Hayagrīva Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. Brahmā teaches Nārada the **Hayagrīva mantras** that confer **all knowledge and Brahman-realisation** — the horse-necked Viṣṇu as lord of learning. *(Source: Hayagrīva Up.)*
- **Dattātreya Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. **Dattātreya** (the Trimūrti-as-one avatāra) as Brahman; gives his **mantras and the divine names**, with both Tantric and Advaitic colouring. *(Source: Dattātreya Up.)* [late text]
- **Tārasāra Upaniṣad** — Śukla-Yajurveda. The **"essence of the tāraka"** — the **Oṃ Nārāyaṇa / tāraka-Brahman** name that ferries (*tārayati*) across saṃsāra; identifies the syllables with Viṣṇu's forms. *(Source: Tārasāra Up.)*
- **Nārāyaṇa Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. Declares **Nārāyaṇa the all**, the source of the gods and worlds, and gives the **aṣṭākṣara ("Oṃ namo nārāyaṇāya")** as the supreme mantra. *(Source: Nārāyaṇa Up.)*
- **Avyakta Upaniṣad** — Sāmaveda. On the **unmanifest (avyakta)** as Viṣṇu/Nārāyaṇa, the cosmogony of the manifest from the unmanifest. *(Source: Avyakta Up.)* [late text]
- **Tripādvibhūti-mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. A long, late text on **Nārāyaṇa's "three-quarter glory" (tripād-vibhūti)** — the transcendent Vaikuṇṭha realm beyond the manifest fourth; not the same as the Yajurvedic *Mahānārāyaṇa* (see [`./mahanarayana-upanishad.md`](./mahanarayana-upanishad.md)). *(Source: Tripādvibhūti-mahānārāyaṇa Up.)* [late text] [disputed/confused naming]
- **Kalisantaraṇa (Kali-saṃtaraṇa / "Hare Kṛṣṇa") Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. The famous short text giving the **sixteen-name Hare-Rāma–Hare-Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra** as the means of **crossing the Kali age**; charter of the *nāma-saṃkīrtana* movements. See [`./kalisantarana-upanishad.md`](./kalisantarana-upanishad.md). *(Source: Kalisantaraṇa Up.)*

## 3. The Śaiva Upaniṣads (~14–15)

The Śiva/Rudra-centred group — the **Śruti backing of Śaiva theology, the Pañcākṣara, Bhasma/Rudrākṣa lore, and the Pāśupata/Vīraśaiva** streams.

- **Atharvaśiras Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. An early, influential Śaiva text: **Rudra is the sole reality**, the gods declare themselves not-other-than-him; the **Paśupata-vrata** of ash, and **Oṃ as Rudra**. *(Source: Atharvaśiras Up.)*
- **Atharvaśikhā Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. On the **praṇava (Oṃ)** with its four *mātrā*s identified with the four faces and forms of Śiva; meditation on Oṃ as Śiva. *(Source: Atharvaśikhā Up.)*
- **Kaivalya Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. A celebrated, much-quoted text: **liberation (kaivalya)** through meditation on **Śiva/Maheśvara as the ātman** — *"I am Śiva, the Brahman,"* the Self that is past/present/future. See [`./kaivalya-upanishad.md`](./kaivalya-upanishad.md). *(Source: Kaivalya Up.)*
- **Kālāgnirudra Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. **Kālāgni-Rudra** teaches the **tripuṇḍra** — the three horizontal ash-lines of the Śaiva forehead-mark, their materials, mantras and meaning. *(Source: Kālāgnirudra Up.)*
- **Akṣamālikā Upaniṣad** — Ṛgveda. On the **rosary (akṣa-mālā / japa-mālā)** — the symbolism of its beads, the syllables of the alphabet on them, and the rules of *japa*. *(Source: Akṣamālikā Up.)*
- **Rudrahṛdaya Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. The **"heart of Rudra"** — affirms the **non-difference of Śiva and Viṣṇu (Hari-Hara)** and of Śiva and the Goddess; Rudra as the heart of all. *(Source: Rudrahṛdaya Up.)*
- **Bṛhajjābāla Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. A long text on the **sacred ash (bhasma / vibhūti)** and **rudrākṣa** — their preparation, application, and power; Kālāgni-Rudra to Bhusuṇḍa. *(Source: Bṛhajjābāla Up.)* [late text]
- **Bhasmajābāla Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. On the **holy ash (bhasma)** — its making from sacred fire, the **tripuṇḍra**, and Śiva-worship; identifies the ash-marked devotee with Śiva. *(Source: Bhasmajābāla Up.)*
- **Rudrākṣajābāla Upaniṣad** — Sāmaveda. On the **rudrākṣa bead** — its origin from Śiva's tear-drops, the merit of wearing it, and the faces (*mukha*) of the beads. *(Source: Rudrākṣajābāla Up.)* [folk etiology]
- **Gaṇapati Upaniṣad (Gaṇapati-Atharvaśīrṣa)** — Atharvaveda. The principal **Gāṇapatya** text: **Gaṇeśa is Brahman**, the **gaṇa-bīja "gaṃ"**, and the Gaṇapati-Gāyatrī — *"tvam eva sākṣād brahmāsi."* *(Source: Gaṇapati-Atharvaśīrṣa Up.)* [sectarian core]
- **Pañcabrahma Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. On the **five faces / five Brahmans of Śiva** — Sadyojāta, Vāmadeva, Aghora, Tatpuruṣa, Īśāna — the **Sadāśiva** mantra-cosmology central to **Śaiva Siddhānta** and Āgama. *(Source: Pañcabrahma Up.)*
- **Śarabha Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. **Śiva as Śarabha** (the lion-bird hybrid) who subdues **Narasiṃha** — a Śaiva polemical assertion of Śiva's supremacy over Viṣṇu's Man-Lion. *(Source: Śarabha Up.)* [sectarian polemic]
- **Jābāli (Jābāla) Upaniṣad** — Sāmaveda. A **Pāśupata** text: **Paśupati, paśu (the bound soul) and pāśa (the bond)**, and the **tripuṇḍra/bhasma**; distinguish from the Sannyāsa *Jābāla*. *(Source: Jābāli Up.)* [disputed/confused naming]
- **Dakṣiṇāmūrti Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. On **Śiva as Dakṣiṇāmūrti**, the silent south-facing teacher of *jñāna*; gives his **mantra and dhyāna**, the guru-as-Śiva ideal. *(Source: Dakṣiṇāmūrti Up.)*

## 4. The Śākta Upaniṣads (~8)

The Goddess-centred group — the **Śruti charter of Śrīvidyā / Śāktism**, built around the **Devī as the supreme Brahman** and the **Śrīcakra / Śrīvidyā (Tripurā) mantra**.

- **Devī Upaniṣad (Devī-Atharvaśīrṣa)** — Atharvaveda. The flagship: the gods ask **"Who art thou, great Goddess?"** and **Devī answers "I am Brahman"** (echoing the *Devī-sūkta*); gives the **navākṣarī / Devī mantra** and Goddess-Gāyatrī. *(Source: Devī-Atharvaśīrṣa Up.)* [sectarian core]
- **Tripurā Upaniṣad** — Ṛgveda. A concise, weighty **Śrīvidyā** text on **Tripurā / Tripurasundarī**, the **three cities/states**, the **kāmakalā** and the **Śrīcakra**; with a famed commentary by **Bhāskararāya**. *(Source: Tripurā Up.)*
- **Tripurātāpinī (Tripurā-tāpinī) Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. The **tāpinī** expansion of Tripurā-vidyā: the **fifteen-syllable Śrīvidyā mantra (pañcadaśī)** dissected, the Śrīcakra, and the Goddess as Brahman. *(Source: Tripurātāpinī Up.)* [late text]
- **Bhāvanā Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. The **"contemplation" text** — interiorises the **Śrīcakra onto the practitioner's own body** (the *yoginī*s, *cakra*s, and the guru-lineage as inner realities); a core meditative Śrīvidyā manual. *(Source: Bhāvanā Up.)*
- **Saubhāgya-lakṣmī Upaniṣad** — Ṛgveda. On **Lakṣmī as the Goddess of auspicious fortune (saubhāgya)**; her mantras, a **ṣaṭ-cakra kuṇḍalinī-yoga**, and meditation uniting **Śrī-yoga and Vedānta**. *(Source: Saubhāgyalakṣmī Up.)*
- **Sarasvatī-rahasya Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. The **"secret of Sarasvatī"** — the **Sarasvatī-vidyā** (a Sarasvatī Tantric mantra) wrapped in an Advaitic teaching that the Goddess is the **non-dual Brahman/knowledge**. *(Source: Sarasvatī-rahasya Up.)*
- **Sītā Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. **Sītā as Mūla-Prakṛti / the supreme Śakti** — identified with Lakṣmī and with the **praṇava**, the consort-power through whom Rāma/Viṣṇu acts; a Śākta–Vaiṣṇava bridge text. *(Source: Sītā Up.)*
- **Annapūrṇā Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. **Annapūrṇā** (the food-giving Goddess) frames an **Advaita/jīvan-mukti** teaching (Ribhu/Nidāgha-style) — *manonāśa* (extinction of mind) and the marks of the liberated. *(Source: Annapūrṇā Up.)*
- **Bahvṛca Upaniṣad** — Ṛgveda. A very short, important Śākta declaration: **the Goddess (Mahā-tripurasundarī) alone existed in the beginning** and is the **ātman/Brahman** — a Śākta restatement of the *aham brahmāsmi* mahāvākya. *(Source: Bahvṛca Up.)* [sectarian core]

## 5. Shared Features and Where They Sit

- **The tāpanī genre.** Several Vaiṣṇava and Śākta texts share the **-tāpanī(ya)** form (Rāma-, Nṛsiṃha-, Gopāla-, Tripurā-tāpinī) — a recognisable **mantra-exposition genre** that "heats" (ascetically refines) the devotee through the deity's name-mantra; often in paired **pūrva-/uttara-** halves (mantra-and-ritual, then Advaitic resolution). [genre marker]
- **Tantric / Śrīvidyā affiliation.** The Śākta group, and the more esoteric Śaiva/Vaiṣṇava texts (Rāma-rahasya, Garuḍa, Gaṇapati, Tripurā/Bhāvanā), are **Tantric in method** — *bīja*-mantras, *yantra*/*cakra* worship, *nyāsa*, and the **Śrīcakra** — the direct **Śruti face of the Āgama/Tantra corpus** in [`../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md`](../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md). [scriptural-to-Tantra bridge]
- **Sectarian polemic vs. harmonising.** Some texts assert one deity **over** another (**Śarabha**: Śiva subdues Nṛsiṃha), while others **harmonise** them (**Rudrahṛdaya**, **Sītā**: Hari-Hara and Śiva-Śakti non-difference) — the catalogue preserves both without merging. [do not merge traditions]
- **Roots upstream.** They personalise seeds already in the *mukhya* set — the **Śvetāśvatara's** theistic **Rudra/Īśa** and the **Devī-sūkta**/**Nārāyaṇa-sūkta** of the Vedas — see [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md) and [`../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md). *(Source: Śvetāśvatara Up.; Ṛgveda Devī-sūkta)*
- **The practice-cousins.** Editors set these sectarian groups beside the **Yoga** and **Sannyāsa** Upaniṣads as the great minor-Upaniṣad blocks — the sectarian set on *the deity*, the Yoga set on *technique* ([`./yoga-upanishads-overview.md`](./yoga-upanishads-overview.md)), the Sannyāsa set on *renunciation* ([`./sannyasa-upanishads-overview.md`](./sannyasa-upanishads-overview.md)). *(Source: Muktikā Up.)*
