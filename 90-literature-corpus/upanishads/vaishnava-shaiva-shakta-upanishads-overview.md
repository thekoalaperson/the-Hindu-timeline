# The Vaiṣṇava, Śaiva, and Śākta (Sectarian) Upaniṣads

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/upanishads/`](./README.md) — a cross-text overview/catalogue of the three largest **sectarian (sampradāya) sub-groups** into which modern editors sort the **Muktikā** canon: the **14 Vaiṣṇava**, **14 Śaiva**, and **8–9 Śākta Upaniṣads**. These are the minor (*gauṇa*) Śruti texts that re-read the impersonal **Brahman** of the older Upaniṣads as a **named sectarian deity** — Viṣṇu/Rāma/Kṛṣṇa/Nṛsiṃha, Śiva/Rudra/Gaṇapati, or the Goddess (Devī/Tripurā/Lakṣmī) — and so form the **Śruti charter of theistic and Tantric (Śrīvidyā) devotion**. Frame-files: [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md) (the *mukhya* core they grow out of); [`../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) (the Vedas, the four strata, the Muktikā 108-list, the cosmic clock); [`../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md`](../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md) (the Āgama/Tantra stream they feed). Practice-cousins: [`./yoga-upanishads-overview.md`](./yoga-upanishads-overview.md), [`./sannyasa-upanishads-overview.md`](./sannyasa-upanishads-overview.md). ## 1. What the Sectarian Upaniṣads Are

- **Three deity-defined clusters of the 108 — an editors' grouping, not the Muktikā's.** The **Muktikā Upaniṣad** names its **108 Upaniṣads** in **1.30–39** and then, in the prose that follows, sorts them **only by parent Veda** (with each Veda's *śānti* verse): **Ṛgveda 10, Śukla-Yajurveda 19, Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda 32, Sāmaveda 16, Atharvaveda 31**. It does **not** group them by deity. The **Vaiṣṇava / Śaiva / Śākta** blocks (beside the Yoga, Sannyāsa and Sāmānya-Vedānta blocks) are a **modern editorial classification** — chiefly the Adyar Library volumes, and scholars such as Deussen and Tinoco. *(Source: Muktikā Up. 1.30–39 and the Veda-wise prose list after 1.49; Adyar Library sectarian editions)* [scholarly]
- **The Adyar groupings.** The standard reference editions are the **Adyar Library's *Vaiṣṇava Upaniṣads* (1923), *Śaiva Upaniṣads*, and *Śākta Upaniṣads* (1925)**. All three were **edited by Pandit A. Mahadeva Sastri** with the Advaita commentary of **Upaniṣad-brahmayogin**, the Kāñcīpuram renunciant Rāmacandrendra (born mid-18th c.; fl. c. 1800). He is credited with commentaries on all 108. Each volume sets out one possible membership for its group. *(Source: Adyar Library catalogue, "The 108 Upaniṣad-s"; Adyar Library Series 8–10; Wikipedia, Upanishad Brahmayogin)* [scholarly]
- **The shared "deity-as-Brahman" move.** Every text in all three groups performs the same operation: it **identifies its chosen deity with the absolute Brahman** of the *mukhya* Upaniṣads — *"this Viṣṇu/Rudra/Devī is the One without a second, the ātman, Oṃ."* The metaphysics is borrowed Vedānta; the **personalisation** is the sectarian contribution. [the unifying thesis]
- **Counts and assignments differ by edition.** The exact membership is **not fixed**, and some texts sit on the borderline. The **Mahā Upaniṣad** is Vaiṣṇava for Deussen but Sāmānya for Tinoco. **Annapūrṇā** is Śākta in some lists but Sāmānya for Tinoco. The Muktikā's Atharvavedic **"Mahānārāyaṇa"** is the *Tripādvibhūti-mahānārāyaṇa*, not the Taittirīya *Mahānārāyaṇa*. The **Sītā** is claimed by both Śākta and Vaiṣṇava readers. Treat the numbers (14 / 14 / 8–9) as **conventional, not canonical**. *(Deussen 1980, p. 799 n. 1; Tinoco 1996, pp. 87–89, as reported in Wikipedia's Vaiṣṇava Upaniṣads article; Tinoco p. 87 on Annapūrṇā, and the Śākta label in Wikipedia's Muktikā list)* [count varies by edition] [disputed]
- **Mostly late and Atharvanic.** None belongs to the ten/twelve *mukhya* set; they are **minor, post-classical** texts. The Muktikā attaches **20 of the 37 texts below to the Atharvaveda** and **8 to the Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda**. The rest go to the Sāmaveda (4), the Ṛgveda (4) and the Śukla-Yajurveda (1, the Tārasāra). The most Tantric of them are the latest. *(Source: Muktikā Up., Veda-wise prose list)* [late text] [scholarly]
- **The "two clocks."** *Academic:* a **late layer** of the canon, post-dating the rise of Pāñcarātra/Bhāgavata, Pāśupata/Śaiva-siddhānta, and Śrīvidyā Tantra. Estimates vary. Mahony dates the minor Upaniṣads broadly to **c. 100 BCE–1100 CE**. Lamb puts the Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī among the earliest sectarian texts, **before the 7th c. CE**. Olivelle thinks the Atharvavedic sectarian texts were likely composed in the **2nd millennium CE, down to about the 16th c.** Cush places the Śākta group in the **12th–15th c. CE**. [scholarly] *Variant (orthodox / traditional):* eternal *apauruṣeya* Śruti "seen" by ṛṣis and handed down from before the **Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; see Kali-Yuga onset constant)**, not "authored" in a CE stratum. The Kalisantaraṇa itself is set "at the end of the Dvāpara" (§1). Both frames are recorded; neither is corrected. [scripture vs. academic dating]

## 2. The Vaiṣṇava Upaniṣads (~14)

The Viṣṇu-centred group, dominated by the **tāpanī genre** — mantra-expositions in which the deity's name-mantra is the path to liberation.

- **Rāma-tāpanīya (Rāma-pūrva- & Rāma-uttara-tāpanī)** — Atharvaveda. Exalts **Rāma as para-Brahman**. It sets out the **six-syllable (ṣaḍakṣara) Rāma-mantra**, given in coded form and conventionally read as *rāṃ rāmāya namaḥ*, and a **Rāma-yantra** (*pūrva* 1.13, 4.67 in the sanskritdocuments edition). In the *uttara*, **Śiva recites Rāma's mantra at Kāśī**. Rāma then grants that every creature dying in Śiva's Avimukta field is freed, and that Śiva will **whisper the mantra into the right ear of the dying** (*uttara*, Śiva–Rāma ślokas 1–8). It is the chief Śruti text of Rāma-bhakti. See [`./ramatapaniya-upanishad.md`](./ramatapaniya-upanishad.md). *(Source: Rāmatāpanī Up.)*
- **Rāma-rahasya** — Atharvaveda. The **"secret of Rāma"**: Sanaka and the other yogins, the ṛṣis, and Prahlāda and other Viṣṇu-devotees question **Hanumān**. He expounds Rāma's *aṅga*-deities and the Rāma-mantras with their *bīja*s. It is a Tantric Rāma-vidyā text. *(Source: Rāmarahasya Up. 1.2 ff.)* [late text]
- **Nṛsiṃha-tāpanīya (pūrva- & uttara-)** — Atharvaveda. The major Vaiṣṇava-tāpanī: **Narasiṃha as Brahman** via the **anuṣṭubh "mantra-rāja"** (*ugraṃ vīraṃ mahāviṣṇum…*). It also describes the six-spoked **Sudarśana mahā-cakra** diagram (*pūrva* 5). The nine-*khaṇḍa* *uttara* is strongly **Advaitic (Oṃ / praṇava)**. See [`./nrisimha-tapaniya-upanishad.md`](./nrisimha-tapaniya-upanishad.md). *(Source: Nṛsiṃhatāpanī Up.; the nine-khaṇḍa count from Wikipedia's Nrisimha Tapaniya article, since the sanskritdocuments file breaks off in the uttara)*
- **Gopāla-tāpanī (pūrva- & uttara-)** — Atharvaveda. **Kṛṣṇa as Gopāla = Brahman**, built on the **eighteen-syllable Gopāla mantra** (*klīṃ kṛṣṇāya govindāya gopījanavallabhāya svāhā*). The *uttara* has the gopīs crossing the Yamunā to the sage **Durvāsas** and a glorification of **Mathurā**. It is a foundation text for **Gauḍīya/Caitanya** theology. See [`./gopala-tapaniya-upanishad.md`](./gopala-tapaniya-upanishad.md). *(Source: Gopālatāpanī Up.)*
- **Kṛṣṇa Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. It opens with the forest-sages seeing **Rāma** and longing to embrace him. He promises they will do so when they are **born as gopīs in the Kṛṣṇa-avatāra** (vv. 1–2). This is the text's own account of where the gopīs came from. The rest allegorises Kṛṣṇa's world. The **gopīs and cows are the ṛcas**, the flute is Rudra (v. 8), the 16,108 wives are Upaniṣadic ṛcas (v. 13), and **Kaṃsa is Kali** (v. 15). *(Source: Kṛṣṇa Up.)*
- **Vāsudeva Upaniṣad** — Sāmaveda. **Vāsudeva teaches Nārada** the rite of the **ūrdhva-puṇḍra (Vaiṣṇava forehead-mark)** made with **gopī-candana**, a clay said to come from Vaikuṇṭha and to lie in the Cakra-tīrtha. It is applied with the Viṣṇu-gāyatrī or the Keśava-and-other names. A householder marks **twelve places** of the body; a renunciant (*yati*) marks three with the praṇava. The three upright lines are read as the three letters of Oṃ. It also teaches meditation on Hari as the flame in the heart, and closes with rules for gopī-candana and ash. *(Source: Vāsudeva Up.)*
- **Garuḍa Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. A **Garuḍa brahma-vidyā** against snake-venom, with *nyāsa*, a *dhyāna* of Garuḍa adorned with the great Nāgas, and long *huṃ phaṭ* spells invoking **Garuḍa** as poison-remover. Its lineage runs Brahmā → Nārada → Bṛhatsena → Indra → Bharadvāja. It is an apotropaic/Tantric text. *(Source: Garuḍa Up.)* [apotropaic]
- **Hayagrīva Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. Brahmā teaches Nārada the **Hayagrīva mantras** and the one-syllable *bīja*. These are said to confer **all Vedic and Purāṇic learning and Brahman-realisation**, the horse-necked Viṣṇu being lord of learning. *(Source: Hayagrīva Up. 1–5)*
- **Dattātreya Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. In "Satya-kṣetra" **Nārāyaṇa teaches Brahmā** to meditate on **Dattātreya** as the *tāraka*. It gives the one-syllable *bīja* (*dāṃ*) and graded six-, eight-, twelve- and sixteen-syllable mantras. It also gives an anuṣṭubh name-verse (*dattātreya hare kṛṣṇa unmattānandadāyaka…*). The colouring is both Tantric and Advaitic. *(Source: Dattātreya Up.)* [late text]
- **Tārasāra Upaniṣad** — Śukla-Yajurveda. The **"essence of the tāraka"**. It opens with Bṛhaspati and Yājñavalkya on **Avimukta**, where Rudra imparts the *tāraka* to the dying. Then **Yājñavalkya tells Bhāradvāja** that the *tāraka* is **Oṃ namo nārāyaṇāya**. Its syllables are mapped onto Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Rudra and other cosmic principles. Oṃ is then divided into eight parts, each identified with a member of **Rāma's circle**: Jāmbavān (*a*), Upendra the monkey-lord (*u*), Hanumān (*m*), Śatrughna, Bharata, Lakṣmaṇa, **Sītā**, and finally **Rāma** as the Supreme. *(Source: Tārasāra Up. 1–2)*
- **Nārāyaṇa Upaniṣad** (also called the *Nārāyaṇa-Atharvaśīrṣa*) — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. Declares that **Brahmā, Rudra, Indra and all the worlds are born from Nārāyaṇa** and that "Nārāyaṇa alone is all this". It gives the **aṣṭākṣara (*Oṃ namo nārāyaṇāya*)** as the supreme mantra. Its first four sections are labelled the "head" (*śiras*) of the Ṛg-, Yajur-, Sāma- and Atharva-veda in turn. *(Source: Nārāyaṇa Up. 1–5)*
- **Avyakta Upaniṣad** (also titled the *Avyakta-nṛsiṃha*, as in the sanskritdocuments edition) — Sāmaveda. A cosmogony: the formless light splits into a red (Puruṣa) and a green (Māyā) half, from which the golden egg and **Parameṣṭhin** arise. After a thousand years of *brahmacarya* he "sees" the **anuṣṭubh vidyā**, the Nṛsiṃha mantra-rāja. Through it he beholds **Nṛsiṃha** embraced by Śrī. This links it to the Nṛsiṃha-tāpanī. *(Source: Avyakta Up. 1–2)* [late text]
- **Tripādvibhūti-mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. In it **Parameṣṭhin (Brahmā) questions Mahāviṣṇu (Nārāyaṇa)**. It is a long, late text in eight *adhyāya*s on **Nārāyaṇa's "three-quarter glory" (tripād-vibhūti)**, the transcendent **Vaikuṇṭha** realm beyond the manifest fourth. It is not the same as the Yajurvedic *Mahānārāyaṇa* (see [`./mahanarayana-upanishad.md`](./mahanarayana-upanishad.md)). *(Source: Tripādvibhūti-mahānārāyaṇa Up.)* [late text] [disputed/confused naming]
- **Kalisantaraṇa (Kali-saṃtaraṇa / "Hare Kṛṣṇa") Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. **At the end of the Dvāpara, Nārada asks Brahmā** how to cross the Kali age. Brahmā gives the **sixteen-name mahā-mantra** (§§1–2). It is the charter of the *nāma-saṃkīrtana* movements. See [`./kalisantarana-upanishad.md`](./kalisantarana-upanishad.md). *(Source: Kalisantaraṇa Up.)*
  - *Variant (Kalisantaraṇa text):* the order is **"hare rāma hare rāma rāma rāma hare hare / hare kṛṣṇa hare kṛṣṇa kṛṣṇa kṛṣṇa hare hare"** — the Rāma half first.
  - *Variant (Gauḍīya/ISKCON practice):* in common recitation the **Kṛṣṇa half comes first** (see the deep-dive file above). [later practice]

## 3. The Śaiva Upaniṣads (~14–15)

The Śiva/Rudra-centred group — the **Śruti backing of Śaiva theology, the Pañcākṣara, Bhasma/Rudrākṣa lore, and the Pāśupata ash-vow**. The 14 below follow the usual modern list. It includes the **Gāṇapatya** Gaṇapati Upaniṣad, which some lists set apart.

- **Atharvaśiras Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. Often counted among the earlier sectarian texts. The gods go to heaven and ask **Rudra** "who are you?" He answers: **"I alone was in the beginning, am, and shall be; there is no other apart from me"** (§1). The gods then praise him in the litany *yo vai rudraḥ sa bhagavān…* (§2). It teaches **Oṃ as Rudra** and the **Pāśupata-vrata** of smearing ash "for release from the bond (*pāśa*)" (§5). *(Source: Atharvaśiras Up.)* [dating varies — scholarly]
- **Atharvaśikhā Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. **Pippalāda, Aṅgiras and Sanatkumāra ask Atharvan** what is to be meditated on. The answer is **Oṃ**, whose four *mātrā*s are matched with earth / atmosphere / heaven / the Soma-world, the four Vedas, four fires and the deities **Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Rudra, and Puruṣa**. The text closes that **"Śiva alone, the bestower of good, is to be meditated upon"**, leaving all else (§§1–3). *(Source: Atharvaśikhā Up.)*
- **Kaivalya Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. A celebrated, much-quoted text. **Āśvalāyana asks Parameṣṭhin (Brahmā)**, who teaches **liberation (kaivalya)** through meditation on **Umā's consort, the three-eyed, blue-throated Lord** (v. 7). This Lord is "all that was and will be" (v. 9). The disciple then realises *"I am Sadāśiva… I am of the form of Śiva"* (vv. 18, 20). See [`./kaivalya-upanishad.md`](./kaivalya-upanishad.md). *(Source: Kaivalya Up.)*
- **Kālāgnirudra Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. **Sanatkumāra asks Kālāgni-Rudra** about the **tripuṇḍra**, the three horizontal ash-lines of the Śaiva forehead-mark. Each line is matched with a fire, a letter of Oṃ, a world, a Veda, a soma-pressing and a deity (**Maheśvara, Sadāśiva, Mahādeva**). *(Source: Kālāgnirudra Up.)*
- **Akṣamālikā Upaniṣad** — Ṛgveda. **Prajāpati asks Guha (Skanda)** about the **rosary (akṣa-mālā)**: its materials, its threading, and the **letters *a* … *kṣa***: fifty (*a* … *ḷa*) consecrated into its beads, and *kṣa* into the crest-bead (*śikhāmaṇi*). *(Source: Akṣamālikā Up.)*
- **Rudrahṛdaya Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. **Śuka asks his father Vyāsa** which god contains all gods. On Rudra's right stand the Sun, Brahmā and the three fires; on his left **Umā, Viṣṇu and Soma**. Then: **"Umā is Viṣṇu himself"** (v. 5). "Those who bow to Govinda bow to Śaṅkara; those who hate Virūpākṣa hate Janārdana" (vv. 6–7). All that is masculine is Īśāna, all that is feminine is Umā (v. 9). It thus affirms **Hari–Hara non-difference** by identifying Viṣṇu with Śiva's Śakti. *(Source: Rudrahṛdaya Up. vv. 2–11)*
- **Bṛhajjābāla Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. A long text in several *brāhmaṇa*s on the **sacred ash (bhasma / vibhūti)** and **rudrākṣa**: their preparation, application, and power, taught by **Kālāgni-Rudra to Bhusuṇḍa**. *(Source: Bṛhajjābāla Up.)* [late text]
- **Bhasmajābāla Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. **Bhusuṇḍa of the Jābāla line approaches Śiva on Kailāsa** to learn about the **holy ash (bhasma)**: its making from sacred fire, the **tripuṇḍra**, and Śiva-worship. It also glorifies **Kāśī**, where those who die enter Śiva himself. *(Source: Bhasmajābāla Up.)*
- **Rudrākṣajābāla Upaniṣad** — Sāmaveda in the Muktikā's Veda-wise list (Wikipedia's Śaiva table gives Atharvaveda). **Bhusuṇḍa asks Kālāgni-Rudra**, who tells how he closed his eyes **for the destruction of Tripura**. **Tear-drops** fell from them and became the rudrākṣa trees. The text then sets out the merit of the beads and their **one to fourteen faces (*mukha*)**, each tied to a deity. *(Source: Rudrākṣajābāla Up.)* [etiological myth]
- **Gaṇapati Upaniṣad (Gaṇapati-Atharvaśīrṣa)** — Atharvaveda. The principal **Gāṇapatya** text: **Gaṇeśa is Brahman**. *"tvam eva pratyakṣaṃ tattvam asi… tvam eva sarvaṃ khalv idaṃ brahmāsi; tvaṃ sākṣād ātmāsi nityam"* (§1); *"tvaṃ pratyakṣaṃ brahmāsi"* (§4). It analyses the **bīja "gaṃ"** (§7) and gives the **Gaṇapati-Gāyatrī** *ekadantāya vidmahe…* (§8). Section numbers vary between editions. See [`./ganapati-atharvashirsha-upanishad.md`](./ganapati-atharvashirsha-upanishad.md). *(Source: Gaṇapati-Atharvaśīrṣa Up.)* [sectarian core]
- **Pañcabrahma Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. **Paippalāda asks what arose first**, and Śiva (Maheśa) answers, addressing Śākala, with the **five faces / five Brahmans of Śiva**: Sadyojāta, Vāmadeva, Aghora, Tatpuruṣa, Īśāna. It also teaches the **Pañcākṣara** (*na-ma-śi-vā-ya*, vv. 24–25). This Sadāśiva mantra-cosmology is shared with **Śaiva Āgama/Siddhānta**. *(Source: Pañcabrahma Up.)*
- **Śarabha Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. **Paippalāda asks Brahmā** which of Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Rudra is supreme. The answer is Rudra, who as **Śarabha** "slew Nṛsiṃha, destroyer of the world". He tore Viṣṇu with his claws and, wearing the skin, **became Vīrabhadra** (vv. 4–6). The text also praises Rudra for cutting off Brahmā's fifth head (v. 7). It is a Śaiva polemical assertion of Śiva's supremacy over Viṣṇu's Man-Lion. *(Source: Śarabha Up.)* [sectarian polemic]
- **Jābāli Upaniṣad** — Sāmaveda. A **Pāśupata** text. **Paippalādi asks Jābāli**, who learnt from Ṣaḍānana (Skanda), who learnt from Īśāna. The teaching is on **Paśupati, paśu (the bound soul)**, and release through **bhasma and tripuṇḍra**. Distinguish it from the Sannyāsa *Jābāla*. *(Source: Jābāli Up.)* [disputed/confused naming]
  - *Variant (Jābāli vs. Kālāgnirudra):* both give near-identical tripuṇḍra paragraphs, but they name the three lines' deities differently. Jābāli has **Prajāpati, Viṣṇu, Mahādeva**; Kālāgnirudra has **Maheśvara, Sadāśiva, Mahādeva**.
- **Dakṣiṇāmūrti Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. **Śaunaka and the sages**, gathered for a great sacrifice under the Bhāṇḍīra banyan in Brahmāvarta, ask the long-lived **Mārkaṇḍeya** how he lives so long. He answers: by knowing **Śiva as Dakṣiṇāmūrti**, the south-facing teacher under the banyan. The text gives Dakṣiṇāmūrti's **mantras and dhyāna**, the guru-as-Śiva ideal. *(Source: Dakṣiṇāmūrti Up.)*

## 4. The Śākta Upaniṣads (~8)

The Goddess-centred group — the **Śruti charter of Śrīvidyā / Śāktism**, built around the **Devī as the supreme Brahman** and the **Śrīcakra / Śrīvidyā (Tripurā) mantra**. The Adyar *Śākta Upaniṣads* volume prints **eight** texts. Nine are listed below because the **Annapūrṇā** is included as a borderline member (classed Sāmānya by Tinoco).

- **Devī Upaniṣad (Devī-Atharvaśīrṣa)** — Atharvaveda. The flagship. The gods ask **"Who art thou, great Goddess?" (*kāsi tvaṃ mahādevi*)**. She answers **"I am of the nature of Brahman"** and goes on to quote the Ṛgvedic **Devī-sūkta** (*ahaṃ rudrebhir vasubhiś carāmi…*, RV 10.125). The text gives the **navākṣarī** Devī mantra in coded form and the Goddess-Gāyatrī *mahālakṣmīś ca vidmahe…* (the sanskritdocuments reading). See [`./devi-upanishad.md`](./devi-upanishad.md). *(Source: Devī-Atharvaśīrṣa Up.)* [sectarian core]
- **Tripurā Upaniṣad** — Ṛgveda. A concise, weighty **Śrīvidyā** text in short Vedic-style verses on **Tripurā / Tripurasundarī**: the **three cities (*tisraḥ purāḥ*)** (v. 1), the nine yonis, nine cakras and nine yoginīs of the **Śrīcakra** (v. 2), and the **kāmakalā**. Verse 8 is traditionally read as the fifteen-syllable *kādi* vidyā in coded form. The Bahvṛca (§3) names the *kādi* vidyā outright. **Bhāskararāya** wrote a famous commentary on it. *(Source: Tripurā Up.)*
- **Tripurātāpinī (Tripurā-tāpinī) Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. The **tāpinī** expansion of Tripurā-vidyā. It reads the Goddess out of a "hundred-syllable" (*śatākṣarī*) vidyā built from three Ṛgvedic verses: the **Gāyatrī**, the *jātavedase* verse and the *tryambakaṃ* verse. It then expounds the **fifteen-syllable Śrīvidyā mantra (pañcadaśī)**, the Śrīcakra, and the Goddess as Brahman. *(Source: Tripurātāpinī Up.)* [late text]
- **Bhāvanā Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. The **"contemplation" text**. It opens: "the **Śrīguru is the Śakti that is the cause of all**". It then maps the **Śrīcakra onto the practitioner's own body**: the nine-gated body is the nine-fold Śrīcakra and the island of nine gems, with its powers and *yoginī*s inside. It is a core meditative Śrīvidyā manual; Bhāskararāya also wrote a commentary on it. *(Source: Bhāvanā Up.)*
- **Saubhāgya-lakṣmī Upaniṣad** — Ṛgveda. **The gods ask the Lord** about **Lakṣmī as the Goddess of auspicious fortune (saubhāgya)**. The answer is worship built on the verses of the **Śrī-sūkta** (*hiraṇyavarṇāṃ…*), with its *yantra*s. The text then teaches a **nine-cakra (*nava-cakra*) yoga** running from the *ādhāra* (brahma-cakra) up to the ninth, the *ākāśa-cakra*, uniting **Śrī-worship and Vedānta**. *(Source: Saubhāgyalakṣmī Up.)*
- **Sarasvatī-rahasya Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. **The ṛṣis ask Āśvalāyana**, who gives the **"ten-verse" (*daśaślokī*) Sarasvatī mahā-mantra**: Ṛgvedic Sarasvatī verses (e.g. *pāvakā naḥ sarasvatī*, RV 1.3.10) each fused with a *bīja*. These are wrapped in an Advaitic teaching that the Goddess is the **non-dual Brahman/knowledge**. *(Source: Sarasvatī-rahasya Up.)*
- **Sītā Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. **The gods ask Prajāpati "Who is Sītā?"** She is **Mūla-Prakṛti** and the **prakṛti of the praṇava**; her three syllables are glossed one by one. Her threefold power is **icchā-, kriyā- and sākṣāt-śakti**, and she is identified with Śrī, Bhū and Nīlā. She is the consort-power through whom Rāma/Viṣṇu acts — a Śākta–Vaiṣṇava bridge text. *(Source: Sītā Up.)*
- **Annapūrṇā Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. The yogin **Nidāgha asks the sage Ṛbhu** for the knowledge of the Self. Ṛbhu says he won it by worshipping the food-giving Goddess **Annapūrṇā** with her mantra (*… namo bhagavaty annapūrṇe…*, 1.5–8), until she appeared before him (1.9). He then teaches an **Advaita / jīvan-mukti** doctrine: *manonāśa* (extinction of mind) and the marks of the liberated. *(Source: Annapūrṇā Up. 1)* [classification disputed: Śākta / Sāmānya]
- **Bahvṛca Upaniṣad** — Ṛgveda. A very short, important Śākta declaration. **"The Goddess alone was in the beginning"**; she emitted the egg of the world and bore Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Rudra (§§1–2). She is the *śāmbhavī-vidyā* — "*kādi*, *hādi* or *sādi*" vidyā (§3). As **Mahā-tripurasundarī** she is the inner awareness, "the one reality called **Lalitā**", the non-dual Brahman (§5). All the **mahāvākyas** (*prajñānaṃ brahma*, *ahaṃ brahmāsmi*, *tat tvam asi*…) are said to point to her. *(Source: Bahvṛca Up.)* [sectarian core]

## 5. Shared Features and Where They Sit

- **The tāpanī genre.** Several Vaiṣṇava and Śākta texts share the **-tāpanī(ya)** form (Rāma-, Nṛsiṃha-, Gopāla-, Tripurā-tāpinī) — a recognisable **mantra-exposition genre**, often in paired **pūrva-/uttara-** halves (mantra-and-ritual, then Advaitic resolution). The gloss that the title means the deity's name-mantra "heats" (ascetically refines) the devotee is a traditional/interpretive reading of the word. [genre marker] [interpretive etymology]
- **The atharvaśīrṣa/-śiras family.** A second recurring label: the **Atharvaśiras** itself, the **Nārāyaṇa** (also called *Nārāyaṇa-Atharvaśīrṣa*), the **Gaṇapati** (which calls itself "this *atharvaśīrṣa*", §11 in the sanskritdocuments edition) and the **Devī-Atharvaśīrṣa**. Each presents a deity as the "head" or crowning teaching of the Atharvan lore. [genre marker]
- **Tantric / Śrīvidyā affiliation.** The Śākta group, and the more esoteric Śaiva/Vaiṣṇava texts (Rāma-rahasya, Garuḍa, Gaṇapati, Tripurā/Bhāvanā), are **Tantric in method** — *bīja*-mantras, *yantra*/*cakra* worship, *nyāsa*, and the **Śrīcakra** — the direct **Śruti face of the Āgama/Tantra corpus** in [`../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md`](../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md). [scriptural-to-Tantra bridge]
- **Sectarian polemic vs. harmonising.** Some texts assert one deity **over** another (**Śarabha**: Śiva-Śarabha slays and flays Nṛsiṃha). Others **harmonise** them: **Rudrahṛdaya** has Hari–Hara non-difference through "Umā is Viṣṇu"; **Sītā** has the Śakti non-different from Rāma/Viṣṇu; the **Kṛṣṇa Up.** has Rudra as Kṛṣṇa's flute. The catalogue preserves both without merging. [do not merge traditions]
- **Roots upstream.** They personalise seeds already in the *mukhya* set — the **Śvetāśvatara's** theistic **Rudra/Īśa** and the **Devī-sūkta**/**Nārāyaṇa-sūkta** of the Vedas — see [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md) and [`../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md). *(Source: Śvetāśvatara Up.; Ṛgveda Devī-sūkta)*
- **The practice-cousins.** Modern editors (notably the Adyar series) set these sectarian groups beside the **Yoga**, **Sannyāsa** and **Sāmānya-Vedānta** Upaniṣads as the great minor-Upaniṣad blocks. The sectarian set is on *the deity*, the Yoga set on *technique* ([`./yoga-upanishads-overview.md`](./yoga-upanishads-overview.md)), and the Sannyāsa set on *renunciation* ([`./sannyasa-upanishads-overview.md`](./sannyasa-upanishads-overview.md)). *(Source: Adyar Library series; not the Muktikā, which groups by Veda only)*

### Shared frames and borrowed passages

- **The Avimukta / tāraka frame.** The prose of the Sannyāsa **Jābāla Up. §1** recurs almost word for word at the head of the **Rāma-uttara-tāpanī** and the **Tārasāra**. In it Bṛhaspati asks Yājñavalkya about Kurukṣetra = **Avimukta**, where "Rudra expounds the *tāraka* Brahman to a creature whose breaths are departing". In those two texts (not in the Jābāla, where Atri asks next about Avimukta) Bhāradvāja then asks "what is the *tāraka*?", and each **fills in a different saving mantra**: the Rāma *ṣaḍakṣara* in the Rāma-tāpanī, and *Oṃ namo nārāyaṇāya* followed by the Rāma-circle reading of Oṃ in the Tārasāra. *(Source: Jābāla Up. 1–2; Rāmottaratāpanī; Tārasāra Up. 1)* The Rāmottaratāpanī later repeats Jābāla §2 as well (Atri on Avimukta between the Varaṇā and the Nāśī). [shared passage]
- **The tripuṇḍra paragraph.** The same three-line exposition of the ash-mark appears in the **Kālāgnirudra** and the **Jābāli**, with different deities for the lines (see the variant under Jābāli above). The **Bhasmajābāla** and **Bṛhajjābāla** expand the same ash-lore. [shared passage]
- **Recurring interlocutors.** **Paippalāda/Pippalāda** is the questioner in the Atharvaśikhā, Śarabha and Pañcabrahma. **Paippalādi** questions Jābāli in the Jābāli. **Bhusuṇḍa** questions **Kālāgni-Rudra** in the Bṛhajjābāla and Rudrākṣajābāla, and Śiva in the Bhasmajābāla. **Nārada** is the questioner in the Hayagrīva, Vāsudeva and Kalisantaraṇa. These figures stitch the groups together across their nominal Vedas. [cross-text pattern]
