# Chāndogya Upaniṣad — Dedicated Deep-Dive

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/`](../README.md) — a per-text deep-dive into the **Chāndogya Upaniṣad**, one of the two oldest and longest of the *mukhya* Upaniṣads. ## 1. Identity, Placement, Dating

- **Parent Veda & school** — Chāndogya belongs to the **Sāmaveda** (the Veda of *sāman*, the melodic chant); the name derives from *chandoga*, "singer of the chant." Transmitted in the **Kauthuma / Rāṇāyanīya śākhā**. The text is the **last eight of the ten chapters** of the **Chāndogya Brāhmaṇa** (also called Tāṇḍya / Tāṇḍya-Mahā-Brāhmaṇa tradition); the first two Brāhmaṇa chapters are domestic-ritual (mantra-brāhmaṇa) material and not part of the Upaniṣad proper. *(Source: Chāndogya Up.; Śaṅkara's bhāṣya intro)*
- **Scale** — **Eight prapāṭhakas** (chapters), subdivided into khaṇḍas (volumes/sections): roughly 13 + 24 + 19 + 17 + 24 + 16 + 26 + 15 sections. Together with the Bṛhadāraṇyaka it is one of the two **oldest and longest** Upaniṣads. Ninth in the **Muktikā** list of 108. *(Source: Chāndogya Up.; Muktikā Up.)*
- **Dating (two clocks)** —
  - *Academic:* early prose, **c. 7th–6th c. BCE**, **pre-Buddhist**, second-oldest principal Upaniṣad after Bṛhadāraṇyaka (Patrick Olivelle, 1996/1998); the text is itself an **anthology of pre-existing units**, so layers may differ in age. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (orthodox / traditional):* eternal apauruṣeya Śruti, "seen" by ṛṣis and re-compiled by **Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa** at the Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; see Kali-Yuga onset constant), not "authored" in a BCE stratum. This file records both frames and corrects neither. [scripture vs. academic dating]
- **Commentaries** — **Ādi Śaṅkara** (c. 8th c. CE) wrote a full **bhāṣya** on the Chāndogya, one of his ten canonical Upaniṣad commentaries; it is a primary Advaita proof-text. Also commented by **Madhvācārya** (Dvaita) and **Rangarāmānuja** (Viśiṣṭādvaita lineage). The Chāndogya is among the most frequently cited Upaniṣads in **Bādarāyaṇa's Brahma-Sūtra**, especially Śāṇḍilya-vidyā, Pañcāgni-vidyā, Sad-vidyā, Bhūma-vidyā and Dahara-vidyā. *(Source: Śaṅkara's Chāndogya-bhāṣya; Brahma-Sūtra)*
- **Unifying motif** — uniquely among Upaniṣads it foregrounds **speech, chant (sāman), syllable and Om** as the ladder to liberating knowledge, beginning from ritual symbol and ascending to pure non-dual Being.

## 2. The Mahāvākya — *Tat tvam asi* (6.8–6.16)

- **The great saying** — **"Tat tvam asi"** ("That thou art" / "You are That"), Chāndogya **6.8.7**, the **mahāvākya assigned to the Sāmaveda** in the fourfold Vedānta scheme. It identifies the individual (tvam) with the one Being / Sat (tat) underlying all. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 6.8.7)*
  - Cross-reference: the four mahāvākyas are catalogued together in [`principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md) (§1) and unpacked in [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md).
- **The setting: Śvetaketu & Uddālaka Āruṇi (6.1–6.7)** — Śvetaketu returns from twelve years of Vedic study "conceited, thinking himself learned." His father **Uddālaka Āruṇi** asks whether he sought the teaching "by which the unheard becomes heard, the unthought thought, the unknown known." Śvetaketu had not, so Uddālaka teaches him the **Sad-vidyā** (knowledge of Being). *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 6.1)*
- **Sat as sole origin (6.2)** — *"In the beginning this was Being alone (sad eva...), one only, without a second"* — explicitly rejecting the rival view that "in the beginning was Non-being." Being willed "May I be many," and emitted heat (tejas), water (āpas), food (anna); all subsequent forms are recombinations of these. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 6.2)* — compare the cosmogonic accounts in [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md).
- **The "essence" analogies (6.1, 6.4)** — by knowing one lump of **clay** all clay is known; by one nugget of **gold**, all gold; by one pair of **nail-scissors**, all iron — the modification is "merely a name, a verbal handle," the reality is the substance. So too one essence (Sat) underlies all names-and-forms. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 6.1.4–6)*
- **Ninefold repetition** — the refrain **"sa ya eṣo'ṇimā... tat tvam asi Śvetaketo"** ("That which is the finest essence — this whole world has that as its Self. That is the Real. That is the Self. That thou art, Śvetaketu") closes **each of the nine teaching units 6.8 through 6.16**, hammered home **nine times** with a fresh parable each round. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 6.8.7–6.16.3)*
- **The parables (one per round):**
  - **Deep sleep (6.8)** — in dreamless sleep the self merges into Being ("has come to its own"), as the bird tied by a string settles after circling.
  - **Banyan / fig-seed (6.12)** — Uddālaka has Śvetaketu split a **nyagrodha (banyan) fruit**, then a seed, then asks what is inside the broken seed: "nothing." "That subtle essence you cannot perceive — from that very essence this great tree exists. Believe it: *tat tvam asi*." *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 6.12)*
  - **Salt in water (6.13)** — salt dropped in water vanishes from sight but pervades every drop by taste; so Being, though imperceptible, is wholly present everywhere and is the Self. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 6.13)*
  - **Honey / bees & rivers (6.9–6.10)** — bees gather nectar from many trees into one honey that no longer "knows" its separate sources; rivers flowing east and west lose their names in the ocean — so beings, merged into Being, lose individuality yet are not annihilated. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 6.9–6.10)*
  - **Blindfolded man / Gandhāra (6.14)** — a man led blindfold from Gandhāra and abandoned in the wild finds his way home once someone removes the blindfold and points the direction; the guru is that guide to the Self.
  - **The dying man (6.15)** and the **ordeal by hot axe (6.16)** — truth (Sat) saves as the innocent man grasping the heated axe is unburnt; capping the *tat tvam asi* sequence.
  - *Variant (sampradāya reading):* Advaita (Śaṅkara) reads *tat tvam asi* as a statement of **absolute identity** (jīva = Brahman). **Rāmānuja** (Viśiṣṭādvaita) reads it as identity-in-difference (Brahman as inner Self of all). **Madhva** (Dvaita) parses it **"sa-ātmyam"** / *atat tvam asi* — "thou art not That" / "thou belongest to That," preserving jīva–Brahman difference. [sampradāya-disputed]

## 3. The Udgītha & Om Meditations (Ch. 1–2)

- **Om as Udgītha (1.1)** — the Upaniṣad opens: *"Om ity etad akṣaram udgītham upāsīta"* — "One should meditate on the syllable **Om**, the Udgītha." Om is named the **essence (rasa) of essences**: the essence of beings is earth, of earth water, of water plants, of plants man, of man speech, of speech the Ṛc, of the Ṛc the Sāman, and the essence of the Sāman is the **Udgītha (Om)**. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 1.1)*
- **Devas vs. Asuras / the breath-organs (1.2)** — gods and demons contend; the gods meditate on each sense-faculty (speech, smell, eye, ear, mind) as Udgītha but each is "pierced with evil" by the Asuras. Only the **mukhya prāṇa (vital breath in the mouth)** is unpierceable — it shatters the demons "as a clod hurled on rock." Prāṇa is thus the true Udgītha. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 1.2)* — compare the Kauṣītaki prāṇa-doctrine noted in [`principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).
- **Cosmic correspondences of the chant (1.3–1.13)** — Om mapped to sun, breath, and the five-/seven-fold structure of the sāman; mystic glosses on the chant's parts.
- **"Udgītha of the dogs" satire (1.12)** — the sage **Baka Dālbhya** (Glāva Maitreya) sees dogs file in like priests chanting "Om, let us eat! Om, let us drink!" — a pointed satire on **greedy, mechanical priestcraft** and a reminder the chant must be inwardly understood. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 1.12)* [notable for its rare comic register in Śruti]
- **The threefold dharma (2.23)** — *"Trayo dharma-skandhāḥ"*: three branches of duty are **yajña (sacrifice), svādhyāya (study/recitation), and dāna (charity)** — austerity (tapas) is a fourth path; the brahmacārin who dwells lifelong in the teacher's house is highest. A **forerunner of the later āśrama (life-stage) theory**. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 2.23)* — see [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md).

## 4. The Honey Doctrine, Śāṇḍilya-vidyā & "Sarvam khalv idam Brahma" (Ch. 3)

- **Madhu-vidyā — the Honey Doctrine (3.1–3.11)** — the **sun is the honey of the gods**; the sky is the cross-beam of the hive, the atmosphere the comb, the sun-rays the brood-cells, and the **four Vedas (Ṛg, Yajus, Sāman, Atharva) are the flowers** whose nectar (the hymns) the gods feed on. The sun, viewed esoterically, "neither rises nor sets" for the knower — who attains perpetual day in Brahman. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 3.1–3.11)*
- **Gāyatrī as Brahman (3.12)** — the **Gāyatrī metre** is glossed as all that exists; "whatever is, is the Gāyatrī," which is Brahman, four-footed and beyond the cosmos.
- **Śāṇḍilya-vidyā & the great saying (3.14)** — the section opens with **"Sarvaṃ khalv idaṃ Brahma"** — *"All this, verily, is Brahman"* — from which it all springs, into which it dissolves, by which it breathes. The sage **Śāṇḍilya** teaches: the Self within the heart (*antar-hṛdaye*) is "smaller than a grain of rice... yet greater than the earth, the sky, the heavens, all worlds"; it is the **manomaya, prāṇa-śarīra Self** of golden light. "This is my Self within the heart; this is Brahman; into it I shall enter on departing hence." *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 3.14)*
  - This passage is a **chief Brahma-Sūtra adhikaraṇa** (the "Śāṇḍilya-vidyā"); paired with the dahara-vidyā of ch. 8 as the two great "Self-in-the-heart" meditations.
- **Krishna Devakīputra (3.17)** — life is figured as a **soma-sacrifice**; ethics (including **ahiṃsā**, tapas, dāna, ārjava=uprightness, satya) are its ritual fees (dakṣiṇā). The section names **"Kṛṣṇa, son of Devakī"** receiving instruction from the sage **Ghora Āṅgirasa** — one of the **earliest textual mentions of Kṛṣṇa**, of interest to historians of the later Bhagavad-Gītā tradition. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 3.17)* — cf. [`../../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md`](../../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md). [much-discussed; identification with Gītā-Kṛṣṇa is disputed]

## 5. Satyakāma Jābāla & the Upakosala Story (Ch. 4)

- **Saṃvarga-vidyā — Jānaśruti & Raikva (4.1–4.3)** — the proud, wealthy king **Jānaśruti Pautrāyaṇa** overhears flamingoes praise the poor cart-man sage **Raikva**, "in whom all merit converges." Jānaśruti seeks him out; Raikva (who calls the king "Śūdra!") finally teaches the **saṃvarga-vidyā**: **air (Vāyu) and breath (prāṇa) as the "absorbers"** into which fire, sun, moon, water dissolve. Knowledge, not wealth, is supreme. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 4.1–4.3)*
- **Satyakāma Jābāla (4.4–4.9)** — the boy **Satyakāma** wishes to study; asked his lineage (gotra), he honestly reports his mother **Jabālā** told him she conceived him "moving about as a servant in my youth" and does not know the father — "so I am Satyakāma Jābāla." The teacher **Gautama (Hāridrumata)** replies: *"None but a true brāhmaṇa could speak so frankly; your honesty proves your lineage,"* and accepts him. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 4.4)* — a cornerstone text on **truth-telling (satya) and merit over birth**; widely cited that spiritual fitness rests on character, not caste.
  - Sent to herd 400 lean cattle, Satyakāma is taught the four **quarters (pādas) of Brahman** by a **bull, fire (Agni), a flamingo (haṃsa), and a diver-bird (madgu)** — the Self as the Radiant, the Endless, the Luminous, the Abode. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 4.5–4.8)*
- **Upakosala Kāmalāyana (4.10–4.15)** — Satyakāma's own pupil, neglected, is taught by the **three sacred fires** (Gārhapatya, Anvāhārya/Dakṣiṇāgni, Āhavanīya) that "**prāṇa is Brahman, joy (ka) is Brahman, space (kha) is Brahman**." The teacher then reveals the **"person in the eye" (akṣi-puruṣa)** as the Self, immortal and fearless, and describes the **path of the gods (devayāna)** leading the liberated through the worlds of fire, day, the bright fortnight, the northern sun, to Brahman — *"and they do not return."* *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 4.15)*

## 6. Pañcāgni-vidyā & the Two Paths of Rebirth (Ch. 5)

- **The contending organs (5.1–5.2)** — the fable of speech, eye, ear, mind, and breath each departing the body to prove its supremacy; when **prāṇa** stirs to leave, all others fail — so prāṇa is **"jyeṣṭha and śreṣṭha"** (eldest and best). *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 5.1)*
- **The setting (5.3)** — **Śvetaketu** (now grown) goes to the assembly of the **Pañcāla king Pravāhaṇa Jaivali**, who poses **five questions** Śvetaketu cannot answer: Where do creatures go after death? How do they return? Why is the "other world" never filled? ...His father **Uddālaka** also cannot answer, so both go to the king — a notable instance of **kṣatriya transmission of esoteric knowledge to brāhmaṇas**. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 5.3)*
- **Pañcāgni-vidyā — the Five Fires (5.4–5.9)** — rebirth modeled as oblations in **five cosmic fires (agni)**, the soul (clothed in subtle "water" / śraddhā) passing through each: **(1) the heavenly world** (dyu-loka; offering = faith → Soma/moon); **(2) the rain-cloud** (Parjanya; offering = Soma → rain); **(3) the earth** (pṛthivī; offering = rain → food); **(4) man** (puruṣa; offering = food → semen); **(5) woman** (yoṣā; offering = semen → the fetus/birth). The soul thus cycles **moon → rain → plant → food → seed → womb**. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 5.4–5.9)* — a foundational saṃsāra mechanism; cf. [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/narakas-and-afterlife.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/narakas-and-afterlife.md).
- **The Two Paths (Devayāna / Pitṛyāna) (5.10)** —
  - **Devayāna ("path of the gods")** — those of faith and meditative knowledge pass at death through **flame → day → bright fortnight → the six months of the northern sun (uttarāyaṇa) → the year → sun → moon → lightning**, led by a non-human being **to Brahman; from there they do not return** (no rebirth). *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 5.10.1–2)*
  - **Pitṛyāna ("path of the fathers")** — those who pursue ritual, charity and social works pass through **smoke → night → dark fortnight → the six months of the southern sun (dakṣiṇāyana) → the realm of the fathers → moon**; they dwell there as Soma-food of the gods until merit exhausts, then **return** as rain → become rice, barley, herbs, trees, sesame, beans — and re-enter wombs according to conduct. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 5.10.3–6)*
  - **The "third place"** — those of neither path become **"small recurring creatures"** (insects: "be born and die"), a perpetual lowly cycle. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 5.10.8)*
  - *Note:* the same two-paths doctrine appears in **Bṛhadāraṇyaka 6.2** (the Pañcāgni / Jaivali parallel) and is echoed in **Bhagavad-Gītā 8.23–26** (śukla/kṛṣṇa gati). [cross-text parallel]
- **Vaiśvānara-vidyā (5.11–5.24)** — six learned householders, unable to define the universal Self, consult king **Aśvapati Kaikeya**, who teaches **Ātman Vaiśvānara** — the cosmic Self whose head is the heavens, eye the sun, breath the wind, trunk space, bladder water, feet the earth — "the One in the Many," worshipped also in the digestive fire and the daily meal (prāṇāgni-hotra). *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 5.11–5.18)*

## 7. Sanatkumāra–Nārada & the Bhūman (Ch. 7)

- **The setting (7.1)** — the sage **Nārada** approaches **Sanatkumāra** ("Skanda") confessing: "I know the Vedas, all the sciences, grammar, mathematics, logic, ethics, the arts — but I know only the texts (mantravid), not the Self (ātmavid); I have heard the knower of the Self crosses sorrow. I am in sorrow; lead me across." *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 7.1)*
- **The ascending ladder (7.1–7.15)** — Sanatkumāra leads Nārada up a graded hierarchy, each rung "greater than" the last: **Name → Speech → Mind → Will (saṅkalpa) → Thought (citta) → Meditation (dhyāna) → Understanding (vijñāna) → Strength (bala) → Food → Water → Heat (tejas) → Space (ākāśa) → Memory → Hope → Prāṇa (vital breath)**. One who knows prāṇa is an **ativādin** — "one who speaks of the higher." *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 7.1–7.15)*
- **Bhūman — the Infinite/Plenitude (7.16–7.26)** — beyond all, **"yo vai bhūmā tat sukham"** — *"the Infinite (bhūman) alone is bliss; in the finite (alpa) there is no happiness."* The Bhūman is **"where one sees nothing else, hears nothing else, knows nothing else"** — undivided, immortal, resting on its own greatness. It is **"below, above, behind, before, to the right, to the left — it is the whole; I am all this; the Self (Ātman) is all this."** The knower becomes **svarāj** (self-ruling, sovereign in all worlds). *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 7.23–7.25)* — Śaṅkara: the turning point is the leap from finite plural objects to the partless Self.

## 8. Dahara-vidyā — Space in the Heart (Ch. 8)

- **Brahmapura — the city of Brahman (8.1)** — *"In this city of Brahman (the body) is a dwelling, a small lotus; within it a small space (dahara ākāśa). What is within that, one should seek."* When asked what is inside so tiny a space, the answer: **"as vast as this whole cosmos is contained within it"** — heaven and earth, fire and wind, sun and moon, all that one has and has not. The body decays but **this inner space does not age or die** — it is the Self (Ātman), free of evil, ageless, deathless, sorrowless, hunger- and thirst-less, of true desire and true resolve. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 8.1)* — the classic **dahara-vidyā**; pairs with the Śāṇḍilya-vidyā (3.14) and is a major Brahma-Sūtra topic.
- **True desires (satya-kāma) (8.2–8.3)** — the Self's "true desires" are real but covered by falsehood (anṛta); the man who finds the Ātman within attains every world and all desires.
- **Indra & Virocana with Prajāpati (8.7–8.12)** — gods and demons each send a pupil — **Indra** and **Virocana** — to learn the Self from **Prajāpati**, who tests them over a **101-year discipleship** through four answers: (1) the **bodily self** (reflection in water/eye) — Virocana takes this and demons thus "worship the body"; (2) the **dreaming self**; (3) the **deep-sleep self**; rejecting each, Indra alone persists to (4) the **true Self** — the serene one (saṃprasāda) who rises above the body "as a horse shakes off its hair," the bodiless immortal light. *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 8.7–8.12)* — a key passage on the **four states of the self**, anticipating the Māṇḍūkya's waking/dream/sleep/turīya.
- **Close (8.15)** — the disciple, having completed study, marries, raises a household upholding ahiṃsā, and at life's end **"reaches the world of Brahman and does not return — does not return."** *(Source: Chāndogya Up. 8.15)*

## 9. Why the Chāndogya Matters

- **Twin pillar of Vedānta** — supplies **two of the four mahāvākyas' weight** (Tat tvam asi, and arguably the Sad-vidyā substrate), the Pañcāgni rebirth model, and the most-cited "Self-in-the-heart" meditations — making it, with Bṛhadāraṇyaka, the empirical bedrock of the Brahma-Sūtra.
- **Sampradāya battleground** — *Tat tvam asi* is the single verse over which **Advaita, Viśiṣṭādvaita and Dvaita** most sharply diverge; see [`../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/adi-shankara.md`](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/adi-shankara.md) and [`../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/ramanuja-and-madhva.md`](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/ramanuja-and-madhva.md).
- **Open-ended rosters flagged** — the precise khaṇḍa-counts per chapter vary slightly by recension/edition; the identification of Chāndogya's **Kṛṣṇa Devakīputra** with the Bhagavad-Gītā's Kṛṣṇa is a **scholarly open question**, not settled. [disputed]
