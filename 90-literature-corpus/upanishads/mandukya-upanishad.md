## 1. Identity, Placement, Dating

- **Parent Veda & name** — The Māṇḍūkya belongs to the **Atharvaveda**. The name is usually traced to the *maṇḍūka* ("frog") — variously read as a ṛṣi-lineage (Māṇḍūka Gārgya / the Māṇḍūkeya śākhā) or as an allusion to the "frog-leap" by which awareness moves between the states of consciousness. *(Source: Māṇḍūkya Up.; traditional etymologies)*
- **Scale** — The **shortest of the principal (mukhya) Upaniṣads**: just **12 short prose verses (mantras)**. Listed **6th** in the **Daśopaniṣad** (the ten Śaṅkara commented on), in the mnemonic *"Īśa-Kena-Kaṭha-Praśna-Muṇḍa-Māṇḍūkya-Taittiri..."*. *(Source: Daśopaniṣad mnemonic; Muktikā Up.)*
- **Dating (two clocks)** —
  - *Academic:* **late prose stratum**, generally placed **around the start of the Common Era** (c. 1st c. BCE–2nd c. CE), among the latest of the principal Upaniṣads — Olivelle groups it with Praśna as late prose. Some scholars (e.g. analyses noting its terseness and its absorption of state-of-consciousness analysis already present in Bṛhadāraṇyaka) place it slightly earlier. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (orthodox / traditional):* eternal apauruṣeya Śruti, "seen" (not authored) by ṛṣis and re-compiled by **Vyāsa** at the Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; see Kali-Yuga onset constant); not a "BCE-stratum composition." This file records both frames and corrects neither. [scripture vs. academic dating]
  - *Note on the Kārikā:* **Gauḍapāda's Māṇḍūkya-kārikā** is academically dated **c. 6th–7th c. CE** (Gauḍapāda being, by tradition, the *parama-guru* — teacher's teacher — of Ādi Śaṅkara). [scholarly]
- **Mahāvākya** — **Ayam ātmā Brahma** ("This Self is Brahman," v.2) — the **mahāvākya assigned to the Atharvaveda** in the fourfold Vedānta scheme; it also appears at Bṛhadāraṇyaka 2.5.19. *(Source: Māṇḍūkya Up. 2)* — catalogued with the other three great sayings in [`principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md) and unpacked in [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md).
- **Commentaries** — **Ādi Śaṅkara** (c. 8th c. CE) wrote a **bhāṣya** that treats the **Upaniṣad and Gauḍapāda's Kārikā as a single continuous text**, commenting verse-by-verse on both; this is one of his ten canonical Upaniṣad commentaries and a primary Advaita proof-text. **Ānandagiri** wrote a sub-commentary (ṭīkā). *(Source: Śaṅkara's Māṇḍūkya-kārikā-bhāṣya)*

## 2. The 12 Verses — Structure & Content

The 12 mantras move from a declaration about Oṃ, through the systematic analysis of the four states (one verse per state), to the nature of the syllable's "measures" (mātrās).

- **Verse 1 — Oṃ is all** — *"Om ity etad akṣaram idaṁ sarvam"* — "Oṃ — this syllable is all this. Its further explanation: all that is past, present, and future is verily Oṃ; and whatever transcends the three times, that too is Oṃ." Oṃ is set up as the audible symbol of the whole of reality, temporal and trans-temporal. *(Source: Māṇḍūkya Up. 1)* — compare Oṃ-as-Udgītha in [`chandogya-upanishad.md`](./chandogya-upanishad.md) §3.
- **Verse 2 — the foundational equation** — *"Sarvaṁ hy etad brahma, ayam ātmā brahma, so 'yam ātmā catuṣpāt"* — "All this is verily Brahman. This Self (Ātman) is Brahman. This Self has **four quarters (catuṣpāt)**." The Self is now to be examined in its four "feet" / quarters. *(Source: Māṇḍūkya Up. 2)*
- **Verse 3 — first quarter: Vaiśvānara (waking)** — the waking state (**jāgrat**); consciousness turned **outward** (*bahiḥ-prajña*); enjoyer of gross objects through seven limbs and nineteen mouths; called **Vaiśvānara** ("common to all men") or **Viśva**. *(Source: Māṇḍūkya Up. 3)*
- **Verse 4 — second quarter: Taijasa (dream)** — the dream state (**svapna**); consciousness turned **inward** (*antaḥ-prajña*); enjoyer of subtle objects (the impressions/vāsanās of waking); called **Taijasa** ("the luminous," made of light/mind). *(Source: Māṇḍūkya Up. 4)*
- **Verse 5 — third quarter: Prājña (deep sleep)** — the deep dreamless sleep state (**suṣupti**); a state where "one desires no desire and sees no dream," a **mass of consciousness (prajñāna-ghana)**, blissful, the **gateway** (*dvāra*) to the other two states; called **Prājña** ("the knower," potential/causal consciousness). *(Source: Māṇḍūkya Up. 5)*
- **Verses 6–7 — the fourth: Turīya** — verse 6 calls Prājña "the lord of all, the knower of all, the inner controller, the source of all." Verse 7 then defines the **Fourth (Turīya / caturtha)** by negation: *"not inward-conscious, not outward-conscious, not both, not a mass of consciousness, not conscious, not unconscious; unseen, unrelated, ungraspable, indefinable, unthinkable, indescribable; the essence of the knowledge of the one Self; the cessation of all phenomena (prapañcopaśama); tranquil (śānta), auspicious (śiva), non-dual (advaita)."* This is held to be the Self to be realized. *(Source: Māṇḍūkya Up. 6–7)* — see the consciousness-states framing cross-linked at [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/README.md`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/README.md).
- **Verses 8–11 — Oṃ and its mātrās (measures)** — verse 8 states the Self **is** Oṃ "from the standpoint of the syllable," its quarters being the **mātrās**: **A** = Vaiśvānara, **U** = Taijasa, **M** = Prājña. Verses 9–11 give the meditative fruit of each letter (the A-knower obtains all desires and becomes first; the U-knower exalts the flow of knowledge and is impartial; the M-knower measures-and-merges the universe). *(Source: Māṇḍūkya Up. 8–11)*
- **Verse 12 — the amātra (measureless) fourth** — *"the partless (amātra) Oṃ is the Fourth — beyond conventional usage, the cessation of phenomena, auspicious, non-dual. Thus Oṃ is verily the Self. He who knows this enters the Self by the Self."* The **silence after AUM** (the soundless "fourth measure") = **Turīya**. *(Source: Māṇḍūkya Up. 12)*

## 3. The Four States ↔ AUM Mapping (Reference Table)

| Quarter | State (avasthā) | Self / "person" | Field of experience | AUM mātrā |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st (pāda) | **Jāgrat** — waking | **Vaiśvānara / Viśva** | gross, outward objects | **A** (आ) |
| 2nd | **Svapna** — dream | **Taijasa** | subtle, inward (mental impressions) | **U** (उ) |
| 3rd | **Suṣupti** — deep sleep | **Prājña** | undifferentiated bliss; causal/seed | **M** (म) |
| 4th | **Turīya** — "the fourth" | the Ātman itself | non-dual awareness, *prapañcopaśama* | **amātra** (the silence) |

- **The teaching device** — the three ordinary states are common experience; their shared, ever-present **witness** is Turīya, neither produced by nor lost in any of them. Turīya is not a fifth experience added to the four but the **substratum/background** in which the three arise and dissolve. *(Source: Māṇḍūkya Up. 7, 12; Śaṅkara bhāṣya)*
- **Turīyātīta note** — later Advaita and some yogic traditions speak of *turīyātīta* ("beyond the fourth"); the Māṇḍūkya itself stops at Turīya as the non-dual Self. [late text / sampradāya elaboration]

## 4. Gauḍapāda's Māṇḍūkya-Kārikā (Gauḍapādīya-Kārikā / Āgama-Śāstra)

- **What it is** — **215 metrical verses (kārikās)** in four chapters (**prakaraṇas**), traditionally **appended to and read with** the Upaniṣad. It is the **earliest extant systematic treatise of Advaita Vedānta** — the doctrine stated in argued, philosophical form **before Śaṅkara**, whose *parama-guru* Gauḍapāda is by tradition. Also titled the **Āgama-śāstra**. *(Source: Gauḍapāda, Māṇḍūkya-kārikā)*
- **Prakaraṇa 1 — Āgama (Tradition), 29 verses** — the **only chapter that directly glosses the Upaniṣad's mantras**; expounds the four quarters/AUM analysis and frames the scriptural basis. The remaining three chapters are Gauḍapāda's **independent philosophical compositions**. *(Source: Kārikā I)*
- **Prakaraṇa 2 — Vaitathya (Unreality / "false-ness"), 38 verses** — argues that the **waking world is as unreal as the dream world**: both are objects appearing within consciousness, both sublated, neither self-existent. Establishes the illusory status of duality. *(Source: Kārikā II)*
- **Prakaraṇa 3 — Advaita (Non-duality), 48 verses** — the positive doctrine: only **non-dual consciousness (Ātman = Brahman)** is real; the apparent jīva and world are like space enclosed in pots (*ghaṭākāśa*) — seemingly many, really the one undivided space. Introduces the *asparśa-yoga* ("touchless / contactless union"), yoga free of all relation. *(Source: Kārikā III)*
- **Prakaraṇa 4 — Alātaśānti ("Quenching of the Firebrand"), 100 verses** — the longest chapter; a sustained dialectical demolition of causation and origination. Its title supplies the famous **firebrand (alāta) image** (see §5). Notably uses **Buddhist (esp. Madhyamaka/Yogācāra) vocabulary** — *dharma, saṁsāra, ajāti, nirvāṇa-like quiescence* — which has fueled the "crypto-Buddhist" debate; yet doctrinally Gauḍapāda affirms a **permanent non-dual Self**, which Mahāyāna denies, so the work is read as Vedāntic in purport. [scholarly-disputed] *(Source: Kārikā IV)*

## 5. Ajāta-vāda (Non-origination) & Key Doctrines

- **Ajāta-vāda / Ajāti-vāda** — Gauḍapāda's signature thesis: **nothing is ever really born (a-jāta, "unborn")**. There is no genuine origination, change, bondage, seeker, or liberation — these belong to the realm of appearance only; from the standpoint of the absolute, the non-dual Self alone *is*, eternally unborn and changeless. This is the **strongest form of non-dualism** in the tradition, even sharper than Śaṅkara's vivarta-vāda. *(Source: Kārikā III–IV)*
- **The firebrand (alāta-cakra)** — a glowing brand whirled in the dark produces apparent circles, lines, and shapes of fire; **no such figures are really created or destroyed** — they are the one fire appearing as many through motion. So **consciousness** appears as the manifold of subject-object experience without any real arising of plurality; when the "motion" (avidyā/vikalpa) stills, the appearances vanish, revealing changeless awareness. *(Source: Kārikā IV, Alātaśānti)*
- **Asparśa-yoga** — the "touchless yoga," the discipline of resting as the relationless Self that never contacts (and so is never bound by) objects; Gauḍapāda calls it hard for ordinary yogins because it abandons even the subtle duality of meditator-and-meditation. *(Source: Kārikā III)*
- **Dream–waking parity** — a recurring argument: because dream objects are "real" within the dream and unreal on waking, and waking objects are likewise consciousness-dependent and sublatable (in deep sleep, in jñāna), **no ontological privilege** separates waking from dream. *(Source: Kārikā II)*

## 6. The Muktikā Claim — "The Māṇḍūkya Alone"

- **The dialogue** — in the **Muktikā Upaniṣad** (a late Upaniṣad framed as Rāma instructing Hanumān on mukti), Rāma lays out a **graded path of study** among the 108 Upaniṣads and singles out the Māṇḍūkya. *(Source: Muktikā Up. 1.26–29)*
- **The famous verse** — *"māṇḍūkyam ekam eva alaṁ mumukṣūṇāṁ vimuktaye"* — **"The Māṇḍūkya alone is enough for the liberation of the seeker."** If knowledge does not arise even then, one should study the **ten (Daśopaniṣad)**; failing that, the **thirty-two**; and one who still seeks release as death nears should study the full **108**. *(Source: Muktikā Up. 1.26–29)*
- **Traditional reading** — "sufficient" is taken to mean the Māṇḍūkya (with Gauḍapāda's Kārikā and Śaṅkara's bhāṣya) delivers **direct knowledge (jñāna) of Turīya**, the non-dual Self — not a gradual practice but the immediate recognition of one's own nature — hence no further text is strictly needed for the qualified aspirant. *(Source: Śaṅkara's intro; Advaita tradition)* [traditional]

## 7. Cross-Links & Significance

- **Charter text of Advaita** — the Māṇḍūkya + Kārikā is the **seedbed of classical Advaita Vedānta**: ajāta-vāda, the four-state/Turīya schema, and the world-as-appearance thesis all pass from Gauḍapāda → Śaṅkara into the mainstream non-dual tradition. See the Advaita lineage in [`../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/adi-shankara.md`](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/adi-shankara.md) and the darśana framing in [`../../05-concepts-and-dharma/atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md`](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md).
- **Consciousness-states schema** — the jāgrat / svapna / suṣupti / turīya analysis is the canonical Hindu map of the **states of consciousness**; cross-link the cosmology section's treatment of states and time in [`../../00-time-and-cosmology/README.md`](../../00-time-and-cosmology/README.md).
- **Oṃ / AUM symbology** — the syllable's letter-by-letter meditation connects to the broader Praṇava theology; the **Praśna Upaniṣad's** 5th question on Oṃ with one/two/three mātrās is its closest sibling — both Atharvan. See [`principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md) and Oṃ-as-Udgītha in [`chandogya-upanishad.md`](./chandogya-upanishad.md).
- **Open roster flag** — minor/sectarian "Māṇḍūkya"-adjacent or Oṃ-meditation Upaniṣads exist in the late Atharvan corpus; this file treats only the **mukhya Māṇḍūkya + Gauḍapāda Kārikā**. Rosters of minor Atharvan Upaniṣads are open-ended. [folk / late text]
