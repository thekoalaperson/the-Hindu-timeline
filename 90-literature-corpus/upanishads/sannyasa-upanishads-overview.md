# The Sannyāsa Upaniṣads

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/upanishads/`](./README.md) — an overview and catalogue of the **Sannyāsa (renunciation) group** of the **Muktikā** canon: the ~17–19 Upaniṣads that form the primary **Śruti charter of Hindu monasticism**. They define the rite of taking *sannyāsa*, the typology of renouncers (*paramahaṃsa*, *avadhūta*, etc.), monastic *dharma*, and the doctrinal underpinning of the **Daśanāmī** orders. Frame-files: [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md) (the *mukhya* core); [`../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) (the four Vedas, four strata, and the cosmic clock); [`../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md`](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md) (the Vedānta darśana these texts feed). Master index: [`../../README.md`](../../README.md).

## 1. What the Group Is

- **A thematic, not Vedic, cluster.** Of the **108 Upaniṣads** of the **Muktikā** list, a subset of roughly **17–19** is classed as *Sannyāsa Upaniṣads* — grouped by **subject (renunciation)** rather than by parent Veda (their nominal Vedic attributions are scattered across all four Vedas). *(Source: Muktikā Up.; Olivelle, *Saṃnyāsa Upaniṣads* 1992)*
- **Count varies.** Lists give **17, 18, or 19** depending on which borderline texts (e.g. *Brahma*, *Yājñavalkya*, *Śāṭyāyanīya*, *Parabrahma*, *Maṇḍala-brāhmaṇa*) are admitted. The commonly cited core is about a dozen. [count varies by source]
- **Mostly minor and late.** None belongs to the ten/twelve *mukhya* set on which the classical *bhāṣyas* were written; they are **"minor" Upaniṣads**, several visibly **post-classical**, composed (academically) across a long span from a few centuries BCE down into the **early/mid 1st millennium CE and later**. [scholarly] [late text]
- **The one exception in citation-weight.** Within this corpus, only the **Jābāla Upaniṣad** is ever actually quoted — for its ruling on **immediate renunciation** (one may renounce *the very day* dispassion dawns). The rest are catalogued here for completeness, not because the corpus leans on them.

## 2. The Rite and Doctrine They Codify

- **The act of saṃnyāsa.** These texts script the **renunciation rite (praiṣa)**: the candidate performs his own symbolic **funeral / ātma-śrāddha**, recites the *praiṣa* formula renouncing all beings' fear of him (*"abhayaṃ sarva-bhūtebhyo mattaḥ"* — "from me, let all beings be free of fear"), and discards the sacred fire, sacred thread, and tuft. *(Source: Sannyāsa group, esp. Jābāla & Āruṇi Up.)*
- **The threefold staff and beyond.** They classify renouncers partly by the **staff (daṇḍa)** carried — the **tridaṇḍin** (three-staffed) vs. **ekadaṇḍin** (single-staffed) distinction that later sorts **Vaiṣṇava** from **Advaita/Daśanāmī** ascetics. *(Source: Nāradaparivrājaka & Bhikṣuka Up.)*
- **The four (and six) kinds of renouncer.** The most influential schema (esp. *Bhikṣuka* and *Nāradaparivrājaka*) grades ascetics by spiritual attainment:
  - *Kuṭīcaka* — dwells in a hut, still eats at family/relatives' homes, carries the triple staff.
  - *Bahūdaka* — wanders, begs from virtuous houses, ochre robe and triple staff.
  - *Haṃsa* — a more advanced, perpetually wandering ascetic.
  - *Paramahaṃsa* — the highest grade, beyond marks and rules, single staff or none.
  - *Variant (six-fold expansion):* later texts add **Turīyātīta** and **Avadhūta** above the *paramahaṃsa*, giving six grades — the *avadhūta* having "cast off" (ava-dhū) all bonds and conventions entirely. *(Source: Nāradaparivrājaka & Bṛhat-Sannyāsa Up.)* [late text]
- **Monastic dharma.** They prescribe the renouncer's conduct: **possessionlessness**, **begged single meal**, ceaseless **wandering (parivrājya)** except the **four-month rains-retreat (cāturmāsya)**, **non-attachment**, constant meditation on *Oṃ* and the *mahāvākyas*, and the inner ideal that the true *sannyāsin* has internalised all ritual fire. *(Source: Sannyāsa group passim)*
- **Charter of the Daśanāmī orders.** This corpus supplies the **Śruti backing** for the monastic system traditionally organised under **Ādi Śaṅkara** — the **Daśanāmī ("ten-named") ekadaṇḍin** sannyāsins and their **maṭha**-network. The Upaniṣads predate/underlie the order; the order reads itself back into them. *(Source: Sannyāsa group; Daśanāmī tradition)* [traditional]

## 3. Catalogue of the Key Texts

- **Jābāla Upaniṣad (Jābālopaniṣad)** — attached to the **Śukla Yajurveda**; the **most important and earliest** of the group, and **the only one cited in this corpus**. A dialogue chain (Bṛhaspati–Yājñavalkya; Atri, Janaka, and others question Yājñavalkya). *(Source: Jābāla Up.)*
  - **Immediate renunciation** — its signature ruling: *"yad ahar eva virajet tad ahar eva pravrajet"* — **"the very day one becomes detached, that very day let him go forth,"** dispensing with the orthodox sequence of life-stages (āśramas). [the load-bearing teaching]
  - Also teaches **Avimukta** (Vārāṇasī / the spot between the brows) as the seat of liberation, and the **praiṣa** renunciation formula.
- **Āruṇi / Āruṇeya Upaniṣad** — nominally **Sāmaveda**; among the **oldest** Sannyāsa texts. The sage **Āruṇi (Uddālaka)** asks **Prajāpati** how to abandon all rites; the answer details the **physical relinquishments** of renunciation (giving up sons, wealth, sacred thread, study). *(Source: Āruṇeya Up.)*
- **Paramahaṃsa Upaniṣad** — nominally **Śukla Yajurveda**; **Nārada** questions **Prajāpati** on the *paramahaṃsa*, the supreme renouncer who has cast off staff, tuft, and thread and wears only **knowledge** as his mark. *(Source: Paramahaṃsa Up.)*
- **Nāradaparivrājaka Upaniṣad** ("Nārada the Wanderer") — nominally **Atharvaveda**; one of the **longest and most systematic**, in nine *upadeśas*. A full **manual of the wandering monk**: eligibility, the renunciation ceremony, the grades of ascetic, permitted possessions, and conduct. *(Source: Nāradaparivrājaka Up.)* [late text]
- **Kaṭhaśruti Upaniṣad** (also **Kaṭhaśruta**) — nominally **Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda** (Kaṭha school); a short prose text on the **renunciation rite and the disposal of the sacred fires** by the renouncer. *(Source: Kaṭhaśruti Up.)*
  - *Note — easily confused (catalogue caveat):* distinguish the **Kaṭhaśruti** (a Sannyāsa text) from the **Kaṭha-rudra** Upaniṣad (a separate short Yajurvedic text) and from the *mukhya* **Kaṭha Upaniṣad** (the Naciketas dialogue, treated in [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md)). [disputed/confused naming]
- **Nirvāṇa Upaniṣad** — nominally **Ṛgveda**; a terse, **aphoristic** text whose cryptic *sūtra*-like lines describe the inner state of the **paramahaṃsa**: his monastery is solitude, his doctrine non-duality, his begging-bowl the cosmos. *(Source: Nirvāṇa Up.)* [late text]
- **Bhikṣuka Upaniṣad** ("the Mendicant") — nominally **Śukla Yajurveda**; very short, and the **locus classicus for the four grades** (*kuṭīcaka, bahūdaka, haṃsa, paramahaṃsa*), naming each and its mode of life. *(Source: Bhikṣuka Up.)*
- **Other texts of the group (briefly):**
  - *Āśrama Upaniṣad (Āśramopaniṣad):* enumerates the **four āśramas** and their sub-types, including four kinds of ascetic. *(Source: Āśrama Up.)*
  - *Jābāladarśana / Maṇḍala-brāhmaṇa / Advayatāraka:* yoga-leaning renunciation texts blending **aṣṭāṅga / nāda / taraka-yoga** with the *paramahaṃsa* ideal. *(Source: respective Up.)* [late text]
  - *Turīyātīta-avadhūta Upaniṣad:* defines the **avadhūta / turīyātīta**, the renouncer "beyond the fourth state," free of all convention. *(Source: Turīyātītāvadhūta Up.)* [late text]
  - *Yājñavalkya, Śāṭyāyanīya, Paramahaṃsa-parivrājaka, Kuṇḍika, Brahma, Saṃnyāsa (Bṛhat-/Laghu-):* further short charters of the renouncing life, several admitting **householder-to-monk** transitions and re-stating the *praiṣa* and the four/six grades. *(Source: respective Up.)* [count varies]

## 4. The Two Clocks

- *Academic / text-historical:* the group spans a **wide range** — the **Jābāla** and **Āruṇi** may reach back to the **last centuries BCE** (the Jābāla is cited by name in later literature and is among the older minor Upaniṣads), while the **yoga-** and **avadhūta-**flavoured texts are demonstrably **medieval**, some perhaps as late as the 2nd millennium CE. The corpus as a whole charts the **historical growth of organised Hindu monasticism**. [scholarly]
- *Orthodox / traditional:* all are **eternal, apauruṣeya Śruti** "seen" by the ṛṣis and fixed at the **Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; see Kali-Yuga onset constant)** — not "authored" in datable strata. The renunciation they describe is timeless *mokṣa*-dharma, not a historical institution. Both clocks are recorded here; neither is corrected. [scripture vs. academic dating]
- **Placement on the timeline.** Because their *content* (the renunciation rite, the wandering monk) is **scripturally framed** but their *composition* is **historically datable**, they sit squarely in the **Kali-Yuga "scriptural" band** of this knowledge base — narrative material whose internal voice is timeless but whose textual reality belongs to the post-Vedic age. [two clocks]
