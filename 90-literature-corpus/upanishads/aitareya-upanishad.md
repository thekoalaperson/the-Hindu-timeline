# Aitareya Upaniṣad

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/upanishads/`](./README.md) — a per-text deep-dive into the **Aitareya Upaniṣad**, the **Ṛgveda's representative in the principal (mukhya) set**, embedded in the **Aitareya Āraṇyaka** (chapters 4–6 of the second Āraṇyaka, i.e. Āraṇyaka II, adhyāyas 4–6 → the Upaniṣad's three adhyāyas). It carries the Ṛgveda's great saying (mahāvākya) *Prajñānaṃ Brahma* ("Consciousness is Brahman"), an Ātman-as-sole-creator cosmogony, the doctrine of **threefold birth**, and the entry of the Self into the body through the **suture of the skull**. Parent Veda: [`../vedas/rigveda.md`](../vedas/rigveda.md); parent Brāhmaṇa of the same school: [`../vedas/brahmanas/aitareya-brahmana.md`](../vedas/brahmanas/aitareya-brahmana.md). Cross-text mukhya overview: [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md). For the Śruti two-clocks framing: [`../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md). Master index: [`../../README.md`](../../README.md).

## 1. Identity, Placement, Dating

- **Parent Veda & exact location** — the Aitareya is the **sole mukhya Upaniṣad of the Ṛgveda**, lodged in the **Aitareya Āraṇyaka** of the **Aitareya (Śākala) śākhā**; specifically it comprises **adhyāyas 4–6 of the second Āraṇyaka** (Āraṇyaka II.4–6), which form the Upaniṣad proper in three chapters. *(Source: Aitareya Āraṇyaka II.4–6; Aitareya Up.)* — see [`../vedas/rigveda.md`](../vedas/rigveda.md).
- **School-sibling of the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa** — it belongs to the same **Aitareya recension** that gives us the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa; the whole layer (Brāhmaṇa → Āraṇyaka → Upaniṣad) is traditionally tied to the seer **Mahidāsa Aitareya**, "son of Itarā," after whom the texts are named. *(Source: Aitareya tradition; Chāndogya Up. III.16.7 names a long-lived Mahidāsa Aitareya)* — see [`../vedas/brahmanas/aitareya-brahmana.md`](../vedas/brahmanas/aitareya-brahmana.md). [traditional attribution]
- **The Mahidāsa legend** — a stock account holds that **Mahidāsa**, slighted as the son of a co-wife (Itarā) and favoured by the goddess **Mahī / Earth**, received the teaching and was granted exceptional long life; the texts of the school bear his matronymic *Aitareya*. *(Source: commentarial tradition; Sāyaṇa's intro)* [folk] [traditional attribution]
- **Placement in the principal set** — counted among the **ten/eleven Upaniṣads Śaṅkara commented on (Daśopaniṣad)** and standard in the **mukhya** lists (…Praśna–Muṇḍaka–Māṇḍūkya–Taittirīya–**Aitareya**–Chāndogya–Bṛhadāraṇyaka…). *(Source: Daśopaniṣad; Muktikā Up.)* — catalogued in [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).
- **Form** — short **prose** in **three adhyāyas** (the first sub-divided into three khaṇḍas); no dialogue frame — a meditative cosmogony and self-inquiry rather than a teacher–pupil narrative. *(Source: Aitareya Up. I–III)*
- **Dating (two clocks)** —
  - *Academic:* one of the **older prose Upaniṣads**, broadly **first half / mid-1st millennium BCE**, of the same Brāhmaṇa-Āraṇyaka stratum as the Bṛhadāraṇyaka and Chāndogya. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (orthodox / traditional):* eternal *apauruṣeya* Śruti "seen" by the ṛṣis, not authored in a datable century, and fixed in transmission at the **Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; the Kali-Yuga onset constant)**. Both clocks are recorded; neither is corrected. [scripture vs. academic dating]

## 2. The Ātman Cosmogony — Self Alone, Then the Worlds (Adhyāya I.1)

- **The Self alone, in the beginning** — *"ātmā vā idam eka evāgra āsīt, nānyat kiñcana miṣat"* — **"The Ātman (Self) alone was this, in the beginning; nothing else whatsoever winked (was active)."** The Self resolves: *"Let me create the worlds."* *(Source: Aitareya Up. I.1.1)* — the cosmos issues from a single conscious Self, not from inert matter. [the keynote cosmogony]
- **The four worlds projected** — the Self emits **four worlds**: **ambhas** (the waters / the heaven beyond the sky), **marīci** (the light-rays / the inter-space), **mara** (the mortal / the earth), and **āpas** (the waters beneath the earth). *(Source: Aitareya Up. I.1.2)* — a fourfold spatial cosmos drawn out of the Self.
- **The world-guardians sought** — the Self then resolves to create **guardians (loka-pālas)** for these worlds, and draws forth a **Puruṣa (cosmic Person)** from the waters and gives him shape. *(Source: Aitareya Up. I.1.3–4)*

## 3. The Cosmic Puruṣa — Organs and Presiding Deities (Adhyāya I.1–2)

- **The Person brooded over** — the Self **broods (tapas)** over the formed Puruṣa, and from him the bodily organs and their faculties break open, each followed by its cosmic counterpart. *(Source: Aitareya Up. I.1.4)*
- **Organ → faculty → deity series** — as the Person is heated, **the mouth opens → from the mouth, speech → from speech, Agni (fire)**; **nostrils → breath → Vāyu (air)**; **eyes → sight → Āditya (the sun)**; **ears → hearing → the Quarters (diś)**; **skin → hairs → the herbs and trees**; **heart → mind → the Moon (Candra)**; **navel → the out-breath (apāna) → Death (Mṛtyu)**; **generative organ → semen → the Waters**. *(Source: Aitareya Up. I.1.4)* — a systematic mapping of microcosm (the senses) onto macrocosm (the gods). [adhidaiva–adhyātma correlation]
- **The deities fall into the cosmic ocean** — these presiding deities, once emitted, **fall into the great ocean** and are afflicted by **hunger and thirst**; they ask the Self for an abode in which to settle and eat food. *(Source: Aitareya Up. I.2.1–2)*
- **Cow and horse rejected, man accepted** — the Self offers them a **cow**, then a **horse**, as dwellings; the deities reject both as "not enough." When a **human form (puruṣa)** is offered, they exclaim *"Well made!"* (*su-kṛtam*) — hence man is *su-kṛta*, "the well-made." *(Source: Aitareya Up. I.2.3)*
- **The gods enter their seats in man** — the Self commands each deity to **enter its proper station** in the human body: **Agni becomes speech and enters the mouth; Vāyu becomes breath and enters the nostrils; Āditya becomes sight and enters the eyes; the Quarters become hearing and enter the ears; herbs and trees become hairs and enter the skin; the Moon becomes mind and enters the heart; Death becomes the down-breath and enters the navel; the Waters become semen and enter the generative organ.** *(Source: Aitareya Up. I.2.4)* — the cosmic powers are re-internalised as the living person's senses. [adhidaiva → adhyātma return]
- **Hunger and thirst left over** — hunger and thirst ask for their own place; the Self assigns them **a share in the very deities**, so that whatever offering is made to any god, hunger and thirst are partners in it. *(Source: Aitareya Up. I.2.5)*

## 4. Food, and the Entry of the Self Through the Skull (Adhyāya I.3)

- **The making and pursuit of food** — the Self creates **food (anna)**; the food tries to flee and the Self attempts to seize it by **speech, breath, sight, hearing, skin, mind, and the generative organ** in turn — each fails — until it is **grasped by the down-breath (apāna)**, so that it is by the breath that one truly lives on food. *(Source: Aitareya Up. I.3.1–10)*
- **"By what way shall I enter?"** — the Self reflects: *"How could this body subsist without me?"* and wonders by which door to enter the form it has made. *(Source: Aitareya Up. I.3.11)*
- **Entry through the suture of the skull** — the Self **cleaves the crown of the head (the sagittal suture, *sīman / vidṛti*) and enters the body by that door**; this entry-point is called the **place of bliss (nāndana)**. *(Source: Aitareya Up. I.3.12)* — a celebrated locus for the later **brahmarandhra** of yoga; the Self dwells indwelling, looking out through the very senses it has made. [entry-of-the-Self motif]
- **Seeing Brahman everywhere** — having entered, the Self **perceives this whole world**, and exclaims (in some readings) *"idam adarśam"* — "I have seen this," a play later glossed as the name **Idandra → Indra**, the indwelling seer "cryptically called Indra." *(Source: Aitareya Up. I.3.13–14)* [name-etymology motif]

## 5. The Threefold Birth (Adhyāya II.1)

- **The doctrine of three births** — *"puruṣe ha vā ayam ādito garbho bhavati…"* — a man is **born three times**. *(Source: Aitareya Up. II.1)* [trayo janma — threefold birth]
  - **First birth — in the womb** — the Self, present in a man **as the seed (retas)**, is the gathered vigour of all his limbs; when he deposits it in a woman, he **engenders it** — this is its **first birth**, the embryo conceived. *(Source: Aitareya Up. II.1.1–2)*
  - **Second birth — at delivery** — the woman nourishes the embryo as her own self; the father tends it before and after delivery, and **the child born is his own self continued for the maintenance of the worlds** — this is the **second birth**, parturition. *(Source: Aitareya Up. II.1.3)*
  - **Third birth — through one's offspring / spiritual rebirth** — when the father, his duties done and his lifespan ending, **departs**, he is **born again** through his offspring (who carries him on), or — read spiritually — is **re-born after death**. *(Source: Aitareya Up. II.1.4)* — the verse closes with **Vāmadeva**, who, *"while yet in the womb,"* knew all the births of the gods and so **broke the bonds and ascended to the heavenly world**, attaining immortality. *(Source: Aitareya Up. II.1.5; quoting Ṛgveda IV.27.1)* [Vāmadeva — womb-knowledge motif]

## 6. *Prajñānaṃ Brahma* — Consciousness Is Brahman (Adhyāya III.1)

- **The opening inquiry** — *"ko 'yam ātmeti vayam upāsmahe? kataraḥ sa ātmā?"* — **"Who is this Ātman whom we worship? Which is the Self?"** — the third chapter turns from cosmos to the questioner's own awareness. *(Source: Aitareya Up. III.1.1)*
- **The Self as the seat of all knowing** — that **"by which one sees, hears, smells, speaks, and discriminates the sweet and the unsweet"** — the **heart and mind**, named by many terms: *saṃjñāna* (consciousness), *ājñāna* (command), *vijñāna* (understanding), *prajñāna* (cognition/insight), *medhā* (intelligence), *dhī*, *smṛti*, *saṃkalpa*, *kratu*, *asu*, *kāma*, *vaśa* — **all are names of Prajñāna (consciousness)**. *(Source: Aitareya Up. III.1.2)* — the long synonym-list collapses every faculty into one conscious ground.
- **The pan-cosmic reach of consciousness** — guided by this Prajñāna are **Brahmā, Indra, Prajāpati, all the gods, the five great elements, all beings small and great, the born and the to-be-born, the moving and the unmoving**; *"prajñā-netraṃ… prajñāne pratiṣṭhitam"* — **all this is led by consciousness, established in consciousness; the world is led by consciousness; consciousness is its ground.** *(Source: Aitareya Up. III.1.3)* — the identification of the inquiring awareness with all gods and beings.
- **The mahāvākya — *Prajñānaṃ Brahma*** — the chapter culminates in the Ṛgveda's **great saying (mahāvākya)**: *"prajñānaṃ brahma"* — **"Consciousness (insight) is Brahman."** *(Source: Aitareya Up. III.1.3)* — one of the four canonical mahāvākyas (one per Veda): the Ṛgveda's contribution, beside *Tat tvam asi* (Sāmaveda/Chāndogya), *Ayam ātmā brahma* (Atharva/Māṇḍūkya), and *Ahaṃ brahmāsmi* (Yajur/Bṛhadāraṇyaka). [the Ṛgveda mahāvākya]
- **Liberation by this knowledge** — knowing the Self as this very consciousness-Brahman, **Vāmadeva and his like rose from this world and, attaining all desires in the heavenly world, became immortal**. *(Source: Aitareya Up. III.1.4)* — the realised one is the final word of the text.

## 7. Commentary, Reception & Significance

- **Śaṅkara's bhāṣya** — **Ādi Śaṅkara** (c. 8th c. CE) wrote an **Aitareyopaniṣad-bhāṣya**, reading the cosmogony as the *sṛṣṭi-krama* upaadhi for non-dual Ātman and *Prajñānaṃ Brahma* as the identity-statement of the inner consciousness with Brahman; this commentary secures the text's front-rank mukhya status. *(Source: Śaṅkara, Aitareyopaniṣad-bhāṣya)* [scholarly]
- **The Ṛgveda's mahāvākya seat** — the Aitareya is the text habitually cited for the **Ṛgvedic mahāvākya**, making it the Ṛgveda's voice in the standard four-Veda scheme of great sayings. *(Source: Vedānta mahāvākya tradition)*
- **A creation account on the "two clocks"** — the Ātman-cosmogony (worlds → Puruṣa → gods → man → entry of the Self) is a **scriptural-time** account of origination, not a datable historical event; it sits alongside the Nāsadīya and Puruṣa-sūkta cosmogonies of its own Veda. *(Source: Aitareya Up. I; cf. Ṛgveda X.90, X.129)* — see [`../vedas/rigveda.md`](../vedas/rigveda.md). [scripture clock]
- **Standard editions** — rendered in **Hume's *Thirteen Principal Upanishads*** and **Radhakrishnan's *The Principal Upaniṣads***, and in Max Müller's *Sacred Books of the East*. *(Source: principal set)* — cross-text table in [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).
