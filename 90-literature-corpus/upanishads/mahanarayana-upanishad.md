# Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad — Dedicated Deep-Dive

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/upanishads/`](./README.md) — a per-text deep-dive into the **Mahānārāyaṇa (Yājñikī) Upaniṣad**, prapāṭhaka 10 of the **Taittirīya Āraṇyaka** (Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda) and the great **liturgical reservoir** of mantras still recited daily in Vaiṣṇava–Smārta practice: the **Nārāyaṇa-anuvāka (Nārāyaṇa Sūkta)**, the **Medha-sūktas**, the **Antaryāmin** and **sannyāsa** passages. Parent forest-text in [`../vedas/aranyakas.md`](../vedas/aranyakas.md); parent Veda in [`../vedas/yajurveda.md`](../vedas/yajurveda.md); the embedded hymn-liturgy is catalogued in [`../vedic-deities-and-suktas.md`](../vedic-deities-and-suktas.md); the closest theistic sibling-Upaniṣad of the same Veda is [`./svetashvatara-upanishad.md`](./svetashvatara-upanishad.md). Master index: [`../../README.md`](../../README.md).

## 1. Identity, Placement, Dating

- **Parent text & school** — the Mahānārāyaṇa **is** prapāṭhaka (praśna) **10 of the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka**, the concluding forest-layer of the **Taittirīya śākhā of the Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda**; it is therefore not a free-standing book but the **last, most theistic stratum** appended after the Taittirīya Upaniṣad (TĀ praśnas 7–9). *(Source: Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10; Mahānārāyaṇa Up.)* See [`../vedas/aranyakas.md`](../vedas/aranyakas.md).
- **The name(s)** — "Mahā-nārāyaṇa" ("the great [Upaniṣad] of Nārāyaṇa"), after its core hymn proclaiming **Nārāyaṇa as supreme Brahman**; also called the **Yājñikī Upaniṣad** ("the sacrificial one"), reflecting its overwhelmingly **liturgical/ritual** content. *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up.; Muktikā tradition)*
- **Canonical status** — counted among the **Muktikā list of 108 Upaniṣads** and classed with the **Yajurveda group**; a "first-tier minor" Upaniṣad — too liturgically central to ignore, yet outside the ten/twelve *mukhya* set on which the classical Vedānta *bhāṣyas* were written. *(Source: Muktikā Up.; Mahānārāyaṇa Up.)*
- **Recensions** — two principal recensions differ in length and verse-count:
  - *Variant — Āndhra (Drāviḍa / South Indian) recension (Mahānārāyaṇa Up.):* the longer, with c. **64 anuvākas**, the form most used in South Indian recitation. [regional]
  - *Variant — Atharvan / Northern recension (Mahānārāyaṇa Up.):* a shorter arrangement with a different anuvāka numbering. [regional]
  - Because of this, citations by **anuvāka number vary between editions**; cross-references below name the passage rather than rely on a single numbering. [disputed numbering]
- **Dating (two clocks)** —
  - *Academic:* one of the **latest layers of the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka**, post-dating the older ritual books and even the embedded Taittirīya Upaniṣad; roughly **c. 5th–2nd c. BCE**, its developed **Nārāyaṇa / Vaiṣṇava theism** marking it as a late, accretive compilation that kept growing. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (orthodox / traditional):* eternal *apauruṣeya* Śruti "seen" by the ṛṣis and fixed at the Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; see Kali-Yuga onset constant), not "authored" in a BCE stratum. Both clocks are recorded; neither is corrected. [scripture vs. academic dating]
- **Why it matters in one line** — it is the **single largest source of living Vedic liturgy** in the Yajurveda: the mantras a Smārta or Śrī-Vaiṣṇava actually says at the **sandhyā** twilight-prayer, at **deity-bathing (abhiṣeka)**, at **prāṇāyāma**, and at **renunciation** are mostly drawn from this one prapāṭhaka. [liturgically central]

## 2. The Nārāyaṇa-anuvāka (Nārāyaṇa Sūkta)

- **Nārāyaṇa as the Supreme** — the famous opening *"sahasra-śīrṣaṃ devaṃ viśvākṣaṃ viśva-śambhuvam… nārāyaṇaṃ mahā-jñeyaṃ viśvātmānaṃ parāyaṇam"* — the **thousand-headed, all-eyed, all-blessing God; Nārāyaṇa, the great One to be known, the cosmic Self (viśvātman) and supreme refuge (parāyaṇa).** *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up., Nārāyaṇa-anuvāka)* — the **Vedic root of Vaiṣṇava-supremacy theology**; catalogued in [`../vedic-deities-and-suktas.md`](../vedic-deities-and-suktas.md).
- **The lotus of the heart (dahara-vidyā)** — a meditation locating the deity in the **heart-lotus**: in the citadel of the body, in the small lotus hanging "a span below the throat, a span above the navel," is a **subtle flame**, and **in the midst of that flame dwells the Supreme Self (Paramātman) — Brahmā, Śiva, Hari, Indra, the imperishable.** *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up., Nārāyaṇa-anuvāka)* — a Vaiṣṇava recasting of the **dahara-ākāśa** "small space within the heart" of the Chāndogya; cf. [`./chandogya-upanishad.md`](./chandogya-upanishad.md).
- **"nārāyaṇāya vidmahe" — the Nārāyaṇa Gāyatrī** — the **anuvāka also supplies the Nārāyaṇa gāyatrī-style triplet** *"nārāyaṇāya vidmahe, vāsudevāya dhīmahi, tan no viṣṇuḥ pracodayāt"* used as a daily Vaiṣṇava invocation. *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up.)* [liturgically central]
- **Embedded Puruṣa-sūkta** — the chapter carries a **Yajurvedic recension of the Puruṣa-sūkta** (cosmic-Man hymn, cf. Ṛgveda 10.90), continuous with the older TĀ 3 material, anchoring Nārāyaṇa to the sacrificial **Puruṣa**. *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up.; cf. Ṛgveda 10.90)*

## 3. The Embedded Sūkta-Liturgy (the pañca-sūkta nucleus)

- **Bhū Sūkta (Bhū Sūktam)** — invocation of the **Earth-goddess Bhūmi / Bhūdevī**, the all-supporting earth (in Vaiṣṇava theology Viṣṇu's earth-consort), drawn from this prapāṭhaka rather than the Ṛgvedic core. *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up.)* See [`../vedic-deities-and-suktas.md`](../vedic-deities-and-suktas.md).
- **Durgā Sūkta** — *"jātavedase sunavāma somam…"* — a hymn to **Durgā / Agni-Durgā** as the boat that carries one across danger, gathered here into the liturgical set. *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up.)*
- **The pañca-sūkta context** — together with the **Śrī Sūkta**, **Nīla Sūkta** and **Puruṣa Sūkta**, the Nārāyaṇa, Bhū and Durgā hymns supply the standard **pañca-sūkta ("five hymns") recitation** of Vaiṣṇava and Smārta **abhiṣeka** and pūjā (lists vary slightly by sampradāya). *(Source: liturgical tradition)* [traditional]
  - *Note — overlap, not duplication:* these hymns are **shared liturgy**, also occurring in other Yajurvedic/Atharvanic settings; the Mahānārāyaṇa is the **anthologising frame** that bound them to Nārāyaṇa-worship, not necessarily their sole origin. [overlap]

## 4. The Medha-sūktas (the mantras for intellect/memory)

- **Medhā-prayers** — the Mahānārāyaṇa preserves the **Medha-sūktas**, short hymns invoking **Medhā (the goddess/power of intelligence, memory and retention)** and **Agni, Indra, Savitṛ, Sarasvatī** to grant *medhā* — sharpness of mind. *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up., Medha-sūktas)* — also noted as TĀ Medha-sūktas in [`../vedas/yajurveda.md`](../vedas/yajurveda.md).
- **"medhāṃ me devo savitā…"** — the recurrent refrain begging **Savitṛ, Sarasvatī and the Aśvins to deposit *medhā*** in the reciter, classically said by **students before Vedic study (upākarma/svādhyāya)** and at the start of learning. *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up.)* [liturgically central]
- **"medhā-sūkta" usage** — because of these verses the Upaniṣad is the **scriptural source for the "intelligence-and-memory" pārāyaṇa** still recited (especially for students) across South India. [regional]

## 5. The Antaryāmin and the Theistic Brahman

- **The inner controller (Antaryāmin)** — the Upaniṣad names the Supreme as the **Antaryāmin**, the **inner ruler dwelling within and governing all beings** from the heart-cave — Nārāyaṇa as the indwelling controller of the cosmos and of the individual self. *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up.)* — the Yajurvedic **Antaryāmin** doctrine (developed at length in the Bṛhadāraṇyaka's Antaryāmin-brāhmaṇa, see [`./brihadaranyaka-upanishad.md`](./brihadaranyaka-upanishad.md)) here becomes **explicitly Vaiṣṇava**, a key proof-text for **Rāmānuja's** śarīra-śarīrin (body–soul) theology.
- **Brahman as Nārāyaṇa** — it carries the much-quoted equation **"all this, whatever exists, is pervaded by Nārāyaṇa within and without"** — collapsing the impersonal *nirguṇa* Brahman of the older Upaniṣads into the **personal Nārāyaṇa**, exactly as the [`./svetashvatara-upanishad.md`](./svetashvatara-upanishad.md) collapses it into Rudra-Śiva. [theistic turn]
- **Tat-tvam echoes & mahāvākya material** — being part of the Taittirīya complex it shares the **"satyaṃ jñānam anantaṃ brahma"** horizon; its theism supplies later **Vaiṣṇava Vedānta** with Śruti warrant for a *saguṇa* Absolute. *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up.; cf. Taittirīya Up.)*

## 6. The Sannyāsa Mantras (renunciation liturgy)

- **The formula of renunciation** — the Mahānārāyaṇa supplies the **mantras of saṃnyāsa**, including the renouncer's declaration *"oṃ bhūḥ saṃnyastaṃ mayā…"* and the cry **"abhayaṃ sarva-bhūtebhyo mattaḥ"** ("from me, let there be fearlessness to all beings") — the **praiṣa** uttered at initiation into the renunciate (saṃnyāsin) order. *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up., sannyāsa anuvāka)* [liturgically central]
- **Renunciation exalted over rites** — the famous verse **"na karmaṇā na prajayā dhanena, tyāgenaike amṛtatvam ānaśuḥ"** — **"not by action, not by progeny, not by wealth, but by renunciation (tyāga) alone did some attain immortality"** — appears here, the cornerstone of the **sannyāsa-as-highest-path** position. *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up.)* [pivotal renunciation verse]
- **Ritual-to-renunciation pivot** — the Upaniṣad thus **stands at the seam between Vedic ritualism (karma-kāṇḍa) and the renunciate path (saṃnyāsa)**, providing both the world-sustaining sandhyā/yajña mantras *and* the world-abandoning renunciation formulae in the same text. [bridge text]

## 7. The Sandhyā, Prāṇāyāma and Expiation Mantras

- **Sandhyā-vandana liturgy** — the prapāṭhaka furnishes a large share of the **twilight-prayer (sandhyā-vandana)** mantras of the Yajurvedin — invocations of the waters, the Sun, the directions and the **ārghya** offering. *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up.)* [liturgically central]
- **Prāṇāyāma mantra** — it supplies the **prāṇāyāma vyāhṛti-formula** (*bhūḥ, bhuvaḥ, suvaḥ…* with the Gāyatrī and its "head" *āpo jyotī raso'mṛtaṃ brahma…*) recited while regulating the breath. *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up.)*
- **Aghamarṣaṇa / expiation** — verses for the **washing-away of sin (aghamarṣaṇa)** and purificatory **prāyaścitta** are gathered here. *(Source: Mahānārāyaṇa Up.)*
- **Mṛtyuñjaya neighbourhood** — the broader Taittirīya Āraṇyaka furnishes the **Tryambakam / Mṛtyuñjaya** mantra; recitation tradition often groups it with the Mahānārāyaṇa material (though it is properly elsewhere in the TĀ corpus). *(Source: Taittirīya Āraṇyaka; cf. [`../vedas/yajurveda.md`](../vedas/yajurveda.md))* [overlap]

## 8. Why the Mahānārāyaṇa Matters

- **The living-liturgy Upaniṣad** — more than any *mukhya* Upaniṣad, its verses are **spoken daily, not merely studied**: sandhyā, abhiṣeka, prāṇāyāma, study-invocations and renunciation rites all draw on it, making it the **operational prayer-book of Smārta–Vaiṣṇava orthopraxy**. [liturgically central]
- **The Vaiṣṇava counterpart of the Śvetāśvatara** — the two latest Yajurvedic Upaniṣads form a **theistic pair**: Śvetāśvatara crystallises **Rudra-Śiva** as Brahman, Mahānārāyaṇa crystallises **Nārāyaṇa-Viṣṇu** as Brahman; together they supply Śruti warrant to the rival **Śaiva** and **Vaiṣṇava** theologies. See [`./svetashvatara-upanishad.md`](./svetashvatara-upanishad.md).
- **An anthology, not a treatise** — its character is **compilatory**: it gathers pre-existing sūktas (Nārāyaṇa, Puruṣa, Bhū, Durgā, Medha) and ritual formulae into one Vaiṣṇava-framed forest-appendix, which is why scholarship reads it as a **late, layered accretion** to the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka. [scholarly]
