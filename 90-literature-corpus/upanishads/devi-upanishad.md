# Devī Upaniṣad (Devī Atharvaśīrṣa)

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/upanishads/`](./README.md) — a per-text deep-dive into the **Devī Upaniṣad**, also called the **Devī Atharvaśīrṣa** ("Atharvan head-text of the Goddess"): the principal **Śākta minor Upaniṣad**, in which the **Great Goddess (Mahādevī / Mahālakṣmī / Durgā)** declares in her own voice *"ahaṃ brahma-svarūpiṇī"* ("I am of the nature of Brahman") and is hymned by the gods as the **source and substrate of the entire pantheon**. The supreme Śākta counterpart to the Śaiva pair [`./svetashvatara-upanishad.md`](./svetashvatara-upanishad.md) and [`./kaivalya-upanishad.md`](./kaivalya-upanishad.md); for the Vaiṣṇava/Śaiva/Śākta sectarian-Upaniṣad framing see [`./vaishnava-shaiva-shakta-upanishads-overview.md`](./vaishnava-shaiva-shakta-upanishads-overview.md). For the Goddess herself see [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-forms-and-navadurga.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-forms-and-navadurga.md); for her Śrīvidyā/Tantric ritual world see [`../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md`](../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md). Master index: [`../../README.md`](../../README.md).

## 1. Identity, Placement, Dating

- **Parent Veda & class** — the Devī Upaniṣad is an **Atharvaveda** text and one of the small group of **"Atharvaśīrṣa / śīras" Upaniṣads** (head-texts) that attach a deity's self-revelation to the Atharvan corpus (cf. the Gaṇapati, Sūrya, Nārāyaṇa and Rudra Atharvaśīrṣas). It is counted among the **Śākta Upaniṣads** of the **Muktikā** list of 108. *(Source: Devī Up.; Muktikā tradition)*
- **The names** — circulated as **Devī Upaniṣad** and, in its liturgical use, as the **Devī Atharvaśīrṣa** or **Devī-atharva-śīrṣa-stotra**; *atharvaśīrṣa* marks it as a **"crown/head text"** of the Atharvan school recited as a hymn (stotra), not a discursive treatise. *(Source: Devī Up.)*
- **Canonical status & length** — a **short text of roughly 32 verses (mantras)**, mostly metrical and hymnic; grouped with the **Śākta Upaniṣads** (the cluster including the Sītā, Tripurā, Tripurātāpinī, Bhāvanā, Saubhāgya-lakṣmī, Sarasvatī-rahasya and Bahvṛca Upaniṣads). Among these it is the **best-known and most-recited**, functioning as the Śākta movement's flagship Śruti-class proof-text. *(Source: Devī Up.; Muktikā Up.)* — see the cluster survey in [`./vaishnava-shaiva-shakta-upanishads-overview.md`](./vaishnava-shaiva-shakta-upanishads-overview.md).
- **Dating (two clocks)** —
  - *Academic:* a **late, sectarian (Śākta) minor Upaniṣad**, composed well after the *mukhya* corpus — generally placed in the **post-classical / early-medieval centuries CE**, drawing on the **Devī Māhātmya** (Durgā Saptaśatī, c. 5th–6th c. CE) and Śrīvidyā Tantra; the bulk of the Śākta Upaniṣads are among the **latest accretions** to the 108-canon. [scholarly] [late text]
  - *Variant (orthodox / traditional):* eternal *apauruṣeya* Śruti "seen" by the ṛṣis and fixed at the Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; see the Kali-Yuga onset constant), not "authored" in a CE stratum. Both clocks are recorded here; neither is corrected. [scripture vs. academic dating]
- **Why it matters in one line** — it is the **Śākta "I am Brahman" text**: where the Śvetāśvatara makes **Rudra-Śiva** Brahman and the Kaivalya teaches *"śivo'ham,"* the Devī Upaniṣad puts the **mahāvākya in the Goddess's own mouth** — the scriptural warrant that the supreme reality is **feminine, the Mahāśakti who is herself Brahman**. [pivotal Śākta charter]

## 2. The Frame — The Gods Question the Goddess

- **The opening question (v. 1)** — **all the gods (devas / sarve devāḥ)** approach the Goddess and ask: *"mahā-devi, kā tvam?"* — **"Great Goddess, who are you? Whence have you arisen?"** *(Source: Devī Up. 1)* — a reversal of the usual frame: the *pantheon itself* interrogates the Goddess about her own origin and nature.
- **The Self-Declaration — "I am Brahman" (v. 2)** — the Goddess answers in the **first person**: *"ahaṃ brahma-svarūpiṇī"* — **"I am of the very nature of Brahman."** From me proceeds the world as **prakṛti and puruṣa**, the void and the plenum (*śūnyaṃ cāśūnyaṃ ca*), bliss and non-bliss; **I am all this, the knowable and unknowable, the below and the beyond.** *(Source: Devī Up. 2)* — the **doctrinal core**: identity of the Goddess with the impersonal Brahman, asserted by herself, not predicated of her by another.

## 3. The Goddess as Source and Substrate of the Gods

- **She is the manifest pantheon (v. 3–6)** — the Goddess enumerates herself as the **all-gods**: *"ahaṃ rudrebhir vasubhiś carāmi, aham ādityair uta viśvadevaiḥ"* — **"I move with the Rudras and Vasus, with the Ādityas and All-gods"** — and as **Mitra-Varuṇa, Indra-Agni, the two Aśvins**, sustaining **Soma, Tvaṣṭṛ, Pūṣan and Bhaga**. *(Source: Devī Up. 3)* — this passage is **lifted from the Ṛgvedic Devī-Sūkta / Vāk-Sūkta (ṚV 10.125)**, the hymn of the ṛṣikā **Vāc Āmbhṛṇī** in which Speech declares her sovereignty over the gods; the Upaniṣad re-aims that ancient self-praise at the **Śākta Mahādevī**. [shared Ṛgvedic self-praise; Vāk-Sūkta source]
- **She bestows and withdraws all (v. 4–6)** — *"ahaṃ suve pitaram asya mūrdhan"* — **"I bring forth the Father (Heaven) on the world's summit"**; she is the one who **bends the bow of Rudra** to slay the foe, who **enters heaven and earth**, who gives wealth to the worshipper and makes the sage and seer. *(Source: Devī Up. 4–6; cf. ṚV 10.125)* — the gods do not *grant* her power; **their functioning is her self-distribution.**
- **The cosmic vastness (v. 7)** — *"ahaṃ eva vāta iva pra vāmi, ārabhamāṇā bhuvanāni viśvā; paro divā para enā pṛthivyā, etāvatī mahinā saṃ babhūva"* — **"I breathe forth like the wind, setting in motion all the worlds; beyond the sky, beyond this earth — so vast have I become in my greatness."** *(Source: Devī Up. 7; = ṚV 10.125.8)* — the Goddess as the **all-pervading, world-originating power** larger than the cosmos she upholds.

## 4. The Goddess Identified with Mahālakṣmī, Durgā and the Brahmavidyās

- **The pañcāyatana of goddesses (middle verses)** — the gods, recognizing her, praise the Goddess as **the one who appears as Mahālakṣmī, Mahākālī and Mahāsarasvatī**, as **Durgā / Bhadrakālī**, and as the **Śrī** seated in the lotus — the single Mahāśakti behind the multiple Devī-forms. *(Source: Devī Up.)* — see [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-forms-and-navadurga.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-forms-and-navadurga.md) and [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/lakshmi.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/lakshmi.md).
- **The Durgā invocation (v. ~22–24)** — the text incorporates the famous **"jātavedase sunavāma somam"** Durgā-Sūkta verse (ṚV 1.99.1 / Taittirīya Āraṇyaka) and the prayer to the **fire-borne Durgā who carries the devotee across difficulties (durga) as a boat across water**. *(Source: Devī Up.; cf. Durgā-Sūkta, TĀ 10)* — binds the abstract Goddess-Brahman to the **Durgā worshipped in Navarātri and crisis-rites**.
- **She is the Gāyatrī and the Vedas (closing identifications)** — the Goddess is hymned as **Gāyatrī, Sāvitrī, the mother of the Vedas (veda-mātā)**, the **praṇava (Oṃ)** itself, and the **bindu / nāda / kalā** of mantric metaphysics — collapsing Vedic and Tantric symbol-systems into the one Devī. *(Source: Devī Up.)* — anchors her in **Śrīvidyā** sound-cosmology; cf. [`../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md`](../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md).

## 5. The Mantras — Devī Gāyatrī and the Navārṇa

- **The Devī Gāyatrī (v. ~25)** — the text supplies a **Gāyatrī of the Goddess**: *"mahā-devyai ca vidmahe, durgāyai ca dhīmahi, tan no devī pracodayāt"* — **"May we know the Great Goddess, may we meditate on Durgā; may that Goddess impel us"** — the Śākta counterpart to the solar Gāyatrī, embedding Devī-worship in the standard *vidmahe–dhīmahi–pracodayāt* gāyatrī-frame. *(Source: Devī Up.)* — variants of the second line name **Kātyāyanī, Kanyākumārī or Mahālakṣmī** by tradition. [regional liturgical variants]
- **The Navārṇa (nine-syllable) mantra** — the Upaniṣad enshrines the **navārṇa / navākṣarī mantra** — *"oṃ aiṃ hrīṃ klīṃ cāmuṇḍāyai vicce"* — the central **bīja-mantra of Durgā/Cāmuṇḍā** used in Devī Māhātmya recitation and Śākta initiation, here given Śruti-class sanction. *(Source: Devī Up.)* — the *navārṇa* is the **liturgical heart** linking this Upaniṣad to the Saptaśatī and the Navadurgā; see [`../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md`](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md).
  - *Variant (Śrīvidyā reading):* in **Śrīvidyā** practice the same Upaniṣad is read toward **Lalitā-Tripurasundarī** and the **Śrīcakra**, with the bījas **aiṃ (Sarasvatī/Vāk), hrīṃ (the māyā-bīja / Bhuvaneśvarī) and klīṃ (Kāma-bīja)** glossed as the goddess's three powers; the **mahā-tripura-sundarī** identification is foregrounded over the martial Durgā one. [regional / sampradāya variant]
- **The seed-syllable theology (closing)** — the Goddess is said to dwell as **hrīṃ** (the *hrīṃkāra*, her root bīja) and to be reached through it; recitation of her **bīja and name** is declared the means of approach. *(Source: Devī Up.)*

## 6. The Phala-śruti — Fruit of Recitation

- **Times and merit of recitation (closing verses)** — the **phala-śruti** prescribes recitation **at the three sandhyās, on the eighth lunar day (aṣṭamī), during Navarātri, and before the Devī's image**, promising **destruction of sin, removal of obstacles and great fortune**; one who recites it **becomes pure, attains the Goddess's world, and is freed.** *(Source: Devī Up., closing verses)* — a standard recitation-merit formula attaching the meditative hymn to **living Durgā/Devī pūjā**.
- **Ritual placement** — in practice the Devī Atharvaśīrṣa is chanted as a **stotra in Durgā and Lalitā worship**, in **Navarātri liturgy**, in **kumārī-pūjā** and as a daily Śākta recitation — the **single most common Upaniṣadic text in Goddess worship**, parallel in function to how the Kaivalya/Śatarudrīya serve Śiva-pūjā. [practice-centred]

## 7. Doctrinal Contrast with the Advaitic *Mukhya* Upaniṣads

- **Saguṇa-feminine vs. nirguṇa-neuter** — the *mukhya* Upaniṣads teach an **impersonal, gender-neutral *nirguṇa* Brahman** ("neither woman, man nor neuter," Śvetāśvatara 5.10); the Devī Upaniṣad **personalizes and feminizes** that absolute — Brahman *is* the Mahādevī, a worshipped, named **Person-Goddess (saguṇā)** who is simultaneously the *nirguṇa* substrate. [the Śākta theistic recasting]
- **Śakti as the very being of Brahman** — where Advaita treats *māyā/śakti* as Brahman's dependent power, Śākta reading (following this text) makes **Śakti ontologically primary** — the Goddess is not Brahman's power but **Brahman-as-power**, the active reality of which the static absolute is merely the ground. [Śākta vs. Advaita on śakti]
- **Continuity with the *mukhya* claim** — yet the **mahāvākya identity** (*"ahaṃ brahma-svarūpiṇī"*) is structurally the same move as *"ahaṃ brahmāsmi"* (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.4.10) and *"śivo'ham"* (Kaivalya) — the Devī Upaniṣad **claims the non-dual summit for the feminine**, completing the sectarian triad: **Vaiṣṇava (the tāpanīya/Nārāyaṇa group), Śaiva (Śvetāśvatara–Kaivalya), and Śākta (Devī)**. See [`./vaishnava-shaiva-shakta-upanishads-overview.md`](./vaishnava-shaiva-shakta-upanishads-overview.md).

## 8. Why the Devī Upaniṣad Matters

- **The Śākta charter-text in Śruti** — it gives the **Goddess-as-Supreme-Brahman** doctrine a footing in the *Muktikā* canon, the warrant by which Śāktas read the Upaniṣadic absolute as **the Mother** and treat their Tantric mantras (navārṇa, the bījas, the Devī Gāyatrī) as **Vedic-grade**.
- **Bridge from Ṛgvedic Vāc to medieval Devī** — by re-deploying the **Devī-Sūkta (ṚV 10.125)** it draws an unbroken line from the **archaic ṛṣikā Vāc Āmbhṛṇī's "I am the sovereign"** to the **medieval Mahādevī**, presenting Śākta theology as the unfolding of the oldest Śruti rather than an innovation. [continuity claim]
- **Liturgical keystone** — it is the **most-recited Upaniṣad in Goddess worship**, the Śruti-class container for the **navārṇa mantra and Devī Gāyatrī** used across Navarātri, Durgā-pūjā and Śrīvidyā.
- **Two clocks held open** — its **late-CE academic dating** (post–Devī Māhātmya) and the **traditional eternal-Śruti** frame are both recorded here without reconciliation; verse-counts (~30–32) and the Durgā-vs-Lalitā reading vary by recension and sampradāya. [disputed] [scripture vs. academic dating]
