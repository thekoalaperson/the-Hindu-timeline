# Taittirīya Upaniṣad

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/upanishads/`](./README.md) — a per-text deep-dive into the **Taittirīya Upaniṣad**, a foundational prose *mukhya* Upaniṣad of the **Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda** (Taittirīya śākhā), embedded in the **Taittirīya Āraṇyaka** and arranged in **three vallīs ("creepers")**: the **Śīkṣā-vallī** (phonetics and the graduation charge), the **Brahmānanda-/Ānanda-vallī** (the *pañca-kośa* and the calculus of bliss), and the **Bhṛgu-vallī** (the Bhṛgu–Varuṇa inquiry into Brahman). For the cross-text *mukhya* overview see [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md); for the Brāhmaṇa that shares its school see [`../vedas/brahmanas/taittiriya-brahmana.md`](../vedas/brahmanas/taittiriya-brahmana.md); for the Āraṇyaka layer that houses it see [`../vedas/aranyakas.md`](../vedas/aranyakas.md). Section index: [`./README.md`](./README.md). Master index: [`../../README.md`](../../README.md).

## 1. Identity, Placement, Dating

- **Parent Veda & school** — the Taittirīya belongs to the **Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda**, transmitted in the **Taittirīya śākhā**; it forms the **seventh, eighth and ninth chapters (prapāṭhakas) of the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka**, the three vallīs being Āraṇyaka chapters 7–9. *(Source: Taittirīya Up.; Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 7–9)* — see [`../vedas/aranyakas.md`](../vedas/aranyakas.md).
- **The name** — "Taittirīya" derives from the **Taittirīya school**, traditionally traced to the sage **Tittiri** (pupil of Yāska/Vaiśampāyana in the legend of the "partridge"-recension of the Black Yajurveda), not from an incipit. *(Source: Taittirīya śākhā tradition)* — see [`../vedas/brahmanas/taittiriya-brahmana.md`](../vedas/brahmanas/taittiriya-brahmana.md).
- **Structure — three vallīs** — **(1) Śīkṣā-vallī** (also Saṃhitī-vallī), 12 anuvākas on phonetics, ritual meditations and the convocation; **(2) Brahmānanda-vallī / Ānanda-vallī**, on the sheaths and bliss; **(3) Bhṛgu-vallī**, the Bhṛgu–Varuṇa dialogue. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. I–III)*
- **Canonical placement** — counted among the **principal (mukhya) Upaniṣads** and named in the **Daśopaniṣad** mnemonic (*Īśa–Kena–Kaṭha–Praśna–Muṇḍa–Māṇḍūkya–**Taittiri**–Aitareya…*). *(Source: Daśopaniṣad mnemonic; Muktikā Up.)* — catalogued in [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).
- **Śaṅkara's bhāṣya** — **Ādi Śaṅkara** (c. 8th c. CE) wrote a full **Taittirīya-Upaniṣad-bhāṣya**, one of his ten canonical commentaries; his reading of the *pañca-kośa* and *"Satyaṃ jñānam anantaṃ Brahma"* is the standard Advaita treatment. *(Source: Śaṅkara, Taittirīya-bhāṣya)* [scholarly]
- **Dating (two clocks)** —
  - *Academic:* an **early prose Upaniṣad**, broadly **6th–5th c. BCE**, among the older layer (with the Bṛhadāraṇyaka and Chāndogya), antedating the major verse Upaniṣads. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (orthodox / traditional):* eternal *apauruṣeya* Śruti "seen" (not authored) by the ṛṣis, fixed in transmission by **Vyāsa's** redaction at the **Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; Kali-Yuga onset constant)**, not a BCE-stratum composition. Both clocks are recorded; neither is corrected. [scripture vs. academic dating]

## 2. Śīkṣā-vallī — Phonetics and the Graduation Charge (I)

- **The śānti and the partridge-line** — the vallī opens with the **"Śaṃ no Mitraḥ"** peace-invocation and is bound up with **śīkṣā**, the Vedāṅga of phonetics — the first of the six limbs, teaching **varṇa (letter), svara (accent), mātrā (quantity), bala (force), sāma (modulation), santāna (junction/sandhi)**. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. I.1–I.2)*
- **The Saṃhitā-upaniṣad (combination-meditations)** — meditations on the **five "combinations" (saṃhitā)** — over the worlds, the lights, knowledge, progeny, and the self — each paired with a *pūrva-rūpa, uttara-rūpa, sandhi* etc., teaching that the joins of speech mirror cosmic joins. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. I.3)*
- **The Vyāhṛti / Mahānāmnī meditation** — meditation on **bhūr, bhuvar, svar (and mahar)** as the worlds, the gods, the Vedas and the prāṇas, fixing the meditator in Brahman. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. I.5)*
- **The Person in the heart-space** — the **puruṣa in the cavity of the heart (antar-hṛdaya ākāśa)**, by which one rises through the suture of the skull to Indra/Brahman — an early "ascent" passage. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. I.6)*
- **The convocation charge (Bhṛgu-vallī's famous I.11)** — at the close, the teacher dismisses the **graduating student (the *samāvartana* / convocation)** with the most-quoted ethical injunction in the corpus:
  - **Satyaṃ vada — "speak the truth."** *(Source: Taittirīya Up. I.11.1)*
  - **Dharmaṃ cara — "practise dharma / walk in righteousness."** *(Source: Taittirīya Up. I.11.1)*
  - **Svādhyāyān mā pramadaḥ — "do not neglect your own (Vedic) study,"** and continue the householder's line (*prajātantuṃ mā vyavacchetsīḥ*). *(Source: Taittirīya Up. I.11.1)*
  - **The four "be-a-god" injunctions** — **mātṛdevo bhava, pitṛdevo bhava, ācāryadevo bhava, atithidevo bhava** — *"be one to whom the mother is a god, the father is a god, the teacher is a god, the guest is a god."* *(Source: Taittirīya Up. I.11.2)* — the classical charter of reverence to mother, father, guru and guest.
  - **The discernment clause** — do only blameless deeds, follow only the good conduct of the teachers, and in doubt act as a thoughtful, gentle, dharma-loving Brahmin would act. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. I.11.2–4)* — one of the earliest scriptural appeals to the conduct of the wise (*śiṣṭācāra*) as a moral guide.
- **Reception** — *"Satyaṃ vada, dharmaṃ cara"* and *"ācāryadevo bhava"* became the standard **Indian convocation/graduation blessing**, recited at *samāvartana* and at modern university ceremonies. [scholarly]

## 3. Brahmānanda-/Ānanda-vallī — The Sheaths and the Calculus of Bliss (II)

- **The mahāvākya-like definition** — the vallī opens by declaring **"Satyaṃ jñānam anantaṃ Brahma"** — *"Brahman is the Real, Knowledge, the Infinite"* — he who knows it as set in the cave of the heart, in the highest heaven, attains all desires together with the omniscient Brahman. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. II.1.1)* [the Taittirīya's near-*mahāvākya* defining-statement of Brahman]
- **From Brahman, the elements** — from this Self (Ātman) arose **space (ākāśa), thence air (vāyu), fire (agni), water (āpas), earth (pṛthivī)**, thence plants, food, and man — a graded cosmogony. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. II.1.1)*
- **The doctrine of the five sheaths (pañca-kośa)** — the locus classicus: man is enveloped in **five concentric sheaths (kośas)**, each "inner and other than" the last, like nested birds/persons:
  - **Annamaya-kośa — the food/physical sheath**, made of and sustained by food (*anna*); the body that is "born of food, grows by food, ends in food." *(Source: Taittirīya Up. II.1–II.2)*
  - **Prāṇamaya-kośa — the vital/breath sheath**, the body of the five prāṇas, inner to the food-sheath, by which the gods, men and beasts breathe and live. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. II.2–II.3)*
  - **Manomaya-kośa — the mental sheath**, formed of mind (*manas*) — "where words turn back, with the mind, unable to reach it"; the sheath of will, doubt and the Vedas (Yajus its head, Ṛk its right side, etc.). *(Source: Taittirīya Up. II.3–II.4)*
  - **Vijñānamaya-kośa — the intellect/knowledge sheath**, formed of discriminative cognition (*vijñāna*), the sheath that **performs sacrifice and rites and "knows"** — faith its head, ṛta and satya its sides. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. II.4–II.5)*
  - **Ānandamaya-kośa — the bliss sheath**, the innermost, formed of bliss (*ānanda*) — love its head, joy and delight its sides, Brahman its tail/support; the closest envelope to the Ātman. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. II.5)* [the five-sheath analysis adopted across Vedānta]
- **"He who knows Brahman attains the Supreme"** — and the great fear-removing line: **"Whence words return with the mind, not having reached it — he who knows the bliss of Brahman fears nothing"** (*yato vāco nivartante, aprāpya manasā saha; ānandaṃ brahmaṇo vidvān na bibheti kutaścana*). *(Source: Taittirīya Up. II.4.1, II.9.1)*
- **"If Brahman were not, who could breathe?"** — the assertion that Brahman, the bliss, is the very condition of life: *"for if this bliss (ānanda) in the ether of the heart were not, who indeed could live, who breathe?"* *(Source: Taittirīya Up. II.7.1)*
- **The calculus of bliss (ānanda-mīmāṃsā)** — the famous **graded measure of bliss**: take one unit of human bliss (a young, learned, well-ordered king of the whole earth in his prime); a hundredfold of that is the bliss of the *manuṣya-gandharvas*, again a hundredfold the *deva-gandharvas*, then the *pitṛs*, the *ājāna-devas*, the *karma-devas*, the *devas*, **Indra, Bṛhaspati, Prajāpati,** and finally **Brahman** — each rung a hundred times the last; *"and this is the bliss of one free from desire and versed in the Veda."* *(Source: Taittirīya Up. II.8.1–4)* [the unique Upaniṣadic "scale of beatitude"]

## 4. Bhṛgu-vallī — The Bhṛgu–Varuṇa Inquiry into Brahman (III)

- **The request** — **Bhṛgu, son of Varuṇa (Vāruṇi Bhṛgu)**, approaches his father **Varuṇa** and asks: *"Teach me Brahman."* *(Source: Taittirīya Up. III.1.1)*
- **The defining clue** — Varuṇa gives the working definition and sends him to win it by *tapas*: **"That from which these beings are born, by which when born they live, and into which on departing they enter — seek to know That; That is Brahman"** (*yato vā imāni bhūtāni jāyante…*). *(Source: Taittirīya Up. III.1.1)* [the classic causal definition of Brahman]
- **The five successive answers (won by tapas)** — Bhṛgu performs austerity and returns five times, each time grasping Brahman as a deeper layer, matching the kośa-ladder:
  - *First (annaṃ Brahma):* he understands **food (anna)** to be Brahman — from food beings are born and live. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. III.2)*
  - *Second (prāṇo Brahma):* unsatisfied, he performs more tapas and grasps **breath/life (prāṇa)** as Brahman. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. III.3)*
  - *Third (mano Brahma):* he then knows **mind (manas)** to be Brahman. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. III.4)*
  - *Fourth (vijñānaṃ Brahma):* he then knows **understanding/knowledge (vijñāna)** to be Brahman. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. III.5)*
  - *Fifth (ānando Brahma):* finally he knows **bliss (ānanda)** to be Brahman — *"from bliss beings are born, by bliss they live, into bliss they enter."* *(Source: Taittirīya Up. III.6)* — the inquiry climbs the same five sheaths to the bliss-Self.
- **The Bhārgavī Vāruṇī vidyā** — this realization "established in the supreme heaven" is named the **Bhārgavī Vāruṇī Vidyā** — Bhṛgu's knowledge taught by Varuṇa. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. III.6.1)*
- **The food-hymns and the "I am food" chant** — the vallī closes with injunctions **never to despise food** (*annaṃ na nindyāt*, *annaṃ na paricakṣīta*), to make and share food abundantly, and the exultant realization-chant **"Aham annam, aham annam, aham annādaḥ"** — *"I am food, I am food, I am the eater of food!"* — the liberated knower's joyous identification with the whole. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. III.7–III.10)*

## 5. Significance & Cross-Links

- **The pañca-kośa template** — the five-sheath model (annamaya → ānandamaya) became the standard Vedānta map of the person and the meditative method of "discriminating the Self from its envelopes," ubiquitous from Śaṅkara to modern Vedānta. *(Source: Taittirīya Up. II; Śaṅkara's bhāṣya)* [scholarly]
- **A defining-statement of Brahman** — *"Satyaṃ jñānam anantaṃ Brahma"* functions as the Taittirīya's great Brahman-formula, set beside the Chāndogya's *tat tvam asi* and the Bṛhadāraṇyaka's *ahaṃ brahmāsmi* in the standard four-*mahāvākya* set (though the Taittirīya's own most-cited line is this definition rather than a classic *mahāvākya*). *(Source: Taittirīya Up. II.1)* — see [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md).
- **Bridge from Brāhmaṇa to Vedānta** — sharing the Taittirīya school with the **Taittirīya Saṃhitā, Brāhmaṇa and Āraṇyaka**, this Upaniṣad is the Vedānta capstone of that recension's ritual corpus. *(Source: Taittirīya śākhā)* — see [`../vedas/brahmanas/taittiriya-brahmana.md`](../vedas/brahmanas/taittiriya-brahmana.md) and [`../vedas/aranyakas.md`](../vedas/aranyakas.md).
- **Living liturgical use** — the Śīkṣā-vallī's charge and the *śānti* mantras are recited daily in Taittirīya-school households and remain the template for the Indian **convocation blessing**. [scholarly]
- **Two clocks held open** — the **early-prose 6th–5th c. BCE** date and the **eternal-Śruti / Vyāsa-redaction** frame are both recorded here without reconciliation. [scripture vs. academic dating]
