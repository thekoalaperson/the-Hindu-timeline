# Īśa (Īśāvāsya) Upaniṣad

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/upanishads/`](./README.md) — a per-text deep-dive into the **Īśa (Īśāvāsya) Upaniṣad**, the **40th and final chapter of the Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā** of the **Śukla (White) Yajurveda** and the **shortest of the principal Upaniṣads** (18 verses): the only *mukhya* Upaniṣad embedded directly in a Saṃhitā rather than in a Brāhmaṇa or Āraṇyaka. It opens with the famous *"Īśāvāsyam idaṃ sarvam"* ("all this is pervaded by the Lord"), holds together **action and renunciation**, **knowledge and ignorance**, and closes with the **cremation-ground prayer** *Hiraṇmayena pātreṇa* and *Agne naya supathā*. For the parent Veda see [`../vedas/yajurveda.md`](../vedas/yajurveda.md); for the cross-text *mukhya* overview see [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md); for the Śruti corpus and Muktikā framing see [`../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md). Master index: [`../../README.md`](../../README.md).

## 1. Identity, Placement, Dating

- **Parent Veda & exact location** — the Īśa is the **40th and last adhyāya (chapter) of the Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā**, the Saṃhitā of the **Śukla (White) Yajurveda**; it is therefore also called the **Saṃhitopaniṣad** and is the only principal Upaniṣad lodged *inside* a Saṃhitā. *(Source: Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā 40; Īśa Up.)*
- **The two names** — *Īśa* ("the Lord / Ruler") and *Īśāvāsya* ("pervaded-by-the-Lord") both come from the **first word(s) of verse 1**, the standard Upaniṣadic naming-by-incipit. *(Source: Īśa Up. 1)*
- **Two recensions (śākhā)** — the text survives in the **Kāṇva** and **Mādhyandina** recensions of the Śukla Yajurveda, which differ in **verse order and several readings** (e.g. the relative placement of the *vidyā/avidyā* and *saṃbhūti/asaṃbhūti* pairs, and minor sandhi); both are canonical and are reproduced in tradition, not corrected. *(Source: Kāṇva & Mādhyandina recensions)* [disputed ordering]
- **Scale & form** — extremely short: **18 mantras** (17 in some numberings), almost wholly **metrical**, with no narrative frame and no named teacher — a dense **verse-Upaniṣad** rather than a dialogue. *(Source: Īśa Up.)*
- **Canonical first place** — by long convention the Īśa stands **first in the standard list of the principal Upaniṣads** (Īśa, Kena, Kaṭha, Praśna, Muṇḍaka, Māṇḍūkya, Taittirīya, Aitareya, Chāndogya, Bṛhadāraṇyaka, Śvetāśvatara…), a placement followed by Śaṅkara's commentaries and by Hume's and Radhakrishnan's English collections. *(Source: traditional mukhya list; Śaṅkara)*
- **Śaṅkara's bhāṣya** — the Īśa is one of the **ten/eleven Upaniṣads Śaṅkara wrote a bhāṣya on**, securing its front-rank *mukhya* status; his Advaita reading harmonizes the action-and-renunciation verses as addressed to different competencies (adhikāra). *(Source: Śaṅkara, Īśopaniṣad-bhāṣya)* [scholarly]
- **Dating (two clocks)** —
  - *Academic:* among the **older verse-Upaniṣads**, but as the metrical capstone of the Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā it is usually placed **a little after the great prose Upaniṣads** (Bṛhadāraṇyaka, Chāndogya) — broadly **mid-first-millennium BCE**, with the embedded-in-Saṃhitā position making its stratigraphy debated. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (orthodox / traditional):* eternal *apauruṣeya* Śruti "seen" by the ṛṣis, not authored in a datable century; fixed in transmission at the Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; see Kali-Yuga onset constant). Both clocks are recorded; neither is corrected. [scripture vs. academic dating]

## 2. The Opening — *Īśāvāsyam Idaṃ Sarvam* (v. 1)

- **All is pervaded by the Lord (v. 1a)** — *"īśāvāsyam idaṃ sarvaṃ yat kiñca jagatyāṃ jagat"* — **"All this, whatever moves in this moving world, is pervaded / is to be enveloped by the Lord (Īśa)."** *(Source: Īśa Up. 1)* — the foundational **panentheist / theistic monism** of the text: the whole changing universe is indwelt by, and to be "covered over" by, the one Lord. [the keynote verse]
- **Renounce-and-enjoy (v. 1b)** — *"tena tyaktena bhuñjīthāḥ, mā gṛdhaḥ kasya svid dhanam"* — **"By that renunciation enjoy; covet no one's wealth."** *(Source: Īśa Up. 1)* — the ethic that follows from the keynote: enjoy the world as the Lord's gift, by **non-grasping (tyāga)**, claiming nothing as one's own.

---

## 3. The Action–Renunciation Synthesis (v. 2)

- **Work a full lifespan (v. 2)** — *"kurvann eveha karmāṇi jijīviṣec chataṃ samāḥ"* — **"Doing works indeed here, one should wish to live a hundred years."** *(Source: Īśa Up. 2)* — the famous endorsement of **active life**: action (karma) performed without attachment does **not bind** — *"na karma lipyate nare"* ("no action clings to such a man"). [karma-jñāna balance]
- **The text's signature non-rejection** — unlike Upaniṣads that simply commend withdrawal, the Īśa holds the **householder's active life and the seeker's renunciation in one frame**: liberating knowledge is compatible with a full span of disinterested work. *(Source: Īśa Up. 1–2; cf. Bhagavad-gītā karma-yoga)* — Śaṅkara reads this as **two distinct paths for two competencies**; later Vedāntins read it as **one path of action-as-worship**. [interpretive variants]

## 4. The Demon-Worlds & the Indwelling Ātman (v. 3–8)

- **The joyless worlds (v. 3)** — *"asuryā nāma te lokā andhena tamasāvṛtāḥ"* — those who **slay the Self (ātma-han)** go after death to **sunless worlds wrapped in blind darkness**. *(Source: Īśa Up. 3)*
- **The unmoving mover (v. 4–5)** — the Self **"moves not, yet is swifter than the mind"; "it moves, and it moves not; it is far, and it is near; within all, and outside all."** *(Source: Īśa Up. 4–5)* — the classic **paradox of the immanent-yet-transcendent Brahman**.
- **Seeing the Self in all beings (v. 6–7)** — *"yas tu sarvāṇi bhūtāny ātmany evānupaśyati, sarva-bhūteṣu cātmānam"* — he who sees **all beings in the Self and the Self in all beings** no longer hates or shrinks; **"what delusion, what grief, can touch the seer of oneness?"** *(Source: Īśa Up. 6–7)* — verse shared in substance with the Bhagavad-gītā (6.29) and echoed in the Kaivalya; see [`./kaivalya-upanishad.md`](./kaivalya-upanishad.md).
- **The all-encompassing Lord (v. 8)** — *"sa paryagāc chukram akāyam avraṇam…"* — the Self is **bright, bodiless, woundless, sinewless, pure, untouched by evil**; the **wise, omniscient, self-existent (svayambhū)** who has duly ordered the worlds through the eternal years. *(Source: Īśa Up. 8)*

## 5. The Balanced-Pair Verses — *Vidyā/Avidyā* and *Saṃbhūti/Asaṃbhūti* (v. 9–14)

- **Into darkness either way (v. 9, 12)** — *"andhaṃ tamaḥ praviśanti ye 'vidyām upāsate; tato bhūya iva te tamo ya u vidyāyāṃ ratāḥ"* — **those devoted only to ignorance (avidyā) enter blind darkness, but into still greater darkness go those given solely to knowledge (vidyā).** *(Source: Īśa Up. 9)* — paralleled in v. 12 for **asaṃbhūti (the unmanifest / non-becoming)** vs. **saṃbhūti (becoming / the manifest)**. *(Source: Īśa Up. 12)* [the balanced-pair structure]
- **Crossing death by holding both (v. 11, 14)** — *"vidyāṃ cāvidyāṃ ca yas tad vedobhayaṃ saha, avidyayā mṛtyuṃ tīrtvā vidyayāmṛtam aśnute"* — **"He who knows both vidyā and avidyā together crosses death by avidyā and attains immortality by vidyā."** *(Source: Īśa Up. 11)* — the same structure is repeated for **saṃbhūti and asaṃbhūti** in v. 14. *(Source: Īśa Up. 14)*
- **What the pairs mean (interpretive variants)** —
  - *Variant (Śaṅkara / Advaita, Īśopaniṣad-bhāṣya):* *vidyā* = meditation/upāsanā on deities and *avidyā* = ritual works (karma); together they purify and qualify for liberating knowledge — neither alone suffices. **Saṃbhūti / asaṃbhūti** he reads as the **manifest (Hiraṇyagarbha) and the unmanifest (prakṛti / avyākṛta)**. *(Source: Śaṅkara)* [scholarly]
  - *Variant (other commentators):* the pairs are read as **knowledge vs. ignorance** and **being vs. non-being (the Manifest vs. the Unmanifest Brahman)**, the verse insisting that **both poles must be integrated** rather than one rejected. *(Source: Īśa Up. 9–14, comm. tradition)* [disputed gloss]
- **The recension difference here** — the **Kāṇva and Mādhyandina texts order these balanced-pair verses differently**, which is the single most-discussed textual variation in the Upaniṣad. *(Source: Kāṇva & Mādhyandina recensions)* [disputed ordering]

## 6. The Death / Cremation Prayer (v. 15–18)

- **The golden vessel (v. 15)** — *"hiraṇmayena pātreṇa satyasyāpihitaṃ mukham; tat tvaṃ pūṣann apāvṛṇu satya-dharmāya dṛṣṭaye"* — **"The face of Truth is covered by a golden vessel (disc); unveil it, O Pūṣan, for me devoted to the truth, that I may see."** *(Source: Īśa Up. 15)* — a prayer to **Pūṣan / the solar deity** to remove the dazzling disc hiding ultimate reality; widely read as the **dying person's or the cremation officiant's prayer**.
- **"That Person yonder, He am I" (v. 16)** — addressing **Pūṣan, the sole seer (ekarṣi), Yama, Sūrya, son of Prajāpati**, the speaker asks the rays to gather and the radiant form to be seen — *"yo 'sāv asau puruṣaḥ so 'ham asmi"* — **"the Person there in the sun, He am I."** *(Source: Īśa Up. 16)* — an explicit **identity of the individual with the solar Puruṣa / Brahman** at the moment of death.
- **Dissolution of the body (v. 17)** — *"vāyur anilam amṛtam, athedaṃ bhasmāntaṃ śarīram"* — let the **breath return to the immortal Wind**, and **this body end in ashes**; then the dying invocation *"Oṃ, krato smara, kṛtaṃ smara"* — **"O mind/will, remember; remember what was done."** *(Source: Īśa Up. 17)* — a literal **cremation-ground mantra**, releasing the elements to their cosmic sources.
- **Agne naya supathā (v. 18)** — *"agne naya supathā rāye asmān, viśvāni deva vayunāni vidvān; yuyodhy asmaj juhurāṇam eno, bhūyiṣṭhāṃ te nama-uktiṃ vidhema"* — **"O Agni, lead us by the good path to prosperity, O God who knowest all our deeds; keep far from us crooked-going sin; to thee we offer the fullest word of homage."** *(Source: Īśa Up. 18; = Ṛgveda 1.189.1)* — the closing **prayer to Agni** (also a Ṛgvedic verse) for guidance along the right path. [shared Ṛgvedic mantra]

## 7. The *Pūrṇam* Śānti-Mantra

- **The peace-invocation (śānti-pāṭha)** — the Īśa is prefaced (in recitation practice) by the famous **Pūrṇam mantra**: *"Oṃ pūrṇam adaḥ pūrṇam idaṃ, pūrṇāt pūrṇam udacyate; pūrṇasya pūrṇam ādāya pūrṇam evāvaśiṣyate. Oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ"* — **"That is full, this is full; from the full the full proceeds; taking the full from the full, the full alone remains."** *(Source: śānti-pāṭha of the Śukla Yajurveda Upaniṣads; cf. Bṛhadāraṇyaka V.1.1)* — a statement of the **infinitude (pūrṇa = the Absolute) that loses nothing in manifesting the world**; it is the shared peace-chant of the Śukla-Yajurveda Upaniṣads, conventionally attached to the Īśa. [shared śānti-mantra]

## 8. Why the Īśa Matters

- **The shortest gateway to Vedānta** — at 18 verses it is the **briefest principal Upaniṣad and the conventional first** of the set; its opening line is among the most-quoted in all Hindu thought, summarizing **theistic non-dualism** in five words. *(Source: Īśa Up. 1)*
- **The action–renunciation charter** — *"kurvann eveha karmāṇi"* makes the Īśa the Upaniṣadic **warrant for engaged life over world-flight**, a key proof-text for the **karma-yoga** synthesis later developed in the Bhagavad-gītā. *(Source: Īśa Up. 2)*
- **The integration ethic** — its **vidyā/avidyā** and **saṃbhūti/asaṃbhūti** verses refuse the easy rejection of either pole — knowledge *and* works, the unmanifest *and* the manifest — a hallmark of its balanced vision. *(Source: Īśa Up. 9–14)*
- **A living funeral text** — verses 15–18 are recited at **death and cremation**, giving this abstract Upaniṣad a permanent place in **antyeṣṭi (last-rites) liturgy**. *(Source: Īśa Up. 15–18)*
- **Two clocks, two recensions held open** — the **Kāṇva-vs-Mādhyandina** verse-ordering, the 17/18 count, and the **academic mid-millennium-BCE vs. eternal-Śruti** datings are all recorded here without reconciliation. [disputed] [scripture vs. academic dating]

## Sources

- **Īśa / Īśāvāsya Upaniṣad** (primary), = **Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā, chapter 40**, Śukla Yajurveda, **Kāṇva and Mādhyandina recensions** — all verses cited inline: the *Īśāvāsyam idaṃ sarvam* opening (v. 1), the *kurvann eveha karmāṇi* action verse (v. 2), the indwelling-Self verses (v. 4–8), the *vidyā/avidyā* and *saṃbhūti/asaṃbhūti* pairs (v. 9–14), and the *Hiraṇmayena pātreṇa* / *Agne naya supathā* death prayer (v. 15–18).
- **Śaṅkara, Īśopaniṣad-bhāṣya** — Advaita commentary; harmonization of the action and renunciation verses by adhikāra, and the manifest/unmanifest reading of saṃbhūti/asaṃbhūti.

- **R. E. Hume, *The Thirteen Principal Upanishads*** and **S. Radhakrishnan, *The Principal Upaniṣads*** — standard English collections placing the Īśa first in the *mukhya* set; F. Max Müller, *Sacred Books of the East* (Upaniṣads).
