# The Yoga Upanishads

> Part of [`90-literature-corpus/upanishads/`](./README.md) — a cross-text overview/catalog of the **~20 Yoga Upaniṣads** of the **Muktikā** canon: the Śruti-genre carriers of **haṭha / laya / mantra / dhyāna** yoga and of the **ṣaṭ-cakra (six-chakra), kuṇḍalinī, nāda and praṇava (Oṃ)** system — the principal **scriptural bridge between the classical Upaniṣads and Haṭha/Tantra yoga**. For the *mukhya* core they grow out of see [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md); for the Śruti corpus and Muktikā framing see [`../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md`](../shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md); for the **Yoga darśana** (Patañjali) and **Sāṃkhya** they presuppose see [`../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md`](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md); for the **Āgama/Tantra** stream they feed into see [`../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md`](../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md). ## 1. What the Yoga Upaniṣads Are

- **A thematic sub-group of the 108.** Of the **108 Upaniṣads** enumerated in the **Muktikā Upaniṣad's** closing list, modern editors recognize a cluster of about **twenty "Yoga Upaniṣads" (Yoga-Upaniṣad-s)** — minor (gauṇa) Śruti texts whose unifying subject is **yoga practice** rather than pure Brahman-metaphysics. *(Source: Muktikā Up.; A. Mahadeva Sastri, *The Yoga Upaniṣads*)*
- **The standard count: ~20.** The canonical edition — **A. Mahadeva Sastri's *The Yoga Upaniṣads*** (Adyar, 1920), with the commentary of **Upaniṣad-Brahma-Yogin** — fixes **twenty** texts: Advaya-tāraka, Amṛtabindu (Brahmabindu), Amṛtanāda, Kṣurikā, Tejobindu, Triśikhi-brāhmaṇa, Darśana, Dhyānabindu, Nādabindu, Pāśupata-brahma, Brahma-vidyā, Maṇḍala-brāhmaṇa, Mahā-vākya, Yoga-kuṇḍalinī, Yoga-cūḍāmaṇi, Yoga-tattva, Yoga-śikhā, Varāha, Śāṇḍilya, Haṃsa. *(Source: Mahadeva Sastri 1920)* [count varies by edition]
- **Why they matter in one line.** They are the **Śruti pedigree of yoga** — the texts that move the word *yoga* from the Kaṭha/Śvetāśvatara/Maitrī hints into a **full technical manual of breath, channels, locks, chakras and inner sound**, supplying the scriptural authority later claimed by the **Haṭha-yoga-pradīpikā, Gheraṇḍa-saṃhitā and Śiva-saṃhitā** and by **Tantra**.
- **The "two clocks."** *Academic:* a **late, post-classical layer** of the canon (most placed **c. 100 BCE – 1300 CE**, the bulk in the **early-medieval centuries**), drawing on Pātañjala-Yoga, Sāṃkhya and emerging Tantra/Haṭha. [scholarly] *Variant (orthodox / traditional):* eternal *apauruṣeya* Śruti "seen" by ṛṣis and fixed at the Dvāpara–Kali junction (~3102 BCE; see Kali-Yuga onset constant), not "authored" in a CE stratum. Both frames are recorded; neither is corrected. [scripture vs. academic dating]
- **Parent Vedas.** Affiliations vary by list — most attach to the **Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda** or the **Atharvaveda**, a few to the **Śukla Yajurveda** or **Sāmaveda**; the same text is often assigned differently across editions. *(Source: Muktikā Up.)* [disputed affiliation]

## 2. The Shared System They Teach

- **Ṣaḍaṅga / aṣṭāṅga yoga.** Several give a **six-limbed (ṣaḍaṅga)** yoga — *āsana, prāṇa-saṃyamana, pratyāhāra, dhāraṇā, dhyāna, samādhi* — rather than Patañjali's eight; others import the full **aṣṭāṅga**. *(Source: Amṛtanāda, Dhyānabindu, Yogacūḍāmaṇi Ups.)*
- **The four yogas.** The **Yogatattva** names the classic fourfold scheme — **mantra-yoga, laya-yoga, haṭha-yoga, rāja-yoga** — the earliest neat Śruti statement of the four. *(Source: Yogatattva Up.)*
- **Nāḍīs and prāṇa.** The body's **72,000 channels (nāḍī)**, the three principal **iḍā, piṅgalā, suṣumnā**, and the **ten vital airs (prāṇa, apāna, samāna, udāna, vyāna…)** are mapped for breath-control. *(Source: Dhyānabindu, Yogaśikhā Ups.)*
- **The ṣaṭ-cakra (six chakras).** The serpent-channel **suṣumnā** is threaded by the **six chakras — mūlādhāra, svādhiṣṭhāna, maṇipūra, anāhata, viśuddha, ājñā** — capped by the thousand-petalled **sahasrāra**; among the **oldest Śruti attestations of the chakra system** later standard in Tantra. *(Source: Yogacūḍāmaṇi, Yogaśikhā Ups.)* [foundational for the chakra schema]
- **Kuṇḍalinī.** The coiled **kuṇḍalinī-śakti** sleeping at the **mūlādhāra**, roused by breath/bandha to ascend the suṣumnā and unite with **Śiva at the sahasrāra** — yielding liberation; the **Yogakuṇḍalinī** is named for it. *(Source: Yogakuṇḍalinī, Yogacūḍāmaṇi Ups.)*
- **Nāda (inner sound) & laya.** **Laya-yoga** dissolves the mind by **listening to the unstruck inner sound (anāhata-nāda)** heard within; the **Nādabindu** and **Haṃsa** systematize the **ten nādas** (from cricket-hum to thunder). *(Source: Nādabindu, Haṃsa Ups.)*
- **Praṇava / Oṃ & the bindu.** Meditation on **Oṃ (praṇava)** resolved into **bīja, bindu (point), nāda and kalā** — and on **a-u-m + the silent fourth (turīya)** carried from the Māṇḍūkya into yogic interiorization. *(Source: Amṛtabindu, Dhyānabindu, Nādabindu Ups.)*
- **Bandhas, mudrās & khecarī.** Locks (**mūla-, uḍḍiyāna-, jālandhara-bandha**), seals (**mudrā**), and the **khecarī-mudrā** (tongue turned back to taste the **amṛta** dripping from the cranial moon) — distinctively **Haṭha** technique appearing already in these Śruti texts. *(Source: Yogakuṇḍalinī, Yogatattva Ups.)*

## 3. The Key Texts — Catalog

- **Yogatattva Upaniṣad ("Essence of Yoga")** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. A **frame dialogue (Viṣṇu/Hiraṇyagarbha teaching)** giving the **four yogas** (mantra/laya/haṭha/rāja), the **eight aṅgas**, **āsanas, prāṇāyāma, the siddhis (yogic powers)** and the obstacles to practice; among the most systematic of the group. *(Source: Yogatattva Up.)*
- **Yogaśikhā Upaniṣad ("Crest of Yoga")** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. The **longest** Yoga Upaniṣad; **Śiva instructs** on the union of prāṇa and mind, the **nāḍīs and chakras**, kuṇḍalinī, and **haṭha + rāja** as one integrated yoga culminating in the *jīvan-mukta*. *(Source: Yogaśikhā Up.)*
- **Yogacūḍāmaṇi Upaniṣad ("Crest-Jewel of Yoga")** — Sāmaveda (by common attribution). A concise **manual of the six chakras, the nāḍīs, the prāṇas, bandhas and mudrās**, and the **haṃsa-mantra (so'ham)**; a key early chakra-list text. *(Source: Yogacūḍāmaṇi Up.)*
- **Yogakuṇḍalinī (Yogakuṇḍalī) Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. Devoted to **rousing kuṇḍalinī** by **bhastrikā breath + the bandhas**, the **khecarī-mudrā** and the drinking of cranial **amṛta**; ends in **rāja-yoga / Brahman**. *(Source: Yogakuṇḍalinī Up.)*
- **Dhyānabindu Upaniṣad ("Point of Meditation")** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. Meditation on **Oṃ and the haṃsa**, the **bindu**; the **six chakras, the nāḍīs, the prāṇas and ṣaḍaṅga-yoga**; teaches that even mountain-sized sin is burnt by yoga. *(Source: Dhyānabindu Up.)*
- **Nādabindu Upaniṣad ("Point of Sound")** — Ṛgveda (by common attribution). The classic **nāda / laya-yoga** text: the **Oṃkāra figured as the haṃsa-bird**, the dissolution of mind in the **ten inner sounds (nādas)**, and the **jīvan-mukta** who is unaffected by the body. *(Source: Nādabindu Up.)*
- **Haṃsa Upaniṣad ("The Swan/So'ham")** — Śukla-Yajurveda. **Sanatkumāra–Gautama** dialogue on the **haṃsa-vidyā / ajapā-gāyatrī** (the spontaneous *so'ham* of the breath), the **eight-petalled heart-lotus** and the **ten nādas**; the *paramahaṃsa* ideal. *(Source: Haṃsa Up.)*
- **Amṛtabindu Upaniṣad ("Point of Immortality"; = Brahmabindu)** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. A **very short, famous** text: the **mind alone is the cause of bondage and release**, the **two Brahmans (śabda / aśabda)**, and meditation on **Oṃ** to still the mind — bridging Vedānta and yoga. *(Source: Amṛtabindu Up.)*
- **Amṛtanāda Upaniṣad ("Sound of Immortality")** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. Sets out the **six-limbed (ṣaḍaṅga) yoga** and the dissolution of **Oṃ through its mātrās into the soundless (amātra)** state. *(Source: Amṛtanāda Up.)*
- **Tejobindu Upaniṣad ("Point of Light")** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. A long **Advaita-leaning** Yoga text: a **fifteen-limbed yoga**, the *jīvan-mukti* and *videha-mukti*, and **Śiva/Brahman** as pure consciousness; meditation is hard "as on a subtle point of light." *(Source: Tejobindu Up.)*
- **Advayatāraka Upaniṣad ("Saviour of Non-Duality")** — Śukla-Yajurveda. The **tāraka- and amanaska-yoga** of the **inner/outer/intermediate light-vision (lakṣya)** and the *guru* as the one who reveals the non-dual; defines **guru** etymologically (*gu* = darkness, *ru* = its remover). *(Source: Advayatāraka Up.)*
- **Maṇḍala-brāhmaṇa Upaniṣad** — Śukla-Yajurveda. **Yājñavalkya receives from the Sun-Puruṣa** the **aṣṭāṅga / tāraka-yoga**, **śāmbhavī-mudrā**, the inner **lights** and the *jīvan-mukta*. *(Source: Maṇḍalabrāhmaṇa Up.)*
- **Śāṇḍilya Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. A large compendium: the **eight aṅgas with ten yamas and ten niyamas**, **nāḍī-purification, prāṇāyāma, the chakras and kuṇḍalinī**, framed as **Śāṇḍilya questioning Atharvan**. *(Source: Śāṇḍilya Up.)*
- **Varāha Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. **Viṣṇu as the Boar (Varāha)** teaches the sage **Ṛbhu**: the *tattvas*, the **jīvan-mukta and videha-mukta**, and a **practical yoga** of nāḍīs, prāṇa and meditation. *(Source: Varāha Up.)*
- **Triśikhi-brāhmaṇa Upaniṣad** — Śukla-Yajurveda. The Sun teaches a **ṛṣi (Triśikhi)** the unity of **jñāna- and karma-yoga**, then a full **aṣṭāṅga-yoga** with chakras and prāṇa. *(Source: Triśikhibrāhmaṇa Up.)*
- **Darśana (Jābāla-Darśana) Upaniṣad** — Sāmaveda. **Dattātreya teaches Sāṃkṛti** a complete **aṣṭāṅga-yoga** — yamas/niyamas, the nine "doors," the nāḍīs, the chakras and kuṇḍalinī. *(Source: Darśana Up.)*
- **Kṣurikā Upaniṣad ("The Razor")** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. A short text using the **"knife" of concentration** to **cut the body's nāḍī-knots** and sever attachment, freeing the yogi. *(Source: Kṣurikā Up.)*
- **Brahmavidyā Upaniṣad** — Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda. On the **science of Oṃ** — its **body, seat and dissolution (laya)** through **bīja, śakti, nāda, bindu and kalā** — and the **haṃsa**. *(Source: Brahmavidyā Up.)*
- **Pāśupata-brahma Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. The **haṃsa / so'ham** and **praṇava** combined with **Pāśupata (Śaiva)** doctrine — the inner sacrifice and the *jīvan-mukta*. *(Source: Pāśupatabrahma Up.)*
- **Mahāvākya Upaniṣad** — Atharvaveda. A brief text on the **"great saying"** and the **inner sun/light (arka)** realization, joining mahāvākya-jñāna to meditative yoga. *(Source: Mahāvākya Up.)*

## 4. Where They Sit in the Stream

- **Roots upstream.** They unfold seeds in the **Kaṭha** (chariot allegory; "the firm holding of the senses, that is yoga," 2.3.11), the **Śvetāśvatara** (posture, breath-restraint, the body as kindling-stick, the visions of yoga, 2.8–2.15), and the **Maitrī** (its **ṣaḍaṅga-yoga**) — see [`../principal-upanishads-detail.md`](../principal-upanishads-detail.md). *(Source: Kaṭha, Śvetāśvatara, Maitrī Ups.)*
- **The darśana alongside.** They presuppose and often paraphrase **Patañjali's Yoga-sūtra** and **Sāṃkhya** metaphysics (puruṣa/prakṛti, the guṇas) — the classical **Yoga darśana** detailed in [`../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md`](../upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md). *(Source: Yoga-sūtra; Yogatattva, Triśikhibrāhmaṇa Ups.)*
- **Bridge downstream.** Their **chakra, nāḍī, kuṇḍalinī, bandha, mudrā and nāda** material is the direct ancestor of the **Haṭha-yoga manuals (Haṭha-yoga-pradīpikā, Gheraṇḍa-saṃhitā, Śiva-saṃhitā)** and of **Tantra/Āgama** subtle-body theory — see [`../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md`](../agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md). [scriptural-to-practice bridge]
- **The Saṃnyāsa cousins.** Editors set them beside the **Saṃnyāsa Upaniṣads** (on renunciation) as the two great minor-Upaniṣad "practice" groups — the Yoga set on *technique*, the Saṃnyāsa set on *the renunciate's life*. *(Source: Muktikā Up.)*
