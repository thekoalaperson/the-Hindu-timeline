# Upavedas, Vedāngas & the Six Darśanas

> Part of the **[Literature Corpus](./)** — the texts that *record* the cosmic timeline. This node maps the **secondary/applied Vedic literature**: the four **Upavedas** (applied sciences), the six **Vedāngas** (limbs that preserve and decode the Vedas), and the six orthodox **Darśanas** (philosophical viewpoints) with their Vedānta sub-schools. These are the interpretive and technical apparatus around the [Vedas & Samhitas](./shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md), composed and transmitted within the current [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/), mostly in [Treta](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)–[Dvapara](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and early [Kali Yuga](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) by tradition; academically c. 1500 BCE – 500 CE.

Traditional schema: the corpus radiates outward from the four Vedas. Each Veda has an **Upaveda** (applied science) and is served by the shared **Vedāngas** (auxiliary disciplines). The **Darśanas** are the systematic philosophies built atop the Vedic revelation. Standard count: **4 Upavedas, 6 Vedāngas, 6 Darśanas** — but the membership of the Upaveda list varies by tradition (see variants).

---

## 1. The Four Upavedas (उपवेद — "applied / subsidiary knowledge")

Each Upaveda is a body of applied/technical knowledge subordinated to a parent Veda. The standard parent-Veda assignment follows the **Charanavyuha** (a parishishta attributed to Shaunaka); other lists differ on both membership and parentage.

- **Āyurveda — science of life & medicine** (parent: **Atharvaveda**). The "knowledge of longevity"; diagnosis, eight branches (*ashtanga*: internal medicine, surgery, ENT, pediatrics, toxicology, rejuvenation, aphrodisiacs, psychiatry/demonology). Founding mythic transmission: from **Brahma → Daksha Prajapati → Ashvins → Indra → Dhanvantari/Bharadvaja → human sages** (*Charaka Samhita*, *Sushruta Samhita*). Core texts: **Charaka Samhita** (internal medicine, redacted by Charaka from Agnivesha's school), **Sushruta Samhita** (surgery), **Ashtanga Hridaya/Sangraha** of Vagbhata. Divine physician **Dhanvantari** emerges at the [Samudra Manthana](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/01-satya-yuga.md) bearing the pot of amrita (*Bhagavata Purana*, *Vishnu Purana*).
  - *Variant (parentage):* some sources attach Āyurveda to the **Rigveda** rather than Atharvaveda; the *Sushruta Samhita* itself calls Āyurveda an **upānga (limb) of the Atharvaveda**.
- **Dhanurveda — science of warfare & archery** (parent: **Yajurveda**). The "knowledge of the bow"; military science, weaponry, formations, martial arts (the root tradition behind *dhanurvidya* and later regional arts). Mythic teachers: **Vishvamitra, Parashurama, Drona, Bhishma** as masters (*Mahabharata*, *Agni Purana* ch. on Dhanurveda, *Dhanurveda Samhita*). Classifies weapons into four classes: *mukta* (thrown), *amukta* (held), *muktamukta* (thrown/withdrawn), *yantramukta* (machine-launched).
  - *Variant (text):* the extant **Vasishtha Dhanurveda** and **Dhanurveda sections of the Agni Purana** are the main surviving redactions; no single complete primary "Dhanurveda" survives — it is reconstructed from epic and Puranic embeddings. [late/composite]
- **Gāndharvaveda — science of music, dance & aesthetics** (parent: **Samaveda**). The "knowledge of the gandharvas (celestial musicians)"; vocal/instrumental music, dance, and broadly the performing and fine arts; sometimes extended to poetics and erotics. Foundational text: **Natya Shastra** of Bharata Muni (drama, music, dance, rasa theory) — itself called a "fifth Veda" (*Natya Shastra* 1.1–1.5). Mythic origin: Brahma distills it from the four Vedas (recitation from Rig, song from Sama, gesture from Yajur, rasa from Atharva) (*Natya Shastra*).
  - *Variant (scope):* some traditions restrict Gāndharvaveda to **music alone**; others (e.g., the broader aesthetic reading) include **sculpture, painting, poetry, and kama-shastra** under it.
- **Sthāpatyaveda — science of architecture & engineering** (parent: **Rigveda**). The "knowledge of building/structures"; town planning, temple architecture, sculpture-iconography, *vastu*. Core literature: **Vastu Shastra**, **Mayamata**, **Manasara**, **Shilpa Shastras**. Mythic architects: **Vishvakarma** (architect of the devas) and **Maya** (architect of the asuras) (*Mahabharata* — Maya builds the Pandavas' hall at Indraprastha; *Matsya Purana*, *Agni Purana* on vastu).

**VARIANT — membership of the Upaveda list:**
- *Variant (Charanavyuha / classical):* the four are **Āyurveda, Dhanurveda, Gāndharvaveda, Sthāpatyaveda** (the list used above and most common today).
- *Variant (alternative fourth):* several authorities replace **Sthāpatyaveda with Arthaveda / Arthashastra** (statecraft & economics, parent often given as Atharvaveda), citing Kautilya's tradition.
- *Variant (parent-Veda swaps):* assignments are not uniform across sources — e.g., Gāndharvaveda is almost always Samaveda, but Āyurveda (Rig vs Atharva), Dhanurveda (Yajur), Sthāpatyaveda/Artha (Rig vs Atharva) vary by list (Charanavyuha vs later compilations). Catalog: treat the parent-Veda mapping as **tradition-dependent, not fixed.**

## 2. The Six Vedāngas (वेदाङ्ग — "limbs of the Veda")

Auxiliary disciplines required to correctly **recite, preserve, understand, and ritually apply** the Vedas. Traditional mnemonic body-image (from the *Pratishakhya/Shiksha* tradition): Shiksha = nose, Kalpa = hands, Vyakarana = mouth, Nirukta = ears, Chhanda = feet, Jyotisha = eyes. Functionally paired: **Shiksha + Chhanda** (correct recitation), **Vyakarana + Nirukta** (correct meaning), **Kalpa + Jyotisha** (correct ritual time & method).

- **Shikshā — phonetics & pronunciation.** Letters, accent (*svara*), pitch, duration, euphony (*sandhi*) for flawless Vedic chanting. Foundational: the **Pratishakhyas** (Veda-specific) and the **Paniniya Shiksha** (attributed to Panini's school). Goal: preserve oral text with zero corruption.
- **Kalpa — ritual procedure / liturgy.** The "applied science of the Vedas"; rules for sacrifice and ceremony. Sub-genres: **Shrauta Sutras** (public/fire rituals), **Grihya Sutras** (domestic rites), **Dharma Sutras** (law/conduct), **Shulba Sutras** (altar geometry — earliest Indian mathematics). Authors/schools: **Baudhayana, Apastamba, Katyayana, Ashvalayana, Drahyayana** (*Kalpa Sutra* corpus).
  - *Note:* the **Shulba Sutras** (esp. Baudhayana) contain a statement of the Pythagorean theorem and altar-area transformations — the geometric root recorded here. [scholarly significance]
- **Vyākarana — grammar.** Analysis of word formation to fix correct usage. Supreme text: **Ashtadhyayi of Pāṇini** (~4th c. BCE) — c. 4,000 sutras, the foundational generative grammar. Commentarial triad ("trimuni"): **Pāṇini (sutras) → Kātyāyana (Varttikas) → Patañjali (Mahābhāshya)**. Also **Vararuchi** in the commentarial tradition.
  - *Variant (authorship of Mahabhashya's Patanjali):* tradition sometimes identifies the grammarian Patañjali with the **Yoga-sutra Patañjali**; most scholars treat them as **distinct persons.** [disputed identity]
- **Nirukta — etymology & semantics.** Derivation and meaning of (especially obscure Vedic) words; root (*dhatu*) analysis. Foundational: the **Nirukta of Yāska** (~5th–6th c. BCE), itself a commentary on the **Nighantu** (a Vedic glossary/word-list). Yaska preserves earlier etymological debate (e.g., with Gargya, Aupamanyava).
- **Chhanda(s) — prosody / meter.** Vedic and classical meters (*Gayatri, Anushtubh, Trishtubh, Jagati,* etc.), counted by syllables or morae. Foundational: the **Chandah Shastra of Pingala** — which, in enumerating meter combinations, encodes **binary numeration, the "Pascal's triangle" (Meru-prastara), and an early reference to zero/combinatorics.** [scholarly significance]
- **Jyotisha — astronomy & timekeeping (and astrology).** Determines the auspicious/correct time for rituals via positions of nakshatras, sun, moon. Foundational: the **Vedanga Jyotisha** (attributed to **Lagadha**), the oldest Indian astronomical text. Later flowering: **Aryabhatiya** (Aryabhata, 499 CE), **Surya Siddhanta** (the frame source for this timeline's [cosmology constants](../00-time-and-cosmology/)), **Brahmasphutasiddhanta** (Brahmagupta).
  - *Variant (split):* later tradition divides Jyotisha into three skandhas — **Siddhanta (mathematical astronomy), Samhita (mundane/omens), Hora (predictive/horoscopic astrology).**

**VARIANT — ordering:** the six are sometimes listed in a "recitation→meaning→application" sequence (Shiksha, Chhanda, Vyakarana, Nirukta, Jyotisha, Kalpa) and sometimes by the body-limb mnemonic; **membership of the six is fixed and undisputed**, only the order varies.

## 3. The Six Darśanas (षड्दर्शन — orthodox / āstika philosophical "viewpoints")

The six **āstika** (Veda-accepting) systems, vs. the **nāstika** (Veda-rejecting: Buddhism, Jainism, Charvaka — cataloged in [heterodox traditions](./README.md)). Traditionally grouped in **three complementary pairs**: Nyaya–Vaisheshika (logic + atomism), Samkhya–Yoga (enumeration + practice), Mimamsa–Vedanta (ritual + knowledge). Each rests on a **root sutra text** ascribed to a founder-sage, plus a *bhashya* (commentary) layer.

- **Nyāya — logic & epistemology.** Founder: **Gautama (Akshapada Gautama)**. Root text: **Nyaya Sutras**. Doctrine: realism; liberation through correct knowledge via four *pramanas* (perception, inference, comparison, testimony); 16 categories of debate/logic. Key commentary: **Nyaya Bhashya of Vatsyayana**; later **Navya-Nyaya** ("new logic") of **Gangesha** (*Tattvachintamani*, 14th c.).
- **Vaiśeṣika — atomism & natural categories.** Founder: **Kaṇāda (Uluka)**. Root text: **Vaisheshika Sutras**. Doctrine: reality reduced to categories (*padarthas*); matter built from indivisible **atoms (*anu/paramanu*)** combining to form the world; pluralistic realism. Often treated as an **offshoot of / merged with Nyaya** (the combined **Nyaya-Vaisheshika** school).
- **Sāṃkhya — dualist enumeration / metaphysics.** Founder: **Kapila** (mythic; said to be an avatara of Vishnu in *Bhagavata Purana*). Root text traditionally the **Samkhya Sutras**, but the authoritative classical text is the **Sāṃkhya-kārikā of Īśvarakṛṣṇa** (~4th–5th c. CE). Doctrine: dualism of **Purusha (consciousness)** and **Prakriti (primordial matter)**; the **25 tattvas**; world evolves from Prakriti's three *gunas* (sattva, rajas, tamas); liberation by discriminative knowledge.
  - *Variant (theism):* classical Samkhya is **non-theistic / atheistic** (*nirishvara*) — no creator God is required; this is its signature divergence from Yoga. [doctrinal variant]
- **Yoga — disciplined practice / meditation.** Founder/codifier: **Patañjali**. Root text: **Yoga Sutras** (~2nd c. BCE–4th c. CE). Doctrine: shares Samkhya metaphysics **plus Ishvara (a special Purusha/God)**; the **eight limbs (ashtanga: yama, niyama, asana, pranayama, pratyahara, dharana, dhyana, samadhi)**; goal *kaivalya* (isolation/liberation). Chief commentary: **Yoga Bhashya of Vyasa**.
  - *Variant:* Yoga = "**Seshvara Samkhya**" (Samkhya-with-God), the theistic counterpart to atheistic Samkhya.
- **Mīmāṃsā (Purva Mimamsa) — ritual exegesis & hermeneutics.** Founder: **Jaimini**. Root text: **Purva Mimamsa Sutras**. Doctrine: the Vedas are **eternal and authorless (*apaurusheya*)**; meaning lies in **action/ritual (*dharma*)**; develops rules of textual interpretation; classically holds the Veda's authority does **not** depend on a God. Major sub-schools: **Bhatta (Kumarila Bhatta)** and **Prabhakara (Guru Prabhakara)**.
- **Vedānta (Uttara Mimamsa) — knowledge of Brahman.** Founder: **Bādarāyaṇa (often identified with Vyasa)**. Root text: **Brahma Sutras (Vedanta Sutras)**. The "**prasthana-trayi**" (three foundations) of all Vedanta: **Upanishads + Brahma Sutras + Bhagavad Gita**. Doctrine concerns the nature of Brahman, Atman, and liberation — and splits into multiple sub-schools (below).

### Vedānta sub-schools (each = a different reading of the prasthana-trayi)

- **Advaita — non-dualism.** **Adi Shankara (Śaṅkarācārya, ~8th c. CE).** Brahman alone is real; the world is *maya* (appearance); **Atman = Brahman** (identity). Liberation = realizing this non-difference. Texts: Shankara's *bhashyas* on the prasthana-trayi, *Vivekachudamani* (attrib.).
- **Vishishtadvaita — qualified non-dualism.** **Rāmānuja (~11th–12th c. CE).** Brahman (identified with Vishnu/Narayana) is one but qualified by real souls and matter as its "body"; *bhakti* and *prapatti* (surrender) central. Text: *Sri Bhashya*.
- **Dvaita (Tattvavada) — dualism.** **Madhvācārya (~13th c. CE).** Five eternal distinctions; God (Vishnu), souls (*jiva*), and matter (*jada/maya*) are **distinct realities**; liberation by grace + bhakti.
- **Dvaitadvaita — dualistic non-dualism.** **Nimbārka (~13th c.).** Soul/world both distinct from and dependent on Brahman (Krishna); Bhedabheda lineage.
- **Shuddhadvaita — pure non-dualism.** **Vallabha (~15th–16th c.).** World is real and is Brahman (Krishna); rejects maya-as-illusion; *pushtimarga* devotion.
- **Achintya Bheda Abheda — inconceivable difference & non-difference.** **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (~15th–16th c.)** / Gaudiya Vaishnava. Soul and God are simultaneously and inconceivably one and different.
  - *Variant (count):* tradition variously lists **three** core Vedanta schools (Advaita, Vishishtadvaita, Dvaita) or **five/six** (adding Bhedabheda/Dvaitadvaita, Shuddhadvaita, Achintya-bhedabheda). Some also add **Bhedabheda (Bhaskara)** and **Akshara-Brahma / Suddhadvaita lineages.** Catalog all; the "three vs. more" framing is itself the variant.

**VARIANT — counting the systems:**
- *Variant (six āstika):* the standard **shad-darshana** = Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Mimamsa, Vedanta (as above).
- *Variant (broader doxographies):* texts like the **Sarva-darshana-sangraha (Madhava-Vidyaranya, 14th c.)** enumerate far more systems (16), including Charvaka, Buddhist and Jain schools, Pashupata/Shaiva, Panini-darshana, etc. — i.e., "darshana" used in the wide sense (all viewpoints), not just the six orthodox.
- *Variant (pairing vs. independence):* some hold the six are **three real schools in pairs** (Nyaya-Vaisheshika, Samkhya-Yoga, Mimamsa-Vedanta); others treat all six as independent.
