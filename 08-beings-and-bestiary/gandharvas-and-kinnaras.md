# Gandharvas & Kinnaras

> Part of [08 · Beings & Bestiary](README.md) → the **celestial-musician roster** of the heavenly courts.
> Cosmic address: residents of *Gandharvaloka* / *Svarga* / the slopes of *Kailāsa* (see [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md)).
> Paired class: the [Apsarās](apsaras.md) (their dancer-wives). For marriage by mutual consent (*gandharva vivāha*) cross-link [05 · Concepts & Dharma](../05-concepts-and-dharma/).

The **gandharvas** (गन्धर्व) are the male celestial musicians and singers of Hindu cosmology — handsome, fragrant-robed virtuosi who play the heavenly arts at Indra's court, **guard the Soma** (the sacred draught), husband the [apsarās](apsaras.md), and stand as a liminal class between *devas* and humans. The **kinnaras** (किन्नर, "what-man?") and **kimpuruṣas** (किम्पुरुष, "what-man?") are a parallel hybrid order — half-horse/half-human (India) or half-bird/half-human (later, Southeast-Asian iconography) — who serve as choristers in **Kubera's** court on Kailāsa. Both are large but **finite** classes: the *Atharvaveda* fixes the gandharvas at **6,333**, yet only a finite set of **named** musicians recurs across the Vedas, Mahābhārata, Rāmāyaṇa, Purāṇas and Jain/Buddhist cosmologies. This node catalogs the named roster and every major count/list/etymology variant.

---

## Etymology & Nature (variants)

- **Gandharva** - Classically derived from *gandha* ("fragrance") + *arva*, "those who deal in fragrances/celestial scents"; Vedic *gandharva* is a single sky-spirit (a guardian of Soma and knower of divine secrets) before the term becomes a plural **class**. *(Source: Ṛgveda; later Purāṇic systematization.)*
- *Variant (count):* the *Atharvaveda* (11.5.2 tradition) names **6,333 gandharvas**. *(Source: Atharvaveda.)* — treat the full roster as **open-ended**; only named exemplars are cataloged below.
- *Variant (origin):* gandharvas are variously called creatures **of Prajāpati, of Brahmā, of Kaśyapa & Pradhā, of the Munis, of Ariṣṭā, or of Vāc**. *(Source: Mahābhārata; Purāṇic genealogies.)*
- *Variant (Bhāgavata):* produced from **Brahmā's laughter** (alongside other semi-divine orders). *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa.)*
- **Kinnara / Kimpuruṣa** - Both names are riddling compounds: *kim* ("what?") + *nara/puruṣa* ("man") — "is-this-a-man?" — for the half-human hybrids. Indian iconography: human torso + horse's head/body, or man's head + horse's body; later Southeast-Asian art renders the female **kinnarī** as half-bird. *(Source: classical Sanskrit lexicons; wisdomlib.)* Often treated as **the same tribe** as the kimpuruṣas. [disputed]

## Realm, Soma-Guardianship & Function

- **Gandharvaloka / Indraloka** - Gandharvas usually dwell in **Svarga**, serving at Indra's court, but also hold their **own world, Gandharvaloka**. *(Source: Purāṇic cosmology.)*
- **Soma-guardians** - A core Vedic office: gandharvas **guard the Soma** and play music for the devas in their palaces. *(Source: Ṛgveda; Atharvaveda.)*
- **Husbands of the apsarās** - The standing pairing: gandharvas (music) + [apsarās](apsaras.md) (dance) form the heavenly performing arts. *(Source: Purāṇas; Nāṭyaśāstra tradition.)*
- **Gandharva-yāma / -velā** - The pre-dawn watch sacred to gandharvas, when humans should not roam — the temporal trigger of the Arjuna–Aṅgāraparṇa duel (below). *(Source: MBh Ādi Parva.)*

## The Gandharva Form of Marriage (*gandharva vivāha*)

- **Gandharva vivāha** - One of the **eight classical forms of marriage** (*aṣṭa-vivāha*); a union **by mutual consent and mutual affection, without formal ritual, dowry, or parental sanction** — the "love-match." Named for the gandharvas, paragons of spontaneous desire. *(Source: Manusmṛti 3.21–34; Mahābhārata.)*
- **Archetypal instance** - **Duṣyanta & Śakuntalā** wed by gandharva rite in the forest hermitage of Kaṇva — the locus classicus, dramatized by Kālidāsa. *(Source: MBh Ādi Parva; Abhijñānaśākuntalam.)* — see [Śakuntalā & Bharata](../04-deep-dives/shakuntala-bharata.md).

## The Named Gandharvas

### Chitraratha / Aṅgāraparṇa (king of the gandharvas)
- **Chitraratha** ("of the bright chariot"), also **Aṅgāraparṇa** ("of the blazing-coal wings/chariot") - The **chief / king of the gandharvas**, ruler of their host. Famed encounter: confronting the **Pāṇḍavas** at the Gaṅgā at the gandharva-hour as they walked toward Pāñcāla, he challenged **Arjuna**; Arjuna burned his chariot with the **Āgneyāstra** and felled him. Spared at the plea of his wife **Kumbhīnasī** (and Yudhiṣṭhira), he gifted Arjuna the **Cākṣuṣī-mantra** (supernatural sight, devised by Manu, transmitted via Soma → Viśvāvasu → Chitraratha) in exchange for the **Āgneyāstra**, then recounted the **Tapatī–Saṃvaraṇa** tale, the **Vasiṣṭha–Viśvāmitra** feud, and the Vāsiṣṭha/Bhārgava sagas; he conceded that **Arjuna's brahmacarya** was the deciding factor in his defeat. *(Source: MBh Ādi Parva, Caitrarathaparva 169–172.)* — see [Vishwāmitra & Vasiṣṭha](../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md).
  - *Variant:* "Chitraratha" is also borne by other figures (a king of Aṅga; an Aṃśa); the gandharva-king is the one identified with **Aṅgāraparṇa**. [disputed]

### Viśvāvasu (the Soma-knower)
- **Viśvāvasu** - A pre-eminent gandharva, a **link in the Cākṣuṣī-mantra transmission** (Soma → Viśvāvasu → Chitraratha). In the **apsarā tales** he is named as consort/father — e.g., father of **Pramadvarā** (by Menakā), the maiden wed to Ruru. A recurring "knower of secrets" in Vedic-flavored episodes. *(Source: MBh Ādi Parva; Ṛgvedic gandharva imagery.)*

### Tumburu (best of the gandharvas)
- **Tumburu** (Tumbaru) - Often named **the foremost gandharva musician**; son of sage **Kaśyapa and Pradhā**. Perpetual musical companion/rival of **Nārada** — the two are paired as the great celestial singers, and a tradition holds Tumburu's vīṇā-craft superior, the source of Nārada's humbling and tapas. Attends Śiva and Viṣṇu in devotional song. *(Source: MBh; Purāṇas.)* — see [Nārada](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/) for the rivalry.

### Hāhā & Huhū (the singing pair)
- **Hāhā & Huhū** (also Haha & Huhu) - A celebrated **gandharva duo**, archetypal master-singers named together in lists from the Vedas through the Jain *Tiloyapaṇṇatti*. In the **Gajendra-mokṣa** frame, **Huhū**, sporting in water, **pulled the leg of sage Devala** (var. Devala/Durvāsa) and was cursed to become the **crocodile (makara/grāha)** that seizes the elephant **Gajendra** — released when Viṣṇu liberates Gajendra. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.2–4; MBh; Tiloyapaṇṇatti.)*
  - *Variant:* a popular retelling makes **both Hāhā and Huhū** the cursed pair after a singing-superiority quarrel. [folk]
  - *Variant (lineage):* **Vijaya**, a gandharva on pilgrimage, is named **son of Huhū**. *(Source: Garga Saṃhitā.)*

### Chitrasena (Arjuna's music-master)
- **Chitrasena** - A leading gandharva of Indra's court who **taught Arjuna music, song and dance** during his celestial sojourn in Amarāvatī (after Urvaśī's curse). Later **defeats and captures Duryodhana** at the Ghoṣa-yātrā, only for the Pāṇḍavas to rescue him — a sharp inversion of dharma. *(Source: MBh Vana Parva, Ghoṣa-yātrā Parva.)*

### Chitrāngada
- **Chitrāngada** - A named gandharva of the roster; also the name of a Kuru prince (Bhīṣma's half-brother) **slain by a like-named gandharva** in a duel over the name itself. *(Source: MBh Ādi Parva.)* [disputed — name shared across a man and a gandharva]

### Other named gandharvas
- **Drumila** - Named in the standard gandharva lists of Indra's court. *(Source: Purāṇic rosters.)*
- **Caṇḍavega** ("fierce-swift") - A gandharva who appears in the allegorical **Purañjana** narrative (the 360 gandharvas of Time besieging the city-of-the-body). *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.27.)*
  - *Sub-roster:* the Purañjana allegory deploys **360 gandharvas (and 360 gandharvīs)** as the **days/nights of the year** — Caṇḍavega is their leader. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.)*
- **Sūryavarcas, Yugapā, Tṛṇapā** and others - Filler names in extended MBh/Purāṇic gandharva enumerations; rosters vary by recension. *[late text]*

### Cross-tradition names (variants)
- *Variant (Buddhist, DN.20 / DN.32):* gandharva (gandhabba) leaders include **Panāda, Opamañña, Nala, Cittasena (= Chitrasena), Mātali, Janesabha**. *(Source: Dīgha Nikāya.)*
- *Variant (Jain, Tiloyapaṇṇatti):* the *vyantara* gandharvas include **Hāhā, Huhū, Nārada, Tumbara (= Tumburu), Vāsava, Kadamba**. *(Source: Tiloyapaṇṇatti.)*

## Kinnaras & Kimpuruṣas

- **Kinnara** - Half-human hybrid (human + horse), **paradigmatic lover and celestial musician**; like the **yakṣas**, kinnaras are **attendants of Kubera**, dwelling in his paradise on **Kailāsa** and singing in his court. *(Source: classical Sanskrit literature; wisdomlib.)*
  - *Variant (form):* India = half-**horse**/half-man; later Southeast Asia = half-**bird** (the female **kinnarī**, half-bird/half-woman). [late text]
- **Kimpuruṣa** - Closely identified with (or the same tribe as) the kinnaras; "is-this-a-man?" hybrids, sometimes glossed as **half-man/half-woman** (hermaphrodite) or **lion-/horse-headed**. Frequent the slopes of Kailāsa as musicians. *(Source: Mahābhārata; Bhāgavata & Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇas.)* [disputed — leonine vs. equine head]
- **Kimpuruṣa, son of Agnīdhra** - The eponymous **king/ancestor** of the kimpuruṣas; son of **Agnīdhra** by the apsarā **Pūrvacitti**, granted the **Kimpuruṣa-varṣa** as his hereditary portion. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 5.2.)* — see [Apsarās → Pūrvacitti](apsaras.md).
- **Kimpuruṣa-varṣa** - One of the **nine varṣas of Jambūdvīpa**, the land lying between the **Hemakūṭa** ranges, named for and peopled by the kimpuruṣas; its presiding worship is of **Rāma & Hanumān** (Bhāgavata). *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 5; Purāṇic geography.)* — see [06 · Sacred Geography](../06-sacred-geography/).
- **Kinnaras under Kubera** - Together with yakṣas and guhyakas, kinnaras **guard Kubera's lotus-ponds and treasures** and provide music at Alakā/Kailāsa. *(Source: Purāṇas; Meghadūta imagery.)* — see [Kubera](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/).
- *Variant (Jain):* kinnaras and kimpuruṣas are both **vyantara** (peripatetic) celestials; the kimpuruṣa class has two lords, **Satpuruṣa & Mahāpuruṣa**. *(Source: Jain cosmology, Tiloyapaṇṇatti.)*

## Relations Summary

- **Gandharva ↔ Apsarā** - canonical husband/wife performing pair. *(Source: Purāṇas.)* — see [Apsarās](apsaras.md).
- **Gandharva ↔ Deva** - musicians and Soma-guards of Indra's court. *(Source: Vedas.)*
- **Kinnara/Kimpuruṣa ↔ Kubera** - choristers and treasure-guards on Kailāsa. *(Source: Purāṇas.)*
- **Tumburu ↔ Nārada** - the two supreme celestial singers / rivals. *(Source: MBh, Purāṇas.)*
- **Roster status:** the **named** musicians are finite and cataloged here; the **total population** (6,333 gandharvas per the Atharvaveda; 360 in the Purañjana allegory) is treated as **open-ended/symbolic**, varying by text.
