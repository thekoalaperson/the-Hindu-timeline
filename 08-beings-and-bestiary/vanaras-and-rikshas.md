# Vānaras, Ṛkṣas & the Epic Animal-Races

> Part of [08 · Beings & Bestiary](README.md) → the **divinely-fathered animal races** of the epics: the monkey-hosts (*vānara*), the ape/langur division (*golāṅgūla*), and the bear-host (*ṛkṣa*).
> Cosmic address: created on **Brahmā's command** on the eve of Rāma's advent, so their great age is **[Tretā-yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current 28th Mahāyuga; a handful (Jāmbavān, Mainda, Dvivida, Hanumān) persist into **Dvāpara** and beyond.
> Canonical narrative homes: [Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md) · [Sugrīva & Vālī](../04-deep-dives/characters/sugriva-and-vali.md) · [Hanumān](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md).

The **vānaras** (वानर) are the forest-dwelling monkey-people of the Rāmāyaṇa — shape-shifting (*kāmarūpin*), mountain-hurling, speech-and-dharma-knowing beings deliberately **engineered by the gods as an army for Viṣṇu's Rāma-avatāra**. With them march two sibling races: the **golāṅgūlas** ("cow-tailed" apes/langurs) and the **ṛkṣas** (bears) under the primeval **Jāmbavān**. Unlike ordinary beasts, each great chief is the **son of a specific deva**, so the muster of Kiṣkindhā is effectively the pantheon fighting Rāvaṇa by proxy — the divine order intervening in animal form because Rāvaṇa's boon protected him from gods, not from men and beasts. This node is the class-level home: the two origin accounts, the deva-father roster, the three divisions, and the survivors who carry the race into the Kṛṣṇa cycle.

## Origin I — Brahmā's Command (Vālmīki, Bāla-kāṇḍa 17)

- **The commission of the host** — After Viṣṇu agrees to be born as **Rāma**, **Brahmā commands the devas, gandharvas and celestials to beget mighty sons in monkey form** — valorous, shape-shifting at will (*kāmarūpin*), knowing of expedients, equal to the gods in strength — upon apsaras-, gandharvī-, yakṣa-, nāga-, kinnara- and vānara-women, expressly **to assist Viṣṇu** in the coming war. (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa 17.) — thumbnail in [Bāla-kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md).
- **Jāmbavān first, from Brahmā's yawn** — Before the mass begetting, **Brahmā himself brings forth Jāmbavān**, the bear-lord, traditionally said to have sprung from the Creator **as he yawned** — making the ṛkṣa-king the eldest of the whole host. (Vālmīki, Bāla 17.)
- **Scale of the race** — The text counts the host in **many thousands** of bears, golāṅgūlas and monkeys, dwelling on mountains and in forests, gathering about **Vālī and Sugrīva**; later kāṇḍas inflate the war-muster past countability (see the number-ladder below). The class is therefore **open-ended**; only the deva-fathered chiefs form a finite named roster. (Vālmīki, Bāla 17; Yuddha-kāṇḍa 28.)
- **Where the newborn race was settled** — The commissioned host is not left scattered: the sarga closes by settling them on **Mount Ṛkṣavat** and the other mountains, in the caves and forests and by the waters, where they multiply awaiting Rāma's advent. The vānara race thus has a **creation-era homeland** named in the text, generations before Kiṣkindhā is ever built. (Vālmīki, Bāla 17.30–36.)
- **Why animals, not gods** — Rāvaṇa's boon from Brahmā covered devas, dānavas, gandharvas, yakṣas and nāgas, but in his contempt he never asked immunity from **men and animals** — hence a human avatāra with a monkey-and-bear army. (Vālmīki, Bāla 15–17; cf. [Rākṣasas](rakshasas.md).)

## Origin II — Ṛkṣarajas and the Enchanted Pool (an interpolated sarga) [interpolation]

**Read the stratification before the story.** This is not simply "late Uttara-kāṇḍa" material. The pool-transformation is a **prakṣipta (interpolated) sarga even within the Uttara-kāṇḍa** — Shastri prints it as an **appendix to chapter 37**, and the Baroda Critical Edition leaves it **outside the constituted text** altogether. It is therefore a stratum *later than the already-late Uttara*, and it is contradicted by two **older, core-text witnesses** that the tag "[late text]" would otherwise bury.

- **The two core-text witnesses** — In the oldest stratum Ṛkṣarajas is simply **the father of Vālī and Sugrīva**, with no pool and no sex-change: the southern search-party names him so before Sampāti (**Kiṣkindhā 57.5**), and in the Madhuvana scene Hanumān's companions remind Sugrīva that the grove "was never set free by **Ṛkṣarajas your father**, nor by you, nor by Vālī" (**Sundara 63**). Neither passage is disputed, and both antedate the interpolation. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 57.5; Sundara 63.)
- **The first vānara from Brahmā's tears** — In the appended back-story, a vānara named **Ṛkṣarajas** arises from **Brahmā** — in the common telling, from tears that fell from the meditating Creator's eyes. He is made the first monkey-lord. (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara-kāṇḍa appendix to sarga 37.) [interpolation]
- **The gender-change and the two divine sons** — Bathing in an enchanted pool, Ṛkṣarajas is transformed into a **beautiful woman**; both **Indra** and **Sūrya** are smitten. Indra's seed falls on the hair (*vāla*) → **Vālī**; Sūrya's on the neck (*grīvā*) → **Sugrīva** — the etymologies of both names. Restored to male form, Ṛkṣarajas is enthroned by Brahmā's arrangement as **king of the vānaras at Kiṣkindhā**, with Vālī his heir. (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara-kāṇḍa appendix to sarga 37.) [interpolation] — full treatment: [Sugrīva & Vālī §I](../04-deep-dives/characters/sugriva-and-vali.md).
  - *Variant (Kathāsaritsāgara):* Vālī is son of Indra by **Aruṇa/Aruṇī**, the charioteer of the sun in female form. [variant]
  - *Note (unreconciled doublet):* **Bāla 17** has Indra and Sūrya beget Vālī and Sugrīva within the mass commission; the appended sarga gives the separate Ṛkṣarajas romance. The two accounts sit side by side in the received text and tradition does not harmonize them — but they are not of equal weight: Bāla 17 and the Kiṣkindhā/Sundara witnesses are constituted text, the pool-romance is not. [disputed]

## The Deva-Father Roster (Vālmīki, Bāla-kāṇḍa 17)

The canonical table of **which god fathered which chief** — the epic's own "order of battle in heaven":

| Deva father | Vānara/Ṛkṣa chief | Role in the epic |
|---|---|---|
| **Brahmā** | **Jāmbavān** (ṛkṣa) | Eldest counsellor; king of the bears — [Jāmbavān](../04-deep-dives/characters/jambavan.md) |
| **Indra** | **Vālī** | King of Kiṣkindhā, slain by Rāma — [Sugrīva & Vālī](../04-deep-dives/characters/sugriva-and-vali.md) |
| **Sūrya** | **Sugrīva** | Rāma's ally-king; commands the search and the war |
| **Bṛhaspati** | **Tāra** (male) | "Foremost of monkey counsellors," wise in policy — cf. [Bṛhaspati](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md) |
| **Kubera** | **Gandhamādana** | A war-chief of the muster — cf. [Kubera](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/kubera.md) |
| **Viśvakarman** | **Nala** | The **engineer**: builder of the ocean-bridge — [Nala, Nīla & Suṣeṇa](../04-deep-dives/characters/nala-nila-and-sushena.md), cf. [Viśvakarman](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishvakarma-and-tvashtar.md) |
| **Agni** | **Nīla** | Fire-lustrous general (*senāpati*), killer of Prahasta — [Nala, Nīla & Suṣeṇa](../04-deep-dives/characters/nala-nila-and-sushena.md), cf. [Agni](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/agni.md) |
| **The Aśvins** | **Mainda & Dvivida** | Twin ape-brothers of surpassing beauty and strength |
| **Varuṇa** | **Suṣeṇa** | The **physician** of the host — [Nala, Nīla & Suṣeṇa](../04-deep-dives/characters/nala-nila-and-sushena.md), cf. [Varuṇa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/varuna.md). *(The identification of Varuṇa's son, Tārā's father and the army's physician as one Suṣeṇa is a **harmonization**, not a single textual statement — see the note below the table.)* [disputed] |
| **Parjanya** | **Śarabha** | A troop-leader |
| **Vāyu** | **Hanumān** | Greatest of all — body adamant, speed of Garuḍa — [Hanumān](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md), cf. [Vāyu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vayu.md) |

- **The staff of the host — Nala, Nīla and Suṣeṇa** — Three of the deva-fathered chiefs hold offices rather than commands, and each carries one famous set-piece: **Nala** throws the **Setu** across the sea by inherited craft, not buoyancy — **nothing floats in Vālmīki**, and **Sāgara** bears the causeway's weight (Yuddha 22); **Nīla** is the army's *senāpati*, musters the host on a fifteen-night deadline and kills Rāvaṇa's commander-in-chief **Prahasta** with a boulder (Kiṣkindhā 29; Yuddha 58); **Suṣeṇa** is its physician, who reads the fallen Lakṣmaṇa and names the mountain **Oṣadhi (Mahodaya)** and its four herbs (Yuddha 101). Full treatment: **[Nala, Nīla & Suṣeṇa](../04-deep-dives/characters/nala-nila-and-sushena.md)**; sacred-geography home for the bridge: [Rāmeśvaram & the Setu](../06-sacred-geography/rameswaram-setu-bandha.md).
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas):* the stones float because Rāma's **Name** is inscribed on them — the source of the famous floating-stones image, which is Tulsīdās's, not Vālmīki's. [late text]
- **The two herb-flights are not one errand** — Hanumān is sent for healing herbs **twice**. The **first** follows **Indrajit's brahmāstra** (Yuddha 73) and is sent by **[Jāmbavān](../04-deep-dives/characters/jambavan.md)** to an **unnamed** peak between **Ṛṣabha** and **Kailāsa** (Yuddha 74); the **second** follows **Rāvaṇa's** Maya-made *śakti* felling Lakṣmaṇa and is prescribed by **[Suṣeṇa](../04-deep-dives/characters/nala-nila-and-sushena.md)** to **Oṣadhi (Mahodaya)** (Yuddha 101). Indrajit's **nāga-pāśa** (Yuddha 45–50) belongs to neither — those bonds are undone by **Garuḍa's** touch and no errand is run.
- **Kesarī and Añjanā — Hanumān's earthly parents** — **Kesarī**, a vānara chief (his name "lion-maned"), is Hanumān's earthly father; his wife **Añjanā** is an apsaras (**Puñjikasthalā**) reborn in vānara form under a curse — see [Apsarās](apsaras.md). **Vāyu** is the divine begetter, so Hanumān is *Kesarī-nandana* and *Vāyu-putra* at once. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 66; Uttara-kāṇḍa 35–36.)
  - **Kesarī and Śambasādana** — Kesarī went from Mount **Mālyavān** to **Gokarṇa** and there, at the prayer of the gods and seers, slew the demon **Śambasādana** at a shrine by the sea. This is **genuine Vālmīki**, spoken by Hanumān himself when Sītā asks who he is — it simply does not stand in either of the birth-account sargas (Kiṣkindhā 66, Uttara 35–36), which is why it is so often mistaken for a retelling. (Vālmīki, **Sundara-kāṇḍa 35**.)
  - **Añjanā's curse — what the text does and does not say** — Vālmīki says only that Puñjikasthalā, foremost of apsarases, took birth as the daughter of the vānara chief **Kuñjara** *by reason of a curse*, and became Kesarī's wife. **No curser is named, no terms are given, and no release-condition is stated** — the release is simply the bearing of Hanumān. The **Bṛhaspati / Indra / Durvāsā** chain that supplies a curser is folk and Purāṇic Hanumān-birth tradition, not the epic. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 66.) [folk, for the curser]
    - *Name-collision note:* this Puñjikasthalā is **not** the apsarā Puñjikasthalā whom Rāvaṇa violates on the way to Brahmā's world, and for whom Brahmā curses him — a wholly separate figure and a separate curse, confessed by Rāvaṇa himself at **Yuddha 13**. Two apsarases, one name; do not merge them. — see [Rākṣasas](rakshasas.md).
- **Aṅgada** — Vālī's son by Tārā, made crown-prince after Vālī's death; leader of the decisive southern search-party and envoy to Rāvaṇa's court. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā–Yuddha.)
- **The three Suṣeṇas problem** — The roster row above is a **harmonization the text never performs**: Bāla 17 names a Suṣeṇa begotten by **Varuṇa**, the Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa gives Tārā a father named **Suṣeṇa**, and the Yuddha-kāṇḍa gives the army a physician named **Suṣeṇa**. Tradition treats all three as one vānara and the corpus follows it for convenience, but the identification is inference, not statement. Full treatment: [Nala, Nīla & Suṣeṇa](../04-deep-dives/characters/nala-nila-and-sushena.md). [disputed]
- **The scale of the host — the number-ladder** — The epic's own census is not a round figure but a **ladder of multipliers**, recited to Rāvaṇa by his spy **Śuka**: a hundred lakhs make a *koṭi*; a hundred thousand *koṭi* a **śaṅku**; and thereafter each unit is a hundred thousand of the last — **mahāśaṅku, vṛnda, mahāvṛnda, padma, mahāpadma, kharva, mahākharva, samudra, ogha**, and finally **mahaugha**. The muster is deliberately pushed past countability rather than counted. (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa 28.33–43.)

## The Three Divisions: Vānara, Golāṅgūla, Ṛkṣa

- **Vānaras proper** — The main monkey-host; etymology traditionally *vana* + *nara*, "man of the forest" (or "is it a man?" — *vā nara*). They live in cities (Kiṣkindhā has palaces, ministers, coronations), marry, and observe dharma — a full parallel society in animal form. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa.)
- **Golāṅgūlas ("cow-tailed")** — A distinct division of the host, black-faced and mighty; **Gavākṣa** is named their troop-lord, marching with his own crores. The naming belongs not to the muster-sarga but to **Sāraṇa's spy-catalogue**, delivered to Rāvaṇa from the rampart as he identifies each chief in the besieging army. (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa 27.)
- **Ṛkṣas (bears)** — The bear-host, counted alongside the monkeys from Bāla 17 onward. Its lords are **Dhūmra**, named *lord of all the bears*, and **[Jāmbavān](../04-deep-dives/characters/jambavan.md)** — and the same spy-catalogue states plainly that the mountain-like chief who is **Dhūmra's younger brother**, like him in form but greater in prowess and commander over the generals themselves, **is Jāmbavān**. The brotherhood is therefore **textual, not "tradition."** (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa 27.10–11.)
  - *Note (internal doublet):* a Jāmbavān who is Dhūmra's *younger brother* sits unreconciled beside Bāla 17's Jāmbavān brought forth directly by **Brahmā** (from the Creator's yawn) as the eldest of the whole host. Both stand in the received text; the epic does not choose between them. [disputed]
  - *Scholarly note:* since *ṛkṣa* in older Sanskrit can also mean a star/constellation, and the epic bears behave exactly like the vānaras, some scholars have debated whether the ṛkṣas were originally a separate "bear" race at all or another forest-people; the received epic treats them plainly as bears. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Are the vānaras "monkeys" at all?** — Modern interpreters split: literal celestial monkeys; **forest-dwelling human tribes** totemically described (a reading common in rationalist and some regional scholarship); or mythic beings sui generis. The text itself gives them tails, fur and leaping power *and* speech, statecraft and Vedic learning — the ambiguity is old. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Regional continuity** — In and around **Hampi–Anegundi** (Karnataka), living tradition identifies the local landscape as Kiṣkindhā and honors langurs and bears as descendants of the host — see [Kiṣkindhā · Hampi–Anegundi](../06-sacred-geography/kishkindha-hampi-anegundi.md). [regional] [oral tradition]

## Kiṣkindhā — the Vānara Kingdom in Action (summaries; canonical homes linked)

- **The feud of the royal brothers** — Vālī (Indra's son) and Sugrīva (Sūrya's son) fall out over the sealed demon-cave; Vālī usurps wife and throne; the exile Sugrīva allies with Rāma (*agni-sākṣi* pact), and Rāma kills Vālī from concealment — the epic's central dharma-debate. Full treatment: **[Sugrīva & Vālī](../04-deep-dives/characters/sugriva-and-vali.md)**. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 4–26.)
  - **Whose sons the feud-victims are** — The two demons whose killing frames the whole quarrel are **brothers, and both sons of Maya the dānava-architect**: **Māyāvī**, "elder brother of Dundubhi and son of the demon Maya," who calls Vālī out at the gate of Kiṣkindhā and flees into the earth-cavern (Kiṣkindhā 9); and **Dundubhi** the buffalo-demon, whose carcass Vālī flings **a single yojana** and whose blood-drops bring Mataṅga's curse on the hill (Kiṣkindhā 11). Because Maya is also the father of **Mandodarī**, Rāvaṇa's chief queen, Vālī's two feud-victims are **Rāvaṇa's own brothers-in-law** — the vānara quarrel and the Laṅkā household are kin by marriage before the war ever begins. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 9, 11.) — see [Maya Dānava](maya-danava.md).
- **The four-quarter muster** — Sugrīva summons the world's vānaras — chiefs Gaja, Gavākṣa, Gavaya, Śarabha, Gandhamādana, Mainda, Dvivida, Nīla, Suṣeṇa and more — and dispatches search-parties east (Vinata), west (Suṣeṇa), north (Śatabali), and south under **Aṅgada with Hanumān, Jāmbavān and Nīla**. Full treatment: **[Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md)**. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 37–45.)
  - *Roster note:* **Nala** is not named in the southern party's own muster-list at Kiṣkindhā 41, though rosters and retellings routinely place him there; take the sarga's names as the roster and the fuller lists as tradition. [disputed]
- **Jāmbavān, the elder of the host** — Brahmā's own issue and the oldest creature in the epic, the ṛkṣa-rāja is the army's memory and judgment rather than its arm. On the southern shore he tops the chiefs' ladder of leaps at **ninety** yojanas — ten short of the crossing — and explains the shortfall by the epic's deepest memory, that in his prime he **circumambulated Viṣṇu-Trivikrama** as the Lord strode the worlds in the [Vāmana avatāra](../04-deep-dives/vamana-bali.md); then he reminds the despondent Hanumān of his forgotten Vāyu-born powers, and the leap to Laṅkā follows — the hinge into the [Sundara-kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md). In the war he rules the search-party's mandate, vets strategy, and sends the first Sañjīvanī errand. Full treatment: **[Jāmbavān](../04-deep-dives/characters/jambavan.md)**. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 65–66; Sundara 59–60; Yuddha 74.)
- **The war** — Vānaras, golāṅgūlas and ṛkṣas fight with trees, boulders, fangs and nails against the arms of Laṅkā; Nala bridges the sea, Nīla leads the van, Aṅgada and Hanumān fell champions; the fallen host is **revived by Indra's boon** at war's end — Indra's own grant to Rāma, not a collective act of "the gods." See [Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md) and [Laṅkā trail](../06-sacred-geography/lanka-ramayana-trail-ashoka-vatika.md). (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa 120.)
- **Allied animal-races of the epic** — The vulture-brothers **Jaṭāyu and Sampāti** (Aruṇa's sons) serve the same divine plan from the sky — [Jaṭāyu & Sampāti](../04-deep-dives/characters/jatayu-and-sampati.md); the **squirrel** who carries pebbles for the Setu and is stroked by Rāma (the stripes on its back) is a beloved extra-Vālmīki tale. [folk]

## Vānaras After the Rāmāyaṇa — the Dvāpara Threads

- **Jāmbavān and the Syamantaka** — Generations later the bear-king seizes the sun-jewel **Syamantaka** from a lion; **Kṛṣṇa**, tracking it, fights him in his cave for days on end — **twenty-eight** in the Bhāgavata's count, twenty-one in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa's [variant] — until the exhausted Jāmbavān recognizes his opponent as **Rāma returned**, surrenders the gem and gives his daughter **Jāmbavatī** in marriage. Because he thus sees both Rāma and Kṛṣṇa in the flesh, tradition often appends him to the eight [chirañjīvis](../04-deep-dives/the-chiranjivis.md) as a ninth. [folk] Full treatment: **[The Syamantaka Jewel](../04-deep-dives/syamantaka-jewel.md)**; his own dossier: [Jāmbavān](../04-deep-dives/characters/jambavan.md). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.56; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.13.)
- **Dvivida vs Balarāma (Bhāgavata 10.67)** — The Aśvin-born twin **Dvivida**, in the Bhāgavata's telling a friend of **Narakāsura**, turns rogue after Naraka's death: he ravages Ānarta, sinks ships, flattens forests and defiles sacrifices, then insults **Balarāma's** revels on **Raivataka hill**. In a battle of hurled trees and boulders Balarāma **batters the great ape to death with his fists**. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.67; the episode also in Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.36.) — summarized at [Balarāma](../04-deep-dives/characters/balarama.md).
  - *Note:* the Bhāgavata's rogue Dvivida sits oddly beside the Rāmāyaṇa's loyal Aśvin-twin; the texts do not explain the turn, and some traditions treat them as the same being fallen, others as a namesake. [disputed]
- **Hanumān and Bhīma** — The greatest vānara survives into Dvāpara as a **chirañjīvi**: he humbles his Vāyu-born brother **Bhīma** on the Gandhamādana path (the immovable tail) and rides Arjuna's banner at Kurukṣetra — see [Vana Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md) and [Hanumān](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md). (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva.)
- **Succession of Kiṣkindhā** — At the Mahāprasthāna Sugrīva comes to follow Rāma out of the world, **having first installed Aṅgada** on the throne of Kiṣkindhā; in the same audience Rāma fixes **Jāmbavān** (with **Mainda** and **Dvivida**) on earth *until the Kali Yuga begins*. The vānara kingdom then passes from the epic stage. (Vālmīki, Uttara-kāṇḍa 108.)

## The Two Clocks

- **Scriptural/cyclic** — The race is created in **Tretā-yuga** for Rāma's war; stragglers (Jāmbavān, Dvivida, Hanumān) act in **Dvāpara**; Hanumān alone persists through **Kali** as a chirañjīvi. (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa; Bhāgavata Purāṇa.)
- **Academic/historical** [scholarly] — The Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa's core (Kāṇḍas 2–6) is dated roughly **7th–4th c. BCE with growth into the early centuries CE**; the **Bāla- and Uttara-kāṇḍas** — which carry both origin stories above — are widely judged **later additions**, so the class-mythology of the vānaras is among the epic's younger strata. The Bhāgavata Purāṇa (Dvivida, Syamantaka) is commonly placed c. **6th–10th c. CE**. The identification of Kiṣkindhā with **Hampi–Anegundi** is medieval-to-modern sacred geography, not archaeology. [scholarly]

- **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa** *(vulgate / Southern-recension sarga numbers)* — Bāla-kāṇḍa 15–17 (Rāvaṇa's boon; Brahmā's command; Jāmbavān from the yawn; the deva-father roster; **17.30–36**, settlement on Mt. Ṛkṣavat); Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 4–66 (Kiṣkindhā and the muster; **9** and **11**, Māyāvī and Dundubhi, sons of Maya; **29**, Sugrīva's fifteen-night muster-order to Nīla the army-commander; **41**, the four search-parties; **57.5**, Ṛkṣarajas as father in the core text; **65**, the leap-ladder and Jāmbavān's Trivikrama memory; **66**, the rousing of Hanumān and Añjanā's curse); Sundara-kāṇḍa **35** (Hanumān's self-introduction; Kesarī and Śambasādana) and **63** (Ṛkṣarajas named Sugrīva's father at Madhuvana); Yuddha-kāṇḍa **22** (Nala's Setu, Sāgara bearing it), **27** (Sāraṇa's spy-catalogue — Gavākṣa, Dhūmra, Jāmbavān as Dhūmra's younger brother), **28.33–43** (Śuka's number-ladder), **45–50** (Indrajit's nāga-pāśa, undone by Garuḍa), **58** (Nīla kills Prahasta), **73–74** (Indrajit's brahmāstra and Jāmbavān's herb-errand to the unnamed peak) and **101** (Suṣeṇa's prescription and Oṣadhi/Mahodaya), **120** (Indra's revival boon); Uttara-kāṇḍa **108** (Aṅgada installed, Jāmbavān till Kali). The Ṛkṣarajas pool-transformation is **not** constituted text — it stands as an **appendix to Uttara-kāṇḍa 37** in Shastri and outside the Baroda Critical Edition. [interpolation]
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** — 10.56 (Jāmbavān, Syamantaka, Jāmbavatī); 10.67 (Dvivida-vadha) [late text].
- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** — 4.13 (Syamantaka); 5.36 (Dvivida) — parallel Purāṇic tellings.
- **Mahābhārata** — Vana Parva (Hanumān–Bhīma meeting).
- **Kathāsaritsāgara** — variant parentage of Vālī. [variant]

- Goldman, R. & S. (tr.), *The Rāmāyaṇa of Vālmīki* — stratification of Bāla- and Uttara-kāṇḍas and dating of the core epic. [scholarly]
