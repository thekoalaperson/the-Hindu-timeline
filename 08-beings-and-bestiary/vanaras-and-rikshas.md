# Vānaras, Ṛkṣas & the Epic Animal-Races

> Part of [08 · Beings & Bestiary](README.md) → the **divinely-fathered animal races** of the epics: the monkey-hosts (*vānara*), the ape/langur division (*golāṅgūla*), and the bear-host (*ṛkṣa*).
> Cosmic address: created on **Brahmā's command** on the eve of Rāma's advent, so their great age is **[Tretā-yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current 28th Mahāyuga; a handful (Jāmbavān, Mainda, Dvivida, Hanumān) persist into **Dvāpara** and beyond.
> Canonical narrative homes: [Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md) · [Sugrīva & Vālī](../04-deep-dives/characters/sugriva-and-vali.md) · [Hanumān](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md).

The **vānaras** (वानर) are the forest-dwelling monkey-people of the Rāmāyaṇa — shape-shifting (*kāmarūpin*), mountain-hurling, speech-and-dharma-knowing beings deliberately **engineered by the gods as an army for Viṣṇu's Rāma-avatāra**. With them march two sibling races: the **golāṅgūlas** ("cow-tailed" apes/langurs) and the **ṛkṣas** (bears) under the primeval **Jāmbavān**. Unlike ordinary beasts, each great chief is the **son of a specific deva**, so the muster of Kiṣkindhā is effectively the pantheon fighting Rāvaṇa by proxy — the divine order intervening in animal form because Rāvaṇa's boon protected him from gods, not from men and beasts. This node is the class-level home: the two origin accounts, the deva-father roster, the three divisions, and the survivors who carry the race into the Kṛṣṇa cycle.

---

## Origin I — Brahmā's Command (Vālmīki, Bāla-kāṇḍa 17)

- **The commission of the host** — After Viṣṇu agrees to be born as **Rāma**, **Brahmā commands the devas, gandharvas and celestials to beget mighty sons in monkey form** — valorous, shape-shifting at will (*kāmarūpin*), knowing of expedients, equal to the gods in strength — upon apsaras-, gandharvī-, yakṣa-, nāga-, kinnara- and vānara-women, expressly **to assist Viṣṇu** in the coming war. (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa 17.) — thumbnail in [Bāla-kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md).
- **Jāmbavān first, from Brahmā's yawn** — Before the mass begetting, **Brahmā himself brings forth Jāmbavān**, the bear-lord, traditionally said to have sprung from the Creator **as he yawned** — making the ṛkṣa-king the eldest of the whole host. (Vālmīki, Bāla 17.)
- **Scale of the race** — The text counts the host in **many thousands** of bears, golāṅgūlas and monkeys, dwelling on mountains and in forests, gathering about **Vālī and Sugrīva**; later kāṇḍas inflate the war-muster to crores. The class is therefore **open-ended**; only the deva-fathered chiefs form a finite named roster. (Vālmīki, Bāla 17; Yuddha-kāṇḍa musters.)
- **Why animals, not gods** — Rāvaṇa's boon from Brahmā covered devas, dānavas, gandharvas, yakṣas and nāgas, but in his contempt he never asked immunity from **men and animals** — hence a human avatāra with a monkey-and-bear army. (Vālmīki, Bāla 15–17; cf. [Rākṣasas](rakshasas.md).)

## Origin II — Ṛkṣarajas and the Enchanted Pool (Uttara-kāṇḍa) [late text]

- **The first vānara from Brahmā's tears** — In the Uttara-kāṇḍa's back-story (told to Rāma after the war), a vānara named **Ṛkṣarajas** arises from **Brahmā** — in the common telling, from tears that fell from the meditating Creator's eyes. He is made the first monkey-lord. (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara-kāṇḍa; the kāṇḍa as a whole is widely held a later stratum [late text] [scholarly].)
- **The gender-change and the two divine sons** — Bathing in an enchanted pool, Ṛkṣarajas is transformed into a **beautiful woman**; both **Indra** and **Sūrya** are smitten. Indra's seed falls on the hair (*vāla*) → **Vālī**; Sūrya's on the neck (*grīvā*) → **Sugrīva** — the etymologies of both names. Restored to male form, Ṛkṣarajas is enthroned by Brahmā's arrangement as **king of the vānaras at Kiṣkindhā**, with Vālī his heir. (Vālmīki, Uttara-kāṇḍa.) — full treatment: [Sugrīva & Vālī §I](../04-deep-dives/characters/sugriva-and-vali.md).
  - *Variant (Kathāsaritsāgara):* Vālī is son of Indra by **Aruṇa/Aruṇī**, the charioteer of the sun in female form. [variant]
  - *Note (unreconciled doublet):* Bāla 17 has Indra and Sūrya beget Vālī and Sugrīva within the mass commission; the Uttara-kāṇḍa gives the separate Ṛkṣarajas romance. The two accounts sit side by side in the received text and tradition does not harmonize them. [disputed]

## The Deva-Father Roster (Vālmīki, Bāla-kāṇḍa 17)

The canonical table of **which god fathered which chief** — the epic's own "order of battle in heaven":

| Deva father | Vānara/Ṛkṣa chief | Role in the epic |
|---|---|---|
| **Brahmā** | **Jāmbavān** (ṛkṣa) | Eldest counsellor; king of the bears (see §Jāmbavān) |
| **Indra** | **Vālī** | King of Kiṣkindhā, slain by Rāma — [Sugrīva & Vālī](../04-deep-dives/characters/sugriva-and-vali.md) |
| **Sūrya** | **Sugrīva** | Rāma's ally-king; commands the search and the war |
| **Bṛhaspati** | **Tāra** (male) | "Foremost of monkey counsellors," wise in policy — cf. [Bṛhaspati](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md) |
| **Kubera** | **Gandhamādana** | A war-chief of the muster — cf. [Kubera](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/kubera.md) |
| **Viśvakarman** | **Nala** | The **engineer**: builder of the ocean-bridge — cf. [Viśvakarman](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishvakarma-and-tvashtar.md) |
| **Agni** | **Nīla** | Fire-lustrous general, commander-in-chief of the army — cf. [Agni](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/agni.md) |
| **The Aśvins** | **Mainda & Dvivida** | Twin ape-brothers of surpassing beauty and strength |
| **Varuṇa** | **Suṣeṇa** | The **physician** of the host; father of queen Tārā — cf. [Varuṇa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/varuna.md) |
| **Parjanya** | **Śarabha** | A troop-leader |
| **Vāyu** | **Hanumān** | Greatest of all — body adamant, speed of Garuḍa — [Hanumān](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md), cf. [Vāyu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vayu.md) |

- **Nala the bridge-builder** — Son of the divine architect, Nala directs the vānaras in throwing the **Setu** across the sea to Laṅkā in five days, stones and trees floating at his art (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa 22) — sacred-geography home: [Rāmeśvaram & the Setu](../06-sacred-geography/rameswaram-setu-bandha.md).
- **Nīla the general** — Agni's son leads the army's van and duels the rākṣasa general Prahasta (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa).
- **Suṣeṇa the healer** — Varuṇa's son; in the Yuddha-kāṇḍa he directs the treatment of the wounded and calls for the Himālayan herb-mountain when **Lakṣmaṇa** falls — the errand Hanumān answers by carrying the mountain itself (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa).
- **Kesarī and Añjanā — Hanumān's earthly parents** — **Kesarī**, a vānara chief (his name "lion-maned"), is Hanumān's earthly father; his wife **Añjanā** is an apsaras (**Puñjikasthalā**) reborn in vānara form under a curse — see [Apsarās](apsaras.md). **Vāyu** is the divine begetter, so Hanumān is *Kesarī-nandana* and *Vāyu-putra* at once. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 66; Uttara-kāṇḍa 35–36.)
  - *Tradition:* Kesarī is credited with slaying an elephant-shaped demon (**Śambasādana**) that tormented the sages — the deed behind his name and blessing. (Uttara-kāṇḍa tradition / retellings.) [disputed]
- **Aṅgada** — Vālī's son by Tārā, made crown-prince after Vālī's death; leader of the decisive southern search-party and envoy to Rāvaṇa's court. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā–Yuddha.)

## The Three Divisions: Vānara, Golāṅgūla, Ṛkṣa

- **Vānaras proper** — The main monkey-host; etymology traditionally *vana* + *nara*, "man of the forest" (or "is it a man?" — *vā nara*). They live in cities (Kiṣkindhā has palaces, ministers, coronations), marry, and observe dharma — a full parallel society in animal form. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa.)
- **Golāṅgūlas ("cow-tailed")** — A distinct division of the host, black-faced and mighty; the war-musters name **Gavākṣa** as their king, marching with his own crores. (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa 4 muster.)
- **Ṛkṣas (bears)** — The bear-host, counted alongside the monkeys from Bāla 17 onward. Its lords are **Jāmbavān** (see below) and **Dhūmra**, named king of the ṛkṣas in the Yuddha-kāṇḍa muster; tradition often makes Dhūmra Jāmbavān's brother. (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa 4.)
  - *Scholarly note:* since *ṛkṣa* in older Sanskrit can also mean a star/constellation, and the epic bears behave exactly like the vānaras, some scholars have debated whether the ṛkṣas were originally a separate "bear" race at all or another forest-people; the received epic treats them plainly as bears. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Are the vānaras "monkeys" at all?** — Modern interpreters split: literal celestial monkeys; **forest-dwelling human tribes** totemically described (a reading common in rationalist and some regional scholarship); or mythic beings sui generis. The text itself gives them tails, fur and leaping power *and* speech, statecraft and Vedic learning — the ambiguity is old. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Regional continuity** — In and around **Hampi–Anegundi** (Karnataka), living tradition identifies the local landscape as Kiṣkindhā and honors langurs and bears as descendants of the host — see [Kiṣkindhā · Hampi–Anegundi](../06-sacred-geography/kishkindha-hampi-anegundi.md). [regional] [oral tradition]

## Kiṣkindhā — the Vānara Kingdom in Action (summaries; canonical homes linked)

- **The feud of the royal brothers** — Vālī (Indra's son) and Sugrīva (Sūrya's son) fall out over the sealed demon-cave; Vālī usurps wife and throne; the exile Sugrīva allies with Rāma (*agni-sākṣi* pact), and Rāma kills Vālī from concealment — the epic's central dharma-debate. Full treatment: **[Sugrīva & Vālī](../04-deep-dives/characters/sugriva-and-vali.md)**. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 4–26.)
- **The four-quarter muster** — Sugrīva summons the world's vānaras — chiefs Gaja, Gavākṣa, Gavaya, Śarabha, Gandhamādana, Mainda, Dvivida, Nīla, Suṣeṇa and more — and dispatches search-parties east (Vinata), west (Suṣeṇa), north (Śatabali), and south under **Aṅgada with Hanumān, Jāmbavān, Nala and Nīla**. Full treatment: **[Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md)**. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 37–45.)
- **Jāmbavān awakens Hanumān** — At the southern shore the old bear-king reminds the despondent Hanumān of his forgotten Vāyu-born powers; the leap to Laṅkā follows — the hinge into the [Sundara-kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md). (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 65–66.)
- **The war** — Vānaras, golāṅgūlas and ṛkṣas fight with trees, boulders, fangs and nails against the arms of Laṅkā; Nala bridges the sea, Nīla leads the van, Aṅgada and Hanumān fell champions; the fallen host is **revived by Indra's boon** at war's end. See [Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md) and [Laṅkā trail](../06-sacred-geography/lanka-ramayana-trail-ashoka-vatika.md). (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa.)
- **Allied animal-races of the epic** — The vulture-brothers **Jaṭāyu and Sampāti** (Aruṇa's sons) serve the same divine plan from the sky — [Jaṭāyu & Sampāti](../04-deep-dives/characters/jatayu-and-sampati.md); the **squirrel** who carries pebbles for the Setu and is stroked by Rāma (the stripes on its back) is a beloved extra-Vālmīki tale. [folk]

## Jāmbavān — the Bear Across the Yugas

- **Primordial age** — Born of Brahmā at the creation of the host (Bāla 17), Jāmbavān tells Hanumān he is now too old to leap the sea, though once, in his prime, he **circumambulated Viṣṇu-Trivikrama** as the Lord strode the worlds in the [Vāmana avatāra](../04-deep-dives/vamana-bali.md) — making him a witness of prior cosmic events. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 66.)
- **Counsellor of the war** — The ṛkṣa-rāja is the army's memory and judgment: he vets strategy, identifies the fallen, and sends Hanumān for the herb-mountain. (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa.)
- **Into Dvāpara: the Syamantaka fight** — Generations later Jāmbavān seizes the sun-jewel **Syamantaka** from a lion; **Kṛṣṇa**, tracking it, fights him in his cave for days on end — **twenty-eight days** in the Bhāgavata's count (*Bhāgavata* 10.56), twenty-one in other Purāṇic tellings [variant] — until the exhausted bear recognizes his opponent as **Rāma returned**, surrenders the gem and gives his daughter **Jāmbavatī** to Kṛṣṇa in marriage. Full treatment: **[The Syamantaka Jewel](../04-deep-dives/syamantaka-jewel.md)**. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.56; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.13.)
- **Quasi-chirañjīvi** — Because he sees both Rāma and Kṛṣṇa in the flesh, tradition often appends Jāmbavān as a **ninth immortal** — see [The Chirañjīvis](../04-deep-dives/the-chiranjivis.md). [folk]

## Vānaras After the Rāmāyaṇa — the Dvāpara Threads

- **Dvivida vs Balarāma (Bhāgavata 10.67)** — The Aśvin-born twin **Dvivida**, in the Bhāgavata's telling a friend of **Narakāsura**, turns rogue after Naraka's death: he ravages Ānarta, sinks ships, flattens forests and defiles sacrifices, then insults **Balarāma's** revels on **Raivataka hill**. In a battle of hurled trees and boulders Balarāma **batters the great ape to death with his fists**. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.67; the episode also in Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.36.) — summarized at [Balarāma](../04-deep-dives/characters/balarama.md).
  - *Note:* the Bhāgavata's rogue Dvivida sits oddly beside the Rāmāyaṇa's loyal Aśvin-twin; the texts do not explain the turn, and some traditions treat them as the same being fallen, others as a namesake. [disputed]
- **Hanumān and Bhīma** — The greatest vānara survives into Dvāpara as a **chirañjīvi**: he humbles his Vāyu-born brother **Bhīma** on the Gandhamādana path (the immovable tail) and rides Arjuna's banner at Kurukṣetra — see [Vana Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md) and [Hanumān](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md). (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva.)
- **Succession of Kiṣkindhā** — At Rāma's departure Sugrīva follows him, having crowned **Aṅgada**; the vānara kingdom passes from the epic stage. (Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara-kāṇḍa tradition.) [late text]

## The Two Clocks

- **Scriptural/cyclic** — The race is created in **Tretā-yuga** for Rāma's war; stragglers (Jāmbavān, Dvivida, Hanumān) act in **Dvāpara**; Hanumān alone persists through **Kali** as a chirañjīvi. (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa; Bhāgavata Purāṇa.)
- **Academic/historical** [scholarly] — The Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa's core (Kāṇḍas 2–6) is dated roughly **7th–4th c. BCE with growth into the early centuries CE**; the **Bāla- and Uttara-kāṇḍas** — which carry both origin stories above — are widely judged **later additions**, so the class-mythology of the vānaras is among the epic's younger strata. The Bhāgavata Purāṇa (Dvivida, Syamantaka) is commonly placed c. **6th–10th c. CE**. The identification of Kiṣkindhā with **Hampi–Anegundi** is medieval-to-modern sacred geography, not archaeology. [scholarly]
