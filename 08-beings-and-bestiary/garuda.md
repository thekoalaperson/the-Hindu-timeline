# Garuḍa — King of Birds, Mount of Viṣṇu

[Beings & Bestiary](./README.md) · [Master Index](../README.md) — Vainateya, the golden-winged sun-bird; son of Vinatā and Kaśyapa, devourer of serpents, bearer of Viṣṇu, and the emblem of the Garuḍa-dhvaja. This file gives the full biography; for his role purely as a vehicle see [vāhanas](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md), for his prey-kin see [nāgas and serpents](./nagas-and-serpents.md), and for his vulture descendants see the [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/) cycle.

## Names, Epithets, and the Vedic Antecedent

- **Garuḍa** - "King of Birds" (pakṣirāja), the great golden bird (suvarṇa-kāya). His name is variously glossed as "wings of speech / devourer" (from *gṛ*). Born of Vinatā, hence the patronymic **Vainateya** (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva).
- **Suparṇa** - "He of Beautiful / Excellent Wings," the name he earned when he let a single feather fall (see amṛta-quest below) so that beholders cried *su-parṇa* (Mahābhārata, Āstīka section, Ādi Parva).
- **Tārkṣya** - patronymic from the sage Tṛkṣa; in Purāṇas and epics treated as a name of Garuḍa, though originally a distinct Vedic solar steed/bird (Purāṇas, Mahābhārata).
- **Garutmān** - "the winged one"; the *Ṛgveda* names a celestial winged deva Garutmān, identified in the Mahābhārata explicitly with Garuḍa, "fast, able to shapeshift and enter anywhere" (Ṛgveda; Mahābhārata).
- **Śyena ("eagle/falcon")** - Vedic antecedent: the *Ṛgveda* hymn celebrates the Śyena who bore down **Soma/amṛta** from heaven to earth — the mytheme later attached to Garuḍa's theft of amṛta (Ṛgveda; cf. Soma-pavamāna hymns). [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Vedic philology):* Garuḍa is best read as a Purāṇic-epic synthesis of three Vedic bird-figures — **Garutmān, Śyena, and Tārkṣya/Tārkṣya Ariṣṭanemi** — only fully fused into one personality in the Mahābhārata. [scholarly]
- **Other epithets** - Khageśvara/Pakṣirāja (lord of birds), Nāgāntaka / Sarpārāti (ender/enemy of serpents), Viṣṇuratha / Viṣṇu-vāhana (chariot/mount of Viṣṇu), Amṛtāharaṇa (stealer of nectar), Gaganeśvara (lord of the sky), Kaśyapī (Kaśyapa's son).

## Birth from Vinatā's Egg

- **Parentage** - Son of the prajāpati **Kaśyapa** by **Vinatā**, daughter of Dakṣa; his maternal aunt and rival is **Kadrū**, mother of the nāgas. Kaśyapa, pleased, granted Vinatā two sons of surpassing strength and Kadrū a thousand serpent-sons (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva, Āstīka). See [the prajāpatis](./the-prajapatis.md).
- **The two eggs** - Vinatā laid two eggs which she tended for **five hundred years** without hatching, while Kadrū's serpent-eggs had already broken open (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva).
- **Aruṇa, the broken egg** - Impatient and shamed, Vinatā cracked one egg early; from it emerged **Aruṇa**, formed only to the waist (his lower body unmade). Enraged, Aruṇa cursed his mother to **slavery for five hundred years**, to be ended only by the son in the second egg, then flew up to become the **charioteer of Sūrya** / the red dawn (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva). See [the thirty-three devas](./the-thirty-three-devas.md) for Sūrya's retinue.
- **Garuḍa hatches** - At the appointed time the second egg burst and Garuḍa emerged fully formed, vast and blazing like cosmic fire — so radiant the devas mistook him for Agni and praised him; he diminished his heat at their plea (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva).

## The Wager of Kadrū vs. Vinatā and Vinatā's Enslavement

- **The bet on Uccaiḥśravas** - The sisters disputed the color of **Uccaiḥśravas**, the divine horse risen from the churning of the ocean: Vinatā said pure white; Kadrū said white with a black tail. The loser would become the winner's slave (dāsī) (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva). See [nāgas and serpents](./nagas-and-serpents.md) for the nāga side of this episode.
- **Kadrū's deceit** - Kadrū commanded her nāga sons to coil over the horse's tail and blacken it. The honest serpents who refused she cursed to **burn in Janamejaya's snake-sacrifice** (linking forward to the Sarpa-satra and the rescue by **Āstīka**) (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva).
- **Enslavement** - The tail appeared dark; Vinatā lost and became Kadrū's slave, and by extension the infant Garuḍa served the nāgas — the root of his lifelong hatred (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva).

## The Amṛta-Quest

- **The price of freedom** - When Garuḍa demanded his mother's release, the nāgas set their price: bring them the **amṛta** (nectar of immortality) from the devas (Mahābhārata, Āstīka section, Ādi Parva).
- **Provisioning** - Advised by his father Kaśyapa, the starving Garuḍa ate an elephant (Gaja) and a tortoise (Kūrma) locked in eternal combat; he perched on a great bough that snapped, beneath which hung the tiny **Vālakhilya** ascetics — he carefully spared them, earning their blessing (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva).
- **Battle with the devas** - Reaching the amṛta, Garuḍa routed the assembled gods — **Indra, the Vasus, Rudras, Sādhyas, Aśvins, Gandharvas, and Yakṣas** — scattering them with the wind of his wings and his beak and talons (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva).
- **The threefold guard** - He doused a ring of encircling **fire** with rivers carried in his mouths (texts give him many heads/mouths for the feat), shrank his body to pass a **whirling razor-wheel**, and crushed the two venom-spewing **serpent guardians** by blinding them with dust (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva).
  - *Variant (folk/retellings):* he "expands to ninety heads and ninety mouths" to gulp the rivers — a number found in popular tellings, not fixed in the critical text. [folk]
- **Indra's vajra and the unbreakable feather** - In flight Indra hurled the **vajra** (thunderbolt forged from sage **Dadhīci's** bones). Garuḍa was unharmed, but out of reverence for the weapon and the sage shed a single feather; all who saw its beauty named him **Suparṇa** (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva). See [vāhanas](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md).
- **The boon of immortality without amṛta** - Impressed, **Viṣṇu** offered Garuḍa boons. Garuḍa asked to be **immortal and ageless without drinking the amṛta**, and to perch **above Viṣṇu**; Viṣṇu granted both, set him on his banner (above him on the flagstaff) and made him his **mount** (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva).
- **Indra's counter-boon** - Indra in turn obtained from Garuḍa the right to **recover the amṛta** and asked the boon that the **nāgas become Garuḍa's food** (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva).
- **The trick that frees Vinatā** - Garuḍa delivered the amṛta-pot to the nāgas, securing his mother's release, but set it on sharp **kuśa grass** and bade them purify themselves first. While they bathed, **Indra snatched the nectar back**; the nāgas, licking the grass for spilled drops, split their tongues — hence the **forked tongue of serpents** (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva).

## Mount of Viṣṇu and the Garuḍa-dhvaja

- **Viṣṇuratha** - From the amṛta episode onward Garuḍa is the swift vehicle that bears **Viṣṇu / Nārāyaṇa** (and Kṛṣṇa) across the worlds, often depicted lifting Viṣṇu's feet with his rear hands (Mahābhārata; Viṣṇu Purāṇa; Bhāgavata Purāṇa).
- **Garuḍa-dhvaja** - Viṣṇu's banner bears the figure of Garuḍa; "Garuḍadhvaja" is itself an epithet of Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa. The **Heliodorus pillar** (Besnagar, c. 113 BCE) is an actual *Garuḍa-stambha* erected by the Greek devotee Heliodorus to **Vāsudeva**, the earliest dated physical witness to Garuḍa-Vaiṣṇava cult. [scholarly] See [artifacts & symbols](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/).
- **Iconography** - Anthropomorphic (man's torso with beak, wings, talons) or fully zoomorphic (great eagle/kite); golden-bodied, often four-armed bearing a pot of amṛta or a parasol, frequently shown trampling or holding nāgas.

## Eternal Enmity with the Nāgas

- **Sarpārāti** - By Indra's boon and the wager-grievance, Garuḍa preys on serpents perpetually; he is invoked against snakes, poison, and the venom of slander (Mahābhārata; Purāṇas). See [nāgas and serpents](./nagas-and-serpents.md).
- **The pact of Sumukha / the bone-mountain** - In one famous episode Garuḍa, devouring a nāga a day, is restrained when the nāga **Sumukha** is protected by Indra and Viṣṇu; Garuḍa's pride is humbled (his wing droops under a single feather of Viṣṇu) and a truce/tribute system is described (Mahābhārata; Udyoga/Bhāgavata traditions). [variant rosters]
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata/folk):* the serpents arrange to send Garuḍa one snake per day as offering at a fixed spot, ending the indiscriminate slaughter. [folk]

## Offspring and the Suparṇa / Bird-Race

- **Wife and son** - Garuḍa's wife is named **Unnati** (also called Vināyakā in some lists); a son **Sumukha** is named (Mahābhārata; Purāṇas). [variant rosters]
- **The Suparṇas** - Garuḍa is patriarch of the **Suparṇas / Saupaṛṇeyas**, the celestial bird-race; the Mahābhārata gives a long, open-ended roster of his bird-sons (e.g., Sumukha, Sunāman, Suvarcas, Surūpa, and many more — list not closed). [flag: open-ended roster]
- **Aruṇa's line — the vulture-brothers** - Garuḍa's elder (half-formed) brother **Aruṇa**, by his wife **Śyenī**, fathered **Sampāti** and **Jaṭāyu**, the noble vultures of the Rāmāyaṇa — thus Garuḍa is their **paternal uncle** (Rāmāyaṇa; Mahābhārata). See the [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/) cycle.
  - *Variant (some Purāṇic/folk tellings):* Sampāti and Jaṭāyu are made **sons of Garuḍa himself** rather than nephews. [folk] [disputed]
- **Rāmāyaṇa cameo** - Garuḍa appears to **Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa** to free them from the nāga-noose **nāgapāśa** of Indrajit, dispelling the serpent-arrows by his mere presence (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa).

## Garuḍa-vidyā, the Garuḍopaniṣad, and the Garuḍa Purāṇa

- **Garuḍa-vidyā (anti-venom mantra-science)** - A body of mantra and ritual invoking Garuḍa to neutralize **snake-venom and poison** of every kind (animal, weapon, supernatural); the practitioner identifies with the serpent-eater. Core of the Vedic **Tārkṣya/Suparṇa** spell-tradition continued into Tantra. (Atharvaveda-affiliated traditions; āgamic Garuḍa-tantras.)
- **Garuḍopaniṣad** - A minor **Vaiṣṇava Upaniṣad** attached to the **Atharvaveda**, consisting of charms (Garuḍa-vidyā) said to prevent and cure snakebite and all poison; among the 108 Upaniṣads of the Muktikā list. [late text]
- **Garuḍa Purāṇa** - One of the eighteen **Mahāpurāṇas** (a *sāttvika*/Vaiṣṇava Purāṇa), framed as Viṣṇu's discourse **to Garuḍa**; famed especially for its second part (the **Pretakalpa / Preta-khaṇḍa**) on death, the afterlife journey, funeral rites, and rebirth, hence its recitation after a death (Garuḍa Purāṇa). See [literature corpus](../90-literature-corpus/).

## Garuḍa Beyond the Hindu Frame

- **Buddhist garuḍas** - A whole race of golden winged beings (Pāli *garuḷa* / *suparṇa*) in perpetual war with the nāgas; one of the classes attending the Buddha (Pāli Canon; Jātakas). [cross-tradition]
- **Jain Garuḍa** - Yakṣa attendant of certain Tīrthaṅkaras and a mount-emblem in Jain iconography (Jain āgama/iconography). [cross-tradition]
- **Southeast Asia** - National/royal emblem in **Indonesia (Garuda Pancasila)** and **Thailand (Khrut)**, descending directly from the Indic Viṣṇu-Garuḍa cult.
