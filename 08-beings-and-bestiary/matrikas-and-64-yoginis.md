# The Mātṛkās & the 64 Yoginīs

> Part of [08 · Beings & Bestiary](./README.md) → the **fierce divine-feminine roster**: emanated Mother-Shaktis and their multiplying retinue of Tantric goddesses.
> Cosmic address: emanations of the [Great Goddess](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md) and of the male devas, active in the deva-age battles of [Brahma's current life](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/README.md). See also the [Daśa Mahāvidyā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md), [Sati–Daksha–Parvati](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md), and [Sacred Geography → Shakti Pithas](../06-sacred-geography/README.md).

The **Mātṛkās** (मातृका, "the little/divine Mothers") are a cluster of fierce goddesses born as the personified **śaktis (energies) of the male gods** — each carrying her father-deity's complexion, weapon, vāhana (mount) and banner, but in a wild, battle-hungry, sometimes child-devouring register. They emerge in two great myth-cycles: the **Devī Māhātmya** (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa) war on Śumbha–Niśumbha and Raktabīja, and the **Andhakāsura** myth (Matsya/Vāmana/Kūrma Purāṇas, *Īśānaśivagurudevapaddhati*). The standard set is **seven (Sapta-Mātṛkā)**, expanded to **eight (Aṣṭa-Mātṛkā)** and **nine (Nava-Mātṛkā)** in Nepal and Tantra. From these Mothers the tradition multiplies the retinue to the **64 Yoginīs (Chausaṭh Yoginī)** — or **81** at Bhedaghat — worshipped in the open-air circular hypaethral temples of the medieval Yoginī cult. **No single canonical roster of the 64 exists**: the scholar Vidya Dehejia compared ~30 differing lists. This node catalogs the Mothers individually and every major count/name variant.

## Part I — The Mātṛkās (The Divine Mothers)

### The Seven (Sapta-Mātṛkā) — standard Devī Māhātmya list

Each is the śakti of a deva, sharing his form, mount and weapon. *(Source: Devī Māhātmya / Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa ch. 8; Matsya Purāṇa 179; Varāha Purāṇa.)*

- **Brāhmaṇī / Brāhmī** - Śakti of **[Brahmā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md)**. Four-faced, yellow-complexioned; carries rosary (*akṣamālā*), *kamaṇḍalu* (water-pot), book, noose; vāhana **haṃsa (swan/goose)**. The "first" Mother, embodying creative speech. (Source: Devī Māhātmya 8; Matsya P. 179)
- **Māheśvarī / Raudrī** - Śakti of **[Śiva/Maheśvara](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)**. White/three-eyed; *triśūla* (trident), *ḍamaru* (drum), *akṣamālā*, serpent and crescent; vāhana **Nandī (bull)**. (Source: Devī Māhātmya 8)
- **Kaumārī / Kārttikeyī** - Śakti of **[Kārttikeya/Skanda](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md)**. Six-headed like her source; carries **śakti (spear)**, axe, bow-and-arrows; vāhana **peacock (mayūra)**. (Source: Devī Māhātmya 8)
- **Vaiṣṇavī / Nārāyaṇī** - Śakti of **[Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)**. Bears **śaṅkha (conch), cakra (discus), gadā (mace), lotus, bow**, cylindrical *kirīṭa* crown; vāhana **Garuḍa**. (Source: Devī Māhātmya 8)
- **Vārāhī** - Śakti of **Varāha** (Viṣṇu's boar-avatāra). Boar/sow-faced; carries *daṇḍa* (rod/staff of punishment), plough (*hala*), goad, vajra or sword, *pānapātra* (drinking-bowl); vāhana **buffalo (mahiṣa)** [variant: elephant/Garuḍa]. Independently a major Tantric goddess (Daṇḍanāyikā, "Commander" of Lalitā's army in the Lalitā-cult). (Source: Devī Māhātmya 8; Lalitā Sahasranāma tradition)
- **Indrāṇī / Aindrī / Māhendrī** - Śakti of **[Indra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md)**. Dark, **thousand-eyed**; carries **vajra (thunderbolt)**, goad, noose, lotus; vāhana **Airāvata (elephant)**. (Source: Devī Māhātmya 8)
- **Cāmuṇḍā / Cāmuṇḍī** - Śakti of **Caṇḍī/Devī herself** (not of a male god — the odd one out). Emaciated, black, sunken-eyed, fanged, garlanded with severed heads (*muṇḍa-mālā*), seated on a corpse/preta; *triśūla*, *ḍamaru*, sword, *pānapātra*; vāhana **jackal/corpse/owl**. Named for slaying the demons **Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa**. The bridge-figure between the Mātṛkās and the [Mahāvidyā Kālī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md). (Source: Devī Māhātmya 7–8)

### The Eighth (Aṣṭa-Mātṛkā) — variants of the added Mother

- *Variant (Devī Māhātmya):* **Nārasiṃhī / Pratyaṅgirā** - śakti of **Narasiṃha** (Viṣṇu's man-lion); lion-faced woman who tosses her mane to scatter stars; the most common 8th. (Source: Devī Māhātmya 8)
- *Variant (Varāha Purāṇa):* the 7th–8th given as **Yāmī / Yāmyā** (śakti of [Yama](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md), with staff/*kāladaṇḍa*, mount buffalo) and **Yogeśvarī / Māhāyogeśvarī** (emanated from Śiva's flaming mouth — see Andhaka myth). [disputed]
- *Variant (Nepal / Newar Aṣṭamātṛkā):* **Mahālakṣmī** or **Śrī/Lakṣmī** replaces Nārasiṃhī as the 8th; the eight are aligned to the **eight cardinal directions** as city-guardians (digpāla) of Kathmandu, Bhaktapur, Patan. [folk]
- *Variant (Vināyakī):* some lists add **Vināyakī / Gajānanā / Vighneśvarī** — the elephant-headed śakti of **[Gaṇeśa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md)** — as a Mother. [late text]

### The Ninth and beyond — Nava-Mātṛkā and larger sets

- **Nava-Mātṛkā (9):** the eight + a central ninth, usually **Mahālakṣmī**, **Cāṇḍikā/Mahākālī**, or **Yogeśvarī** at the centre of the maṇḍala (Bhaktapur tradition). This 9-fold scheme is the seed of the **81 Yoginīs** (9×9). (Source: Śrī Matottara Tantra; Nepalese tradition)
- *Variant (16 Mātṛkās):* the **Devī Purāṇa** and marriage/ritual contexts invoke a set of **sixteen Mātṛkās** (incl. Gaurī, Padmā, Śacī, Medhā, Sāvitrī, Vijayā, Jayā, Devasenā, Svadhā, Svāhā, Mātaras, Lokamātaras, etc.) worshipped before auspicious rites. [disputed]
- *Variant (7 + Yogeśvarī = 8):* in the **Andhaka** cycle the leader **Yogeśvarī**, born of Śiva's mouth-flame, heads the group, making an alternate eightfold set. (Source: *Īśānaśivagurudevapaddhati*)

### The Mātṛkās in iconography & cult

- **Standard slab (Sapta-Mātṛkā paṭṭa):** the seven carved in a row, flanked by **Vīrabhadra** (Śiva's wrath-form, see [Sati–Daksha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)) at one end and **Gaṇeśa** at the other — the canonical Gupta-period and later panel. (Source: Elephanta, Ellora, Aihole, Osian temple sculpture)
- **Tantric placement:** the eight Mātṛkās occupy the **second enclosure (bhūpura) of the Śrī Chakra**, govern the eight **varga** (consonant-groups) of the Devanāgarī alphabet (hence *mātṛkā* = "the matrix of letters/sound"), and pair with the **Aṣṭa-Bhairava**. (Source: Śākta-Tantra; Lalitā tradition)

## Part II — The Origin Myths (variant catalog)

### (A) Devī Māhātmya — Śumbha–Niśumbha & Raktabīja

- **Emanation from the gods** - As Durgā/Cāṇḍikā battles the asura generals, the **Mātṛkās burst forth as śaktis from the bodies of the gods** (Brahmā, Śiva, Skanda, Viṣṇu, Narasiṃha, Indra) — each a feminine double of her source — and join the Goddess to annihilate the demon host. (Source: Devī Māhātmya / Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa 8)
- *Variant (emanated from Devī herself):* in another verse-cluster the Mātṛkās issue **from Caṇḍikā's own body** and at the battle's end **re-merge into her**, asserting that all Mothers are one Mahādevī. (Source: Devī Māhātmya 8, 10)
- **Cāmuṇḍā & Caṇḍa-Muṇḍa** - **Kālī** springs from Durgā's frowning brow, beheads the generals **Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa**, and is renamed **Cāmuṇḍā**. (Source: Devī Māhātmya 7)
- **Raktabīja ("blood-seed")** - The asura **Raktabīja** spawns a clone from **every drop of his blood** that touches earth. Cāmuṇḍā/Kālī **spreads her tongue and drinks the blood** mid-fall (in some tellings the Mātṛkās collectively lap it up) so no clones can form, and the Goddess slays him bloodless. (Source: Devī Māhātmya 8)

### (B) The Andhakāsura myth — the multiplying Mothers

- **The blood-cloning demon Andhaka** - When **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** spears the asura **Andhaka**, each fallen blood-drop becomes a new Andhaka. To check the flood Śiva emits **Yogeśvarī** from the flames of his mouth; **Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Maheśvara, Kumāra, Varāha, Indra and Yama** each send their śakti to follow her and drink the blood. (Source: *Īśānaśivagurudevapaddhati*; Matsya Purāṇa 179; Vāmana Purāṇa)
- **The Mothers' rampage** - Having drunk the blood, the Mātṛkās, drunk on power, **begin devouring gods, demons and creatures indiscriminately**; Viṣṇu/Śiva must pacify them (in some texts Narasiṃha emits gentle Mothers to subdue the fierce ones). This "dangerous Mother" motif underlies their later propitiatory, child-protecting cult. (Source: Matsya Purāṇa; Vāmana Purāṇa)
- *Variant (Vāmana Purāṇa):* the Mātṛkās are created by various gods and number differently across recensions; some add **Mahālakṣmī, Vārāhī-led groups**. [disputed]
- **Skanda connection** - The Mātṛkās are anciently tied to **Skanda/Kārttikeya** as his foster-mothers and the band of fever/seizure-bringing *Mātṛ-gaṇa* in the **Mahābhārata** (*Vana/Shalya Parva*); the malign **child-snatching Mothers (bāla-graha)** of the *Suśruta Saṃhitā* are an early stratum. (Source: Mahābhārata, Āraṇyaka & Śalya Parva; Suśruta Saṃhitā)

## Part III — The 64 Yoginīs (Chausaṭh Yoginī)

The **Yoginīs** are an expanded retinue — fierce, often theriocephalic (animal-headed), Tantric goddesses born by **multiplying the Mātṛkās**: each of 8 Mothers leads 8 attendants → **8 × 8 = 64**; or each of 9 Mothers leads 8 → **9 × 9 = 81**. They grant the **eight siddhis** (occult powers) and are worshipped collectively, never alone, in **circular open-roofed (hypaethral) temples** of the c. 9th–12th-century **Yoginī cult**. *(Source: Vidya Dehejia,* Yoginī Cult and Temples; *Kaulajñānanirṇaya; Agni/Skanda/Kālikā Purāṇas.)*

### Count / list variants (the roster is OPEN-ENDED)

- **64 (Chausaṭh):** the canonical number — 8 squared, an auspicious "even more potent" multiple. Most temples and most Purāṇic *nāmāvalīs* use 64. (Source: Agni P.; Skanda P.; Kālikā P.)
- **81:** the Bhedaghat scheme, 9 Mātṛkās × 9 (see below). (Source: Śrī Matottara Tantra, *Mūla-cakra*)
- **42:** an alternate Tantric count (attested but rarely tabulated). [disputed]
- **No agreement on names:** the **Agni, Skanda and Kālikā Purāṇas each give TWO differing lists**; Dehejia found **~30 lists that "rarely corresponded."** Treat any single 64-roster as one tradition among many. (Source: Dehejia; *Yoginī* surveys)

### A representative 64-roster (Skanda Purāṇa, *Kāśī Khaṇḍa*)

Theriocephalic and ferocious names — first ~49 attested; here as a sample of the type. *(Source: Skanda Purāṇa, Kāśī Khaṇḍa, Yoginī-nāmāvalī.)*

- *List (Skanda P. partial):* **Gajānanā** (elephant-faced), **Siṃhamukhī** (lion-faced), **Gṛdhrāsyā** (vulture-faced), **Kākatuṇḍikā** (crow-beaked), **Uṣṭragrīvā** (camel-necked), **Hayagrīvā** (horse-necked), **Vārāhī** (boar-faced), **Śarabhānanā**, **Ulūkikā** (owl-faced), **Śivāravā**, **Mayūrī** (peahen-faced), **Vikaṭānanā**, **Aṣṭavakrā**, **Koṭarākṣī**, **Kubjā**, **Vikaṭalocanā**, **Śuṣkodarī**, **Lalajjihvā** (lolling-tongued), **Svadaṃṣṭrā**, **Vānarānanā** (monkey-faced), **Ṛkṣākṣī** (bear-eyed), **Kekarākṣī**, **Bṛhattuṇḍā**, **Surapriyā**, **Kapālahastā** (skull-handed), **Raktākṣī** (red-eyed), **Śukī** (parrot), **Śyenī** (hawk), **Kapotikā** (dove), **Pāśahastā** (noose-handed), **Daṇḍahastā** (staff-handed), **Pracaṇḍacaṇḍavikramā**, **Śiśughnī**, **Pāpahantrī**, **Kālī**, **Rudhirapāyinī** (blood-drinker), **Vasādhayā**, **Garbhabhakṣā** (foetus-eater), **Śavahastā** (corpse-handed), **Antramālinī** (entrail-garlanded), **Sthūlakeśī**, **Bṛhatkukṣī**, **Sarpāsyā** (snake-faced), **Pretavāhanā** (corpse-mounted), **Daṇḍaśūkākarā**, **Krauñcī** (heron), **Mṛgaśīrṣā** (deer-headed), **Vṛṣānanā** (bull-faced)** …** [completed to 64 in the source]. [disputed]
- *Variant (Kālikā Purāṇa nāmāvalī):* strongly Mahāvidyā-coloured — incl. **Mahākālī, Durgā, Śivā, Ugracaṇḍā, Caṇḍikā, Cāmuṇḍā, Bhairavī, Bhadrakālī** etc. (Source: Kālikā Purāṇa)
- *Variant (popular devotional list):* many modern temple lists run **Bahubhakṣiṇī, Bhayaṅkarī, Mahābalā, Indrākṣī, Bhīṣaṇī, Karālī …** etc. — folk-canonized, not text-uniform. [folk]
- **Note:** because the Mātṛkās themselves head the troupes, the **8/9 Mothers are counted *among* the 64/81** in some schemes and held *separate as leaders* in others. (Source: *Yoginī* tradition)

## Part IV — The Yoginī Temples (Chausaṭh Yoginī Mandir)

About a dozen survive, all **circular, roofless (hypaethral), with a cell-lined inner wall** and a central shrine to Bhairava/Śiva-Devī — the maṇḍala made stone. A **UNESCO tentative serial nomination** groups them. *(Source: ASI; UNESCO Tentative List 6804; Dehejia.)*

- **Hirapur (Odisha)** - The smallest and best-preserved: **c. 864 CE**, attributed to the **Bhauma-Kara queen Hīrādevī**. Circular, **~25 ft diameter**, hypaethral; **56 of 64** black-stone yoginī images survive in wall-niches, each standing on her vāhana; central shrine of **Kālī/Mahāmāyā** on a human head, attended by Bhairavas. Named yoginīs incl. **Yoginī 63 – Sūrya-putrī** (on a horse) and **Yoginī 64 – Vāyu-vīṇā** (on a blackbuck/antelope). (Source: [Wikipedia: Chausath Yogini Temple, Hirapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chausath_Yogini_Temple,_Hirapur); ASI)
- **Bhedaghat / Gauri-Shankar (Jabalpur, MP)** - The largest: **early 11th c.**, **Kalachuri** king **Yuvarāja II** of Tripuri. Circular, **~125 ft diameter**, **81 cells** (not 64). The 81 follow the **Mūla-cakra of the Śrī Matottara Tantra** — **9 Mātṛkās each leading 8 yoginīs = 9 groups of 9**. Inscribed Mātṛkās: **Vārāhī, Brāhmaṇī, Māheśvarī, Indrāṇī, Vaiṣṇavī, Cāṇḍikā** (riding a corpse in a cremation-ground). Named yoginīs incl. **Kāmadā** ("Giver of Sexual Love") and the horse-faced **Erudī** (perfectly preserved). A later Gaurī-Śaṅkara temple sits at the centre. (Source: [Wikipedia: Chausath Yogini Temple, Bhedaghat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chausath_Yogini_Temple,_Bhedaghat))
- **Ranipur-Jharial (Odisha)** - Circular hypaethral, **64 niches**, central **Naṭeśa-Śiva** (three-faced dancing Śiva); c. 9th–10th c. (Source: ASI; Dehejia)
- **Khajuraho (MP)** - The **oldest surviving** Yoginī temple (c. 900 CE), and exceptionally **rectangular**, with **65 cells** (64 + a larger central one); only stone foundations and a few images remain. (Source: ASI; Dehejia)
- **Mitaoli / Morena (MP)** - **Ekattarso Mahadeva**, c. 1323 CE, **64-celled circular** temple often cited as an inspiration for the **Indian Parliament building's** circular plan; central Śiva shrine. (Source: ASI)
- **Other sites:** **Dudahi, Badoh** (MP), **Lokhari** (UP — animal-headed yoginī reliefs), **Shahdol/Surada** etc. — many ruined or with scattered/looted images. The corpus is **open-ended**; several yoginī sculptures are in museums (British Museum, etc.). (Source: Dehejia; ASI)

### The Yoginī cult — context

- **Kaula/Tantric milieu:** the cult belongs to **Kaula and Kāpālika Śākta-Tantra** (c. 700–1200 CE), tied to the **Śrī-vidyā / Mahāvidyā** matrix; yoginī-worship granted **flight, siddhis, and union with the Yoginī-network (yoginī-melaka)**. (Source: *Kaulajñānanirṇaya* of Matsyendranātha; Dehejia)
- **Decline:** the cult waned by the 13th c. under temple-centred bhakti and later iconoclasm; the open-air temples were abandoned, many images defaced — which is why the rosters survive only fragmentarily. (Source: Dehejia)
- **Living folk worship:** **Chausaṭh Yoginī** survives in folk and Tantric ritual, often invoked as a collective with **Bhairava/Kāla-Bhairava** and the **Sapta-Mātṛkā**. [folk]
