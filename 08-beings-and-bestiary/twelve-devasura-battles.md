# The Twelve Devāsura Saṅgrāmas — the Matsya War-Roster

> Part of [08 · Beings & Bestiary](README.md) → the **canonical numbered list** of the primordial wars between gods and demons, complementing the open-ended [Asura roster & Deva-Asura wars](asura-wars-and-roster.md).
> Cosmic address: **cross-yuga** — the twelve wars are strung across the whole early history of the present kalpa, from the Vārāha lift at creation's dawn through the Vedic Vṛtra-slaying; the *enumeration itself* is a Purāṇic frame-device, recited when Śukra's story is told.

Where most Purāṇic war-material is open-ended, the **Matsya Purāṇa (47.40 ff.)** does something rare: it **counts**. Twelve *devāsura saṅgrāmas* — twelve named, numbered wars between the devas and the asuras — are enumerated as a fixed roster: **Nārasiṃha, Vāmana, Vārāha, Amṛta-manthana, Tārakāmaya, Āḍībaka, Traipura, Andhakāra, Dhvaja, Vārtraghna, Hālāhala, Kolāhala**. The list is the tradition's own **table of contents to its war-mythology**: some entries map onto the most famous avatāra- and Śaiva-battles in the corpus, while others (Dhvaja, Hālāhala, Kolāhala) are bare names whose full narratives no longer survive — fossil evidence that the war-cycle was once even larger. This node gives the enumeration, identifies each war with its canonical deep-dive file, catalogs the identification disputes, and records the frame-story in which the Matsya embeds the list (the shattered asuras, Śukra's quest for power, and Bhṛgu's curse on Viṣṇu).

---

## The Enumeration (Matsya Purāṇa 47.40–75)

- **The twelve saṅgrāmas, in the Matsya's order** — (1) **Nārasiṃha**, (2) **Vāmana**, (3) **Vārāha**, (4) **Amṛta-manthana** ("the churning for nectar"), (5) **Tārakāmaya** ("the war on account of Tārā"), (6) **Āḍībaka** (Āḍīvaka), (7) **Traipura** ("of the Three Cities"), (8) **Andhakāra** (the Andhaka war), (9) **Dhvaja** ("of the Flag/Standard"), (10) **Vārtraghna** ("the Vṛtra-slaying"), (11) **Hālāhala**, (12) **Kolāhala** ("the Uproar"). In these wars the Daityas and Dānavas were repeatedly crushed by the devas with Viṣṇu at their side. (Matsya Purāṇa 47.40 ff.)
- **The frame** — the list is not told for its own sake: it opens the Matsya's account of **why Śukra (Kāvya Uśanas), guru of the asuras, sought new power from Śiva**, and (further back) why Viṣṇu must descend among mortals at all. The wars are the *precondition*: twelve defeats have left the asura hosts broken. (Matsya Purāṇa 47.) — see the frame-story section below and [Bṛhaspati & Śukra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md).
- *Variant (parallel lists):* essentially the same twelve-war enumeration recurs in the **Vāyu Purāṇa** (in its Śukra/vaṃśa chapters, ch. 97–98 in common editions) and the **Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa** — the two texts that share the Matsya's oldest *pañcalakṣaṇa* stratum — and the **Padma Purāṇa** likewise counts the **Tārakāmaya as the fifth** devāsura war (Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa 12). [scholarly] *(Source: Vāyu Purāṇa; Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa; Padma Purāṇa, Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa 12; chapter numbering varies by edition.)*
- *Variant (ordering vs the Daśāvatāra):* the war-list opens **Nārasiṃha → Vāmana → Vārāha**, whereas the standard [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) sequence runs **Varāha before Narasiṃha before Vāmana**. The twelve-war roster is thus an **independent ordering tradition**, not a re-statement of the avatāra list — a sign it crystallized separately. [scholarly] *(Source: comparison of Matsya 47 with the avatāra sequences.)*

## The Twelve Wars, one by one

- **1. Nārasiṃha** — the war culminating in **Viṣṇu as the Man-Lion** slaying **Hiraṇyakaśipu**, the boon-armored Daitya emperor, at twilight, on the threshold, with claws — for the sake of his devotee-son Prahlāda. (Matsya Purāṇa 47; full narrative Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.) → canonical file: [Prahlāda & Narasiṃha](../04-deep-dives/prahlada-narasimha.md).
- **2. Vāmana** — the war (better: the bloodless conquest) in which **Viṣṇu as the Dwarf** begged three paces from the asura-emperor **Bali**, grew to **Trivikrama**, strode over the worlds and **bound Bali**, sending him to rule below — the devas' sovereignty restored without a duel. (Matsya Purāṇa 47; full narrative Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.15–23.) → canonical file: [Vāmana & Bali](../04-deep-dives/vamana-bali.md).
- **3. Vārāha** — the war of **Viṣṇu as the Boar**, who dove into the cosmic ocean, **raised the sunken Earth on his tusks** and slew the Daitya **Hiraṇyākṣa** in the thousand-year underwater duel. (Matsya Purāṇa 47; full narrative Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.13–19.) → canonical file: [Varāha & Hiraṇyākṣa](../04-deep-dives/varaha-hiranyaksha.md).
- **4. Amṛta-manthana** — the **churning of the milk-ocean** counted *as a war*: the joint deva-asura enterprise (Mandara the churn, Vāsuki the rope) that broke into open battle **over the nectar**, Viṣṇu as **Mohinī** diverting the amṛta to the gods and the interloper **Rāhu** beheaded at the drinking. (Matsya Purāṇa 47; full narratives Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.5–12, Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.9; the Matsya carries its own churning-narrative in its later chapters.) → canonical files: [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md), [Kūrma avatāra](../04-deep-dives/kurma-avatara.md), [Mohinī avatāra](../04-deep-dives/mohini-avatara.md); for Rāhu see [Navagraha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md).
- **5. Tārakāmaya** — the great war **"on account of Tārā"**: **Soma (the Moon) abducts Tārā**, wife of Bṛhaspati; Śukra and the asura hosts back Soma, the devas back Bṛhaspati, and the worlds shake until **Brahmā** compels Tārā's return — pregnant with **Budha**, seed of the Lunar dynasty. The Matsya tells the Tārā story itself in its early lineage chapters (around adhyāya 23–24). (Matsya Purāṇa 23–24, 47; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.6; Padma Purāṇa, Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa 12.) → canonical file: [Soma, Tārā & the Tārakāmaya War](../04-deep-dives/soma-tara-budha.md).
  - *Variant (named kills, Harivaṃśa/Brahmāṇḍa):* in the Tārakāmaya fighting **Viṣṇu slays the Dānava Kālanemi** and Indra slays Virocana — the war's most-cited duels. *(Source: Harivaṃśa 1.25 tradition; Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa.)*
  - *Variant (label-confusion):* "Tārakāmaya" is also loosely applied to the **war against Tārakāsura** ended by Skanda — a **distinct conflict** (and one *not* named in the Matsya's twelve). The two are kept separate here. [disputed] *(Source: Purāṇic vs Bhāgavata usage.)* → see [Tārakāsura-vadha](../04-deep-dives/tarakasura-vadha.md).
- **6. Āḍībaka (Āḍīvaka)** — the strangest entry: tradition identifies it with the **feud of Vasiṣṭha and Viśvāmitra fought in bird-form** — the two seers, mutually cursed, battling as the **āḍi** water-bird and the **baka** (crane/heron) — a ṛṣi-duel counted, anomalously, among the devāsura wars. The identification rests on the epic-Purāṇic lexicon tradition (so Vettam Mani's *Purāṇic Encyclopaedia*, s.v.), and how a sage-feud earned a slot in a god-demon war-list is nowhere explained in the Matsya's terse notice. [disputed] [scholarly] *(Source: Matsya Purāṇa 47 (bare name); Mahābhārata Vasiṣṭha-Viśvāmitra cycle; Vettam Mani, Purāṇic Encyclopaedia.)* → for the feud itself see [Viśvāmitra & Vasiṣṭha](../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md).
- **7. Traipura** — the war of the **Three Cities**: the triple flying fortresses of gold, silver and iron built by **Maya** for Tāraka's three sons, destructible only by a single arrow at their once-in-a-millennium alignment; **Śiva as Tripurāntaka**, on the chariot assembled from the cosmos itself, burns all three with one shaft. The Matsya carries one of the longest tellings, including **Maya's nectar-well (vāpī)** that revived the slain asuras until Viṣṇu drained it. (Matsya Purāṇa, Tripura cycle chs. 129–140 [chapter numbering varies]; Mahābhārata Karṇa Parva; Śiva Purāṇa.) → canonical file: [Tripura-dahana](../04-deep-dives/tripura-dahana.md); for the architect see [Maya Dānava](maya-danava.md).
- **8. Andhakāra** — the **Andhaka war**: the blind Daitya-reared son born of the darkness when Pārvatī covered Śiva's eyes, who conquered the worlds and lusted after the Goddess (not knowing her his mother); **Śiva impales him on the triśūla** while the **Mātṛkās** drink the multiplying blood-drops — the Matsya is a primary source for both the sweat-birth and the Mothers. (Matsya Purāṇa, Andhaka chapters (ch. 179 in common editions); Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra-saṃhitā.) → canonical file: [Andhakāsura-vadha](../04-deep-dives/andhakasura-vadha.md); see also [Mātṛkās & the 64 Yoginīs](matrikas-and-64-yoginis.md) and the [Śiva myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md).
- **9. Dhvaja** — "the war of the **Flag/Standard**." The Matsya gives the name in the roster, but **no developed narrative of a Dhvaja war survives** in the extant Purāṇic corpus; the entry is best read as the fossil of a lost war-story that the list's compilers still knew. [scholarly] [disputed] *(Source: Matsya Purāṇa 47 (bare name); absence noted in Purāṇic scholarship.)*
- **10. Vārtraghna** — "the **Vṛtra-slaying**": the foundational combat in which **Indra**, with the **vajra forged from the sage Dadhīci's bones**, kills the drought-serpent **Vṛtra** and releases the pent-up waters — the one entry that reaches back to the Ṛgveda itself. (Matsya Purāṇa 47; Ṛgveda 1.32; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 6.9–13.) → see [Dadhīci](../04-deep-dives/dadhichi.md), [Indra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/indra.md), and the [asura war-roster](asura-wars-and-roster.md) §II.
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata):* Vṛtra as the reborn devotee **Citraketu** — the demon who preaches bhakti to Indra mid-battle. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 6.)* → see [Citraketu](../04-deep-dives/citraketu.md).
- **11. Hālāhala** — a bare name in the roster. It **echoes the Hālāhala/Kālakūṭa poison** of the ocean-churning (swallowed by Śiva), but the churning already holds slot 4, so the war intended here is **unidentified**; no surviving Purāṇic narrative expands it. [disputed] [scholarly] *(Source: Matsya Purāṇa 47 (bare name); for the poison see Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.7.)* → poison episode at [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md).
- **12. Kolāhala** — "the **Uproar**": the twelfth and last, likewise a name without a surviving story. [disputed] *(Source: Matsya Purāṇa 47 (bare name).)*
  - *Editorial cross-reference:* the Matsya elsewhere (ch. 24) narrates a **deadlocked devāsura war decided by the mortal king Raji**, whom both sides court and whose price is Indra's throne; the text does **not** say whether that war corresponds to any of the twelve, but it belongs to the same war-cycle. *(Source: Matsya Purāṇa 24; Viṣṇu Purāṇa IV.9.)* → see [Raji and his sons](../03-lineages-vamsha/raji-and-his-sons.md).

## The Frame-Story: why the Matsya counts the wars

The enumeration is the springboard for the Matsya's **Śukra–Bhṛgu cycle**, one of the corpus's great explanations of the avatāra doctrine:

- **The asuras broken** — after the twelve defeats the Daitya-Dānava hosts are shattered and leaderless; their preceptor **Śukra (Kāvya Uśanas)** resolves to win from **Śiva** new mantras and power that the devas' guru Bṛhaspati does not possess, and departs for a terrible long vow. (Matsya Purāṇa 47.) → see [Bṛhaspati & Śukra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md).
- **The beheading of Śukra's mother** — while Śukra is away the defenseless asuras shelter in his **mother's hermitage**; when the devas attack even there, she **paralyzes Indra** by her yogic power — and **Viṣṇu beheads her with the cakra**: a god slaying a woman, and a brahmin's wife. (Matsya Purāṇa 47.)
- **Bhṛgu's curse — the charter of the human avatāras** — her husband, the sage **Bhṛgu**, revives her and **curses Viṣṇu to be born again and again among mortals** (seven births, in the common reading of the passage) for the woman-slaying — the Matsya thereby framing **Viṣṇu's earthly avatāra-descents as the fruit of a ṛṣi's curse**, an origin quite different from the Gītā's self-willed descent. (Matsya Purāṇa 47.) → see [Bhṛgu](../04-deep-dives/bhrigu.md) and [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Jayantī and the false guru** — Śukra endures his vow; **Jayantī, Indra's daughter**, serves and weds him for a term of years, during which **Bṛhaspati impersonates Śukra** and, teaching in his form, deludes the asuras away from their true preceptor — so that when the real Śukra returns his own disciples reject him, and he curses them. Deva-craft, not battle, completes what the twelve wars began. (Matsya Purāṇa 47; parallel telling Devī-Bhāgavata 4.) [late text] → see [Devī-Bhāgavata](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/devi-bhagavata-upapurana.md).

## The Two Clocks

- **Scriptural/cyclic** — the twelve wars span the deep past of the present kalpa: **Vārāha** at the kalpa's dawn (Earth raised from the waters), **Nārasiṃha/Vāmana** in the Satya-Tretā sequence of the avatāra cycle, the **churning** and **Tārakāmaya** in the primordial divine prehistory before the Lunar dynasty, **Vārtraghna** in the Vedic-primordial layer, **Traipura/Andhakāra** in the Satya-Tretā Śaiva cycle. The list itself never assigns yuga-dates — it is a war-count, not a chronology. (Matsya Purāṇa 47; placements per the individual narratives.)
- **Academic/historical** — the Matsya Purāṇa is dated in its core to roughly the **3rd–5th centuries CE**, with strata reaching several centuries later; the twelve-war list, shared nearly verbatim with the Vāyu and Brahmāṇḍa, is assigned to this **early shared stratum**, making it one of the oldest systematic *inventories* of Hindu war-myth — valuable precisely because three of its twelve entries preserve names of wars whose stories were already lost to the compilers' successors. [scholarly] *(Source: Purāṇic studies consensus on Matsya-Vāyu-Brahmāṇḍa dating; V. S. Agrawala, "Matsya Purāṇa — A Study".)* → text profile: [Matsya Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/matsya-purana.md).
