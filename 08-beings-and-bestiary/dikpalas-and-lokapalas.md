# The Dikpālas — Guardians of the Directions

> Part of [08 · Beings & Bestiary](README.md) → the **directional-guardian frame**: the deva-regents who hold the eight (or ten) quarters of space and so fix the cosmic compass.
> Cosmic address: each presides over one **diś** (direction) from his world on the rim of [Meru](../06-sacred-geography/meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md); collectively they bound the manifest world that the [bestiary classes](README.md) inhabit, with their seats invoked in temple [vāstu](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md) and daily *saṃkalpa*.
> Several are full [devas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) in their own right; their planetary aspects link to the [Navagraha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md), and Kubera doubles as lord of the [yakṣas](yakshas-and-guhyakas.md).

The **Dikpālas** (दिक्पाल, "direction-protectors"; also **Dikpati, Aṣṭa-dikpāla**) — equivalently the **Lokapālas** (लोकपाल, "world-protectors") — are the **deva-regents assigned one to each quarter of space**, who guard the cardinal and intermediate directions and thereby orient the cosmos, the maṇḍala, the temple, and the ritual ground. The system is a **synthesis of two strata**: an older **Vedic Lokapāla** scheme of four/six world-guardians (Indra, Agni, Yama, Varuṇa, Vāyu, Soma) and a later **Tantric–Purāṇic Aṣṭa-dikpāla** scheme of eight, in which the folk/post-Vedic gods **Kubera** and **Īśāna** were added to fill the remaining corners. The closed roster is therefore **eight** (*aṣṭa-dikpāla*); a **ten-fold** (*daśa-dikpāla*) expansion adds **Brahmā** at the zenith (*ūrdhva*) and **Viṣṇu / Ananta** at the nadir (*adho*). Each guardian carries a fixed bundle — **direction, vāhana (mount), āyudha (weapon), śakti (consort), graha (planet), and a world** — and most have a paired **diggaja** (directional world-elephant). Membership is largely fixed, but **several slots carry variants** (Soma/Candra vs. Kubera in the north; Nirṛti's gender; Pṛthivī or Sūrya entering some Vedic lists), all cataloged below. *(Source: Wikipedia "Guardians of the directions"; Mahavidya.ca "The Dikpalas"; Mythlok.)*

Some Vāstu lists swap Sun↔Indra differently or rotate; treat as a **late, school-dependent layer**. [late text] *(Source: Mythlok; Rudraksha-Ratna "Lokapala".)*

## The Eight — guardian by guardian

### East — Indra

- **Indra** (इन्द्र) - **King of the devas** and lord of the **East** (*pūrva*), the senior Lokapāla. Wields the **vajra** (thunderbolt) forged from sage Dadhīci's bones, rides the white elephant **Airāvata**, and reigns over **Svarga / Amarāvatī** (his celestial city) and **Indraloka**. *(Source: Mahavidya.ca; Wikipedia.)*
  - *Consort:* **Śacī / Indrāṇī** ("the mighty"), counted among the Mātṛkās — see [Mātṛkās & 64 Yoginīs](matrikas-and-64-yoginis.md).
  - *Vedic note:* the **oldest and most prominent** Lokapāla; in the four-fold Vedic scheme Indra holds the East over rain, storm, and war. *(Source: Mahavidya.ca, "older gods of the Vedas".)*
  - *Graha/diggaja:* Sun; elephant **Airāvata** (whose mate is **Abhramu**). Cross-link [Devas & Lokas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).

### Southeast — Agni

- **Agni** (अग्नि) - God of **fire** and lord of the **Southeast** (*āgneya*, named for him). Rides a **ram (meṣa)**, bears a **flaming spear/śakti** (or javelin), often two-headed and many-tongued. Rules the fire-realm; as the **mouth of the gods** he conveys oblation. *(Source: Mahavidya.ca; Wikipedia "Agni".)*
  - *Consort:* **Svāhā** (the ritual exclamation personified, by which oblations reach the gods).
  - *Vedic note:* second only to Indra in Ṛgvedic hymn-count; a core Vedic Lokapāla. *(Source: Wikipedia "Agni".)*
  - *Graha/diggaja:* Venus; elephant **Puṇḍarīka** (mate **Kapilā**).

### South — Yama

- **Yama** (यम) - God of **death and dharma**, lord of the **South** (*dakṣiṇa*) and judge of the dead. Rides a **buffalo (mahiṣa)**, carries the **daṇḍa** (rod of punishment) and **pāśa** (noose of death); rules **Yamaloka / Saṃyamanī** (his city of judgment) in the southern under-region. *(Source: Mahavidya.ca; Wikipedia.)*
  - *Consort:* **Yamī / Yamunā** (his twin sister) in Vedic kinship; **Varāhī** as his Mātṛkā-śakti in Tantric lists. [variant]
  - *Epithets:* **Dharmarāja, Pitṛpati** (lord of the ancestors), **Antaka, Kāla**. The yakṣa of the [Yakṣa Praśna](yakshas-and-guhyakas.md) is Yama in disguise.
  - *Graha/diggaja:* Mars; elephant **Vāmana** (mate **Piṅgalā**).

### Southwest — Nirṛti

- **Nirṛti** (निरृति) - Deity of the **Southwest** (*nairṛta*), embodiment of **decay, dissolution, misfortune, and the realm of the dead's corruption**. Depicted **dark-skinned, dark-haired, dark-clad**, riding a **corpse, lion, or man**, bearing a **sword (khaḍga)**. *(Source: Mahavidya.ca; Wikipedia.)*
  - *Variant (gender):* In the **Vedas, Nirṛti is a goddess** of destruction, decay, and sorrow (Ṛgveda hymns avert her); in the **Purāṇic Dikpāla scheme the regent is often a male rākṣasa-king, "Nirṛta," or the rākṣasa lord** of the SW. Both forms are attested. [disputed] *(Source: Mahavidya.ca; Wikipedia.)*
  - *Variant (substitution):* some lists give the SW to a **rākṣasa-regent** (the "Nairṛta" demon) rather than the goddess; in Buddhist maṇḍala-sets the SW guardian is likewise a rākṣasa. [scholarly]
  - *Consort:* **Khaḍginī** (or Nirṛti herself when the regent is male).
  - *Graha/diggaja:* Rāhu (the SW being the inauspicious quarter); elephant **Kumuda** (mate **Anupamā**).

### West — Varuṇa

- **Varuṇa** (वरुण) - God of the **cosmic waters, the ocean, and ṛta (cosmic order)**, lord of the **West** (*paścima*). Rides the **makara** (crocodile/sea-monster), wields the **pāśa** (noose with which he binds the guilty and the sinner); rules the **western ocean** and the waters generally. *(Source: Mahavidya.ca; Wikipedia.)*
  - *Consort:* **Vāruṇī** (goddess of wine/spirits, who emerged at the [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)).
  - *Vedic note:* an **Āditya** and one of the **highest Vedic deities** — originally sovereign of the moral order and the night sky (paired with Mitra), later narrowed to the waters and the West. *(Source: Mahavidya.ca, "older gods of the Vedas".)*
  - *Graha/diggaja:* Saturn; elephant **Añjana** (mate **Tāmrakarṇī**).

### Northwest — Vāyu

- **Vāyu** (वायु) - God of **wind, air, and breath (prāṇa)**, lord of the **Northwest** (*vāyavya*, named for him). Rides a **deer/antelope (mṛga)**, bears a **goad (aṅkuśa)** and/or **banner (dhvaja)**; rules the wind-realm and the vital airs. *(Source: Mahavidya.ca; Wikipedia.)*
  - *Consort:* **Vāyavī** (or **Bhāratī**).
  - *Progeny:* father of **Hanumān** and **Bhīma** (the "wind-born") — see [Hanumān](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md).
  - *Vedic note:* a core Vedic Lokapāla; **Pavana, Anila, Mātariśvan** are his names.
  - *Graha/diggaja:* Moon; elephant **Puṣpadanta** (mate **Śubhradantī**).

### North — Kubera

- **Kubera** (कुबेर) - God of **wealth** and **king of the [yakṣas/guhyakas](yakshas-and-guhyakas.md)**, lord of the **North** (*uttara*). Rides a **man (Naravāhana)** or a horse, bears the **gadā (mace)** and a **money-pot**; rules **Alakā** on Mount Kailāsa and the northern quarter of treasure. *(Source: Mahavidya.ca; Wikipedia "Kubera".)*
  - *Consort:* **Kauberī / Bhadrā / Ṛddhi** ("Prosperity").
  - *Variant (north slot):* in the **older Vedic six-fold list the North (or the lunar quarter) is held by Soma / Candra**, the moon-god; **Kubera is a post-Vedic / folk-cult addition** who displaced or doubles Soma in the North. [variant] *(Source: Mahavidya.ca, "Kubera and Isana are from folk cults"; Wikipedia.)*
  - *Graha/diggaja:* Mercury; elephant **Sārvabhauma** (mate **Aṅganā**). Full court: [Yakṣas & Kubera's Court](yakshas-and-guhyakas.md).

### Northeast — Īśāna

- **Īśāna** (ईशान) - A **form of Śiva/Rudra**, "the ruler / the one from whom the universe originates," lord of the **Northeast** (*aiśānya* / *īśānya*) — the most auspicious quarter. Rides the **bull Nandī**, bears the **triśūla (trident)**; associated with **knowledge, liberation, and prosperity**. *(Source: Mahavidya.ca; Wikipedia.)*
  - *Consort:* **Īśānī / Pārvatī**.
  - *Note:* one of Śiva's **five faces (Pañcānana)** is **Īśāna** (facing up/NE); the Dikpāla and the Sadāśiva face share the name. Cross-link [Śiva Myth-Cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md).
  - *Variant (NE slot):* some older lists give the NE or a corner to **Sūrya** or to **Soma**; Īśāna, like Kubera, is a **folk/post-Vedic addition** completing the eight. [variant] *(Source: Mahavidya.ca.)*
  - *Graha/diggaja:* Jupiter; elephant **Supratīka** (mate **Añjanavatī**).

## The two added quarters — the Daśa-dikpāla (ten)

When space is reckoned with the vertical axis, two more guardians complete the **ten directions** (*daśa-diś*). *(Source: Wikipedia "Guardians of the directions"; Mahavidya.ca.)*

- **Brahmā** (ब्रह्मा) - Guardian of the **Zenith** (*ūrdhva*, "up"; sometimes placed between NE and E). The **creator**, four-faced and four-armed, riding the **goose/swan (haṃsa)**; he caps the world-axis above. *(Source: Mahavidya.ca; Wikipedia.)* — see [Trimūrti](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).
- **Viṣṇu / Ananta** (विष्णु / अनन्त) - Guardian of the **Nadir** (*adho*, "down"; sometimes placed between SW and S). The **preserver**, four-armed and dark-blue, holding **conch, discus, mace/lotus**; in the nadir-role often given as **Ananta / Śeṣa**, the world-bearing serpent on whom Viṣṇu reclines — see [Nāgas & Serpents](nagas-and-serpents.md). *(Source: Mahavidya.ca; Wikipedia.)*
  - *Variant (nadir-regent):* the down-direction is variously assigned to **Viṣṇu**, to **Ananta/Śeṣa**, or to **Vāsuki/the nāga-kings** as bearers of the lower worlds. [variant]

## The Diggajas — the directional world-elephants

The **Aṣṭa-diggaja** ("eight elephants of the quarters") are mythical elephants that **uphold the earth at the directions**, born at creation and (per the *Mātaṅga-līlā*) serving as the **vāhanas of the Dikpālas**. Headed by Airāvata, ordained by Brahmā (Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Canto 8). Each has a named mate. *(Source: Wikipedia "Ashtadiggajas"; wisdomlib "Mātaṅga-līlā"; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.)*

- **Airāvata** (E) — mate **Abhramu**; Indra's mount.
- **Puṇḍarīka** (SE) — mate **Kapilā**.
- **Vāmana** (S) — mate **Piṅgalā**.
- **Kumuda** (SW) — mate **Anupamā**.
- **Añjana** (W) — mate **Tāmrakarṇī**.
- **Puṣpadanta** (NW) — mate **Śubhradantī**.
- **Sārvabhauma** (N) — mate **Aṅganā**; Kubera's lokapāla-elephant.
- **Supratīka** (NE) — mate **Añjanavatī**.
  - *Variant (name-spellings/order):* lists differ slightly across the *Mātaṅga-līlā*, *Amarakośa*, and Purāṇas (e.g. **Puṇḍarīka/Vāmana** sometimes swapped S↔SE; **Añjana/Pīṣṇa** variants in the W); the **eight is fixed, the spellings are not**. [disputed] *(Source: wisdomlib "Mātaṅga-līlā"; Wikipedia "World Elephant".)*

## Historical layering & variant schemes

- **Vedic Lokapālas (the older stratum)** - The earliest scheme names **four to six** world-guardians: **Indra (E), Agni (SE), Yama (S), Varuṇa (W), Vāyu (NW), Soma/Candra (N or the lunar quarter)** — all **deep Vedic gods**. *(Source: Mahavidya.ca, "the first six are older gods of the Vedas".)*
- **Tantric–Purāṇic Aṣṭa-dikpāla (the eight)** - **Kubera (N) and Īśāna (NE) are added from folk/post-Vedic cults**, displacing/doubling Soma and completing the eight corners used in **Tantra, Vāstu, and temple ritual**. [scholarly] *(Source: Mahavidya.ca; Wikipedia.)*
- **Daśa-dikpāla (the ten)** - + **Brahmā (zenith)**, **Viṣṇu/Ananta (nadir)**.
- *Variant (substitutions):* some lists insert **Pṛthivī (Earth)** as a guardian, give the nadir to **Vāsuki**, or rotate the **graha** assignments; Vedic vs. Tantric vs. Vāstu schools differ. [disputed] *(Source: Wikipedia; Rudraksha-Ratna.)*
- *Variant (Nava-sanga — Indo-Javanese):* the **Balinese/Javanese Nawa Sanga** is a **nine-fold** maṇḍala of Śiva-centered guardians with **Śiva at the center** and eight deities (Iśvara, Maheśvara, Brahmā, Rudra, Mahādeva, Śaṅkara, Viṣṇu, Sambhu) at the quarters — a regional re-systematization. [scholarly] *(Source: Wikipedia "Guardians of the directions".)*
- *Ritual use:* the Dikpālas are invoked in the **dik-bandhana** (sealing the directions) opening tantric/pūjā rites, in **Vāstu Puruṣa Maṇḍala** temple orientation, and as **corner-deities (parivāra-devatā)** carved on temple plinths/maṇḍapas. *(Source: Mythlok; Exotic India "Ashtadikpalakas".)* — see [temple architecture](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md).

> **Roster note:** the **eight (and ten) guardians are closed and canonical**, but the **consort-names, planet-assignments, Nirṛti's gender, the north/NE substitutions (Soma↔Kubera, Sūrya↔Īśāna), and diggaja spellings are open at the edges** — cataloged above per source.
