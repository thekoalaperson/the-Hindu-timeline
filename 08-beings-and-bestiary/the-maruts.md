# The Maruts — Storm-Gods of the Rudra Host

> Part of [08 · Beings & Bestiary](README.md) → the **Marut troop** (*marut-gaṇa*): the Vedic storm/wind-gods, **sons of Rudra and Pṛśni**, battle-companions of [Indra](../90-literature-corpus/vedic-deities-and-suktas.md), and — in the Purāṇic re-telling — the **49 broken pieces of Diti's embryo** turned into devas.
> Roster context: a sub-class of the **Rudra-host**, kin to the **11 Rudras** of [the-thirty-three-devas.md](the-thirty-three-devas.md); the working storm-pantheon of the [Vedas](../90-literature-corpus/vedic-deities-and-suktas.md). For the wider god-list see the [33 devas](the-thirty-three-devas.md); for the master index see [the timeline root](../README.md).

The **Maruts** (*marut*, plural *marutaḥ*; also **Marutaṇ, Rudras, Rudriyas, Marutvats**) are the **storm-troop** of the Ṛgveda — a single, fiercely cohesive **gaṇa** ("troop, band") of young warrior-gods who ride the thunderstorm: wind, lightning, thunder, and the bursting monsoon rain. They are sung in some **33+ whole hymns** of the Ṛgveda and invoked in scores more, second in storm-imagery only to Indra himself, whose chariot-comrades they are in the slaying of **Vṛtra**. Two distinct accounts of who they *are* run side by side in the tradition: the **Vedic genealogy** (sons of **Rudra** and the dappled cow-goddess **Pṛśni**, brothers born together, self-arising from the storm) and the **Purāṇic/Rāmāyaṇa origin-myth** (the *Marut-utpatti*: **49 devas born of an asura-mother**, Diti, when Indra split her embryo seven-times-seven). This node catalogs both clocks — Vedic and Paurāṇic — every **count variant** (7, 21, 27, 49, 60, 180), the troop's weapons and golden ornaments, the term **Marutvat**, and the **seven wind-skandhas** of cosmic air.

---

## The frame: a troop, not a list

- **Marut-gaṇa — "the troop"** - The defining feature: the Maruts act and are praised **as a collective band**, rarely as named individuals. They march, shout, and fight **in formation** like a war-host (*śardha, gaṇa, vrāta*). Their unity is theological, not just numeric — they are a single storm seen as many. *(Source: Ṛgveda Marut-sūktas, e.g. RV 1.85, 1.87, 5.52–61.)*
- **Etymology** - From **√mṛ / mar** ("to gleam, shine," or "to crush/die") or from **√ru / rud** ("to roar, cry") — the latter punned in the Purāṇic name-myth (*mā rud* = "do not weep"). Yāska's **Nirukta** derives *marut* from their *roaring* and *measuring out* (the rains). *(Source: Yāska, Nirukta; traditional etymology.)*
  - *Variant (folk etymology):* the Rāmāyaṇa/Purāṇic *Marut-utpatti* fixes the name to Indra's cry **"mā rodīḥ / mā ruda"** ("do not cry") to the weeping embryo-fragments — a *post hoc* word-play, not the historical root. [folk] *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa Bāla-kāṇḍa 46–47; Vāmana Purāṇa.)*
- **Two parentage-clocks** - **Vedic:** sons of **Rudra** ("the **Rudras / Rudriyas**") by **Pṛśni** the cow. **Purāṇic:** sons of **Kaśyapa** by **Diti** (mother of the [asuras](asura-wars-and-roster.md)), making them **devas born of an asura-womb** — a deliberate paradox of the later myth. *(Source: Ṛgveda — Rudra/Pṛśni; Rāmāyaṇa & Purāṇas — Kaśyapa/Diti.)*

## I. The Vedic Maruts — sons of Rudra and Pṛśni

- **Sons of Rudra (the Rudras)** - Repeatedly called **Rudrasya sūnavaḥ** / **Rudriyāḥ** ("Rudra's sons," "the Rudra-ones"); their father's fierce, healing, and destructive nature is theirs. Hence the bestiary kinship with the **eleven Rudras** of [the 33 devas](the-thirty-three-devas.md). *(Source: RV 1.85.1; 2.33; 5.57–58.)*
- **Sons of Pṛśni** - **Pṛśni** ("the dappled / speckled one") is the **mottled storm-cow** (the cloud-cow), their mother; the Maruts are her **"sons of the cow," Pṛśni-mātaraḥ** ("having Pṛśni for mother"). *(Source: RV 1.85.2; 5.52.16; 5.60.5.)*
  - *Variant (born of the cloud/ocean):* some verses make them spring from **the laughing lightning** or the **drops of the sky**, self-generated from the storm rather than literally born. *(Source: RV 1.168; 1.38.)*
- **Brothers of equal age, none eldest** - A famous self-description: **"None of you is the eldest, none the youngest — all are alike in greatness"** (*na vaḥ jyeṣṭho... kanīyān*), the very emblem of the troop's equality. *(Source: RV 5.59.6; 5.60.5.)*
- **Companions of Indra (the Marutvat Indra)** - The Maruts are **Indra's war-band**, lending their roar and force as he slays **Vṛtra** and releases the waters; Indra as their lord is titled **Marutvat** ("accompanied by the Maruts"). Yet the Ṛgveda also preserves a **quarrel-and-reconciliation** (the *Marut-Indra* dispute, RV 1.165, 1.170–171) where Agastya mediates their share of the offering. *(Source: RV 1.165 — the Indra–Marut dialogue; 1.85.9; 8.7.)*
- **Friends of Vāyu, Rudra, Agni, Parjanya** - Allied with **[Vāyu/Vāta](../90-literature-corpus/vedic-deities-and-suktas.md)** (wind) and **Parjanya** (rain-cloud); they bring the monsoon, split mountains, fell trees, and shake the earth. *(Source: RV 1.38; 5.53–55; 1.64.)*

### Weapons, mounts, and golden ornaments

- **Lightning-spears and the bow** - They wield **lightnings (vidyut)** as spears/lances, **golden axes**, and shoot **shafts of rain**; described with **iron tusks** and roaring **like lions** or bellowing bulls. *(Source: RV 1.39; 1.85; 5.54; 5.57.)*
- **Golden ornaments** - Conspicuously bedecked: **golden breast-plates (rukma)** on their chests, **golden helmets**, **anklets (khādi)** on feet and arms, jeweled necklaces — a host that glitters as it storms. *(Source: RV 5.54.11; 5.56–57; 2.34.)*
- **Chariots and ruddy/spotted deer** - Ride **golden cars** drawn by **ruddy horses** or **spotted deer (pṛṣatī)** — the dappled does of the cloud-mother. *(Source: RV 1.37–39; 1.64; 5.55.)*
- **Direction & nature** - Sometimes seated in the **north-west**; violent, generous (showering rain = wealth), and also **healers** (inheriting Rudra's medicines, the *jalāṣa*). *(Source: RV 2.33; 8.20; 5.53.)*

### Vedic count variants

- **Three times seven = 21** - The classic Vedic enumeration: **"thrice-seven" (tri-saptāḥ)** Maruts — **21**. *(Source: RV 1.133.6; tradition.)*
- **Three times sixty = 180** - The *largest* Ṛgvedic count: **"thrice sixty Maruts"** (*triḥ ṣaṣṭi*) — **180** — the troop swollen to a storm-army. *(Source: RV 8.96.8.)*
- **Twenty-seven (27)** - A count given in the Vedic strata and echoed by later reckonings. *(Source: Vedic enumeration; cf. Brāhmaṇa lists.)*
- **Seven (7)** - The minimal/primary count: **seven Maruts**, or **seven troops** — the seed-number that the Purāṇic myth squares into 49. *(Source: Vedic & Purāṇic.)* [open-ended] The named individual Maruts are **not consistently rostered** anywhere; the troop is treated as a unit, so any "list of 49 names" is a late, non-canonical reconstruction. [flag]

## II. The Purāṇic Marut-utpatti — the 49 from Diti's broken womb

This is the tradition's chief *origin-story* and the one most retold: the Maruts as **devas born of the asura-mother Diti**, the result of Indra shattering her embryo into **seven, then seven-times-seven (49)**. Found in the **Rāmāyaṇa Bāla-kāṇḍa (sargas 46–47)**, narrated by **Viśvāmitra** to Rāma, and across the Purāṇas (**Vāmana, Viṣṇu, Brahmāṇḍa, Vāyu, Mārkaṇḍeya, Bhāgavata**).

- **Diti's vow (the payovrata / "embryo of revenge")** - **Diti**, wife of **Kaśyapa** and mother of the slain [asuras](asura-wars-and-roster.md), grieving for sons killed by Indra, begs Kaśyapa for **a son who will kill Indra**. He grants a child of overwhelming power **on condition of a strict vow** — a **100-year** (in some tellings 1,000-year) penance of purity (the *Indra-śatru* embryo). *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa Bāla-kāṇḍa 46; Vāmana P. 71.)*
- **The interrupted vow (the lapse of purity)** - Near the vow's end (with **~10 years / a fraction left**), Diti commits an **impurity**: she sleeps **with her head where her feet should lie** / **with hair untied / at the forbidden twilight hour** — a breach of ritual rule. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa Bāla-kāṇḍa 46–47.)*
  - *Variant (the breach):* tellings differ on the exact lapse — sleeping at dusk with wet/loose hair, or head-and-feet reversed, or napping by day. All converge on **ritual impurity opening the womb to Indra**. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa; Purāṇic variants.)*
- **Indra enters the womb** - Seizing the impurity, **Indra makes himself minute** and **enters Diti's womb**, intending to destroy the foe-to-be before birth. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa Bāla-kāṇḍa 47; Vāmana P. 71.)*
- **The seven-fold split (vajreṇa)** - With his **vajra (thunderbolt)** Indra **cuts the embryo into seven pieces**. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa Bāla-kāṇḍa 47; Vāmana, Viṣṇu, Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇas.)*
- **"Mā ruda" — do not weep** - Each of the seven fragments **cries out**; Indra, relenting, says **"mā rodīḥ / mā ruda"** ("**do not cry!**"). The crying continuing, he **cuts each of the seven again into seven**, yielding **49** — and the cry **mā-rud** becomes their name **Marut**. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa Bāla-kāṇḍa 47; Vāmana P. 71.)* [folk etymology]
- **Diti's boon — they become gods** - **Diti**, waking and forgiving Indra, asks that her **49 fragment-children be made devas**, companions of Indra, ranged through the heavens. Indra grants it: **"they shall move as gods (deva-rūpeṇa), for they are your offspring."** Thus **asura-born become deva** — the paradox at the myth's heart. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa Bāla-kāṇḍa 47; Purāṇas.)*
- **Count = 49 (7 × 7)** - The Purāṇic standard: **forty-nine Maruts**, organized as **seven groups of seven**. *(Source: Vāmana P. 71; Viṣṇu P.; Brahmāṇḍa P.)*
  - *Variant (49 as Kaśyapa's sons):* the **Vāmana Purāṇa (ch. 71)** frames all 49 explicitly as **sons of Kaśyapa** (i.e. by Diti). *(Source: Vāmana Purāṇa 71.)*
  - *Variant (Rāmāyaṇa's seven-realms placement):* the Rāmāyaṇa has Diti ask that **the seven become rulers of seven ethereal regions** under Indra — folding the storm-troop into the **wind-stations** (see §III). *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa Bāla-kāṇḍa 47.)*

## III. The seven Vāyu-skandhas — cosmic wind-stations

The Rāmāyaṇa and Viṣṇu Purāṇa identify the Maruts (in their seven-troop reckoning) with the **seven "trunks/courses of wind" (vāyu-skandha)** that hold the cosmos in motion — each a named **wind that pervades a celestial zone**.

- **Āvaha** (आवह) - the wind pervading **clouds, thunderbolts, rain, meteors** — the lowest, weather-bearing air ("the in-bearer"). *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa II; Rāmāyaṇa Bāla 47.)*
- **Pravaha** (प्रवह) - the wind of the **solar orbit** ("the flowing-forth"); drives the sun. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.)*
- **Saṃvaha / Saṃhava** (संवह) - the wind of the **lunar orbit**. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.)*
- **Udvaha** (उद्वह) - the wind of the **stars / nakṣatra (galactic) sphere** ("the up-bearer"). *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.)*
- **Vivaha / Vivaha** (विवह) - the wind of the **planetary spheres (grahas)**. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.)*
- **Parivaha** (परिवह) - the wind of the **Seven-Sages sphere (Saptarṣi / Ursa Major)**; named also **Marīci**, the **chief among purifiers** ("Of purifiers I am the wind," Bhagavad Gītā 10.31 = *pavatām asmi mārutaḥ*). *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa II; Bhagavad Gītā 10.31.)*
- **Parāvaha / Varāvaha** (परावह) - the highest wind, of the **polar / Dhruva (north-pole) region**, that turns the whole star-wheel. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.)*
  - *Variant (names of the seven):* spellings and zone-assignments differ slightly across Purāṇas (Vāyu, Liṅga, Matsya) — e.g. **Āvaha, Pravaha, Saṃvaha, Udvaha, Vivaha, Parivaha, Parāvaha** is the common septad; some texts swap *Nivaha* in for one course. [late text] *(Source: Viṣṇu/Vāyu/Matsya Purāṇas.)*

## IV. Marutvat, Marutvats, and the seven gaṇas

- **Marutvat** (मरुत्वत्) - "**possessing/accompanied by the Maruts**" — chiefly an epithet of **Indra** as their lord and battle-leader; also a soma-pressing/offering-share named for them (the **Marutvatīya** graha in śrauta ritual). *(Source: RV; Śrauta-sūtras; Brāhmaṇas.)*
- **Marutvats / Marutgaṇas as a deva-class** - In the Purāṇic god-lists the **Marutvats / Maruts** appear as a **standing class of devas** (alongside Sādhyas, Viśvedevas, etc.) — the storm-troop institutionalized as a celestial order serving Indra. *(Source: Viṣṇu/Bhāgavata Purāṇas.)*
- **Seven gaṇas (troops) of seven** - The 49 are grouped into **seven troops**, mapped onto the seven wind-courses (§III); thus **7 leaders × 7 = 49**, the Purāṇic squaring of the Vedic "seven." Individual Marut-names within each troop are **not fixed in canon** and vary by text. [open-ended roster] *(Source: Vāmana/Viṣṇu Purāṇas; tradition.)*
- **All-counts summary** - **7** (primary/troop-leaders), **21** (thrice-seven, RV 1.133.6), **27** (Vedic/Brāhmaṇa), **49** (Purāṇic, 7×7), **60 / 180** (thrice-sixty, RV 8.96.8). The number is **deliberately open** — a troop, not a census. *(Source: Ṛgveda; Purāṇas — collated.)*
