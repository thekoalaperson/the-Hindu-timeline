# The Prajāpatis & Brahmā's Mind-Born Sons

> Part of [08 · Beings & Bestiary](README.md) → the **progenitor-class roster**: the *manasaputras* ("mind-born sons") whom Brahmā emits at the dawn of creation, and the **Prajāpatis** ("lords of progeny") through whose marriages every class of being — devas, asuras, nāgas, birds, beasts, sages, men — is descended.
> Cosmic address: the very first generation of the present *kalpa* (see [01 · Brahmā's Current Life](../01-manvantaras/README.md)); the brahmana-pole of cosmic administration. These same figures are the original [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md) and the patriarchs of the [Sūrya-/Candra-vaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
> Up-link to repo [`../README.md`](../README.md).

The **Prajāpati** (प्रजापति, "lord of creatures / progeny") is at once a *title*, an *office*, and a *roster*. In the oldest stratum (Ṛgveda, Brāhmaṇas) **Prajāpati** is a creator-god in his own right, later **identified with Brahmā**. In the Purāṇic frame the word names the **secondary creators** — the mind-born sons whom Brahmā produces precisely because his own direct ("sattvic") creation of the [Kumāras](#the-four-kumāras--the-celibate-secession) refuses to procreate. Through these Prajāpatis, and above all through **Dakṣa's daughters married to Kaśyapa**, the entire population of the cosmos is bred — making this node the **genealogical root of the whole bestiary**. The counts and membership are **not fixed**: the canonical figures are **7** (the mind-born seers), **10** (Manusmṛti), or **21** (Mahābhārata), with the rosters catalogued below. *(Source: Wikipedia "Prajapati"; Manusmṛti 1.34–35; Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva 12.207/12.384; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.7; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.12.)*

---

## 1. The Mind-Born Sons (Mānasaputras) — Origin

- **Why mind-born?** - At the start of the *kalpa* Brahmā, charged to populate the worlds, first emits offspring **from his mind/body without sexual union** — *mānasaputra* ("mind-son"), *mānasika sṛṣṭi* ("mental creation"). These are the **first conscious beings**, born pure (*sattvic*), and are simultaneously titled **Prajāpatis** and (most of them) **Brahmarṣis** and the first **[Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md)**. *(Source: Manusmṛti 1.34–35; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.7; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.12; Wisdomlib "Mind-born son".)*
- **The failed first creation** - Brahmā's *very first* mind-sons, the **four Kumāras**, declined to procreate and took to eternal celibacy (§2). To get creation moving Brahmā then emitted the **procreating Prajāpatis** — the seven seers plus Dakṣa, Bhṛgu, Nārada — and from his anger the **Rudras**, and from his own body the first man **Manu Svāyambhuva** and woman **Śatarūpā**. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.12.4–28; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.7.)*
- **Emitted from the limbs/faculties (Purāṇic catalogue)** - Different texts assign each mind-son a *birth-organ*: e.g. **Marīci from the mind/eyes, Atri from the eyes, Aṅgiras from the mouth, Pulastya from the ear, Pulaha from the navel, Kratu from the hand, Vasiṣṭha from the breath/prāṇa, Bhṛgu from the skin, Dakṣa from the thumb/breath, Nārada from the lap, the Kumāras from the mind** — lists vary by Purāṇa. [disputed] *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.12; Matsya Purāṇa; Wisdomlib "Manasaputra".)*
- **They are also the Saptarṣi** - The seven procreating mind-sons (Marīci, Atri, Aṅgiras, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu, Vasiṣṭha) are mapped onto the **seven stars of the Great Bear** and constitute the **first manvantara's Saptarṣi** — for the full per-manvantara rotation and all variant seven-lists see [saptarishi.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md). *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.7, 3.1; Mahābhārata.)*

## 2. The Roster Variants — 7 vs 10 vs 21 Prajāpatis

There is **no single canonical count**. The three principal lists:

- **Seven Prajāpatis (the mind-born seers; commonest ancient set)** - **Marīci, Aṅgiras, Atri, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu, Vasiṣṭha** — identical to the Purāṇic [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md). *(Source: Wikipedia "Prajapati"; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.7.)*
- **Ten Prajāpatis (Manusmṛti 1.34–35)** - the seven seers **+ Dakṣa, Bhṛgu, Nārada** → **Marīci, Aṅgiras, Atri, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu, Vasiṣṭha, Dakṣa, Bhṛgu, Nārada**. This is the roster most often meant by "the ten Prajāpatis / ten sons of Brahmā." *(Source: Manusmṛti 1.34–35; Wikipedia "Prajapati".)*
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata 3.12 nine + later):* the Bhāgavata first names **nine** mind-sons — Marīci, Atri, Aṅgiras, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu, Bhṛgu, Vasiṣṭha, **Dakṣa** — adding **Nārada** (and the Kumāras, Manu, Rudra) separately, so the count drifts 9/10. [disputed] *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.12.21–27.)*
  - *Variant (substitutions):* some lists swap **Prachetas/Pracetasas, Vivasvat, or Kaśyapa** in for one of the ten; Kaśyapa (Marīci's son) is so central as the universal father that he is sometimes counted a Prajāpati himself. *(Source: vedicfeed; Hindu FAQs "Prajapatis – the 10 sons of Lord Brahma".)*
- **Twenty-one Prajāpatis (Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva 12.384 / Hari­vaṃśa)** - a maximal list folding in the great gods and Manus: **Brahmā, Rudra, Manu, Dakṣa, Bhṛgu, Dharma, Tapa, Yama, Marīci, Aṅgiras, Atri, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu, Vasiṣṭha, Parameṣṭhī, Sūrya, Candra, Kardama, Krodha, Vikṛta**. *(Source: Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva ch. 384; Wisdomlib "Prajapati".)*
  - *Variant (Rāmāyaṇa-type 16/17 list):* a related enumeration gives roughly sixteen — substituting **Kaśyapa, Pracetas, Śeṣa, Ariṣṭanemi** for some of the Mahābhārata names. [disputed] *(Source: Wikipedia "Prajapati".)*
- **Roster note** - The Prajāpati group is therefore **open-membership**: a finite *core* (the 7 seers, Dakṣa, Bhṛgu, Nārada, the Kumāras) is stable, but the wider list expands to 21 by absorbing Brahmā himself, the Rudras, the Sun and Moon, and the Manus. Membership and birth-organ assignments are flagged [disputed] above.

## 3. The Seven Seer-Prajāpatis (the procreating mind-sons)

Each is a Prajāpati, a Brahmarṣi, the first [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md), and the founder of a brahmana *gotra*.

- **Marīci** (मरीचि, "ray of light") - The **eldest** mind-son; father (by **Kalā**, a daughter of Kardama/Devahūti) of **Kaśyapa** — making Marīci the **grandfather of devas, asuras and nearly all beings**. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.12, 4.1; Viṣṇu Purāṇa.)*
- **Atri** (अत्रि) - Mind-son and Saptarṣi; husband of **Anasūyā** (a daughter of Kardama, paragon of chastity); father of **Soma/Candra** (the Moon, ancestor of the Candravaṃśa), **Dattātreya** (the Trimūrti-avatāra), and **Durvāsas** (the irascible sage). *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.1; Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa.)* — see [Candravaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
- **Aṅgiras** (अङ्गिरस्) - Mind-son; husband of **Śraddhā/Smṛti**; patriarch of the **Āṅgirasa** seers; father of **Bṛhaspati** (guru of the devas) and **Utathya**; ancestor of **Bharadvāja** and the **Gautamas**. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Mahābhārata.)*
- **Pulastya** (पुलस्त्य) - Mind-son; through his son **Viśravas** he is the **ancestor of Kubera** (wealth-god) and of the **rākṣasas** — Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa, Vibhīṣaṇa. The transmitter of the Viṣṇu Purāṇa to Parāśara. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.1; Rāmāyaṇa Uttara Kāṇḍa.)* — see [rākṣasas](rakshasas.md), [yakṣas → Kubera](yakshas-and-guhyakas.md).
- **Pulaha** (पुलह) - Mind-son and Saptarṣi; in some lists father of various animal-classes (kimpuruṣas, śarabhas) and of certain rākṣasa/piśāca lines; the most lightly attested of the seven. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa; Bhāgavata Purāṇa.)*
- **Kratu** (क्रतु, "sacrificial resolve") - Mind-son and Saptarṣi; said to father the **Vālakhilyas** — the 60,000 thumb-sized sages who escort the Sun's chariot. Childless in some accounts; adopts in others. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa.)*
- **Vasiṣṭha** (वसिष्ठ) - The greatest mind-son; **kulaguru of the Sūryavaṃśa** (Ikṣvāku dynasty, hence guru of Rāma); husband of **Arundhatī** (the faint star beside the Bear); owner of the wish-cow **Nandinī/Kāmadhenu**; eternal rival of **Viśvāmitra**. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa; Mahābhārata; Viṣṇu Purāṇa.)* — see [Sūryavaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).

## 4. The Other Core Prajāpatis (Dakṣa, Bhṛgu, Nārada)

- **Dakṣa** (दक्ष, "the skilled/able") - The **arch-Prajāpati of procreation** — through his **many daughters** (§6) he is the *grandfather of literally all beings*. Born from Brahmā's thumb/breath; husband of **Prasūti** (daughter of Manu Svāyambhuva) and/or **Asikni/Vīriṇī/Pāñcajanī** (daughter of the ocean-king). Father of **Satī** (Śiva's first wife) — whose death at his sacrifice triggers the **Dakṣa-yajña** catastrophe; Dakṣa is **beheaded by Vīrabhadra** and restored with a **goat's head**. The supreme *orthodox*-sacrifice patriarch, foil to the antinomian Śiva. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.1–7; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.7; Mahābhārata.)* — full cycle at [sati-daksha-and-parvati.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md).
  - *Variant (re-birth as Pracetas's son):* after the goat-head episode Dakṣa is **reborn in the next manvantara** as son of the **ten Pracetas** (sons of Prācīnabarhis) and **Māriṣā**, and again fathers daughters — the "second Dakṣa." [late text] *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.15; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 6.4–6.)*
- **Bhṛgu** (भृगु) - Mind-son and Prajāpati; patriarch of the **Bhārgava** clan; husband of **Khyāti** (a daughter of Dakṣa); father of **Lakṣmī's foster-line / the sage Śukra (Uśanas)** — *guru of the asuras* — and of **Cyavana**. Famous for **kicking Viṣṇu's chest** (the *Bhṛgu-test* deciding the supreme god), earning the foot-mark **Śrīvatsa**. Reputed compiler of the **Bhṛgu Saṃhitā** (astrology) and contributor to the Manusmṛti. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.89; Mahābhārata, Ādi/Anuśāsana Parva; Padma Purāṇa.)*
- **Nārada** (नारद) - Mind-son and Prajāpati; the **deva-ṛṣi** (divine sage), eternal wanderer between the worlds with his **vīṇā (Mahatī)**, chanting *"Nārāyaṇa"*; **mischief-making catalyst** of countless myths (the *kalaha-priya*, "lover of strife," whose "stirring" is always providential). Cursed by Dakṣa to **wander ceaselessly** for persuading Dakṣa's sons (the Haryaśvas & Śabalāśvas) to renounce procreation. Teacher of **Vālmīki, Prahlāda, Dhruva, Vyāsa**; reciter of the **Nārada Bhakti Sūtra**. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.5, 6.5; Mahābhārata; Nārada Purāṇa.)*

## 5. The Four Kumāras — the Celibate Secession {#the-four-kumāras--the-celibate-secession}

- **The four boy-sages** - Brahmā's **very first** mind-sons, born fully realized and choosing **eternal childhood (≈5 years old) and celibacy (naiṣṭhika-brahmacarya)** rather than procreation — the "failed first creation" that forced Brahmā to emit the other Prajāpatis. Roving the universe naked as innocents, masters of **jñāna** and the path of **nivṛtti** (renunciation). *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.12.4–7, 4.22; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.7.)*
  - **Sanaka** (सनक, "ancient") - eldest of the four.
  - **Sanandana** (सनन्दन, "joyful").
  - **Sanātana** (सनातन, "eternal").
  - **Sanatkumāra** (सनत्कुमार, "ever-young") - the most prominent; teacher of **Nārada** in the Chāndogya Upaniṣad (7), and of the **Sanatkumāra Saṃhitā**.
  - *Variant (a fifth, Sanatsujāta):* some texts add **Sanatsujāta** (teacher of Dhṛtarāṣṭra in the *Sanatsujātīya*, Mahābhārata Udyoga Parva) as a fifth Kumāra, or identify him with Sanatkumāra. [disputed] *(Source: Mahābhārata, Udyoga Parva 41–46.)*
- **The Jaya–Vijaya curse** - The Kumāras' wrath at being barred from **Vaikuṇṭha** by the gatekeepers **Jaya & Vijaya** curses the pair to three demonic births — becoming **Hiraṇyākṣa/Hiraṇyakaśipu, Rāvaṇa/Kumbhakarṇa, Śiśupāla/Dantavakra** — the engine of three of Viṣṇu's avatāra-descents. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.15–16, 7.1.)* — see [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Founders of a sampradāya** - The Kumāras head the **Kumāra/Nimbārka sampradāya** (one of the four Vaiṣṇava lineages). *(Source: tradition.)* — see [acharyas-and-sampradayas](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md).

## 6. Dakṣa's Daughters — the Womb of All Creation

Dakṣa's daughters are the **great branching-point of the cosmos**: married to Kaśyapa they produce *every class of being*; married to Candra they *are* the lunar mansions; married to Dharma they personify the virtues. The **count is wildly variant** (the heart of the variant-policy here).

- **Count variants** - *60 daughters* by Vīriṇī/Asiknī (Mahābhārata Ādi Parva, Harivaṃśa); *24 daughters* by Prasūti (Purāṇas, e.g. Bhāgavata 4.1); the Mahābhārata elsewhere says *"sixteen"* or *"fifty"* daughters become the mothers of all beings. The most-cited working number for the cosmologically crucial set is **13 (sometimes 17) married to Kaśyapa**. [disputed] *(Source: Mahābhārata Ādi Parva; Harivaṃśa; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.1; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.15; Wikipedia "List of Daksha's daughters".)*

### The 13 wives of Kaśyapa → all the classes of beings

The single most important table in Hindu genealogy — see also [README §hierarchy](README.md) and the per-class files linked below.

- **Aditi** (अदिति, "boundless") → the **Ādityas / devas** — 12 solar gods incl. **Indra, Vivasvat (Sūrya), Viṣṇu (Vāmana)**. *(Source: Bhāgavata 6.6; Mahābhārata Ādi.)* — see [devas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
- **Diti** (दिति) → the **Daityas** — titan-demons **Hiraṇyākṣa, Hiraṇyakaśipu**, and the **Maruts** (after Indra split her embryo). *(Source: Bhāgavata 6.18; Viṣṇu Purāṇa.)* — see [asura-wars](asura-wars-and-roster.md).
- **Danu** (दनु) → the **Dānavas** — 40+ ocean/sky demons incl. **Vṛtra, Namuci, the Maya-architect, the Nivātakavacas**. *(Source: Mahābhārata Ādi 65; Bhāgavata 6.6.)* — see [asura-wars](asura-wars-and-roster.md).
- **Kadrū** (कद्रू) → the **Nāgas** — serpent-folk **Ananta/Śeṣa, Vāsuki, Takṣaka, Karkoṭaka**, et al. *(Source: Mahābhārata Ādi 16, 35.)* — see [nāgas](nagas-and-serpents.md).
- **Vinatā** (विनता) → **Garuḍa** (king of birds, Viṣṇu's mount) and **Aruṇa** (the Sun's charioteer); ancestress of the great eagles **Jaṭāyu & Sampāti**. *(Source: Mahābhārata Ādi 16, 20–24.)*
- **Surasā** (सुरसा) → the **Nāgas** in some texts (rival claim to Kadrū), or flying serpents/herons; the sea-rākṣasī who tests **Hanumān**. *(Source: Mahābhārata; Rāmāyaṇa Sundara Kāṇḍa.)* [disputed]
- **Surabhi / Kāmadhenu** (सुरभि) → **cattle and buffaloes** (the *gomātā* lineages), and the Rudras in some lists. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.15; Bhāgavata.)*
- **Tāmrā** (ताम्रा) → **birds of prey and water-birds** — her daughters (Śyenī, Bhāsī, Krauñcī, Dhṛtarāṣṭrī, Śukī…) mother eagles, vultures, owls, parrots, swans. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.21; Rāmāyaṇa.)*
- **Krodhavaśā** (क्रोधवशा, "ruled by anger") → **fanged beasts and biting/clawing creatures** (her many daughters mother carnivores, serpents, and assorted predators). *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.21; Rāmāyaṇa Araṇya Kāṇḍa.)*
- **Khaśā / Khasā** (खशा) → the **Yakṣas and Rākṣasas** (or the demonic *Khasas*). *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.21; Mahābhārata.)* — see [yakṣas](yakshas-and-guhyakas.md), [rākṣasas](rakshasas.md).
- **Muni** (मुनि) → the **Apsaras** (or Gandharvas/celestial nymphs). *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.21.)* — see [apsaras](apsaras.md), [gandharvas](gandharvas-and-kinnaras.md).
- **Irā / Ilā** (इरा) → **trees, creepers, grasses and all flora** (the green world). *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.21.)*
- **Ariṣṭā** (अरिष्टा) → the **Gandharvas** (celestial musicians). *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.21.)* — see [gandharvas](gandharvas-and-kinnaras.md).
  - *Variant (additional Kaśyapa-wives):* lists add **Kāṣṭhā** (→ horses, hooved beasts), **Timi** (→ aquatic creatures/fish, whence *timiṅgila* whales), and **Sarāmā** (→ wild beasts/dogs, mother of the deva-bitch **Saramā**), pushing the count to 17. Membership and offspring-assignments **vary by Purāṇa**. [disputed] *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.21; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 6.6; Wikipedia "List of Daksha's daughters".)*

### The 27 wives of Candra (Soma) — the Nakṣatras

- **The 27 lunar-mansion daughters** - Dakṣa gives **27 daughters to Candra/Soma (the Moon)**; each **is** a *nakṣatra* (lunar asterism) through which the Moon travels in his monthly circuit: **Aśvinī, Bharaṇī, Kṛttikā, Rohiṇī, Mṛgaśīrṣa, Ārdrā, Punarvasu, Puṣya, Āśleṣā, Maghā, Pūrva-Phalgunī, Uttara-Phalgunī, Hasta, Citrā, Svātī, Viśākhā, Anurādhā, Jyeṣṭhā, Mūla, Pūrvāṣāḍhā, Uttarāṣāḍhā, Śravaṇa, Dhaniṣṭhā, Śatabhiṣā, Pūrva-Bhādrapadā, Uttara-Bhādrapadā, Revatī**. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 6.6, 9.14; Mahābhārata.)*
  - **Rohiṇī** - the Moon's **favorite**; his neglect of the other 26 provokes Dakṣa's curse that the Moon **waxes and wanes** (consumption/*kṣaya-roga*) — the mythic origin of the lunar phases; partial relief is granted at **Somanātha/Prabhāsa**. *(Source: Bhāgavata 9.14; Skanda Purāṇa.)*
  - *Variant (28th mansion):* some traditions add **Abhijit** as a 28th nakṣatra-daughter, giving the Moon **28** wives. [disputed] *(Source: astronomical tradition; Mahābhārata.)*

### The daughters married to Dharma — the personified virtues

- **The 10–13 Dharma-wives** - Dakṣa gives daughters to **Dharma** who *are* the moral abstractions: commonly **Śraddhā (faith), Maitrī (friendship), Dayā (compassion), Śānti (peace), Tuṣṭi (contentment), Puṣṭi (nourishment), Kriyā (action), Unnati (advancement), Buddhi (intellect), Medhā (wisdom), Titikṣā (forbearance), Hrī (modesty), Mūrti (form)**. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.7; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.1.)*
  - **Mūrti** → mothers **Nara-Nārāyaṇa** (the twin sage-avatāra of Viṣṇu, ancestors of Arjuna-Kṛṣṇa). *(Source: Bhāgavata 4.1.)*

### The other notable Dakṣa-daughters

- **Satī** (सती) → married **Śiva**; self-immolates at the Dakṣa-yajña and is reborn as **Pārvatī**. *(Source: Śiva Purāṇa; Bhāgavata 4.2–7.)* — see [sati-daksha-and-parvati.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md).
- **Svāhā** (स्वाहा) → married **Agni**; her name is the **oblation-cry** of every fire-sacrifice; mother of **Pāvaka, Pavamāna, Śuci** (the three sacred fires). *(Source: Mahābhārata Vana Parva; Bhāgavata.)*
- **Svadhā** (स्वधा) → married the **Pitṛs** (ancestral fathers); her name is the **śrāddha-oblation-cry** to the ancestors. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa; Bhāgavata 4.1.)*
- **Khyāti** → married **Bhṛgu** (§4); **Sambhūti** → **Marīci**; **Smṛti** → **Aṅgiras**; **Anasūyā** → **Atri**; **Ūrjā** → **Vasiṣṭha**; **Prīti** → **Pulastya**; **Kṣamā** → **Pulaha**; **Sannati** → **Kratu** — i.e. Dakṣa's daughters are also the **wives of the seven seer-Prajāpatis**, closing the genealogical loop. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.10; Bhāgavata 4.1.)*

## 7. The Sons of Dakṣa who Refused — the Haryaśvas & Śabalāśvas

- **Haryaśvas** (हर्यश्व) - Dakṣa's **first 1,000 (or 10,000) sons**, sent to procreate; **Nārada** dissuades them with a riddle about the futility of worldly creation, and they **renounce and disperse**, never to return. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 6.5; Viṣṇu Purāṇa.)*
- **Śabalāśvas** (शबलाश्व) - Dakṣa's **next 1,000 sons**, sent after the first vanished; **Nārada converts them too**. Enraged, Dakṣa **curses Nārada to wander the worlds eternally** (§4). Only after these losses does Dakṣa beget **daughters** (§6) — so the **female line becomes the channel of all creation**. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 6.5; Viṣṇu Purāṇa.)*

---

## 8. The Two Streams of Creation — Pravṛtti vs Nivṛtti

- **Procreating Prajāpatis (pravṛtti, "outgoing")** - The seven seers, Dakṣa, Bhṛgu — through whom the worlds are **filled** with progeny; the path of **action and householder dharma**. *(Source: Bhāgavata 3.12; Viṣṇu Purāṇa.)*
- **Renouncing mind-sons (nivṛtti, "returning")** - The **four Kumāras** and **Nārada** — who **refuse progeny** for the path of **knowledge and devotion**; their "failure" to populate is the cosmic charter of the renouncer's path. The whole creation-story is thus a **dialectic of binding and liberation** built into the first generation. *(Source: Bhāgavata 3.12; 6.5.)*
