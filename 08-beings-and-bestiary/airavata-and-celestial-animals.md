# Airāvata, Uccaiḥśravas & the Manthana Treasures (Living Beings)

> Part of [08 · Beings & Bestiary](README.md) → the **named divine animals** who are full beings, not mere mounts: the elephant-king, the horse-king, and the world-elephants who hold up the quarters.
> Cosmic frame: most surfaced at the **[Samudra-manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)** (the churning), are claimed by [Indra](the-thirty-three-devas.md) or the asuras, and reappear as [vāhanas](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md) and as the world-bearers paired with the [Dikpālas](dikpalas-and-lokapalas.md).
> See also [master index](../README.md) and the broader [ratna roster of the churning](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md).

The **Manthana treasures** include several **living beings who are persons in the mythology**, not objects: **Airāvata** the white elephant of Indra, **Uccaiḥśravas** the white horse, the cow **Surabhi/Kāmadhenu**, the tree **Pārijāta**, and the goddess **Lakṣmī** herself. This file is dedicated to the **animal-beings** — chiefly Airāvata and Uccaiḥśravas — and the **diggaja / aṣṭa-diggaja** system of world-elephants that grows out of Airāvata's lineage. The unifying thread is the **Durvāsas-garland insult**, which is what triggers the churning in the first place. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8; Mahābhārata Ādiparva & Āstīkaparva; Viṣṇu Purāṇa; Matsya Purāṇa; Amarakośa.)

## The triggering episode — Durvāsas, Airāvata & the lost fortune

- **Durvāsas gifts a garland to Indra** - the sage Durvāsas (an aṃśa of Śiva) offers Indra a never-fading celestial garland (a wreath of *santānaka* / *vaijayantī* flowers swarming with bees). Indra, seated on Airāvata, carelessly places it on the elephant's head. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.5–8.6; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.9.)*
- **Airāvata casts it down** - irritated by the bees (or the scent), Airāvata flings the garland to the ground and tramples it. Durvāsas, enraged at this contempt for his *prasāda*, curses Indra and the devas to be **stripped of śrī (fortune, vigour), subject to decay, defeat and old age**. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.9; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.5.)*
  - *Variant (Airāvata Wikipedia summary):* the curse makes the demigods "subject to old age and death," and reversing it requires the devas to churn the ocean for *amṛta* — making the elephant's act the literal cause of the [Samudra-manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md). [scholarly]
- **Consequence** - the cursed, weakened devas lose to the asuras, flee to Brahmā and Viṣṇu, and are told to **churn the Milk Ocean** in alliance with the asuras to recover *amṛta* and the lost treasures — among which Airāvata and Uccaiḥśravas themselves re-emerge. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.6; Mahābhārata Ādiparva 1.16–17.)*

## Airāvata — the four-tusked white elephant of Indra

- **Airāvata** (ऐरावत, "produced from water / son of Irāvat") - the **gajendra (king of elephants)**, pure milk-white, the *vāhana* of [Indra](dikpalas-and-lokapalas.md), and the guardian-elephant of the **East**. Also called **Abhra-mātaṅga** ("elephant of the clouds"), **Nāga-malla** ("fighting elephant"), and **Arka-sodara / Arka-bandhu** ("brother of the Sun," as both rose from the churning). *(Source: Mahābhārata; Amarakośa; Matsya Purāṇa.)*
- **Form variants [cataloged]** - the texts disagree sharply on his anatomy:
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata):* a **white elephant with four tusks** rose at the churning; Devendra (Indra) caught and tamed it. [the four-tusk form named in this file's title]
  - *Variant (common Purāṇic):* **three heads, six tusks, three trunks**.
  - *Variant (expanded Purāṇic):* **ten tusks, five trunks, ten teeth** — the numbers read as the **ten directions**.
  - *Variant (Thai/Erawan tradition):* **three or even thirty-three heads** (matching the Thirty-three [devas](the-thirty-three-devas.md)). [folk]
- **Origin variants [cataloged]** - three incompatible births:
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa):* born **from the churning of the Ocean of Milk** (Samudra-manthana) as one of the *ratnas*. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.9.)*
  - *Variant (Rāmāyaṇa):* born **to Irāvatī**, a descendant of Kaśyapa — hence the patronymic "Airāvata." *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa.)*
  - *Variant (Mātaṅgalīlā / elephant-lore):* born when **Brahmā chanted sacred hymns over the two halves of the eggshell** from which Garuḍa hatched — Airāvata first, then seven more male and eight female elephants (the seed of the aṣṭa-diggaja system). *(Source: Mātaṅgalīlā; cf. wisdomlib.)* [late text]
  - *Reconciling gloss:* commentators note **each Manvantara has its own Indra**, hence its own Airāvata — so multiple "origins" are multiple Airāvatas across the cosmic ages. (See [Manvantaras](../01-manvantaras/README.md).) [scholarly]
- **Consort** - **Abhramu** (अभ्रमु, "cloud-weaver"), the female elephant who is Airāvata's mate; the pair are imagined as **rain-makers**, drawing water from the netherworld and spraying it into the clouds so Indra can release it as rain. *(Source: Matsya Purāṇa; Mātaṅgalīlā.)*
- **Rain-and-war role** - Airāvata is the **mount on which Indra slays Vṛtra**; the elephant–water–rain link makes him a fertility and monsoon symbol, and he **stands sentinel at the gate of Svarga** (Indra's heaven). *(Source: Mahābhārata; Ṛgvedic Vṛtra cycle.)*
- **As a serpent-name [disputed cross-listing]** - the Mahābhārata's nāga catalogue also lists an **"Airāvata"** among the great serpents (a separate homonymous being, often conflated). See [nāgas](nagas-and-serpents.md). *(Source: Mahābhārata Ādiparva, nāga list.)*

### Gajendra-mokṣa — the elephant-king's liberation

- **Gajendra** - an elephant-king (a former human king, **Indradyumna**, cursed to elephant-birth) who, while gathering lotuses on **Mount Trikūṭa** in the Milk Ocean, is seized by the leg by a **crocodile (makara / the gandharva Hūhū cursed by sage Devala)**. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.2–8.4.)*
- **The cry and the rescue** - after a thousand-year struggle, exhausted, Gajendra lifts a lotus and cries to **Viṣṇu/Hari**; Viṣṇu flies in on Garuḍa and beheads the crocodile with the **Sudarśana-cakra**, freeing both. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.3–8.4.)*
- **The liberation** - Gajendra attains **sārūpya-mukti** (a form like the Lord's) and goes to Vaikuṇṭha; the crocodile, freed of its curse, regains gandharva form. The episode is read as the soul (*jīva*) trapped by *saṃsāra* (the crocodile) and saved by total surrender (*prapatti*). *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.4; Śrī-Vaiṣṇava commentary — see [acharyas](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md).)*
- *Note:* Gajendra is **a distinct elephant-being from Airāvata** — both share Skandha 8 of the Bhāgavata, and both stand for the elephant as the supreme animal-devotee; the prompt groups them as the great elephant-beings of the Manthana book. [clarification]

## The aṣṭa-diggaja — eight world-elephants of the quarters

The **diggajas** (दिग्गज, "directional elephants") are the **eight (sometimes four) elephants who stand at the rim of the world bearing the quarters of space on their heads**; their stirring causes earthquakes. First fixed as a set of eight in the **Amarakośa** of Amarasiṃha (c. 5th c. CE). They are paired one-to-one with the [Dikpālas](dikpalas-and-lokapalas.md) and with the directional female elephants (*dik-kariṇī*). *(Source: Amarakośa; Matsya Purāṇa; Mātaṅgalīlā; Wikipedia "Ashtadiggajas" / "World Elephant".)*

| Direction (diś) | Diggaja (male) | Consort (dik-kariṇī) | Paired Dikpāla |
|---|---|---|---|
| East (Pūrva) | **Airāvata** | Abhramu / Abhrā | [Indra](dikpalas-and-lokapalas.md) |
| Southeast (Āgneya) | **Puṇḍarīka** | Kapilā | Agni |
| South (Dakṣiṇa) | **Vāmana** | Piṅgalā | Yama |
| Southwest (Nairṛti) | **Kumuda** | Anupamā | Nirṛti |
| West (Paścima) | **Añjana** | Tāmrakarṇī / Tāmraparṇī | Varuṇa |
| Northwest (Vāyavya) | **Puṣpadanta** | Śubhadantī | Vāyu |
| North (Uttara) | **Sārvabhauma** | Aṅganā | Kubera |
| Northeast (Aiśānya) | **Supratīka** | Añjanāvatī | Īśāna |

- **Origin of the eight** - per Mātaṅgalīlā, all eight (and their mates) sprang from **Brahmā's chanting over the eggshell of Garuḍa**; the males from the right half, the females from the left — making them **Airāvata's brothers/lineage**, the elephant equivalent of the Dikpāla compass. *(Source: Mātaṅgalīlā; wisdomlib "aṣṭadiggajas".)* [late text]
- **Cosmological function** - they **uphold and stabilize the earth**; their fatigue or shifting is a mythic cause of earthquakes, and they anchor the *maṇḍala* used in temple [vāstu](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md). *(Source: Matsya Purāṇa; World-Elephant tradition.)*
- *Variant (roster spelling/order):* names appear in slightly different transliterations and pairings across sources (e.g., Supratīka sometimes placed in other corners; female names vary as Abhrā/Abhramu, Tāmrakarṇī/Tāmraparṇī). [open-ended roster] *(Source: Amarakośa vs. Mātaṅgalīlā vs. Purāṇic lists.)*
- **Supratīka (homonym) [disputed]** - the Mahābhārata also names a war-elephant / a former being **Supratīka**; the northeast diggaja and the epic elephant share the name but are catalogued separately. *(Source: Mahābhārata; Wikipedia "Supratika".)*

## Uccaiḥśravas — the seven-headed white horse

- **Uccaiḥśravas** (उच्चैःश्रवस्, "loud-neighing" / "long-eared") - the **king and prototype of horses (aśva-rāja)**, **snow-white**, **seven-headed**, and **flying**; he arose at the [Samudra-manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md) alongside Lakṣmī and the other ratnas, "born from the *amṛta*." *(Source: Mahābhārata Ādiparva 1.16–17; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.8; Viṣṇu Purāṇa.)*
- **Who won him [cataloged]:**
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata / common):* **Indra seized him as his vāhana** when he surfaced from the churning. *(Source: Mahābhārata Ādiparva.)*
  - *Variant (Vaiṣṇava/asura tradition):* taken by the **asura-king Bali**, who used the horse "to attain many impossible things"; the asuras claim him in the division of treasures. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Vaiṣṇava retellings.)* [cataloged]
- **Krishna's self-identification** - in the Bhagavad-gītā (10.27, *Vibhūti-yoga*), Kṛṣṇa declares: **"Among horses know Me as Uccaiḥśravas, born of nectar (amṛta-udbhavam)."** *(Source: Bhagavad-gītā 10.27.)*
- **Made king of horses** - the Viṣṇu Purāṇa records that when **Pṛthu** was installed as earth's first king, Uccaiḥśravas was **made the king of horses (aśva-rāja)** in the parallel installation of category-kings. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa.)*
- *Connection to Sūrya's horses [disputed]:* some traditions link the white horse-prototype to the **seven horses of Sūrya's chariot** / to the solar horse, given his seven heads and the "brother of the Sun" motif shared with Airāvata. [folk]

### The Kadrū–Vinatā wager over his tail

- **The bet** - the co-wives of Kaśyapa — **Kadrū** (mother of the nāgas) and **Vinatā** (mother of [Garuḍa](nagas-and-serpents.md) and Aruṇa) — wager over the **colour of Uccaiḥśravas's tail**: Vinatā says **white**, Kadrū says **black**. **The loser becomes the winner's slave (dāsī).** *(Source: Mahābhārata Ādiparva, Āstīkaparva 1.18–20.)*
- **The deception** - Kadrū orders her **nāga sons to coil around the tail as black hairs**, darkening it. The reluctant serpents who refuse are cursed by their mother **to perish in the snake-sacrifice (sarpa-satra) of King Janamejaya** — a curse that drives the entire frame-story of the Mahābhārata's nāga lore. *(Source: Mahābhārata Āstīkaparva.)*
- **The outcome** - at dawn the sisters go to the ocean's edge; the tail looks **black**, so **Kadrū wins and Vinatā is enslaved**. *(Source: Mahābhārata Āstīkaparva.)*
- **The downstream chain [major consequence]** - Vinatā's bondage is later **redeemed by her son Garuḍa**, who is forced to fetch the **amṛta from Indra's heaven** to ransom his mother — this is the origin of Garuḍa's theft of the nectar, his enmity with the serpents, and his recruitment as Viṣṇu's [vāhana](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md). *(Source: Mahābhārata Ādiparva, Garuḍa cycle.)*
- *Variant (timing):* some retellings place the wager **before**, others **right at** the churning when the horse first appears; the constant is that the tail-deception begets the **nāga–Garuḍa enmity** and the snake-sacrifice curse. [cataloged]

## The other living Manthana treasures (cross-reference)

- **Surabhi / Kāmadhenu** - the wish-fulfilling **divine cow** who rose at the churning, given to the *ṛṣis* for ghee/oblations; mother of cattle and source of the *pañcagavya*. See [sacred fauna](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/sacred-flora-and-fauna.md). *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.8; Mahābhārata.)*
- **Pārijāta** - the **wish-granting coral tree** taken to Indra's heaven (later carried off by Kṛṣṇa for Satyabhāmā). A being-like treasure, plant rather than animal. See [sacred flora](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/sacred-flora-and-fauna.md). *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa; Harivaṃśa.)*
- **Lakṣmī (Śrī)** - the goddess of fortune, the supreme "treasure," who chose Viṣṇu; her emergence is the restoration of the very *śrī* lost to the Durvāsas curse. See [deities](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md). *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.8.)*
- **The full ratna list** is catalogued in the deep-dive: [Samudra-manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md).
