# Kāmadhenu, Surabhi & the Wish-Granting Cows

> Part of [08 · Beings & Bestiary](README.md) → the **bovine divinities** — the cosmic wish-fulfilling cow(s), their daughters, and the sacred cattle of the sages.
> Cosmic address: born of the **Samudra Manthana** (or of Dakṣa / Prajāpati), enthroned in **Goloka** above the three worlds and dwelling also in **Rasātala / Pātāla** (see [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md)).
> Cross-links: [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md) · [Viśvāmitra & Vasiṣṭha](../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md) · [Sacred Flora & Fauna](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/sacred-flora-and-fauna.md) · master index [↑](../README.md)

The **Kāmadhenu** (कामधेनु, "wish-milking cow") — most often identified with **Surabhi** (सुरभि, "the fragrant / sweet-smelling one") — is the **mother of all cattle**, a cow of plenty (*go-mātā*) who yields whatever is desired: milk, ghee, food, wealth, soldiers, even *amṛta*. She is at once a single cosmic being, the womb of every bovine line, a roster of named daughters distributed across the lokas, and a recurring **plot-engine** in the epics: whenever a king covets a sage's cow, war follows. Sources cannot be reconciled into one biography — the texts give multiple births, multiple residences, and multiple "the Kāmadhenu" (Surabhi, Nandinī, Śabalā, the cow of Jamadagni). Scholar **Vettam Mani** harmonizes this by holding that *there may be more than one Kāmadhenu, all incarnations of the one original mother-cow*. This node catalogs every named cow, every birth-variant, the deities lodged in her body, and the three great cow-conflicts, flagging the open-ended rosters where the sources leave them so.

---

## Identity & Names (variants)

- **Kāmadhenu / Surabhi** - the wish-granting mother of cows; the names are used interchangeably in most Purāṇic strata, though some texts make Surabhi the cosmic original and Kāmadhenu a title or emanation. *(Source: MBh; Purāṇas; Vettam Mani, Purāṇic Encyclopaedia.)*
  - *Variant (Rāmāyaṇa):* Vasiṣṭha's cow is named **Śabalā** ("the dappled/brindled one"), explicitly called the daughter of Surabhi. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa.)*
  - *Variant (epithets):* also **Sabalā, Nandā, Kapilā, go-mātā** ("mother-cow"); in iconography the **hybrid Kāmadhenu** with a woman's crowned head, eagle wings and peacock tail is a late poster-art form (held to have influenced Persian Burāq imagery). [late text] *(Source: Philadelphia Museum of Art; comparative iconography.)*
- **A class, not only an individual** - "Kāmadhenu" functions as a type: any cow that grants all wishes (Vasiṣṭha's, Jamadagni's, the four quarter-cows of Pātāla) is a Kāmadhenu. The full roster of such cows is **open-ended**. [flagged: open roster] *(Source: epic + Purāṇic usage.)*

## Birth & Origin (catalog of variants)

- **From the Samudra Manthana** - Surabhi/Kāmadhenu rises among the **fourteen ratnas** churned from the Ocean of Milk by devas and asuras; Brahmā then assigns her to yield the milk and ghee that sustain Vedic sacrifice, and gives her to the seven *ṛṣis*. *(Source: MBh Ādi Parva; Viṣṇu, Bhāgavata Purāṇas.)* — see [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md).
  - *Variant (recipients):* in some tellings the churned cow is taken by the sages for *yajña*; in others she is divided so her offspring populate the four quarters. *(Source: Purāṇic strata.)*
- **From Dakṣa's belch** - in the **Anuśāsana Parva**, after Dakṣa Prajāpati drinks the *amṛta* risen from the churning, Surabhi is born from his **belch (udgāra)**; from her in turn spring **many golden Kapilā cows**, the "mothers of the world." *(Source: MBh Anuśāsana Parva.)*
- **From Brahmā's mouth** - the **Udyoga Parva** narrates that Brahmā, having drunk *amṛta*, vomited and Surabhi emerged; froth from her mouth fell to earth and produced the four guardian-cows. *(Source: MBh Udyoga Parva.)*
- **From Prajāpati's breath** - the **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa** has Prajāpati create Surabhi from his own breath (*prāṇa*). [early text] *(Source: Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa.)*
- **As Kaśyapa's daughter by Krodhavaśā** - the **Rāmāyaṇa** and Vaṃśa-lists make Surabhi a daughter of Dakṣa (via his daughter **Krodhavaśā**) and wife of **Kaśyapa**, mother of the cattle-line through her own daughters. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa; Purāṇic genealogy.)* — see [Lineages / Vaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/).
  - *Variant (Purāṇic):* the Viṣṇu and Bhāgavata Purāṇas call Surabhi a daughter of Dakṣa and wife of Kaśyapa, mother of cows **and buffaloes**. *(Source: Viṣṇu, Bhāgavata Purāṇas.)*

## Deities Residing in Her Body (the cow as cosmos)

The sacred cow is read as a living map of the universe — every limb is a deity or domain, the theological ground for cow-veneration (*go-pūjā*) and the gift of the cow (*go-dāna*).

- **Horns** - the **Trimūrti**: Brahmā at the tip, Viṣṇu in the middle, Śiva at the base. *(Source: Purāṇic / devotional tradition.)*
- **Four legs** - the four **Vedas** (Ṛg, Yajur, Sāma, Atharva); elsewhere the legs are the **Himālayas** upholding the cosmos. *(Source: Purāṇic tradition.)*
- **Eyes** - the **Sun (Sūrya)** and **Moon (Candra)** gods. *(Source: devotional tradition.)*
- **Shoulders** - **Agni** (fire) and **Vāyu** (wind). *(Source: devotional tradition.)*
- **The whole form** - all the **devas, ṛṣis, tīrthas and the goddess Lakṣmī/Devī** are said to dwell within her; the dung and urine (*pañcagavya*, with milk, curd, ghee) are purifying. As an aspect of **Devī** she is the fertile Mother Earth (**Pṛthivī / Bhūmi**), and the earth itself is figured as a cow milked by gods, sages and men for their respective sustenance. *(Source: Purāṇic; Bhāgavata cow-as-earth motif; cf. Pṛthu milking the Earth-cow.)*

## Abodes (where she dwells)

- **Goloka** - the cow-heaven **above the three worlds**. In the **Anuśāsana Parva**, Surabhi performs austerities to Brahmā at Mount Kailāsa for ten thousand years; pleased, Brahmā confers goddesshood on her, decrees that all beings shall worship her, and grants her **Goloka**. *(Source: MBh Anuśāsana Parva.)* In Vaiṣṇava (Gauḍīya) theology Goloka becomes Kṛṣṇa's supreme cow-realm. *(Source: Brahma-saṃhitā; Bhāgavata.)*
- **Rasātala / Pātāla** - she also dwells in the netherworld, in **Varuṇa's oceanic city**; her ever-flowing sweet milk is said to form **Kṣīroda / Kṣīra-sāgara**, the cosmic ocean of milk. *(Source: MBh Udyoga Parva.)*
- **With the sages** - she resides in the hermitages of **Vasiṣṭha** and **Jamadagni**, serving their ritual needs and defending them — the trigger for the two great cow-wars below. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa; MBh.)*

## Her Daughters & the Cattle Lines (variants)

- **The cattle through Surabhi's daughters** - in the Rāmāyaṇa genealogy Surabhi's daughters are **Rohiṇī** (mother of all cattle) and **Gandharvī** (mother of all horses). *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa.)*
  - *Variant:* other lists give Surabhi daughters **Vimalā / Anala / Viśvarūpā**; the Krodhavaśa-line in the Rāmāyaṇa branches into many animal-mothers. [disputed] *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa; Purāṇic Vaṃśa.)*
- **The four quarter-cows (dik-dhenu)** - dwelling in **Rasātala**, four daughters guard the directions and uphold the heavens: **Saurabhī (east), Haṃsikā (south), Subhadrā (west), Sarvakāmadughā / Dhenu (north)**. *(Source: MBh Udyoga Parva.)*
- **The Kapilā cows** - golden cows born of Surabhi (from Dakṣa's belch), called the **mothers of the world**; *Kapilā* (the tawny/brown cow) is held the holiest gift, and gift of a Kapilā ferries the soul across the *Vaitaraṇī* river. *(Source: MBh Anuśāsana Parva; dāna-dharma literature.)*
- **Nandinī** - Vasiṣṭha's wish-cow, the most storied daughter (see below). *(Source: MBh Ādi Parva; Rāmāyaṇa.)*

## Nandinī, the Vasus & Bhīṣma (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva)

- **Nandinī** - daughter of Surabhi and the wish-fulfilling cow (*homa-dhenu*) of sage **Vasiṣṭha**, kept in his Himālayan hermitage; she grants every desire and supplies his rites. *(Source: MBh Ādi Parva, Sambhava Parva.)*
- **The theft by the Vasus** - the **eight Vasus**, roaming with their wives near Vasiṣṭha's āśrama, are urged by the wife of the Vasu **Dyaus (Prabhāsa)** to seize Nandinī (and her calf) for the cow's wish-granting power; the eight steal her. *(Source: MBh Ādi Parva.)*
- **Vasiṣṭha's curse** - the enraged sage curses all eight Vasus to be **born as mortals**. On their pleading he relents: seven (mere accomplices) will be freed soon after birth, but **Dyaus/Prabhāsa**, the instigator, must endure a **long mortal life**. *(Source: MBh Ādi Parva.)*
- **The Bhīṣma link** - to discharge the curse, **Gaṅgā** bears the eight Vasus as her sons by King **Śāntanu**, drowning the first seven at birth to release them; the eighth, **Dyaus**, survives as **Devavrata — Bhīṣma**, condemned to a long life and the terrible vow that gives him his name. Thus Nandinī's theft is the karmic root of the entire Bhīṣma narrative. *(Source: MBh Ādi Parva.)* — see [Lineages / Vaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/).
  - *Variant (which cow):* some retellings name the stolen cow **Surabhi** rather than Nandinī, conflating mother and daughter. [disputed] *(Source: variant MBh recensions / folk.)*

## Śabalā / Nandinī & the Viśvāmitra–Vasiṣṭha War (Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa)

- **The feast** - King **Viśvāmitra** (then still a *kṣatriya*), visiting Vasiṣṭha's hermitage with his whole army, is lavishly feasted when the sage's cow **Śabalā** (Kāmadhenu) produces, on command, a banquet for the entire host. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa, ~ch. 51–55.)* — see [Viśvāmitra & Vasiṣṭha](../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md).
- **The refusal** - dazzled, Viśvāmitra demands the cow, offering vast wealth and a hundred thousand cattle; Vasiṣṭha refuses, declaring Śabalā inseparable from his spiritual life. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa.)*
- **The cow's army** - when Viśvāmitra tries to drag Śabalā off by force, she — empowered by Vasiṣṭha's tapas — generates whole **armies from her body** (variously from her dung, udder, foam, hump): **Pahlavas, Śakas, Yavanas, Kāmbojas, Mlecchas, Hāritas, Kirātas, Barbaras**, who rout the king's forces. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa, ch. 54–55.)*
- **The consequence** - humiliated that *brahma-tejas* (spiritual power) crushed his *kṣatra-bala* (martial power), Viśvāmitra renounces his throne and undertakes the austerities that make him a **Brahmarṣi** — the cow-quarrel is the seed of his entire transformation. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa.)*
  - *Variant (Vedic backdrop):* the rivalry of Vasiṣṭha and Viśvāmitra is rooted in the **Ṛgveda** (Maṇḍalas 3 & 7) and the **Battle of the Ten Kings (Dāśarājña)**, where Sudās favors Vasiṣṭha as *purohita* over Viśvāmitra. [scholarly] *(Source: Ṛgveda 7; Indological scholarship.)*

## The Cow of Jamadagni & Kārtavīrya-Arjuna / Paraśurāma

- **Jamadagni's Kāmadhenu/Surabhi** - sage **Jamadagni** (father of **Paraśurāma**, the 6th avatāra of Viṣṇu) keeps a wish-cow who feeds any number of guests. *(Source: MBh Vana/Śānti Parva; Bhāgavata Purāṇa.)*
- **The covetous king** - the thousand-armed Haihaya emperor **Kārtavīrya-Arjuna (Sahasrabāhu / Sahasrārjuna)**, feasted by the cow, demands her; Jamadagni refuses (she is needed for his rites). The king's men seize the cow (or her **calf**) by force and wreck the hermitage. *(Source: MBh; Bhāgavata, Padma, Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇas.)*
  - *Variant (earliest, MBh):* Kārtavīrya destroys Jamadagni's āśrama and carries off **the calf of the Kāmadhenu**; Paraśurāma slays the king to recover it; the king's sons then murder Jamadagni in revenge. *(Source: MBh, earliest stratum.)*
- **Paraśurāma's vengeance** - Paraśurāma cuts off Kārtavīrya's **thousand arms** and beheads him; after the king's sons kill the unarmed Jamadagni (whom divine grace later revives), Paraśurāma vows to rid the earth of *kṣatriyas* and exterminates the warrior race **twenty-one times**, filling the lakes of **Samanta-pañcaka (Kurukṣetra)** with their blood. *(Source: MBh Vana Parva; Bhāgavata Purāṇa.)* — see [Lineages / Vaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/).
- **Twin moral** - both this and the Viśvāmitra episode encode the same lesson: a king who covets the cow of a sage (i.e. who violates *brahma-dharma* for greed) brings ruin on his line. The cow is the flashpoint of the *kṣatra*-vs-*brāhmaṇa* tension running through the epics. *(Source: comparative reading of MBh & Rāmāyaṇa.)*

## Symbolism (cow as earth, Vedas, abundance)

- **Cow = Earth (Pṛthivī/Bhūmi)** - the earth is repeatedly figured as a cow "milked" of her wealth; in the **Pṛthu** myth the gods, sages, ancestors, nāgas and men each milk the Earth-cow for grain, *soma*, etc. *(Source: Bhāgavata, Viṣṇu Purāṇa.)*
- **Cow = Vedas / sacrifice** - her milk and ghee are the substance of *yajña*; her four legs the four Vedas; she is the *yajña-dhenu*, the very body of ritual. *(Source: Brāhmaṇa & Purāṇic tradition.)*
- **Cow = abundance & ahiṃsā** - emblem of *non-erotic fertility*, motherly sustenance, prosperity and wealth (*go-dhana*, "cattle = wealth"); her inviolability anchors the ethic of **cow-protection (go-rakṣā)** and the sin of *go-hatyā*. *(Source: Dharmaśāstra; devotional tradition.)*
- **Cow = soteriology** - gift of a cow (esp. a Kapilā) at death ferries the soul across the **Vaitaraṇī**; *pañcagavya* purifies. *(Source: Garuḍa Purāṇa; dāna literature.)* — see also [Sacred Flora & Fauna](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/sacred-flora-and-fauna.md).

## Two Clocks (scripture vs. scholarship)

- **Scriptural:** Surabhi/Kāmadhenu is eternal, churned at the dawn of this *kalpa* from the Ocean of Milk and present through the *yugas* in the hermitages of the sages (Vasiṣṭha, Jamadagni) and in Goloka/Pātāla. *(Source: MBh, Rāmāyaṇa, Purāṇas.)*
- **Academic [scholarly]:** the cow's sanctity reflects the centrality of cattle wealth in **Vedic pastoral society**; the cow-quarrel narratives crystallize the historical **brāhmaṇa–kṣatriya** contest (Ṛgveda 7, Dāśarājña). The militant "army-from-the-cow" motif and the hybrid winged Kāmadhenu icon are **later** literary/iconographic developments. *(Source: Indological scholarship.)*
