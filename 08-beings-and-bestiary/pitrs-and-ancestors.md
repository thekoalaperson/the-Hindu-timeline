# The Pitṛs — Ancestral Orders & Pitṛ-loka

Breadcrumb: [Beings & Bestiary](./README.md) · [Master Index](../README.md) · See also [Narakas & Afterlife](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/narakas-and-afterlife.md) · [Varṇa, Āśrama & Saṃskāras](../05-concepts-and-dharma/varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md)

The **Pitṛs** ("Fathers," देवाः पितरः *devāḥ pitaraḥ*) are the divine ancestral orders — a distinct cosmic class older than the gods, ranked beside devas, asuras, ṛṣis, and yakṣas. They are simultaneously the *progenitors* of devas, asuras, and humans, and the *recipients* of the funerary cult (śrāddha, tarpaṇa) by which deceased human ancestors (*manuṣya-pitaraḥ*) are merged into their company. Two clocks operate here: scriptural cosmology treats the Pitṛs as primordial demiurgic beings; academic study reads the doctrine as a Vedic-into-Purāṇic systematization of an Indo-European ancestor cult fused with lunar and *pañcāgni* (five-fire) eschatology.

---

## The Sevenfold Pitṛ — Incorporeal & Corporeal Orders

The Purāṇas (Vāyu and Brahmāṇḍa give the fullest, near-identical accounts; followed by Viṣṇu, Mārkaṇḍeya, Matsya, Harivaṃśa, Padma) enumerate **seven classes of divine Pitṛs**: **three incorporeal (अमूर्ति *amūrti* / arūpa)** and **four corporeal (समूर्ति *samūrti* / sarūpa)**. Each is the ancestral order joined by a particular category of beings or by a specific *varṇa*. [scholarly]

### Incorporeal (arūpa) — 3 orders
- **Vairājas (Virajas)** - the incorporeal Pitṛs born of Virajā/Brahmā; foremost order, dwelling in formless light. The Pitṛs of the great ascetics / yogis (and in some accounts of householders of high merit). Their mind-born daughter **Menā** (Mainā) wed Mount Himavat, making them maternal grandsires of Pārvatī and Gaṅgā (Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa, Viṣṇu Purāṇa).
- **Agniṣvāttas** ("tasted by fire" / "cooked by Agni") - the **Pitṛs of the gods (devas)**; those who in past life kindled no sacrificial fires yet attained the realm (one folk-etymology) versus those purified through fire. Their daughter **Acchodā** became the lake/river Acchodā (Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa, Mārkaṇḍeya). In Theosophical reuse these become the "fire-givers" of mind — a [late]/non-traditional gloss, flagged here to avoid conflation.
- **Barhiṣads (Barhiṣadas)** ("seated on barhis / sacred grass") - the **Pitṛs of the asuras (demons)**; those who, in former lives, did maintain the sacred grass and ritual fires. Among the householder-Pitṛs in some lists (Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa, Mārkaṇḍeya).

### Corporeal (sarūpa) — 4 orders, one per varṇa
- **Somapas (Somasads / Saumyas)** - the **Pitṛs of the Brāhmaṇas**; soma-drinkers, sprung from Bhṛgu's line (or from the *kratu*/sacrifice). Daughters/lineage tie them to the priestly clans (Manusmṛti 3 implies the Brāhmaṇa-first ordering; Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa).
- **Haviṣmants (Haviṣmān / Haviṣmatas)** - the **Pitṛs of the Kṣatriyas**; "possessed of oblation (havis)," from Aṅgiras' line. Their daughter **Yaśodā** in some accounts.
- **Ājyapas** ("drinkers of clarified butter, ājya") - the **Pitṛs of the Vaiśyas**; from Pulastya's (or Kardama's) line. Daughter **Virajā/Nandā** in variant lists.
- **Sukālins (Sukālas)**, also called **Manasas** - the **Pitṛs of the Śūdras**; from Vasiṣṭha's (or Marīci's) line. The *Manasa* epithet ("mind-born") attaches to this order in Vāyu/Brahmāṇḍa.

- *Variant (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa, Pitṛ-kalpa):* a wholly **different sevenfold naming** of the Pitṛs as **Vara, Vareṇya, Varada, Puṣṭida, Tuṣṭida, Viśvapātṛ (Viśvapā), Dhātṛ** — a benediction-themed list rather than the *amūrti/samūrti* scheme; catalog both, do not harmonize. [scholarly]
- *Variant (varṇa-assignment):* the **Somapa→Brāhmaṇa, Haviṣmat→Kṣatriya, Ājyapa→Vaiśya, Sukālin→Śūdra** mapping is the standard Vāyu/Brahmāṇḍa/Mārkaṇḍeya scheme; the three incorporeal orders are then assigned **Vairāja→ascetics, Agniṣvātta→devas, Barhiṣad→asuras** (and in some readings also to Brāhmaṇa householders), so a few Brāhmaṇa-Pitṛ overlaps exist across lists. [disputed]
- *Variant (number & origin):* some texts speak of the Pitṛs as **born of Brahmā** at creation; others as **sons of the seven (or other) Ṛṣis** or of the Prajāpatis; Manu (Manusmṛti 3.194-201) traces classes of Pitṛs from Marīci-class sages and from Manu Svāyambhuva's sons. Open-ended roster — names and parentages vary by text. [disputed]

## Pitṛs as Progenitors — Forefathers of Devas, Asuras & Humans

- **Primordial demiurges** - the Pitṛs are not merely "the dead": in cosmogony they precede and beget the three great classes — **devas (via Agniṣvātta), asuras (via Barhiṣad), and humankind/varṇas (via the four corporeal orders)** — so every order of being has a Pitṛ-source (Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa).
- **Mind-born daughters (mānasī kanyā)** - each Pitṛ-class generated a daughter who seeds further lineages: **Menā** (→ Himavat → Pārvatī/Gaṅgā), **Acchodā** (→ lake/river), **Narmadā** (from the Somapas → the sacred Narmadā river), among others — linking the Pitṛs into the river- and mountain-genealogies (Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa, Viṣṇu Purāṇa). See [Ṛṣi Lineages & Gotras](./rishi-lineages-and-gotras.md) and [The Prajāpatis](./the-prajapatis.md).
- **Debt to the ancestors (pitṛ-ṛṇa)** - every twice-born is born owing three debts; the *pitṛ-ṛṇa* (debt to ancestors) is discharged by begetting a son and by śrāddha — the structural reason the Pitṛs are both ancestors and a cult (Taittirīya Saṃhitā 6.3.10; Manusmṛti, Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa). Cross-link [Varṇa, Āśrama & Saṃskāras](../05-concepts-and-dharma/varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md).

## Pitṛ-loka — Geography, Yama & the Moon

- **Pitṛ-loka in Bhuvarloka** - the world of the Fathers sits in the **Bhuvarloka** (the mid-region/antarikṣa between Bhūr and Svar), the realm "between heaven and earth" where the souls of the three preceding generations dwell (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 2; Purāṇic loka-scheme). It is the destination of "the most devout of Brāhmaṇas" in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa, and is governed in its judicial aspect by **Yama**, lord of the dead, who conducts the dying from earth to Pitṛ-loka.
- **Chandraloka / the Moon as gateway** - the Pitṛs are pre-eminently **lunar**: the dark fortnight is theirs, the Moon (Soma/Candra) is their food and their world. The **southern path** of the sun and the moonlit road lead the soul to the lunar sphere (**Chandraloka**), identified with or adjacent to Pitṛ-loka; from there merit-exhausted souls return to rebirth (Chāndogya Upaniṣad 5.10; Bhagavad Gītā 8.25). The waxing/waning of the moon was read as the Pitṛs drinking and being replenished by Soma.
- **Yama's double role** - Yama is first of mortals to die and so **king of the Pitṛs / Pitṛpati**, presiding over Pitṛ-loka, while in his retributive aspect he administers the **Narakas**; the two functions are kept distinct from the Pitṛ-paradise. See [Narakas & Afterlife](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/narakas-and-afterlife.md) and [Dikpālas & Lokapālas](./dikpalas-and-lokapalas.md) (Yama as southern guardian).
- *Variant (loka-identity):* early texts disagree on whether **Pitṛ-loka and Deva-loka are one or two**: the Ṛgveda/some Brāhmaṇas treat them as nearly indistinct; the Atharvaveda and later Brāhmaṇas separate them and rank Deva-loka higher (so liberation, not the Pitṛ-paradise, becomes the goal). [scholarly]

## The Subtle-Body Cosmology — Hunger, Thirst & Sustenance by Śrāddha/Tarpaṇa

- **Hunger and thirst of the dead** - the departed in the ancestral world subsist in a subtle/astral body (sūkṣma-śarīra) that experiences **hunger and thirst**; un-fed Pitṛs suffer. The **Skanda Purāṇa** warns that failing the *amāvasyā* (new-moon) offerings leaves one's Pitṛs starving. The **Garuḍa Purāṇa, Preta-khaṇḍa** is the locus classicus for this preta→pitṛ transition, detailing post-death rites, the building-up of the new body by *piṇḍa*, and the soul's year-long journey to Yama.
- **Piṇḍa-dāna (śrāddha)** - offerings of cooked **rice-balls (piṇḍa)** on death-anniversary (tithi) and prescribed days literally nourish and constitute the preta's body, advancing it from ghost (preta) to ancestor (pitṛ). Manusmṛti 3 governs the ritual frame (invited Brāhmaṇas, the Viśvedevas, prohibitions). [scholarly]
- **Tarpaṇa** - daily water-libations with **tila (black sesame), kuśa/darbha grass, and mantra**, poured through the *pitṛ-tīrtha* (the part of the hand between thumb and index finger), satisfy (*tarp-*, "to satiate") the Pitṛs (Garuḍa Purāṇa; Dharmaśāstra).
- **Brāhmaṇas as living Pitṛs** - in śrāddha the fed Brāhmaṇa **stands in for / channels** the ancestors; Manusmṛti 3 declares the Pitṛs and Viśvedevas present in the rite, so feeding the worthy Brāhmaṇa feeds the Fathers. [late text]
- **Lunar descent** - the **Garuḍa Purāṇa (Preta-khaṇḍa)** holds that the Pitṛs descend from Pitṛ-loka during the **kṛṣṇa-pakṣa** (dark fortnight) to receive piṇḍa and tarpaṇa from descendants — the cosmological reason the dark fortnight and new moon are the ancestral times.

## The Three Immediate Generations vs. the Divine Pitṛs

- **The triad of named ancestors** - the household śrāddha addresses **three immediate paternal generations**: **pitā (father), pitāmaha (grandfather), prapitāmaha (great-grandfather)** — plus, in fuller rites, the matrilineal triad (maternal grandfather etc.). These are the **manuṣya-pitaraḥ** (human Fathers), distinct from the cosmic divine orders (Manusmṛti 3.221-224; Garuḍa Purāṇa).
- **Promotion up the ladder** - on the death of a person, the great-grandfather (prapitāmaha) is held to "graduate" out of the individually-named triad and **merge into the collective divine Pitṛs (the sevenfold orders)**; the dead father enters as new pitā. Thus the human dead are continually absorbed into the eternal Pitṛ-classes — the mechanism uniting the two senses of "Pitṛ." [scholarly]
- **Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa** - the rite (usually at the close of the first year) by which the new preta is **joined (sa-piṇḍī-karaṇa) to the three forefathers**, completing the transition from isolated ghost to enrolled ancestor (Garuḍa Purāṇa; Dharmaśāstra).
- **Gotra and the Pitṛs** - offerings are made within one's **gotra** lineage; the divine Pitṛ-classes correspond to the Ṛṣi-gotra streams (Somapa↔Bhṛgu, Haviṣmat↔Aṅgiras, etc.). See [Ṛṣi Lineages & Gotras](./rishi-lineages-and-gotras.md).

## Pitṛ-yāna vs. Deva-yāna — The Two After-Death Paths

The **two roads of the dead** (Chāndogya Upaniṣad 5.3-10's *pañcāgni-vidya*; Bṛhadāraṇyaka 6.2; Kauṣītaki 1; Bhagavad Gītā 8.23-27):

- **Deva-yāna (devayāna) — "path of the gods"** - the **sunlit, northern (uttarāyaṇa), bright-fortnight, fire/flame** road. Taken by knowers of Brahman and forest-dwelling ascetics of knowledge and faith; it leads through arcis (flame) → day → bright fortnight → northern course → sun → lightning → Brahma-loka, and **there is no return** (*akṛta-punarāvṛtti*) — it culminates in liberation (Chāndogya 5.10.1-2; Gītā 8.24).
- **Pitṛ-yāna (pitṛyāṇa) — "path of the Fathers"** - the **moonlit, southern (dakṣiṇāyana), dark-fortnight, smoke** road. Taken by performers of sacrifice, charity, and obligatory householder duty; it runs through smoke → night → dark fortnight → southern course → the **Moon (Pitṛ-loka/Chandraloka)**. There the soul enjoys the fruits of karma, then — merit spent — **returns to earth** for rebirth via rain → plants → food → seed (Chāndogya 5.10.3-6; Gītā 8.25-26).
- **The third, lowest course** - those of neither path become "small creatures" in a cycle of recurrent birth-and-death (Chāndogya 5.10.8) — outside both roads.
- *Variant (Gītā framing):* Bhagavad Gītā 8.27 treats the two paths as **śukla (bright) and kṛṣṇa (dark) gatī**; the yogin who knows them is "deluded by neither," so the contrast becomes a **soteriological** (mokṣa vs. saṃsāra) rather than merely topographical teaching. [scholarly]

## Pitṛ-pakṣa / Mahālaya — The Ancestral Fortnight

- **Pitṛ-pakṣa** - the **dark fortnight (kṛṣṇa-pakṣa) of the month of Bhādrapada/Āśvina** (Sep-Oct), 16 lunar days dedicated to śrāddha for all ancestors; the recommended season for venerating the Pitṛs (per the Pitṛ-pakṣa tradition; Garuḍa Purāṇa rationale of descent in the dark fortnight).
- **Mahālaya Amāvāsyā (Sarva-pitṛ Amāvāsyā)** - the **new-moon that closes Pitṛ-pakṣa**: the supreme day of "all-ancestors" śrāddha, on which offerings reach even ancestors whose death-tithi is unknown or who left no descendant. In the East, **Mahālaya** also inaugurates the Durgā-Pūjā season (the Goddess's invocation). [folk]/regional
- **Tithi-śrāddha within the fortnight** - each lunar day of the fortnight corresponds to the death-tithi of an ancestor; the Mahābhārata praises performing śrāddha on each successive day for distinct merits (beautiful spouse, worthy children, etc.).
- **Gayā-śrāddha** - pilgrimage śrāddha at **Gayā** (the *Phalgu*/Viṣṇupada) is held the supreme means of liberating ancestors, especially during Pitṛ-pakṣa (Garuḍa, Vāyu Purāṇa; pilgrimage tradition). See [Sacred Geography](../06-sacred-geography/README.md).
