# Yakṣas, Guhyakas & Kubera's Court

> Part of [08 · Beings & Bestiary](README.md) → the **earth-bound semi-divine roster**: nature-spirits, treasure-guardians, and the retinue of the wealth-god.
> Cosmic address: residents of *Alakā* (on/near [Meru–Kailāsa](../06-sacred-geography/meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md)), of forests, lakes, trees and buried hoards across the *Bhūloka*.
> Their lord Kubera is a [Dikpāla / Lokapāla](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) of the North; the most famous yakṣa-myth is the [Yakṣa Praśna](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md).

The **yakṣas** (यक्ष; fem. **yakṣiṇī** / **yakṣī**) are a broad class of **nature-spirits and treasure-custodians** — associated with water, trees, forests, wilderness, fertility, and above all the **hidden wealth** buried in the earth and in the roots of trees. They are *not* devas and *not* asuras but a distinct semi-divine order, ruled by **Kubera**, treasurer of the gods. Literature describes a **fundamental split**: **benevolent** yakṣas (auspicious tutelaries, guardians of treasure, attendants of Kubera) versus **malevolent** yakṣas — a darker, *bhūta*-like (ghost) type that haunts the wilds and devours travellers, shading into the rākṣasa. The **guhyakas** ("the hidden/secret ones," *guhya* = "secret") are an overlapping designation, often used as a synonym or near-synonym for the yakṣas of Kubera's mountain court; Kubera himself bears the title *Guhyādhipa* ("lord of the hidden"). This node catalogs the named roster, Kubera's court, the two moral classes, and all major list/count variants. The total yakṣa population is an **open-ended class** (texts speak of hosts/legions); only a finite set of **named** figures recurs. *(Source: Wikipedia "Yaksha"; Britannica "Yaksha"; HinduWebsite.)*

## Kubera — Lord of Wealth & King of the Yakṣas

- **Kubera** (कुबेर; also **Kuvera, Kuber**) - The **god of wealth** and **god-king of the yakṣas/guhyakas**; treasurer (*dhanādhyakṣa*) of the devas. **Dikpāla of the North** and a **Lokapāla** (world-protector). Iconographically a dwarfish, pot-bellied figure, often one-eyed, holding a mace and money-pot, attended by personified treasures. *(Source: Wikipedia "Kubera"; Britannica.)*
  - *Epithets:* **Vaiśravaṇa** ("son of Viśravas / of Fame" — patronymic), **Ailavila / Ailabila** (matronymic), **Dhanādhipati / Dhanada** ("lord-giver of wealth"), **Yakṣarāja** ("king of yakṣas"), **Guhyādhipa / Guhyakeśvara** ("lord of the hidden ones / of the guhyakas"), **Rājarāja** ("king of kings"), **Naravāhana** ("borne by men"). *(Source: Wikipedia "Kubera".)*
- **Parentage** - Son of the sage **Viśravas** (son of Pulastya, one of the [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md)) and **Ilavidā / Ilibilā** (daughter of sage Bharadvāja) — hence Kubera is **grandson of Prajāpati Pulastya**. *(Source: Purāṇas; Wikipedia "Kubera".)*
  - *Half-brother of Rāvaṇa:* through Viśravas's second wife, the rākṣasī **Kaikasī**, Kubera's half-siblings are **Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa, Vibhīṣaṇa** and **Śūrpaṇakhā** — so the wealth-god and the demon-king of Laṅkā are kin. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa Uttara Kāṇḍa.)* — cross-link [Lineages](../03-lineages-vamsha/).
- **Family** - Wife **Bhadrā / Ṛddhi** ("Prosperity," in some texts a daughter of the asura Mura). Sons **Nalakūbara** and **Maṇigrīva** (the pair cursed by Nārada to become trees, liberated by infant Kṛṣṇa — the *yamalārjuna* episode); the title **Mayurāja** ("king of the man-like beasts/kimpuruṣas") is also given as a son/aspect. Daughter **Mīnākṣī** ("fish-eyed") in some lists. *(Source: Wikipedia "Kubera"; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.10 for Nalakūbara/Maṇigrīva.)*
  - *Note:* Nalakūbara is consort of the apsarā **Rambhā**; his curse on Rāvaṇa (head to burst if he forces a woman) later protects Sītā — see [Apsarās](apsaras.md).

### Alakā — the City of Treasure

- **Alakā / Alakāpurī** ("curl-city") - Kubera's gold-and-jewel capital in the **Himālayas, on or near Mount Kailāsa**, to which he removed after **Rāvaṇa drove him from Laṅkā** and seized his flying chariot. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa; Wikipedia "Kubera".)*
  - *Alternate city-names (variants):* **Prabhā** ("splendour"), **Vasudhārā** ("bejeweled"), **Vasusthalī** ("abode of treasures"). *(Source: Wikipedia "Kubera".)*
  - **Caitraratha** - Kubera's pleasure-grove/garden of jewel-leaved trees, tended for his court. *(Source: Mahābhārata; Purāṇas.)*
  - *Literary fame:* Alakā is the destination-city of Kālidāsa's **Meghadūta**, where the exiled yakṣa's wife pines. [late text]
- **Pుṣpaka Vimāna** - Kubera's self-moving aerial chariot (a gift of Brahmā), famously **stolen by Rāvaṇa** and later used by Rāma to return to Ayodhyā. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa.)*
- **Mount** - **Naravāhana** ("borne by men/spirits"); as Lokapāla he is also given the elephant **Sārvabhauma** of the northern quarter. *(Source: Wikipedia "Kubera".)*

### The Nidhis — Personified Treasures

- **Nidhis** - Kubera's hoards, often **personified** as attendants standing beside him. The two most-named are **Padma** ("lotus") and **Śaṅkha** ("conch"). *(Source: Wikipedia "Kubera"; Purāṇic iconography.)*
  - *Variant (the Nava-nidhi / nine treasures):* a fuller esoteric list of **nine nidhis** attributed to Kubera — **Padma, Mahāpadma, Śaṅkha, Makara, Kacchapa, Mukunda, Kunda, Nīla, Kharva** (membership and spelling vary by source). [disputed] *(Source: late Purāṇic / Tantric lists; cf. Amarakośa traditions.)*

## Kubera's Court & War-Captains (the Named Guhyakas)

Kubera's court is thronged by **yakṣas, guhyakas, kiṃpuruṣas, vidyādharas, gandharvas, kinnaras** and certain **rākṣasas**; Śiva and Pārvatī (his neighbours on Kailāsa) frequent it. *(Source: Wikipedia "Kubera".)*

- **Maṇibhadra** (मणिभद्र) - **Chief attendant of Kubera and general (senāpati) of the yakṣa army**; a popular cult-figure in Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism, invoked for **protection of travellers and merchants** and success in commerce. In the Rāmāyaṇa he leads yakṣa forces; a treasure-guardian par excellence. *(Source: Wikipedia "List of Yakshas"; Mahābhārata; Rāmāyaṇa.)*
- **Pūrṇabhadra** (पूर्णभद्र) - A great yakṣa lord, in many sources the **brother of Maṇibhadra**; together Maṇibhadra and Pūrṇabhadra are named as the **two Indras (overlords) of the yakṣa class**, both attendants of Kubera. Father of **Harikeśa** (below). *(Source: wisdomlib "Pūrṇabhadra"; Jain Bhagavatī-sūtra; Matsya Purāṇa.)*
  - *Variant (Matsya Purāṇa):* Harikeśa's father is named **Ratnabhadra**, a yakṣa dwelling on **Mount Gandhamādana** — taken as an alias of Pūrṇabhadra. [disputed] *(Source: Matsya Purāṇa 18.)*
- **Harikeśa** (हरिकेश; "tawny-haired") - A yakṣa **devotee of Śiva**. Because he worshipped Śiva while his father Pūrṇabhadra was a devotee of Kubera, he was **banished from home**; Śiva then **adopted him as an attendant (gaṇa)**, set him as a guardian of **Kāśī (Vārāṇasī)** — in some tellings posted at **Maṇikarṇikā** — granting him lordship over disease-spirits and a share of wealth-protection. *(Source: Matsya Purāṇa 18; Śiva Purāṇa; wisdomlib.)*
- **Pañcika / Pāñcika** - Commander-in-chief of Vaiśravaṇa's (Kubera's) army in the wider yakṣa tradition; consort of the yakṣiṇī **Hārītī** (chiefly Buddhist, but pan-Indian). *(Source: Wikipedia "List of Yakshas".)* [scholarly]
- **Padma & Śaṅkha** - The two personified nidhi-treasures, attending in human form (see above). *(Source: Purāṇic iconography.)*
- *Note:* Kubera's retinue overlaps the **kiṃpuruṣas** (man-beasts) and **vidyādharas** (sky-faring sorcerer-spirits), catalogued among the semi-divine classes — see [Beings & Bestiary README](README.md).

## The Yakṣa Praśna — the Riddle at the Lake

- **The yakṣa of the lake (Dharma-Baka)** - The single most celebrated yakṣa episode. In the **Vana Parva** (Āraṇya Parva), at the close of the Pāṇḍavas' twelve-year exile, a yakṣa **guards a lake** and forbids drinking until his questions are answered. One by one the four younger Pāṇḍavas ignore him, drink, and **fall dead**. **Yudhiṣṭhira** alone answers — a famous catechism of **dharma, conduct, cosmology and wisdom** (the texts count well over a hundred / ~120 question-pairs). Satisfied, the yakṣa **restores his brothers to life** and reveals himself to be **Yama-Dharma**, Yudhiṣṭhira's divine father, testing him. Also called the **Dharma-Baka Upākhyāna** ("Legend of the Virtuous Crane"), since the deity first appears as a **crane (baka)**. *(Source: Mahābhārata, Vana Parva [Āraṇyaka/Yakṣa Praśna sub-parva]; Wikipedia "Yaksha Prashna".)* — full episode: [Vana Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md).
  - *Famous answer:* asked "What is the greatest wonder?" Yudhiṣṭhira replies that **day after day creatures die, yet the living believe themselves immortal** — there is no greater wonder.
  - *Variant on identity:* though "a yakṣa" frames the encounter, the revealed being is **Yama / Dharma** himself, not a true member of Kubera's class — i.e. a deity *in the form of* a yakṣa. [disputed]

## Yakṣas in the Epics & Other Named Figures

- **Sthūṇa / Sthūṇākarṇa** - The yakṣa who, out of compassion, **exchanged his sex** with **Śikhaṇḍin(ī)** so she could become male and fulfil her vengeance against Bhīṣma; **Kubera, finding his attendant female, cursed the exchange to be permanent** (until Sthūṇa's death). Pivotal to the **fall of Bhīṣma** at Kurukṣetra. *(Source: Mahābhārata, Udyoga Parva; Wikipedia "Shikhandi".)* — see [Udyoga Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/05-udyoga-parva.md).
- **Suketu** - A yakṣa **king** in the Rāmāyaṇa; through a boon his daughter **Tāḍakā (Tāṭakā)** was born, who (cursed by Agastya) became the yakṣiṇī-turned-demoness slain by Rāma. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa Bāla Kāṇḍa; Wikipedia "List of Yakshas".)*
- **Tāḍakā / Tāṭakā** - Daughter of Suketu, originally of yakṣa stock, cursed into a man-eating ogress haunting the Daṇḍaka forest; **first being killed by Rāma**. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa Bāla Kāṇḍa.)* [a yakṣiṇī corrupted to malevolence]
- **Nalakūbara** - Kubera's son (see Court, above); recurs as Rambhā's consort and curser of Rāvaṇa. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa Uttara Kāṇḍa.)*
- **The exiled yakṣa of the Meghadūta** - An unnamed guhyaka of Alakā, banished a year for neglecting duty, who sends a cloud-messenger to his wife — the literary archetype of the love-lorn yakṣa. *(Source: Kālidāsa, Meghadūta.)* [late text]
- *Note:* Beyond these, named yakṣas proliferate in Buddhist and Jain canons (e.g. the **24 yakṣa-attendants of the Jain Tīrthaṅkaras**), which are open-ended and largely outside the Hindu spine.

## The Two Moral Classes & the Yakṣiṇīs

Hindu literature splits the yakṣa-kind by **temperament**: *(Source: Wikipedia "Yaksha"; Britannica; HinduWebsite.)*

- **Benevolent yakṣas** - Auspicious nature-tutelaries, **guardians of buried treasure**, attendants of Kubera, givers of fertility and prosperity; worshipped at village shrines, often shown as gatekeepers (*dvārapālas*). Generally kindly, though **mischievous, capricious, or amorous**.
- **Malevolent yakṣas** - A darker, **bhūta-like (ghost) type** that haunts forests, mountains and lonely roads, **waylaying and devouring travellers** — barely distinguished from the rākṣasa. *(Source: Britannica; cf. Buddhist Jātaka man-eating yakṣas.)*

### Yakṣiṇīs (the female form)

- **Yakṣiṇī / Yakṣī** (यक्षिणी) - The female yakṣas. **Benign** yakṣiṇīs are worshipped as tutelaries and attend Kubera; **malign** ones are poltergeist-like spirits that **haunt, possess, and curse** in folk belief. Strongly associated in art with the **aśoka tree** — the *śālabhañjikā* / tree-goddess motif, a yakṣiṇī with foot on the trunk and hand on a flowering branch — found as gatekeepers on Buddhist and Hindu temples. *(Source: Wikipedia "Yakshini"; HinduWebsite.)*
  - **Hārītī** - A once child-devouring yakṣiṇī **reformed into a protectress of children** and fertility-goddess; consort of Pāñcika. *(Source: Wikipedia "List of Yakshas".)* [chiefly Buddhist, pan-Indian]
  - *Variant (the 36 Yakṣiṇīs):* the Tantric **Uddāmareśvara Tantra** (with the *Tantrarāja Tantra*) catalogs **thirty-six named yakṣiṇīs** with their mantras and sādhanās — a finite esoteric roster (e.g. Vicitrā, Vibhramā, Haṃsī, Bhīṣaṇī, Janarañjikā, Viśālā, Madanā, Ghaṇṭā, Kālakarṇī, Mahābhayā, Mahendrī, Śaṅkhinī, Caṇḍrī, Śmaśānā, Vaṭayakṣiṇī, Manoharā, Pramodā, Anurāgiṇī, Naṭī, Kāmeśvarī, Ratipriyā, Padminī, Naṭinī, Madanāturā, Anurāgiṇī, Subhagā, Niṭilā, Surasundarī…) — names/order vary across manuscripts. [disputed] *(Source: Uddāmareśvara Tantra; Tantrarāja Tantra.)*
  - *Folk yakṣiṇīs:* regional traditions (notably **Kerala**, e.g. the tree-dwelling, blood-seeking **yakṣi** of *Malayāḷa* folklore) preserve a distinct, often dangerous, female yakṣa figure haunting **pāla/banyan trees**. [folk] — this roster is **open-ended**.
