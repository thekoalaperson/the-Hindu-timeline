# Rākṣasas — The Night-Wanderers

> Part of [08 · Beings & Bestiary](README.md) → the **demon-race roster**: the shape-shifting, night-roaming man-eaters (*niśācara*, *naktaṃcara*) who haunt the forests, devour flesh, defile the *yajña*, and besiege the sages — catalogued **beyond** Rāvaṇa's immediate Laṅkā clan.
> Cosmic address: foes of the *Bhūloka*, kin of the **Nairṛti** (south-west) quarter; their dynastic seat is **Laṅkā** before they fall to **Rasātala / Pātāla** (see [devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md)).
> Origin from **Brahmā** (and the line of **Pulastya** of the [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md)); their epic theatres are the [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/) and the [Mahābhārata](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/).

The **rākṣasas** (राक्षस; fem. **rākṣasī**) are a class of **shape-shifting, fierce, often cannibalistic demons** — paradigmatic *enemies of dharma*: they disrupt sacrifices, eat sages and travellers, grow huge or shrink at will by *māyā*, and wander most powerfully **at night** (hence *niśācara*, "night-roamer," and the title of this node). Yet the class is **moral-fluid** — Vibhīṣaṇa is righteous, Ghaṭotkaca dies a hero for the Pāṇḍavas, Kaikasī's line is half-brahmin. The name itself is folk-etymologized two ways: from **√rakṣ "to guard"** (Brahmā's primordial *guardians* of the waters/creation) and from the cry **"rakṣāma!"** ("let us protect/devour!"). This node catalogs the roster **beyond the Laṅkā nucleus** (Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa, Indrajit, Vibhīṣaṇa, Śūrpaṇakhā — held for their own dynastic file, [Paulastya / rākṣasa vaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/paulastya-rakshasa-vamsha.md)): the **origin myth**, the **founding line** (Heti & Praheti → Sukeśa → Mālyavān/Sumālī/Mālī), the **Rāmāyaṇa forest-demons** (Tāḍakā, Mārīca, Subāhu, Khara, Dūṣaṇa, Viradha, Kabandha), the **Mahābhārata rākṣasas** (Hiḍimba, Hiḍimbā, Ghaṭotkaca, Baka, Kirmīra, Jaṭāsura, Alambuṣa), the **Nairṛtas**, and **rākṣasa-vivāha**. The total population is an **open-ended host** (texts speak of legions); only a finite set of **named** figures recurs. *(Source: Wikipedia "Rakshasa"; Britannica "Rakshasa"; Rāmāyaṇa Uttara Kāṇḍa; New World Encyclopedia.)*

## Origin — the Birth of the Race

- **The guardians of the waters (Vālmīki's own account)** - In the **Uttara Kāṇḍa**, Brahmā creates the waters and then brings forth beings **to guard them**. Tormented by hunger and thirst, they come back to him for direction, and he divides them by their own answer: those who cry *"rakṣāma!"* — **"we shall protect"** — become the **rākṣasas**; those who cry *"yakṣāma!"* — "we shall worship/sacrifice" — become the **yakṣas**. The race is thus named for **guardianship**, from **√rakṣ "to protect,"** and its enmity with dharma is a later fall, not an origin. Heti and Praheti are born at once from this creation. *(Source: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa 4.)*
  - *Variant (Purāṇic — the hunger etymology):* the widely-repeated gloss in which the cry means **"let us eat!"** and the rākṣasas are born of Brahmā's **hunger (kṣudh)** from a form composed of foulness is **Purāṇic, not Vālmīki's**; the epic's own etymology is the protective one. Both readings circulate, and the corpus keeps them apart. *(Source: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.5; Purāṇic etymological tradition.)* [disputed]
  - *Variant (Brahmā's foot):* a parallel tradition has rākṣasas issue **from Brahmā's foot** (the lowest, *tamasic* limb), matching their station at the bottom of the cosmic ladder. *(Source: Wikipedia "Rakshasa"; Britannica.)*
  - *Variant (other parentages):* texts variously descend rākṣasas from **Pulastya**, from **Khasā** (a daughter of Dakṣa, wife of Kaśyapa), or from **Nirṛti & Nirṛta** (the goddess/god of decay) — hence the synonym **Nairṛta**. [disputed] *(Source: Wikipedia "Rakshasa"; New World Encyclopedia.)*
- **Pulastya line (the dynastic account)** - The *named, royal* rākṣasas descend from the Prajāpati **Pulastya** (a [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md), mind-born son of Brahmā) through his son **Viśravas** and Viśravas's rākṣasī wife **Kaikasī** — making **Rāvaṇa** a great-grandson of Brahmā and **half-brother of the wealth-god Kubera** (see [Yakṣas → Kubera](yakshas-and-guhyakas.md)). *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa Uttara Kāṇḍa; Britannica "Ravana".)*

## The Founding Dynasty — Heti, Praheti & the Lords of Laṅkā

The **Uttara Kāṇḍa** gives a full *pre-Rāvaṇa* genealogy of the rākṣasa kings, the original masters of Laṅkā before Kubera and then Rāvaṇa. This is the **deep root** of the entire demon-clan.

- **Heti** (हेति) & **Praheti** (प्रहेति) - The **two first-born rākṣasas**, brothers sprung directly at creation. **Praheti** turned ascetic and went to the forest; **Heti** founded the dynasty by marrying. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa Uttara Kāṇḍa, sargas 4–5.)*
- **Heti's marriage** - Heti wedded **Bhayā** (भया, "Fear"), sister of **Kāla** (Time/Death); their son was **Vidyutkeśa** ("lightning-haired"). *(Source: Uttara Kāṇḍa.)*
- **Vidyutkeśa × Sālakaṭaṅkaṭā** - Vidyutkeśa married **Sālakaṭaṅkaṭā** (शालकटङ्कटा), daughter of **Sandhyā** (the goddess Twilight); she bore a son and **abandoned the infant** on a mountain. *(Source: Uttara Kāṇḍa.)*
  - *Note:* Sālakaṭaṅkaṭā becomes herself a named rākṣasī-mother invoked in later folk and Tantric lists.
- **Sukeśa** (सुकेश) - The abandoned infant, found and **blessed by Śiva and Pārvatī**. Śiva makes him at once **equal to his mother in age**, grants him **immortality**, and gives him an **aerial car that traverses space** — a *vimāna*, not a city. Sukeśa is the great patriarch from whom the Laṅkā kings spring. *(Source: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa 4.)*
  - **Pārvatī's grant is race-wide, not personal.** Where Śiva's boon falls on the foundling alone, the goddess's falls on the whole race: she decrees that **rākṣasīs shall conceive and deliver in the same instant, and the children shall at once attain the age of their mothers**. This is the mechanism behind the race's explosive growth and its instantly-grown warriors — a species-level demographic boon, and one of the epic's most consequential. *(Source: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa 4.)*
  - *Note:* the "flying city" of the retellings conflates this aerial car with **Laṅkā**, which Sukeśa's sons neither build nor are given — **Viśvakarmā** had already raised it at Indra's command (below).
- **Sukeśa × Devavatī** - Sukeśa married **Devavatī**, daughter of the gandharva **Grāmaṇī**; they had **three sons** — the celebrated triad: *(Source: Uttara Kāṇḍa.)*
  - **Mālyavān** (माल्यवान्) - Eldest; the **wise elder-statesman** of the rākṣasas; survives into the Rāmāyaṇa as **Rāvaṇa's maternal grand-uncle and counsellor**, who urges him to return Sītā. *(Source: Uttara Kāṇḍa; Yuddha Kāṇḍa 35–36.)* — dossier: [Sumālī & Mālyavān](../04-deep-dives/characters/sumali-and-malyavan.md).
  - **Sumālī** (सुमाली) - Middle brother; **grandfather of Rāvaṇa** through his daughter **Kaikasī**, whom he deliberately sends to seduce the sage Viśravas to breed a champion who could overthrow Kubera. *(Source: Uttara Kāṇḍa.)* — dossier: [Sumālī & Mālyavān](../04-deep-dives/characters/sumali-and-malyavan.md).
  - **Mālī** (माली) - Youngest; the war-hawk; **slain by Viṣṇu's discus** in the great deva–rākṣasa war. *(Source: Uttara Kāṇḍa.)*
- **Laṅkā was already built — and not for them** - The three brothers do not commission the city. **Viśvakarmā** had raised golden **Laṅkā on Mount Trikūṭa** in the southern sea **at Śakra's (Indra's) command**, and it is Viśvakarmā himself who tells them of it — "it is called Laṅkā, and was constructed by me at Śakra's command" — whereupon they take up residence in a city built for another. The client is Indra; the rākṣasas are only its **first occupants**. *(Source: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa 5; see [Sacred Geography](../06-sacred-geography/).)*
- **The war with Viṣṇu & the fall to Pātāla** - Grown arrogant from Brahmā's boons, the three harried the devas; **Viṣṇu** warred on them, **killed Mālī**, and routed the rest. **Mālyavān and Sumālī fled with the survivors to Rasātala / Pātāla**, abandoning Laṅkā — which **Kubera** then occupied, until Rāvaṇa seized it back. *(Source: Uttara Kāṇḍa.)*

### The grandchildren of Sumālī & Mālyavān (the cousin-host)

- **Sons of Sumālī** - By his wife **Ketumatī**, Sumālī's ten sons — the **uncles of Rāvaṇa**: **Prahasta, Akampana, Vikaṭa, Kālakāmukha, Dhūmrākṣa, Daṇḍa, Suparśva, Saṃhrādi, Praghasa, Bhāsakarṇa**. *(Source: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa 5.)*
  - **Prahasta** (प्रहस्त) - The most famous of these uncles; **Rāvaṇa's prime minister and commander-in-chief**, the counsellor whose embassy to Kubera argues Laṅkā back into rākṣasa hands, and the first of the king's own house to fall in the war — **Nīla** shatters his skull with a boulder. *(Source: Uttara Kāṇḍa 11; Yuddha Kāṇḍa 58.)* — dossier: [Prahasta](../04-deep-dives/characters/prahasta.md).
  - *Variant (spelling & membership):* the ten names drift across recensions and translations (Kālakāmukha/Kālikāmukha, Suparśva/Supārśva, Saṃhrādi/Saṃhrāda); the **count of ten is stable, the spellings are not**. Lists that slip **Anala, Anila, Hara** or **Sampāti** into this roster have imported Mālī's sons (below). [disputed] *(Source: Vālmīki recensions; Wikipedia "Sumali".)*
  - *Note (three names that collide with the war-books):* **Praghasa** and **Bhāsakarṇa** here, and **Virūpākṣa** among Mālyavān's seven, are also the names of three of the five *senāpatis* Hanumān kills in the Aśoka grove (*Sundara* 46.2–3), where the text gives the five no patronymic and no house, only their rank. The epic then reuses the names again on living rākṣasas — a Praghasa and a Virūpākṣa fall at *Yuddha* 43.24–25, another Virūpākṣa at *Yuddha* 96.30–32, all after the grove-battle. No passage identifies the sets, and the corpus does not merge them. [disputed] — see [Paulastya / rākṣasa vaṃśa §7](../03-lineages-vamsha/paulastya-rakshasa-vamsha.md) and [Akṣakumāra](../04-deep-dives/characters/akshakumara.md).
- **Daughters of Sumālī** - The same sarga gives Sumālī **four daughters** — **Rākā, Puṣpotkaṭā, Kaikasī and Kumbhīnasī** — and they carry more of the plot than the ten sons do. *(Source: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa 5.)*
  - **Kaikasī** (कैकसी; also **Nikaṣā**) - Sent by her father to win the sage **Viśravas**; **mother of Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa, Śūrpaṇakhā and Vibhīṣaṇa** in that birth-order — the Laṅkā nucleus, held for the dynastic file. *(Source: Uttara Kāṇḍa 9.)* — see [Paulastya / rākṣasa vaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/paulastya-rakshasa-vamsha.md).
  - **Rākā and Puṣpotkaṭā** - Sumālī's other two daughters, named in the same roster. The Purāṇic genealogies distribute Viśravas's rākṣasa children among several of these sisters (**Khara** to Rākā, Rāvaṇa and Kumbhakarṇa to Puṣpotkaṭā); **Vālmīki does not** — Uttara 9 gives all four of the Laṅkā nucleus to **Kaikasī** alone. The rosters are not to be merged. *(Source: Uttara Kāṇḍa 5, 9; Purāṇic genealogies.)* [disputed]
  - **Kumbhīnasī** - Sumālī's fourth daughter, named in this roster and nowhere else. She is **not** the Kumbhīnasī whom Madhu carries off — that one is Mālyavān's granddaughter, below; two women of the same house carry the name. *(Source: Uttara Kāṇḍa 5.)* [disputed: the two are routinely merged]
- **Sons of Mālyavān** - By his wife **Sundarī**: **Vajramuṣṭi, Virūpākṣa, Durmukha, Suptaghna, Yajñakopa, Matta and Unmatta**, with a daughter **Analā**. *(Source: Uttara Kāṇḍa 5.)*
  - **Kumbhīnasī, daughter of Analā** - Mālyavān's granddaughter, and the woman who carries the corpus's longest unbroken chain: seized out of the inner apartments by the asura **Madhu**, she becomes the mother of **Lavaṇa**, whom **Śatrughna** kills to found **Mathurā** — the Rāmāyaṇa's forward link into the Kṛṣṇa country. Rāvaṇa marches on Madhupura to avenge her and is met by Kumbhīnasī herself, pleading for her husband's life; because Analā stands as their mother's sister and the girl is therefore their sister in dharma, he **forgives the abduction** and takes Madhu as an ally instead. *(Source: Uttara Kāṇḍa 25.)* — cf. [Bharata, Śatrughna & their wives](../04-deep-dives/characters/bharata-shatrughna-and-their-wives.md).
- **Sons of Mālī** - By his wife **Vasudā**: **Anala, Anila, Hara and Sampāti** — who survive the fall of the dynasty and become **Vibhīṣaṇa's four counsellors**, the ministers who cross with him to Rāma. They are Mālī's, **not** Sumālī's, and are frequently misfiled into the uncle-list. *(Source: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa 5.)*

## The Rāmāyaṇa Forest-Demons (the Bāla & Araṇya Kāṇḍa roster)

These are the rākṣasas **Rāma battles before Laṅkā** — the obstacles of the *tapovana* and the Daṇḍaka.

- **Tāḍakā / Tāṭakā** (ताटका) - A **yakṣiṇī cursed into a rākṣasī** (by sage Agastya, after her demon-husband Sunda was killed). She and her sons ravage the **forest of the sage Viśvāmitra**; **Rāma's first kill** — at Viśvāmitra's command he slays a *female*, a famous dharmic crux. Daughter of the yakṣa **Suketu**; wife of **Sunda**; mother of Mārīca & Subāhu. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa Bāla Kāṇḍa.)*
- **Mārīca** (मारीच) - Son of Tāḍakā; defiler of Viśvāmitra's sacrifice, flung **a hundred yojanas** into the sea by Rāma's *Mānavāstra* and spared (the text's own unit; modern-mileage conversions of it are glosses, not the epic's measure). Later **coerced by Rāvaṇa** into becoming the **golden deer (Mārīca-mṛga)** that lures Rāma away so Sītā can be abducted — knowing it means his death, he chooses death-by-Rāma; slain by Rāma's arrow, dying he mimics Rāma's voice to deceive Sītā. *(Source: Bāla & Araṇya Kāṇḍa; Wikipedia "Maricha".)*
- **Subāhu** (सुबाहु) - Brother of Mārīca, son of Tāḍakā; with Mārīca **rains flesh and blood on Viśvāmitra's yajña**; **killed by Rāma's Āgneyāstra**. *(Source: Bāla Kāṇḍa.)*
- **Khara** (खर) - Warlord of the **Daṇḍaka / Janasthāna**; after Lakṣmaṇa mutilates Śūrpaṇakhā, Khara leads **14,000 rākṣasas** against Rāma and is **annihilated single-handedly**. His kinship is **maternal-cousin**, not brother: Rāvaṇa posts him to Janasthāna with Dūṣaṇa, Triśiras and Śūrpaṇakhā after her husband **Vidyujjihva** is killed, as her protector-kin. The Araṇya Kāṇḍa's loose use of "brother" for him is idiom, not pedigree. *(Source: Araṇya Kāṇḍa; Uttara Kāṇḍa 24.)*
  - **Dūṣaṇa** (दूषण) - Khara's twin/lieutenant and general of his host; falls with Khara to Rāma. *(Source: Araṇya Kāṇḍa.)*
  - **Triśiras** (त्रिशिरस्, "three-headed") - The **third commander** of the Janasthāna host with Khara and Dūṣaṇa; also slain by Rāma in the same battle. *(Source: Araṇya Kāṇḍa.)*
  - **Akampana** - Survivor of Janasthāna who flees to Laṅkā to **inform Rāvaṇa** and incite the abduction of Sītā. *(Source: Araṇya Kāṇḍa.)* [note: distinct from / sometimes conflated with Sumālī's son Akampana]
- **Viradha** (विराध) - A giant **rākṣasa of the Daṇḍaka** who seizes Sītā; impossible to kill by weapon (a boon), so Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa **bury him alive in a pit**. Dying, he reveals he was the **gandharva Tumburu**, cursed by Kubera; his death **releases him back to heaven**. *(Source: Araṇya Kāṇḍa.)* — a *redemption-by-death* motif.
- **Kabandha** (कबन्ध, "headless trunk") - A **headless** monster with a mouth in his belly and **two arms, each a yojana long**, who seizes Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa; they **sever both arms**; cremated, he rises in his celestial form and **directs Rāma to Sugrīva** — a pivotal plot-hinge. In the base text he **identifies himself as the dānava Danu, son of Śrī**, disfigured by Indra's blow and cursed by the sage Sthūlaśiras. *(Source: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya Kāṇḍa 69–73.)* — canonical home: [Kabandha](../04-deep-dives/characters/kabandha.md).
  - *Variant (former identity as a gandharva):* the identification of Kabandha with the **gandharva Viśvāvasu** belongs to the harmonized/commentarial stratum, not the base text's self-identification. [disputed] *(Source: Vālmīki recensions and commentaries.)*
- **Ayomukhī** - Rākṣasī of the Daṇḍaka who lusts after Lakṣmaṇa; he disfigures her (nose, ears, breasts), paralleling Śūrpaṇakhā. [folk-emphasis in retellings] *(Source: Araṇya Kāṇḍa.)*
- **Kālanemi** (कालनेमि) - Rākṣasa **uncle of Rāvaṇa**, sent to **delay Hanumān** fetching the Saṃjīvanī herb by posing as a hermit; killed by Hanumān. **He does not appear in Vālmīki at all** — the episode is proper to the later Rāmāyaṇas: the *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa*, Raṅganātha's Telugu, the Kṛttivāsī Bengali and Tulsīdās's *Rāmcaritmānas*, from which the popular retellings take it. *(Source: Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa; Raṅganātha, Kṛttivāsī and Tulsīdās recensions; a separate asura Kālanemi in the Bhāgavata.)* [late text] [text-overlap]

## The Mahābhārata Rākṣasas (the forest foes of the Pāṇḍavas)

In the epic of the Bhāratas the rākṣasas cluster around **Bhīma**, the Pāṇḍava strongman who is their nemesis — and, through Hiḍimbā, their **in-law**.

- **Hiḍimba** (हिडिम्ब) - **Cannibal rākṣasa** of the forest near Vāraṇāvata; smells the sleeping Pāṇḍavas and sends his sister to fetch them as food; **killed by Bhīma** in a wrestling-match when she instead falls in love. *(Source: Mahābhārata Ādi Parva.)*
- **Hiḍimbā / Hiḍimbī** (हिडिम्बा) - His sister, a **rākṣasī who weds Bhīma** by *gāndharva/rākṣasa* union; mother of **Ghaṭotkaca**; releases Bhīma to rejoin his brothers after the boy is born. A rare **sympathetic rākṣasī**, worshipped as a goddess in **Himachal (Hiḍimbā Devī temple, Manali)**. [folk-cult] *(Source: Ādi Parva; Wikipedia "Hidimbi".)*
- **Ghaṭotkaca** (घटोत्कच, "pot-headed") - Son of Bhīma & Hiḍimbā; **half-rākṣasa Pāṇḍava hero** who matures instantly, fights with full *rākṣasa-māyā* and aerial powers; a **decisive warrior at Kurukṣetra** who is finally slain on the 14th night by Karṇa's **Śakti** — the one-use divine weapon Karṇa had hoarded for Arjuna, so his death **saves Arjuna's life**. Father of **Barbarīka / Añjanaparvan**. *(Source: Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva.)* — cross-link [Mahābhārata deep-dive](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/).
- **Baka / Bakāsura** (बक) - **Cannibal rākṣasa of Ekacakrā** who extorts a cartload of food *and a human victim* from the townsfolk by turns; **Bhīma** (disguised) eats the food himself and **breaks Baka's back**, freeing the town. *(Source: Mahābhārata Ādi Parva.)*
- **Kirmīra** (किर्मीर) - **Brother of Baka** (and friend of Hiḍimba), a master of illusion who ambushes the Pāṇḍavas entering the Kāmyaka forest to **avenge his kin**; **killed bare-handed by Bhīma**. *(Source: Mahābhārata Vana/Āraṇyaka Parva.)*
- **Jaṭāsura** (जटासुर) - A rākṣasa who **infiltrates the Pāṇḍavas disguised as a brahmin**, then abducts Yudhiṣṭhira, Nakula, Sahadeva and Draupadī (seeking their weapons and Draupadī); **slain by Bhīma** on his return. His son **Alambuṣa** later avenges him at Kurukṣetra. *(Source: Mahābhārata Vana Parva; Wikipedia "Jatasura".)*
- **Alambuṣa** (अलम्बुष) - **Son of Jaṭāsura** (or of Ṛśyaśṛṅga in another line [disputed]); a fearsome **illusion-wielding rākṣasa fighting for the Kauravas** at Kurukṣetra; duels Ghaṭotkaca and the Yādava hero Sātyaki, and is finally **killed by Ghaṭotkaca**. *(Source: Mahābhārata Droṇa Parva.)*
- **Mucukunda / Iḍāveṣa & misc.** - Lesser named forest-rākṣasas appear in the Vana Parva and the **Rāmopākhyāna** (the epic's retelling of the Rāmāyaṇa); the roster here is **open-ended**. *(Source: Mahābhārata Vana Parva.)* [open-ended]

## The Nairṛtas — Rākṣasas as the South-West Quarter

- **Nairṛta / Nairṛtas** (नैर्ऋत) - A standard **synonym for rākṣasas**, derived from **Nirṛti**, goddess/god of **decay, dissolution, calamity** and guardian of the **south-west (Nairṛti) direction**. As a *dik-pāla*-class power, **Nirṛti is the lokapāla of the south-west**, and the rākṣasas are her people/quarter. *(Source: Vedic/Purāṇic dikpāla lists; Wikipedia "Nirṛti"; see [devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).)*
  - *Variant (gender/identity of Nirṛti):* **Ṛgvedic Nirṛti is a goddess** of destruction; later lists make **Nirṛta a male rākṣasa-dikpāla** of the SW (sometimes equated with a rākṣasa-king or with Rudra-aspect). [disputed] *(Source: Wikipedia "Nirṛti".)*
- **Yātudhāna / Yātu** - Vedic name for malign sorcerer-demons **identified with rākṣasas** in the Ṛgveda and Atharvaveda — the oldest stratum of the class. *(Source: Ṛgveda; Atharvaveda.)*
- **Brahma-rākṣasa** (ब्रह्मराक्षस) - A **special sub-class**: the ghost of a **brahmin who misused his learning** or died with unfulfilled rites, becoming a fierce, intelligent rākṣasa — a bridge between the rākṣasa and the *bhūta/preta* classes. [folk / late text] *(Source: Purāṇic and folk tradition; Wikipedia "Brahmarakshasa".)*

## Rākṣasa-Vivāha — Marriage by Seizure

- **Rākṣasa-vivāha** (राक्षस विवाह) - The **seventh of the eight classical forms of Hindu marriage** (Manusmṛti 3.20–34): **marriage by forcible abduction** of a weeping, non-consenting maiden after her kinsmen are slain, wounded, or their homes broken open. Named for the rākṣasas because it embodies their *seizure-by-force* nature. *(Source: Manusmṛti 3.33; Wikipedia "Marriage in Hinduism".)*
  - *Classification:* counted among the **four condemned (aprasásta) rites** — **Āsura, Gandharva, Rākṣasa, Paiśāca** — as against the four approved (Brāhma, Daiva, Ārṣa, Prājāpatya). *(Source: Manusmṛti; Atharvaveda traditions.)*
  - *Permitted to whom:* the Dharmaśāstra texts **grudgingly sanction the rākṣasa rite for the Kṣatriya (warrior) class** — marriage-by-capture as a heroic prerogative; **Bhīṣma's abduction of the Kāśī princesses** (Ambā, Ambikā, Ambālikā) and **Kṛṣṇa's carrying-off of Rukmiṇī** are the epic exemplars. *(Source: Manusmṛti 3.24; Mahābhārata; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.)*
  - *Note vs. Paiśāca:* the **eighth and worst** form, **Paiśāca-vivāha** (intercourse with a sleeping/intoxicated/insane woman), is named for the *piśācas*, not rākṣasas — see the planned **piśācas-and-bhūtas** file.
