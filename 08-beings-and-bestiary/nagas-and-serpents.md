# Nāgas & the Serpent Races

> Part of [08 · Beings & Bestiary](./README.md) → the **serpent-race roster** — the half-human, half-snake divinities of the netherworld.
> Cosmic address: dwellers of **Pātāla / Nāgaloka** and its jewelled capital **Bhogavatī** (see [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) and [06 · Sacred Geography](../06-sacred-geography/)).
> Genealogy: children of **Kaśyapa** & **Kadrū**; eternal foes of [Garuḍa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/) (son of Kaśyapa & Vinatā). For the cosmic serpent who upholds Viṣṇu, cross-link [02 · Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/).

The **nāgas** (नाग, "serpent / cobra") are a divine-to-semi-divine race of **half-human, half-serpent** beings — multi-hooded cobra-bodied below the waist, human (often jewel-crowned) above — who rule the subterranean realm of **Pātāla**, guard its treasures, control rains and waters, and stand in eternal blood-feud with the eagle **Garuḍa**. They are the **thousand sons of the sage Kaśyapa by his wife Kadrū** ("the tawny one"), making them half-brothers of the *devas*, *asuras*, *gandharvas*, *garuḍas* and other Kaśyapa-born orders. The class is **finite-but-vast**: tradition counts **1,000 chief serpents** plus uncountable descendants, yet only a finite set of **named** nāgas recurs across the Vedas, Mahābhārata, Rāmāyaṇa, Purāṇas and folk/Tantric cults. This node catalogs the named kings, the canonical sub-lists (the **Aṣṭanāgas**), the great myth-cycles (Garuḍa enmity, the **sarpa-satra**, Kāliya, Manasā), and every major count/name variant attributed to source. The full roster is **open-ended** — flagged where the sources themselves leave it so.

## Nature, Genealogy & Class (variants)

- **Nāga** - Sanskrit for "serpent / cobra," secondarily an elephant; the race is **anantphaṇa** (many-hooded), venomous, shape-shifting (often appearing fully human or fully serpent), keepers of gems, waters and esoteric knowledge. *(Source: Mahābhārata; Purāṇas; wisdomlib.)*
- **Parentage** - Born of **Kaśyapa** and **Kadrū**, daughter of Dakṣa. Kadrū chose the boon of **1,000 nāga sons of equal splendour**; her co-wife **Vinatā** chose two sons of surpassing strength — **Aruṇa** and **Garuḍa** — seeding the cousins' enmity. *(Source: MBh Ādi Parva, Āstīka Parva.)* — see [Lineages / Vaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/).
- *Variant (count):* the **1,000** chief serpents are headed by a canonical short-list (the **Aṣṭanāga**, eight); the Mahābhārata's snake-sacrifice section names **scores** of additional nāgas burned in the fire (an effectively open roster). *(Source: MBh Āstīka Parva.)*
- *Variant (class):* nāgas are reckoned variously **divine, semi-divine, or demonic** depending on text — worshipped as rain/fertility/poison-curing deities in folk and Tantric strata, but cast as Garuḍa's prey and Janamejaya's quarry in epic. [disputed] *(Source: epic vs. folk/Purāṇic strata.)*
- *Variant (Buddhist/Jain):* a parallel nāga order guards the Buddha (**Mucalinda** shelters him from storm) and the Jīna; the same beings appear across Indic and Southeast-Asian cosmologies. *(Source: Buddhist canon; comparative.)*

## Realm: Pātāla, Nāgaloka & Bhogavatī

- **Pātāla / Nāgaloka** - The lowest of the seven nether-worlds, an underground realm **filled with gems, gold and earthly treasures**, lit by the jewels on the nāgas' hoods; the homeland of the serpent race. *(Source: Purāṇic cosmology; Bhāgavata Purāṇa.)* — see [06 · Sacred Geography](../06-sacred-geography/).
- **Bhogavatī** ("the city of coils / of enjoyment") - The **capital city of the nāgas**, seat of **Vāsuki**, deep beneath the earth (some texts place a Bhogavatī on the Gaṅgā or near the source of waters). *(Source: Mahābhārata; Rāmāyaṇa; Purāṇas.)*
- **Ramaṇaka Dvīpa** - An island/abode of serpents in the ocean, the rightful home from which **Kāliya** fled Garuḍa to hide in the Yamunā. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.16–17.)*
- **Function** - Nāgas are lords of **waters, springs, lakes and rain**, guardians of treasure and of the earth's stability, and keepers of esoteric/medicinal knowledge (snake-venom lore, *garuḍa-vidyā*'s counterpart). *(Source: Purāṇic & Tantric traditions.)*

## The Aṣṭanāga — Eight Chief Serpents (canonical sub-list)

The most worshipped nāgas, the **Aṣṭanāgas**, are eight of Kadrū's thousand sons (lists vary; the wisdomlib/Purāṇic set below is the common eight).

- **Ananta / Śeṣa** ("the endless / the remainder") - The **eldest and foremost**, the thousand-hooded cosmic serpent who **upholds the earth and the worlds** on his hoods and forms the **couch of Viṣṇu** (Śeṣaśāyī / Anantaśayana) afloat the cosmic ocean. At each *pralaya* the cosmos rests on him; he incarnates as **Balarāma** (and, by some accounts, Lakṣmaṇa). The recension-leader of all nāgas, he withdrew from his brothers' plot against Vinatā in disgust and won Brahmā's favour. *(Source: MBh; Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa.)* — see [02 · Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/).
- **Vāsuki** - **King of the nāgas**, ruler of Bhogavatī; served as the **churning-rope** wound round Mount Mandara at the **Samudra Manthana** (devas at the tail, asuras at the head), vomiting the **Hālāhala** poison that Śiva drank; worn by **Śiva** as his neck-ornament. Brother and protector of **Manasā**; orchestrator of **Āstīka's** rescue of the snakes. *(Source: MBh; Bhāgavata Purāṇa.)*
- **Takṣaka** - The **red-hued** serpent-chief who ruled the **Khāṇḍava forest** (burned by Arjuna & Agni) and, avenging that, slew King **Parīkṣit** with his venom — the act that triggered Janamejaya's **sarpa-satra**; saved at the last by Indra's shelter and Āstīka's boon. *(Source: MBh Ādi Parva.)* — see [04 · Deep Dives](../04-deep-dives/).
- **Karkoṭaka** ("crab-/black serpent") - A black naga who **bit and disfigured King Nala** — a benign bite that hid Nala from Kali's persecution and later let him resume his form; a teacher of dice/disguise to Nala. Dwells among the chief nāgas; a guardian of treasure. *(Source: MBh Nala-Damayantī episode, Vana Parva.)*
- **Śaṅkha (Śaṅkhapāla)** - A fiery, many-headed naga; in some lists conducts **Balarāma's** departing soul to the nether-world. *(Source: Purāṇic lists; wisdomlib.)*
- **Padma** - A lotus-red, single-headed naga holding a **portion of the earth** (the north-west quarter in some accounts). *(Source: Purāṇic lists; wisdomlib.)*
- **Mahāpadma (Śaṅkhapadma)** - A white naga bearing a **trident mark** on the hood, given a prime place among the serpents. *(Source: Purāṇic lists; wisdomlib.)*
- **Gulika (Kulika)** - A red, single-headed, intensely venomous cobra; important in **astrology / muhūrta** lore. *(Source: Purāṇic lists; wisdomlib.)*

- *Variant (Aṣṭanāga roster):* Tantric/āgamic worship lists the eight as **Ananta, Vāsuki, Takṣaka, Karkoṭaka, Padma, Mahāpadma, Śaṅkha(pāla), Kulika/Gulika** — but several manuscript traditions swap members (e.g. inserting **Śeṣa** as a ninth, or **Pingala** / **Kambala-Aśvatara** in place of Śaṅkha). [disputed] *(Source: Purāṇic & Tantric variant lists.)*

## Other Named Nāgas & Serpent-Figures

- **Pingala** ("tawny / reddish-brown") - A naga named among the serpents of the underworld; in some Aṣṭanāga and *nāga-pūjā* lists he replaces or supplements the standard eight. (Distinct from the homonymous Pingala of *prosody* and the Haṭhayoga *nāḍī*.) *(Source: Purāṇic naga-lists.)* [disputed]
- **Kāliya** - The hundred-hooded, poison-spewing naga who fled **Garuḍa** from **Ramaṇaka Dvīpa** and infested a pool of the **Yamunā** at Vṛndāvana, boiling its waters with venom. The boy **Kṛṣṇa** leapt from a *kadamba* tree into his coils and **danced upon his hoods** (*Kāliya-mardana / Kāliya-daman*), subduing but sparing him on the plea of his wives, banishing him back to Ramaṇaka. The festival **Nāga-naṭhaiyā / Nāga-nṛtya** commemorates the dance. *(Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.16.)*
- **Mucalinda (Mucilinda)** - The naga who coiled seven times and spread his hood to **shelter the meditating Buddha** from a seven-day storm — the Indic serpent's protective aspect carried into Buddhism. *(Source: Buddhist canon.)* [scholarly]
- **Kambala & Aśvatara** - A celebrated pair of musician-nāga brothers, attendants/charioteers in some Purāṇic and dramatic lists. *(Source: Purāṇic lists.)*
- **Elāpatra (Elapatra)** - A great naga-king who, as a man, asks the Buddha about release; in epic strata an attendant in the snake-sacrifice lists. *(Source: epic & Buddhist sources.)* [scholarly]
- **Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Airāvata, Nahuṣa, Nīla, Dhanañjaya, Bāhuka, Kauravya** - Among the **scores of nāgas named** in the Mahābhārata's snake-sacrifice as falling (or escaping) the fire; **Airāvata's** line is prominent (Ulūpī, below); **Nahuṣa** appears both as a serpent and as a fallen Cakravartin king. *(Source: MBh Āstīka Parva.)* — the full burning-list is **open-ended**.
- **Ulūpī** - Nāga princess of the **Airāvata** line, daughter of **Kauravya**, who wedded **Arjuna** in Nāgaloka and bore **Irāvān/Aravān**; later revives Arjuna after his death by **Babhruvāhana**. *(Source: MBh.)* — see [03 · Lineages / Vaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/).

## Manasā — Serpent-Goddess (the sister)

- **Manasā** (मनसा) - **Goddess of snakes** and **destroyer of poison** (*Viṣaharī*), the **sister of Vāsuki** and the **thousand nāgas**, daughter of **Kaśyapa** (Purāṇic: his *manas*-born / mind-born daughter, hence *Manasā*; epithets **Ayonisambhavā** "not womb-born," **Nityā** "eternal"). Wife of the ascetic **Jaratkāru** and **mother of Āstīka** — the very son foretold to save the serpent race. Chief of the folk pantheons of **Bengal, Bihar, Odisha, Assam, Jharkhand** etc., invoked against **snakebite** and for **fertility & prosperity**; by the 14th c. assimilated to **Śaiva** myth as the one who tended Śiva after he drank the Hālāhala. *(Source: Purāṇas; Mangal-Kāvya tradition; Bengali Manasā-maṅgala.)* [folk]
- *Variant (parentage):* alternatively cast as a **daughter of Śiva** (born of his seed/will) in the Bengali *Manasā-maṅgala*, where she battles the merchant **Chand Saudagar** and the **Behula–Lakhindar** episode dramatizes her cult's rise. [folk] *(Source: Manasā-maṅgala.)*

## The Garuḍa Enmity (origin of the feud)

- **The wager of the co-wives** - **Kadrū** and **Vinatā** bet on the colour of the celestial horse **Uccaiḥśravas** (white, with — Kadrū claimed — a black tail). To win, Kadrū bade her sons **cling to the horse's tail** to falsify it black; the dutiful refused, were **cursed by their mother to burn in Janamejaya's fire**, but most complied. Vinatā lost and was **enslaved** to Kadrū. *(Source: MBh Āstīka Parva.)*
- **Garuḍa's ransom** - Vinatā's son **Garuḍa** freed his mother from bondage by fetching the **Amṛta** from the devas for the nāgas' price; he tricked the snakes (who licked sharp *kuśa*-grass for the spilt nectar and split their tongues — the **forked tongue** aetiology) and won the boon to make **serpents his eternal food**. *(Source: MBh Āstīka Parva; Bhāgavata Purāṇa.)*
- **Consequence** - Thereafter Garuḍa (and his line) preys on nāgas, who hide in Pātāla, the Yamunā (Kāliya), or under divine protection (Mucalinda → Buddha; Vāsuki → Śiva). *(Source: epic & Purāṇic.)*

## The Sarpa-Satra — Janamejaya's Snake-Sacrifice

The frame-story of the **Mahābhārata** itself, set generations after the war.

- **Cause** - **Parīkṣit** (grandson of Arjuna), cursed by the sage's son **Śṛṅgin** for insulting the rishi **Śamīka**, is slain on the seventh day by **Takṣaka's** bite (Takṣaka having bribed/blocked the healer **Kāśyapa** en route). *(Source: MBh Ādi Parva.)* — see [03 · Lineages](../03-lineages-vamsha/).
- **The vow** - Parīkṣit's son **Janamejaya**, king of Hastināpura, swears to **exterminate the entire serpent race** and undertakes the **sarpa-satra** (snake-sacrifice): priests pour oblations into a great fire that **draws nāgas of every size from across the worlds to fall into the flames**. *(Source: MBh Āstīka Parva.)*
- **Takṣaka's flight** - The chief target, **Takṣaka**, terrified, takes refuge with **Indra**, who shields him on his own throne; the mantras grow strong enough to drag **Indra and Takṣaka together** toward the fire. *(Source: MBh Āstīka Parva.)*
- **Āstīka's rescue** - **Vāsuki**, dreading annihilation, sends his nephew **Āstīka** (son of **Manasā/Jaratkāru** and the sage **Jaratkāru**) — half-nāga, half-brahmin, born precisely to fulfil Kadrū's curse-with-an-escape. Āstīka praises the sacrifice so eloquently that Janamejaya offers any boon; Āstīka asks that **the sacrifice cease**, and the king (having already burned millions) relents — **sparing Takṣaka and the surviving nāgas** and ending the enmity. *(Source: MBh Āstīka Parva.)*
- **Frame** - It is **at this very sarpa-satra** that **Vaiśampāyana** (pupil of **Vyāsa**) first recites the **Mahābhārata** to Janamejaya — the epic's outermost narrative frame. *(Source: MBh.)* — cross-link [90 · Literature Corpus](../90-literature-corpus/).
- *Variant (name of Āstīka):* "Āstīka" ("There-is / he-who-is") glosses his father Jaratkāru's utterance **asti** ("it is") to his pregnant nāga-wife. *(Source: MBh Āstīka Parva.)*

## Open-Ended Roster Note

The eight **Aṣṭanāgas** are a fixed worship-set, but the **1,000 sons of Kadrū** and the **sarpa-satra burning-list** are explicitly **open / uncountable** in the sources; manuscript lists differ on membership and ordering (especially Padma/Mahāpadma/Śaṅkha/Kulika/Pingala swaps). Folk and Tantric strata add region-specific serpent deities (e.g. Manasā's Bengal cult) not found in the epic. Treat any "complete" list as text-relative.
