# Nandi (Nandikeshvara): Shiva's Bull, Gana-Chief, and Gatekeeper

> Part of [08 · Beings & Bestiary](README.md) → the **foremost attendant of Śiva**: the white bull *vāhana*, the chief of the *gaṇas*, the *dvārapāla* of Kailāsa, and the patron of sacred music and dance.
> Cosmic address: ever-present at Śiva's side on *Kailāsa* (see [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md)); counted neither among the [Thirty-three Devas](the-thirty-three-devas.md) nor the named [celestial animals](airavata-and-celestial-animals.md) of the churning, but as Śiva's own grace-born servant.

**Nandi** (नन्दि, "the joyful one"), also **Nandin**, **Nandikeśvara** (नन्दिकेश्वर), and **Nandīśvara** (नन्दीश्वर), is the **white humped bull (vṛṣabha)** who kneels couchant before the sanctum of every Śaiva temple, the **foremost of Śiva's gaṇas** and commander of his host, the **gatekeeper (dvārapāla) of Kailāsa**, the **bearer of devotees' whispered prayers** into the god's ear, and the **lord and teacher of music and dance**. His most distinctive trait is a **dual identity** — at once the literal *bull mount* and an *anthropomorphic bull-faced sage-chieftain* — which the Purāṇas never fully merge and which this node catalogs as-is. (Source: Śiva Purāṇa; Liṅga Purāṇa; Skanda Purāṇa; Vāyu Purāṇa.)

## The dual identity — bull vs. anthropomorphic chieftain [cataloged]

- **Two distinct figures fused under one name** — the tradition carries **two Nandis** that later devotion collapses into one: (1) **Nandī the bull** (*vṛṣabha*), Śiva's mount and the *dharma*-bull, and (2) **Nandikeśvara / Nandīśvara the gaṇa-lord**, a bull-faced, three-eyed, anthropomorphic chieftain who leads Śiva's host and guards his gate. *(Source: Śiva Purāṇa; Liṅga Purāṇa; scholarly consensus.)* [scholarly]
  - *Variant (vāhana reading):* Nandi is purely the **bull-vehicle** on which Śiva rides, the couchant *vṛṣabha* of temple iconography facing the *liṅga*. *(Source: temple tradition; Śaiva Āgamas.)*
  - *Variant (gaṇa-lord reading):* Nandikeśvara is a **person** — a club-bearing, four-armed, bull-headed *adhyakṣa* (overseer) who stands at Śiva's door, distinct from any animal. *(Source: Liṅga Purāṇa; iconographic texts.)*
  - *Reconciling gloss:* later devotion treats the bull as the **theriomorphic body** Nandikeśvara can assume — the gaṇa-chief *as* the bull — so the same name covers both registers. [scholarly]
- **The bull as Dharma** — the bull on which Śiva rides is read as **Dharma incarnate**, the four legs being the four feet of *dharma* (austerity, purity, compassion, truth) that erode across the [yugas](../01-manvantaras/README.md). *(Source: Purāṇic gloss; cf. Bhāgavata Dharma-bull motif.)* [scholarly]

## Birth to the sage Śilāda through Śiva's grace

- **Childless Śilāda's penance** — the brahmin sage **Śilāda** (Śālaṅkāyana gotra), grieved at being childless and at the prospect of an *ayonija* (un-womb-born), immortal, *deathless* son, performs severe *tapas* to Indra and then to **Śiva** for such a child. *(Source: Śiva Purāṇa; Liṅga Purāṇa.)*
- **Nandi born from the sacrificial ground** — pleased, Śiva grants the boon; **Nandi arises in the midst of Śilāda's sacrificial field**, found in the furrow or sprung from the *yajña* — a being who is **a portion (aṃśa) of Śiva himself**, instantly radiant and adorned. *(Source: Śiva Purāṇa, Śatarudra-saṃhitā; Liṅga Purāṇa.)*
  - *Variant (prophesied mortality):* two visiting sages (Mitra and Varuṇa) declare the boy **short-lived and mortal**, alarming Śilāda. Nandi, hearing this, undertakes his own *tapas* to Śiva. *(Source: Śiva Purāṇa.)*
- **Granted deathlessness and union with Śiva** — Śiva appears, embraces Nandi, bathes him in the *Gaṅgā*, gives him a **bull-faced/horned crest, a third eye, and ten arms** (in the anthropomorphic register), and declares him **ageless, deathless, and never to be parted from himself** — "you shall be dearer to me than my own son." *(Source: Śiva Purāṇa; Liṅga Purāṇa.)*
- **Marriage to Suyaśā / Marudvatī** — Śiva weds Nandi to **Suyaśā** (in some texts **Marudvatī**), daughter of the Maruts, fixing him among Śiva's intimate household. *(Source: Śiva Purāṇa.)* [regional]
  - *Variant (alternate parentage):* other passages make Nandi a son of **Kaśyapa and Surabhi/Kāmadhenu** — i.e., literally a divine bull-calf of the wish-cow — rather than Śilāda's grace-son. See [Kāmadhenu & divine cattle](kamadhenu-and-divine-cattle.md). *(Source: Purāṇic variant; wisdomlib "Nandi".)* [cataloged]

## Appointment as gaṇa-chief and gatekeeper of Kailāsa

- **Made lord of the gaṇas (gaṇapati / gaṇeśvara)** — Śiva appoints Nandi **commander of his entire host** — the *gaṇas*, *bhūtas*, and *pramathas* — a rank styled **Nandikeśvara / Nandīśvara**, "Lord Nandi," and the *adhyakṣa* of all Śiva's attendants. *(Source: Liṅga Purāṇa; Śiva Purāṇa; Vāyu Purāṇa.)*
- **Dvārapāla of Kailāsa** — Nandi stands as the **gatekeeper at the door of Śiva's abode**, controlling all access to the god in his meditation; none may enter without his leave. *(Source: Śiva Purāṇa; Skanda Purāṇa.)*
- **Bearer of whispered prayers** — because he sits closest to Śiva, devotees **whisper their petitions into Nandi's ear** at the temple, trusting him to convey them to the lord — the origin of the still-living custom of speaking into the bull's ear before the *darśana* of the *liṅga*. *(Source: temple tradition; Śaiva Āgamas.)* [scholarly]
- **Witness and attendant at Śiva's deeds** — Nandi attends the **Tāṇḍava**, the marriage to Pārvatī, the destruction of **Dakṣa's sacrifice** (leading the *gaṇa*-host that wrecks it in some accounts), and the swallowing of *hālāhala*. *(Source: Śiva Purāṇa; Vāyu Purāṇa.)*

## The curse of Rāvaṇa

- **Rāvaṇa mocks Nandi at Kailāsa** — when the *rākṣasa*-king **Rāvaṇa** comes to Kailāsa and is barred by the gatekeeper, he **derides Nandi's monkey-like (or bull-like) face**. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa, Uttarakāṇḍa; Śiva Purāṇa.)*
- **Nandi curses him — death by monkeys** — the insulted Nandi curses Rāvaṇa that **monkeys (vānaras) will one day arise to destroy his race and his city** — a curse fulfilled by the *vānara*-army of Rāma. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa, Uttarakāṇḍa.)*
  - *Variant (the mountain-lifting sequel):* enraged, Rāvaṇa tries to **uproot Kailāsa itself**; Śiva pins the mountain with his toe, crushing Rāvaṇa beneath until he sings the *Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra* in praise — the curse of Nandi and the toe-press are often told as one continuous episode. *(Source: Rāmāyaṇa, Uttarakāṇḍa; Śaiva tradition.)* [cataloged]

## Teacher and transmitter of music and dance

- **Nandikeśvara, lord of music and dance (saṅgīta & nṛtya)** — Nandi is venerated as the **master of rhythm and the dance**, the one who keeps time for Śiva's **Tāṇḍava** and presides over the heavenly arts alongside the [gandharvas and kinnaras](gandharvas-and-kinnaras.md). *(Source: Śaiva tradition; Naṭarāja iconography.)*
- **Reciter of the Nandikeśvara Kāśikā** — the short **Nandikeśvara Kāśikā** (a grammatico-mystical text on the *Māheśvara Sūtras*, the fourteen drumbeats of Śiva's *ḍamaru*) is ascribed to **Nandikeśvara** as its speaker, expounding the cosmic meaning of the alphabet that issued from the drum. *(Source: Nandikeśvara Kāśikā, with Upamanyu's commentary.)* [scholarly]
- **Traditional author of the Abhinaya-darpaṇa** — the classical dance manual **Abhinaya-darpaṇa** ("Mirror of Gesture") is attributed to **Nandikeśvara**, making him the legendary fountainhead of *Bharatanāṭyam* gesture-grammar (*hasta*, *abhinaya*); a lost/cited **Bharatārṇava** is likewise ascribed to him. *(Source: Abhinaya-darpaṇa, attributed to Nandikeśvara.)* [scholarly]
  - *Variant (dating/authorship):* the historical Nandikeśvara of the dance-treatises is a **human ācārya (or title) of uncertain date**, distinct on the *historical clock* from the mythic gaṇa-lord of the *scriptural clock* — the tradition fuses the two, but the texts are post-classical compositions. [disputed]

## The Nandi maṇḍapa of temple tradition

- **The couchant bull before the sanctum** — in Śaiva temple architecture a **Nandi image faces the *liṅga***, couchant (*nisanna*), gazing eternally toward Śiva; the worshipper's line of sight passes **over Nandi's body to the deity**. *(Source: Śaiva Āgamas; temple tradition.)*
- **The Nandi maṇḍapa** — a **dedicated pavilion (Nandi-maṇḍapa)** axial to the *garbhagṛha* houses the bull; monumental examples include the colossal monolithic Nandis of **Lepākṣi, Chamundi Hill (Mysuru), Tanjore (Bṛhadīśvara), Hampi, and the Kāśī Viśvanātha** complex. *(Source: temple tradition; iconographic surveys.)* [scholarly]
- **Iconographic marks** — the bull is **white (or pearl-grey)**, humped (the Indian zebu, *Bos indicus*), bedecked with bells, garlands, and a caparison, often **fitted with a third eye** to echo Śiva. *(Source: Śaiva iconography.)*
- **Nandi as kṣetrapāla and tīrtha** — at major *jyotirliṅga* and *sthala* sites Nandi functions as a **guardian of the precinct** in his own right and receives independent worship. See [sacred geography](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md). *(Source: Sthala-purāṇas.)* [regional]

## Two clocks — placing Nandi

- **Scriptural clock:** Nandi is **timeless** — grace-born of Śiva, ageless and deathless, present across every *yuga* and *kalpa* at Śiva's side; his curse of Rāvaṇa, his role at Dakṣa's sacrifice, and his attendance on the Tāṇḍava are *cross-yuga* events on the cosmic timeline. *(Source: Śiva Purāṇa; Liṅga Purāṇa.)*
- **Historical clock:** the **Nandikeśvara of the dance- and grammar-treatises** (Abhinaya-darpaṇa, Nandikeśvara Kāśikā) is a **post-classical textual tradition**, the divine name lending authority to human-authored *śāstra*. [scholarly]
