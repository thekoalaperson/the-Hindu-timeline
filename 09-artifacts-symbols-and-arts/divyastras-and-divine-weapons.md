# Divyāstras — Divine Weapons

> Part of [09 · Artifacts, Symbols & Arts](./README.md) → the **celestial arsenal**: the mantra-bound astras and the named signature *āyudhas* (weapons) of the gods and epic heroes.
> Cosmic context: wielded across the Mahābhārata and Rāmāyaṇa by [Rāma](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/), the Pāṇḍavas, [Karṇa](../04-deep-dives/karna.md) and the gods of [02 · Deities](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md). Many originate in the [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md) or were forged by **Viśvakarmā** from the pared-down radiance of [Sūrya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md).

Hindu tradition draws a fundamental distinction between two classes of armament. An **astra** (अस्त्र) is a *missile / projected* weapon — typically an ordinary arrow, dart, or even a blade of grass — that is **charged by a mantra** and **presided over by a specific deity** (its *adhiṣṭhātṛ devatā*), whose power it discharges. A **śastra** (शस्त्र) is a *hand-held* weapon (sword, mace, club) wielded by muscle alone. The grandest named weapons of the gods — Sudarśana Cakra, Triśūla, Vajra, Pināka, Gāṇḍīva — are *āyudhas*: permanent divine objects, often personified as **Āyudhapuruṣas** (weapon-deities). This node catalogs (A) the mantra-invoked astras, (B) the signature divine weapons and bows, and (C) the mechanics of invocation/withdrawal. Per repo policy, **all variant origins, owners and counts are cataloged to source.** The astra roster is **open-ended** — the epics name dozens, and no single text is exhaustive. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*; *Ayudhapurusha*)

---

## A · The Astras (mantra-invoked missile weapons)

### The Brahma-class (supreme, deva-killing)

- **Brahmāstra** - The supreme created weapon, presided over by **Brahmā**; destroys entire armies, targets and lands; impossible to counter except by another Brahmāstra or a higher astra (Brahmaśiras). Invoked on an ordinary arrow with the proper mantra; a single discharge could level a region and leave it barren for years. Known to [Rāma](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/), Arjuna, Aśvatthāman, Karṇa, Bhīṣma, Droṇa and others. (Source: Wikipedia, *Brahmastra*; *Astra (weapon)*; Mahābhārata)
  - *Variant (epic detail):* its misuse causes drought and famine where it lands; when two Brahmāstras collide unchecked they threaten universal destruction, requiring sages to intervene (see Withdrawal, below). (Source: Mahābhārata, Sauptika Parva)
- **Brahmaśiras** (Brahmā-śiras, "Brahmā's head") - An intensified Brahmāstra said to be **four times its power**; can slay devas and erase beings across time. In the Mahābhārata only **Arjuna and Aśvatthāman** possessed it (and Karṇa/Droṇa in some accounts); its catastrophic duel ends the war's aftermath. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*; Grokipedia, *Brahmashirastra*; MBh Sauptika Parva)
- **Brahmāṇḍāstra** ("egg-of-Brahmā weapon") [late text] - A still higher escalation named in later/regional retellings, capable of destroying creation itself; treated as the apex of the Brahmā-series. Membership of the Brahmā-tier (astra / śiras / āṇḍa) varies by source. [disputed] (Source: popular Purāṇic / epic compendia)
- **Brahmadaṇḍa** ("rod of Brahmā") - A **defensive** Brahmā-weapon: a staff/rod that absorbs and repels even higher-energy astras. Famously, Vasiṣṭha's brahmadaṇḍa swallowed the Brahmāstra hurled by Viśvāmitra — the humiliation that turned the warrior-king toward brahminhood (see [Viśvāmitra & Vasiṣṭha](../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md)). (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*; Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa)

### The Vaiṣṇava-class (Viṣṇu's astras)

- **Nārāyaṇāstra** - Viṣṇu's weapon; releases a torrent of arrows, discs and missiles whose **power increases with the target's resistance** — to survive it one must *submit* and lay down arms (so Kṛṣṇa instructed the Pāṇḍava army when Aśvatthāman loosed it). **One-use only**; a second invocation rebounds on the wielder. Droṇa received it directly from Viṣṇu and passed it to Aśvatthāman. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*; MBh Droṇa Parva)
- **Vaiṣṇavāstra** - Viṣṇu's infallible astra; **destroys any target irrespective of its nature**, undeflectable. Requires direct transmission from Viṣṇu; held by Bhagadatta (who used it against Arjuna, only for Kṛṣṇa to absorb it onto his own chest as the Vaijayantī garland). (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*; MBh Droṇa Parva)

### The Pāśupata-class (Śiva's astras)

- **Pāśupatāstra** - The personal, inconceivably destructive weapon of **Śiva** (as Paśupati), able to annihilate any target up to creation itself; manifests with a different fearsome head on each summoning. Uniquely, it may be **discharged by thought, by sight, by spoken word, or by bow**. Arjuna won it from Śiva (in his Kirāta hunter form) after single combat — the central boon of his Himālayan tapas. Rāma and a very few others also held it. Never to be used on a lesser foe. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*; MBh Vana Parva, *Kirātārjunīya*) — see [Śiva myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)
- **Māheśvarāstra** - A fiery beam loosed as if from Śiva's **third eye**, reducing beings to ash. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Rudrāstra** - Invokes the destructive power of Rudra to annihilate the target. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)

### The elemental / Dikpāla astras (presided by the Lokapālas)

> These pair off as natural counters — fire vs. water, wind vs. mountain, snake vs. eagle — the standard "rock-paper-scissors" grammar of epic astra-duels. See the guardians in [Dikpālas & Lokapālas](../08-beings-and-bestiary/dikpalas-and-lokapalas.md).

- **Āgneyāstra** - Weapon of **Agni**; emits inextinguishable flames that ordinary means cannot quench. Countered by Vāruṇāstra. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Vāruṇāstra** - Weapon of **Varuṇa**; releases torrential water; the standard counter to Āgneyāstra. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Visoṣaṇa / Visoshana** - Indra's drying weapon, the counter to **Vāruṇāstra** (evaporates its flood). (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Vāyavyāstra** (Vāyavāstra) - Weapon of **Vāyu**; raises a gale that lifts whole armies and scatters illusions. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Śailāstra / Sailastra** - A Vāyu-class weapon used to counter wind-weapons. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Aindrāstra / Indrāstra** - Weapon of **Indra**; conjures a sky-darkening shower of thousands of arrows. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*; MBh)
- **Sūryāstra** - Weapon of **Sūrya**; emits dazzling light that dispels darkness, dries water and can discharge fire. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Candrāstra / Chandrāstra** - Weapon of **Soma/Candra**; freezing cold; counters the Sūryāstra. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Bhaumāstra** - Weapon of **Bhūmi** (Earth); opens earth-tunnels and can summon jewels/serpents from the ground. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Parvatāstra** ("mountain weapon") - Causes mountains to fall from the sky upon the target. Presiding deity unstated in the lists. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Antardhānāstra** - Weapon of **Kubera**; makes things, people or places vanish. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Mānavāstra** - Weapon of **Manu**; hurls the target hundreds of thousands of *yojanas* away rather than killing. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)

### Serpent and counter-serpent astras

- **Nāgāstra** - Presided over by the **Nāgas**; takes serpent form in flight, unerring of aim, venomous on impact. Karṇa's nāgāstra (the serpent Aśvasena, Takṣaka's son) was loosed at Arjuna's head; Kṛṣṇa pressed the chariot into the earth so it struck only his crown. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*; MBh Karṇa Parva) — see [Nāgas & Serpents](../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md)
- **Nāgapāśa** ("serpent-noose") - Binds the target in coils of venomous snakes. **Indrajit (Meghanāda)** used it to bind Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, who were freed only when **Garuḍa** appeared and the snakes fled. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*; Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa)
- **Gāruḍāstra / Sauparṇa** - Invokes **Garuḍa**; the standard counter to Nāgāstra and Nāgapāśa (snakes scatter at the eagle's approach). (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Vāruṇapāśa** - Varuṇa's seven-loop noose, used to bind and torment sinners. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)

### Mind-affecting & illusion astras (and their counters)

- **Sammohanāstra** - A **Gandharva** weapon; collapses an entire army into a hypnotic trance/swoon. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Prajñāstra** - Restores senses and clarity of thought; the counter to Sammohanāstra. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Mohinīāstra** - Weapon of **Mohinī**; a mesmerizing song that also dispels enemy sorcery. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Tvāṣṭrāstra** (Twashtar) - Weapon of **Tvaṣṭṛ**; makes the enemy host **mistake one another for foes** and turn on themselves. Said to be possessed only by **Arjuna and Rāma**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Śabdavedhāstra / Sabdavedastra** - Prevents/strips invisibility (a "sound-piercing" weapon). (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Jyotikṣāstra** - A Sūrya-class weapon that floods dark areas with light. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)

### One-shot kill-weapons (śaktis & named arrows)

- **Vāsava-śakti / Vasavi Shakti** - Indra's infallible **one-use** dart, given to [Karṇa](../04-deep-dives/karna.md) in exchange for his birth-armor and earrings (*kavaca-kuṇḍala*). Reserved for Arjuna, Karṇa was forced to spend it on **Ghaṭotkaca** instead — the night Kṛṣṇa called Bhīma's son's death a gift. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*; MBh Karṇa/Droṇa Parva)
- **Añjalikāstra** - Indra's crescent-bladed arrow with which **Arjuna beheaded Karṇa**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*; MBh Karṇa Parva)
- **Bhārgavāstra** - The astra-corpus of **Paraśurāma** (Bhārgava); a mysterious mass-destruction weapon known only to Paraśurāma and his pupil **Karṇa** — the knowledge Karṇa lost to a curse at the decisive moment. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*; MBh) — see [Karṇa](../04-deep-dives/karna.md)
- **Pāśupata of the Teen Bāṇa (Barbarīka's three arrows)** [folk] - Three infallible arrows granted by Śiva to **Barbarīka** (Ghaṭotkaca's son): one marks all targets to be destroyed, one marks all to be saved, the third destroys/binds the marked — sufficient to end the entire war in a moment. Strongly regional/folk (Khāṭū Śyām tradition). (Source: folk Mahābhārata traditions)
- **Vaijayantī / Maghavāna / Visoshana** and similar minor named astras recur in regional lists; the catalog is **open-ended**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)

## B · The Signature Divine Weapons (āyudhas & bows)

> These are not invoked-and-spent like astras but are **permanent divine objects**, often given personality as **Āyudhapuruṣas**. A recurring origin-myth: when **Saṃjñā** could not bear **Sūrya's** heat, **Viśvakarmā** ground the sun on his lathe; from the shavings of solar radiance he forged **Viṣṇu's Sudarśana Cakra, Śiva's Triśūla, and the Puṣpaka Vimāna** (and in some tellings other weapons). (Source: Wikipedia, *Sudarshana Chakra*, *Ayudhapurusha*) — see [Navagraha / Sūrya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md)

### Viṣṇu's arsenal (the four attributes of the *chatur-bhuja*)

- **Sudarśana Cakra** - Viṣṇu's infallible spinning **discus** (su- "good/auspicious" + darśana "vision"); held in the upper-right hand. Conventionally **108 serrated edges**; symbol of *kāla* (time) and *dharma*. Flies at his thought-command and returns; stopped only by Viṣṇu's own will. Beheaded **Śiśupāla** (see [Śiśupāla](../04-deep-dives/shishupala.md)), severed **Rāhu** at the [Churning](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md), cut **Satī's** corpse into 51 pieces (the Śākta pīṭhas), and obscured the sun at Kurukṣetra to trick Jayadratha into the open. Personified as **Cakrapuruṣa / Cakrarāja**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sudarshana Chakra*)
  - *Variant (origin):* forged by Viśvakarmā from Sūrya's pared radiance (Purāṇic). (Source: Wikipedia, *Sudarshana Chakra*)
  - *Variant (Rāmāyaṇa):* Viṣṇu **seized the disc** after slaying the dānava **Hayagrīva** atop Mt. Cakravāna. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sudarshana Chakra*)
  - *Variant (Śaiva):* Viṣṇu obtained the cakra by worshipping **Śiva**, who gifted it after Viṣṇu offered his own eye as the thousandth lotus — hence "Sudarśana." [disputed] (Source: Śiva Purāṇa tradition)
  - *Variant (count):* historical depictions show **sixteen-spoked** wheels rather than 108 edges. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sudarshana Chakra*)
- **Pāñcajanya** - Viṣṇu's/Kṛṣṇa's conch (*śaṅkha*), made from the demon-shell **Pañcajana**; its blast opens the Bhagavad Gītā and rallies armies. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ayudhapurusha*; MBh; see [Bhagavad Gītā](../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md))
- **Kaumodakī** - Viṣṇu's divine **mace** (*gadā*); infallible, army-destroying; personified as a goddess **Gadādevī/Kaumodakī**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ayudhapurusha*)
- **Nandaka** - Viṣṇu's sacred, indestructible **sword** ("source of joy"), emblem of pure knowledge cutting ignorance. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ayudhapurusha*)
- **Padma** - The lotus held in the fourth hand, completing the *śaṅkha-cakra-gadā-padma* tetrad. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ayudhapurusha*)

### Śiva's arsenal

- **Triśūla** - Śiva's three-pronged **trident**; infallible, destroys anything except Śiva and Pārvatī themselves. The three prongs = the triads: creation–preservation–destruction; past–present–future; the three guṇas; the three *nāḍīs* (iḍā/piṅgalā/suṣumnā). (Source: Wikipedia, *Trishula*; *Ayudhapurusha*) — see [Śiva myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)
- **Pināka** - Śiva's mighty **bow** with which he burned the three flying cities of **Tripurāsura** (whence his epithet *Pinākī*). In the Rāmāyaṇa, the bow at Sītā's *svayaṃvara* that Rāma breaks is identified with Śiva's bow held in Mithilā. (Source: Wikipedia, *Pinaka*; *Ayudhapurusha*)
  - *Variant:* Viśvakarmā forged **Pināka (Śiva) and Śārṅga (Viṣṇu)** as twin bows to settle the Śiva–Viṣṇu superiority contest; Viṣṇu "won," and Śiva's bow passed to the kings of Mithilā — making the Sītā-svayaṃvara bow the original Pināka. (Source: mysutradhar / divine-bows tradition; Rāmāyaṇa)
- **Chandrahāsa** ("moon-laughter") - Śiva's curved **sword**, gifted to **Rāvaṇa** for his devotion; returns to harm the wielder if used unjustly. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Vijaya** ("victory") - A great **bow** crafted by **Viśvakarmā** for Śiva to destroy Tripura; later given to **Paraśurāma**, who bestowed it on his pupil **Karṇa**. Often debated against the Gāṇḍīva. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vijaya (bow)*; *Astra (weapon)*) — see [Karṇa](../04-deep-dives/karna.md)
  - *Variant (count of the Great Bows):* the canonical "great bows" are usually listed as **Pināka (Śiva), Śārṅga (Viṣṇu), Gāṇḍīva (Brahmā/Arjuna), Vijaya (Śiva→Karṇa)** and **Kodaṇḍa (Rāma)**. Lists give four or five; membership varies. [disputed] (Source: mysutradhar, *All About Divine Bows*)

### Indra's arsenal

- **Vajra** - Indra's **thunderbolt**, forged from the **spine/bones of the sage Dadhīci**, who gave up his body so the gods could make the one weapon able to slay **Vṛtra**. Emblem of indestructible firmness (whence Vajrayāna's "diamond"). (Source: Wikipedia, *Ayudhapurusha*; hindumirror) — see [Dadhīci](../04-deep-dives/dadhichi.md) and [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md)
- **Airāvata, Aṅkuśa & nooses** - Indra's elephant-goad and *pāśa* sometimes counted among his āyudhas; his bow is the **rainbow** (*Indra-dhanus*). (Source: Wikipedia, *Ayudhapurusha*)

### The heroes' bows and the Devī's weapons

- **Gāṇḍīva** - Arjuna's invincible **bow**, made by **Brahmā**; "equal to a hundred thousand bows," indestructible, worshipped by devas, gandharvas and dānavas; strung with 108 celestial strings. Held in succession by **Brahmā → Prajāpati → Indra → Soma → Varuṇa**, who gave it to Arjuna (with two inexhaustible quivers) at the **burning of the Khāṇḍava forest**, when Agni begged Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa to help him consume the forest. (Source: Wikipedia, *Gandiva*; Dharmawiki; MBh Ādi Parva)
  - *Variant (ownership tenure):* Śiva 1000 yrs → Brahmā ~503 yrs → Indra ~580 yrs → Candra ~500 yrs → Varuṇa ~100 yrs → Arjuna (numbers vary by recension). [disputed] (Source: MBh; Anand Neelakantan summary)
- **Śārṅga** - **Viṣṇu's bow** (made of horn, *śṛṅga*); wielded by **Rāma** and later **Kṛṣṇa** (whence Kṛṣṇa's epithet *Śārṅgapāṇi/Śārṅgin*). (Source: Wikipedia, *Sharnga*; *Ayudhapurusha*)
- **Kodaṇḍa** - **Rāma's** personal bow in the Rāmāyaṇa (whence *Kodaṇḍa-Rāma*); sometimes equated with Śārṅga, sometimes listed separately. [disputed] (Source: mysutradhar, *All About Divine Bows*)
- **Vela / Vel** - The divine **spear** of **Kārttikeya/Murugan**, gift of Pārvatī; emblem of valor and the slaying of Tāraka/Sūrapadma. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*) — see [Gaṇeśa & Kārttikeya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md)
- **Vibhūti / sacred-ash astra** - A **Devī** weapon: ash that marks the enemy's vital points so arrows follow unerringly. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*) — see [Devī Māhātmya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)

### The Āyudhapuruṣas (personified weapons)

- The Sudarśana (Cakrapuruṣa), Kaumodakī (Gadādevī), Pāñcajanya, Nandaka, Śārṅga, Pināka, Triśūla and Vajra are each given **anthropomorphic deity-forms** in temple iconography and *dhyāna-ślokas*, worshipped as living attendants of the god. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ayudhapurusha*; HD Asian Art)

## C · How Astras Are Invoked & Withdrawn

- **Acquisition** - Astra-knowledge (*astra-vidyā*) passes **guru → disciple by oral mantra**, only to those of proven character; it is also won by **tapas** directly from the presiding deity (Arjuna's Pāśupata from Śiva, his elemental astras from the Lokapālas during his heavenly sojourn). (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*; MBh Vana Parva)
- **Invocation (*sandhāna*)** - The warrior nocks an ordinary arrow (or grass-blade, water, etc.), recites the **deity-specific mantra** that "arms, directs and disarms" the astra, and looses it; the presiding deity's power then manifests. The Pāśupata is exceptional in firing by thought/word/sight as well as bow. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Counter-astras** - Most astras have a designated counter (Āgneya ↔ Vāruṇa; Nāga ↔ Gāruḍa; Sammohana ↔ Prajñā; Sūrya ↔ Candra; Vāruṇa ↔ Visoṣaṇa), so duels are exchanges of cancelling powers rather than single shots. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
- **Restrictions & one-shot rules** - Several astras may be **used only once** (Nārāyaṇāstra, Vāsava-śakti); re-invoking the Nārāyaṇāstra makes it **rebound on the wielder**. The Nārāyaṇāstra is defeated **only by total submission** — Kṛṣṇa had the Pāṇḍava host drop their weapons and dismount so it found no resistance to feed on. Violating an astra's conditions can kill the user. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*; MBh Droṇa Parva)
- **Withdrawal (*upasaṃhāra*)** - A properly trained wielder can **recall** an astra mid-flight with the withdrawal-mantra. The defining episode: after Kurukṣetra, **Arjuna and Aśvatthāman** loose Brahmaśiras at each other; **Vyāsa and Nārada** stand between the weapons and order both withdrawn. **Arjuna, knowing the recall, withdraws his.** **Aśvatthāman does not know the withdrawal** and cannot recall it; forced to redirect it, he aims it at **Uttarā's womb** to end the Pāṇḍava line, killing the unborn **Parīkṣit** — whom **Kṛṣṇa** revives (the *Sauptika/Aiṣīka* climax). For this, Aśvatthāman is cursed with the loss of his head-jewel and a wound that festers for millennia. (Source: Wikipedia, *Brahmastra*; Grokipedia, *Brahmashirastra*; MBh Sauptika Parva; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1)
- **Open-endedness** - No single text catalogs all astras; the Mahābhārata, Rāmāyaṇa, and Purāṇas each name overlapping but distinct sets, and regional/folk traditions (Barbarīka, Khāṭū Śyām) add more. Treat the full roster as **open-ended**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*)
