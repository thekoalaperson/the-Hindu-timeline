# Artifacts, Symbols & Arts — Index

> Section root: `09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/` — the **made and wielded things of the cosmos**: the divine weapons (*astra/śastra*), the named relics and treasures, the animal-vehicles (*vāhana*) the gods ride, the auspicious symbols and emblems of the tradition, and the 64 fine arts (*kalā*) by which culture itself is reckoned divine. Up-link: repo [`../README.md`](../README.md).
> Where [`02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/) catalogs the **wielders** and [`08-beings-and-bestiary/`](../08-beings-and-bestiary/) the **classes of beings**, this section catalogs the **objects and disciplines** that pass between them — the iconographic *attributes* that identify a deity (cakra, triśūla, vīṇā), the loot of the [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/), and the *vidyās* and *kalās* a perfected being masters. Per repo policy, **all variant lists, counts, makers, and ownership-chains are cataloged to source**; several rosters here are explicitly **open-ended**.

---

## How this section is sorted

The material divides into **four families**, each with its own detail file. The dividing line is function:

- **Weapons** (*āyudha*) — things made to strike. Split into mantra-invoked *astras* and hand-held *śastras*.
- **Relics & treasures** — named objects of power that are *not* primarily weapons (jewels, vessels, trees, vehicles, garments) — plus the **living** sacred objects, the plants and animals that embody deities.
- **Vāhanas** — the living mounts; a sub-class of attribute, but large enough for its own roster.
- **Symbols & arts** — the abstract: emblems/marks (*Om*, svastika, tilaka) and the disciplines (the 64 *kalās*, the *vidyās*). The kalā-roster has since been **expanded into a full arts shelf** — performing, musical, painted, carved, cast, built and household arts, each with its own detail file (see below).

Many objects belong to a deity as a fixed **iconographic attribute** — Viṣṇu's four hands canonically hold **śaṅkha** (conch), **cakra** (discus), **gadā** (mace), **padma** (lotus); Śiva holds **triśūla** + **ḍamaru**; Sarasvatī the **vīṇā**. These attributes cross-link back to [`02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/). A recurring sub-theme is the **personified attribute**: weapons and emblems depicted as semi-divine beings, the **āyudhapuruṣas** (e.g. Cakra-puruṣa, Gadā-devī Kaumodakī, Sudarśana, Pāñcajanya). (Source: Wikipedia, *List of mythological objects (Hindu mythology)*; *Ayudhapurusha*)

## The files (each holds a one-bullet-per-item exhaustive roster)

### Weapons — the celestial arsenal

- **[`divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md`](divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md)** — the full catalogue of **astras** (mantra-invoked super-weapons presided over by a deity) and **śastras** (hand-held arms). Anchors: **Brahmāstra** (Brahmā; once-per-lifetime, scorches the land) and its escalations **Brahmaśira / Brahmāṇḍāstra**; **Pāśupatāstra** (Śiva's mind-released ultimate, held by Arjuna, Indrajit, Viśvāmitra); **Nārāyaṇāstra** (Viṣṇu's auto-multiplying barrage); **Vaiṣṇavāstra**; **Āgneyāstra** (fire, Agni), **Vāruṇāstra** (water, Varuṇa), **Vāyavyāstra** (wind), **Indrāstra/Vajra** (Indra's thunderbolt, forged from sage Dadhīci's bones), **Garuḍāstra**, **Nāgāstra/Nāgapāśa** vs. **Garuḍāstra** counter, **Sūryāstra**, **Mohanāstra** (stupefaction), **Sammohanāstra**, **Parvatāstra**, **Anjalikāstra** (slew Karṇa). Named **named arms**: **Sudarśana Cakra** (Viṣṇu's 108-edged discus, ground from sun-dust by Viśvakarmā), **Triśūla** (Śiva's trident), **Vajra**, **Gadā Kaumodakī** (Viṣṇu's mace), **Śārṅga** (Viṣṇu's bow) & **Gāṇḍīva** (Brahmā-made bow with 108 strings, Varuṇa→Arjuna), **Vijaya** (Karṇa's bow), **Pināka** & **Ājagava** (Śiva's bow; the broken bow of the [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/)), **Paraśu** (Paraśurāma's axe), **Nandaka** (Viṣṇu's sword), **Khaṭvāṅga**. *Variants:* astra-rosters differ sharply by text (Vedic vs. epic vs. Purāṇic); the set is **open-ended** and presiding-deity attributions disagree. (Source: Wikipedia, *Astra (weapon)*; *List of mythological objects*)

### Relics, treasures & vehicles

- **[`divine-objects-and-treasures.md`](divine-objects-and-treasures.md)** — non-weapon objects of power, many surfacing in the **[Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/)** (churning of the milk-ocean). Anchors: **Amṛta** (nectar of immortality) & the **Kumbha** that held it; **Kaustubha** (Viṣṇu's chest-jewel) and **Syamantaka** (the sun-gem of the Kṛṣṇa cycle); **Cintāmaṇi** (wish-granting jewel); **Kāmadhenu/Surabhi** (wish-cow) & calf **Nandinī**; **Kalpavṛkṣa** & **Pārijāta** (wish-trees of Svarga); **Akṣaya-pātra** (Sūrya's inexhaustible food-vessel of the Pāṇḍavas); **Pāñcajanya** (Kṛṣṇa's conch) & **Devadatta** (Arjuna's); **Puṣpaka Vimāna** (Viśvakarmā→Brahmā→Kubera→Rāvaṇa→Rāma's flying chariot); divine armor **Kavaca-Kuṇḍala** (Karṇa's birth-armor & earrings); **Vaijayantī** (Viṣṇu's garland); **Sañjīvanī** (the resurrection-herb of the Rāmāyaṇa). *Open-ended* — every Purāṇa adds local relics. (Source: Wikipedia, *List of mythological objects (Hindu mythology)*; *Samudra Manthana*)
- **[`vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md`](vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md)** — the **vāhanas**, living vehicles that double as a deity's attribute and tamed-force symbol. Roster: **Garuḍa** (eagle-man; Viṣṇu), **Nandi** (bull; Śiva), **Mūṣika** (mouse; Gaṇeśa), **Haṃsa** (goose/swan; Brahmā & Sarasvatī), **Mayūra/Paravāṇi** (peacock; Kārttikeya), **Airāvata** (white elephant; Indra) & **Uccaiḥśravas** (white horse; churning-loot), **Siṃha/Vyāghra** (lion/tiger; Durgā), **Ulūka** (owl; Lakṣmī), **Mahiṣa** (buffalo; Yama), **Makara** (sea-monster; Gaṅgā, Varuṇa), **Mendha/Aja** (ram; Agni), **Gṛdhra/Kāka** (vulture/crow/raven; Śani; also buffalo, iron chariot). *Variant:* several deities have **multiple/regional** vāhanas (Lakṣmī: owl *or* elephants; Śani: many). (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*; Dolls of India, *Hindu Vahanas*)
- **[`sacred-flora-and-fauna.md`](sacred-flora-and-fauna.md)** — the **living sacred objects**: plants and animals as divine bodies, deity-attributes, and ritual requisites. (I) sacred plants & trees — **Tulsī** (a goddess in plant-body), **Bilva**, **Aśvattha**, **Vaṭa**, **Padma**, **Darbha/Kuśa**, **Soma**; (II) sacred animals — the **cow** (Kāmadhenu's body), **Nandi**, **Garuḍa**, **Hanumān & the vānaras**, **Jaṭāyu & Sampāti**, **Kāliya** & the serpents, **Gajendra/Airāvata**, **Jāmbavān**. Rosters explicitly **open-ended** — Vedic, epic, Purāṇic and folk strata each add species. (Source: Wikipedia, *Tulasi in Hinduism*; *Kamadhenu*; *Kusha grass*)

### Symbols & arts

- **[`sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md`](sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md)** — the abstract marks and auspicious emblems. Anchors: **Om/Auṃ/Praṇava** (the primordial sound-symbol of Brahman); **Svastika** (auspiciousness/the sun's turning); **Śrīvatsa** (Viṣṇu's chest-curl) & **Nandyāvarta**; **Tilaka/Bindi** (sectarian forehead-marks — Vaiṣṇava *ūrdhva-puṇḍra*, Śaiva *tripuṇḍra* [tri-ash-lines]) cross-linking [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/); **Padma** (lotus, purity); **Śaṅkha** (conch, *Nāda-Brahma*); **Kalaśa/Pūrṇa-kumbha** (the full-pot of abundance); **Śrī Yantra** & the wider **yantra/maṇḍala** geometry; **Rudrākṣa** & **Tulasī** beads; **Dhvaja** (banners — Garuḍa-dhvaja, Vṛṣabha-dhvaja); the **Aṣṭamaṅgala** ("eight auspicious things," whose membership **varies by list**). (Source: Wikipedia, *Category:Hindu symbols*; *Ashtamangala*)
- **[`sixty-four-kalas.md`](sixty-four-kalas.md)** — the **Catuḥṣaṣṭi Kalā**, the 64 fine arts a cultured/perfected person masters (Kṛṣṇa learned all 64 + the 14 **vidyās** in 64 days under Sāndīpani), enumerated in Vātsyāyana's *Kāmasūtra*; Śiva is *Kalā*-master and the Devī (Lalitā-sahasranāma) embodies all 64. Full numbered list — *gīta* (song), *vādya* (instruments), *nṛtya* (dance), *citra/ālekhya* (painting), and so on through 64 — plus the **14 vidyās** (4 Vedas + 6 Vedāṅgas + Mīmāṃsā, Nyāya, Purāṇa, Dharmaśāstra). *Variants:* the membership **shifts by century**; Yaśodhara's 13th-c. *Jayamaṅgalā* commentary lists **512** sub-arts; Jain, Buddhist, and South-Indian lists differ. [disputed] (Source: Wikipedia, *Kalā*; Dharmawiki, *64 Kalas*)

### The arts shelf — the kalās expanded into full disciplines

Single lines of the 64-kalā roster are raised into full detail files; each keeps the **two-clocks** frame (a scriptural/revealed origin vs. the [scholarly] dated text- and craft-history).

- **[`natya-shastra-and-the-classical-arts.md`](natya-shastra-and-the-classical-arts.md)** — the **Nāṭya Śāstra** of **Bharata Muni**, the *Nāṭyaveda* ("fifth Veda") Brahmā distilled from the four Vedas: **rasa theory**, the four-fold *abhinaya*, the *nṛtta–nṛtya–nāṭya* scheme, the **108 karaṇas** of the *Tāṇḍava-lakṣaṇa* — root of the classical dance grammar. Historically c. 200 BCE–200 CE. [scholarly] (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra*, trans. Ghosh; Abhinavagupta, *Abhinavabhāratī*)
- **[`sangita-classical-music-svara-raga-tala.md`](sangita-classical-music-svara-raga-tala.md)** — **saṅgīta** (gīta–vādya–nṛtya), expanding kalās #1–2: **svara**, the 22 **śrutis**, **rāga** (first named in Mataṅga's *Bṛhaddeśī*), **tāla**, and the Carnatic **melakarta** / Hindustani **thāṭ** frameworks; divine origins in the Sāmaveda, Sarasvatī's vīṇā and Nārada. (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* chs. 28–33; *Dattilam*; *Bṛhaddeśī*)
- **[`chitra-kala-painting-traditions.md`](chitra-kala-painting-traditions.md)** — **citra-kalā**, expanding kalā #5 *Ālekhya*: the *Viṣṇudharmottara Purāṇa*'s **Citrasūtra** (Mārkaṇḍeya–king Vajra dialogue, earliest surviving painting-treatise) and the living devotional schools — temple, scroll, cloth and wall painting. (Source: *Citrasūtra*, trans. Kramrisch; *Kāmasūtra* 1.3)
- **[`murti-lakshana-and-iconometry.md`](murti-lakshana-and-iconometry.md)** — **mūrti-lakṣaṇa** & the proportional canon (**tālamāna**): the units, ratios and *dhyāna* prescriptions by which the *śilpin* lays out an efficacious icon fit for *prāṇa-pratiṣṭhā*; surviving redactions c. 6th–12th c. CE. [scholarly] (Source: *Citrasūtra*; *Bṛhat Saṃhitā* ch. 58; *Mānasāra*)
- **[`pancha-loha-bronze-casting.md`](pancha-loha-bronze-casting.md)** — the **pañca-loha / aṣṭadhātu** alloys and the **lost-wax** (*madhūcchiṣṭa-vidhāna*) casting of the metal mūrti; the **Cōḻa bronzes** (Naṭarāja of the Ānanda-Tāṇḍava) as the craft's summit, still cast by Svāmimalai *sthapati* lineages. [scholarly] (Source: *Mānasāra*; *Mayamata*; *Mānasollāsa*)
- **[`temple-architecture-nagara-dravida-vesara.md`](temple-architecture-nagara-dravida-vesara.md)** — the tripartite temple typology **Nāgara / Drāviḍa / Vesara**: the temple as *vimāna* and house of god, its plan on the vāstu-puruṣa-maṇḍala; the orders' historical emergence c. 5th c. CE onward. [scholarly] (Source: Wikipedia, *Hindu temple architecture*; śilpa/vāstu śāstras)
- **[`temple-iconographic-motifs.md`](temple-iconographic-motifs.md)** — the temple's guarding ornament: **kīrtimukha** (the self-devouring "Face of Glory" from the Jalandhara/Rāhu myth), **yāḷi/vyāla**, **makara-toraṇa**, **dvārapāla** and the gajalakṣmī lintel. (Source: *Skanda Purāṇa*; *Śiva Purāṇa*, Rudra-saṃhitā; Coomaraswamy, *The Face of Glory*)
- **[`rangoli-kolam-floor-art.md`](rangoli-kolam-floor-art.md)** — the sacred **floor-art** (rangoli, kōlam, ālpanā, muggu…), expanding kalā #7 *Taṇḍula-kusuma-balivikāra*: the daily-renewed threshold-maṇḍala inviting Śrī-Lakṣmī; regional vocabularies cataloged, **never merged**. (Source: *Kāmasūtra* 1.3; Wikipedia, *Rangoli*, *Kolam*)
- **[`mehndi-and-ritual-body-adornment.md`](mehndi-and-ritual-body-adornment.md)** — drawing on the **body**: *mehndi* (henna), *alaktaka* (red lac) and the *ṣoḍaśa-śṛṅgāra* ("sixteen adornments") of the auspicious/bridal body, expanding the cosmetic kalās #6, #9, #14, #20. (Source: *Kāmasūtra* 1.3; Wikipedia, *Mehndi*, *Henna*)
