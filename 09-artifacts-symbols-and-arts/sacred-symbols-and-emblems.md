# Sacred Symbols & Emblems

> Sits in [`09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/`](README.md) — the **sign-language of the tradition**: the auditory, geometric, iconographic and ritual emblems through which Hindu *darśana* (sacred seeing) is encoded. A sacred symbol is at once a **meaning-bearer** (the *padma* = transcendence), a **deity-attribute** (the *cakra* in Viṣṇu's hand), and a **ritual instrument** (the *kalaśa* on the altar, the *japa-mālā* in the hand).
> Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md) · master index [`The Hindu Timeline`](../README.md). Deities these emblems mark → [`../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/); the same objects as *weapons* → [`./divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md`](divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md); the *living* sacred symbols (cow, Tulsī, Pīpal) → [`./sacred-flora-and-fauna.md`](sacred-flora-and-fauna.md); meditation/yoga uses → [`../05-concepts-and-dharma/yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md`](../05-concepts-and-dharma/yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md).

This file catalogs the **non-living emblems** of Hinduism in groups: (I) the **primal sound & sign** (Om, Svastika, Śrīvatsa, Nandyāvarta, Śatkoṇa); (II) **yantras, maṇḍalas & sacred geometry**; (III) **body-marks** (Tilaka, Vibhūti, Rudrākṣa, Bindi, colours); (IV) the **deity hand-attributes** (Lotus, Conch, Cakra, Triśūla, Ḍamaru, Vīṇā, Akṣamālā, Kamaṇḍalu); (V) **ritual & temple emblems** (Kalaśa, Dīpa/Āratī, Liṅga, Śālagrāma, Nandī, Pādukā, Dhvaja, Aṣṭamaṅgala, Gopuram); and (VI) the **iconographic vocabulary** (Naṭarāja, mudrās). Per repo policy, every variant interpretation and source-text attribution is cataloged to source; several readings are explicitly multi-valent — Vedic, Tantric, Purāṇic and folk strata each layer meaning onto the same sign.

## I. The Primal Sound & Sign

- **Om / Auṃ — the Praṇava** — the supreme sacred syllable, the **sound-form of Brahman** and "the universal name of the Lord," called *praṇava* ("that which is sounded forth") and *omkāra*. Decomposed as **A–U–M + the silence (turīya)**: the three letters are read as the waking/dream/deep-sleep states, the **[Trimūrti](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md)** (Brahmā–Viṣṇu–Śiva), and the three Vedas (Ṛg–Yajus–Sāma). The *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* is wholly devoted to it; *Bhagavad Gītā* 8.13 makes uttering "Om" at death the gate to the supreme goal. (Source: Wikibooks, *Religious Symbols of Hinduism*; Study.com, *Om Symbol*)
  - *Variant — graphic vs. sonic:* the familiar **Devanāgarī glyph** (with its lower curve = waking, upper curve = deep sleep, side curve = dream, crescent + dot = *māyā* and the absolute) is a relatively **late visual codification**; the Vedic reality is the *sound*. (Source: HinduScriptures, *Hindu Symbology*) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Tantric):* equated with the seed of the **Anāhata** (unstruck) sound and used as the universal *bīja* heading nearly every mantra. (Source: SivaSakti, *Yantra*)
- **Svastika / Swastika** — the bent-armed cross meaning **"well-being / it-is-good"** (*su-asti*), the premier **auspiciousness-sign**, drawn at thresholds, on ledgers (*Śubh-Lābh*), and to open every *pūjā* and yantra. Attested in the **Indus Valley Civilization**, Neolithic onward. (Source: Hindu American Foundation, *10 Things About the Swastika*; The Gaudiya Treasures, *Hindu Symbols*)
  - *Variant — right vs. left facing:* the **right-facing (clockwise)** form denotes the universe's *evolution / pravṛtti* and the **sun/Sūrya**; the **left-facing (sauvastika)** denotes *involution / nivṛtti* and is linked to night/Kālī. Some traditions tie the two to the two aspects of **Brahmā**. (Source: Wikibooks, *Religious Symbols of Hinduism*) [disputed — interpretations vary by school]
  - *Note:* the Hindu/Jain/Buddhist svastika is **unrelated** to and predates the 20th-c. Nazi *Hakenkreuz* by millennia. (Source: Hindu American Foundation)
- **Śrīvatsa — Viṣṇu's chest-curl** — the auspicious **curl/whorl of hair on the right of [Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md)'s chest** (a triangular or endless-knot mark in later art), one of his **two permanent body-marks** (paired with the **Kaustubha** gem and the **Vaijayantī** garland as his standing chest-attributes), and the **seat of [Śrī / Lakṣmī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)** on his breast — whence the name *Śrī-vatsa*, "beloved of Śrī." (Source: List of Mythological Objects, Wikipedia; Ethnic Tanjore Arts) [iconographic]
  - *Origin-myth:* the mark is read as the **footprint of the sage Bhṛgu**, who kicked Viṣṇu's chest to test the three gods; the Lord, far from angered, honoured the foot-mark and embraced it — Lakṣmī, born of the same ocean, took her seat there. (Source: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.89) [Purāṇic]
  - *Theological gloss:* in Vaiṣṇava reading the **Kaustubha = the *jīva*** (the collective souls) borne on the Lord's heart and the **Śrīvatsa = *prakṛti*** (primordial nature) — cross-ref the fuller Kaustubha note in [`./divine-objects-and-treasures.md`](divine-objects-and-treasures.md). (Source: Vaiṣṇava commentary tradition) [scholarly/theological]
  - *Aṣṭamaṅgala & cross-tradition:* the Śrīvatsa is counted among the **Aṣṭamaṅgala** (below) and recurs across the dharmic traditions — it is the standard **chest-emblem of the Jain tīrthaṅkaras**, marking the *jina*'s breast in icon and relief. (Source: Aṣṭamaṅgala, Wikipedia; Śrīvatsa, Wikipedia) [shared Hindu-Jain symbol]
- **Nandyāvarta — the auspicious labyrinth** — an elaborated, multiply-turned **variant of the svastika** (a square "wandering" spiral of nine points), an emblem of **good fortune and the right unfolding of the cosmos**; like the Śrīvatsa it is enrolled among the **Aṣṭamaṅgala** and figures prominently in Jain auspicious-sign lists. (Source: Aṣṭamaṅgala, Wikipedia; Nandyavarta, Wikipedia) [shared Hindu-Jain symbol]
- **Śatkoṇa — the hexagram** — two interlocking equilateral triangles, "six-cornered," symbolising the **union of Śiva (upward triangle, puruṣa/fire) and Śakti (downward triangle, prakṛti/water)**; the cosmic balance from which the *bindu*'s unmanifest potential manifests outward. Forms the structural heart of the Śrī Yantra. (Source: Grokipedia, *Shatkona*; Buddhas Art of Healing, *Sri Yantra*)

## II. Yantras, Maṇḍalas & Sacred Geometry

- **Yantra** — a **geometric "instrument/machine" for the mind**, a diagrammatic body of a deity/mantra used in Tantric worship and meditation; concentric forms (square *bhūpura* gate → lotus petals → triangles → *bindu*) draw consciousness from the periphery to the centre. (Source: SivaSakti, *Introduction to Yantra*; Rudraksha-Ratna, *Yantra*)
- **Śrī Yantra / Śrī Cakra** — the supreme yantra, the **geometric form of the goddess [Tripurasundarī / Lalitā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md)** and of cosmic creation. **Nine interlocking triangles** (five Śakti-down, four Śiva-up) generate **43 smaller triangles** around the central **bindu**, the source-point of manifestation; the union of divine masculine/feminine. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sri Yantra*; Symbolsage, *Sri Yantra*)
  - *Variant (form):* worshipped flat (*bhū-pṛṣṭha*) or as the raised three-dimensional **Mahāmeru** pyramid. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sri Yantra*)
- **Bindu** — the **dimensionless central point**: pure consciousness, the seed of creation, the place where all duality dissolves into unity; the goal-point of every yantra and the dot crowning the Om-glyph. (Source: Buddhas Art of Healing, *Sri Yantra*)
- **Maṇḍala** — a **circular sacred diagram** of the cosmos used in ritual, temple-floor layout (*vāstu-puruṣa-maṇḍala*) and meditation; the square-in-circle plan underlies temple ground-plans. (Source: SivaSakti, *Yantra*; Mandalas for the Soul) [overlaps temple iconography below]

## III. Body-Marks — Tilaka, Ash, Beads & Colour

- **Tilaka / Tilak** — the **sacred forehead mark** applied at the *ājñā* (third-eye) point, denoting devotion, sectarian affiliation, and the awakening of inner vision. Two great families divide Hindu sectarianism: (Source: Knowledge of India, *35 Hinduism Symbols*; rrudraksha.com, *Why We Apply Tilak*)
  - **Ūrdhva-puṇḍra (Vaiṣṇava)** — *vertical* lines (a "U" / "Y" shape), worn by devotees of **[Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md)**/Kṛṣṇa; the two side lines = the **lotus feet of the Lord**, the central line (often a red *Tulasī*/Lakṣmī streak or *śrīcūrṇa*) = the Goddess/Tulasī. The *Vāsudeva Upaniṣad* reads its lines as the three Vedas. Each sampradāya has a distinct shape (Śrī-Vaiṣṇava, Mādhva, Gauḍīya, etc.). (Source: Wikipedia, *Urdhva Pundra*; Gupt Vrindavan Dham, *What is Tilak*)
  - **Tripuṇḍra (Śaiva)** — *three horizontal lines* of **Vibhūti (sacred ash)** across the brow, often with a *bindu* "third eye," worn by devotees of **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)**. The *Kālāgni-Rudra Upaniṣad* reads the three lines as the three sacred fires, the three syllables of Om, the three *guṇas*, three worlds, three *ātmans*, and the three powers — culminating in Maheśvara. (Source: Wikipedia, *Tripundra*)
  - *Variant — Śākta:* a single round **red *kuṃkuma* dot/tilaka** marks Devī worship. (Source: rrudraksha.com)
- **Vibhūti / Bhasma** — **sacred grey ash** (ideally from *yajña*-fire or dried cow-dung), smeared by Śaivas; a memento mori — "the body returns to ash" — and a sign of having burnt away ego and *vāsanās*; Śiva himself is *bhasmāṅga* (ash-smeared). (Source: HinduScriptures, *Hindu Symbology*; Tripundra, Wikipedia)
- **Rudrākṣa** — the **seed of *Elaeocarpus*, "Rudra's / Śiva's teardrops,"** strung into *mālās* worn by Śaivas and used to count *japa*; classed by **mukhī (faces)** — the rare *eka-mukhī* (one-faced) being supreme. Born of Śiva's tears of compassion (over Tripura / over the world). (Source: Japamala, Wikipedia; Rudraksha-Ratna, *Hindu Symbols*) [cross-link [`./sacred-flora-and-fauna.md`](sacred-flora-and-fauna.md)]
- **Akṣamālā / Japa-mālā** — the **prayer-bead loop** (classically **108 beads + 1 *meru*/guru bead**) for counting mantra-recitations (*japa*); a hand-attribute of **Brahmā, Sarasvatī, and ascetics**, signifying continual remembrance (*smṛti*). Materials: Rudrākṣa (Śaiva), **Tulasī** (Vaiṣṇava), crystal/coral (Śākta). (Source: Japamala, Wikipedia)
- **Bindi / Bindu (forehead dot)** — the round mark at the brow-centre over the *ājñā cakra*; a **point of concentrated energy and auspiciousness**, and (as red *kuṃkuma* on the parting/forehead) a **mark of marriage (saubhāgya)** for women. (Source: Facts and Details, *Hindu Symbols*; Knowledge of India)
- **The sacred colours** — chromatic emblems: **Saffron / Bhagvā** = fire, renunciation, the *sannyāsin*'s robe, and Hindu/dharmic identity (the **Bhagvā dhvaja**); **Kuṃkuma (red)** = Śakti, marriage, life-blood, auspiciousness; **Haldi (turmeric-yellow)** = purity and fertility; **white** = *sattva*/purity. (Source: Knowledge of India, *35 Hinduism Symbols*; Facts and Details) [folk-to-scriptural spread]

## IV. Deity Hand-Attributes (Āyudha-puruṣas)

> Many of these double as **weapons** — catalogued in [`./divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md`](divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md) — but here are read as *symbols held in the hand* (the four hands of Viṣṇu being the canonical set).

- **Padma — the Lotus** — the supreme symbol of **purity, divinity and detachment** ("rooted in mud, untouched by the water"). The **cosmic lotus** rises from Viṣṇu's navel bearing **[Brahmā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md)** (whence *Padmanābha*); **[Lakṣmī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)** is *Padmā/Kamalā*; most deities sit lotus-throned (*padmāsana*). (Source: Ethnic Tanjore Arts, *Sacred Symbols of Vishnu*) [fuller botanical entry in [`./sacred-flora-and-fauna.md`](sacred-flora-and-fauna.md)]
- **Śaṅkha — the Conch** — **Viṣṇu's Pāñcajanya**, held in the upper-left hand; blowing it sounds the **primeval Om / the cosmic sound (nāda)** that announces auspicious beginnings, opens *āratī*, and routs evil. The right-spiralling **dakṣiṇāvarta** conch is rare and most sacred. (Source: Shankha, Wikipedia; Ethnic Tanjore Arts)
- **Cakra — the Sudarśana** — **Viṣṇu's spinning discus** ("auspicious vision"), held in the upper-right hand; the **wheel of time** and of cosmic order/*dharma*, severing ignorance and adharma. Personified as a minor deity (*Sudarśana-puruṣa*). (Source: Sudarshana Chakra, Wikipedia; HD Asian Art)
- **Gadā — the Mace** — Viṣṇu's **Kaumodakī**, symbol of sovereign power and the force that subdues ego/time. (Source: Ethnic Tanjore Arts; Hindutva.online, *Vishnu's Weapons*)
- **Triśūla — the Trident** — **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)'s** three-pronged emblem; the three prongs = creation–preservation–destruction, the three *guṇas*, the three *kālas* (past/present/future), and the three powers (will, knowledge, action). Also borne by **[Durgā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-forms-and-navadurga.md)**. (Source: Knowledge of India, *35 Hinduism Symbols*)
- **Ḍamaru — the hourglass-drum** — Śiva's two-headed drum; its beat is the **primal sound (nāda) from which the cosmos arises and is regulated**, the rhythm of creation in the **Tāṇḍava** dance. Its two triangles meeting at the waist echo the Śatkoṇa (Śiva–Śakti). (Source: List of Mythological Objects, Wikipedia; HinduScriptures)
- **Vīṇā** — the stringed instrument of **Sarasvatī** (the *Kacchapī/Mahatī*), emblem of **knowledge, music, and the harmony of the arts**; also Nārada's. Marks the deification of sound-as-learning. (Source: Knowledge of India; HinduScriptures) [iconographic]
- **Kamaṇḍalu** — the ascetic's **oblong water-pot** (gourd/copper, with handle/spout), carried by **Brahmā, Śiva, ṛṣis and *sannyāsins***; the **emblem of asceticism and self-sufficiency**, holding sacred/*amṛta* water. (Source: List of Mythological Objects, Wikipedia)
- **Aṅkuśa & Pāśa** — the **elephant-goad** and **noose**, attributes of **[Gaṇeśa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md)** and Devī: the goad drives the soul onward / curbs ego; the noose binds worldly attachment to draw the devotee in. (Source: Knowledge of India, *35 Hinduism Symbols*) [iconographic]

## V. Ritual & Temple Emblems

- **Kalaśa / Pūrṇa-kumbha** — the **"full vase"**: a metal pot of water topped with mango-leaves and a coconut, the Vedic **"overflowing full vase," source-of-life** symbol of abundance, wisdom and immortality; held to contain **amṛta**. Invoked as the seat of all deities/tīrthas at the start of *pūjā* and crowning temple *śikharas*. (Source: Kalasha, Wikipedia; Eshwar Bhakti, *What is Kalash*)
- **Dīpa / Dīyā & Āratī** — the **oil/ghee lamp** whose flame **dispels ignorance (tamas) and awakens the divine (jyoti)**; **Āratī** is the circling of the lit lamp before the deity, light returning to its source. The *Dīpāvalī* festival generalises the lamp as the victory of light. (Source: Learn Religions, *Symbolism in Hindu Rituals*; Knowledge of India)
- **Ghaṇṭā — the ritual hand-bell** — the **core *pūjā* instrument** rung at *āratī* and through every stage of worship; paired with the **Śaṅkha** (conch), its sound **drives away inauspiciousness (*aśubha*)** and **summons/invokes the deity's presence**. The **ghaṇṭā-nāda (bell-sound)** is read as **nāda / Om made audible** — the unstruck primal sound rendered hearable, sanctifying the space and the offering. (Source: Learn Religions, *Symbolism in Hindu Rituals*; Knowledge of India, *35 Hinduism Symbols*)
  - *Cosmological reading of its parts:* the **body/cup of the bell = the cosmos (Virāṭ)**; the **clapper/tongue = Sarasvatī (vāk, the power of speech)**; the **handle (*mukha*) is crowned by the vehicle/emblem of the presiding deity — Garuḍa (Viṣṇu), Nandī (Śiva), Hanumān, or a Cakra** — so that the bell embodies the whole deity-form in miniature. (Source: HinduScriptures, *Hindu Symbology*; Learn Religions) [iconographic]
  - *Invocation (dhyāna-śloka):* rung while reciting **"*āgamārthantu devānāṃ gamanārthantu rākṣasām…*"** — "for the **coming-near of the gods** and the **departure of the demons**," the bell at once **calls the deities close and banishes negative forces**. (Source: Āgamic *ghaṇṭā* invocation; HinduScriptures) [scriptural — daily *pūjā-vidhi*]
  - *Ritual placement:* fixed in the Āgamic **ghaṇṭā-nyāsa** (the consecratory "placing" of the bell before use) and in the daily **pūjā-vidhi**, where it is sounded at the lamp-circling and at each *upacāra* (service of offering). (Source: Āgama/*pūjā-vidhi* tradition; HinduScriptures) [āgamic]
- **Śiva Liṅga** — the **aniconic emblem of [Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** as the formless infinite, set in the **Yoni-pīṭha** base (Śakti): together the **union of the unmanifest puruṣa and generative prakṛti**, the cosmic pillar of light (*jyotirliṅga*) without beginning or end. (Source: HinduScriptures, *Hindu Symbology*; Knowledge of India)
- **Śālagrāma / Śālagrāma-śilā** — the **black, naturally chakra-marked ammonite stones** of the Gaṇḍakī river, the **aniconic self-manifest (svayambhū) emblem of [Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md)**; the Vaiṣṇava counterpart to the Liṅga, born of the Vṛndā/Tulasī myth. (Source: List of Mythological Objects, Wikipedia) [cross-link [`./sacred-flora-and-fauna.md`](sacred-flora-and-fauna.md) for the Tulsī/Vṛndā origin]
- **Nandī** — the **white bull**, Śiva's **vāhana and gatekeeper**, couchant facing the *garbhagṛha* of every Śiva temple; emblem of **steadfast dharma, strength and devotion (the ideal *bhakta*)**. (Source: Knowledge of India; HinduScriptures) [as a mount, see [`./vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md`](vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md)]
- **Pādukā / the feet-motif** — the **sacred sandals / footprints (Viṣṇu-pāda, the guru's pādukā)** worshipped as the deity's or master's living presence in absence; Bharata enthroned **Rāma's pādukās** to rule Ayodhyā in his stead. Emblem of surrender at the **lotus-feet (caraṇa-kamala)**. (Source: Knowledge of India, *35 Hinduism Symbols*; HinduScriptures) [folk-to-epic]
- **Dhvaja — the temple flag/banner** — the **dhvaja-stambha** (flagstaff) before the sanctum and the fluttering flag atop the *gopuram* announce the deity's domain and the victory of dharma; each deity has an emblem-flag (Garuḍa for Viṣṇu, Nandī/bull for Śiva, the *Kapi-dhvaja* monkey for Arjuna). The **saffron Bhagvā Dhvaja** is the pan-Hindu banner. (Source: Knowledge of India; HinduScriptures)
- **Aṣṭamaṅgala — the "eight auspicious things"** — a **standard set of eight auspicious emblems** invoked/depicted together at temple thresholds, weddings, coronations and *pūjā* to attract good fortune; the same grouping recurs across Hindu, Jain and Buddhist art with **membership that varies by list and tradition**. Common Hindu/Jain members include the **Śrīvatsa**, **Nandyāvarta**, **Svastika**, **Kalaśa / Pūrṇa-kumbha** (full vase), **Śaṅkha** (conch), **Darpaṇa** (mirror), **Bhadrāsana** (auspicious seat/throne) and **Matsya-yugma** (pair of fish); other lists substitute the *vardhamāna* (powder-flask), *cāmara* (fly-whisk) or *aṅkuśa*. (Source: Aṣṭamaṅgala, Wikipedia; Wikipedia, *Category:Hindu symbols*) [membership disputed — varies by list]
- **Gopuram & temple iconography (basics)** — the towering, sculpture-encrusted **gateway-tower** of (esp. Dravidian) temples; with the **śikhara/vimāna** (sanctum-spire) it images **Mount Meru, the cosmic axis**. The plan follows the **vāstu-puruṣa-maṇḍala**; the windowless **garbhagṛha** ("womb-house") holds the *mūrti*, approached through descending light, mirroring the yantra's path to the *bindu*. (Source: SivaSakti, *Yantra*; Mandalas for the Soul) [scholarly]

## VI. Iconographic Vocabulary

- **Naṭarāja — the Cosmic Dancer** — **Śiva as Lord of the Dance**, the most condensed Hindu symbol-icon: the **Ānanda-Tāṇḍava** within a **ring of fire (prabhā-maṇḍala = saṃsāra/cosmos)**, holding the **ḍamaru** (creation's sound) and **agni** (destruction), the **abhaya-mudrā** (fearlessness/preservation) and the **gaja-hasta** pointing to the **raised foot (release/mokṣa)**, trampling the dwarf **Apasmāra** (ignorance/forgetfulness). The Five Acts (*pañcakṛtya*): creation, preservation, dissolution, concealment, grace. (Source: HinduScriptures, *Hindu Symbology*; Knowledge of India)
- **Mudrās — sacred hand-gestures** — codified hand-signs that "seal" energy and meaning in icon, dance and meditation:
  - *Abhaya-mudrā:* raised open palm — **"fear not," protection**.
  - *Varada-mudrā:* downturned open palm — **boon-giving, charity**.
  - *Dhyāna-mudrā:* cupped hands in the lap — **meditation/samādhi**.
  - *Jñāna / Cin-mudrā:* thumb-and-forefinger circle — **knowledge, the union of *ātman* and Brahman** (used in *japa* and *prāṇāyāma*; see [`../05-concepts-and-dharma/yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md`](../05-concepts-and-dharma/yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md)).
  - *Añjali-mudrā:* joined palms — **Namaste, reverence**, "the divine in me bows to the divine in you."
  (Source: Knowledge of India, *35 Hinduism Symbols*; HinduScriptures, *Hindu Symbology*)
