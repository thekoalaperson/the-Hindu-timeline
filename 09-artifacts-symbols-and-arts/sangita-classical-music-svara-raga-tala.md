# Saṅgīta: The Classical Music System — Svara, Śruti, Rāga, Tāla and the Melakarta/Thāṭ Frameworks

> Sits in [`09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/`](README.md) — the **science of musical sound**: *saṅgīta* (the union of *gīta* song, *vādya* instrument and *nṛtya* dance) raised from the bare roster-items #1 *Gīta* and #2 *Vādya* of the [Sixty-Four Kalās](sixty-four-kalas.md) into a full classical discipline. The performance-theology and rasa/tāṇḍava frame are in [`natya-shastra-and-the-classical-arts.md`](natya-shastra-and-the-classical-arts.md); the divine origins (Sāmaveda, Sarasvatī's vīṇā, Nārada) anchor in [`../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md); the theory-text corpus in [`../90-literature-corpus/README.md`](../90-literature-corpus/README.md).

**Saṅgīta** (*saṃ-gīta*, "well-sung; concerted music") is in the classical reckoning the threefold art of **gīta–vādya–nṛtya** — vocal music, instrumental music and dance — of which song (*gīta*) is held supreme and the others derivative. Two clocks govern its history: a **scriptural** clock that traces music to the Sāmaveda's chant and to divine musicians (the Gandharvas, Sarasvatī, Nārada), and a **historical** clock that follows a continuous theory-text lineage from the *Nāṭya Śāstra* (c. 200 BCE–200 CE) through the *Saṅgīta-ratnākara* (13th c.) to the Carnatic/Hindustani systematizations of the 16th–17th centuries. [scholarly]

---

## I. The divine origin of music

- **Music as the Sāmaveda made audible** — the **Sāmaveda** is the Veda of *melody*: its verses are *gāna* (chant), and the classical tradition derives all *gīta* from Sāmic recitation, the **sāman**-singing in three (later seven) registers being the seed of the svara-scale. Music is classed under the **Gāndharvaveda**, the upaveda of the Sāmaveda. (Source: Sāmaveda tradition; *Nāṭya Śāstra* 28) [scriptural]
- **The celestial musicians** — the **Gandharvas** (heavenly singers) and **Kinnaras**, the goddess **Sarasvatī** with her **vīṇā** (titled *Kalānidhi*, "treasury of arts"), and the sage **Nārada** — divine vīṇā-player and reputed transmitter of music to humans — embody the art's heavenly source. Hence music itself is *gāndharva*. (Source: Purāṇic tradition) — see [`../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md). [scriptural]
- **Nāda-brahman — sound as the absolute** — later theory (Śārṅgadeva's opening) identifies musical sound (*nāda*) with **Brahman** itself: *nāda* arising from the union of breath (*prāṇa*) and fire in the body, distinguished into *āhata* (struck/audible) and *anāhata* (unstruck/transcendent) sound. (Source: *Saṅgīta-ratnākara* 1, *Svarādhyāya*) [scholarly]

## II. Sapta-svara — the seven notes and their mythic associations

- **The seven svaras** — the heptatonic scale is **Ṣaḍja (Sa), Ṛṣabha (Ri/Re), Gāndhāra (Ga), Madhyama (Ma), Pañcama (Pa), Dhaivata (Dha), Niṣāda (Ni)**, abbreviated **Sa Ri Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni**. *Ṣaḍja* ("born of six") is so named as the note from which the other six arise; **Sa and Pa are *acala* (fixed/immovable)**, the rest variable. (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* 28; *Dattilam*)
- **Animal-cry associations** — the *Nāṭya Śāstra* keys each svara to a creature's natural cry: **Ṣaḍja = the peacock (mayūra)**, **Ṛṣabha = the bull/cow (ṛṣabha/go)**, **Gāndhāra = the goat (chāga)**, **Madhyama = the krauñca heron**, **Pañcama = the cuckoo/kokila (in spring)**, **Dhaivata = the horse/frog (some lists the neigh)**, **Niṣāda = the elephant (gaja)**. (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* 28) [scriptural]
  - *Variant (later mnemonic tradition):* lists also attach each svara to a presiding sage/deity, a colour, a *cakra* and a *rasa* — the correspondences differ by text and are not standardized. [variant]

---

## III. The 22 śrutis — the microtonal substrate

- **Dvāviṃśati-śruti — the twenty-two microtones** — beneath the seven svaras the octave is divided into **22 *śruti*s** ("that which is heard"), the smallest perceptible pitch-intervals; the svaras are pitched upon particular śrutis. The canonical distribution gives **Sa = 4 śrutis, Ri = 3, Ga = 2, Ma = 4, Pa = 4, Dha = 3, Ni = 2** (4-3-2-4-4-3-2). (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* 28; *Dattilam*) [scholarly]
- **The two experimental vīṇās (sāraṇā-catuṣṭayī)** — Bharata demonstrates śruti empirically with **two identically-tuned vīṇās**: one held fixed (*dhruvā*), the other (*calā*) progressively lowered by removing one śruti at a time, proving the 22-fold division and the *grāma*-structure. The locus classicus of Indian acoustic experiment. (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* 28, the *sāraṇā* passages) [scholarly]

---

## IV. Grāma, Mūrcchanā and Jāti — the archaic scale-systems

- **The grāmas (parent scales)** — the *gāndharva* music of the *Nāṭya Śāstra* rests on **two (or three) *grāma*s** ("villages/collections" of notes): the **Ṣaḍja-grāma** and the **Madhyama-grāma**, differing by the placement of *Pañcama* (one śruti lower in the Madhyama-grāma). A third, the **Gāndhāra-grāma**, is named as belonging to the heavens/Gandharvas and lost to earth. (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* 28; *Dattilam*) [scriptural for the Gāndhāra-grāma]
- **Mūrcchanā** — the **seven modal rotations** of each grāma: starting the scale successively from each of its seven svaras yields **7 × 2 = 14 mūrcchanās** (the ancestor of the modal/scale concept and, distantly, of the *that/mela*). (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* 28; *Saṅgīta-ratnākara*)
- **Jāti — the melodic "mother-classes"** — the **18 *jāti*s** (7 *śuddha* "pure" + 11 *vikṛta* "modified") are the rule-bound melodic types of gāndharva music, each defined by *aṃśa* (predominant note), *graha* (initial), *nyāsa* (final), ambitus and characteristic phrases. The **jāti is the direct conceptual ancestor of the rāga**. (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* 28–29; *Dattilam*) [scholarly]

---

## V. The emergence of Rāga

- **Rāga — the melodic framework** — *rāga* ("that which colours/delights the mind") is the central concept of later Indian music: not a mere scale but a **melodic personality** — an ascent/descent (*ārohaṇa/avarohaṇa*), a hierarchy of notes (*vādī/saṃvādī* sonant/consonant), characteristic phrases (*pakaḍ/svara-saṃcāra*) and a *bhāva/rasa* — within which a musician improvises.
- **Bṛhaddeśī of Mataṅga — the locus classicus** — the **first surviving text to define and name *rāga*** is Mataṅga Muni's ***Bṛhaddeśī*** (c. 6th–8th c. CE), which gives the famous etymology *"raṅjayati iti rāgaḥ"* ("that which colours [the mind] is rāga") and shifts the centre of theory from *jāti* to *rāga*, formalizing the **deśī** (regional) melodic types. (Source: Mataṅga, *Bṛhaddeśī*) [scholarly]
- **Rasa and time associations** — each rāga is tied to a **rasa/mood** and, especially in the later Hindustani system, to a **time of day or season** (*samaya/ṛtu*): morning, evening, night and seasonal rāgas (e.g. the monsoon **Megh/Malhār**, the spring **Vasanta/Hindōḷa**). The **rāgamālā** painting tradition further personifies rāgas as deities and lovers, with the **rāga/rāgiṇī** (masculine/feminine) family-scheme. (Source: later Hindustani lakṣaṇa tradition; rāgamālā corpus) [regional/late text]
  - *Variant (rāga–rāgiṇī systems):* the grouping of rāgas into **6 male rāgas each with wives (rāgiṇīs) and sons (putra-rāgas)** is itself non-standard — the **Hanumat**, **Śiva/Someśvara**, **Bharata** and **Kallinātha** mata (schools) give different families. [disputed]

---

## VI. Tāla and Laya — the rhythmic system

- **Tāla — the metric cycle** — *tāla* ("clapping; measure") is the cyclic time-framework, counted in *mātrā*s (beats) grouped into *aṅga*s and marked by claps, waves and finger-counts; the cycle returns to the emphatic **sama** (the first beat). The archaic system used *mārga-tāla*s built on the **laghu/guru/pluta** durations; the modern systems use the **deśī-tālas**.
- **Laya — tempo** — the threefold tempo **vilambita (slow), madhya (medium), druta (fast)**, governing the density of the rhythmic surface. (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* 31–33; *Saṅgīta-ratnākara* 5, *Tāla-adhyāya*)
  - *Variant (the two living tāla-canons):* Carnatic codifies the **sapta-tāla** (seven tālas) generated with five *jāti*s into a **35-tāla** scheme (extended to 175 with *gati/naḍai*); Hindustani uses named *tāls* such as **Tīntāl (16), Jhaptāl (10), Ektāl (12), Rūpak (7)** marked by *bols* on the **tablā/pakhāwaj**. The two are not interchangeable. [scholarly]

---

## VII. Gamaka — ornamentation

- **Gamaka — the soul of melody** — *gamaka* ("shaking; ornament") is the **microtonal oscillation, slide and grace** applied to svaras, without which a rāga is held to be lifeless; the *Saṅgīta-ratnākara* enumerates **15 gamakas**, and Carnatic theory the **daśavidha-gamaka** ("ten kinds"). It is the feature that most distinguishes Indian melody from fixed-pitch systems. (Source: *Saṅgīta-ratnākara* 3; later Carnatic lakṣaṇa) [scholarly]

---

## VIII. Mārga vs. Deśī — the great divide

- **Mārga ("the path")** — the ancient, pan-Indian, śāstra-codified **gāndharva** music of the *Nāṭya Śāstra* and *Dattilam* — believed pleasing to the gods, governed by jāti and grāma, ritually and cosmically efficacious. [scholarly]
- **Deśī ("of the country/region")** — the **regional, current, ever-changing** practical music of human enjoyment, the domain of **rāga**, theorized first by Mataṅga. The whole later tradition is, in this sense, *deśī*. (Source: Mataṅga, *Bṛhaddeśī*; Śārṅgadeva, *Saṅgīta-ratnākara*) — the same mārga/deśī axis structures dance in [`natya-shastra-and-the-classical-arts.md`](natya-shastra-and-the-classical-arts.md).

## IX. The classifying frameworks — Melakarta and Thāṭ

- **The bifurcation of the tradition** — after the *Saṅgīta-ratnākara* (the **last text authoritative for both** northern and southern music), the streams diverge into **Hindustani** (North, with Perso-Arabic contact) and **Carnatic** (South, conservative of the Sanskrit theory). [scholarly]
- **Svaramelakalānidhi (Rāmāmātya, 1550)** — at the **Vijayanagara** court, Rāmāmātya's ***Svaramelakalānidhi*** introduces the **mela** ("group") as a *parent-scale* classifying device, organizing rāgas under a set of generative scales — the seed of the melakarta system. (Source: Rāmāmātya, *Svaramelakalānidhi*) [scholarly]
- **The 72 Melakarta (Veṅkaṭamakhin, 1660)** — Veṅkaṭamakhin's ***Caturdaṇḍī-prakāśikā*** establishes the **complete, mathematically exhaustive 72-melakarta system**: by permuting the variable forms of Ri/Ga/Ma/Dha/Ni, exactly **72 parent (*janaka*) scales** are derived, each generating "child" (*janya*) rāgas; the scheme is mapped by the **kaṭapayādi** mnemonic so a rāga's name encodes its mela-number. The foundation of all modern Carnatic music. (Source: Veṅkaṭamakhin, *Caturdaṇḍī-prakāśikā*) [scholarly]
- **The 10 Thāṭ (Bhātkhaṇḍe, early 20th c.)** — Hindustani music groups its rāgas under **ten *thāṭ*s** (parent-scales: **Bilāval, Kalyāṇ, Khamāj, Bhairav, Pūrvī, Mārvā, Kāfī, Āsāvarī, Bhairavī, Tōḍī**), the classification fixed by **V. N. Bhātkhaṇḍe** — a deliberately practical ten against the South's exhaustive seventy-two. (Source: Bhātkhaṇḍe, *Hindustani Sangeet Paddhati*) [scholarly]

## X. The theory-text lineage

- **Nāṭya Śāstra (Bharata, c. 200 BCE–200 CE), chs. 28–33** — the *svara*, *śruti*, *grāma*, *mūrcchanā*, *jāti*, instrument (*vādya*) and *tāla* chapters; the fountainhead. (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra*)
- **Dattilam (Dattila, c. 1st–4th c. CE)** — a concise companion to NŚ on *gāndharva*: śruti, svara, grāma, mūrcchanā, jāti and tāna. (Source: *Dattilam*)
- **Bṛhaddeśī (Mataṅga, c. 6th–8th c.)** — the *rāga*-defining text; the mārga→deśī turn. (Source: *Bṛhaddeśī*)
- **Saṅgīta-ratnākara (Śārṅgadeva, 13th c.)** — the "Ocean of Music," composed at the **Yādava** court of Devagiri; the seven-chapter synthesis (svara, rāga, prakīrṇaka, prabandha, tāla, vādya, nartana) authoritative for both later traditions. (Source: *Saṅgīta-ratnākara*) — fuller note in [`natya-shastra-and-the-classical-arts.md`](natya-shastra-and-the-classical-arts.md).
- **Svaramelakalānidhi (Rāmāmātya, 1550)** and **Caturdaṇḍī-prakāśikā (Veṅkaṭamakhin, 1660)** — the Vijayanagara/Tanjore mela-texts that build the Carnatic system. (Source: as above) — these and the rest of the corpus are indexed in [`../90-literature-corpus/README.md`](../90-literature-corpus/README.md).
