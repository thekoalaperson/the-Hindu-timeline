# Sacred Flora & Fauna

> Sits in [`09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/`](README.md) — the **living sacred objects**: the plants and animals the tradition treats as divine bodies, deity-attributes, and ritual instruments. A sacred plant or animal is at once a **botanical/zoological species**, an **earthly embodiment of a deity** (Tulsī *is* a goddess; the cow *is* Kāmadhenu's body), and a **ritual requisite** (Bilva-leaf for Śiva, Darbha-grass for *yajña*).
> Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md). Deities they embody → [`02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/); the animals that are *mounts* → [`vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md`](vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md); the serpents as a class → [`../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md`](../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md); origin-events (Samudra Manthana) → [`../04-deep-dives/`](../04-deep-dives/).

This file catalogs (I) the **sacred plants & trees** (Tulsī, Bilva, Ashvattha, Vaṭa, Padma, Darbha/Kuśa, Soma, and the wider *vṛkṣa* roster); and (II) the **sacred animals** (cow, Nandi, Garuḍa, Hanumān & the vānaras, Jaṭāyu & Sampāti, Kāliya & serpents, the elephant Gajendra/Airāvata, Jāmbavān the bear, and the minor sacred fauna). Per repo policy, **every variant origin-myth, alternate name, source-text attribution and count is cataloged to source**. Several rosters here are explicitly **open-ended** — the Vedic, epic, Purāṇic and folk strata each add sacred species.

## I. Sacred Flora — Plants & Trees (*vṛkṣa, oṣadhi*)

### The premier worship-plants

- **Tulsī / Tulasī** (holy basil, *Ocimum tenuiflorum*) — the most sacred plant of Vaiṣṇavism, worshipped as the **goddess Tulasī/Vṛndā**, an *avatāra* of **[Lakṣmī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)** and beloved of **[Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)** / Kṛṣṇa / Viṭhobā. A Tulsī plant on a raised **Tulsī-vṛndāvana** altar stands in the courtyard of devout Vaiṣṇava homes; her leaves are obligatory in Viṣṇu-pūjā and float in *caraṇāmṛta*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Tulasi in Hinduism*; Dolls of India)
  - *Variant — the Vṛndā/Jālandhara myth (Padma, Skanda, Śiva Purāṇas):* **Vṛndā**, the chaste wife of the *asura* **Jālandhara**, made him invincible by her *pātivratya*; Viṣṇu broke it by assuming her husband's form, letting Śiva slay Jālandhara. The heartbroken Vṛndā **cursed Viṣṇu to become stone** (whence the **Śālagrāma** ammonite-stones of the Gaṇḍakī river) and immolated herself; from her arose the Tulsī plant, which Viṣṇu vowed to wed each year. (Source: Wikipedia, *Tulasi*; rrudraksha.com)
  - *Variant — the Śaṅkhacūḍa myth (Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa):* here **Tulasī** does penance to win Viṣṇu, is first married to the *asura* **Śaṅkhacūḍa**; after Śiva slays him, Viṣṇu decrees her body becomes the **Gaṇḍakī river** and her hair the **Tulsī plant**. (Source: WebFetch, Wikipedia, *Tulasi in Hinduism*) [text-variant]
  - *Ritual — Tulasī Vivāha:* the ceremonial **marriage of Tulsī to Viṣṇu/Śālagrāma**, performed Prabodhinī Ekādaśī → Kārttika Pūrṇimā, ending the four-month *Cāturmāsya* and opening the wedding season. (Source: Wikipedia, *Tulasi*)
- **Bilva / Bēl** (Bengal quince, *Aegle marmelos*) — the sacred tree of **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)**, found in/around every Śiva temple; the **trifoliate Bilva-leaf** (*bilvapatra*) is Śiva's supreme offering, its three leaflets read as **Brahmā–Viṣṇu–Maheśa**, the three *guṇas*, or Śiva's three eyes/the trident. Offering it on a *liṅga*, especially on **Mahā-Śivarātri**, is held to wash away sin. (Source: Dolls of India, *Tulsi and Bilva*; LinkedIn/Shintre)
  - *Variant:* the tree is also linked to **[Lakṣmī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)** (said to be born of/reside in it in some Purāṇic strands), making Bilva a rare Śaiva-Śākta crossover plant. (Source: Devshoppe, *Sacred Trees*) [folk]
- **Durvā / Dūrvā grass** (Bermuda grass, *Cynodon dactylon*) — the sacred grass of **[Gaṇeśa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)**, offered in *durvāṣṭamī* (21 blades) pūjā; said to have absorbed cooling *amṛta* during the [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/). Completes the triad **Tulsī (Viṣṇu) – Bilva (Śiva) – Durvā (Gaṇeśa)** of deity-favoured plants. (Source: LinkedIn/Shintre, *Tulsi, Bilva, Durva*)

### The cosmic trees

- **Ashvattha / Pīpal / Peepal** (sacred fig, *Ficus religiosa*) — the holiest tree, the **cosmic world-tree**. The *Bhagavad Gītā* (15.1) describes the **Aśvattha with roots above and branches below** — the manifest cosmos rooted in Brahman. Tradition lodges **Viṣṇu in its roots, Brahmā in its trunk, Śiva in its leaves**; Kṛṣṇa says in the Gītā "among trees I am the Aśvattha." The Buddha attained enlightenment under it (the **Bodhi tree**). Circumambulated, never cut. (Source: ArtFactory; GreenStories, *Sacred Trees*)
  - *Variant — Akṣaya-vaṭa:* the "**imperishable banyan**" at **Prayāga** (and at Gayā, Kurukṣetra) survives the cosmic dissolution; **Mārkaṇḍeya** saw the infant Kṛṣṇa/Viṣṇu on its leaf floating the *pralaya*-waters. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sacred tree*; Ayurvedum) [overlaps Vaṭa below]
- **Vaṭa / Banyan** (*Ficus benghalensis*) — the **immortality-tree**, its aerial roots and endless spread symbolising longevity, the cosmos, and the unending Trimūrti; sacred to **Yama** and to **Sāvitrī** (women fast and tie threads round it on **Vaṭa-Sāvitrī** for husbands' long life). Sages teach beneath it; **Dakṣiṇāmūrti-Śiva** (the silent guru) sits under a banyan. (Source: ArtFactory; Devshoppe)
- **The Pañcavaṭī** (five sacred trees grouped in temple/hermitage groves) — *standard set:* **Vaṭa** (banyan), **Aśvattha** (pīpal), **Bilva**, **Āmalakī** (Indian gooseberry/āmla), and **Aśoka** (*Saraca asoca*). (Source: Wikipedia, *Sacred tree*)
  - *Variant (list-membership):* expanded lists swap in **Udumbara** (cluster fig, *Ficus racemosa*), **Nimba** (neem, sacred to Śītalā/Māriyamman), and **Śamī** (*Prosopis*, worshipped on Vijayadaśamī — the Pāṇḍavas hid their arms in a Śamī during exile). The roster is **open-ended**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sacred tree*)
- **Kalpavṛkṣa & Pārijāta** — the **wish-fulfilling celestial trees** of Svarga, churned from the [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/); the **Pārijāta** (night-jasmine, coral-tree) was carried off from Indra's heaven by **Kṛṣṇa** for Satyabhāmā. (Cross-link: catalogued as treasures in [`divine-objects-and-treasures.md`](divine-objects-and-treasures.md).) (Source: Wikipedia, *List of mythological objects*)

### The water-flowers

- **Padma / Kamala** (the lotus, *Nelumbo nucifera*) — the supreme symbol-flower of **purity, divinity, fortune, and detached transcendence** ("rooted in mud, untouched by water"). The **cosmic lotus** rises from Viṣṇu's navel bearing **[Brahmā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)** the creator (*Padmanābha*); **[Lakṣmī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)** is **Padmā/Kamalā**, seated/standing on a lotus; **Sarasvatī** and most deities are lotus-throned (*padmāsana*). The **white lotus** ranks topmost among sacred plants. (Source: Ayurvedum, *Sacred Plants*; Wisdomlib)
- **Nīlotpala / Kumuda** (the blue/white water-lily) — held by goddesses (Tārā, Durgā) and a frequent Devī attribute alongside the red/pink padma. (Source: Wisdomlib, *Sacred plant*) [iconographic]

### The ritual grasses & the Soma

- **Darbha / Kuśa / Pavitra** (*Desmostachya bipinnata*) — the indispensable **ritual grass** of Vedic *yajña*. A **purifier** that "wards off impurity," it forms the *barhis* (sacrificial seat-mat), is twisted into the **pavitra ring** worn on the priest's right ring-finger during *homa* and *tarpaṇa*, and is laid in *śrāddha* rites for the ancestors; the Vedas hold **Darbha and water equal** in purifying power. Said to have sprung where *amṛta*-drops fell at the churning, or from Viṣṇu's Kūrma-hair. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kusha grass*; Ayurvedum)
  - *Variant (naming):* "**Kuśa**," "**Darbha**," and "**Pavitram**" are used interchangeably for the same grass; some texts distinguish Kuśa (the tip) from Darbha (the blade). (Source: Wikipedia, *Kusha grass*)
- **Soma / Sauma** (the pressed plant-juice deified as the god **Soma/Candra**) — the central **Vedic entheogen-oblation**, pressed between stones, filtered through wool, mixed with milk and offered/drunk in the *Soma-yajña*; "king of plants," carrier of immortality, later identified with the **moon**. (Source: Vedanet, *The Secret of the Soma Plant*)
  - *Variant (botanical identity) [disputed]:* the original Soma species is **lost/unknown** — candidates proposed include **Ephedra**, **Sarcostemma/Cynanchum** (the modern ritual *Somalatā* leafless creeper), the **Amanita muscaria** mushroom (Wasson), Syrian rue (*Peganum harmala*), and others; the *Atharvaveda* names **five chief herbs** of which **Soma is best** (with barley, darbha, and others). The identity is **open-ended**. (Source: Vedanet; Maalbro, *5 Sacred Plants*) [scholarly][disputed]
- **Bhāṅg / Vijayā** (cannabis) — named among the Atharvaveda's **five sacred plants**; associated with **Śiva** (the ascetic intoxicant) and consumed ritually at **Mahā-Śivarātri** and **Holi**. (Source: Maalbro; LinkedIn, *Cannabis in Atharva Veda*) [folk/textual]

### Other sacred flora (open-ended)

- **Āmalakī / Āmla** (*Phyllanthus emblica*) — sacred to **Viṣṇu/Dhanvantari**; worshipped on **Āmalakā Ekādaśī**; the Dhātrī-tree of health. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sacred tree*) [Vaiṣṇava]
- **Nimba / Neem** — sacred to the smallpox/heat goddesses **Śītalā / Māriyamman**, planted at their shrines. (Source: Devshoppe) [folk]
- **Aśoka** — the "sorrowless" tree (*Saraca asoca*) under which **Sītā** was held in Rāvaṇa's **Aśoka-vāṭikā**; sacred to Kāmadeva and fertility. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sacred tree*; [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/))
- **Sañjīvanī** — the legendary **resurrection-herb** of the [Rāmāyaṇa]( ../04-deep-dives/), whose mountain (Droṇagiri) Hanumān carried whole; an **open-ended/unidentified** species. (Source: Wikipedia, *List of mythological objects*) [also in [`divine-objects-and-treasures.md`](divine-objects-and-treasures.md)]
- **Rudrākṣa & Tulsī beads** — seed-rosaries (*mālā*) sacred respectively to Śiva and Viṣṇu; catalogued as symbols in [`README.md`](README.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Category:Hindu symbols*)

## II. Sacred Fauna — Animals (*paśu, mṛga, pakṣin*)

### The cow & the bull

- **The cow (*go, gāu*) — *Go-mātā*** — the supreme sacred animal, venerated as the earthly embodiment of **Kāmadhenu/Surabhi**; symbol of non-violence (*ahiṃsā*), motherhood, the earth, and selfless giving (its five products, the *pañcagavya*, purify). Cow-protection (*go-rakṣā*) and cow-gift (*go-dāna*) are cardinal merits. (Source: A-Z Animals, *Sacred Status of Cows*; earthstoriez)
  - **Kāmadhenu / Surabhi** — the **divine mother-cow of plenty** who grants every desire; depicted as a white cow with a **woman's head & breasts, bird's wings, and a peacock's tail**, or as a cow **containing all the gods in her body**. Churned from (or pre-existing the) [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/); kept by the sage **Vasiṣṭha** (her seizure by king **Viśvāmitra** sparks their famous feud) and by **Jamadagni** (Kārtavīrya's theft of her triggers Paraśurāma's vengeance). Cross-link: the wish-cow as treasure in [`divine-objects-and-treasures.md`](divine-objects-and-treasures.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Kamadhenu*; Nirvaan Arts)
    - *Variant (her children):* her calf **Nandinī** (also a wish-cow, kept by Vasiṣṭha) and the **four/many directional cows**; **Nandi the bull** is reckoned **her descendant**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kamadhenu*; NewsGuru)
    - *Variant (origin) [disputed]:* born of the churning (*Bhāgavata*/*Mahābhārata*) vs. created from Brahmā/Dakṣa or from Prajāpati's mouth in other texts. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kamadhenu*)
- **Nandi (the bull, *Vṛṣabha*)** — the white bull of **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)**, his **vāhana, gate-guardian, and gaṇa-chief**; couchant before every Śiva shrine, eternally gazing at the *liṅga*; whispering one's wish in Nandi's ear conveys it to Śiva. Embodies **strength, fertility, *dharma*, and unwavering *bhakti***. Reckoned a descendant of Kāmadhenu. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kamadhenu*; NewsGuru) — full treatment in [`vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md`](vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md).
  - *Variant:* older strata distinguish **Nandīśvara/Nandikeśvara**, the anthropomorphic gaṇa, from the bull he merged with. (Source: Wikipedia, *Nandi*) [scholarly]

### The birds of Viṣṇu — Garuḍa and the vultures

- **Garuḍa (the eagle/kite-man)** — vāhana and banner (*Garuḍa-dhvaja*) of **[Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)**, **king of birds**, deathless sworn enemy of the **Nāgas/serpents**. Son of sage **Kaśyapa** and **Vinatā**; won *amṛta* from Indra to ransom his enslaved mother, whence Viṣṇu granted him immortality. The mere recitation of his name (the *Garuḍa-mantra*) neutralises snake-venom. (Source: World History Encyclopedia, *Garuda*; Wikipedia) — see [`vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md`](vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md) and [`../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md`](../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md).
  - *Variant (his sons):* in one tradition Garuḍa (with **Unnati/Vinatā**) fathers **Sampāti and Jaṭāyu**; the *Rāmāyaṇa* mainstream instead makes them sons of **Aruṇa** (Garuḍa's brother, Sūrya's charioteer). (Source: WebSearch, Wikipedia, *Sampati*; apnisanskriti) [genealogy-variant]
- **Jaṭāyu** — the noble **vulture/eagle king**, friend of King **Daśaratha**; he fought **Rāvaṇa** to save the abducted **Sītā**, had his wings severed, and died telling **Rāma** the direction of her captor — Rāma performed his funerary rites as for a father. The selfless death-for-dharma exemplar. (Source: TemplePurohit, *Jatayu and Sampati*; [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/))
- **Sampāti** — Jaṭāyu's **elder brother, king of vultures**; in youth he shielded Jaṭāyu from the sun and **lost his wings/feathers** (burnt). Wingless on the shore, he told the search-party that Sītā was in **Laṅkā** — the clue that launched Hanumān's ocean-leap; his wings regrew on completing the dharma. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sampati*; TemplePurohit)
  - *Variant:* the brothers are sons of **Aruṇa** (and his wife **Śyenī**) — Aruṇa being the deity of dawn and Garuḍa's brother — in the prevailing *Rāmāyaṇa* genealogy. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sampati*)

### The monkeys (vānaras) & the bear

- **Hanumān** — the **monkey-god** (*vānara*), son of the wind-god **[Vāyu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)** (and **Añjanā**), held a partial *aṃśa* of **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)** (*Rudrāvatāra*); supreme devotee (*parama-bhakta*) of **Rāma**, *cirajīvī* (immortal), embodiment of strength, celibacy, devotion and selfless service. Leapt the ocean to Laṅkā, found Sītā, carried the **Sañjīvanī** mountain to revive Lakṣmaṇa. Worshipped across India; sindūr-smeared. (Source: A-Z Animals; [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/))
- **The vānaras (the monkey host of the Rāmāyaṇa)** — the divinely-fathered ape-army that built **Rāma's bridge** (*Setu*) and stormed Laṅkā:
  - **Sugrīva** — vānara king of **Kiṣkindhā**, son of **Sūrya**; Rāma's ally after Rāma slew his usurping brother **Vālin**. (Source: [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/))
  - **Vālin / Bāli** — Sugrīva's mighty brother, son of **Indra**, slain by Rāma's arrow. (Source: [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/))
  - **Aṅgada** — Vālin's son, the boy-prince envoy to Rāvaṇa's court. (Source: [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/))
  - **Nala & Nīla** — the engineer-vānaras (sons of Viśvakarmā/Agni) who **architected the floating bridge**. (Source: [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/))
  - *Variant (count):* the host is reckoned in the **crores**; named leaders vary by recension, but Hanumān, Sugrīva, Aṅgada, Nala, Nīla, Tāra, Gavākṣa, Gavāya, Suṣeṇa (the vānara physician) recur. The roster is **open-ended**. (Source: [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/))
- **Jāmbavān / Jāmbavanta (the bear-king, *Ṛkṣarāja*)** — the ancient **king of the bears** (an Asiatic/sloth bear), born of Brahmā, a *cirajīvī* who served across yugas; in the *Rāmāyaṇa* he counsels and reminds **Hanumān** of his powers before the ocean-leap. In the **Kṛṣṇa cycle** he fights Kṛṣṇa for **21 days** over the **Syamantaka jewel** (recognising him as Rāma's return) and gives his daughter **Jāmbavatī** to Kṛṣṇa as wife. (Source: Wikipedia/Alchetron, *List of legendary creatures*; WebSearch) — the Syamantaka gem → [`divine-objects-and-treasures.md`](divine-objects-and-treasures.md).

### The serpents

- **Kāliya** — the dreaded **many-hooded venomous Nāga** who poisoned the **Yamunā** at Vṛndāvana; he had fled there from **Ramaṇaka-dvīpa** fearing **Garuḍa**. The boy **Kṛṣṇa** leapt into the river, was coiled, then **danced on Kāliya's hoods** (the *Kāliya-mardana / Kāliya-daman*) until the serpent submitted; spared at the plea of his Nāgapatnīs, he departed with Kṛṣṇa's footprint on his hood as protection from Garuḍa. (Source: tell-a-tale, *Kaliya Daman*; Wikipedia) — serpent-class roster in [`../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md`](../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md).
- **Śeṣa / Ananta / Ādiśeṣa** — the **thousand-hooded cosmic serpent** on whom Viṣṇu reclines (**Śeṣaśāyī**) across the *pralaya*-ocean; he bears the earth and is reborn as **Lakṣmaṇa** and **Balarāma**. (Source: [`../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md`](../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md))
- **Vāsuki** — the Nāga-king who served as the **churning-rope** of [Mt Mandara](../04-deep-dives/) at the Samudra Manthana; garlands Śiva's neck. (Source: [`../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md`](../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md))
- *Note:* living-snake worship (**Nāga-pañcamī**), the cobra round Śiva's neck, and the Nāga-stones at temple-roots make the serpent both **feared poison-power and revered fertility/water-guardian**. Full theology and roster → [`../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md`](../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *List of legendary creatures*)

### The elephant

- **Gajendra (the elephant-king) & Gajendra-mokṣa** — the famous **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** parable: the elephant-king **Gajendra**, seized in a lake by the crocodile **Hūhū/Grāha**, struggled for **a thousand years** until, surrendering utterly, he raised a lotus and cried to Viṣṇu — who flew in on **Garuḍa**, slew the crocodile with the **Sudarśana cakra**, and liberated him (*mokṣa*). The archetype of total surrender (*prapatti/śaraṇāgati*). (Source: A-Z Animals; NewsGuru)
  - *Variant:* in the back-story Gajendra was a king (Indradyumna) and the crocodile a *gandharva* (Hūhū), both cursed into animal form and freed by the encounter. (Source: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.2–8.4)
- **Airāvata** — the four-tusked **white elephant of [Indra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)**, churned from the [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/); the cloud-and-rain elephant, progenitor of the **Aṣṭa-diggaja** (the eight elephants of the directions). Full treatment in [`vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md`](vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md). (Source: Wikipedia; NewsGuru)
- **[Gaṇeśa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)** — the **elephant-headed god** himself sanctifies the species; the elephant embodies wisdom, memory, auspicious beginnings, and obstacle-removal. (Source: A-Z Animals)

### Other sacred fauna (open-ended)

- **Haṃsa (swan/goose)** — the **paramahaṃsa** that separates milk from water (truth from untruth); mount of Brahmā & Sarasvatī. (Source: [`vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md`](vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md))
- **Mayūra (peacock)** — Kārttikeya's mount; serpent-devourer, splendour-and-vanity-conqueror. (Source: [`vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md`](vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md))
- **Mūṣika (mouse/rat)** — Gaṇeśa's mount; conquered desire. (Source: [`vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md`](vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md))
- **Vānara, Ṛkṣa, Vyāghra/Siṃha, Aśva (Uccaiḥśravas), Makara, Yāli/Vyāla** — the temple-bestiary of mount, guardian and emblem animals catalogued as vāhanas and beings respectively in [`vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md`](vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md) and [`../08-beings-and-bestiary/`](../08-beings-and-bestiary/). The list of locally-sacred animals (the **monkey, cow, elephant** trinity plus regional serpents, peacocks, eagles) is **open-ended**. (Source: A-Z Animals; NewsGuru)
