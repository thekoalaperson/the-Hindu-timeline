# Divine Objects & Treasures

> Sits in [`09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/`](README.md) — the **non-weapon objects of power**: wish-granters (cow, tree, jewel), the named gems and vessels, the conches, and the loot of the churning.
> Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md). Origin-event for most of these: the **[Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)**.

This file catalogs the *relics and treasures* — objects that are not primarily weapons (those live in [`divine-weapons-astras-and-shastras.md`](divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md)) and are not living mounts (those live in [`vahanas-divine-mounts.md`](vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md)). The spine is the triad of **wish-fulfillers** (*Kāmadhenu* / *Kalpavṛkṣa* / *Cintāmaṇi*), the **named gems** (*Kaustubha*, *Syamantaka*), the **conches**, and the **fourteen ratnas** of the churning. Per repo policy, **every variant name, count, owner-chain and maker is cataloged to source**; several rosters here are explicitly **open-ended** (each Purāṇa adds local relics).

## I. The Three Wish-Fulfillers (the *cintāmaṇi*-triad)

A recurring symbolic triad — the **cow**, the **tree**, and the **jewel** — each granting whatever its possessor desires. (Source: The Sanatana Project, *Kalpavriksha, Kamadhenu & Chintamani*)

### Kāmadhenu / Surabhi — the wish-cow

- **Kāmadhenu** ("cow of wishes") - The divine *cow of plenty*, mother of all cattle, who yields her owner anything desired. Most often emerges from the **[Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)** (ratna #2) and is given to the **Saptarṣi** (seven sages); passes into the keeping of **Vasiṣṭha**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kamadhenu*; *Bhagavata Purana* 8.8)
- **Surabhi** ("the fragrant/sweet-smelling one") - The most common alternate name; in many texts Surabhi is the *mother* and Kāmadhenu a title or daughter, so the relationship between names **varies by text**. Daughter of **Dakṣa** (or of Kaśyapa and Krodhavaśā in other genealogies); mother of cows and, in some lists, of buffaloes and the eleven Rudras' cattle. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kamadhenu*)
  - *Variant (Mahabharata / Puranic):* **Surabhi, Nandinī, and Śabalā** are given as her *different names* — i.e. one cow under three names. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kamadhenu*)
  - *Variant (other texts):* **Nandinī** is her *daughter* (a separate "cow of plenty"), and **Śabalā/Sabala** another distinct calf — i.e. a lineage of cows, not one. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kamadhenu*; Dolls of India, *Kamadhenu*)
- **Nandinī** - The wish-cow (mother or daughter of Surabhi) who resides with sage **Vasiṣṭha**; her abduction-attempt by king **Viśvāmitra** (then Kauśika) triggers the great Vasiṣṭha–Viśvāmitra feud — when seized, Nandinī conjures whole armies (Pahlavas, Śakas, Yavanas, Kāmbojas) to rout the king, setting Viśvāmitra on the path to brahmin-hood. See [Viśvāmitra & Vasiṣṭha](../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Kamadhenu*; *Ramayana*, Bāla Kāṇḍa; *Mahabharata*, Ādi Parva)
  - *Variant:* in the Vasiṣṭha-vs-**Kārtavīrya Arjuna** episode, the cow that conjures warriors and is coveted/seized is named **Kāmadhenu/Surabhi/Nandinī** depending on the recension — the same "cow conjures armies" motif is reused. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kamadhenu*)
- **Iconography** - Depicted as a white cow with a woman's head and breasts, eagle wings, and a peacock's tail; her body is said to house all the gods. (Source: Dolls of India, *Kamadhenu*) [folk/iconographic]

### Kalpavṛkṣa / Kalpataru — the wish-tree

- **Kalpavṛkṣa** ("wish-fulfilling tree," also **Kalpataru, Kalpadruma, Kalpapādapa**) - The divine tree of Indra's heaven (**Svarga**) that grants whatever is asked of it; emerges at the **[Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)** alongside Kāmadhenu, and **Indra** bears it to his paradise. Rooted in **Nandana-vana** (Indra's pleasure-garden). Shared across **Hinduism, Jainism, Buddhism, and Sikhism**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kalpavriksha*; *Bhagavata Purana* 8.8)
  - *Variant (the five kalpa-trees, *pañca-vṛkṣa*):* some lists give **five** wish-trees of Svarga — **Mandāra, Pārijāta, Santāna, Kalpavṛkṣa, and Haricandana**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kalpavriksha*) [late text]
- **Pārijāta** - Often **identified with** the Kalpavṛkṣa, or counted as one of the five; the *coral-tree* with never-fading, ever-fragrant night-blooms. Described as having **roots of gold, a silver trunk, lapis-lazuli boughs, coral leaves, pearl flowers, gemstone buds, and diamond fruit**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kalpavriksha*)
  - **Pārijātaharaṇa** (the stealing of the Pārijāta) - **Kṛṣṇa** uproots the Pārijāta from Indra's heaven and plants it in **Satyabhāmā's** courtyard (so its flowers fall in co-wife **Rukmiṇī's** yard), provoking a brief war with Indra. (Source: *Hari-vamśa* / *Bhagavata Purana* 10; Wikipedia, *Parijata*) [Krishna cycle]
  - *Terrestrial counterpart:* identified with the Indian coral tree (*Erythrina indica*) or the night-jasmine (*Nyctanthes arbor-tristis*), but iconographically drawn as a magnolia/frangipani (*campaka*). (Source: Wikipedia, *Kalpavriksha*)

### Cintāmaṇi — the wish-jewel

- **Cintāmaṇi** ("thought-gem," the wish-granting jewel) - The jewel that fulfills its bearer's every thought/desire; in some accounts found **at the root of the Kalpavṛkṣa**. Associated with **Viṣṇu** (on his person) and with the **Nāga-loka** / serpent-king treasuries, and with the goddess **Lakṣmī**. Shared with Buddhism (the *cintāmaṇi* of Avalokiteśvara/the *cakravartin*) and Jainism. (Source: The Sanatana Project; Enlightenment Thangka, *Cintāmaṇi*; Wikipedia, *Cintamani*)
  - *Variant:* the Cintāmaṇi is sometimes equated with, or said to be guarded by, the **Nāga** Vāsuki/Śeṣa, or with **Gaṇeśa** (the "Cintāmaṇi Vināyaka" of Theur); in tantra it is the jewel held by the great goddess. (Source: Wikipedia, *Cintamani*) [regional/tantric]

## II. The Named Gems (*maṇi/ratna*)

- **Kaustubha** - The supreme jewel worn by **Viṣṇu** on his chest; emerges from the **[Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)** (ratna #5) and Viṣṇu claims it. Paired iconographically with the **Śrīvatsa** curl and the **Vaijayantī** garland as his chest-marks; said to outshine all gems and to be the concentrated radiance of all jewels. (Source: *Bhagavata Purana* 8.8; *Vishnu Purana*; Wikipedia, *Kaustubha*)
  - *Variant:* in some Vaiṣṇava theology the Kaustubha represents the **jīva** (the collective souls) borne on the Lord's heart, while the Śrīvatsa represents *prakṛti* (primordial nature). (Source: Vaiṣṇava commentary tradition) [scholarly/theological]
- **Syamantaka** ("the brilliant/restrainer") - The **sun-gem** of the Kṛṣṇa cycle; originally the jewel of **Sūrya** (source of his brilliance), gifted to the Yādava king **Satrājit**. It yields **eight bhāras of gold daily** and wards off all calamity (drought, flood, famine) from the land that holds it — but only for the virtuous. (Source: Wikipedia, *Syamantaka*; *Vishnu Purana*; *Bhagavata Purana* 10.56)
  - **The Syamantaka affair** - Satrājit gives it to his brother **Prasena**, who is killed by a lion while hunting; the lion is slain by **Jāmbavān** (the bear-king of the [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/)), who takes the gem to his cave. **Kṛṣṇa**, falsely accused of theft, tracks and fights Jāmbavān for 28 days; Jāmbavān, recognizing Kṛṣṇa as Rāma/Viṣṇu, yields the gem **and** his daughter **Jāmbavatī** in marriage. Kṛṣṇa returns it to Satrājit, who gives Kṛṣṇa his daughter **Satyabhāmā**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Syamantaka*; *Bhagavata Purana* 10.56–57; Bhagavatam-katha)
  - *Note:* Kaustubha and Syamantaka are **distinct gems** — Kaustubha is Viṣṇu's eternal chest-jewel; Syamantaka is a worldly sun-gem of the Dvāraka era. (Source: Wikipedia, *Syamantaka*)

## III. Amṛta & the Vessel

- **Amṛta** ("non-death," the nectar of immortality) - The goal of the entire **[Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)**; conferring deathlessness on the **Devas** who drink it. Also called *Pīyūṣa, Sudhā*. The contested theft is resolved by Viṣṇu's **Mohinī** form. (Source: *Bhagavata Purana* 8.8–8.9; Wikipedia, *Amrita*)
- **The Kumbha / Kalaśa (the pot)** - The vessel of amṛta, borne up from the ocean by **Dhanvantari** (the divine physician, an aspect/avatar of Viṣṇu and patron of **Āyurveda**), who rises **last** of the ratnas. (Source: *Bhagavata Purana* 8.8)
  - **Kumbh Mela etiology** - In popular/Puranic tradition, drops from the *kumbha* fell at four sites during the deva–asura scramble — **Prayāg, Haridvāra, Ujjain, Nāsik** — origin of the **Kumbh Mela**; see [Sacred Geography](../06-sacred-geography/README.md). [largely medieval/folk — not in the early epic]

## IV. The Conches (*śaṅkha*)

The **śaṅkha** (conch) is both a divine attribute (one of Viṣṇu's four) and a war-trumpet; its sound is *Nāda-Brahma* and *Oṃ*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shankha*)

- **Pāñcajanya** - **Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa's** conch; one of Viṣṇu's four primary attributes. Its name is glossed as the union of the *pañca* (five) elements/senses. **Kṛṣṇa** sounds it to open the Kurukṣetra war (*Bhagavad-gītā* 1.15). (Source: Wikipedia, *Panchajanya*; [Bhagavad-gītā](../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md))
  - *Variant (Mahabharata origin):* Viṣṇu slew the demon **Pañcajana** on Mount Cakravān and seized the conch in which the demon dwelt — named after the demon. (Source: Wikipedia, *Panchajanya*; *Mahabharata*)
  - *Variant (Skanda Purana origin):* the demon **Pañcajana**, in whale-form, swallowed the son of Kṛṣṇa's guru **Sāndīpani**; Kṛṣṇa slew him and took the conch — which had **previously belonged to Varuṇa** — though the boy had to be recovered separately from Yama's realm. (Source: Wikipedia, *Panchajanya*; *Skanda Purana*)
- **Devadatta** ("god-given") - **Arjuna's** conch, sounded beside Kṛṣṇa's Pāñcajanya at the war's opening. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shankha*; *Bhagavad-gītā* 1.15)
- **The five Pāṇḍava conches** (full roster, *Bhagavad-gītā* 1.16):
  - **Anantavijaya** ("endless victory") - **Yudhiṣṭhira's** conch.
  - **Pauṇḍra / Pauṇḍra-Khaḍga** - **Bhīma's** conch.
  - **Devadatta** - **Arjuna's** conch (above).
  - **Sughoṣa** ("sweet-sounding") - **Nakula's** conch.
  - **Maṇipuṣpaka** ("jewel-flowered") - **Sahadeva's** conch.
  - (Source: *Bhagavad-gītā* 1.15–18; Wikipedia, *Panchajanya*; Bhagavan Bhakthi, *Conch names of the Pandavas*)

## V. The Fourteen Ratnas of the Churning (cross-roster)

The treasures of the **[Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)** — fully enumerated, with variant counts (9–14) and order, in that deep-dive's **§III**. Summary roster (the non-weapon objects of which appear above in detail):

- **Halāhala/Kālakūṭa** (world-poison; drunk by Śiva → *Nīlakaṇṭha*) · **Kāmadhenu/Surabhi** (§I) · **Uccaiḥśravas** (white horse → mounts) · **Airāvata** (white elephant → mounts) · **Kaustubha** (§II) · **Pārijāta/Kalpavṛkṣa** (§I) · **Apsarās** (Rambhā et al.) · **Lakṣmī** (re-emerges, weds Viṣṇu) · **Vāruṇī/Surā** (wine-goddess) · **Candra** (the Moon → Śiva's head) · and, by some lists, **Pāñcajanya** (§IV), **Śārṅga** (Viṣṇu's bow → weapons), **Jyeṣṭhā/Alakṣmī**, the **Tulasī** plant, and others — culminating in **Dhanvantari with the Amṛta-kumbha** (§III).
- *Variant on count:* the canonical **"14 ratnas"** is a **later harmonization** [scholarly]; individual texts list as few as **9**. See [Samudra Manthana §III](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Samudra Manthana*; *Vishnu Purana* 1.9)

## VI. Other Vessels, Garments & Relics

- **Akṣaya-pātra** ("inexhaustible vessel") - A copper bowl given by **Sūrya** to **Yudhiṣṭhira** during the Pāṇḍavas' forest exile (after his penance in the Gaṅgā, on sage **Dhaumya's** counsel); it yields **unlimited food each day until Draupadī takes her own meal**, after which it empties. Central to the **Durvāsā** test — when the irascible sage arrives after Draupadī has eaten, **Kṛṣṇa** eats a single clinging rice-grain from the empty pot and thereby sates Durvāsā and his thousand disciples at the river. (Source: Wikipedia, *Akshaya Patra*; *Mahabharata*, Vana Parva)
- **Kaustubha** & **Vaijayantī** - Viṣṇu's chest-jewel (§II) and his **Vaijayantī** ("garland of victory"), a never-fading five-row forest-flower garland; with the **Śrīvatsa** mark these form his standard chest-attributes. (Source: *Vishnu Purana*; Wikipedia, *Vaijayanti*)
- **Kavaca-Kuṇḍala** - **Karṇa's** invulnerable golden **armor (kavaca)** and **earrings (kuṇḍala)**, born fused to his body as the gift of his father **Sūrya**; Indra extracts them by guile to make Karṇa killable. (Source: *Mahabharata*, Vana/Karṇa Parvas; see [Karṇa](../04-deep-dives/karna.md)) [weapon-adjacent relic]
- **Sañjīvanī (mṛta-sañjīvanī)** - The mountain-herb of resurrection from the **[Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/)**; **Hanumān**, unable to identify it on **Droṇagiri/Gandhamādana**, carries the whole mountain to revive **Lakṣmaṇa**. (Source: *Ramayana*, Yuddha Kāṇḍa) [herb/relic — open-ended; many *divya-auṣadhi* in epic]
- **Puṣpaka Vimāna** - The self-moving flying chariot/palace; made by **Viśvakarmā**, owned **Brahmā → Kubera → Rāvaṇa** (seized), and finally flies **Rāma** home to Ayodhyā. (Source: *Ramayana*; Wikipedia, *Pushpaka Vimana*) [vehicle — catalogued here and under vāhanas]

> **Open-ended.** This roster is *not closed*: every Purāṇa, Sthala-purāṇa, and regional epic adds local relics (divine umbrellas, thrones, mirrors, drinking-bowls, the *cintāmaṇi*-rosaries of saints, etc.). Treat the above as the **pan-Hindu core**, not an exhaustive census.
