# Mehndi, Alaktaka and the Arts of Ritual Body-Adornment (Maṅgala-śṛṅgāra)

> Sits in [`09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/`](README.md) — the **living sacred body-art** of the Hindu household: the henna-stained hand, the lac-reddened foot, the painted forehead-mark, by which the auspicious body (especially the bride's) is turned into a *maṅgala* surface of beauty, blessing and married fortune (*saubhāgya*).
> Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md). The classical curriculum-anchor: the **cosmetic/decorative *kalās*** of Vātsyāyana (#6 *Viśeṣaka-cchedya*, #9 *Daśana-vasanāṅga-rāga*, #14 *Citra-yoga*, #20 *Bhūṣaṇa-yojana*) → [`sixty-four-kalas.md`](sixty-four-kalas.md); its floor-art twin (rangoli/kōlam, the other "drawn auspiciousness") → [`rangoli-kolam-floor-art.md`](rangoli-kolam-floor-art.md); the *tilaka*/svastika/lotus motifs it borrows → [`sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md`](sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md); the henna-plant, lac and turmeric it is made from → [`sacred-flora-and-fauna.md`](sacred-flora-and-fauna.md).

Where [`rangoli-kolam-floor-art.md`](rangoli-kolam-floor-art.md) treats the **drawing of the ground**, this file treats the parallel art of **drawing on the body**: *mehndi* (henna), *alaktaka/ālaktaka* (red lac dye), and the painted marks of the *ṣoḍaśa-śṛṅgāra* ("sixteen adornments"). All three are at once **classical fine arts** (catalogued among the 64 *kalās*), **daily/festival cosmetics**, and **bridal rites of auspiciousness**. Per repo policy, **every regional name, material and association is catalogued to source**; the tradition is **plural and not merged**, and the **scriptural-vs-historical "two clocks"** are kept distinct.

## I. The classical anchor — the cosmetic & decorative kalās

Vātsyāyana reckons body-adornment a cultivated art, listing several cosmetic/decorative skills among the **sixty-four kalās** (*Kāmasūtra* 1.3, *Vidyā-samuddeśa*; full roster → [`sixty-four-kalas.md`](sixty-four-kalas.md)):

- **Viśeṣaka-cchedya (#6)** — *the art of decorating the face/forehead with coloured marks*: cutting/stencilling *tilaka*, *patra-lekhā* (leaf-tracery) and the painted forehead-spot. The single closest classical anchor for painted body-ornament. (Source: *Kāmasūtra* 1.3) [scholarly] — sectarian marks → [`sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md`](sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md)
- **Daśana-vasanāṅga-rāga (#9)** — *colouring the teeth, garments, and the limbs (aṅga-rāga)*: the dyeing/painting of the body itself; the kalā under which **henna-staining and lac-reddening fall** as limb-colouring (*aṅga-rāga*). (Source: *Kāmasūtra* 1.3) [scholarly]
- **Citra-yoga (#14)** — *the mixing and applying of colours / pigment-admixtures*: the technical craft of preparing the paste and the dye. (Source: *Kāmasūtra* 1.3)
- **Bhūṣaṇa-yojana (#20)** — *applying and arranging ornaments*: the jewellery half of the adornment-complex into which the painted arts are set. (Source: *Kāmasūtra* 1.3)
- **Aṅga-rāga / aṅga-rāga-vidhi** — "body-paint, anointing of the limbs": the generic Sanskrit term (in the *Kāmasūtra* and later kāvya) for cosmetic painting/anointing of the body with coloured, scented pastes — the umbrella under which mehndi, alaktaka, candana (sandal) and kuṅkuma marks all sit. (Source: *Kāmasūtra*; classical kāvya usage) [scholarly]

## II. The ṣoḍaśa-śṛṅgāra — the sixteen adornments

- **Ṣoḍaśa-śṛṅgāra (षोडश शृंगार)** — the "**sixteen adornments**" of a woman, a traditional enumeration of the items of *maṅgala-śṛṅgāra* (auspicious bridal/married decoration); the **complete adorned state** by which a wife displays her *saubhāgya* (married good-fortune). The list varies by region and text but characteristically includes — *bindī/tilaka, sindūr* (vermilion in the hair-parting), *kājal* (collyrium), **mehndi** (henna on hands/feet), *kuṅkuma*, *mahāvar/āltā* (red foot-dye = **alaktaka**), bangles, necklace, ear- and nose-rings, girdle, anklets, toe-rings, hair-flowers, fine garments and perfume. (Source: ṣoḍaśa-śṛṅgāra tradition; folk/Purāṇic bridal lore) [folk][regional]
  - *Variant — number and membership:* the **"sixteen" is a fixed auspicious count, not a fixed list**; different regions and texts swap items (some count the *anga-rāga* paste, some the *tilaka*, some the eight ornaments + eight cosmetics). Treat the **complex, not the exact list, as canonical**. (Source: regional custom) [disputed]
- **Devotional grounding** — the sixteen adornments are read as the decoration befitting a goddess-bride and are applied in image-worship to **Pārvatī/Gaurī, Lakṣmī and Sītā**; *Gaurī-pūjā* and *Tīj/Hartālikā* rites by married women centre on donning the full śṛṅgāra for the husband's long life. (Source: vrata custom; *Tīj* lore) [folk][regional]

## III. Mehndi (henna) — the henna-painted body

- **Mehndi / mehendi (Hindi *mehndī*, from Skt *mendhikā*)** — body-art applied with a paste of dried, powdered **henna leaves** (*Lawsonia inermis*, Skt *mendhikā / madayantī*), staining the skin a red-brown; drawn in intricate patterns on the **hands, palms and feet**, the classic "auspicious painting of the body." (Source: Wikipedia, *Mehndi*; *Henna*) [scholarly] — the plant → [`sacred-flora-and-fauna.md`](sacred-flora-and-fauna.md)
- **Bridal mehndi** — the pre-wedding **Mehndi ceremony** (a *saṃskāra*-adjacent women's rite) in which elaborate henna is applied to the bride's hands and feet the night before the wedding; held intensely **auspicious**, a token of *saubhāgya*, fertility and the bond of marriage. (Source: Wikipedia, *Mehndi*; wedding custom) [folk]
  - *Folk belief — depth of colour:* the **darker the henna stains**, the deeper the love between the couple (or the more the mother-in-law's affection) — a near-universal North-Indian bridal saying. (Source: folk custom) [folk]
  - *Folk motif — the groom's name:* the groom's name (or initial) is hidden in the bride's mehndi for him to find — a playful wedding-night convention. (Source: folk custom) [folk]
- **Festival mehndi** — beyond weddings, henna is applied by women at **Karva Chauth, Tīj, Diwali, Bhai Dūj, Eid** and other festivals as a seasonal auspicious adornment. (Source: festival custom) [folk][regional]
- **Devotional association** — in folk imagination the henna-adorned hand is the bride's hand of **Sītā** before her wedding to Rāma, and of the *gopīs/Rādhā* adorning for Kṛṣṇa; *śṛṅgāra* of the divine couple is a standing devotional theme. (Source: folk/bhakti custom) [folk][late text]
- **Regional vocabulary** — the practice runs across the subcontinent under cognate names: **mehndi/mehendi** (Hindi-Urdu), **mehandi** (Bengali), **gōrintāku** (Telugu), **mayilāñci/maruthāni** (Tamil), **mailāñchi** (Malayalam); the Tamil/Malayalam terms derive from the henna-plant's local name. (Source: regional usage) [regional]

## IV. Alaktaka / āltā — the red lac foot-and-hand dye

- **Alaktaka (अलक्तक; also *yāvaka, lākṣā-rasa*)** — a **red dye made from lac** (*lākṣā*, the resin of the lac-insect) and/or safflower, used to **paint the soles of the feet, the toes, the edges of the hands and the fingertips** a bright red; the classical "red foot-dye" of literature, modern Hindi **āltā / mahāvar**, Bengali **ālta**. (Source: classical kāvya; Wikipedia, *Alta (dye)*) [scholarly]
- **Classical literary attestation** — *alaktaka*-reddened feet are a **stock image of feminine beauty in Sanskrit kāvya** (Kālidāsa and later poets), the red dye leaving auspicious footprints; the *lākṣā-rasa* (lac-juice) of the *Mahābhārata*'s **Lākṣā-gṛha** (house of lac) is the same material in a destructive register. (Source: Sanskrit kāvya; *Mahābhārata*, Lākṣā-gṛha episode) [scholarly]
- **Bridal & married use** — *āltā/mahāvar* edging the feet is a core item of the **ṣoḍaśa-śṛṅgāra** (§II), strongly marked in **Bengali, Odia and Eastern bridal custom**, where the bride's red-painted feet leave **auspicious footprints** entering the marital home — echoing the **Lakṣmī-footprint (Lakṣmī-pada)** motif of the doorstep rangoli → [`rangoli-kolam-floor-art.md`](rangoli-kolam-floor-art.md). (Source: Bengali/Odia wedding custom) [folk][regional]
- **Ritual & image-worship** — *āltā* is applied to the **feet of goddess images** (Durgā, Lakṣmī, Kālī) and to the feet of the bride-as-goddess; the red foot is itself a *maṅgala* sign of the resident Śrī. (Source: pūjā custom) [folk][regional]
- *Variant — colourant source:* historically the red came from **lac (lākṣā)**, **safflower (kusumbha)** or **red sandalwood**; modern *āltā* is a cotton-wad or bottled liquid dye. (Source: Wikipedia, *Alta (dye)*) [regional]

## V. Kindred painted marks (the tilaka complex)

- **Tilaka / bindī** — the forehead-mark, the most everyday painted adornment: *kuṅkuma* (vermilion), *candana* (sandal-paste), *bhasma* (ash) or *gopī-candana*, applied as sectarian sign and as auspicious dot. The kalā **Viśeṣaka-cchedya** (#6) is precisely the art of cutting/painting these marks. (Source: *Kāmasūtra* 1.3; tradition) [scholarly] — sectarian marks (Ūrdhva-puṇḍra, Tripuṇḍra) → [`sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md`](sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md)
- **Sindūr (vermilion in the parting)** — the red lead/vermilion line in a married woman's hair-parting (*sīmanta*), the supreme mark of *saubhāgya*; mythically linked to **Pārvatī** and to the wifely vows of **Sītā** and **Sāvitrī**. (Source: bridal custom; Purāṇic lore) [folk]
- **Patra-lekhā / patra-bhaṅga** — delicate **leaf- and creeper-tracery painted on the cheeks, breast and body** with musk, sandal or saffron, a refined cosmetic of classical kāvya heroines; the painted cousin of mehndi's vegetal patterning. (Source: Sanskrit kāvya; cf. *Kāmasūtra* aṅga-rāga) [scholarly]
- **Gopī-candana / ūrdhva-puṇḍra body-marks** — Vaiṣṇava devotees stamp/paint sacred marks on the **arms, chest and forehead**, a devotional body-art distinct from the cosmetic but sharing the painted-auspicious logic. (Source: Vaiṣṇava custom) [regional]

## VI. The two clocks

- **Scriptural clock** — body-adornment is fixed in the **timeless 64-Kalā curriculum** (mastered, with all arts, by **Kṛṣṇa in 64 days** under Sāndīpani → [`sixty-four-kalas.md`](sixty-four-kalas.md)) and in the *kalpa*-old *śṛṅgāra* of goddesses and ideal wives (Pārvatī, Lakṣmī, Sītā); *alaktaka*-reddened feet and *aṅga-rāga* belong to the eternal vocabulary of beauty in kāvya. (Source: *Kāmasūtra* 1.3; classical poetry) [scholarly]
- **Historical clock** — mehndi, āltā and the painted marks are a **continuously-practised living folk art** of the recorded (**kali-historical**) period: attested in classical text as a *kalā*, elaborated in mediaeval kāvya, and surviving as everyday bridal and festival ritual into the present day. (Source: repo "two-clocks" convention) [scholarly]
