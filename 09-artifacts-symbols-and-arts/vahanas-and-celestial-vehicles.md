# Vāhanas & Celestial Vehicles

> Sits in [`09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/`](README.md) — the **living mounts** (*vāhana*, "that which carries") and the **self-moving aerial vehicles** (*vimāna*) of the gods. A vāhana is simultaneously a deity's **vehicle**, a fixed **iconographic attribute** (you name the rider by the mount), and a **tamed-force symbol** — the animal embodies a power or a vice that the deity rides, masters, and dominates.
> Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md). Wielders & owners cross-link to [`02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/); several mounts are themselves classed beings in [`08-beings-and-bestiary/`](../08-beings-and-bestiary/); the named vimanas overlap the relics in [`divine-objects-and-treasures.md`](divine-objects-and-treasures.md).

This file catalogs (I) the **principal deity-vāhana pairings** with symbolism; (II) the **goddess & Devī mounts**; (III) the **Dikpāla / Lokapāla** (guardian-of-directions) mounts; (IV) the **Navagraha** (nine-planet) mounts; (V) **named individual mounts** (Garuḍa, Nandi, Airāvata, Uccaiḥśravas — beings with biographies); and (VI) the **vimānas** (flying chariots/palaces). Per repo policy, **every variant mount, alternate name and count is cataloged to source**; the master roster is **open-ended** — minor and regional deities each add a mount, and several majors carry *multiple* vāhanas.

The dual-symbolism rule (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*): the mount's **positive** aspect mirrors the deity's character, while the animal *also* stands for the **lower force the deity has conquered** — Gaṇeśa's rat is both nimble intelligence *and* the gnawing desires he rides over; Kārttikeya's peacock is both splendour *and* the vanity/serpent it tramples.

## I. The Principal Deity-Vāhana Pairings

- **Garuḍa** (the eagle / kite-man) — vāhana of **[Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)**. The king of birds, sworn enemy of serpents (*Nāgas*); embodies **divine power, speed, fearlessness, and the swift carrying of the soul to liberation**. Son of **Kaśyapa** and **Vinatā**, brother of **Aruṇa** (Sūrya's charioteer); won *amṛta* to free his enslaved mother, whence Viṣṇu granted him deathlessness and made him his mount and banner (**Garuḍa-dhvaja**). A being in his own right — see [`08-beings-and-bestiary/`](../08-beings-and-bestiary/). (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*, *Garuda*)
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu's second mount):* **Ādiśeṣa / Śeṣa-nāga**, the cosmic serpent, on whom Viṣṇu reclines (**Śeṣaśāyī**) across the cosmic ocean — a "couch-vehicle" rather than a flying mount. (Source: LearnReligions, *Vehicles of the Gods*)
- **Nandi** (the bull, *Vṛṣabha*) — vāhana of **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)**. White humped bull symbolising **strength, virility, fertility, unwavering devotion (*bhakti*), purity, and dharma/righteous patience**; couchant before every Śiva temple, gazing at the *liṅga*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*; My Pooja Box)
  - *Variant (Nandi as person):* in older strata Nandi is **Nandikeśvara / Nandīśvara**, an anthropomorphic gaṇa-chief and door-guardian, *distinct from* the bull he later merged with. (Source: Wikipedia, *Nandi (Hinduism)*) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Śiva's banner):* the bull is also Śiva's emblem — the **Vṛṣabha-dhvaja** (bull-banner). (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*)
- **Haṃsa** (the swan / *bar-headed* goose) — shared vāhana of **[Brahmā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)** and **Sarasvatī**. Symbol of **wisdom, discrimination (*viveka*), purity, and transcendence** — the *paramahaṃsa* who separates milk from water (truth from falsehood). (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*; My Pooja Box)
  - *Variant (count):* Brahmā is given **seven swans** in some popular lists (matching Sūrya's seven horses). (Source: LearnReligions, *Vehicles of the Gods*) [folk]
  - *Variant (Sarasvatī's mount):* Sarasvatī rides **either the haṃsa or a peacock (*mayūra*)** depending on iconographic school — the peacock-form overlaps Kārttikeya's. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*; VedicFeed)
- **Mūṣika / Mūṣaka** (the mouse or rat, named **Dinka / Krauñca**) — vāhana of **[Gaṇeśa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)**. The tiny mount under the elephant-headed god embodies **conquered desire and ego**: rats multiply in the dark like useless thoughts, which Gaṇeśa rides and crushes; also speed, sharpness, and the ability to gnaw through every obstacle. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*; MeMeraki)
  - *Variant (alternate Gaṇeśa mounts):* texts and regional icons also give Gaṇeśa a **peacock (Mayūreśvara form), a lion (Vināyaka), or a serpent** as mount — explicitly noted as inconsistent across traditions. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*) [regional]
- **Mayūra** (the peacock, named **Paravāṇi / Parvani**) — vāhana of **[Kārttikeya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)** (Skanda / Murugan / Subrahmaṇya). Symbol of **splendour, majesty, and the destruction of ego and sensory desire** — the peacock devours serpents (the venom of the senses); it reins in the war-god's own vanity. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*; My Pooja Box)
  - *Variant:* the peacock Paravāṇi is in some accounts the transformed *asura* **Sūrapadma**, split into peacock-mount and cock-banner after defeat. (Source: Tamil Skanda tradition) [folk/regional]
- **Airāvata** (the white elephant) — vāhana of **[Indra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)**, king of the gods. Four-tusked (sometimes many-tusked/many-headed) white elephant who **churns clouds and brings rain**; emerged from the **[Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)**. Embodies royal power and the storm. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*; *Bhagavata Purana* 8.8)
  - *Variant (Indra's mount):* popular lists also simply give Indra "the elephant"; his **horse Uccaiḥśravas** (below) is the other churning-born mount associated with him. (Source: LearnReligions)
- **Ulūka** (the owl) — vāhana of **[Lakṣmī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)**. The night-bird dispenses wealth in darkness; warns against **greed, isolation, and spiritual blindness** (the owl, day-blind, must not be dazzled by riches). (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*; LearnReligions)
  - *Variant (Lakṣmī's mounts):* Lakṣmī alternately rides/stands on **elephants** (the **Gaja-Lakṣmī** form, flanked by elephants pouring water) or on the **lotus (*padma*)** — a rare *non-animal* vehicle. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*) [iconographic]
- **Mahiṣa** (the male water-buffalo) — vāhana of **[Yama](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)**, lord of death and the southern quarter. The dark, heavy buffalo symbolises death's inexorable dominion. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*; LearnReligions)
- **Makara** (the composite sea-monster: crocodile/fish/elephant-snout) — vāhana of **Varuṇa** (ocean-god) and of the river-goddess **[Gaṅgā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)**. Symbol of the deep, the primordial waters, and the fertile life of rivers; also the emblem (*makara-dhvaja*) of **Kāmadeva**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*, *Makara*)
- **Kūrma** (the tortoise) — vāhana of the river-goddess **Yamunā** (paired iconographically with Gaṅgā-on-Makara at temple thresholds). (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*)
- **Aja / Meṣa** (the ram) — vāhana of **[Agni](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)**, the fire-god (Dikpāla of the south-east). The ram embodies the leaping, butting force of flame. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*; LearnReligions)
- **Aśva** (the horse) — vāhana of **[Sūrya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)**, drawn as **seven horses** (the seven colours/rays of light, the seven days/metres) yoked to his chariot driven by **Aruṇa**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*; LearnReligions)
- **Mṛga** (the antelope/deer) — vāhana of **Vāyu** (wind-god) and, in some lists, of **Candra** (the moon). Swiftness and the bounding wind. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*)
  - *Variant:* Vāyu is also given **a thousand horses**; Candra a horse-or-antelope-drawn chariot. (Source: LearnReligions) [folk]
- **Śuka** (the parrot) — vāhana of **Kāmadeva** (god of desire) and his consort **Rati**; the parrot of sweet speech and amorous longing. Kāmadeva's banner bears the **makara**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*)
- **Nakula** (the mongoose) — attribute-mount of **Kubera**, lord of wealth, who holds/vomits a jewel-spewing mongoose (the foe of treasure-hoarding serpents). (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*)
- **Śvāna** (the dog) — vāhana of **Bhairava** (the fierce Śiva) and of **Khaṇḍobā**; the loyal, liminal scavenger of cremation-grounds. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*) [Śaiva/folk]
- **Vyāghra** (the tiger) — vāhana of **Ayyappa** (Dharma-śāstā) and a form of the Devī's mount (below). (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*)
- **Go / Dhenu** (the cow) — vāhana of **Dattātreya** (the triple-form sage-god), who walks with a cow (the Vedas/earth) and four dogs. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*) [folk]

## II. The Goddess & Devī Mounts (Śākta roster)

- **Siṃha / Vyāghra** (lion / tiger) — vāhana of **[Durgā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/)** / Pārvatī / Ambikā. The great cat the warrior-goddess rides into battle against **Mahiṣāsura**; given to her, in the *Devī Māhātmya* lineage, by **Himavān** (the Himālaya). Symbol of raw power and conquered ferocity. (Source: *Devī Māhātmya*; Hindu-Blog)
  - *Variant (lion vs. tiger) [disputed]:* the *Devī Māhātmya* says **lion (*siṃha*)**; North-Indian/Vaiṣṇo-Devī tradition makes the goddess **Śerāwālī** ("she of the lion") yet popularly depicts a **tiger**; both circulate and are treated as interchangeable mounts. (Source: Hindu-Blog; The Daily Jagran)
  - *Variant (the mount's name):* the lion is named **Dawon** (also **Somanandi**); some accounts call the tiger-mount of Durgā/Vaiṣṇo-Devī **Sherawali**, and the Kāmākhyā lion **Mahāsiṃha** [disputed — little firm scriptural anchor]. (Source: Wikipedia, *Dawon*; Quora discussions cited therein)
- **Haṃsa / Mayūra** (swan / peacock) — vāhana of **Sarasvatī** (see §I). (Source: VedicFeed)
- **Ulūka / Gaja / Padma** (owl / elephants / lotus) — vāhana(s) of **Lakṣmī** (see §I). (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*)
- **Makara** — vāhana of **Gaṅgā** (see §I).
- **Gardabha / Khara** (the donkey/ass) — vāhana of **Śītalā** (smallpox/fever goddess), who rides a donkey with a broom and water-pot; and a mount-form of **Kālarātri / Dhūmāvatī**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*; LearnReligions) [folk]
  - *Variant:* **Kālī** is given a **donkey or jackal**; **Cāmuṇḍā** a **corpse (the *vetāla* Betāl), water-buffalo, dhole (wild dog), or donkey** — i.e. an open set of liminal/inauspicious mounts. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*) [folk]
- **Kāka** (the crow) — vāhana of **Dhūmāvatī** (the widow-goddess, a Mahāvidyā) and of **Jyeṣṭhā/Alakṣmī**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*) [Śākta/tantric]
- **Nāga / Haṃsa** (serpent / duck) — twin vāhanas of **Manasā**, the snake-goddess. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*) [folk]
- **Kukkuṭa** (the cockerel/rooster) — vāhana of **Bahuchara Mātā** (Gujarat). (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*) [regional/folk]
- **The Mahāvidyās & Mātṛkās** carry mounts mirroring their *purusha*-deities (e.g. **Vārāhī** on a buffalo, **Indrāṇī** on the elephant Airāvata, **Vaiṣṇavī** on Garuḍa, **Brahmāṇī** on the swan) — the **Sapta-Mātṛkā** mounts are the consort-goddesses' inheritance of each god's vāhana. (Source: Wikipedia, *Matrikas*) [Śākta]

## III. The Dikpāla / Lokapāla Mounts (guardians of the directions)

The **eight (or ten) Dikpālas** guard the quarters; each pairs a deity, a direction, and a mount (Source: Wikipedia, *Guardians of the directions*; *Vahana*):

- **East — Indra** on **Airāvata** (white elephant).
- **South-East — Agni** on the **ram (meṣa)**.
- **South — Yama** on the **buffalo (mahiṣa)**.
- **South-West — Nirṛti** (or Sūrya in some lists) on a **human / lion / corpse** [variant].
- **West — Varuṇa** on the **makara** (sea-monster).
- **North-West — Vāyu** on the **antelope / deer (mṛga)**.
- **North — Kubera** on the **horse / man / ram** (and the jewel-mongoose attribute) [variant].
- **North-East — Īśāna** (Śiva-aspect) on **Nandi** (the bull).
- *Variant (ten-fold list):* the up/down guardians **Brahmā** (zenith, on the **haṃsa**) and **Viṣṇu/Ananta** (nadir, on **Śeṣa/Garuḍa**) round out the ten Dikpālas. (Source: Wikipedia, *Guardians of the directions*) [late text]

## IV. The Navagraha Mounts (the nine "planets")

Each of the **nine grahas** rides a distinct vāhana, drawn from Graha-iconography (Source: Wikipedia, *Navagraha*; *Vahana*):

- **Sūrya** (Sun) — chariot of **seven horses**.
- **Candra/Soma** (Moon) — chariot drawn by an **antelope / ten white horses**.
- **Maṅgala/Aṅgāraka** (Mars) — a **ram (or goat)**.
- **Budha** (Mercury) — a **lion / winged carpet / horse** [variant].
- **Bṛhaspati/Guru** (Jupiter) — an **elephant**.
- **Śukra** (Venus) — a **horse / crocodile** [variant].
- **Śani** (Saturn) — a **vulture, raven, or crow** (also a **buffalo / iron chariot**); the dark scavenger in whom the slow planet "represses thieving tendencies" — feeding crows is held to please Śani. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vahana*; VedicFeed) [variant]
- **Rāhu** (north lunar node) — a **black lion / chariot drawn by eight black horses**.
- **Ketu** (south lunar node) — a **vulture / fish**.
- *Variant:* graha-mounts differ noticeably between Vāstu, temple-iconography, and astrological manuals; treat the above as the common temple set. (Source: Wikipedia, *Navagraha*) [disputed]

## V. Named Individual Mounts (mounts with biographies)

Some vāhanas are full **characters** catalogued among beings — not generic animals but named individuals:

- **Garuḍa** — see §I and [`08-beings-and-bestiary/`](../08-beings-and-bestiary/). Son of Vinatā; king of birds; Viṣṇu's mount and banner.
- **Nandi / Nandikeśvara** — see §I. Gaṇa-chief and bull of Śiva; guardian of Kailāsa.
- **Airāvata** — Indra's white elephant; **fourteen-ratna** of the **[Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)**; ancestor of all auspicious elephants (the *diggaja*, world-elephants of the quarters). (Source: *Bhagavata Purana* 8.8)
- **Uccaiḥśravas** ("loud-neighing") — the **seven-headed white horse**, finest of horses, churned from the milk-ocean and taken by Indra; the wager over its tail-colour triggers the **Kadrū–Vinatā** enslavement myth (origin of Garuḍa's nectar-quest and the Nāga–bird enmity). A churning-treasure cross-listed in [`divine-objects-and-treasures.md`](divine-objects-and-treasures.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Uchchaihshravas*; *Bhagavata Purana* 8.8)
- **Paravāṇi** — Kārttikeya's named peacock (§I).
- **Dawon / Somanandi** — Durgā's named lion (§II). [disputed name]
- **Sārameya / the four dogs** — the hounds of Bhairava / Dattātreya / Khaṇḍobā.
- **Jaṭāyu & Sampāti** — though not "mounts," the great vulture-brothers are kin to Garuḍa and serve Rāma; aerial beings catalogued in [`08-beings-and-bestiary/`](../08-beings-and-bestiary/). (Source: *Ramayana*)
- **Matali** — not a mount but **Indra's charioteer**, who drives Indra's celestial *ratha* (lent to Rāma for the Rāvaṇa-duel and to Arjuna for the heaven-journey). (Source: *Ramayana*, *Mahabharata*)

> *On the āyudhapuruṣa overlap:* a mount, like a weapon, can be **personified** — Nandi the bull is also Nandi the man; Garuḍa is bird-and-being. This is the same phenomenon as the weapon-spirits (*āyudhapuruṣas*) in [`README.md`](README.md) §iconography.

## VI. The Vimānas (flying chariots & aerial palaces)

A **vimāna** (विमान, "measuring out / traversing"; Monier-Williams: "a car or chariot of the gods, any self-moving aerial car") is the *machine* counterpart to the living vāhana — a self-moving flying palace or war-chariot. Descriptions run through the **[Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/)**, **[Mahābhārata](../04-deep-dives/)**, and the **Purāṇas**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vimana*)

- **Puṣpaka Vimāna** ("flower-chariot" / **Puṣpaka-ratha**) — the archetypal vimana: a sun-bright, ever-moving aerial palace that **goes everywhere at will** and never lacks room. **Made by Viśvakarmā** (the architect-god) for **Brahmā → given to Kubera → seized (with Laṅkā) by Rāvaṇa**; after Rāvaṇa's fall it flies **Rāma, Sītā and the army home to Ayodhyā**, then is returned to Kubera. Cross-listed in [`divine-objects-and-treasures.md`](divine-objects-and-treasures.md). (Source: *Ramayana*; Wikipedia, *Pushpaka Vimana*, *Vimana*)
- **Uparicara Vasu's vimāna** — a crystalline aerial car granted by **Indra** to King **Uparicara Vasu** (Mahābhārata), letting him move godlike through the sky. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vimana*; *Mahabharata*)
- **Saubha** — the flying fortress-city of the *asura* king **Śālva**, an airborne stronghold that attacks **Dvārakā** and is destroyed by **Kṛṣṇa** (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva). (Source: *Mahabharata*, Vana Parva) [epic]
- **Indra's ratha / Matali's chariot** — the war-chariot of the king of gods, lent to mortal heroes (Rāma at Laṅkā; Arjuna ascending to Svarga), driven by the charioteer **Matali**. (Source: *Ramayana*, *Mahabharata*)
- **The chariots of the Devas** — the *Sun-chariot* (Sūrya's seven-horse car driven by Aruṇa) and the *Moon-chariot* are the Vedic prototypes of the self-moving celestial vehicle. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vimana*)
- **Jain "vimāna" heavens** — in Jain cosmology *vimāna* names the celestial abodes; notable named ones include **Puṣpa-uttara** (Mahāvīra), **Aparājita** (Ariṣṭanemi), **Sarvārtha-siddhi** (Jayanta), **Vijaya** (Dharmanātha). (Source: Wikipedia, *Vimana*) [Jain]

### The four classes (Vaimānika Śāstra) [late text / disputed]

The early-20th-c. *Vaimānika Śāstra* (allegedly channelled c. 1918–1923 by Subbaraya Śāstry, surfacing 1952) is **NOT an ancient text** — a 1974 IISc Bangalore engineering study judged its craft "poor concoctions" — but it furnishes the popular four-fold typology (Source: Wikipedia, *Vimana*, *Vaimanika Shastra*) [late text]:

- **Rukma Vimāna** — conical, gold-coloured.
- **Sundara Vimāna** — rocket-/spindle-shaped.
- **Tripura Vimāna** — a large **multi-tiered** (three-storey) craft for long/multi-level travel.
- **Śakuna Vimāna** — **bird-shaped**, "flies like a bird with hinged wings and tail."
- *Variant (25 Māntrika vimanas of the Tretā-yuga):* the same text names twenty-five, including *puṣpaka, ajamukha, bhrājasvat, jyotirmukha, kowśika, bhīṣma, śeṣa, mayūra, śaṅkara, tripura, trinetra, bheruṇḍa,* etc. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vaimanika Shastra*) [late text]

> **Open-ended.** The vāhana roster is *not closed* — every regional grāma-devatā, kula-devatā and folk goddess rides a local mount (snakes, fish, buffaloes, fowl, even plants), and Purāṇic/Sthala-purāṇa lists disagree on the mounts of even major gods (Sarasvatī swan-or-peacock; Durgā lion-or-tiger; Lakṣmī owl-or-elephant-or-lotus). Treat §I–V as the **pan-Hindu core**, not an exhaustive census. The vimana roster is likewise open-ended across the epics and Purāṇas.
