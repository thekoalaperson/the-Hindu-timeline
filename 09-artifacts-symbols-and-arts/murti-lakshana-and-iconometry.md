# Mūrti-lakṣaṇa & Iconometry

> Sits in [`09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/`](README.md) — the **measuring-rod behind the icon**: the proportional canon (*tālamāna*) and the iconographic grammar (*pratimā-lakṣaṇa*) by which every Hindu *mūrti* is laid out, carved and made recognisable as a particular deity. Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md) · master index [`The Hindu Timeline`](../README.md). Where [`sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md`](sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md) catalogs the *finished* iconography (the mudrās, the Naṭarāja, the hand-attributes), this file catalogs the **rules that produce it** — the units, ratios and *dhyāna* prescriptions. The same building canon governs the shrine that houses the image → [`temple-architecture-nagara-dravida-vesara.md`](temple-architecture-nagara-dravida-vesara.md); the held attributes are also weapons → [`divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md`](divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md); the deities whose proportions these rules encode → [`../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md).

**Mūrti-lakṣaṇa** (the "marks of the image") and **pratimā-lakṣaṇa** ("characteristics of the icon") are the technical core of Hindu *śilpa* (sacred craft): a closed system of measurement and attribute that the *śilpin* (image-maker) must follow so that the image is *prāsādika* (efficacious) and fit to receive the deity through *prāṇa-pratiṣṭhā* (life-installation). The repo elsewhere names these only as Purāṇic chapter-headings (the *pratimā-lakṣaṇa* chapters of Agni, Matsya, Garuḍa); here the canon itself is set out. **Two clocks:** the *śāstra* presents these proportions as eternally revealed (the *Citrasūtra* is dialogue between the sage Mārkaṇḍeya and king Vajra; the rules are *apauruṣeya* in tone), while the **historical** clock places the surviving redactions across c. 6th–12th c. CE, codifying older guild practice. [scholarly]

## I. The Measurement System — Tālamāna

- **Aṅgula — the base unit** — the finger-breadth is the atom of all icon-measure. The *śilpa* texts distinguish the **mānāṅgula** ("measuring finger," a fixed module derived from the image itself) from the *dehalabdhāṅgula* / *mātrāṅgula* (a unit taken from the patron's or priest's own finger). The mānāṅgula is itself built up from minute sub-units — *paramāṇu → ratha-dhūli → vālāgra → likṣā → yūka → yava → aṅgula* (8 *yava* = 1 *aṅgula* in the standard reckoning). (Source: *Mānasāra* ch. on *māna*; *Mayamata*) [scholarly]
- **Tāla — the face-module** — the governing unit of figural proportion is the **tāla** (lit. "span / palm"), defined as the length of the **face from hairline to chin**, equal to **12 aṅgulas**. The whole figure is then expressed as a multiple of *tālas* plus odd *aṅgulas* — hence *aṣṭa-tāla* (8 faces tall), *nava-tāla* (9), *daśa-tāla* (10). (Source: *Bṛhat Saṃhitā* 58, *Pratimā-lakṣaṇa*; *Viṣṇudharmottara* III, *Citrasūtra*)
- **Vitasti / muṣṭi — secondary spans** — a *vitasti* (out-stretched span) = 12 aṅgulas = 1 tāla; the *tāla* proper is the span of the open hand, by which the system takes its name. (Source: *Mānasāra*; *Śilpa Śāstra* tradition)

## II. The Tāla-Schemes — Rank Encoded as Height

The schemes assign **taller, more elongated proportions to higher beings** and squatter proportions to lower/fierce ones — divinity is literally *measured*.

- **Daśa-tāla (10 faces) — the supreme/uttama scheme** — reserved for the **highest deities** (Viṣṇu, Śiva, Sūrya, the Supreme forms) and great cosmic kings. Sub-graded as **uttama-daśatāla (124 aṅgulas), madhyama-daśatāla (120), adhama-daśatāla (116)**, the variation lying in the throat, chest and limb measures. (Source: *Mānasāra* 65; *Mayamata* ch. *pratimā-māna*) [scholarly]
- **Nava-tāla (9 faces) — the standard divine scheme** — the most-used canon for **devas, devīs and minor gods** (and the heroic human ideal); total height ≈ **108 aṅgulas** (9 × 12), a number deliberately echoing the auspicious 108. (Source: *Bṛhat Saṃhitā* 58.4; *Viṣṇudharmottara*, *Citrasūtra*)
- **Aṣṭa-tāla (8 faces) — lesser divinities** — for **yakṣas, lower devas, some goddesses and rākṣasas of rank**. (Source: *Mānasāra*; *Śukranīti* 4)
- **Sapta- to eka-tāla (7 down to 1) — descending ranks** — sapta-tāla for *gaṇas*, *bhūtas* and humans of low degree; the very short *pañca-/catur-tāla* for dwarves (e.g. **Apasmāra** under Naṭarāja's foot), children and *vāmana* forms; *eka-tāla* for the most diminished beings. (Source: *Viṣṇudharmottara* III.35; *Mayamata*) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (counting):* texts differ on whether the *tāla* runs hairline-to-chin or includes the *uṣṇīṣa*/crown, so a "nine-tāla" figure in one redaction equals another's "ten-tāla"; the *Citrasūtra*, *Bṛhat Saṃhitā* and *Mānasāra* are not perfectly concordant. (Source: comparative *śilpa* scholarship) [disputed]

## III. Āyādi & the Six Formulae (Ṣaḍvarga)

- **Ṣaḍvarga / āyādi calculations** — before construction the dimensions are tested through **six formulae** (the *ṣaḍvarga*: **āya, vyaya, yoni, ṛkṣa (nakṣatra), vāra (weekday), tithi**). A candidate measure is multiplied by a fixed factor and divided by a divisor; the **remainder** must fall in an auspicious class — *āya* (gain) must exceed *vyaya* (loss), the *yoni* (one of eight, e.g. *dhvaja*, *siṃha*) must be benign, and the *nakṣatra*/weekday/*tithi* remainders must suit the patron. (Source: *Mānasāra* ch. *āyādi-ṣaḍvarga*; *Mayamata*) [scholarly]
- **Application** — the same *āyādi* arithmetic governs both the icon's height and the temple's plan, binding image and shrine into one metrological scheme (cross-ref the *vāstu-puruṣa-maṇḍala* in [`temple-architecture-nagara-dravida-vesara.md`](temple-architecture-nagara-dravida-vesara.md)). (Source: *Mānasāra*; *Mayamata*)

## IV. Aṅga-pratyaṅga — Proportions of the Parts

- **Limb-by-limb measure** — within a chosen tāla, every *aṅga* (major limb) and *pratyaṅga* (minor part) has a fixed sub-measure in *aṅgulas*: the texts specify the *uṣṇīṣa* (crown-height), *mukha* (face), *grīvā* (neck, ≈ 4 aṅgulas), *ura* (chest), *udara* (belly), *kaṭi* (hips), *ūru* (thighs), *jaṅghā* (shins), and so to the toe. (Source: *Bṛhat Saṃhitā* 58; *Viṣṇudharmottara* III)
- **The face in tālas** — the face is itself divided into **three equal parts** (hairline–brow, brow–nose-tip, nose-tip–chin), each = 4 aṅgulas, governing the eyes, nose and lips. (Source: *Citrasūtra*, *Viṣṇudharmottara* III)
- **The eye-shapes** — the *Citrasūtra* names canonical eye-forms keyed to mood — *padma-patra* (lotus-petal, for the benign), *matsya* (fish), *cāpa* (bow), and the long *utpala* — fixing *bhāva* through geometry. (Source: *Viṣṇudharmottara* III, *Citrasūtra*) [scholarly]
- **Bhaṅga — the body's flexions** — postural axis is graded as **sama-bhaṅga** (erect, symmetrical, for supreme frontal icons), **ābhaṅga** (slight bend), **tribhaṅga** (the three-bend S-curve, e.g. Kṛṣṇa-Veṇugopāla), and **atibhaṅga** (violent flexion, for dancing/fierce forms like Naṭarāja). (Source: *Śilpa Śāstra*; *Mānasāra*) [scholarly]

## V. The Identity-Grammar — What Fixes the Deity

Proportion makes a body; the following *lakṣaṇa* makes it a **named** deity. (The finished forms are cataloged in [`sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md`](sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md); here they are the iconographer's checklist.)

- **Āsana / sthānaka — posture** — standing (*sthānaka*), seated (*āsīna*: *padmāsana*, *sukhāsana*, *lalitāsana*, the royal *mahārājalīlā*), reclining (*śayana*, e.g. Anantaśayana Viṣṇu), or dancing (*nṛtya*). (Source: *Mayamata*; *Agni Purāṇa* pratimā chs.)
- **Mudrā — hand-gesture** — the *abhaya*, *varada*, *jñāna*, *dhyāna*, *kaṭyavalambita* (hand-on-hip) etc. are prescribed per deity and per arm; a single wrong gesture mis-identifies the image. (Source: *Viṣṇudharmottara* III; *Śilpa Śāstra*)
- **Āyudha — held attributes** — the deciding marks: Viṣṇu's *śaṅkha–cakra–gadā–padma* in a prescribed hand-order generate his **24 named forms** (Keśava, Mādhava, …) by permutation; Śiva's *triśūla–ḍamaru*, Devī's many arms, etc. (Source: *Agni Purāṇa*; *Garuḍa Purāṇa* pratimā-lakṣaṇa; *Padma*-order tradition)
- **Vāhana & parivāra — mount and retinue** — the mount beneath (Garuḍa, Nandī, Siṃha, Mūṣika) and the attendant figures fix context and rank. (Source: *Matsya Purāṇa* pratimā chs.; *Viṣṇudharmottara*) [cross-ref [`sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md`](sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md)]
- **Dhyāna-śloka / pratimā-lakṣaṇa verse** — each deity has a **meditation-verse** that verbally fixes complexion, number of faces/arms, attributes, ornaments and consort; the *śilpin* and the worshipper both build the image (in stone, and in the mind's eye) from this *dhyāna*. The Purāṇic *pratimā-lakṣaṇa* chapters are essentially anthologies of such prescriptions. (Source: *Agni Purāṇa*, *Matsya Purāṇa*, *Garuḍa Purāṇa* pratimā-lakṣaṇa chapters) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Āgamic sectarian canons):* the **Śaiva Āgamas** and the Vaiṣṇava **Vaikhānasa** and **Pāñcarātra** Āgamas carry their own *bera-/pratimā-lakṣaṇa* with sect-specific proportions and attributes — e.g. Vaikhānasa fixes the *dhruva-bera* (immovable main icon) versus *kautuka*/*utsava* (festival bronzes) by distinct measures. (Source: *Vaikhānasa Āgama*; Śaiva Āgama *kriyāpāda*) [regional/sectarian]
- **Tāla of the medium** — the texts also key proportion to **material and image-type**: *citra* (in-the-round, fully modelled), *citrārdha* (relief, half-shown), and *citrābhāsa* (painting); the *Citrasūtra* is the locus classicus for the painted (*citrābhāsa*) canon. (Source: *Viṣṇudharmottara* III, *Citrasūtra*) [scholarly]

## VI. Defect & Efficacy — Why the Canon Binds

- **Lakṣaṇa-yukta vs. doṣa** — an image made *to* measure is *prāsādika* (grace-bearing); one with *māna-doṣa* (proportion-fault) is held to bring harm to maker and patron — texts warn that a too-thin, too-fat, broken-limbed or wrongly-gestured icon visits illness, loss or worse. The canon is thus not aesthetic preference but ritual necessity. (Source: *Bṛhat Saṃhitā* 58.30–31; *Mayamata*; *Śukranīti* 4) [scholarly]
- **Śukranīti's craft-ethic** — the *Śukranīti* (4.4) stresses that the maker should follow the *dhyāna* and *māna* rather than a living model, since only the canonical proportions render the image fit for *pūjā*. (Source: *Śukranīti* 4) [late text]
