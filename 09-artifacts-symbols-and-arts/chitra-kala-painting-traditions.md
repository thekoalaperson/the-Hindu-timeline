**Citra-kalā** (the art of the image) is, with sculpture, the great **visual** wing of the 64 *kalās* — Vātsyāyana lists **#5 *Ālekhya*** (drawing/painting) among the cultivated arts, and the *Viṣṇudharmottara Purāṇa* devotes its entire **Citrasūtra** (Book III, the earliest surviving Indian painting-treatise) to it. Below the classical anchor sits the largest living family of devotional visual art in Hinduism: temple, scroll, cloth and folk schools that **narrate** the Purāṇas and epics. Per repo policy **every regional school, medium and lineage is catalogued to source and never merged**. **Two clocks:** on the **scriptural clock** the art is fixed in the *kalpa*-old, *apauruṣeya*-toned Citrasūtra dialogue of **Mārkaṇḍeya and king Vajra**; on the **historical clock** these are dated, continuously-practised guild crafts of the recorded (kali-historical) period.

## I. The classical anchor — the Citrasūtra

- **Ālekhya — the 64-Kalā entry** — the **5th of Vātsyāyana's sixty-four arts**, *ālekhya* ("drawing / painting"), sitting among the decorative arts beside #6 *viśeṣaka-cchedya* (face-marking) and #7 *taṇḍula-kusuma-balivikāra* (floor-design). (Source: *Kāmasūtra* 1.3, *Vidyā-samuddeśa*; see [`sixty-four-kalas.md`](sixty-four-kalas.md) #5) [scholarly]
- **The Citrasūtra of the Viṣṇudharmottara** — the **Citrasūtra** (*Viṣṇudharmottara Purāṇa*, Book III, chs. 35–43) is the **foundational Sanskrit treatise on painting**, cast as the sage **Mārkaṇḍeya** instructing king **Vajra**; it sets out the *ṣaḍaṅga* ("six limbs" of painting), the kinds of pictures, proportion (*tālamāna* shared with sculpture), and the dictum *yathā nṛtte tathā citre* — "as in dance, so in painting." (Source: *Viṣṇudharmottara Purāṇa* III.35–43; Stella Kramrisch, *The Vishnudharmottara*) [scholarly]
- **The ṣaḍaṅga (six limbs)** — the canonical aesthetic of painting: ***rūpabheda*** (knowledge of form/types), ***pramāṇa*** (proportion/measure), ***bhāva*** (emotion/feeling), ***lāvaṇya-yojana*** (infusion of grace), ***sādṛśya*** (likeness), and ***varṇikābhaṅga*** (preparation/use of pigment and brush). (Source: *Citrasūtra*, via Yaśodhara's *Jayamaṅgalā* commentary on *Kāmasūtra* 1.3) [scholarly]
- **The floor-drawing origin-legend** — one widely-told tale makes painting itself born of grief: when a brāhmaṇa's son died, **Brahmā** bade the father draw the boy on the ground so He could breathe life back in — the first painted image. (Source: *Citralakṣaṇa*, the earliest treatise-fragment on image-making) [late text][disputed]

## II. The temple / classical devotional schools

Each is a distinct lineage of medium, palette and patron-temple; **catalogued separately, not merged.**

- *Tanjore / Thañjāvūr painting (Tamil Nadu, c. 16th–18th c. CE):* the gilded **icon-portrait** school under Nāyaka and Maratha patronage; a panel built up with a *gesso* (chalk-and-gum) relief ground, **22-carat gold-leaf**, and inset semi-precious/glass stones, depicting **bālakṛṣṇa**, Rāma, Lakṣmī, Murugan and the temple deities frontally and richly bejewelled. (Source: Wikipedia, *Tanjore painting*) [regional]
- *Mysore painting (Karnataka, Wodeyar patronage):* the sister south-Indian gold-ground school — finer, flatter **gesso (*gesso* of lead-white and gum) low-relief gilding** than Tanjore, delicate line and muted colour, depicting Vaiṣṇava and Śaiva deities and the Dasara goddess **Cāmuṇḍeśvarī**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Mysore painting*) [regional]
- *Pichwai / Pichhवाई (Nāthdwārā, Rajasthan):* large **cloth-hangings (*pichwai* = "that which hangs at the back") suspended behind the icon of Śrīnāthjī** (the child-Kṛṣṇa of the Puṣṭimārga) in the Nāthdwārā haveli-temple; the subject is fixed by the **Vallabha-sampradāya** festival-calendar — Annakūṭa/Govardhana, Rāsa-līlā, Śarad-pūrṇimā — and the recurring cow, lotus and *gopī* motifs. (Source: Wikipedia, *Pichhwai*) [regional] — Puṣṭimārga lineage ↔ [`../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md`](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md)
- *Paubhā (Newar, Kathmandu Valley):* the Newar **scroll-icon on cotton** (Sanskrit *paṭa-bhaṭṭāraka*), the Hindu-Buddhist cousin of the Tibetan *thaṅka*; **paubhā** depict maṇḍalas and deities (Mahālakṣmī, the Aṣṭamātṛkā, Bhairava) for consecration and ritual display in Newar Hindu practice. (Source: Wikipedia, *Paubha*) [regional]

## III. The narrative scroll & cloth schools — the Purāṇas told in panels

These are the **story-telling** schools, where the painting is unrolled or "read" by a bard; the painted scroll *is* the recital aid.

- *Paṭṭacitra (Odisha & Bengal):* cloth-scroll (*paṭṭa* = cloth + *citra* = picture) painting in natural pigments with a black ink outline; the Odisha school is bound to the **Jagannātha cult of Purī** — it paints the **Jagannātha–Balabhadra–Subhadrā** triad, the **Daśāvatāra**, and supplies the **Anasara-paṭṭi**, the substitute paintings worshipped while the wooden icons are repainted, and the **Nava-guñjara**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Pattachitra*) [regional] — Jagannātha *sthala* ↔ [`../06-sacred-geography/README.md`](../06-sacred-geography/README.md)
  - *Bengal Paṭuā / jaḍāno-paṭ (West Bengal):* the long vertical **narrative scroll** unrolled panel-by-panel by the itinerant **Paṭuā** singer, who chants the *paṭer gān* — Rāmāyaṇa, Kṛṣṇa-līlā, Manasā-maṅgala and Caṇḍī episodes. (Source: Wikipedia, *Patua*) [folk][regional]
- *Cheriyal scrolls (Telangana):* the brilliantly-red **Nakāśi narrative scroll**, a vertical strip of up to 40–50 panels painted for the bard-communities (Kunapuli, Gauda) who sing the caste-Purāṇas — the **Mārkaṇḍeya-purāṇa**, Kṣīra-sāgara churning, Rāmāyaṇa and Mahābhārata episodes — village to village. (Source: Wikipedia, *Cheriyal scroll painting*) [folk][regional]
- *Phaḍ (Rajasthan):* the long horizontal **cloth-epic (*phaḍ*)** that serves as a **portable shrine** for the deified folk-heroes; the **Bhopā** priest-bard unrolls it by night and sings the epic before it by lamplight. (Source: Wikipedia, *Phad painting*) [folk][regional]
  - *Pābūjī kī phaḍ (Pābūjī):* the most famous, narrating the **Rāṭhoḍ folk-deity Pābūjī** (held a *aṃśa* of Lakṣmaṇa), sung by the Bhopā-and-Bhopī to the *rāvaṇhattā* fiddle. (Source: Wikipedia, *Phad painting*; J. D. Smith, *The Epic of Pābūjī*) [folk]
  - *Devnārāyaṇ kī phaḍ (Devnārāyaṇ):* the longest phaḍ, the epic of the **Gurjar deity Devnārāyaṇ** (an avatāra of Viṣṇu). (Source: Wikipedia, *Phad painting*; *Dev Narayan*) [folk]

## IV. The folk wall / cloth schools

- *Madhubani / Mithilā painting (Bihar–Nepal Mithilā):* the women's **wall-and-floor ritual painting** of Mithilā, traditionally laid on the *kohbar-ghar* (nuptial chamber); subjects are the **Rāma–Sītā wedding** (Mithilā being Sītā's homeland), Kṛṣṇa, Durgā, Śiva–Pārvatī and the lotus-tank *kohbar* fertility motif, in five named styles (*bharni, kachni, tantrik, godna, kohbar*). (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhubani art*) [folk][regional] — Sītā's Mithilā ↔ [`../06-sacred-geography/README.md`](../06-sacred-geography/README.md)
- *Kalamkari (Andhra Pradesh):* "pen-work" (*kalam* = pen + *kārī* = craft), hand-drawn/block-printed cloth in vegetable dyes with iron-mordant black and **myrobalan**-fixed colour. Two centres: the **Śrīkālahasti** school is **temple-narrative** — friezes of the Rāmāyaṇa, Mahābhārata and Kṛṣṇa-līlā drawn freehand as temple hangings; the **Machilīpaṭnam** school is block-printed and ornamental. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kalamkari*) [regional]

## V. Shared grammar & the two clocks

- **Pigments are earth, mineral and plant** — across the schools colour is *svayambhū* (natural): **lampblack/kohl** for outline, **white** from conch-shell/chalk/lead, **red** from cinnabar/hiṅgula and lac, **yellow** from haritāla (orpiment) and *gorocana*, **blue** from indigo, gold-leaf for the south-Indian icon-schools. (Source: *Citrasūtra* III.40 *varṇa*-prescriptions; school practice) [scholarly]
- **Frontality and attribute identify the deity** — the painted icon obeys the **same *dhyāna* and attribute-canon** as the carved one: Viṣṇu by *śaṅkha-cakra-gadā-padma*, Kṛṣṇa blue and flute-bearing, Jagannātha by his round eyes; the iconographic grammar is shared with [`murti-lakshana-and-iconometry.md`](murti-lakshana-and-iconometry.md) and [`sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md`](sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md). (Source: *Citrasūtra*; *Viṣṇudharmottara* III) [scholarly]
- **The two clocks** — *the scriptural-vs-historical distinction is kept:* on the **scriptural clock** painting is timeless, revealed in the *kalpa*-old Citrasūtra and the eternal 64-Kalā curriculum mastered by Citralekhā and the cultured; on the **historical clock** each named school is a **dated, geographically-rooted living guild craft** of the recorded period — Tanjore/Mysore from the 16th–18th c., Paṭṭacitra and Phaḍ as continuing oral-epic crafts into the present. (Source: repo "two-clocks" convention) [scholarly]
