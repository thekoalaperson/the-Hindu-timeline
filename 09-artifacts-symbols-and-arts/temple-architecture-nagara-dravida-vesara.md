# Temple Architecture — Nāgara, Drāviḍa & Vesara

> Sits in [`09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/`](README.md) — the **built body of the deity**: the Hindu temple (*mandira / devālaya / prāsāda*) is not a meeting-hall but a *vimāna* ("well-measured" cosmic vehicle) and a **literal house of god**, its plan laid on the [vāstu-puruṣa-maṇḍala](../05-concepts-and-dharma/vastu-shastra-and-the-vastu-purusha-mandala.md) and its elevation modelled on the cosmic mountain (Meru/Kailāsa).
> Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md) · master index [`The Hindu Timeline`](../README.md). The temple as a node of pilgrimage (*tīrtha-kṣetra*) → [`../06-sacred-geography/README.md`](../06-sacred-geography/README.md); the emblems that crown and mark it (kalaśa, dhvaja, gopuram, liṅga) → [`./sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md`](sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md); the *śilpa*/*vāstu* treatises that govern it → [`../90-literature-corpus/README.md`](../90-literature-corpus/README.md).

This file lays out the **canonical tripartite classification** of the Hindu temple — **Nāgara** (north), **Drāviḍa** (south), and **Vesara** (the Deccan hybrid) — as codified in the *Śilpa* and *Vāstu Śāstras*. This is a **two-clocks** subject: the *texts* present the three orders as a **timeless, divinely-ordained typology** (the gods themselves prescribe the *prāsāda*-forms), while *art-history* dates the orders' emergence and divergence to the **Gupta-and-after centuries** (c. 5th c. CE onward). Per repo policy, every count, sub-style and text-attribution is cataloged to source; the threefold scheme itself is a **late-text systematization** imposed on a far messier building record. [scholarly]

## I. The Shared Structural Vocabulary

The three orders are *dialects of one grammar* — the same functional parts, differently elevated and named. (Source: Wikipedia, *Hindu temple architecture*; *Vāstu Śāstra*)

- **Garbhagṛha — the "womb-chamber"** — the small, dark, square **sanctum** housing the *mūla-mūrti* (root-image) or *liṅga*; the metaphysical centre, sited over the *brahmasthāna* (god-square) of the [vāstu-puruṣa-maṇḍala](../05-concepts-and-dharma/vastu-shastra-and-the-vastu-purusha-mandala.md). Deliberately womb-like: windowless, undecorated, the point of *darśana*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Garbhagriha*; *Mānasāra*)
- **Maṇḍapa — the pillared hall(s)** — the congregational fore-halls leading to the sanctum; multiplied and named by position — **ardha-maṇḍapa** (entry porch), **mukha-maṇḍapa**, **mahā-maṇḍapa** (great hall), **raṅga-maṇḍapa** (dance-hall), **kalyāṇa-maṇḍapa** (marriage-hall, South). (Source: Wikipedia, *Mandapa*; *Mayamata*)
- **Antarāla — the vestibule** — the short transitional throat linking maṇḍapa to garbhagṛha. (Source: Wikipedia, *Hindu temple architecture*)
- **Pradakṣiṇā-patha** — the **circumambulatory passage** girdling the sanctum, walked clockwise; present in the *sāndhāra* ("with-passage") plan, absent in the *nirandhāra*. (Source: *Mānasāra*; Encyclopaedia Britannica, *Indian temple*)
- **Adhiṣṭhāna / pīṭha** — the moulded **plinth/base** on which the whole rises; **jagatī** = the raised platform/terrace. (Source: *Mayamata*; Wikipedia, *Hindu temple architecture*)
- **Śikhara vs. vimāna — the crowning superstructure** — the defining variable. In the **North the whole tower over the sanctum is the śikhara** ("mountain-peak"); in the **South the whole storeyed tower-shrine is the vimāna**, and the word *śikhara* is restricted to its **domed crowning finial** alone. This terminological split is the single sharpest North/South marker. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shikhara*; *Vimana (architecture)*) [scholarly]
- **Āmalaka & kalaśa — the finials** — the **āmalaka** is the ribbed, cushion-like stone disc (named for the *āmalaka*/myrobalan fruit) crowning a Nāgara śikhara; above it sits the **kalaśa** (pot-finial), the auspicious *pūrṇa-kumbha* that tops nearly every Hindu tower (cross-ref the kalaśa entry in [`./sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md`](sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md)). (Source: Wikipedia, *Amalaka*; *Kalasha*)
- **Gopuram — the gateway-tower** — the monumental, ornate **entrance-tower piercing the boundary wall** (*prākāra*), a *Drāviḍa* speciality; in the great South Indian temple-cities the gopurams grew to dwarf the central vimāna. (Source: Wikipedia, *Gopuram*) [Drāviḍa-defining]

## II. The Three Orders — the Canonical Triad

The threefold scheme is stated in the *śilpa* corpus and the *Purāṇa* building-chapters; the texts variously key the three to **shape** (square/round/octagonal plan or finial) and to **region**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Hindu temple architecture*; *Vāstu Śāstra*)

- **Nāgara — the Northern order** — temples whose sanctum-tower is a **curvilinear *śikhara*** (a beehive/convex-tapering spire), set on a square plan that projects into multiple *ratha* offsets, crowned by **āmalaka + kalaśa**, typically **without a perimeter gopuram**. Prevails from the Himalayas to the Vindhyas; classic exemplars at **Khajurāho, Bhubaneswar, and the Sūrya temple of Modhera**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Nagara architecture*; Encyclopaedia Britannica)
  - *Sub-style — Latina/Rekha-prāsāda:* the **single-spire** mono-tower, the earliest and most basic Nāgara śikhara. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shikhara*) [scholarly]
  - *Sub-style — Śekharī & Bhūmija:* the **multi-spired** elaborations. **Śekharī** clusters subsidiary spirelets (*uru-śṛṅga*) around the main one. **Bhūmija** (a distinct Central-Indian/Mālwā sub-order) arrays **miniature shrines in vertical and horizontal rows of *bhūmis* (storeys)** up the tower-quadrants, kept by tradition between the pure Nāgara and Drāviḍa. (Source: Wikipedia, *Bhumija*; *Nagara architecture*) [regional]
  - *Sub-style — Kaliṅga (Oḍiśā):* the eastern regional Nāgara of Odisha, with its own vocabulary — the sanctum-tower called **deul/rekha-deul**, the hall **jagamohana/piḍhā-deul**, classed into **rekha, piḍhā and khākharā** orders; codified in the Odia *Śilpa-śāstra* **Śilpa-prakāśa** and **Bhuvana-pradīpa**. Exemplars: **Liṅgarāja (Bhubaneswar), Jagannātha (Puri), Sūrya temple (Koṇārka)**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kalinga architecture*; *Shilpa Prakasha*) [regional]
- **Drāviḍa — the Southern order** — temples whose sanctum-shrine is a **storeyed, pyramidal *vimāna*** — receding horizontal **tālas** (storeys) stepping up to a single domed **śikhara** finial (the **stūpī/kalaśa** on top) — set within **concentric walled enclosures (*prākāra*)** entered through towering **gopurams**, the gopurams typically out-scaling the vimāna in mature examples. Prevails south of the Vindhyas/Kṛṣṇā. Exemplars: **Kailāsanātha & the Bṛhadīśvara (Tañjāvūr) Cōḻa temples; Mīnākṣī (Madurai); the Pallava shore-temples of Mahābalipuram**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Dravidian architecture*; Encyclopaedia Britannica) [scholarly]
  - *Feature — gopuram inversion:* uniquely in Drāviḍa the **gateway out-grows the shrine** — successive dynasties added ever-taller gopurams to the outer walls, so the visual climax shifts from centre to perimeter. (Source: Wikipedia, *Gopuram*) [scholarly]
- **Vesara — the Deccan hybrid** — the **intermediate / "mule" order** of the central Deccan, **fusing Nāgara and Drāviḍa**: it keeps the **storeyed, stepped articulation** of the Drāviḍa vimāna but pulls the contour toward a **more rounded, semi-curvilinear** Nāgara-like profile, often on a **stellate (star-shaped) plan**. Developed under the **Cālukyas (Bādāmi/Aihoḷe/Paṭṭadakal experiments) and perfected by the Hoysaḷas (Belūr, Halebīḍu, Somanāthapura) and Rāṣṭrakūṭas**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vesara*; *Badami Chalukya architecture*) [scholarly]
  - *Variant — definitional dispute:* the **name "Vesara" is unstable**. Some *śilpa* texts and modern scholars define the three orders by the **shape of the plan/finial** rather than region — **Nāgara = square, Drāviḍa = octagonal, Vesara = round/apsidal** — so "Vesara" sometimes denotes an *apsidal* plan, not the Deccan hybrid; the geographic and the geometric definitions do **not** coincide. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vesara*; *Vāstu Śāstra*) [disputed]
  - *Variant — fourth/more orders:* some treatises add further types; the *Kāmikāgama* and allied South-Indian texts speak of additional categories beyond the standard three, and several texts substitute their own names for the triad. (Source: *Mānasāra*; Wikipedia, *Hindu temple architecture*) [late text]

## III. The Governing Treatises (the *Śilpa/Vāstu* Corpus)

The architecture is text-bound: a temple is "correct" only if measured per the *śāstra*. Full bibliographic detail → [`../90-literature-corpus/README.md`](../90-literature-corpus/README.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Vāstu Śāstra*; *Shilpa Shastras*)

- **Mānasāra** — the most comprehensive surviving **Vāstu/Śilpa Śāstra**, a systematic manual of measurement, plan, *prāsāda*-types, iconometry and town-planning; the standard reference for the South Indian tradition. (Source: Wikipedia, *Manasara*) [late text — dating disputed]
- **Mayamata** — a South Indian *Vāstu-śāstra* attributed to the *asura*-architect **Maya**, treating ground-plans, foundations, *adhiṣṭhāna*, vimānas and dwellings; a Drāviḍa-tradition companion to the Mānasāra. (Source: Wikipedia, *Mayamata*) [late text]
- **Aparājitapṛcchā** — a Western-Indian (Gujarat/Mālwā) *śilpa* encyclopaedia (attributed to Bhuvanadeva, c. 12th c.), key for **Nāgara** temple-forms, *prāsāda* classification and the *Bhūmija/Śekharī* spires. (Source: Wikipedia, *Aparajitaprccha*) [late text · regional]
- **Bṛhat Saṃhitā (of Varāhamihira, 6th c.)** — its chapters on *prāsāda-lakṣaṇa* give an **early, pre-classification** account of temple types and proportions — one of the oldest datable textual witnesses to temple-form. (Source: Wikipedia, *Brihat Samhita*) [scholarly]
- **Sthāpatya-veda** — the **traditional "Veda of architecture,"** counted an *upaveda* (often of the *Atharvaveda*), the umbrella under which *vāstu-vidyā* and the *śilpa* corpus are placed as revealed knowledge. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sthapatya Veda*; *Vāstu Śāstra*) [traditional framing — the "first clock"]
- **Agni Purāṇa & Matsya Purāṇa (vāstu/temple chapters)** — the two *Purāṇas* carrying substantial **temple- and image-building sections**: the *Agni* details *prāsāda*-types and consecration, the *Matsya* preserves an early list of **prāsāda varieties and the mythic architect-sages** (*Viśvakarmā* and the eighteen teachers of *vāstu*). (Source: Wikipedia, *Agni Purana*; *Matsya Purana*; *Vāstu Śāstra*) [Purāṇic]
  - *Note on the architect-god:* the entire craft is mythically derived from **Viśvakarmā**, the gods' architect, and (in the Drāviḍa stream) the *asura* **Maya** — the *śilpins* trace their guild-lineage to them. Cross-ref Viśvakarmā in [`../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/). (Source: *Matsya Purāṇa*; Wikipedia, *Vishvakarma*)
