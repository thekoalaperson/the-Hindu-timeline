# The Nāṭya Śāstra & the Classical Performing Arts

> Sits in [`09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/`](README.md) — the **theology of performance**: the *Nāṭyaveda* ("fifth Veda") that Brahmā distilled from the four Vedas and gave to the sage **Bharata Muni**, and the rasa-, abhinaya- and dance-systems that descend from it. Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md) · master index [`The Hindu Timeline`](../README.md). The arts here are the *performing* members of the [Sixty-Four Kalās](sixty-four-kalas.md) (gīta–vādya–nṛtya–nāṭya); the **Naṭarāja**/Tāṇḍava iconography is shared with [`sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md`](sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md); the dancing Śiva's myth-cycle is in [`../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md); the texts themselves (Veda, Upaveda, Āgama) are indexed in [`../90-literature-corpus/README.md`](../90-literature-corpus/README.md).

The **Nāṭya Śāstra** (*Nāṭyaśāstra*, "treatise on dramaturgy") of **Bharata Muni** is the foundational text of Indian theatre, dance and music — a Sanskrit work of some **6,000 verses in 36 (or 37) chapters**, conventionally dated c. **200 BCE–200 CE** but claiming a divine, beginning-less origin. It is the source of **rasa theory**, of the four-fold *abhinaya*, of the *nṛtta–nṛtya–nāṭya* scheme, and — through its 4th chapter (*Tāṇḍava-lakṣaṇa*) and the 108 *karaṇas* — of the entire classical-dance grammar. Two clocks: the text's **scriptural** self-dating places its revelation in the cosmic past (a Nāṭyaveda drawn by Brahmā at the Devas' request), while the **historical** clock places Bharata's redaction in the late centuries BCE / early CE. [scholarly]

## I. The divine origin-myth — the Nāṭyaveda as "fifth Veda"

- **The Devas petition Brahmā** — at the dawn of the *Tretā-yuga* (per the text's frame), the gods, finding the masses given to sensual pleasure, ask **Brahmā** for a *krīḍanīyaka* — a "plaything" or diversion — that, unlike the four Vedas, would be **accessible to all four varṇas**, including the śūdras barred from Vedic recitation. (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* ch. 1) [scriptural]
- **The Fifth Veda drawn from the four** — Brahmā enters *yoga*, recalls the four Vedas, and creates the **Nāṭyaveda** by drawing one element from each: **recitation (*pāṭhya*) from the Ṛgveda**, **song (*gīta*) from the Sāmaveda**, **gesture/representation (*abhinaya*) from the Yajurveda**, and **sentiment/aesthetic flavour (*rasa*) from the Atharvaveda**. (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* 1.17–1.18) [scriptural]
- **Transmission to Bharata** — Brahmā confers the Nāṭyaveda on the sage **Bharata Muni**, who teaches it to his **hundred sons** and stages the first plays with the celestial **Apsarās** (for the feminine *lāsya* graces) and **Gandharvas/Kinnaras** (for music). (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* ch. 1)
- **The first performance & the Banner Festival** — the inaugural drama, depicting the gods' victory over the **Asuras** (the *Amṛtamanthana* / *Tripura-dāha* themes), is staged at **Indra's Dhvaja-mahā** (banner festival); the offended Asuras disrupt it, prompting Brahmā to ordain the protective **stage (*raṅga*) and its presiding deities** and the *pūrvaraṅga* preliminaries. (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* chs. 1, 3, 5)
- **Śiva and Pārvatī complete the art** — Brahmā/Bharata's drama lacks the dance-forms; **Śiva** contributes the vigorous **Tāṇḍava** (taught through **Taṇḍu**, his attendant — hence one etymology of *tāṇḍava*), and **Pārvatī** contributes the graceful **Lāsya**. Thus the *aesthetic* descends from Brahmā but the *dance* from the divine couple. (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* ch. 4; Abhinavagupta, *Abhinavabhāratī*) — see [`../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md).

## II. Rasa theory — the eight (and ninth) flavours

- **The doctrine** — *"na hi rasād ṛte kaścid arthaḥ pravartate"* ("no meaning proceeds without rasa," NŚ 6). **Rasa** ("juice, essence, flavour") is the *relished aesthetic emotion* evoked in the spectator; it arises from the combination of **vibhāva** (determinant/stimulus), **anubhāva** (consequent reaction) and **vyabhicāri-bhāva** (transient states), resting on a **sthāyi-bhāva** (a durable, latent emotion). (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* 6, the *rasa-sūtra*)
- **The eight classical rasas + their sthāyi-bhāvas** (NŚ 6):
  - **Śṛṅgāra** (the erotic/amorous) — sthāyi *rati* (love); deity **Viṣṇu**; colour śyāma (dark).
  - **Hāsya** (the comic) — sthāyi *hāsa* (mirth); deity **Pramatha**; colour white.
  - **Karuṇa** (the pathetic/compassionate) — sthāyi *śoka* (grief); deity **Yama**; colour grey.
  - **Raudra** (the furious) — sthāyi *krodha* (anger); deity **Rudra**; colour red.
  - **Vīra** (the heroic) — sthāyi *utsāha* (energy/valour); deity **Indra (Mahendra)**; colour wheat-gold.
  - **Bhayānaka** (the terrible) — sthāyi *bhaya* (fear); deity **Kāla**; colour black.
  - **Bībhatsa** (the odious/disgusting) — sthāyi *jugupsā* (revulsion); deity **Mahākāla**; colour blue.
  - **Adbhuta** (the marvellous) — sthāyi *vismaya* (astonishment); deity **Brahmā**; colour yellow.
- **The ninth rasa — Śānta** — the **peaceful/tranquil** rasa, sthāyi *śama* or *nirveda* (quietude / world-weariness), keyed to *mokṣa*; **not in Bharata's original eight**, it was added later (notably argued by **Abhinavagupta** and **Udbhaṭa**) to make the canonical **nava-rasa** ("nine rasas"). Some Bhakti and Vaiṣṇava aestheticians further add **Bhakti** and **Vātsalya** (parental love) as additional rasas. (Source: Abhinavagupta, *Abhinavabhāratī*; *Daśarūpaka* of Dhanaṃjaya) [disputed — 8 vs 9 vs 11]

## III. Abhinaya — the four modes of representation

- **Abhinaya** ("carrying toward" the spectator) is *acting itself*, divided in NŚ 6–8 into **four** kinds:
  - **Āṅgika** — *bodily*: expression through limbs, *mudrās/hastas* (hand-gestures), gait, the head-eye-neck movements. (Codified later in Nandikeśvara's *Abhinaya Darpaṇa*.)
  - **Vācika** — *vocal/verbal*: speech, intonation, recitation, song, prosody.
  - **Āhārya** — *extraneous*: costume, make-up, ornament, stage-property and set (the *nepathya*).
  - **Sāttvika** — *involuntary/temperamental*: the eight **sāttvika-bhāvas** that betray genuine feeling — *stambha* (paralysis), *sveda* (sweat), *romāñca* (horripilation), *svara-bheda* (voice-change), *vepathu* (trembling), *vaivarṇya* (pallor), *aśru* (tears), *pralaya* (fainting). The hardest and most prized mode. (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* 6–8; Nandikeśvara, *Abhinaya Darpaṇa*)

## IV. Nṛtta–Nṛtya–Nāṭya and the mārga tradition

- **Nṛtta** — *pure, abstract dance*: rhythmic movement and pattern with **no meaning or rasa**, valued for line and tāla. The 108 **karaṇas** (units of movement, *karaṇa* = a transient pose linking *sthāna* + *cārī* + *nṛtta-hasta*) and the **32 aṅgahāras** of NŚ ch. 4 belong here — famously sculpted in the gopuras of **Cidambaram** (Chidambaram) and **Tanjore (Bṛhadīśvara)**. (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* 4)
- **Nṛtya** — *expressive/interpretive dance*: dance that **conveys bhāva and rasa**, combining nṛtta with abhinaya; the storytelling solo-dance register (the later *abhinaya* pieces of Bharatanāṭyam, Odissi, etc.).
- **Nāṭya** — *drama proper*: full theatre, uniting all four abhinayas and dialogue; the most complete art, of which nṛtta and nṛtya are members. (Source: Nandikeśvara, *Abhinaya Darpaṇa*, which fixes this triad)
- **Mārga vs. Deśī** — the **mārga** ("path") tradition is the pan-Indian, śāstra-codified, Bharata-sanctioned classical form; **deśī** ("regional/of-the-country") covers local, folk and provincial styles. Later texts (Śārṅgadeva's *Saṅgīta-ratnākara*) systematize both. (Source: Śārṅgadeva, *Saṅgīta-ratnākara*) [scholarly]

## V. The Gāndharvaveda lineage & the commentarial tradition

- **Gāndharvaveda — the Upaveda of music** — the performing arts are classed as the **Gāndharvaveda**, the *upaveda* (sub-Veda) attached to the **Sāmaveda** (the Veda of melody); it governs *gīta* (song), *vādya* (instruments) and *nṛtya* (dance). It is one of the four Upavedas — see [`sixty-four-kalas.md`](sixty-four-kalas.md) §IV and [`../90-literature-corpus/README.md`](../90-literature-corpus/README.md). (Source: Gāndharvaveda upaveda tradition; *Caraṇavyūha*)
- **Abhinavabhāratī (Abhinavagupta, c. 1000 CE)** — the great Kashmiri Śaiva polymath's commentary on the Nāṭya Śāstra; the **only surviving ancient commentary** and the key to the text's interpretation. Abhinavagupta's *rasa*-aesthetics (rasa as *camatkāra*, a flash of disinterested bliss akin to *brahmānanda*) and his championing of **śānta-rasa** are decisive for all later theory. (Source: Abhinavagupta, *Abhinavabhāratī*) [scholarly]
- **Abhinaya Darpaṇa ("Mirror of Gesture," Nandikeśvara)** — the concise practical manual of *āṅgika abhinaya*: it catalogs the **hastas** (single *asaṃyukta* and combined *saṃyukta* hand-gestures), head/eye/neck/foot movements, and fixes the **nṛtta–nṛtya–nāṭya** definitions used by living dance-styles. (Source: Nandikeśvara, *Abhinaya Darpaṇa*)
- **Saṅgīta-ratnākara ("Ocean of Music & Dance," Śārṅgadeva, 13th c.)** — composed at the **Yādava** court of Devagiri; the great synthesis of the music–dance science between the NŚ and the modern era, authoritative for **both** Hindustani and Carnatic traditions, and the bridge-text for *mārga/deśī* and the later *tāla–rāga* systems. (Source: Śārṅgadeva, *Saṅgīta-ratnākara*) [scholarly]
- *Other anchors:* **Daśarūpaka** (Dhanaṃjaya, on the ten play-types); **Saṅgīta-makaranda**; **Nāṭya-darpaṇa** (Jain); the **Nartana-nirṇaya** (Puṇḍarīka Viṭṭhala) — the deśī-dance corpus.

## VI. Tāṇḍava & Lāsya — the masculine and feminine principles of dance

The core dance-theology pairs two complementary cosmic dances — **Śiva's Tāṇḍava** and **Pārvatī's Lāsya** — read as the *puruṣa* (masculine, vigorous) and *prakṛti/strī* (feminine, tender) poles of all dance. NŚ ch. 4 derives the technique; the **Śiva Purāṇa** and **Śaiva Āgamas** supply the theology.

- **Tāṇḍava** — the **vigorous, forceful, masculine** dance of **Śiva**, expressing cosmic energy, creation–and–destruction, and the violent rasas (raudra, vīra, bhayānaka). Etymologized either from **Taṇḍu** (Śiva's attendant who first demonstrated it to Bharata) or from *taṇḍ-* ("to leap/strike"). Its sub-forms (variously **seven, nine or sixteen** by source) include:
  - *Ānanda-tāṇḍava (NŚ / Śaiva Āgama):* the **"dance of bliss"** of **Naṭarāja** at **Cidambaram** (the *cit-sabhā*/golden hall) — Śiva enacting the **five acts (*pañcakṛtya*)**: *sṛṣṭi* (creation, the *ḍamaru*), *sthiti* (preservation, *abhaya-mudrā*), *saṃhāra* (destruction, *agni*), *tirobhāva* (concealment, the *apasmāra*-dwarf trampled underfoot), *anugraha* (grace, the raised foot). The iconographic anchor — see [`sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md`](sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md). [scriptural]
  - *Rudra/Saṃhāra-tāṇḍava:* the **furious dance of cosmic dissolution** at the *pralaya*, danced in the burning-ground; the **Raudra-tāṇḍava** of destruction.
  - *Ūrdhva-tāṇḍava:* the **"upraised-leg" dance**, foot lifted vertically above the head — the supreme *karaṇa*, danced in the **dance-contest with Kālī** (see below); the **Ūrdhva-jānu** pose. [regional]
  - *The Kālī dance-contest (the Tillai / Cidambaram legend):* to subdue the fierce goddess **Kālī** (Tilai-Kāḷi), Śiva challenges her to a dance and performs the **ūrdhva-tāṇḍava**, dropping an earring and retrieving it with his foot while keeping the leg raised — a pose **modesty forbids Kālī to copy**, conceding the contest. (Source: regional Cidambaram *sthala-purāṇa*; *Kōyil Purāṇam*) [regional/folk]
  - *Other named tāṇḍavas:* **Sandhyā-tāṇḍava** (the twilight dance before the gods), **Tripura-tāṇḍava**, **Saṃhāra-, Ananda-, Uma-, Gauri-, Kālikā-** and **Muni-tāṇḍava** — lists differ by Āgama. [variant]
- **Lāsya** — the **graceful, gentle, lyrical, feminine** dance of **Pārvatī** (Gaurī/Umā), the complement and counterpart of the Tāṇḍava. *Lāsya* (from *las-*, "to play, shine, sport") denotes dance that is **tender, sensuous and śṛṅgāra-laden** — soft of limb, slow of tempo, rich in *bhāva* and amorous *abhinaya*, expressing love, beauty and the gentle rasas. Where the Tāṇḍava is angular, leaping and *uddhata* (forceful), the Lāsya is *sukumāra/lalita* (delicate), curvilinear and emotive. Tradition counts **ten (or more) aṅgas** ("limbs/components") of Lāsya in NŚ ch. 4–19 — e.g. *geyapada, sthitapāṭhya, āsīna, puṣpagaṇḍikā, pracchedaka, trimūḍha/trigūḍha, saindhavākhya, dvimūḍha, uttamottamaka, uktapratyukta* — a structured sequence of song-and-mime episodes. Pārvatī is said to have **taught the Lāsya to the women** (Uṣā, Bāṇāsura's daughter, and thence to the women of Dvārakā in the Kṛṣṇa cycle), grounding the feminine solo-dance lineage. (Source: *Nāṭya Śāstra* 4; Śiva Purāṇa; Śaiva Āgamas) [scriptural]
- **The polarity as theology** — the pair is read cosmologically: **Tāṇḍava = puruṣa**, the destructive-creative cosmic male energy; **Lāsya = prakṛti/strī**, the gentle generative female energy. Together, danced by the **Ardhanārīśvara** couple, they are the two halves of *Naṭarāja-rūpa* — motion (Śiva) and its grace-bearing complement (Śakti). Neither is complete alone; classical solo-dance (Bharatanāṭyam, Odissi, Mohinīyāṭṭam) interweaves both modes. (Source: Śaiva Āgama tradition; Coomaraswamy, *The Dance of Śiva*) [scholarly]
