# Temple Iconographic Motifs: Kīrtimukha, Yāḷi/Vyāla, Makara-toraṇa and Guardian Ornament

> Sits in [`09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/`](README.md) — the **recurring ornamental and apotropaic vocabulary** of the Hindu temple: the devouring *kīrtimukha* mask, the leonine *yāḷi/vyāla*, the crocodile-arch (*makara-toraṇa*), and the allied threshold-guardians (*dvārapāla*, *gajalakṣmī* lintel) that frame, protect and "consecrate" every doorway and pillar.

This file treats the temple's **decorative-protective iconography** — the carved beings and arches that recur on doorframes, pillars, balustrades and image-aureoles — as a body distinct from the **mūrti-iconometry** of the cult image (the *tālamāna* canon kept in [`murti-lakshana-and-iconometry.md`](murti-lakshana-and-iconometry.md)). These motifs are not deities to be worshipped but **ornament with a guarding/auspicious function**: they devour evil, mark the threshold, and crown the gateway. **Two clocks:** the *Purāṇas* give the *kīrtimukha* an eternal origin-myth set in Śiva's combat with Jalandhara/Rāhu, while art-history places the surviving motif-vocabulary across the Gupta-and-after centuries (c. 5th–13th c. CE), inherited and codified by the *Śilpa*/*Āgama* manuals. [scholarly]

---

## I. Kīrtimukha — the "Face of Glory"

- **Kīrtimukha (the grāsa-mask)** — a fierce, horned, lion-or-demon **face with bulging eyes, a gaping fanged mouth and no lower jaw**, often disgorging foliage, beaded chains or *makaras* from its maw; placed at the **apex of doorways, niches (gavākṣa), aureoles and toraṇas** as a devouring guardian of the threshold. The name means "face of glory" (*kīrti* = glory/fame). Also called **grāsa** ("the devourer / morsel") and, regionally, **siṃhamukha** ("lion-face"). (Source: Skanda Purāṇa; Śiva Purāṇa; A. K. Coomaraswamy, "The Face of Glory") [scholarly]
- **Origin-myth — the wrath-being that ate itself** — when the *asura* **Jalandhara** sends **Rāhu** as envoy to demand Śiva surrender Pārvatī, Śiva's wrath bursts from his brow as a ravening lion-faced being. Rāhu begs mercy; with no other prey, Śiva commands the creature to **devour its own body**, leaving only the face. Śiva, pleased, decrees it shall remain forever at his door as **Kīrtimukha** — "no worshipper shall pass me who does not first honour you." Hence its station above the lintel. (Source: Skanda Purāṇa; Śiva Purāṇa; Coomaraswamy 1939)
  - *Variant (Rāhu-conflation):* in some tellings the swallowing-being is identified with, or doubles, **Rāhu** the eclipse-demon (the head-only graha who "swallows" sun and moon) — the self-consuming, jawless face reads as a cousin of the eclipse-myth. (Source: Coomaraswamy, "The Face of Glory") [disputed]
  - *Variant (cosmic Devourer / All-consuming Time):* Coomaraswamy reads the *kīrtimukha* as the iconic **"Face of Glory" / All-devourer** — Death-and-Time (*kāla*) that consumes all and is transcended at the door of the deity, paralleling the *Bṛhadāraṇyaka* "Death as devourer" theme. (Source: Coomaraswamy, "The Face of Glory") [scholarly]
- **On the prabhā / prabhāvalī** — the *kīrtimukha* habitually crowns the **aureole-arch (prabhā, tiruvāsi)** behind bronze and stone images, most famously surmounting the flame-ringed **prabhāvalī of Naṭarāja**, from whose mouth the encircling band of fire and *makaras* issues. The motif thus migrates from architecture onto the image-frame (but is ornament *of the frame*, not part of the *mūrti*'s own iconometry). (Source: Śilpa/Āgama iconography; T. A. Gopinatha Rao) [scholarly]

## II. The Arch-Family — Gavākṣa, Toraṇa, Makara-toraṇa

- **Gavākṣa / kūḍu (the "cow's-eye" / horseshoe-arch)** — the recurring **horseshoe-shaped arch-motif** (literally "ox-eye / dormer-window"), descended from the *caitya*-window of rock-cut architecture; in the South called **kūḍu/kudu**, often enshrining a small face. Massed gavākṣas (the "*candraśālā*" net) clothe the śikhara/vimāna, and a *kīrtimukha* frequently fills the arch-head. (Source: Mānasāra; Śilpa-śāstra tradition; Wikipedia, *Gavaksha*) [scholarly]
- **Toraṇa (the ornamental lintel-arch / gateway)** — the **decorative arched lintel** spanning a doorway, niche or image, and (in its free-standing form) the festal **gateway** before a shrine; the carved *makara-toraṇa* and *citra-toraṇa* hang over sanctum doorways and over enthroned images as a canopy of glory. (Source: Mānasāra; Mayamata; Śilpa/Āgama) [scholarly]
- **Makara-toraṇa (the crocodile-arch)** — the most distinctive temple arch: an arch **springing from a pair of *makaras*** (the composite crocodile-elephant-fish sea-beast) set at the lower jambs, their up-curled snouts and tails generating the foliate band of the arch, which **rises to a kīrtimukha at the crown** — so the arch is "devoured-and-disgorged" between *makara* feet and *grāsa* head. Flanks **sanctum doorways**, niche-images and the *prabhāvalī*. (Source: Śilpa/Āgama doorway-canon; Mayamata; Coomaraswamy) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (the makara as Gaṅgā/Varuṇa vehicle):* the same beast is **Gaṅgā's and Varuṇa's vāhana** and the emblem of Kāma's banner — so the *makara*-jamb also reads as a **water-symbol** purifying the threshold (cross-ref the makara in [`sacred-flora-and-fauna.md`](sacred-flora-and-fauna.md) and the vāhana-roster). (Source: Wikipedia, *Makara (Hindu mythology)*) [scholarly]

## III. Yāḷi / Vyāla / Śārdūla — the Guardian-Beast of Pillar and Balustrade

- **Yāḷi (Tamil) / vyāla / śārdūla (the leonine composite)** — a **rampant, fanged composite beast**, basically **leonine but blended with elephant (trunk/tusks), horse, makara or human** elements, carved **rearing on pillars, brackets, balustrades (vedikā) and the base-mouldings** of South-Indian temples as a protective force; often shown **trampling an elephant** (*gaja-vyāla*) or with a warrior-rider. The Sanskrit *vyāla*/*śārdūla* and the Tamil *yāḷi* name the same guardian-monster. (Source: Mānasāra (pillar-ornament); Mayamata; Śilpa-śāstra) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (named sub-types):* the *śilpa* texts enumerate *vyāla* varieties by the dominant animal — **siṃha-vyāla** (lion), **gaja-vyāla** (elephant), **aśva-vyāla** (horse), **nara-vyāla** (man-faced), **makara-vyāla** and others — used to vary pillar-rows. (Source: Mānasāra; Kāśyapa-śilpa tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant (vyālamālā band):* a continuous frieze of *vyālas* (*vyāla-vari/yāḷi-vari*) runs as a **base-moulding course**, especially in Cōḻa-Pallava and later Vijayanagara work; the colossal pillar-yāḷis of the Vijayanagara/Nāyaka *maṇḍapas* (e.g. Śrīraṅgam, Madurai) are the motif's baroque climax. (Source: Drāviḍa pillar-canon; art-history) [regional]

## IV. Threshold-Guardians — Dvārapāla and the Gajalakṣmī Lintel

- **Dvārapāla (the door-guardian pair)** — the **flanking sentinel-figures** stationed at either jamb of the sanctum and gateway: muscular, club-bearing (*gadā*) or weapon-armed, often tusked and fierce, frequently named for the deity within (e.g. **Jaya & Vijaya** at Vaiṣṇava doors; **Nandin & Mahākāla**, or Caṇḍa & Pracaṇḍa, at Śaiva doors; the **Saptamātṛkā/Gaṅgā-Yamunā** river-goddesses at Gupta-temple jambs). They control entry and embody the deity's outermost guard. (Source: Śilpa/Āgama dvāra-canon; Agni Purāṇa; T. A. Gopinatha Rao) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Gaṅgā-Yamunā jamb-figures):* in early Nāgara doorways the two river-goddesses **Gaṅgā (on her makara) and Yamunā (on her tortoise)** stand at the base of the doorjambs as purifying guardians — a distinct, older threshold convention. (Source: Gupta-temple doorway tradition; art-history) [scholarly]
- **Gajalakṣmī lintel (lalāṭa-bimba)** — the **central block of the doorway-lintel** (*lalāṭa-bimba*, "forehead-image") most often carries **Gajalakṣmī** — Śrī-Lakṣmī seated on a lotus, lustrated by a pair of flanking **elephants pouring water from upraised trunks** — as the **auspicious overseer of the door**; on Śaiva doors the lalāṭa-bimba may instead show Gaṇeśa or a small *liṅga*, on Vaiṣṇava doors Garuḍa or Viṣṇu. The lintel-centre, the *kīrtimukha* above it, and the *dvārapālas* below form one **integrated threshold-program**. (Source: Śilpa/Āgama dvāra-lakṣaṇa; Mayamata; Wikipedia, *Gajalakshmi*) [scholarly]

## V. How the Threshold Reads as a Whole

- **The doorway as a guarding-machine** — read together, the elements stack into a single apotropaic frame: **dvārapālas** at the jambs, **river-goddesses / makara-feet** at the base, the **makara-toraṇa** arching over, the **gajalakṣmī** at the lintel-heart, and the **kīrtimukha** devouring all evil at the crown — every worshipper passes *through the mouth of glory* into the *garbhagṛha*. (Source: Śilpa/Āgama dvāra-canon; Coomaraswamy) [scholarly]
- **Ornament, not icon** — none of these is a *cult image* receiving *prāṇa-pratiṣṭhā*; they are **alaṅkāra** (ornament) with protective force, governed by the *śilpa* chapters on *dvāra*, *stambha* (pillar) and *toraṇa*, not by the *bera-/pratimā-lakṣaṇa* of the enshrined deity (kept distinct in [`murti-lakshana-and-iconometry.md`](murti-lakshana-and-iconometry.md)). (Source: Mānasāra; Mayamata) [scholarly]
