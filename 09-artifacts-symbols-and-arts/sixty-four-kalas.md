# The Sixty-Four Kalās — Arts & Sciences

> Sits in [`09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/`](README.md) — the **disciplines a cultured/perfected being masters**: the *Catuḥṣaṣṭi Kalā* (sixty-four fine arts/skills) and the *vidyās* (branches of knowledge) that, together, define the educated ideal of classical Bhārata.
> Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md). The anchoring myth: **Kṛṣṇa** mastered all **64 kalās + 14 vidyās in 64 days** under guru **Sāndīpani** at Avantī (Ujjayinī) — see [`02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/). Text-source: Vātsyāyana's ***Kāmasūtra*** — see [`90-literature-corpus/`](../90-literature-corpus/).

*Kalā* (कला) = "a part/portion; performing art, skill." In the tradition **Śiva is the master of Kalā** (the *Kalā-mūrti*), **Sarasvatī** the patron-goddess of arts and letters (titled *Kalānidhi*, "treasury of arts," or *Chausaṭh-Kalāmayī*), and the great goddess **Lalitā** is invoked in the *Lalitā-sahasranāma* as **Catuḥṣaṣṭi-kalā-mayī** ("she who embodies the sixty-four arts," nāma 236). The number **64** is itself auspicious (= 8 × 8, the squared aṣṭa). Per repo policy, **every variant name, count, and ordering is cataloged to source**; the roster is **semi-fixed but genuinely variant** — membership, wording, and even the total **shift by text and century**. [disputed]

---

## I. The framing — kalā vs. vidyā

The classical curriculum pairs two registers of learning:

- **Vidyā** (विद्या) - *knowledge / science*: the theoretical, scripture-rooted disciplines (the Vedas and their auxiliaries). Counted as **14** or **18** (see §IV).
- **Kalā** (कला) - *art / applied skill*: the **64** practical and aesthetic accomplishments — song, dance, painting, cookery, gambling, cryptography, gardening, and so on (see §III).
- **The ideal:** "a person knowing the 64 kalās along with the 14 vidyās was said to be **near perfection**." Mastery of the 64 earns a courtesan the honorific **Gaṇikā** and "a seat of honour in the assembly of men" (Vātsyāyana). (Source: *Kāmasūtra* 1.3; Sanskriti Magazine, *64 Kalas and 14 Vidyas*)
- **Gender note:** the *Kāmasūtra* explicitly says the 64 arts are to be learned by **women as well as men** — by daughters of kings, ministers, and by *gaṇikās*. (Source: *Kāmasūtra* 1.3; Wikipedia, *Kalā*)

## II. The anchoring myths (who embodies the 64)

- **Kṛṣṇa at Sāndīpani's āśrama** - After slaying **Kaṃsa** and undergoing the sacred-thread (*upanayana*) rite, **Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma** studied under guru **Sāndīpani** at **Avantī / Ujjayinī** (modern Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh — the *Sandipani Ashram* is still shown there). There, **within 64 days**, Kṛṣṇa is said to have mastered the **14 vidyās and 64 kalās** — each of which would take an ordinary student years. As *guru-dakṣiṇā* he then restored Sāndīpani's drowned son from the sea/Yama. (Source: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.45; Sanskriti Magazine; Vedic Tribe, *64 Kalas and 14 Vidyas*) [Kṛṣṇa cycle]
- **Śiva — Kalā-master** - In the scriptures Śiva is the lord of all arts; the cosmic dance (*Naṭarāja*) and the *Nāṭya/Tāṇḍava* descend from him. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kalā*)
- **Sarasvatī — Kalānidhi** - Goddess of speech, learning, music, and the arts; her **vīṇā** (instrument) makes her the patron of the performing kalās. Cross-link icon-attribute in [`02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/). (Source: Wikipedia, *Kalā*; tradition)
- **Lalitā / Devī — Catuḥṣaṣṭi-kalā-mayī** - In the **[Lalitā-sahasranāma]** (a Śākta text of the *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*), the goddess is the embodiment of all 64 arts (nāma *Catuḥṣaṣṭi-kalā-mayī*) and is attended by the **64 Yoginīs** and the **64 Tantras** (a deliberate numerological echo). (Source: Wikipedia, *Lalita Sahasranama*; isatsang, *Lalita Sahasranama with meaning*) [Śākta]

## III. The Sixty-Four Kalās — the *Kāmasūtra* roster

The canonical enumeration is Vātsyāyana's, in *Kāmasūtra* Book 1, ch. 3 (the *Vidyā-samuddeśa*). Numbering and wording below follow the standard transmitted list (Sanskrit name — gloss). Where popular lists drop, merge, or re-order items, that is flagged. [disputed numbering]

1. **Gīta** - *singing.* (Source: *Kāmasūtra* 1.3)
2. **Vādya** - *playing musical instruments.*
3. **Nṛtya** - *dancing.*
4. **Nāṭya** - *theatre / dramatic performance.* (Often the *gīta–vādya–nṛtya–nāṭya* tetrad is treated as the core "performing arts.")
5. **Ālekhya** - *painting / drawing.*
6. **Viśeṣaka-cchedya** - *decorating the face/forehead with coloured marks (tilaka-cutting / stencils).* Cross-link sectarian marks in [`sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md`](sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md).
7. **Taṇḍula-kusuma-balivikāra** - *making offering-designs (rangoli/maṇḍala) from coloured rice and flowers.*
8. **Puṣpāstaraṇa** - *arranging flowers; making flower-beds/coverlets.*
9. **Daśana-vasanāṅga-rāga** - *colouring the teeth, garments, and limbs (cosmetic dyeing).*
10. **Maṇi-bhūmikā-karma** - *inlaying floors/grounds with jewels (mosaic / jewelled flooring).*
11. **Śayyā-racana** - *arranging / dressing the bed.* (*Variant: "Aayya-racana" in some web transcriptions — a misreading of* Śayyā.)
12. **Udaka-vādya** - *making music upon / with water (the "water-organ," jala-taraṅga type).*
13. **Udaka-ghāta** - *water-sports; splashing/squirting games.*
14. **Citra-yoga** - *mixing/applying colours; the art of pigment admixtures.*
15. **Mālya-grathana-vikalpa** - *stringing and designing garlands/wreaths.*
16. **Śekhara-āpīḍa-yojana** - *arranging crests and chaplets on the head (coronet-setting).*
17. **Nepathya-yoga** - *costuming and theatrical dressing-up / disguise.*
18. **Karṇapatra-bhaṅga** - *decorating the ear; making ear-ornaments (of ivory, leaf, etc.).*
19. **Sugandha-yukti** - *compounding perfumes and aromatics.*
20. **Bhūṣaṇa-yojana** - *applying / arranging ornaments and jewellery.*
21. **Aindrajāla** - *magic; jugglery / illusion ("Indra's net").*
22. **Kaucumāra** - *the "Kuchumāra" arts — cosmetic/aphrodisiac recipes for beautification and rejuvenation* (named after the sage **Kucumāra**). [obscure]
23. **Hasta-lāghava** - *manual dexterity; sleight of hand.*
24. **Citra-śāka-yūṣa-bhakṣya-vikāra-kriyā** - *preparing varied and tasty foods (vegetables, soups, edibles).* (Cookery.)
25. **Pānaka-rasa-rāgāsava-yojana** - *preparing palatable drinks, syrups, and (coloured) liquors/āsavas.*
26. **Sūcī-vāya-karma** - *needlework, sewing, and weaving.*
27. **Sūtra-krīḍā** - *string-figures / thread-play ("cat's cradle"); puppetry with strings.*
28. **Vīṇā-ḍamaruka-vādya** - *playing the vīṇā and the ḍamaru (lute and drum).*
29. **Prahelikā** - *making and solving riddles.*
30. **Durvacaka-yoga** - *constructing words/phrases hard to pronounce or interpret (tongue-twisters).* (*Variant: paired or merged with* Pratimālā, *capping-verse contests.*)
31. **Pustaka-vācana** - *recitation / reading aloud from books (with proper modulation).*
32. **Nāṭikā-ākhyāyikā-darśana** - *staging short plays and telling tales/anecdotes.*
33. **Kāvya-samasyā-pūraṇa** - *completing/solving verse-riddles (filling in a half-verse).*
34. **Paṭṭikā-vetra-bāṇa-vikalpa** - *making cane chairs / shields, and arrows (wicker- and reed-work).*
35. **Tarku-karma** - *spinning (with spindle) and turnery.*
36. **Takṣaṇa** - *carpentry / woodwork.*
37. **Vāstu-vidyā** - *architecture and house-building (engineering).* Cross-link sacred geometry/yantra in [`sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md`](sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md).
38. **Raupya-ratna-parīkṣā** - *testing/assaying silver and gems.*
39. **Dhātu-vāda** - *metallurgy; alchemy (working of metals/ores).*
40. **Maṇi-rāga-jñāna** - *knowledge of tinting/colouring gems.*
41. **Ākara-jñāna** - *mineralogy; knowledge of mines and ore-bodies.*
42. **Vṛkṣāyurveda-yoga** - *arboriculture / plant-medicine (the "āyurveda of trees," gardening and grafting).*
43. **Meṣa-kukkuṭa-lāvaka-yuddha-vidhi** - *the rules of cock-, ram-, and quail-fighting.*
44. **Śuka-sārikā-pralāpana** - *teaching parrots and mynas (śuka/sārikā) to speak.*
45. **Utsādana** - *shampooing; cleansing and massaging the body (with unguents).*
46. **Keśa-mārjana-kauśala** - *skill in dressing/combing and styling the hair.*
47. **Akṣara-muṣṭikā-kathana** - *speaking by signs of letters made with the fingers (manual cipher / finger-talk).*
48. **Mleccha-ita-kutarka-vikalpa** *(also* **Mlecchita-vikalpa***)* - *writing/speaking in code — cryptography and secret languages.* The *only* classical Indian listing of cryptography as a discipline; see Wikipedia, *Mlecchita vikalpa*. (Some lists place this as a distinct entry near the end; others merge with #47.)
49. **Deśa-bhāṣā-jñāna** - *knowledge of provincial/regional dialects and languages.*
50. **Puṣpa-śakaṭikā-nimitta-jñāna** - *making flower-carts and reading omens.* (*Web-list variant:* "**Nirmiti-jñāna** — prediction by heavenly voices/omens.")
51. **Yantra-mātṛkā** - *the science of mechanical contrivances (machines/automata).*
52. **Dhāraṇa-mātṛkā** - *the art of memory / memory-training (mnemonics).* (*Some web lists swap this with* Dharaṇa-mātṛkā = *use of amulets.*)
53. **Sampāṭhya** *(also* **Samvācya***)* - *recitation in concert; difficult repetition; the art of conversation.*
54. **Mānasī kāvya-kriyā** - *composing verse mentally / extempore.*
55. **Kriyā-vikalpa** - *contriving designs for a literary or (medical) work; variant constructions.*
56. **Chalitaka-yoga** *(var.* **Abhidhāna-koṣa-cchando-jñāna** *in re-ordered lists)* - *building shrines / hut-construction; or knowledge of lexicons and metres.* [variant entry]
57. **Abhidhāna-koṣa-cchando-jñāna** - *knowledge of dictionaries (lexicography) and of prosody/metres.*
58. **Vastra-gopana** - *the art of disguising/altering the appearance of cloth (so old looks new, etc.).*
59. **Dyūta-viśeṣa** - *the special art of gambling (dice).*
60. **Ākarṣa-krīḍā** - *games played with dice or by magnetic attraction; board-games of "drawing."*
61. **Bālaka-krīḍanaka** - *making toys for children.*
62. **Vaināyikī vidyā** - *the science of inculcating discipline / good conduct (etiquette and propriety).*
63. **Vaijayikī vidyā** - *the science of gaining victory (strategy, polity, success).*
64. **Vaitālikī vidyā** - *the science of awakening one's master at dawn with music/song (the herald's art).*

> **Open-ended caveat.** No two printed lists agree perfectly: the standard *Kāmasūtra* roster, the Burton/Arbuthnot English numbering, and the widely-circulated web lists differ in spelling, in which compound counts as one art, and occasionally produce **65 items** (e.g. by splitting *Mlecchita-vikalpa* or *Dhāraṇa-mātṛkā*). Treat the **set, not the exact numbering, as canonical**. [disputed] (Source: *Kāmasūtra* 1.3 trans. Burton/Arbuthnot; Wikipedia, *Kalā*; Sanskriti Magazine; indiancontents.com)

## IV. The Vidyās — the branches of knowledge

The companion count. Two figures circulate: **14** (the standard "fourteen lores," *caturdaśa-vidyā*) and **18** (adding the four *Upavedas* of #15–18 below, the *aṣṭādaśa-vidyā*).

### The 14 Vidyās (*caturdaśa-vidyā*)

- **The 4 Vedas** - **Ṛgveda** (hymns), **Yajurveda** (sacrificial formulae), **Sāmaveda** (chants/melody), **Atharvaveda** (spells, applied knowledge). Cross-link [`90-literature-corpus/`](../90-literature-corpus/). (Source: Sanskriti Magazine; Wikipedia, *Vedanga*)
- **The 6 Vedāṅgas** ("limbs of the Veda," the auxiliary sciences):
  - **Śikṣā** - *phonetics / pronunciation.*
  - **Kalpa** - *ritual procedure (the Sūtra literature).*
  - **Vyākaraṇa** - *grammar.*
  - **Nirukta** - *etymology / lexical explanation.*
  - **Chandas** - *prosody / Vedic metre.*
  - **Jyotiṣa** - *astronomy-astrology (with three branches: Siddhānta, Saṃhitā/Medinī, Horā).*
- **The 4 "completing" lores** (added to make 14):
  - **Mīmāṃsā** - *exegesis (Pūrva-mīmāṃsā), the science of ritual interpretation.*
  - **Nyāya** - *logic and epistemology.*
  - **Purāṇa** - *the traditional cosmogonic/mythic histories.* Cross-link [`90-literature-corpus/`](../90-literature-corpus/).
  - **Dharmaśāstra** - *the law-codes / science of dharma.* Cross-link [`05-concepts-and-dharma/`](../05-concepts-and-dharma/).
  - *Variant (Source: Wikipedia, Caturdaśavidyā):* the four are most commonly given as **Purāṇa, Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, and Dharmaśāstra** — i.e. **4 Vedas + 6 Vedāṅgas + these 4 = 14.**

### The 18 Vidyās (*aṣṭādaśa-vidyā*)

- **The 14 above + the 4 Upavedas** ("sub-Vedas," applied sciences):
  - **Āyurveda** - *medicine / science of life* (Upaveda of the Ṛgveda or Atharvaveda by different attributions). Cross-link [`05-concepts-and-dharma/`](../05-concepts-and-dharma/).
  - **Dhanurveda** - *the science of archery / warfare* (Upaveda of the Yajurveda). Cross-link divine weapons in [`divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md`](divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md).
  - **Gāndharvaveda** - *the science of music and the performing arts* (Upaveda of the Sāmaveda).
  - **Arthaśāstra** *(or* **Sthāpatyaveda***)* - *statecraft, economics, and military strategy* (Upaveda of the Atharvaveda; some lists give *Sthāpatya/architecture* here instead). [variant]
  - *Note:* the **18** count and the **64 kalās** are sometimes jointly invoked as the full ancient curriculum: "**18 mahā-vidyās and 64 kalās**." (Source: Vedic Tribe; Sanskriti Magazine) [disputed — 14 vs 18]

## V. List variants & expansions

- *Variant — the 512 sub-arts (Jayamaṅgalā):* the **13th-c. commentary *Jayamaṅgalā* of Yaśodhara** on the *Kāmasūtra* sub-divides the 64 arts further; the tradition that each art has **eight aspects** yields the often-cited figure of **512 (= 64 × 8)** sub-kalās. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kama Sutra*; Exotic India, *Kama Sutra with the Commentary of Jayamangala*; the surviving Jayamaṅgalā has 67 sections of enumeration) [late text]
- *Variant — the 64 of the Śākta/Tantra reckoning:* alongside the arts, the goddess Lalitā is surrounded by **64 Yoginīs** and the **64 Bhairava/Kaula Tantras** — a parallel "64" series, distinct from the arts but numerologically paired. (Source: Wikipedia, *Lalita Sahasranama*) [Śākta]
- *Variant — Śaiva-Āgama / South-Indian lists:* regional teaching-lists (and Jain and Buddhist enumerations of the *bāhattara/72 kalās* or *64 kalās*) differ in membership and even in **total** (Jain texts famously give **72 arts** for men and **64** for women). The roster is therefore **open-ended across traditions**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kalā*; tradition) [disputed]
- *Variant — Burton/Arbuthnot vs. modern translations:* the popular English numbering (Sir Richard Burton & F.F. Arbuthnot, 1883) fixes a particular ordering/wording that several later translations (Daniélou; Doniger & Kakar) revise. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kama Sutra*)
