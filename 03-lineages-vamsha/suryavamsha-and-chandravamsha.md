# Solar & Lunar Dynasties (Vaṃśa)

> Hierarchy: `03-lineages-vamsha/` — the two great Kṣatriya royal genealogies that descend from **Vaivasvata Manu** (7th Manvantara, see [../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/)). Most named epic-era events sit in the **28th Mahāyuga** (Tretā for the Rāma cycle, Dvāpara for the Mahābhārata cycle). Catalogs the **Sūryavaṃśa** (Solar, via Ikṣvāku), the **Candravaṃśa** (Lunar, via Soma/Candra), and the **Saptarṣi** sage-lineages that thread through both.

---

## 0. The Common Root — Vaivasvata Manu

Both royal houses branch from the same node. The cosmological spine:

- **Brahmā → Marīci → Kaśyapa → Vivasvān (Sūrya) → Vaivasvata Manu** (a.k.a. Śrāddhadeva, survivor of the Matsya deluge). Manu's children fork into the two dynasties. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX; *Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva)
- **The Solar fork:** Manu's son **Ikṣvāku** → Sūryavaṃśa (patrilineal, Ayodhyā/Kosala).
- **The Lunar fork:** Manu's daughter/son **Ilā/Sudyumna** (gender-shifting child) unites with **Budha** (son of Candra) → their son **Purūravas** founds the Candravaṃśa. So the Lunar line is technically *matrilineal at its hinge* through Manu's line, grafted onto the Moon's. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.1; *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa*)
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata IX.1):* Ilā is born female, becomes male (Sudyumna) by Śiva/Pārvatī's boon, alternates monthly; fathers three sons as Sudyumna AND mothers Purūravas's father-line as Ilā — texts differ on whether Ilā or Sudyumna is the parent of Budha's wife.

## 1. SŪRYAVAṂŚA — The Solar Dynasty (Ikṣvāku Line)

Seat: **Ayodhyā**, kingdom of **Kosala**. Tutelary sage: **Vasiṣṭha** (hereditary purohita). The "Raghuvaṃśa" is the later, more famous name for the senior branch (after King Raghu).

### 1a. Genealogical spine (senior Ayodhyā branch)
**Ikṣvāku → Vikukṣi → Kakutstha (Purañjaya) → Anenas → … → Yuvanāśva → Māndhātṛ → … → Hariścandra → … → Sagara → Asamañja → Aṃśumān → Dilīpa I → Bhagīratha → … → Raghu → Aja → Daśaratha → Rāma → Kuśa & Lava → … → Bṛhadbala (slain in Mahābhārata war) → … → Sumitra (last king, def. by Mahāpadma Nanda).** (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.2–4; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.6–12; *Raghuvaṃśa* of Kālidāsa)

> *Variant (king-order):* Purāṇas, the *Rāmāyaṇa* (Bāla Kāṇḍa lineage recited by Vasiṣṭha), and Buddhist/Jain lists disagree on the count and order of kings between Ikṣvāku and Daśaratha — the *Rāmāyaṇa* gives a compressed ~34-king list; *Viṣṇu/Bhāgavata* give differing intermediate names. Rāma is counted as roughly the **63rd–80th** king depending on source.

### 1b. Key events / stories (Solar)
- **Ikṣvāku, the founder.** First king of Ayodhyā; eldest son of Manu; his name ("sugarcane/bitter gourd") tied to a birth legend (born of Manu's sneeze in one telling). Establishes the dynasty after the deluge. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.2; *Bhāgavata* IX.6)
- **Vikukṣi "Śaśāda".** Sent to fetch sacrificial meat, eats a hare (śaśa) himself → nicknamed Śaśāda; briefly exiled. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.2)
- **Kakutstha / Purañjaya.** Rides into battle against the Asuras seated on Indra's hump (kakud) in bull form → "Kakutstha"; the patronymic later applied to Rāma. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa*; *Rāmāyaṇa*)
- **Māndhātṛ (Mandhata).** Born from his father Yuvanāśva's side (the king drank consecrated water meant for his queen); universal emperor (cakravartin); name from Indra's "māṃ dhāsyati" ("he shall suckle me"). Slain by Lavaṇāsura's trident in some tellings. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.2; *Mahābhārata* Droṇa/Vana Parva)
- **Muciukunda (Muchukunda).** Aided the devas, granted boon of long sleep; awakened in Dvāpara, his glance incinerates Kālayavana — links Solar line to **Kṛṣṇa's** story. (*Bhāgavata* X; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*) See [../01-manvantaras/.../mahayuga-28-current/dvapara/](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md).
- **Hariścandra, the truthful.** Sells himself, wife (Śaivyā/Taramati) and son to honor a debt to **Viśvāmitra**; tested mercilessly, never lies; restored and ascended to heaven. The archetype of satya. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa*; *Aitareya Brāhmaṇa* [earlier Śunaḥśepa-linked form]; *Devī-Bhāgavata*)
  - *Variant (Aitareya Brāhmaṇa):* the older Hariścandra story centers on the **Śunaḥśepa** sacrifice — Hariścandra vows his son Rohita to Varuṇa, substitutes the boy Śunaḥśepa; the truth-test/auction motif is a later Purāṇic overlay.
- **Sagara & the 60,000 sons.** Performs Aśvamedha; his 60,000 sons dig up the earth seeking the lost horse, disturb sage **Kapila**, are burnt to ash by his glance. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.4; *Rāmāyaṇa* Bāla Kāṇḍa; *Mahābhārata* Vana Parva)
- **Bhagīratha & the descent of Gaṅgā.** Great-great-grandson of Sagara; through epic tapas brings **Gaṅgā** from heaven to liberate the ashes of Sagara's sons; Śiva catches her in his locks. The river named **Bhāgīrathī** for him. (*Rāmāyaṇa* Bāla Kāṇḍa; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*; *Bhāgavata* IX.9) See [../00-time-and-cosmology/](../00-time-and-cosmology/) for sacred-geography notes.
- **Raghu.** So illustrious the senior line is renamed **Raghuvaṃśa**; digvijaya world-conquest; gives away all wealth after the Viśvajit sacrifice (Kālidāsa's Kautsa episode). (*Raghuvaṃśa*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*)
- **Aja & Indumatī.** Raghu's son; svayaṃvara of Indumatī; her death from a falling garland → Aja's grief. (*Raghuvaṃśa* — largely Kālidāsa's literary expansion)
- **Daśaratha.** Father of Rāma; the boon-debt to **Kaikeyī**; accidental killing of **Śravaṇa Kumāra** brings the curse of dying from son-separation grief. (*Rāmāyaṇa* Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa)
- **Rāma, the 7th avatāra of Viṣṇu.** Exile, abduction of **Sītā** by **Rāvaṇa**, alliance with **Sugrīva/Hanumān**, war at Laṅkā, Rāmarājya. The dynasty's apex; Tretā Yuga. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*; *Bhāgavata* IX.10–11; *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa*; regional: *Rāmcaritmānas*, *Kamba Rāmāyaṇa*) See the dedicated Tretā event file [../01-manvantaras/.../mahayuga-28-current/treta/](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md).
- **Kuśa & Lava.** Rāma's twin sons; Kuśa inherits Kosala (capital Kuśāvatī), Lava founds Śrāvastī/Lavapura (folk-linked to Lahore). (*Uttara Kāṇḍa*; *Raghuvaṃśa* 16–17)
- **Bṛhadbala.** Last great Sūryavaṃśī of the epic age; fights for the Kauravas, slain by **Abhimanyu** in the Mahābhārata war — the explicit link between the two dynasties' timelines (Dvāpara). (*Mahābhārata* Droṇa Parva)
- **Sumitra, the end.** Traditionally the last Ikṣvāku king of Ayodhyā, defeated by **Mahāpadma Nanda** of Magadha (~362 BCE per dynastic-list reckoning); flees to Rohtas. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.22; later Purāṇic king-lists)

### 1c. The Videha / Mithilā branch (Janaka line)
A cadet Solar branch from Ikṣvāku's son **Nimi**:
- **Nimi & Vasiṣṭha's mutual curse.** Nimi and his purohita Vasiṣṭha curse each other to bodilessness over a sacrifice scheduling dispute; from Nimi's churned body is born **Janaka** ("the begetter") — hence kings of Mithilā are titled **Janaka** and **Vaideha** ("bodiless-born"). (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.5; *Bhāgavata* IX.13)
- **Sīradhvaja Janaka.** The philosopher-king of Mithilā, father of **Sītā** (found in a furrow, sīra = plough); hosts the bow-svayaṃvara won by Rāma → marries the two dynastic-cousin houses. Interlocutor of **Yājñavalkya** in the *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad*. (*Rāmāyaṇa*; *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa*; *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Up.*)
  - *Variant:* multiple "Janakas" — Purāṇas list a long Mithilā king-roll, so the Janaka of Yājñavalkya and the Janaka of Rāma may be different bearers of the title.

## 2. CANDRAVAṂŚA — The Lunar Dynasty (Soma Line)

Origin from **Candra/Soma** (the Moon). Tutelary descent runs through **Atri** (Saptarṣi). Branches across all of north India; produces both the **Yādavas** (→ Kṛṣṇa) and the **Pauravas/Bhāratas/Kurus** (→ Pāṇḍavas).

### 2a. Genealogical spine (top of the tree)
**Atri → Candra (Soma) → Budha (m. Ilā) → Purūravas → Āyu → Nahuṣa → Yayāti → {five sons}.** (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.6; *Bhāgavata* IX.14; *Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva)

Yayāti's five sons split the dynasty:
- **Yadu** → **Yādava** branch → (Vṛṣṇi, Andhaka) → **Kṛṣṇa & Balarāma**.
- **Turvasu** → minor southern lines (Yāvana-linked in some lists).
- **Druhyu** → Bhoja / northwestern lines.
- **Anu** → Ānava lines (Aṅga, etc.; Karṇa's adoptive Aṅga).
- **Puru** → **Paurava** branch → Bharata → Kuru → Śāntanu → Pāṇḍavas & Kauravas.

### 2b. Key events / stories (Lunar — root section)
- **Birth of Budha & the Tārā scandal.** Candra abducts **Tārā**, wife of **Bṛhaspati** (guru of the devas); a war (Tārakāmaya) erupts; Tārā bears **Budha** (Mercury), of disputed paternity. The Moon's "waning" linked to a curse from this. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.6; *Bhāgavata* IX.14)
- **Purūravas & Urvaśī.** First mortal king of the line; loves the apsarā **Urvaśī** under conditions; loses her when conditions break; eventually reunited / she becomes a gandharva-boon. Oldest layer in *Ṛgveda* X.95 (dialogue hymn); dramatized in Kālidāsa's *Vikramorvaśīya*. (*Ṛgveda* X.95; *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* XI; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*)
- **Nahuṣa's fall.** Becomes interim **Indra**, grows arrogant, demands the saptarṣis carry his palanquin, kicks **Agastya**, is cursed to fall as a python; later liberated by **Yudhiṣṭhira**. (*Mahābhārata* Vana/Udyoga Parva; *Bhāgavata* VI/IX)
- **Yayāti's exchange of old age.** Cursed with premature old age by **Śukra** for wronging **Devayānī** (and favoring the maid **Śarmiṣṭhā**); borrows youth from his son **Puru**; after 1,000 years renounces lust, returns Puru's youth and crowns him over the elder Yadu → reason the Paurava (not Yādava) gets Hastināpura. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.10; *Bhāgavata* IX.18–19)
  - *Variant:* Yadu is said to be **cursed by Yayāti** for refusing to lend his youth — Purāṇas use this to explain why the senior Yādavas were barred from imperial kingship, foreshadowing Kṛṣṇa never taking a throne.

### 2c. YĀDAVA branch (→ Kṛṣṇa)
**Yadu → … → Vṛṣṇi & Andhaka → … → Śūra → Vasudeva → Kṛṣṇa.** Seat: Mathurā, later **Dvārakā**.
- **Kārtavīrya Arjuna (Sahasrabāhu).** A Haihaya (Yādava-Haihaya line); thousand-armed emperor; steals Jamadagni's wish-cow → slain by **Paraśurāma**, triggering the Kṣatriya-extermination cycle. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.11; *Mahābhārata*)
- **Kaṃsa's tyranny & Kṛṣṇa's birth.** Prophecy that Devakī's 8th son kills Kaṃsa; Kṛṣṇa born in prison, raised in Gokula/Vṛndāvana. (*Bhāgavata* X; *Harivaṃśa*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* V)
- **Kṛṣṇa, 8th avatāra of Viṣṇu.** Slays Kaṃsa, founds Dvārakā, central to the Mahābhārata as Pāṇḍava ally and *Bhagavad Gītā* teacher. Dvāpara Yuga; his departure marks the start of **Kali Yuga (3102 BCE)**. (*Bhāgavata*; *Harivaṃśa*; *Mahābhārata*) See [../01-manvantaras/.../mahayuga-28-current/dvapara/](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md).
- **The Yādava self-destruction (Mausala Parva).** Cursed by sages, the Vṛṣṇis kill each other with iron maces at Prabhāsa; Balarāma and Kṛṣṇa depart; Dvārakā sinks into the sea. End of the Yādava line. (*Mahābhārata* Mausala Parva; *Bhāgavata* XI)

### 2d. PAURAVA / BHĀRATA / KURU branch (→ Pāṇḍavas & Kauravas)
**Puru → … → Duṣyanta → Bharata → … → Hastin (founds Hastināpura) → Kuru → … → Śāntanu → {Bhīṣma; Vicitravīrya} → {Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Pāṇḍu, Vidura} → Kauravas & Pāṇḍavas → Parīkṣit → Janamejaya.** (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.19–20; *Bhāgavata* IX.20–22)
- **Duṣyanta & Śakuntalā.** King meets Śakuntalā (daughter of Viśvāmitra & Menakā) in Kaṇva's hermitage; gandharva marriage; ring-curse forgetting (Kālidāsa's *Abhijñānaśākuntala*); their son is **Bharata**. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva; *Abhijñānaśākuntala*)
- **Bharata, the eponymous emperor.** Cakravartin after whom **Bhārata-varṣa (India)** is named; the Bhārata clan descends from him. (*Mahābhārata*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant:* Purāṇas also name a **Bharata** in the Solar line (Rāma's brother) and a Jaḍa-Bharata (Bhāgavata) — three distinct Bharatas; the India-naming Bharata is the Lunar/Pauravā one in most readings (some attribute the name to Ṛṣabha's son Bharata in *Bhāgavata* V).
- **Bhīṣma's vow.** Śāntanu's son Devavrata vows lifelong celibacy and renunciation of the throne so his father can marry **Satyavatī** → becomes **Bhīṣma**; boon of death-at-will. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva)
- **Niyoga of Vyāsa.** Vicitravīrya dies heirless; sage **Vyāsa** (Satyavatī's premarital son) fathers **Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Pāṇḍu, Vidura** by niyoga — the dynasty's continuation through a Saptarṣi-adjacent sage. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva)
- **Pāṇḍavas' divine paternity.** Pāṇḍu cursed to die if he embraces a wife; Kuntī's mantra (boon of Durvāsā) summons gods: **Yudhiṣṭhira (Dharma), Bhīma (Vāyu), Arjuna (Indra)**; Mādrī bears **Nakula & Sahadeva (Aśvins)**. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva)
- **Kurukṣetra War & aftermath.** 18-day war; near-total annihilation of the Kuru host; **Parīkṣit** (Arjuna's grandson) revived in the womb by Kṛṣṇa, becomes the post-war king — his reign opens the historical Kali Yuga. (*Mahābhārata*; *Bhāgavata* I)
- **Janamejaya's snake-sacrifice.** Parīkṣit (cursed, killed by Takṣaka) is avenged by son Janamejaya's sarpasatra — the frame-narrative occasion at which the *Mahābhārata* is first recited (by Vaiśampāyana). (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva)

## 3. SAPTARṢI LINEAGES — The Sage-Genealogies Threading Both Houses

The **Saptarṣi** (Seven Sages) are reconstituted each Manvantara. The kingly dynasties are spiritually steered by these sages and their gotra-descendants (purohitas, curse-givers, fathers-by-niyoga).

### 3a. Saptarṣi of the current (Vaivasvata) Manvantara
**Kaśyapa, Atri, Vasiṣṭha, Viśvāmitra, Gautama, Jamadagni, Bharadvāja.** (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* III.1; *Mahābhārata*; *Bṛhadāraṇyaka*-era gotra tradition)
- *Variant (Bṛhadāraṇyaka / Śatapatha lists):* some texts give **Atri, Bharadvāja, Gautama, Jamadagni, Kaśyapa, Vasiṣṭha, Viśvāmitra**; others substitute **Aṅgiras / Pulastya / Pulaha / Kratu / Marīci** (the original *mānasaputra* mind-born ṛṣis of Svāyambhuva Manvantara). Different Manvantaras have entirely different Saptarṣi rosters (each Purāṇa lists all 14 sets differently).

### 3b. How the sages bind to the dynasties
- **Vasiṣṭha** — hereditary purohita of the **Sūryavaṃśa** (guru of Rāma); rival of Viśvāmitra; cursed-and-reborn with Nimi (Videha origin). (*Rāmāyaṇa*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*)
- **Viśvāmitra** — born a Kṣatriya king (**Kauśika**, of the Lunar-adjacent Kuśika line), earns brahmarṣi status; tests **Hariścandra**; trains Rāma & Lakṣmaṇa; father of **Śakuntalā** (→ Bharata). Bridges Kṣatriya and Brāhmaṇa, Solar and Lunar. (*Rāmāyaṇa*; *Mahābhārata*)
- **Atri** — progenitor-node of the **Candravaṃśa** (Candra born of Atri); with wife **Anasūyā**, parents of **Dattātreya, Durvāsā, Soma** in one tradition. (*Bhāgavata* IV; *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa*)
- **Jamadagni** — father of **Paraśurāma** (6th avatāra), whose annihilation of the Kṣatriyas resets both royal houses; Jamadagni of the **Bhārgava** line. (*Mahābhārata*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*)
- **Gautama** — husband of **Ahalyā** (cursed to stone, redeemed by Rāma's touch); links to the Solar epic. (*Rāmāyaṇa* Bāla Kāṇḍa)
- **Bharadvāja** — hosts Rāma in exile (Citrakūṭa region); in genealogy linked to **Droṇa** (Mahābhārata) via the Bhāradvāja gotra. (*Rāmāyaṇa*; *Mahābhārata*)
- **Kaśyapa** — primordial progenitor (father of devas, asuras, nāgas, all beings via Dakṣa's daughters); the genealogical "above-node" from which Vivasvān → Manu → both dynasties descend. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* I; *Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva)

### 3c. Gotra system
- Brāhmaṇa lineages today still trace to these sages as **gotras** (paternal clan-lines): e.g. Kāśyapa, Vāsiṣṭha, Viśvāmitra/Kauśika, Bhāradvāja, Gautama, Jāmadagnya, Ātreya — the living social residue of the Saptarṣi genealogy. (gotra tradition; *Baudhāyana Śrautasūtra* pravara lists)

## 4. Cross-Dynasty Synchronisms (how the two lines meet)
- **Marriage:** Rāma (Solar) ⟷ Sītā (Solar-Videha cadet, but the Mithilā↔Ayodhyā union joins the two great Ikṣvāku branches). The Solar–Lunar *inter*marriage is rarer; the houses run in parallel.
- **War:** **Bṛhadbala** (last epic Sūryavaṃśī) fights and dies in the **Kurukṣetra** war (Lunar conflict) — the clearest timeline-pin joining Solar and Lunar histories in Dvāpara. (*Mahābhārata* Droṇa Parva)
- **Avatāra anchoring:** **Rāma** (Solar, Tretā) and **Kṛṣṇa** (Lunar/Yādava, Dvāpara) — the 7th and 8th Viṣṇu avatāras incarnate in the two houses respectively, structurally marking the two epics to two yugas.
- **Paraśurāma** (6th avatāra) acts on **both** houses by exterminating Kṣatriyas 21 times — a sage-line (Bhārgava) reset of the royal genealogies between Tretā events.
