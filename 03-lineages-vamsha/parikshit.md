# Parīkṣit — First King of Kali and the Śṛṅgī Curse

> Hierarchy: `03-lineages-vamsha/parikshit.md` — a Pauravā/Kuru-branch life of the [Candravaṃśa / Lunar dynasty](suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md). Up one level: [Solar & Lunar Dynasties (Vaṃśa)](README.md). Parīkṣit is the bridge-king between the two great frame-narratives: his death triggers his son's [Sarpa-satra](janamejaya-sarpa-satra.md) (the Mahābhārata's outer frame) and his final week is the dramatic occasion of the entire *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*.

**Parīkṣit** (Parikṣit, "the tested one") is the **first reigning emperor of the Kali Yuga** — the posthumous son of [Abhimanyu](../04-deep-dives/characters/abhimanyu.md) and Uttarā, grandson of Arjuna, sole survivor of the shattered Kuru line, revived in the womb by Kṛṣṇa, and father of [Janamejaya](janamejaya-sarpa-satra.md). On the **two-clock** distinction: in **scriptural time** his reign opens the Kali Yuga of the present 28th Mahāyuga (Vaivasvata Manvantara), conventionally dated from the war's close at 3102 BCE; in **historical/text-critical time** the *Mahābhārata* uses his death as the literary hinge that motivates the snake-sacrifice at which the epic is recited, and the *Bhāgavata* uses his seven-day vigil as the recitation-frame of its own twelve skandhas. [scholarly]

## Birth and revival in the womb

- **The Kuru line all but extinguished** — after Kurukṣetra, the only seed of the dynasty is the child in Uttarā's womb; on his slaying of the Pāṇḍava sons, **Aśvatthāman** hurls the **Brahmaśiras / Aiṣīka** weapon to abort even the unborn heir, intent on ending the lineage utterly (*Mahābhārata, Sauptika Parva*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.7–8).
- **Revived by Kṛṣṇa** — the child is born stillborn, scorched by the weapon's fire; **Kṛṣṇa** enters the womb / revives the dead infant by the power of his truth and dharma, restoring its life — the *Bhāgavata*'s great proof-text of Kṛṣṇa as protector of the embryo (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.8.13–33; *Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva*).
- **The name "Parīkṣit"** — glossed two ways: *(a)* "he who searches / examines," because as a child he went about **looking for** (parīkṣamāṇa) the luminous being he had seen rescuing him in the womb; *(b)* born when the race was "tried/tested" (parikṣīṇa, "wasted away") to a single survivor (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.12; *Mahābhārata*).
  - *Variant (Viṣṇurāta):* the *Bhāgavata* also calls him **Viṣṇurāta**, "given by Viṣṇu," for having been granted life by the Lord himself (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.12.17).
- **Birth-omens of a great king** — astrologers and the assembled sages read his nativity: he will be a second **Ikṣvāku** in piety, an **Aśvamedha**-performer, a refuge of brāhmaṇas, and devoted to Kṛṣṇa (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.12.17–30).
- **Raised by the Pāṇḍavas** — reared under Yudhiṣṭhira and his great-uncles; upon the Pāṇḍavas' **mahāprasthāna** (great departure) Yudhiṣṭhira installs the boy Parīkṣit on the Hāstinapura throne and Vajra (Kṛṣṇa's great-grandson) at Mathurā (*Mahābhārata, Mahāprasthānika Parva*; *Bhāgavata* I.15).

## The righteous reign

- **Emperor at the Dvāpara→Kali cusp** — Parīkṣit rules from **Hāstinapura** as a *cakravartin* in the mold of his ancestors, married to **Irāvatī** (daughter of Uttara, the Matsya prince), fathering four sons of whom **Janamejaya** is eldest (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.16; *Mahābhārata*).
- **A reign of restored dharma** — under his protection the earth yields, the social order holds, and the king himself performs three **Aśvamedha** sacrifices on the Gaṅgā, brāhmaṇas honoured throughout (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.16).

## The taming of personified Kali

- **Kali enters the world** — in the *Bhāgavata*'s signature episode, the age of **Kali** has begun, and the spirit of the age, embodied as a low-born śūdra-king, is found beating a **bull** (Dharma, standing on one leg) and a **cow** (the earth, Bhūmi) with a club (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.16–17).
- **The bull and cow as Dharma and Earth** — Parīkṣit, touring his realm, comes upon the scene; the four-legged bull (Dharma reduced to one leg in Kali) and the weeping cow (the despoiled Earth) explain their misery, and the king draws his sword to slay the assailant (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.17).
- **Kali spared, then regulated** — the personified **Kali** surrenders and begs for life; as a kṣatriya Parīkṣit will not kill a suppliant, but **banishes** him and then, on his plea for a dwelling, **assigns him fixed residences** so that adharma is confined rather than loosed at large (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.17).
- **The five (or four) abodes of Kali** — the king grants Kali residence wherever **(1) gambling (dyūta), (2) drinking (pāna), (3) illicit sex (strī), (4) slaughter (sūnā / hiṃsā)** prevail; on further entreaty he adds a fifth — **(5) gold (jāta-rūpa / kāñcana)** — whence the deceit, intoxication, lust, enmity and greed that follow wealth (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.17.38–39).
  - *Variant (four vs five):* the core list is the four sin-grounds; the **gold** as the fifth abode is the celebrated addition that seeds the king's own downfall (Kali later acts through a gold crown). [scholarly]
- **Why this matters** — this episode makes Parīkṣit the archetypal **dharma-king who governs evil rather than abolishing it**, and ironically supplies the device of his own ruin: it is from his **golden crown** — Kali's lawful seat — that the fatal lapse of judgement proceeds (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.18).

## The hunt, thirst, and the dead-snake insult

- **The thirsty hunt** — pursuing game, Parīkṣit grows exhausted and parched and enters the hermitage of the sage **Śamīka** seeking water (*Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.18).
- **The silent sage** — Śamīka sits absorbed in deep meditation, vow-bound to silence, and neither sees nor answers the king; Parīkṣit, slighted in his thirst and (in the *Bhāgavata*) with Kali having entered him through the gold of his crown, takes the silence as contempt (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.18.24–30).
- **The garland of a dead snake** — in anger the king lifts a **dead serpent** from the ground with the tip of his bow and **drapes it over the meditating sage's shoulder**, then departs — an act at once unkingly and out of his true character (*Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.18.30–32).
- **Immediate remorse (Bhāgavata)** — the *Bhāgavata* stresses that Parīkṣit repents the deed at once and grieves the affront to a brāhmaṇa, anticipating retribution; the lapse is framed as Kali's doing, not the king's nature (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.18.34–37).

## Śṛṅgī's seven-day curse

- **The boy-sage Śṛṅgī** — Śamīka's young, hot-tempered son **Śṛṅgī** (Śṛṅgin), at play with companions, is told of the insult to his father and blazes with fury (*Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.18.24–48).
- **The death-curse** — touching the water of the Kauśikī (in the epic), Śṛṅgī **curses** the king: within **seven days** the serpent **Takṣaka** shall bite Parīkṣit and burn him to ashes with his venom-fire (*Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.18.37).
- **Śamīka's grief and warning** — emerging from meditation, the calm father **rebukes his son** for cursing a righteous protector-king over so small a fault, and out of compassion sends a disciple (**Gauramukha**) to warn Parīkṣit to guard himself (*Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.18.48–50).
  - *Variant (epic vs Purāṇa staging):* the *Mahābhārata* foregrounds the boy's rashness and the father's diplomatic warning; the *Bhāgavata* foregrounds the king's joyful acceptance of the curse as a chance for liberation. Both name **Takṣaka** and the **seven-day** term. [scholarly]

## The fearless final week — hearing the Bhāgavata

- **Renunciation on the Gaṅgā** — receiving the curse as a blessing, Parīkṣit **abdicates** (or entrusts the realm), seats himself on the bank of the **Gaṅgā** in fast-unto-death (*prāyopaveśa*), and there a great assembly of sages gathers (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.19).
- **The one question** — the king asks: **what should a man about to die do?** — the question that opens the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* proper (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.19.37–38).
- **Śuka's arrival and recitation** — the sixteen-year-old liberated sage **Śukadeva** (Śuka), son of Vyāsa, arrives unbidden; over the **seven days** he recites to Parīkṣit the whole **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** — creation, the *avatāra*s, and the glories of Kṛṣṇa — culminating in liberation-knowledge (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.19 – XII.5).
- **The fortified-tower variant (Mahābhārata)** — by contrast the epic has Parīkṣit **try to cheat the curse**: he raises a single tall pillar / guarded tower, surrounds himself with physicians, mantras and antidotes, and admits no one — a king resisting fate rather than renouncing (*Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva*).
  - *Variant (resist vs renounce):* this is the sharpest divergence between the two primaries — the **Mahābhārata's Parīkṣit fortifies and resists**; the **Bhāgavata's Parīkṣit fasts and listens**. The repo keeps both, unmerged. [scholarly]

## Death by Takṣaka

- **Takṣaka's stratagem** — on the seventh day the nāga **Takṣaka** sets out; he **bribes/turns back the brāhmaṇa-physician Kaśyapa** (who could have neutralised any bite) and, disguising himself, conceals himself as a tiny worm inside a **fruit** carried in by ascetics (*Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva*; *Bhāgavata* I.18–19).
- **The fatal bite** — emerging from the fruit at the appointed hour, Takṣaka **bites the king**; Parīkṣit's body is consumed by serpent-venom-fire — in the *Bhāgavata* his consciousness, fixed on Kṛṣṇa through Śuka's teaching, attains liberation at the instant of death (*Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* XII.6).
- **The trigger of the snake-sacrifice** — Parīkṣit's death is the **cause of the entire [Sarpa-satra](janamejaya-sarpa-satra.md)**: his son **Janamejaya**, on learning how his father died, vows to exterminate the serpent race in a fire-sacrifice — the rite at which **Vaiśampāyana** recites the *Mahābhārata* (*Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva*). See the [Ādi Parva deep-dive](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md).

## Place in the line and the two frames

- **The dynastic spine** — …Pāṇḍu → Arjuna → [Abhimanyu](../04-deep-dives/characters/abhimanyu.md) → **Parīkṣit** → Janamejaya — the genealogical tail of the [lunar line](suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) as it crosses into Kali (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.16, IX.22).
- **Two recitation-frames, one king** — uniquely, Parīkṣit anchors **both** great recitations: his **death** occasions the *Mahābhārata*'s telling (at Janamejaya's sacrifice) and his **dying week** occasions the *Bhāgavata*'s telling (Śuka to Parīkṣit, re-told by Sūta at Naimiṣa) — a deliberate Purāṇic mirroring of the epic's frame (*Mahābhārata, Ādi 1*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.1–4). [scholarly]
