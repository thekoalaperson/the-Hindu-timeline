# The Kāśī Line — Divodāsa, Pratardana, Alarka & Śiva's Exile from Kāśī

> *Hierarchy: [`03-lineages-vamsha/`](README.md) — the **Kāśī (Kāśya) branch** of the **Candravaṃśa** (Lunar dynasty, Kṣatravṛddha sub-line; see the [Sūryavaṃśa–Candravaṃśa overview](suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md)). The dynasty of [Vārāṇasī](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md) whose kings carry three of tradition's strangest honors: a physician-god born into the family ([Dhanvantari](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dhanvantari.md)), a flawless king who kept **Śiva himself locked out of his own city**, and a boy-sage crowned by a lullaby. Scriptural clock: mythic antiquity of the current Mahāyuga; the Kāśī-khaṇḍa telling floats outside yuga-count. Academic clock: the framing texts are late — see §5. [scholarly]*

---

## 0. The genealogical spine — and three Divodāsas to keep apart

- **The Purāṇic spine (Bhāgavata IX.17).** From the Lunar trunk: **Purūravas → Āyu → Kṣatravṛddha → Suhotra → Kāśya → Kāśi → Rāṣṭra → Dīrghatama(s) → Dhanvantari → Ketumān → Bhīmaratha → Divodāsa → Dyumān (= Pratardana) → Alarka → …** — the eponym **Kāśya/Kāśi** gives the city-kingdom its royal house. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.17.1–8)
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa IV.8):* essentially the same chain with spelling wobble (**Kāśa/Kāśirāja**, **Dīrghatapas**); intervening names differ across *Vāyu*, *Brahmāṇḍa* and *Harivaṃśa* recensions — treat the king-list between Kāśya and Divodāsa as an **open/variant roster**. [disputed]
- **Dhanvantari in the line.** The ocean-born physician-god is **reborn as a king of this very dynasty** (son of Dīrghatapas), free of bodily infirmity, entitled to a sacrificial share, and promulgates **Āyurveda in eight divisions**. Canonical home: [Dhanvantari](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dhanvantari.md); the medical school it charters is at [Āyurveda](../05-concepts-and-dharma/ayurveda-and-the-science-of-life.md). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.17.4–5; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.8)
- **Disambiguation — the three Divodāsas.** The name straddles three figures the corpus keeps distinct:
  1. **Ṛgvedic Divodāsa Atithigva** — patron-king of the Bharadvāja hymn-family, for whom **Indra shatters Śambara's ninety-nine forts**; ancestor (or kinsman) of Sudās of the *daśarājña*. A Vedic-period chieftain of the [Sapta-Sindhu world](../06-sacred-geography/sapta-sindhu-and-the-indus-rivers.md), academically unrelated to Vārāṇasī. (*Ṛgveda* Maṇḍala 6 passim; 2.19.6) [scholarly] — see [Indra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/indra.md).
  2. **Divodāsa Dhanvantari, king of Kāśī** — the epic-Purāṇic dynast of this file, whom the *Suśruta Saṃhitā* frame-story makes the surgical teacher of **Suśruta**; texts disagree whether he **is** the reborn Dhanvantari or his **great-grandson who inherits the title/office**. (*Suśruta Saṃhitā* Sūtrasthāna 1 vs *Bhāgavata* IX.17) [disputed]
  3. **Divodāsa (Ripuñjaya) of the Kāśī-khaṇḍa** — the flawless king of the Śiva-exile cycle (§2), whom the *Skanda Purāṇa* treats as a figure of deep mythic antiquity, only loosely stitched to the genealogy above. [late text]
  - *Scholarly note:* the Mahābhārata makes **Bharadvāja** the Kāśī Divodāsa's family priest — very likely an epic reuse of the old Ṛgvedic Divodāsa–Bharadvāja pairing transferred to Vārāṇasī. (*Mahābhārata* Anuśāsana Parva 30) [scholarly]

## 1. The Haihaya–Kāśī wars — a dynasty repeatedly unseated

- **The Vītahavya onslaught (Mahābhārata version).** The **Vītahavyas/Haihayas** — the war-clan of the [Kārtavīrya cycle](../04-deep-dives/jamadagni-parashurama.md) — grind down three generations of Kāśī kings: **Haryaśva** is slain in battle by the sons of Vītahavya; his son **Sudeva** is defeated; Sudeva's son **Divodāsa**, at **Indra's command, builds and fortifies Vārāṇasī** as his stronghold — and is *still* defeated, his army annihilated, the king fleeing alone. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva 30)
- **Bharadvāja's rite and the born-avenger.** The refugee Divodāsa reaches the hermitage of his purohita **Bharadvāja**, who performs a **putrakāmeṣṭi** (son-desiring sacrifice); the son born of it is **Pratardana**, who matures preternaturally fast, leads Kāśī's army back, and **exterminates the sons of Vītahavya to the last man**. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva 30)
  - **Vītahavya becomes a brāhmaṇa.** The defeated Vītahavya flees to **Bhṛgu**, who shelters him and — by declaring "there are only brāhmaṇas here" — *makes the declaration true*: the kṣatriya becomes a brāhmaṇa on the spot, seeding a Bhārgava gotra. A varṇa-crossing mirror of the [caru-swap](gadhi-and-the-caru-swap.md) in the neighboring Kānyakubja line. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva 30)
- **The Bhadraśreṇya war (Purāṇic version).** In the *Harivaṃśa*/*Vāyu* telling the aggressor is the Haihaya king **Bhadraśreṇya**: he seizes Kāśī; Divodāsa strikes back and slays his hundred sons, sparing only the child **Durdama**, who grows up to retake the kingdom "as a debt of hostility"; the see-saw is ended a generation later by Pratardana. The *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* etymologizes the heir's very name from this: **Pratardana**, "the destroyer" (of Bhadraśreṇya's race). (*Harivaṃśa*, Harivaṃśa Parva ~29–32; *Vāyu Purāṇa*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.8) [disputed: which enemy-list is older]
- **Nikumbha's curse — the first emptying of Kāśī.** Interlocked with the wars is an earlier exile-myth: the gaṇa **Nikumbha**, installed at Vārāṇasī and denied/withholding a boon of a son for Divodāsa's queen **Suyaśā**, is dishonored by the furious king, and **curses Vārāṇasī to stand empty for a thousand years**. Divodāsa removes his capital to a new city on the **Gomatī**, while **Śiva and Pārvatī dwell in the emptied Kāśī** — the vacancy engineered, some tellings say, precisely *so that* Śiva could have the city. (*Harivaṃśa*, Harivaṃśa Parva ~29; *Vāyu Purāṇa*) [disputed: whether the curse or the Haihayas empty the city]
  - *Scholarly note:* the Nikumbha-curse story is widely read as the **older seed** of the grand Kāśī-khaṇḍa exile cycle in §2 — the same theme (king vs Śiva's right to the city) inverted and vastly elaborated. [scholarly]

## 2. The Kāśī-khaṇḍa exile cycle — the flawless king who locked Śiva out

The central connected narrative of the *Skanda Purāṇa*'s Kāśī-khaṇḍa (see the [khaṇḍa map](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/skanda-purana-khanda-map.md) and the [Skanda Purāṇa profile](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/skanda-purana.md)); the city-side theology it undergirds is at [Kāśī/Vārāṇasī](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md). [late text]

- **Brahmā installs Ripuñjaya as "Divodāsa."** In an age of anarchy and drought, with no rightful king on earth, **Brahmā** persuades the ascetic prince **Ripuñjaya**, practicing tapas at Kāśī, to accept universal kingship; he takes the throne-name **Divodāsa** — on one iron condition: **all gods must withdraw from the earth to heaven** and leave governance to him alone. Brahmā agrees. (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Kāśī-khaṇḍa, Pūrvārdha ch. 43)
- **Śiva exiled to Mandara.** The condition binds even Śiva: the Lord who calls Kāśī **Avimukta** ("never-forsaken") must forsake it, withdrawing with the newly-wed **Pārvatī** to **Mount Mandara**. Pārvatī is content; Śiva is homesick — the paradox the khaṇḍa milks for chapters: the god of the never-forsaken city, locked out of it by dharma itself. (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Kāśī-khaṇḍa, Pūrvārdha chs. 43, 45)
- **The perfect reign.** Under Divodāsa Kāśī knows no drought, disease, or unrighteousness; he rules the gods' functions himself (rain, prosperity) so well that **no god can find a pretext to return**. Śiva can re-enter only if a **flaw (chidra/doṣa)** is found in the king. (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Kāśī-khaṇḍa, Pūrvārdha ch. 45; Uttarārdha chs. 53 ff.)
- **Emissary I — the 64 Yoginīs.** Śiva dispatches the **[64 Yoginīs](../08-beings-and-bestiary/matrikas-and-64-yoginis.md)** to ferret out a fault. They infiltrate the city in disguise, find nothing — and are so **captivated by Kāśī that they never report back**, settling permanently as the city's resident Yoginīs (their shrine endures at Chausaṭṭhī Ghāṭ). (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Kāśī-khaṇḍa, Uttarārdha ~ch. 53)
- **Emissary II — Sūrya.** The Sun-god goes next, searches in many disguises, finds the king faultless, and — dreading to return with failure — **stays**, splitting into the twelve **Ādityas of Kāśī** (Lolārka and the other sun-shrines of the city). (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Kāśī-khaṇḍa, Uttarārdha ~ch. 54)
- **Emissary III — Brahmā and the ten aśvamedhas.** Brahmā comes as an old brāhmaṇa and tests the king with an impossible demand: patronage for **ten simultaneous horse-sacrifices**. Divodāsa executes all ten flawlessly; Brahmā too remains — and the site is the **Daśāśvamedha Ghāṭ** ("ten-horse-sacrifice" ghat) etiology. (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Kāśī-khaṇḍa, Uttarārdha ~ch. 55)
- **Emissary IV — Gaṇeśa as Ḍhuṇḍhirāja.** [Gaṇeśa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md) enters as an aged brāhmaṇa **astrologer**, charms the households and the queen with dream-readings and prophecy, and plants the hook: **on the eighteenth day a brāhmaṇa will arrive whose counsel the king must follow**. He too stays, becoming **Ḍhuṇḍhirāja/Ḍhuṇḍhi-Vināyaka** — "the sought-out one," chief of Kāśī's Vināyakas, saluted first by every pilgrim to this day. (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Kāśī-khaṇḍa, Uttarārdha ~chs. 56–57)
- **Emissary V — Viṣṇu's deception.** Viṣṇu arrives with **Lakṣmī and Garuḍa**; in the khaṇḍa's telling he takes the guise of a **heterodox (Buddhist) teacher** — Lakṣmī a nun, Garuḍa a disciple — preaching against Vedic rites to nick the city's perfect dharma and manufacture the missing flaw. (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Kāśī-khaṇḍa, Uttarārdha ~ch. 58) [late text]
  - *Scholarly note:* Diana Eck and others read this episode as **mythic memory of Banaras's real religious history** — the Buddhist centuries and their Śaiva supersession compressed into narrative. [scholarly]
- **The one flaw and the counsel.** As foretold, Viṣṇu-as-brāhmaṇa gains audience on the eighteenth day. The world-weary Divodāsa asks what fault he bears; the answer: **only one — he has kept Śiva out of Śiva's own city**. The remedy: install a **Śiva-liṅga** and worship it. (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Kāśī-khaṇḍa, Uttarārdha ~ch. 58)
- **Divodāseśvara and mokṣa.** Divodāsa installs and worships the liṅga thereafter called **Divodāseśvara**, crowns his son, and — his kingship perfectly discharged and perfectly renounced — **attains liberation**, borne by celestial chariot to Śiva's realm (mukti, not defeat: the khaṇḍa is emphatic that the "flaw" hunt failed and grace succeeded). (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Kāśī-khaṇḍa, Uttarārdha ~ch. 58)
- **Śiva's return.** Śiva re-enters Kāśī in triumphal procession with Pārvatī and the whole gaṇa-host, never to leave again — the charter-myth of **Avimukta** and of the [Viśveśvara jyotirliṅga's](../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md) eternal residence. Every emissary who "failed" remains as a permanent stratum of the city's sacred population — Yoginīs, Ādityas, Brahmā's ghat, Ḍhuṇḍhirāja, Bindu-Mādhava. (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Kāśī-khaṇḍa, Uttarārdha ch. 58 and closing māhātmya chapters)

## 3. Pratardana — warrior-king of the Upaniṣads

- **Storming heaven.** **Pratardana Daivodāsi** ("son of Divodāsa") reaches **Indra's beloved abode by fighting and virility** (*yuddhena pauruṣeṇa*) — one of the very few mortals the Upaniṣads admit to heaven by valor rather than rite. (*Kauṣītaki Upaniṣad* 3.1) — full treatment: [Kauṣītaki Upaniṣad](../90-literature-corpus/upanishads/kaushitaki-upanishad.md).
- **The boon turned back on the giver.** Offered a boon, Pratardana asks Indra to **choose on his behalf** whatever is most beneficial to man; Indra answers "**Know me alone**," reveals himself as **prāṇa, the prajñātman** (life-breath as the conscious self), and teaches that for the knower of this no deed — "not even the slaying of mother or father" — clings; Indra cites his own unscathed transgressions (slaying Tvaṣṭṛ's three-headed son, casting ascetics to the wolves) as proof. (*Kauṣītaki Upaniṣad* 3.1–8)
- **Son of Mādhavī (epic variant).** In the Gālava cycle, Divodāsa of Kāśī begets Pratardana on **Mādhavī**, Yayāti's daughter, for the price of 200 of the black-eared white horses — making Pratardana quarter-brother to Vasumanas, Śibi and Aṣṭaka. (*Mahābhārata*, Udyoga Parva ~117) — see [Gālava & Mādhavī](../04-deep-dives/galava-madhavi.md). [disputed: irreconcilable with the Bharadvāja-iṣṭi birth of §1 — the epic itself carries both]
- **The many-named heir.** The *Bhāgavata* piles the aliases on one prince: Divodāsa's son **Dyumān**, "also called **Pratardana, Śatrujit, Vatsa, Ṛtadhvaja and Kuvalayāśva**" — a telescoping that becomes load-bearing for Alarka's story below. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.17.6)

## 4. Alarka — the boy-king raised on a Vedānta lullaby

- **The 66,000-year youth.** **Alarka**, son of Pratardana, holds a unique Purāṇic record, fixed in a memorial verse: "**for sixty-six thousand years no one save Alarka ruled the earth while remaining young**." (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.17.7–8; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.8)
- **Son of Madālasā (Mārkaṇḍeya cycle).** The *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* makes Alarka the fourth son of **Ṛtadhvaja (Kuvalayāśva)** and the jīvanmuktā queen **Madālasā**, who sings her infants the "śuddho'si buddho'si" Ātma-jñāna lullaby; the first three sons renounce in childhood, so Alarka alone is schooled in **rāja-dharma**, with a **ring of secret counsel** for the day worldly life fails. When his brother Subāhu engineers a siege of his kingdom (allied, pointedly, with the **king of Kāśī**), the ring sends Alarka to **[Dattātreya](../04-deep-dives/dattatreya.md)**, who grants him Self-knowledge; Alarka renounces and is liberated. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* ~chs. 16–44) — canonical home: [Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/markandeya-purana.md).
  - *Variant (which Alarka?):* the Mārkaṇḍeya never plants its Alarka in the Kāśī genealogy, but the *Bhāgavata*'s equation **Pratardana = Ṛtadhvaja = Kuvalayāśva** (IX.17.6) is precisely the splice by which tradition makes Madālasā's son and the Kāśī heir one person; some scholars treat them as originally **independent Alarkas** secondarily merged. [disputed] [scholarly]
- **After Alarka — the line runs out.** The spine continues **Santati → Sunītha → … → Satyaketu → Dhṛṣṭaketu → Sukumāra → Vītihotra → Bharga → Bhārgabhūmi**, after which the Kāśī house fades from the Purāṇic vaṃśa-lists as an independent power (historically, Kāśī the *mahājanapada* is absorbed by Kosala and then Magadha). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.17.9–10) [scholarly: the absorption]

## 5. Two-clocks note

- **Scriptural clock.** The vaṃśa-lists set the line in the deep past of the current Mahāyuga (Pratardana's Mādhavī episode ties it to the Yayāti generation-frame); the Kāśī-khaṇḍa exile cycle presents itself as **primordial city-time** — before/outside the yuga sequence, in keeping with Kāśī's claim to stand outside pralaya. The corpus files it as a **cross-yuga charter myth**.
- **Academic clock.** Kāśī as a historical kingdom is a **mahājanapada of c. 8th–6th century BCE**; the *Kauṣītaki Upaniṣad* is dated broadly to the later Vedic period (pre-Buddhist, c. 800–500 BCE); the *Suśruta Saṃhitā* is a layered text of c. mid-1st millennium BCE – early centuries CE; and the **Kāśī-khaṇḍa is a late compilation, c. 13th–14th century CE**, codifying older lore after the early Sultanate destructions of Banaras. The Ṛgvedic Divodāsa belongs to c. 2nd-millennium BCE hymn-history and is not the Kāśī king. [scholarly]

## Sources

- *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*, **Skandha IX.17** — the Kāśya/Kāśī genealogy: Kṣatravṛddha to Dhanvantari, Divodāsa, Dyumān-Pratardana (with aliases), Alarka's 66,000-year reign, and the line to Bhārgabhūmi.
- *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*, **Book IV.8** — parallel genealogy; Dhanvantari's boon-birth and the eightfold Āyurveda; Pratardana named "destroyer of Bhadraśreṇya's race"; the Alarka verse.
- *Mahābhārata*, **Anuśāsana Parva 30** — the Vītahavya/Haihaya wars: Haryaśva, Sudeva, Divodāsa's fortification of Vārāṇasī, Bharadvāja's putrakāmeṣṭi, Pratardana's vengeance, Vītahavya's brāhmaṇization by Bhṛgu; **Udyoga Parva ~117** — Divodāsa, Mādhavī and the birth of Pratardana (Gālava cycle).
- *Harivaṃśa* (Harivaṃśa Parva ~29–32) and *Vāyu Purāṇa* — the Bhadraśreṇya–Durdama war and **Nikumbha's curse** emptying Vārāṇasī; Divodāsa's new capital on the Gomatī.
- *Skanda Purāṇa*, **Kāśī-khaṇḍa, Pūrvārdha chs. 43, 45; Uttarārdha chs. 53–58** — the Divodāsa–Śiva exile cycle: Ripuñjaya's enthronement, the gods' withdrawal, Śiva at Mandara, the Yoginīs, Sūrya, Brahmā (Daśāśvamedha), Gaṇeśa as Ḍhuṇḍhirāja, Viṣṇu's disguises, the Divodāseśvara liṅga, Divodāsa's mokṣa and Śiva's return.
- *Kauṣītaki Upaniṣad* **3.1–8** — Pratardana in Indra's abode; prāṇa as prajñātman.
- *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* ~chs. 16–44 — Madālasā, Ṛtadhvaja and Alarka; Dattātreya's instruction.
- *Ṛgveda* (Maṇḍala 6; 2.19.6 etc.) — Divodāsa Atithigva and Indra vs Śambara. [scholarly]
- *Suśruta Saṃhitā*, Sūtrasthāna 1 — Divodāsa-Dhanvantari as Suśruta's teacher (frame-story).
- Diana L. Eck, *Banaras: City of Light* (1982), ch. on Divodāsa — the standard scholarly retelling and historical reading of the Kāśī-khaṇḍa cycle. [scholarly]
