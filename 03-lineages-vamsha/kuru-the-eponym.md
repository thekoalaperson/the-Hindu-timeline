# Kuru — Eponymous Founder of the Kuru Dynasty

> *Up to: [Lineages (Vaṃśa)](./README.md) · a Pauravā/Bhārata king of the [Candravaṃśa](./suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md), son of [Saṃvaraṇa & Tapatī](./samvarana-tapati.md), after whom the [Mahābhārata](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md) Kuru clan and Kurukṣetra are named.*

**Kuru** is the king who gives his name to the entire **Kuru** (Kaurava) clan — and therefore to *both* warring houses of the *Mahābhārata*, the **Kauravas** and the **Pāṇḍavas**, who are equally "Kurus" — and to the sacred plain of **Kurukṣetra**, the war's battlefield. He is no mere place-name eponym tacked onto the genealogy: he is a first-tier dynastic ancestor of the Lunar (Candra) line's **Pūru/Pauravā → Bhārata** branch, the node at which the clan ceases to be merely "Bhārata" and becomes "Kuru." His great act is the **consecration of Kurukṣetra as a dharma-kṣetra** through prolonged tapas — the ploughing of the field that makes it the holiest ground for death and sacrifice in northern India.

---

## 1. Placement in the Lunar genealogical spine

- **The Pauravā–Bhārata–Kuru descent.** Kuru sits in the long line: **Pūru → … → Duṣyanta → Bharata → … → Hastin (founder of Hastināpura) → … → Saṃvaraṇa → Kuru → … → Śāntanu → Bhīṣma/Vicitravīrya → Dhṛtarāṣṭra & Pāṇḍu → Kauravas & Pāṇḍavas.** Kuru is thus several generations *above* the epic's principal cast, an upstream ancestor rather than an actor in its plot. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva, Sambhava-parvan; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.19; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.22)
- **Son of Saṃvaraṇa and Tapatī.** Kuru is born to King **Saṃvaraṇa** (a Pauravā driven into exile by the Pāñcālas, restored by his purohita **Vasiṣṭha**) and **Tapatī**, daughter of the Sun-god **Vivasvān (Sūrya)** — so the Kuru line carries solar blood on the maternal side, a detail the epic underlines when Arjuna is greeted as "**Tāpatya**" (descendant of Tapatī) by the gandharva Citraratha. See [Saṃvaraṇa & Tapatī](./samvarana-tapati.md). (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva, Caitraratha-parvan ~167–173)
- **The renaming of the clan.** Before Kuru the dynasty is named **Bhārata** (after the emperor Bharata, son of Duṣyanta and Śakuntalā); after Kuru it is the **Kuru** clan. The two names coexist in the epic — the Pāṇḍavas are called both "Bhāratas" and "Kurus" — but "Kuru" becomes the operative clan-name for Hastināpura's royal house. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.19)
  - *Variant (king-order):* the exact number and names of kings between **Hastin** and **Kuru** differ across sources — the *Mahābhārata* inserts **Ajamīḍha → Saṃvaraṇa**, the Purāṇas give differing intermediate names and sometimes additional kings; never harmonise the rosters. [disputed]
  - *Variant (sons of Kuru):* Kuru's sons are commonly listed as **Sudhanvan, Jahnu, Pariṣadaśva (Parīkṣit the elder), and Pravara/Arava** — but the lists and spellings vary between *Mahābhārata*, *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* and *Bhāgavata*; the throne-line continues through one of them down to **Pratīpa → Śāntanu**. [disputed]

## 2. The consecration of Kurukṣetra

This is Kuru's defining deed and the reason a king of his rank is remembered. The field is named **Kurukṣetra** ("Kuru's field/Kuru's-plain") precisely because *he* made it sacred.

- **The ploughing of the field.** Kuru comes to the plain then called **Samanta-pañcaka** (in some strata) on the **Sarasvatī**, and there undertakes to plough it himself. He ploughs the ground with a **golden ploughshare**, intending to make it a field of dharma. (*Mahābhārata* Śalya Parva, Kurukṣetra-māhātmya stratum; *Vāmana Purāṇa*, Kurukṣetra-māhātmya)
  - *Variant (Vāmana Purāṇa):* the *Vāmana Purāṇa* elaborates the implements — **Yama lends his buffalo, Śiva his bull**, and Kuru yokes them to plough; the gods, curious, come to ask what he is sowing. When asked what crop he wants, Kuru answers he is sowing the **eight dharmic virtues** (truth, austerity, kindness, purity, charity, forgiveness, yoga, continence — the lists vary). [late text]
- **What he "sows."** Kuru's tapas takes the form of this perpetual ploughing; he offers to cut up the flesh of his *own body* as the seed, limb by limb, so that the field be sanctified — a self-sacrificial austerity. (*Vāmana Purāṇa*; *Mahābhārata* Śalya Parva māhātmya stratum) [late text]
- **Indra's intervention and the boon.** **Indra** (and in some tellings **Viṣṇu**) is moved/alarmed by the austerity and appears to grant a boon. Kuru asks that the field be made eternally holy and that **whoever dies at Kurukṣetra — in battle or by austerity — attain heaven (svarga), their sins destroyed**. The boon is granted, fixing Kurukṣetra as the supreme **dharma-kṣetra**. (*Mahābhārata* Śalya Parva; *Vāmana Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata Vana/Śalya strata):* the boon is variously stated as *those who die here go to heaven*, or *those who perform austerities or give gifts here reap infinite merit*, or *the dust of Kurukṣetra carried by the wind leads even sinners to the highest goal*. The māhātmya passages stack several formulations. [regional]
- **Why the war is fought there.** This boon is the deep narrative reason the **Bhagavad Gītā** opens by naming the battlefield **"dharma-kṣetre kurukṣetre"** ("on the field of dharma, the field of the Kurus," *Gītā* 1.1): the warriors who fall in the eighteen-day war die on Kuru's consecrated ground and so are promised the heaven his tapas secured. The eponym's boon frames the epic's central carnage as a passage to svarga. (*Mahābhārata* Bhīṣma Parva 23 / *Bhagavad Gītā* 1.1)

## 3. Significance — ancestor, not just eponym

- **A first-tier dynastic ancestor.** Unlike a bare place-name origin, Kuru is the clan's *self-definition*: the house of Hastināpura calls itself **Kuru-vaṃśa** / **Kauravas** after him, and "**Kaurava**" in its widest epic sense means *all* his descendants — Bhīṣma, Dhṛtarāṣṭra's hundred sons, *and* the Pāṇḍavas alike. The narrower later usage (Kaurava = Duryodhana's faction only) is a contraction of his much broader patronymic. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.19) [scholarly]
- **The double eponymy.** Kuru is one of the rare figures who names *both* a **people/clan** (the Kurus, a real historical janapada of the Vedic age, **Kuru-Pañcāla**) and a **sacred geography** (Kurukṣetra). The Vedic **Kuru kingdom** centred on the Sarasvatī–Yamunā doab is the historical-clock residue of the scriptural eponym. [scholarly]
- **Two clocks.** On the *scriptural* clock Kuru lives in the late **Dvāpara** of the 28th Mahāyuga, many generations before the Bhārata war. On the *historical* clock, the **Kuru realm** is a genuine early-Iron-Age (~1200–800 BCE) North-Indian polity attested in the *Atharvaveda* and *Brāhmaṇas* (King **Parikṣit** and the Kuru kings as patrons of Vedic ritual) — so the eponymous ancestor is the charter-figure for a documented Vedic kingdom, exactly as Bharata is for *Bhārata-varṣa*. The two clocks should not be collapsed. [scholarly]
- **Tapatī's solar graft.** Because his mother **Tapatī** is Sūrya's daughter, Kuru is also the point where the Lunar Pauravā line takes on a **solar** strand — a genealogical nicety the epic uses to dignify the Kuru kings as doubly divine-descended. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva, Caitraratha-parvan)

## 4. Cross-references within the war narrative

- **Śalya Parva — Kurukṣetra-māhātmya.** The fullest *Mahābhārata* statement of Kuru's ploughing and the heaven-boon sits in the Sarasvatī/tīrtha sections recited around the **Śalya Parva** (Balarāma's pilgrimage along the Sarasvatī passes the holy sites of the plain). See the [Śalya Parva deep-dive](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md).
- **Ādi Parva — the genealogy.** Kuru's place in the line from Pūru to Śāntanu, and the Saṃvaraṇa–Tapatī parentage, are laid out in the **Ādi Parva** genealogical recitations. See the [Ādi Parva deep-dive](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md).
- **The dynastic spine.** For the full Lunar tree and where Kuru's branch meets the Yādava and other forks, see [Sūrya- & Candra-vaṃśa lineages](./suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).

## Sources

- **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva** (Sambhava-parvan / Ādivaṃśāvataraṇa): the Pauravā–Bhārata–Kuru genealogy from Pūru through Bharata, Hastin, Saṃvaraṇa, Kuru, down to Śāntanu and the Pāṇḍavas; (Caitraratha-parvan ~167–173) the Saṃvaraṇa–Tapatī marriage and the "Tāpatya" patronymic. (Critical Edition; cf. Ganguli tr.)
- **Mahābhārata, Śalya Parva** (Sarasvatī tīrtha-yātrā / Kurukṣetra-māhātmya stratum): Kuru's ploughing of the field with the golden share and the boon that those who die at Kurukṣetra attain heaven; Balarāma's pilgrimage of the plain's holy sites.
- **Mahābhārata, Bhīṣma Parva 23 / *Bhagavad Gītā* 1.1**: "dharma-kṣetre kurukṣetre" — the field as consecrated dharma-ground.
- **Vāmana Purāṇa, Kurukṣetra-māhātmya**: the expanded charter of the consecration — Yama's buffalo and Śiva's bull yoked to the golden plough, Kuru sowing the dharmic virtues, the self-sacrificial austerity, and Indra/Viṣṇu's heaven-boon. [late text]
- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** IV.19: Pauravā genealogy, Kuru's sons, and the throne-line to Śāntanu (with king-order variants).
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (Śrīmad Bhāgavatam) IX.22: Candravaṃśa/Pūru-line genealogy framing Kuru's placement.
- Vedic/historical context: *Atharvaveda* and *Brāhmaṇa*-era references to the **Kuru kingdom** and King **Parikṣit** as the historical-clock residue of the eponym. [scholarly, comparative]
