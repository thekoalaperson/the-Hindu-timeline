# Yādava Vaṃśa — From Yadu to Kṛṣṇa

> *Up to: [Lineages (Vaṃśa)](./README.md) · the senior — but throne-barred — branch of the [Candravaṃśa](./suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md), descending from **Yadu**, eldest son of Yayāti. Its named events run from the primordial Lunar reigns through the **Tretā** Haihaya cycle to its apex and annihilation at the very end of **Dvāpara-yuga** (28th Mahāyuga), when **Kṛṣṇa's** departure opens Kali Yuga.*

The Yādavas are the dynasty that produces the 8th avatāra yet never holds imperial sovereignty: cursed at the root, split into a federation of clans (Haihaya, Vṛṣṇi, Andhaka, Bhoja, Chedi-Vidarbha cousin-lines), ruled by councils and restored kings rather than emperors, and finally erased by its own hand at Prabhāsa. Seats: **Māhiṣmatī** (Haihaya), **Mathurā** (Śūrasena country), and finally **Dvārakā**.

---

## 1. Yadu and the curse at the root

- **Yadu, eldest son of Yayāti.** Born to Yayāti by **Devayānī** (daughter of Śukra), with Turvasu; Śarmiṣṭhā bears Druhyu, Anu and Pūru. Yadu is thus the *senior* Lunar heir. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.10; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.18) See [Yayāti — deep dive](../04-deep-dives/yayati.md).
- **The refusal and the curse.** Yayāti, struck with premature old age by Śukra's curse, asks his sons to exchange their youth for it; **Yadu refuses** and Yayāti curses him: **his descendants shall never possess sovereignty / never be kings in the imperial line**. Pūru accepts and is crowned over his elders — hence the *junior* Paurava branch, not the senior Yādava one, holds Hastināpura. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~84–85; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.10; *Bhāgavata* IX.18–19)
  - *Variant (reading of the curse):* Purāṇic and later commentarial tradition treats the curse as the charter-myth for the Yādava **saṅgha/council polity** — why Kṛṣṇa, though the clan's greatest figure, never takes a throne, and why Ugrasena (not Kṛṣṇa) is king at Mathurā and Dvārakā. [disputed as explicit scripture; the inference is traditional]
- **Yadu's sons — the two great forks.** Yadu has four (some lists five) sons; the genealogies flow through two: **Sahasrajit → the Haihaya branch**, and **Kroṣṭṛ (Kroṣṭā) → the Mathurā/Dvārakā branch** that ends in Kṛṣṇa. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.11; *Bhāgavata* IX.23; *Harivaṃśa*, Harivaṃśa Parva)

---

## 2. The Haihaya sub-branch (Sahasrajit line) — Kārtavīrya Arjuna

- **Descent to Haihaya.** Sahasrajit → Śatajit → **Haihaya** (with brothers Haya and Veṇuhaya), eponym of the Haihayas of **Māhiṣmatī** on the Narmadā. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.11; *Bhāgavata* IX.23)
- **Kārtavīrya Arjuna (Sahasrabāhu).** Son of Kṛtavīrya; worships **Dattātreya** and gains a thousand arms, unmatched sovereignty, and (in Purāṇic praise) rule of the whole earth for 85,000 years. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.11; *Bhāgavata* IX.23; *Mahābhārata*)
  - *Variant (Rāmāyaṇa Uttara Kāṇḍa; Vāyu Purāṇa):* Kārtavīrya captures and humiliates **Rāvaṇa**, who is released at Pulastya's intercession — a synchronism pinning the Haihaya emperor to the Tretā of the Rāma cycle. [late text]
- **The wish-cow and Paraśurāma.** Kārtavīrya seizes sage **Jamadagni's** wish-granting cow (Kāmadhenu/her calf); **Paraśurāma** slays the thousand-armed king; Kārtavīrya's sons murder the unarmed Jamadagni; Paraśurāma then exterminates the Kṣatriyas **twenty-one times**. Canonical treatments: [Paraśurāma](../04-deep-dives/parashurama.md) and [Jamadagni & Paraśurāma](../04-deep-dives/jamadagni-parashurama.md). (*Mahābhārata* Vana/Śānti Parva; *Bhāgavata* IX.15–16; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.11)
- **Haihaya aftermath.** The surviving Haihayas fragment into the **Tālajaṅghas, Vītihotras** and allied hosts, remembered chiefly as the marauders whom later Solar kings (the Sagara cycle) fight. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.11; *Vāyu/Brahmāṇḍa* genealogies)

## 3. The Kroṣṭṛ line — Śaśabindu, Jyāmagha, Madhu, Sātvata

- **Śaśabindu the cakravartin.** In Kroṣṭṛ's descent, **Śaśabindu** is counted a universal emperor of fabulous herds and progeny — the one Yādava the Purāṇas style cakravartin despite the curse. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.12; *Bhāgavata* IX.23)
- **Jyāmagha, "most obedient of husbands."** The exiled, henpecked king Jyāmagha brings home a captive maiden; to appease his fierce queen **Śaibyā** he declares "she is my daughter-in-law" — though they are childless; a son **Vidarbha** is then born (in old age) and duly marries her. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.12; *Bhāgavata* IX.23) → fork to the **Vidarbha/Chedi cousin-lines** (§6).
- **Madhu and the Mādhavas.** A king **Madhu** in this descent gives the clan the patronymic **Mādhava**, borne by Kṛṣṇa; the country around **Mathurā** is likewise linked to a Madhu. (*Harivaṃśa*; *Mahābhārata* usage of "Mādhava")
  - *Variant (Rāmāyaṇa Uttara Kāṇḍa):* Mathurā's older name-legend belongs to a *different* Madhu — the **daitya Madhu** whose son **Lavaṇāsura** is slain by Śatrughna, who founds Madhurā/Mathurā; the Yādava Śūrasenas hold the city later. The two Madhus should not be merged. [disputed]
- **Sātvata and the four-fold split.** Descendant **Sātvata** (whence Kṛṣṇa's epithet *Sātvata* and the Sātvata bhakti tradition) fathers the clan-eponyms: from **Vṛṣṇi** the Vṛṣṇis, from **Andhaka** the Andhakas, from **Mahābhoja** the Bhojas (with Devāvṛdha, Bhajamāna and others heading minor septs). (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.13; *Bhāgavata* IX.24; *Harivaṃśa*)

---

## 4. Vṛṣṇi, Andhaka, Bhoja — the Mathurā federation

- **The federation, not a monarchy.** From here the genealogies interweave: Vṛṣṇis, Andhakas and Bhojas intermarry densely and act as a joint clan-polity under an Andhaka king at Mathurā — the texture behind Kṛṣṇa's role as counsellor rather than king. (*Harivaṃśa*; *Mahābhārata* Sabhā/Śānti Parva)
- **Andhaka line → Ugrasena and Devaka.** Andhaka's descent runs to **Āhuka**, whose sons are **Ugrasena** (king of Mathurā, father of **Kaṃsa**) and **Devaka** (father of **Devakī**). (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.14; *Bhāgavata* IX.24)
- **Vṛṣṇi line → Śvaphalka and Akrūra.** From Vṛṣṇi descends **Śvaphalka**, so holy that rain falls wherever he dwells; his son by Gāndinī is **Akrūra** — Kaṃsa's envoy who fetches Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma to Mathurā (and sees Viṣṇu in the Yamunā), and later the custodian in the **Syamantaka jewel** affair, whose absence from Dvārakā brings drought. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.13, V.13; *Bhāgavata* IX.24, X.36–39, X.56–57) → [Syamantaka jewel](../04-deep-dives/syamantaka-jewel.md), [Kaṃsa-vadha](../04-deep-dives/kamsa-vadha.md).
- **Bhoja line → Kṛtavarmā; Kuntibhoja.** **Kṛtavarmā**, son of Hṛdika, leads the Bhoja/Andhaka contingent **for the Kauravas**, joins the night-massacre of the Sauptika Parva, and is cut down at Prabhāsa. (*Mahābhārata* Bhīṣma–Sauptika, Mausala Parvas) → [Kṛtavarmā](../04-deep-dives/characters/kritavarma.md). **Kuntibhoja**, a Bhoja king, adopts Pṛthā/Kuntī (§5). (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva; *Bhāgavata* IX.24)
- **Śini line → Sātyaki.** From the Vṛṣṇi sept of **Śini**: Śini → Satyaka → **Sātyaki (Yuyudhāna)**, Arjuna's pupil and the great Yādava warrior **on the Pāṇḍava side** — the clan fights on both sides of Kurukṣetra. His feud with Kṛtavarmā over the Sauptika night-slaughter is the spark of the final brawl (§7). (*Mahābhārata* Droṇa, Mausala Parvas; *Bhāgavata* IX.24)

## 5. Śūrasena → Vasudeva and Kuntī; Ugrasena and Kaṃsa

- **Śūra(sena) and his ten sons.** In the Vṛṣṇi descent, **Śūra** (of the Śūrasena country around Mathurā) fathers ten sons — the eldest **Vasudeva**, called **Ānakadundubhi** because drums resounded in heaven at his birth — and five daughters, among them **Pṛthā**, **Śrutaśravā** and **Śrutadevā**. (*Bhāgavata* IX.24; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.14; *Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva)
- **Pṛthā becomes Kuntī.** Śūra gives his first-born daughter Pṛthā to his childless cousin-friend **Kuntibhoja**; as **Kuntī** she becomes mother of the elder Pāṇḍavas — making **Kṛṣṇa and the Pāṇḍavas first cousins** (Vasudeva and Kuntī are siblings), the genealogical hinge of the whole epic alliance. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva; *Bhāgavata* IX.24) → [Kuntī](../04-deep-dives/characters/kunti.md).
- **The aunt-lines to the epic's villains.** Śrutaśravā, married to **Damaghoṣa of Chedi**, is mother of **Śiśupāla**; Śrutadevā's son is **Dantavakra** — so Kṛṣṇa's two great slain cousins are sons of his father's sisters. (*Bhāgavata* IX.24; *Mahābhārata* Sabhā Parva) → [Śiśupāla](../04-deep-dives/shishupala.md).
- **Ugrasena and the usurper Kaṃsa.** **Kaṃsa**, son of the Andhaka king **Ugrasena**, imprisons his father and seizes Mathurā; warned that **Devakī's eighth son** will kill him, he jails Devakī and Vasudeva and slays their first six infants. (*Bhāgavata* X.1–4; *Harivaṃśa* Viṣṇu Parva; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* V.1–4)
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata X.1; Harivaṃśa):* Kaṃsa is the asura **Kālanemi** reborn, slain of old by Viṣṇu — recasting the family feud as a cosmic rematch.
  - *Variant (Padma Purāṇa and later texts):* Kaṃsa is begotten on Ugrasena's queen **Padmāvatī** by a demon (Drumila/Gobhila) in her husband's form — explaining his asura nature within an Andhaka house. [late text] [disputed]
- **Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma.** Vasudeva's sons: **Balarāma** by Rohiṇī (the seventh conception, transferred by Yogamāyā) and **Kṛṣṇa** by Devakī, smuggled to Gokula; they slay Kaṃsa and **restore Ugrasena** — the curse of Yadu kept: no Yādava usurps the seat. (*Bhāgavata* X.1–45; *Harivaṃśa*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* V) → [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md), [Kaṃsa-vadha](../04-deep-dives/kamsa-vadha.md).
- **Flight to Dvārakā.** **Jarāsandha** of Magadha (Kaṃsa's father-in-law) besieges Mathurā seventeen times; with **Kālayavana** also advancing, Kṛṣṇa evacuates the clans to a new sea-fort, **Dvārakā** in Saurāṣṭra. (*Bhāgavata* X.50–52; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* V.22–23; *Harivaṃśa*) → [Mucukunda & Kālayavana](../04-deep-dives/muchukunda-and-kalayavana.md), [Dvārakā — sacred geography](../06-sacred-geography/dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md).

## 6. Cousin-lines: Chedi and Vidarbha

- **Vidarbha.** Jyāmagha's son **Vidarbha** (§3) founds the southern kingdom of that name; his sons include **Kratha, Kaiśika** and (in the *Bhāgavata*) Romapāda, heading the Vidarbha king-lines. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.12; *Bhāgavata* IX.24)
  - **Bhīṣmaka, Rukmiṇī and Rukmī.** In epic times Vidarbha's king **Bhīṣmaka** fathers **Rukmiṇī**, whom Kṛṣṇa carries off from her betrothal to Śiśupāla; her brother **Rukmī** is later slain by Balarāma in a dice-quarrel — Yādava marrying back into the Yādava cousin-line. (*Bhāgavata* X.52–54, X.61; *Harivaṃśa*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* V.26) → [Rukmiṇī-haraṇa](../04-deep-dives/rukmini-harana.md).
  - **Damayantī of Vidarbha.** The earlier Vidarbha princess of the Nala tale, daughter of King Bhīma of Vidarbha. (*Mahābhārata* Vana Parva, Nalopākhyāna) → [Nala & Damayantī](../04-deep-dives/nala-damayanti.md).
- **Chedi.** The *Bhāgavata* runs the Chedi (Caidya) kings out of the Vidarbha descent (Romapāda's line → Uśika → **Cidi**, "from whom the Caidya kings"); in the epic age **Damaghoṣa** of Chedi weds Kṛṣṇa's aunt Śrutaśravā, fathering **Śiśupāla**, slain by Kṛṣṇa at Yudhiṣṭhira's Rājasūya. (*Bhāgavata* IX.24; *Mahābhārata* Sabhā Parva) → [Śiśupāla](../04-deep-dives/shishupala.md).
  - *Variant (Chedi's other dynasty):* the *Mahābhārata* also seats the **Paurava** king [Uparicara Vasu](./uparichara-vasu.md) on the Chedi throne generations earlier — the texts preserve two distinct Chedi dynastic claims side by side. [disputed]

## 7. The Mausala end — the clan destroys itself

Canonical home: [Mausala Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/16-mausala-parva.md).

- **Gāndhārī's curse.** After Kurukṣetra, the bereaved **Gāndhārī** curses Kṛṣṇa: in thirty-six years his Vṛṣṇi–Andhaka kinsmen will slay one another and he will die ignobly in the wilderness. (*Mahābhārata* Strī Parva 25 / Mausala Parva 1)
- **The musala-curse at Piṇḍāraka.** Yādava youths dress **Sāmba** (Kṛṣṇa's son) as a pregnant woman to mock the sages **Viśvāmitra, Kaṇva and Nārada**; the sages curse that "she" will bear an **iron club (musala)** that will destroy the race. The club is powdered and cast into the sea; the powder grows as **erakā reeds**, one surviving lump swallowed by a fish. (*Mahābhārata* Mausala Parva 1–2; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* V.37; *Bhāgavata* XI.1)
- **Prabhāsa.** Sent on pilgrimage to **Prabhāsa**, the drunken clans quarrel over the old Kurukṣetra wounds — Sātyaki beheads Kṛtavarmā for the Sauptika massacre — and, seizing the erakā reeds that turn to iron in their hands, **the Vṛṣṇis and Andhakas annihilate one another**, Kṛṣṇa's own sons among the dead. (*Mahābhārata* Mausala Parva 3–4; *Bhāgavata* XI.30)
- **The departures.** **Balarāma** dies in yoga, the serpent **Śeṣa** issuing from his mouth to the sea; the hunter **Jarā**, finding the fish-swallowed iron in his arrowhead, shoots **Kṛṣṇa's** foot — the avatāra departs. Tradition dates **Kali Yuga from this moment (3102 BCE, scriptural clock)**. (*Mahābhārata* Mausala Parva 4–5; *Bhāgavata* XI.30–31; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* V.37–38)
- **Dvārakā sinks; the remnant.** Arjuna evacuates the women and children; **the sea swallows Dvārakā**; Kṛṣṇa's great-grandson **Vajra** is installed as the surviving Yādava king (at Mathurā/Indraprastha, texts differ). End of the line as a power. (*Mahābhārata* Mausala Parva 7–8; *Bhāgavata* XI.31; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* V.38)

## 8. Two clocks

- **Scriptural clock.** Yadu and the curse sit in the primordial Lunar reigns; the Haihaya–Paraśurāma cycle in **Tretā**; the Mathurā–Dvārakā apex at the very end of **Dvāpara**, with the Mausala events at the Dvāpara–Kali hinge (Kali from 3102 BCE). (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV–V; *Bhāgavata* IX–XI)
- **Historical clock.** [scholarly] The **Vṛṣṇis are among the earliest historically attested cult-clans**: Pāṇini (c. 4th c. BCE) knows Vāsudeva-devotees; Kauṭilya's *Arthaśāstra* cites the Vṛṣṇi **saṅgha** (clan-oligarchy); the **Heliodorus pillar at Besnagar** (c. 113 BCE) and the **Mora well inscription** near Mathurā (early 1st c. CE, the "five Vṛṣṇi heroes") document early Vāsudeva-Kṛṣṇa worship. The Purāṇic genealogies themselves were redacted roughly mid-1st millennium CE and are dynastic charter, not census — the scriptural and historical Yādavas must not be collapsed into each other.
