# The Paulastya Line — the Rākṣasa Vaṃśa of Laṅkā

> *Up to: [Lineages (Vaṃśa)](./README.md) · master [index](../README.md). The **dynastic file** for the house of Laṅkā — the line the [Rākṣasas](../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md) bestiary and the character dossiers point to for the genealogy they deliberately hold back. Two descents meet in it: the brahmin line **Brahmā → [Pulastya](../04-deep-dives/characters/pulastya.md) → [Viśravas](../04-deep-dives/characters/vishravas.md)**, and the rākṣasa line of Brahmā's water-guardians running **Heti → Sukeśa → [Mālyavān, Sumālī, Mālī](../04-deep-dives/characters/sumali-and-malyavan.md)**. They are joined by one marriage — **[Kaikasī](../04-deep-dives/characters/kaikasi.md)** to Viśravas — and the result is **[Rāvaṇa](../04-deep-dives/characters/ravana.md)**. Narrative home for almost everything below: the [Uttara Kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md).*

---

**Scriptural clock:** the two roots reach back to the creation of the present *kalpa*; the dynasty's active career runs through the **[Tretā Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara, and ends with the fall of Laṅkā. **Academic clock:** the whole genealogy is transmitted by the **Uttara Kāṇḍa**, widely held a later stratum of the Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa* and absent from the oldest manuscript layers — a retrospective charter fitted onto enemies already killed in Book 6. The war-book itself (Sundara and Yuddha) knows the individuals but not, consistently, the genealogy: §8 records where the two strata disagree. [scholarly]

## 1. Two roots, one house

- **The dynasty is a hybrid by design, and the epic says so.** Vālmīki keeps the *race* of rākṣasas and the *royal house* of Laṅkā genealogically distinct. The race springs from Brahmā's guardians of the waters; the royal house of the *Rāmāyaṇa*'s antagonist springs from a Prajāpati, and enters the rākṣasa world only by marriage. Rāvaṇa is therefore a **great-grandson of Brahmā** on his father's side and a **grandson of Sukeśa's line** on his mother's, and every characterization of him — Vedic scholar, sāma-chanter, ten-thousand-year ascetic, and man-eating king — is the corpus's reading of that seam. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa* 2–9)
- **Three answers to "where do rākṣasas come from," not one.** The corpus keeps them apart because they answer different questions: **race** — Vālmīki's water-guardian etymology at *Uttara* 4 (*rakṣāmaḥ*, "we shall protect"); **descent** — the Purāṇic line from **Khasā**, a daughter of Dakṣa, through Kaśyapa; **dynasty** — this file. Merging them, as the popular "let us eat" etymology does, loses all three. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 4; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 1.21) — see [Kaśyapa](../04-deep-dives/kashyapa.md) and [Rākṣasas](../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md). [disputed]

## 2. The brahmin descent — Brahmā → Pulastya → Viśravas

- **Brahmā → Pulastya.** **Pulastya** is a **mānasaputra** of Brahmā, emitted without union at the dawn of the *kalpa*, and at once Prajāpati, Brahmarṣi and one of the original [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md). (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 1.7; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.12) — group node: [The Mind-Born Seers](../04-deep-dives/the-mind-born-seers.md).
- **Pulastya → Viśravas: the sentence that founds the line.** At **Tṛṇabindu's** hermitage on Meru's slope, Pulastya's austerities are broken by the singing of the sages' and nāgas' daughters, and he declares that the next maiden to fall under his gaze shall conceive. The one girl who had not heard him — **Tṛṇabindu's own daughter** — walks in and conceives on the spot; her father gives her to the sage in marriage, and the son is named **Viśravas** because the declaration was *viśruta*, "heard abroad." The whole Laṅkā dynasty descends from one sentence spoken in irritation, and this passage is **core Uttara text**, not a regional embellishment. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 2) [core text]
- **Viśravas's two marriages.** By **Ilavidā** (also Ilavilā / Iḍaviḍā / Devavarṇinī), daughter of the sage **[Bharadvāja](../04-deep-dives/bharadvaja.md)**, he fathers **Vaiśravaṇa (Kubera)**, named by Pulastya himself. By the rākṣasī **Kaikasī** he fathers the four of §5. The one sage is the father of both the wealth-god and the demon-emperor. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 3, 9)
- **The patronymic that never leaves.** *Paulastya* — "of Pulastya's line" — is used of Rāvaṇa throughout the epic, and gives this file its name and the brahmin **Paulastya gotra** its eponym. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* passim; gotra-pravara tradition)

## 3. The rākṣasa descent — the water-guardians to the three brothers

Compact spine only; the full race-level treatment, with the recensional drift in every roster, is the canonical home at **[Rākṣasas](../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md)**.

- **Heti & Praheti**, the two first-born rākṣasas, sprung at creation from Brahmā's guardians of the waters. Praheti turns ascetic; **Heti** founds the dynasty, marrying **Bhayā** ("Fear"), sister of **Kāla**. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 4)
- **Vidyutkeśa**, their son, marries **Sālakaṭaṅkaṭā**, daughter of **Sandhyā** (Twilight); she abandons the infant on a mountain. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 4)
- **Sukeśa**, the abandoned child, is found and blessed by **Śiva and Pārvatī** — and Pārvatī's grant is **race-wide, not personal**: rākṣasīs shall conceive and deliver in the same instant, and the children attain their mothers' age at once. This is the demographic engine behind every instantly-grown rākṣasa warrior in the epic. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 4)
- **Sukeśa × Devavatī**, daughter of the gandharva Grāmaṇī → the founding triad: **Mālyavān** (eldest), **Sumālī** (middle), **Mālī** (youngest). (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5) — dossier: [Sumālī & Mālyavān](../04-deep-dives/characters/sumali-and-malyavan.md).

## 4. The tenancies of Laṅkā — who held the city, and how it changed hands

The dynasty's whole history can be read off its address. Laṅkā changes hands **five times**, and only twice by force.

| # | Holder | How it was got | Text |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Viśvakarmā, for Śakra (Indra)** | Built to order on Mt. Trikūṭa | *Uttara* 5 |
| 2 | **Mālyavān, Sumālī, Mālī** | Told of it by its builder; move in | *Uttara* 5 |
| 3 | *(empty)* → **Kubera** | Rākṣasas abandon it for Pātāla after Viṣṇu's war; Viśravas names the empty city to his son | *Uttara* 8, 3 |
| 4 | **Rāvaṇa** | Ceded without a blow on Viśravas's counsel | *Uttara* 11 |
| 5 | **Vibhīṣaṇa** | Consecrated by Rāma after the war | *Yuddha* |

- **The city was never theirs to begin with.** **Viśvakarmā** raised golden Laṅkā on **Mount Trikūṭa** in the southern sea **at Śakra's command** — thirty leagues in extent, walls and terraces of gold — and it is the builder himself who tells the three brothers of it. The client is Indra; the rākṣasas are its first occupants and no more. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5)
- **They lose it by abandoning it.** Grown insolent on their boons, the brothers harry the devas until **Viṣṇu** takes the field. **Mālī** is beheaded by the discus after wounding Garuḍa (*Uttara* 7); **Mālyavān** is driven off by the blast of Garuḍa's wings (*Uttara* 8); and the survivors, led by **Sumālī**, quit Laṅkā altogether for **Pātāla / Rasātala**. No one takes the city from them. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 6–8)
- **Brahmā does not give Kubera Laṅkā.** Brahmā's grant to Kubera is lordship of wealth, station as the **fourth Lokapāla**, the **Puṣpaka**, and equality with the gods — and no city. It is **Viśravas**, asked by his son where he should live, who names the empty golden city on Trikūṭa. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 3) The common account, in which Brahmā bestows Laṅkā, gets both the giver and the sarga wrong.
- **The cession, not the war.** Once Rāvaṇa holds Brahmā's boon, **Sumālī** comes up from Rasātala and urges the claim; Rāvaṇa hesitates out of respect for an elder brother; **[Prahasta](../04-deep-dives/characters/prahasta.md)** supplies the argument — the earth itself once belonged to the Daityas until the gods took it, and dominion follows strength — and goes as envoy. Kubera offers to *share* the dominion, then consults **Viśravas**, who tells him that Rāvaṇa's boons have unhinged his judgment and that he should yield rather than fight a brother. Kubera withdraws to **Mount Kailāsa** and founds Alakā; Rāvaṇa is consecrated in Laṅkā. **No blow is struck.** (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 11)
- **The yakṣa war is a separate, later event.** Kubera afterwards sends an envoy urging his brother back to dharma; Rāvaṇa **kills the messenger** and has the body eaten, marches on Kailāsa, breaks the yakṣa army, strikes Kubera down with a mace and carries off the **Puṣpaka** as spoil. Cession (11) and war (13–15) are two events two sargas apart, and retellings routinely fuse them. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 13–15) — see [Kubera](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/kubera.md) and [Rāvaṇa §IV](../04-deep-dives/characters/ravana.md).
- **The Puṣpaka's own chain of title** runs alongside the city's: Brahmā → Kubera → Rāvaṇa (as spoil) → returned to Kubera after the war → **re-gifted by Kubera to Rāma**, with the standing summons "come whenever I call you to mind." (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 3, 15, 41)

## 5. The marriage that made the dynasty (Uttara Kāṇḍa 9)

- **A policy, not a romance.** Sumālī, up from Rasātala, sees **Kubera pass overhead in the Puṣpaka** and goes back down asking by what means rākṣasa power might be increased. His answer is his unmarried daughter: he sends **Kaikasī** to **Viśravas** expressly so that *"you will beget sons equal to that Lord of Wealth."* (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9)
- **The hour taints the issue.** Kaikasī arrives at **dusk**, in the middle of the sage's evening devotions, and stands silent with her eyes on her feet, scratching the earth with her toe. Viśravas accepts her and rules on the moment rather than the woman: because she came at that hour she will bear **offspring of dark aspect, delighting in the company of doers of evil deeds**. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9)
- **The exception she wins.** Kaikasī objects — *"I do not desire such sons whose nature is depraved"* — and Viśravas concedes exactly one: **the last-born shall be like myself, and virtuous**. That single concession is the textual origin of **Vibhīṣaṇa**, and therefore of the fifth tenancy of Laṅkā. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9)

## 6. The four children, and the half-brother

- **The birth-order is Uttara 9's, and it is not the popular one.** **Rāvaṇa (Daśagrīva)** → **Kumbhakarṇa** → **Śūrpaṇakhā** → **Vibhīṣaṇa**. Śūrpaṇakhā is Vibhīṣaṇa's **elder** sister; Vibhīṣaṇa's birth alone is marked by a rain of flowers and celestial drums. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9)
- **Kubera is not Kaikasī's.** He is Viśravas's son by Ilavidā, born before Kaikasī comes to the hermitage — elder **half-brother** to all four, and the man Kaikasī's goad ("strive to be like Vaiśravaṇa himself") sets her eldest against. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 3, 9)
- **The boons the four take at Gokarṇa.** Rāvaṇa's ten-thousand-year penance, a head into the fire each thousand years, and Brahmā's boon naming **suparṇas, nāgas, yakṣas, daityas, dānavas, rākṣasas and devas** — seven classes, **no gandharvas** — with men and monkeys contemptuously omitted; Kumbhakarṇa's sleep, asked with **Sarasvatī on his tongue** and carrying no waking-clause in this sarga; Vibhīṣaṇa's request for steadfast dharma, to which Brahmā adds **immortality unasked**. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 10) — see [Rāvaṇa](../04-deep-dives/characters/ravana.md), [Kumbhakarṇa](../04-deep-dives/characters/kumbhakarna.md), [Vibhīṣaṇa](../04-deep-dives/characters/vibhishana.md).
- **The marriages of the four (Uttara 12).** **Mandodarī**, daughter of the dānava architect **Maya** and the apsarā Hemā, to Rāvaṇa, with the **infallible śakti** as her dowry — the lance that fells Lakṣmaṇa at *Yuddha* 100. **Vajrajvālā**, daughter of **Virocana**, to Kumbhakarṇa. **Saramā**, daughter of the gandharva king **Śailūṣa**, to Vibhīṣaṇa. **Śūrpaṇakhā** to the Kālakeya dānava **Vidyujjihva**, whom Rāvaṇa himself later kills (*Uttara* 23), consoling the widow with the Daṇḍaka and a garrison under **Khara** (*Uttara* 24). (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 12, 23–24) — see [Mandodarī](../04-deep-dives/characters/mandodari.md) and [Śūrpaṇakhā](../04-deep-dives/characters/shurpanakha.md).

## 7. The collateral branches — the cousin-host

Rosters and their recensional drift are held at **[Rākṣasas](../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md)**; what belongs here is what each branch *does* to the plot.

- **Sumālī's ten sons by Ketumatī** — Prahasta, Akampana, Vikaṭa, Kālakāmukha, Dhūmrākṣa, Daṇḍa, Suparśva, Saṃhrādi, Praghasa, Bhāsakarṇa: the **uncle-generation**, and the war-book's command staff. **[Prahasta](../04-deep-dives/characters/prahasta.md)** is prime minister and *senāpati*, argues the claim to Laṅkā, and is killed by **Nīla** (*Yuddha* 57–58); Dhūmrākṣa and Akampana fall to Hanumān (*Yuddha* 51–56). (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5)
- **Sumālī's four daughters** — **Rākā, Puṣpotkaṭā, Kaikasī, Kumbhīnasī**. Kaikasī carries the main line; one of her sisters is the mother of **Khara**, whom Rāvaṇa names to Śūrpaṇakhā as *"the son of the sister of thy mother"* — the Janasthāna warlord is a **maternal cousin**, not a brother, and his own son is **[Makarākṣa](../04-deep-dives/makaraksha-son-of-khara.md)**. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5, 24)
- **Mālyavān's seven sons by Sundarī and his daughter Analā** — and through Analā the corpus's longest unbroken chain: her daughter **Kumbhīnasī** is seized by the asura **Madhu**; Rāvaṇa marches to avenge her, is met by her plea, and **forgives Madhu** and takes him as an ally (*Uttara* 25); their son **Lavaṇa** survives to be killed by **Śatrughna**, who founds **Mathurā** on the spot — the *Rāmāyaṇa*'s forward link into the Kṛṣṇa country. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5, 25) — see [Bharata, Śatrughna & their wives](../04-deep-dives/characters/bharata-shatrughna-and-their-wives.md).
- **Mālī's four sons by Vasudā** — **Anala, Anila, Hara, Sampāti**: survivors of the dynasty's fall who become **Vibhīṣaṇa's four counsellors** and cross with him to Rāma. They are Mālī's, **not** Sumālī's, and are the roster most often misfiled. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5)
- **Three cousin-names that collide with the war-books.** **Praghasa** and **Bhāsakarṇa** of Sumālī's ten, and **Virūpākṣa** of Mālyavān's seven, are also the names of three of the five senāpatis Hanumān kills in the Aśoka grove (*Sundara* **46.2–3**) — where the text gives the five no patronymic and no house, only their rank. Nothing in either passage identifies the two sets, and the epic reuses the names freely: a **Praghasa** falls to Sugrīva and a **Virūpākṣa** to Lakṣmaṇa at *Yuddha* **43.24–25**, and a further **Virūpākṣa** to Sugrīva at *Yuddha* **96.30–32**, all after the grove-battle. The uncle-generation and the grove-generation are kept apart here, unmerged. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5; *Sundara* 46; *Yuddha* 43, 96) [disputed] — see [Akṣakumāra](../04-deep-dives/characters/akshakumara.md).
- **Two Kumbhīnasīs.** Sumālī's fourth daughter and Mālyavān's granddaughter bear the same name; only the second is Madhu's wife. They are routinely merged. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5, 25) [disputed]

## 8. The last generation — and where the two strata disagree

- **Rāvaṇa's sons.** **Meghanāda / [Indrajit](../04-deep-dives/characters/indrajit-meghnad.md)**, the eldest, by Mandodarī — binder of Indra, master of the Nikumbhilā fire-rite; **[Akṣa (Akṣakumāra)](../04-deep-dives/characters/akshakumara.md)**, killed by Hanumān in the Aśoka grove before the war (*Sundara* 47); **Atikāya** (by Dhānyamālinī), **Narāntaka**, **Devāntaka**, **Triśiras** — all killed in the war-book. Only Indrajit and Akṣa are individually developed by Vālmīki; the roster is open-ended in the later lists. (*Sundara* and *Yuddha Kāṇḍas*; later genealogies) [open-ended roster]
- **Kumbhakarṇa's sons** — **Kumbha** and **Nikumbha**, both killed at Laṅkā (*Yuddha* 76–77).
- **Kubera's sons** — **Nalakūbara** and **Maṇigrīva**. Nalakūbara's curse on his uncle, for violating the apsarā **Rambhā** who had named herself his wife, is that if Rāvaṇa ever again touches a woman who does not desire him his head will split into **seven pieces** (*saptadhā*) — distinct in giver, sarga and number from **Brahmā's** curse over **Puñjikasthalā**, which fixes a **hundred** pieces and which Rāvaṇa confesses himself in his own war-council. (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 26; *Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 13) — see [Tapas, boons & curses](../05-concepts-and-dharma/tapas-boons-and-curses.md).
- **Vibhīṣaṇa's household** — his wife **Saramā**, daughter of the gandharva king Śailūṣa, who exposes the illusory-head deception to Sītā (*Yuddha* 33–34). The identification of **[Trijaṭā](../04-deep-dives/characters/trijata.md)** as their daughter is **not in Vālmīki**; it is later tradition and the corpus records it as such. [late text]
- **The war-book does not carry the Uttara's genealogy.** Two places where the strata pull apart, both recorded rather than smoothed: *Yuddha* **35.6** calls **Mālyavān** Rāvaṇa's *mātāmaha*, "maternal grandfather," where the Uttara's own line makes **Sumālī** the grandfather and Mālyavān his elder brother (maternal great-uncle); and the *Araṇya Kāṇḍa* uses "brother" of **Khara**, whom *Uttara* 24 makes a maternal cousin. Kinship terms for the senior maternal generation are elastic, and the Uttara Kāṇḍa was very likely composed after the books it back-fills. [disputed] [scholarly]
- **The end of the line in Laṅkā.** Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa, Indrajit and the collateral commanders are killed in the war; **Vibhīṣaṇa** — the concession Kaikasī bargained out of a curse — is consecrated king of Laṅkā by Rāma, and rules it with Mālī's four sons as his counsellors. (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa*; *Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5)

## 9. Competing genealogies

- *Variant (Rāmopākhyāna, Mahābhārata):* the epic's condensed Rāma-story has **no Kaikasī**. There **Kubera is Pulastya's own son**, **Viśravas** is produced afterwards out of half of Pulastya's being in anger at Kubera, and to appease that anger Kubera sends **three rākṣasīs** — **Puṣpotkaṭā, Rākā, Mālinī** — from whom the demon brood springs, distributed among them. The generation-count and the mother-scheme both differ from Vālmīki's and the two are not reconcilable. (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva*) — canonical home: [Rāmopākhyāna](../04-deep-dives/ramopakhyana.md). [disputed]
- *Variant (Purāṇic genealogies):* the Purāṇas keep Vālmīki's generation-count (Pulastya → Viśravas → Kubera) but redistribute the rākṣasa children across Sumālī's other daughters — **Khara** to Rākā, **Rāvaṇa and Kumbhakarṇa** to Puṣpotkaṭā. *Uttara* 9 gives all four to **Kaikasī alone**. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 1.8, 1.21; Purāṇic genealogical tradition) [disputed]
- *Variant (Kubera's mother):* **Ilavidā / Ilavilā / Iḍaviḍā / Devavarṇinī**, daughter of **Bharadvāja** or, in scattered lists, of **Tṛṇabindu** — which would make her her own husband's half-sister. The corpus records the spread and adopts none of it as settled. [disputed]
- *Variant (Jain Rāmāyaṇas):* the Jain tradition recasts the whole house as civilized **Vidyādharas** rather than demons, with its own dynastic scheme; catalog against Vālmīki, do not harmonize. — see [Jain & Buddhist Rāmāyaṇas](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/recensions/jain-and-buddhist-ramayanas.md). [scholarly]

## 10. Why this line is built the way it is

- **The genealogy is an argument.** By routing its antagonist through a Prajāpati, the *Rāmāyaṇa* denies itself the easy reading in which evil is foreign to the sacred order. Rāvaṇa's learning, his *tapas*, his Vedic mastery and his brahmin patronymic are not ironies grafted on; they are his inheritance, and the epic's question is what a lineage guarantees. [scholarly]
- **Every transfer of the city is a family matter.** Indra's builders, a father's advice, a brother's yielding, a nephew's claim, a conqueror's grant: Laṅkā is never once taken from an enemy. The one war fought over it — the yakṣa war — is fought *after* the transfer, for a chariot.
- **The women carry the transmissions.** Sumālī's daughter changes the dynasty's blood; Mālyavān's granddaughter opens the line to Mathurā; Maya's daughter brings in the weapon that nearly kills Lakṣmaṇa; and the one concession Kaikasī extracts is the reason there is an heir left to crown. The male roster is a war-casualty list; the female one is the plot.

- **Vālmīki, *Rāmāyaṇa*** (vulgate / Southern-recension sargas; the convention every citation here follows) — ***Uttara Kāṇḍa* 2** (Pulastya at Tṛṇabindu's hermitage; the declaration; the birth and naming of Viśravas); **3** (Viśravas × Ilavidā; the birth and naming of Vaiśravaṇa; Kubera's penance; Brahmā's grant of wealth-lordship, Lokapāla-ship and the Puṣpaka — and no city; Viśravas naming the empty Laṅkā); **4** (the water-guardian origin and the *rakṣāmaḥ*/*yakṣāmaḥ* division; Heti and Praheti; Vidyutkeśa, Sālakaṭaṅkaṭā, Sukeśa; Pārvatī's race-wide fertility boon); **5** (Sukeśa × Devavatī and the three brothers; Viśvakarmā's Laṅkā built at Śakra's command; the children of Mālyavān, Sumālī and Mālī, including Sumālī's four daughters and Mālī's four sons who become Vibhīṣaṇa's counsellors); **6–8** (Viṣṇu's war; Mālī beheaded by the discus; Mālyavān driven off by Garuḍa; the abandonment of Laṅkā for Pātāla); **9** (Sumālī's sight of Kubera in the Puṣpaka; Kaikasī sent, the twilight approach, the ruling on the hour, her objection and the concession of a virtuous last-born; the four children in order; "strive to be like Vaiśravaṇa himself"); **10** (Gokarṇa; the seven-class boon-roster with no gandharvas; Sarasvatī on Kumbhakarṇa's tongue; Vibhīṣaṇa's unasked immortality); **11** (Sumālī's urging, Prahasta's Daitya-precedent argument and embassy, Kubera's offer to share, Viśravas's counsel to yield, the bloodless cession); **12** (the four marriages and the śakti dowry); **13–15** (the murdered envoy, the yakṣa war, the Puṣpaka taken); **23–25** (Vidyujjihva killed, Śūrpaṇakhā widowed and posted to Daṇḍaka under Khara; Kumbhīnasī, Madhu and the forgiveness); **26** (Nalakūbara's *saptadhā* curse); **27** (Sumālī crushed to dust by the Vasu Savitra); **41** (Kubera's re-gift of the Puṣpaka to Rāma). *valmikiramayan.net does not host the Uttara Kāṇḍa; the Hari Prasad Shastri translation (Wisdomlib) is the working text for it.* [scholarly]
- **Vālmīki, *Rāmāyaṇa*** — ***Sundara Kāṇḍa* 44–47** (Jambumālī and the seven sons of Prahasta; the five senāpatis; Akṣa killed by Hanumān), **54** (the burn-roll naming the mansions of Prahasta, Sumālī and the rest); ***Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 13** (Rāvaṇa's own confession of Brahmā's hundred-piece curse over Puñjikasthalā), **33–34** (Saramā; Kaikasī and the minister Aviddha asking for Sītā's release), **35.6, 35–36** (Mālyavān named *mātāmaha*; his counsel and Rāvaṇa's refusal), **43.24–25** (a Praghasa killed by Sugrīva and a Virūpākṣa by Lakṣmaṇa, both after the grove-battle), **57–58** (Prahasta appointed, the omens, and his death at Nīla's hands), **76–77** (Kumbha and Nikumbha), **96.30–32** (a further Virūpākṣa, felled by Sugrīva), **100** (Maya's śakti fells Lakṣmaṇa).
- **Vālmīki, *Rāmāyaṇa*, *Araṇya Kāṇḍa* 19–34** — the Janasthāna garrison under Khara, and the loose "brother" usage the Uttara corrects.
- ***Mahābhārata*, Vana Parva** — the [Rāmopākhyāna](../04-deep-dives/ramopakhyana.md)'s anger-birth of Viśravas and three-rākṣasī scheme. [disputed]
- ***Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 1.7–8, 1.10, 1.21; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.12, 4.1** — the mind-born emission, Pulastya → Viśravas, the seers' wives, and the alternative Khasā descent of rākṣasas.
