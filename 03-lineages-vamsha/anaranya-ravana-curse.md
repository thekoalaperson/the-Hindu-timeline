# Anaraṇya — The Ikṣvāku King Whose Curse Dooms Rāvaṇa

> *Up to: [Lineages (Vaṃśa)](./README.md) · an early king of the senior [Sūryavaṃśa / Ikṣvāku line of Ayodhyā](./suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md), whose dying curse during [Rāvaṇa's digvijaya](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md) is the in-narrative charter binding the [House of Raghu](./raghuvamsha-kalidasa-kings.md) to Rāvaṇa's doom.*

**Anaraṇya** ("the treeless / desert-less one," or "without forest") is a king of the Solar (Ikṣvāku) dynasty of **Ayodhyā** known almost entirely for a single, pivotal episode: defeated by **Rāvaṇa** in the course of the rākṣasa's world-conquest, the dying king pronounces a **curse foretelling that a future descendant of his own line will slay Rāvaṇa**. Because that descendant is **Rāma**, the curse functions as the *Rāmāyaṇa's* internal dynastic charter — a prophecy planted in the deep past of the Solar line that the epic's central conflict will fulfill. The episode is recounted as backstory in the **Uttara Kāṇḍa** (sarga 19), one of a chain of curses Rāvaṇa accumulates that together pre-script his death. [scholarly]

---

## 1. Place in the Ikṣvāku king-list

- **Solar-line king of Ayodhyā.** Anaraṇya belongs to the senior **Sūryavaṃśa** branch seated at Ayodhyā in **Kosala**, the same line that runs Ikṣvāku → Kakutstha → Māndhātṛ → … → Raghu → Daśaratha → Rāma (the full spine is at [suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md §1a](./suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md)). (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.6–12)
- **Purāṇic placement — descendant of Māndhātṛ.** In the Purāṇic genealogies Anaraṇya is set among the early-to-middle kings of the line, in the cluster of descendants of the *cakravartin* **Māndhātṛ** (whose own reign is detailed at [mandhata.md](../04-deep-dives/mandhata.md)) — a son of **Trasadasyu / Pṛthu** in the standard reckoning, several generations above the later Sagara–Bhagīratha–Raghu segment. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.2–4; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.7)
  - *Variant (king-order, [disputed]):* the count and exact father vary by source. The Purāṇas, the *Rāmāyaṇa's* own Bāla Kāṇḍa lineage, and the Uttara Kāṇḍa's framing do **not** agree precisely on where Anaraṇya sits; the *Rāmāyaṇa* treats him simply as an illustrious past Ikṣvāku king of Ayodhyā without fixing a generation-number. This is the same king-order disagreement flagged for the whole line in [suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md §1a](./suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md). [disputed]
  - *Variant (a second Anaraṇya, [disputed]):* some king-lists know more than one bearer of the name in the long Ikṣvāku roll; the *Rāmāyaṇa's* cursing king is identified by the episode, not by a unique slot. Do not conflate the two clocks — the genealogical "Anaraṇya" of a Purāṇic name-list and the narrative "Anaraṇya" of the Uttara Kāṇḍa are the same figure only insofar as the epic claims him as an Ayodhyā ancestor of Rāma. [scholarly]
- **A "bare name" expanded by one episode.** Like most kings between Māndhātṛ and Sagara, Anaraṇya is in the Purāṇas little more than a succession-name; his entire narrative weight comes from the Vālmīki Uttara Kāṇḍa episode, not from the king-lists. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV; *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara Kāṇḍa 19) [scholarly]

## 2. The confrontation with Rāvaṇa (Uttara Kāṇḍa 19)

The episode sits inside the long flashback in which the sage **Agastya** narrates Rāvaṇa's earlier career to Rāma after the war — the same digvijaya sequence catalogued in [7-uttara-kanda.md](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md) (sargas 18–23).

- **Rāvaṇa's digvijaya reaches Ayodhyā.** In the course of his **world-conquest (digvijaya)** — having already subdued yakṣas, nāgas, kings, the Loka-pālas and even **Yama** — Rāvaṇa, drunk on victory, marches against the Solar kings and comes to the reigning Ikṣvāku sovereign of Ayodhyā, **Anaraṇya**. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara Kāṇḍa 19)
- **The king accepts battle.** Unlike many who submitted, Anaraṇya gives battle to defend Ayodhyā and the honor of the Ikṣvāku line; he is the lone king named in this sequence who openly challenges Rāvaṇa rather than yielding. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara Kāṇḍa 19)
- **Defeat and mortal wounding.** Rāvaṇa overpowers and routs Anaraṇya's army; the king himself is struck down, falling mortally wounded before the rākṣasa. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara Kāṇḍa 19)
- **Rāvaṇa's taunt.** Standing over the fallen king, Rāvaṇa mocks the famed might of the Ikṣvākus, asking where their vaunted strength has gone now that their king lies defeated. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara Kāṇḍa 19)

## 3. The dying curse — the dynastic charter

- **Anaraṇya's defiant reply.** The dying king answers that no warrior conquers another by his own strength alone but only as **fate / Time (Kāla)** ordains; his defeat is destiny's doing, not Rāvaṇa's true superiority — and Rāvaṇa has won nothing but the sin of killing a king who fought honorably. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara Kāṇḍa 19)
- **The curse foretelling Rāma.** With his last breath Anaraṇya pronounces the curse: **a king will be born in this very Ikṣvāku line who will take Rāvaṇa's life** — the prophecy that a future scion of his own house (i.e. **Rāma**) will slay the rākṣasa. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara Kāṇḍa 19)
  - *Variant (wording / named descendant, [late text]):* Vālmīki's curse names the **line** rather than naming "Rāma" outright; later retellings and commentaries make the reference to Rāma explicit, and some popular and regional versions tighten it into a direct naming of Daśaratha's son. The forward-reference is unmistakable either way. [late text]
- **The gods affirm it.** As Anaraṇya dies, the gods in heaven sound their approval (drums / a flower-rain in the telling), ratifying the curse as binding fate — sealing it as a decree of *Kāla*, not a mere dying man's wish. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara Kāṇḍa 19)
- **Why it matters — the in-narrative charter.** This curse is the **earliest in-story link tying the Ikṣvāku dynasty directly to Rāvaṇa's destruction**: long before Daśaratha or Rāma are born, the Solar line is already destined to produce Rāvaṇa's killer. It converts the *Rāmāyaṇa's* central war from a contingent quarrel over Sītā into the fulfillment of an ancient dynastic doom, and it slots Rāma into a role pre-assigned to his house. [scholarly]
- **One of a chain of curses.** Anaraṇya's curse is parallel to and reinforces the other death-warrants Rāvaṇa earns in the Uttara Kāṇḍa — **Vedavatī's** vow to be reborn as the cause of his death (→ Sītā) and **Nalakūbara's** curse against forcing an unwilling woman. Together these over-determine Rāvaṇa's fall: he is doomed by an Ikṣvāku king, by his future captive, and by his own lust, all before the main story begins. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara Kāṇḍa 17, 19, 26; see [7-uttara-kanda.md](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md))

## 4. The "two clocks"

- **Scriptural clock.** On the traditional reckoning Anaraṇya is a **Tretā-Yuga** king of the present (28th) Mahāyuga of the Vaivasvata Manvantara, set generations above Daśaratha; his curse is a real prophetic event in sacred time whose fulfillment (Rāma's slaying of Rāvaṇa) lies many reigns later in the same yuga. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV; *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara Kāṇḍa 19)
- **Historical / text-critical clock.** The episode survives only in the **Uttara Kāṇḍa**, the latest stratum of the *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* and widely held to be a supplementary book appended after the core five kāṇḍas. Read in that register, the Anaraṇya curse is a **retrospective narrative device**: it back-fills a destiny-frame onto the older Rāma story, manufacturing a deep-past warrant for the war the epic already tells. The genealogical and the literary "Anaraṇya" must be kept distinct. [scholarly]
