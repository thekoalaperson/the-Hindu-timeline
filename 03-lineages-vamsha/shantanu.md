# Śāntanu — King of Hastināpura

> *Up to: [Lineages (Vaṃśa)](./README.md) · a Pauravā/Bhārata–Kuru king of the [Candravaṃśa](./suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md), descendant of [Kuru](./kuru-the-eponym.md) (whose line begins with [Saṃvaraṇa & Tapatī](./samvarana-tapati.md)); husband of the river-goddess [Gaṅgā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganga.md) and the hinge-king from whom Bhīṣma, Citrāṅgada and Vicitravīrya descend.*

**Śāntanu** is the pivotal king at whom the *Mahābhārata*'s dynastic prelude turns: the last Kuru sovereign before the line fractures, father of **Bhīṣma** by the goddess **Gaṅgā** and of **Citrāṅgada** and **Vicitravīrya** by **Satyavatī**. He is no mere genealogical node in his son's story — he is a king with a full biography of his own: a cursed celestial reborn, a son granted by a boon, a husband twice married under strange conditions, and the ruler whose second love-match forces his eldest son into the most famous vow in the epic. His position in the spine is fixed: **Pratīpa → Śāntanu → {Bhīṣma (no issue); Citrāṅgada; Vicitravīrya}**, the throne passing through Vicitravīrya's widows to Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Pāṇḍu.

---

## 1. Pre-birth identity — the Vasu Mahābhiṣa

- **Mahābhiṣa, the cursed king.** In a former life Śāntanu is **Mahābhiṣa**, a righteous Ikṣvāku/solar king who by a hundred horse-sacrifices and a thousand soma-sacrifices wins **Indra's heaven (svarga)**. In the court of **Brahmā** he and the river-goddess **Gaṅgā** lock eyes when her garment is blown aside by the wind; both betray desire, and Brahmā curses them to be **born among mortals**, decreeing they shall return to heaven once the karma is spent. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva, Saṃbhava-parvan ~96)
- **He chooses Pratīpa's line.** Asked to name the mortal father he will be born to, Mahābhiṣa chooses **Pratīpa** of the Kuru line — and so the solar/celestial king is reborn into the Lunar **Candravaṃśa**. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~96)
- **Why Gaṅgā agrees to wed him.** Separately, the **eight Vasus**, cursed by **Vasiṣṭha** (for stealing his cow Nandinī, at Dyaus's instigation) to be born as men, beg **Gaṅgā** to be their mortal mother and to drown them at once to free them. Gaṅgā, already destined for a mortal husband, accepts — and this husband will be Mahābhiṣa-reborn-as-Śāntanu. The two curses interlock into one marriage. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~96–97)

---

## 2. Birth, the boon to Pratīpa, and the name

- **Pratīpa's tapas and Gaṅgā's proposal.** King **Pratīpa** performs austerities on the Gaṅgā; the goddess, in mortal form, seats herself on his **right thigh** and asks him to wed her. Because the right thigh is the seat reserved for a **son's bride / daughter-in-law**, Pratīpa declines marriage but promises she shall be his **son's wife** — sealing Gaṅgā's later union with Śāntanu. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~97)
- **The son born in old age.** Pratīpa and his queen **Sunandā**, long childless, obtain by penance a son — Mahābhiṣa reborn. Because he is begotten when his father has subdued his passions and attained **śama** (tranquillity / self-control), the boy is named **Śāntanu** ("the tranquil/wholesome one"). (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~97; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.20)
  - *Variant (name-etymology):* a second gloss says all whom Śāntanu touches with his hands attain **śānti** (peace) and renewed youth, hence "Śāntanu"; some tellings add he could restore aged men to youth by his touch. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva) [late text]
- **Brothers and the elder's renunciation.** Pratīpa's eldest son **Devāpi** is born leprous/skin-blemished (or renounces the world) and is barred from the throne; a middle son **Bāhlīka** takes the Bāhlīka kingdom; so the **Hastināpura throne descends to Śāntanu**. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.20; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.22)
  - *Variant (Devāpi):* the Purāṇas dwell on Devāpi withdrawing to the forest as a famous king-turned-ascetic still living in some traditions; the *Mahābhārata* mainly notes the throne passing to Śāntanu. [disputed]

## 3. The Gaṅgā marriage and the drowning of the Vasus

- **Accession at Hastināpura.** On Pratīpa's retirement/death **Śāntanu is consecrated king** of Hastināpura — a famed, just, truthful and devoted sovereign, a great hunter, under whom (the epic says) the realm prospers and even the rains and dharma are well-ordered. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~97–98)
- **The riverbank meeting.** Hunting along the **Gaṅgā**, Śāntanu beholds a woman of unearthly beauty — Gaṅgā in mortal form — and, remembering his father's promise, asks her to be his queen. See [Gaṅgā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganga.md). (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~98)
- **The secret-vow condition.** Gaṅgā consents on one absolute condition: **Śāntanu must never question or restrain her in anything she does, nor speak an unkind word**; the day he does, she will leave him. He agrees, and they live happily. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~98)
- **The seven drowned sons.** Gaṅgā bears eight sons in succession; **the first seven she carries to the river and drowns**, saying "this is for your good," each being one of the **Vasus** released from the curse. Śāntanu, bound by his vow, watches in silent grief and says nothing. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~98–99)
- **The eighth son spared — Devavrata.** When Gaṅgā moves to drown the **eighth**, Śāntanu can bear no more and cries out, forbidding her. The vow is broken. Gaṅgā explains everything — the Vasu curse, her role — and reveals this eighth child is the Vasu **Dyaus** (Prabhāsa), doomed to a **long mortal life** because his was the gravest part in the cow-theft. She names the boy **Gāṅgeya / Devavrata** and **departs**, taking the infant to be reared and educated. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~99)
- **Return of the grown prince.** Years later, grieving by the river, Śāntanu sees a youth damming the Gaṅgā with arrows; Gaṅgā appears, restores to him their **grown, fully-trained son** — schooled in the Vedas by Vasiṣṭha, in arms by **Paraśurāma**, in polity by Śukra/Bṛhaspati traditions — and Śāntanu installs **Devavrata as yuvarāja (crown prince)**. This is the future **Bhīṣma**. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~99–100)

## 4. The Satyavatī match and Bhīṣma's vow

- **The fragrance on the Yamunā.** Some years on, Śāntanu, again hunting, is drawn by a sweet fragrance to **Satyavatī** (Kālī / Matsyagandhā / Yojanagandhā), the fisher-maiden who ferries on the **Yamunā** — daughter (by adoption) of the **Dāśarāja**, the fisher-king of the Dāśa folk. He falls in love and asks her father for her hand. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~100; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.22)
  - *Variant (her origin):* Satyavatī is in fact the daughter of the Cedi king **Uparicara Vasu** by the apsaras-turned-fish **Adrikā**, born with a fishy odour and raised by the fisher-chief; see [Uparicara Vasu](./uparichara-vasu.md). She is already, before this, mother of **Vyāsa** by the sage **Parāśara** (the odour exchanged for divine fragrance). [scholarly]
- **The dowry demand.** The **Dāśarāja refuses** unless Śāntanu swears that **Satyavatī's son — not Devavrata — shall inherit the throne**. Śāntanu, unwilling to disinherit his beloved and worthy eldest, returns home **silently pining**, sick with suppressed longing. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~100)
- **Devavrata learns the cause.** Devavrata, troubled by his father's wasting melancholy, questions the ministers, learns of the fisher-king's condition, and **goes himself to the Dāśarāja** to win the bride for his father. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~100)
- **The double oath — Bhīṣma is born.** Devavrata first vows to the fisher-king that he **renounces the throne** in favour of Satyavatī's sons. The Dāśarāja, still fearing Devavrata's *own future children* might one day contest, presses further — and Devavrata takes the **terrible second vow of lifelong celibacy (brahmacarya)**, swearing never to marry or beget offspring. For this most dreadful oath (*bhīṣma pratijñā*) the gods rain flowers and he is thenceforth called **Bhīṣma** ("the terrible-vowed"). The fisher-king yields Satyavatī. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~100)
- **The boon of death-at-will.** Moved by his son's sacrifice, **Śāntanu grants Bhīṣma the boon of icchā-mṛtyu** — death only when he himself wills it — which is why Bhīṣma can later lie on the bed of arrows awaiting the auspicious hour. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~100)
- **The marriage.** Śāntanu weds **Satyavatī** and brings her to Hastināpura as queen. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~100; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.20)

## 5. The later sons, reign, and death

- **Citrāṅgada.** Satyavatī bears Śāntanu a son **Citrāṅgada**, a fierce warrior who succeeds to the throne (under Bhīṣma's regency) but is **slain young by a gandharva of the same name** in combat on the Kurukṣetra plain, dying without issue. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~100–101; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.20)
- **Vicitravīrya.** The second son **Vicitravīrya**, still a boy, then becomes king under Bhīṣma's protection; Bhīṣma wins for him the **Kāśī princesses Ambikā and Ambālikā** (and the rejected **Ambā**) at their svayaṃvara. Vicitravīrya too **dies young and childless** of consumption, ending Śāntanu's direct male line. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~101–102)
- **Niyoga continues the line.** With both of Śāntanu's later sons dead heirless and Bhīṣma vow-bound, **Satyavatī summons her elder son Vyāsa** to beget, by niyoga on Vicitravīrya's widows, **Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Pāṇḍu** and (by a maid) **Vidura** — so the dynasty descending from Śāntanu's seat continues by levirate, not by his own blood. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~103–105)
- **Śāntanu's reign and death.** Śāntanu reigns long and justly over Hastināpura — the epic's portrait of an ideal Kuru sovereign — and **dies in old age** while Citrāṅgada and Vicitravīrya are still young, leaving **Bhīṣma** as the throne's lifelong guardian. The hinge has turned: from Śāntanu's two marriages flow both the celibate guardian and the disputed succession that will become the war. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva ~100–101)

---

## 6. Two clocks

- **Scriptural clock.** Śāntanu sits in the late **Dvāpara-yuga** of the 28th Mahāyuga, two generations above the war's principal cast (**Śāntanu → Vicitravīrya → Pāṇḍu/Dhṛtarāṣṭra → Pāṇḍavas/Kauravas**), the immediate dynastic prelude to the Bhārata war. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.20) [scholarly]
- **Historical clock.** The name **Śāntanu (Śaṃtanu)** is genuinely Vedic: a **Śaṃtanu** and his brother **Devāpi** appear already in the *Ṛgveda* (X.98, the Devāpi–Śaṃtanu rain hymn) and the *Nirukta*, where Devāpi as purohita wins rain for Śaṃtanu's drought-struck kingdom — early-Iron-Age Kuru material the epic has woven into dynastic legend. The Vedic and epic Śāntanu should not be flatly equated. [scholarly] [comparative]

## Sources

- **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva — Saṃbhava-parvan** (~96–101, critical-edition numbering; cf. Ganguli tr. "Sambhava Parva"): the curse of **Mahābhiṣa** and **Gaṅgā** in Brahmā's court; the **eight Vasus** cursed by Vasiṣṭha and their bargain with Gaṅgā; **Pratīpa's** boon and the naming of **Śāntanu**; Śāntanu's accession, the **Gaṅgā marriage** and secret-vow condition, the **drowning of the seven Vasus** and sparing of **Devavrata/Bhīṣma**, Gaṅgā's departure and the return of the grown prince; the **Satyavatī** match, the **Dāśarāja's** dowry demand, **Bhīṣma's double vow** and the boon of death-at-will; the births and deaths of **Citrāṅgada** and **Vicitravīrya**; Śāntanu's reign and death.
- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** IV.20: the Pratīpa → Śāntanu → Citrāṅgada/Vicitravīrya succession; Devāpi and Bāhlīka; the Satyavatī marriage and the continuation by Vyāsa (with name-order variants).
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (Śrīmad Bhāgavatam) IX.22: the Candravaṃśa/Pūru-line framing of Śāntanu, his marriages, and the descent to Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Pāṇḍu.
- **Ṛgveda** X.98 / *Nirukta* II.10: the Vedic **Devāpi–Śaṃtanu** rain hymn — the historical-clock residue of the name. [scholarly, comparative]
