# Nimi and Vasiṣṭha's mutual curse — the bodiless king and the origin of blinking

> Cosmic address: the founding trauma of the **Videha (Maithila) cadet branch** of the **Sūryavaṃśa** — how **Nimi**, son of **Ikṣvāku**, and his purohita **Vasiṣṭha** cursed each other into bodilessness, why the sage was reborn from a pot, and why every living creature *blinks*. This file is the **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa (Uttara-kāṇḍa) canonical home** for the tale and the **nimeṣa etiology**; the Purāṇic telling and the whole downstream dynasty live in the [Videha line of Mithilā](videha-mithila-line-janaka.md). Scriptural clock: **Satya (Kṛta) Yuga**, the dawn of the Solar line. Academic clock: the Uttara-kāṇḍa is a **[late text]** appendix, c. 200 BCE–200 CE (§6). Parent: [03 — Lineages (Vaṃśa)](README.md) · Master index: [the Hindu Timeline](../README.md).

---

## 1. The quarrel — Nimi's sattra and Vasiṣṭha's prior retainer

- **Nimi, twelfth son of Ikṣvāku.** A righteous king of the young Solar line, Nimi founds a hermitage-realm on the eastern slopes and resolves to perform a **sacrificial session (yajña / sattra)** of enormous duration — the Rāmāyaṇa gives it as lasting **a thousand years** (variously five thousand). To officiate he goes first to the family priest **Vasiṣṭha** and asks him to be chief **ṛtvik**. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara-kāṇḍa sarga 55 [Shastri])
- **"I am already retained by Indra — wait for me."** Vasiṣṭha replies that **Indra (Śakra)** has engaged him first, for a rite of his own, and bids Nimi **wait until he returns** and then he will serve. Nimi, reflecting that human life is fleeting and time uncertain, **says nothing** — and Vasiṣṭha, taking the silence for assent, departs to Indra's sacrifice. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara-kāṇḍa sarga 55 [Shastri])
- **Nimi does not wait.** Holding that the span of life admits no delay, Nimi engages other qualified priests — the sage **Gautama** foremost among them — and begins the sattra without Vasiṣṭha. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara-kāṇḍa sarga 56 [Shastri])
  - *Name-collision note:* the **Gautama** who substitutes here is the officiant-ṛṣi of the tale, not to be conflated with other Gautamas; the Purāṇic version of the same episode likewise names Gautama as the replacement priest. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.5; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.13)
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa IX.13.1–3):* the Purāṇas open the same way but foreground the king's silent reasoning — that "this body is bound to fall" — as the very disregard for embodiment that the curse then makes literal. Kept side by side with the Rāmāyaṇa's stress on the **scheduling slight**.

## 2. The mutual curse — cursed asleep, cursing back

- **Vasiṣṭha returns to find himself supplanted.** His engagement with Indra complete, Vasiṣṭha comes back to Nimi's ground, sees **Gautama** officiating in his place, and is **enraged** at being passed over. He waits to confront the king — but Nimi is **asleep**. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara-kāṇḍa sarga 56 [Shastri])
- **The priest's curse: "Be bodiless."** Un-received and un-honoured, Vasiṣṭha curses the sleeping king: because Nimi slighted him and chose another, **his body shall fall — he shall become videha, "the bodiless."** (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara-kāṇḍa sarga 56 [Shastri])
- **The king's counter-curse.** Nimi wakes, learns he has been cursed **while asleep and guiltless**, and — holding the curse unjust and delivered in anger — **curses his guru back in kind**: since Vasiṣṭha cursed him without cause, blinded by wrath, **his body too shall fall.** Both frames — priest's and king's — drop away, and the two spirits are left disembodied. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara-kāṇḍa sarga 56 [Shastri]) — the priest's fuller portrait: [Viśvāmitra–Vasiṣṭha](../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md).
  - *Distinctive Rāmāyaṇa coloring:* the epic makes the mutual injustice symmetrical and pointed — Vasiṣṭha curses a **sleeping** man, and Nimi's retort turns the guru's own violence back on him. The Purāṇic version keeps the double bodilessness but hangs it on the bare fact that Nimi **began without him**.
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa IV.5; Bhāgavata IX.13.4–6):* same reciprocal curse, no sleeping-king detail; the Purāṇas move straight from "let this Nimi's body fall" to the king's answering "and let yours fall too." [both traditions cataloged, not merged]

## 3. Vasiṣṭha reborn from the kumbha — Mitra, Varuṇa, and Urvaśī

- **The spirit taken to Brahmā.** In the Rāmāyaṇa, the bodiless Vasiṣṭha's vital essence rises to **Brahmā**, who consoles him and directs him to re-enter existence **without a womb (ayonija)** — by lodging in the tejas of **Mitra and Varuṇa**. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara-kāṇḍa sarga 56–57 [Shastri])
- **The pot-birth.** When Mitra and **[Varuṇa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/varuna.md)** later behold the apsaras **Urvaśī**, their seed spills and is caught in a **water-pot (kumbha)**; from that vessel Vasiṣṭha takes a **new body** — the "Maitrāvaruṇi" (son of Mitra-Varuṇa) and "Kumbhayoni / Kumbhasambhava" (pot-born) patronymics both trace to this rebirth. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara-kāṇḍa sarga 57 [Shastri]; cf. *Ṛgveda* 7.33) — the shared jar-birth of the two seers: [Agastya](../04-deep-dives/agastya.md), his pot-brother.
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata IX.13.6; Viṣṇu Purāṇa IV.5):* the Purāṇas compress this to a single beat — the bodiless Vasiṣṭha simply **enters the seed of Mitra-Varuṇa** spilled at the sight of Urvaśī and is born again — omitting the intercession of Brahmā that the Rāmāyaṇa supplies.
  - *Cross-reference:* the same Mitra-Varuṇa / Urvaśī seed is the canonical birth-myth of **Agastya** (and, in the Ṛgvedic seed at 7.33.10–13, of Vasiṣṭha and Agastya **together** from one jar); the apsaras herself belongs to the [apsaras](../08-beings-and-bestiary/apsaras.md) class and to the [Purūravas–Urvaśī](../04-deep-dives/pururavas-urvashi.md) cycle.

## 4. Nimi refuses the body — the wager over embodiment

- **The corpse preserved through the sattra.** Nimi's officiants — Gautama and the others — do not abandon the unfinished rite. His body is **kept from decay in fragrant unguents and aromatics** while the long sacrifice runs to its close. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara-kāṇḍa sarga 57 [Shastri]; *Bhāgavata* IX.13.8)
- **The gods offer to restore him — he declines.** At the sacrifice's end the pleased **devas** propose to return Nimi to his body. He **refuses**: having once known the pain of the body's fall, he will not re-enter a frame only to suffer separation again. In the Bhāgavata's telling the sage-king reasons that the body is **bondage and misery (bandha, kleśa)**; he asks instead for a **bodiless dwelling** among living beings. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara-kāṇḍa sarga 57 [Shastri]; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.13.9–11; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.5)
- **The doctrinal seed of "Videha."** A king who **refuses re-embodiment** founds a house whose very name, **Videha** ("the bodiless"), and royal style **Vaideha** carry a deliberate resonance the later tradition prizes — the same Mithilā line will produce Janakas famed for knowing the Self beyond the body, and will lend its name to **videha-mukti** ("disembodied liberation"). (tradition; developed at the [Videha line of Mithilā](videha-mithila-line-janaka.md))

## 5. Nimi in the eyelids of all creatures — the nimeṣa etiology

- **The boon: to dwell in the eyes of the living.** The gods grant Nimi's request and set him **in the eyes of all creatures**, where he abides as the moving air/breath (**vāyu**). Because of his presence and passage, the eyelids of every being **open and close** — the opening and shutting is his coming and going. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara-kāṇḍa sarga 57 [Shastri])
- **Why beings blink — the folk-etymology.** The **blink** (*nimeṣa*, from the root *nimiṣ-*, "to shut the eye") is read as Nimi's memorial: creatures wink **on account of Nimi**, and the twinkling of the eye bears his name. The unblinking eye becomes, by contrast, the mark of the **gods** (who do not wink) — Nimi's residence is a distinctly mortal, embodied rhythm. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara-kāṇḍa sarga 57 [Shastri]) [folk etymology]
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata IX.13.12; Viṣṇu Purāṇa IV.5):* the Purāṇas give the same grant in a line — Nimi lives "in the eyes of all beings," whence the **opening and closing of the eyelids** — without the Rāmāyaṇa's explicit vāyu mechanism. [cataloged separately]
- **The unit of time.** Independently of the myth, **nimeṣa** ("a blink / twinkling") is fixed as a base **[unit of time](../00-time-and-cosmology/units-of-time.md)** in Hindu horology (Kāṣṭhā = 15 nimeṣa in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa, 18 in Manu/Sūrya-siddhānta) — the aetiology and the chronometry share the same word. [scholarly]

## 6. Mithi, and how the Rāmāyaṇa telling meets the Purāṇic

- **The dynasty must continue.** With the king bodiless, the ṛṣis fear **anarchy (arājaka)** in a realm without an heir, and **churn** Nimi's preserved body so that a son springs forth. This churning-birth is carried by **both traditions** — the Rāmāyaṇa has the sages agitate the body with the **fire-drill (araṇi)**, the Purāṇas by **churning (mathana)**. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara-kāṇḍa sarga 57 [Shastri]; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.5; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.13.13)
- **One birth, three names.** The Bhāgavata packs the house's whole nomenclature into a single verse: **Janaka** (from the extraordinary *birth*, *janana*), **Vaideha** (born of the *bodiless* one, *videha*), and **Mithi / Mithila** (born of *churning*, *mathana*) — and the city he raises is **Mithilā**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.13.13; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.5)
- **What Vālmīki himself carries.** The Rāmāyaṇa's Uttara-kāṇḍa telling (**sarga 57**) carries not only the **curse, the pot-rebirth, and the blink** but the **churning-birth of Mithi** itself: from Nimi's agitated body a great ascetic being arises who, because he was "born of an inanimate body," is named **Mithi, King of Videha** — the ancient **Janaka** and the origin of the race of **Mithilā**. The succession **Nimi → Mithi → Janaka** is thus Vālmīki's own, not merely Purāṇic; the epic gives it a **second** time in the **Bāla-kāṇḍa** (sarga 71), where **Janaka recites his lineage** to Vasiṣṭha and Daśaratha. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara-kāṇḍa sarga 57 & Bāla-kāṇḍa sarga 71 [Shastri]) — full king-roll and the "many-Janakas" problem at the [Videha line of Mithilā](videha-mithila-line-janaka.md).
  - *Reliability note:* the **churning etymology** of "Mithi" is **shared by the Rāmāyaṇa and the Purāṇas** — Vālmīki derives the name from Nimi's body being agitated by the fire-drill, the Purāṇas from *mathana* — not a Purāṇic-only elaboration; this file's proper subject remains the Rāmāyaṇa's distinctive contributions, the mutual curse and the nimeṣa boon. [variants cataloged, not merged]

## 7. Reading the tale — the two clocks and the sequel king

- **Scriptural clock.** Nimi is a **son of Ikṣvāku**, i.e. the second generation of the Vaivasvata Solar line — placed in the **Satya (Kṛta) Yuga** of the current 28th Mahāyuga, at the very dawn of the dynasty that will later run down to Rāma in the Tretā. See the [Sūryavaṃśa–Candravaṃśa overview](suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) and the neighboring dawn-of-the-line tale [Ilā / Sudyumna](ila-sudyumna.md).
- **Academic clock.** The **Uttara-kāṇḍa** is widely held to be a **later appendix** to the Rāmāyaṇa — its Nimi episode, the pot-birth, and the blink-etiology are **[late text]** material, crystallized c. **200 BCE–200 CE** and layering explanatory *aitihya* (traditional etymology) onto an already-old dynastic name. [scholarly] The nimeṣa-from-Nimi derivation is a **folk etymology**; the word's actual root (*ni-* + *miṣ-*, "to shut the eyes") predates any personal name.
- **A later Nimi in the dialogues.** A **King Nimi of Videha** — either the eponym or a throne-bearing Janaka after him — hosts the **nine Yogendras** in the Bhāgavata's Kali-facing bhakti catechism; a distinct episode of the same house, treated at [Nimi & the Nine Yogendras](../04-deep-dives/nimi-nava-yogendra-samvada.md). [do not conflate with the curse-tale here]
