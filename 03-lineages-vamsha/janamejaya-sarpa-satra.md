# Janamejaya and the Sarpa-Satra (Snake Sacrifice)

> Hierarchy: `03-lineages-vamsha/janamejaya-sarpa-satra.md` — a Pauravā/Kuru-branch episode of the [Candravaṃśa / Lunar dynasty](suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md). Up one level: [Solar & Lunar Dynasties (Vaṃśa)](README.md). This is the **outer dramatic frame of the entire Mahābhārata**: the rite at which Vaiśampāyana first recites the epic to King Janamejaya. See the [Ādi Parva deep-dive](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md) (Pauṣya / Pauloma / Āstīka sub-parvas) and the parallel [Uttaṅka](../04-deep-dives/uttanka.md) tale that triggers the same rite.

The **Sarpa-satra** ("serpent session-sacrifice") of **Janamejaya** sits one generation *after* the Kurukṣetra war, at the **Dvāpara→Kali cusp** of the current 28th Mahāyuga. On the two-clock distinction: in **scriptural time** this is a late-Dvāpara / earliest-Kali event of the Vaivasvata Manvantara; in **historical/text-critical time** the frame is the literary device through which the *Mahābhārata* presents itself as already-composed and now being recited — the epic narrates its own occasion of telling. [scholarly]

---

## Who Janamejaya is

- **Janamejaya, the Pauravā/Kuru king** — son of **Parīkṣit**, grandson of **Abhimanyu**, great-grandson of **Arjuna**; the reigning Kuru emperor at **Hāstinapura/Takṣaśilā** in the opening Kali Yuga. The genealogical tail of the lunar spine: **…Pāṇḍu → Arjuna → Abhimanyu → Parīkṣit → Janamejaya** (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.16, IX.22). See the [lunar-line spine](suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md#2d-paurava--bhārata--kuru-branch--pāṇḍavas--kauravas).
- **His brothers** — Janamejaya rules with three brothers, **Śrutasena, Ugrasena, Bhīmasena** (Janamejayas in the plural in some lists), and performs the sacrifice as the eldest (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).
  - *Variant (multiple Janamejayas):* Purāṇic king-lists name more than one **Janamejaya** in the Paurava roll; the snake-sacrificer is specifically **Janamejaya Pārikṣita** ("son of Parīkṣit"), distinguished from earlier homonyms. [disputed]

## The motive — Parīkṣit's death by Takṣaka

- **Parīkṣit's curse** — King Parīkṣit, hunting, slings a dead snake onto the meditating sage **Śamīka**; the sage's son **Śṛṅgin**, enraged, curses the king to die within seven days, bitten by the nāga **Takṣaka** (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva; *Bhāgavata* I.18–19).
- **The seven-day vigil** — Parīkṣit fortifies a pillared tower with physicians, mantras and antidotes; in the *Bhāgavata* he instead renounces, fasts on the Gaṅgā bank, and hears the entire *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* from **Śuka** in his final seven days (*Bhāgavata* I.19–XII.6).
- **Takṣaka's stratagem** — Takṣaka bribes/diverts the physician **Kaśyapa** (who could have cured the bite), enters the tower hidden as a worm in a fruit, and bites the king; Parīkṣit dies, his body consumed by serpent-venom-fire (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).
- **Janamejaya's vow of vengeance** — Crowned as a boy, Janamejaya later learns from his ministers the full circumstance of his father's death and vows to **exterminate the entire serpent race** in a great fire-sacrifice, avenging Parīkṣit upon Takṣaka (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).
  - *Variant (Uttaṅka's goad):* in the **Pauṣya** telling, the sage **Uttaṅka** — robbed of celestial earrings by Takṣaka — arrives at court and inflames Janamejaya's resolve, supplying the proximate trigger for the rite (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Pauṣya Parva). See [Uttaṅka](../04-deep-dives/uttanka.md). [scripture]

## The Sarpa-satra at Takṣaśilā

- **The great serpent session** — Janamejaya commissions a **sarpa-satra** (a *sattra*, a long multi-priest soma-style session) at **Takṣaśilā** (Taxila), with a specially raised altar and a host of *ṛtvij* priests, the **hotṛ Caṇḍabhārgava** officiating (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).
- **Serpents drawn into the fire by mantra** — As the priests pour oblations and chant, the **nāgas are dragged helplessly from everywhere through the air** and fall into the blazing sacrificial pit, burning by the thousands; the venom-fire consumes whole serpent races (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).
- **The snake races burned** — Named serpent clans perish wholesale; the rite is framed as the fulfillment of an ancient **curse of Kadrū** upon her nāga sons (that they burn in Janamejaya's sacrifice) — tying the event back to the [Garuḍa / churning](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md) backstory of the Āstīka Parva (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).
  - *Variant (Kadrū's curse):* the Āstīka Parva supplies the deep cause — Kadrū cursed her serpent-sons for refusing to help her cheat **Vinatā** in the wager over Uccaiḥśravas's tail; Brahmā ratifies a *partial* curse so the wicked nāgas alone perish in Janamejaya's fire. This pre-justifies the sacrifice as cosmic, not merely vengeful. [scripture]
- **An omen of a halt** — As the burning peaks, the priests foresee that a **brāhmaṇa boy** will come and stop the rite; the omen is announced even as Takṣaka eludes the flames (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).

## Takṣaka's flight to Indra

- **Takṣaka takes refuge with Indra** — The chief target, **Takṣaka**, flees to the heaven of **Indra**, who shelters him (the two being old friends/allies); Takṣaka coils around Indra's very throne (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).
- **Dragging Indra himself** — The priests intensify the mantra to name **"Takṣaka with Indra"**; both the serpent and Indra's throne begin to be hauled toward the fire, alarming the gods — the rite threatens to pull a deva into the pit (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).
- **Indra abandons him** — Terrified, Indra releases Takṣaka, who now plummets, half-stunned, toward the flames — the precise instant at which the rescuing intervention lands (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).

## Āstīka's intervention — the rite halted

- **Who Āstīka is** — A boy-sage, son of the ascetic **Jaratkāru** and the **nāga-maiden** of the same name, **Jaratkāru**, who is the **sister of the serpent-king Vāsuki**; thus Āstīka is half-brāhmaṇa, half-nāga — uniquely able to redeem the serpents (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).
  - *Variant (the nāga mother = Manasā):* later/regional tradition identifies the serpent-mother of Āstīka with the goddess **Manasā**, queen of snakes; the core Mahābhārata names her only as **Jaratkāru** (sister of Vāsuki) and does not use the name Manasā. [regional] [late text]
  - *Etymology* — Vāsuki gives his sister to the sage Jaratkāru on condition the marriage produce a saviour-son; when the pregnant wife is sent away she answers "**asti**" ("he exists/it shall be") — whence the child is named **Āstīka** ("He-who-is") (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).
- **Vāsuki's foresight** — Knowing of Kadrū's curse, **Vāsuki** had earlier arranged this very marriage so that a son might one day halt the sacrifice and save the surviving serpents; Āstīka is reared expressly for this purpose (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).
- **Āstīka enters the sacrifice** — The boy walks into Janamejaya's enclosure and praises the rite, the king, the priests and the fire in flawless Sanskrit verse; delighted, Janamejaya offers him a **boon** (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).
- **The boon that stops the fire** — Over the priests' objection (Takṣaka is "almost in the fire"), Āstīka asks that the **sacrifice cease** and the remaining serpents be spared; bound by the granted boon, Janamejaya halts the rite — **Takṣaka and the surviving nāga races are saved** (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva; *Bhāgavata* — abbreviated, XII).
- **Why this matters** — Āstīka's act becomes the etiological charter for the **Nāga-pañcamī** invocation: reciting Āstīka's name is said to protect from snakebite; the nāgas bless him for ending their genocide (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva; festival tradition). [folk]

## The Mahābhārata's own frame

- **Vaiśampāyana recites the epic** — During (or just after) the snake-sacrifice, **Vyāsa** arrives at Janamejaya's court; at his bidding his disciple **Vaiśampāyana** narrates the entire *Mahābhārata* — the history of Janamejaya's own ancestors — to the king as the rite proceeds (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Pauṣya/Āstīka/Aṃśāvataraṇa Parvas).
- **The nested frames** — That recitation is in turn re-narrated by the bard **Ugraśravas Sauti** to the ṛṣis at the **Naimiṣa forest**, who heard him having attended Janamejaya's sacrifice — giving the epic its characteristic **three-layer frame** (Vyāsa → Vaiśampāyana → Sauti) (Mahābhārata, Ādi 1). See [Ādi Parva — the Frame & 19 sub-parvas](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md#the-frame--the-19-sub-parvas).
- **The Pauṣya / Pauloma / Āstīka run** — These three opening *upaparvas* of the Ādi Parva (Ādi 3–58) supply the snake-sacrifice frame end to end: **Pauṣya** (Uttaṅka's quest, the Takṣaka grudge), **Pauloma** (the Bhṛgu/Bhārgava lineage, birth of Cyavana, transmission credentials), and **Āstīka** (Kadrū–Vinatā wager, Garuḍa, the churning for amṛta, the sacrifice and its halting) (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Pauṣya/Pauloma/Āstīka Parvas).

## Aftermath & afterlives

- **The horse-sacrifice expiation** — Some traditions hold Janamejaya later performs an **Aśvamedha** and incurs/expiates brahminicide-adjacent guilt for the mass slaughter of nāgas; he is counselled by sages on the violence of the rite (Mahābhārata, late strata; Purāṇic notices). [disputed]
- **Bhāgavata compression** — The *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* mentions the sarpa-satra only briefly (Skandha XII), its narrative weight thrown instead onto Parīkṣit's seven-day liberation by Śuka; the snake-sacrifice serves there mainly to date the recitation and confirm the Kali-Yuga setting (*Bhāgavata* XII.6).
- **Historical "two-clock" note** — Takṣaśilā (Taxila) is a real historical city; later tradition and some modern writers tried to anchor Janamejaya in post-Bhārata-war regnal lists (the Purāṇic Paurava king-roll continuing to the Nandas). The repo keeps the **scriptural clock** (late-Dvāpara/early-Kali Vaivasvata Manvantara) distinct from any **historical clock** placement of a Kuru king at Taxila. [scholarly]
