# Janamejaya and the Sarpa-Satra (Snake Sacrifice)

The **Sarpa-satra** ("serpent session-sacrifice") of **Janamejaya** sits one generation *after* the Kurukṣetra war, at the **Dvāpara→Kali cusp** of the current 28th Mahāyuga. On the two-clock distinction: in **scriptural time** this is a late-Dvāpara / earliest-Kali event of the Vaivasvata Manvantara; in **historical/text-critical time** the frame is the literary device through which the *Mahābhārata* presents itself as already-composed and now being recited — the epic narrates its own occasion of telling. ## Who Janamejaya is

- **Janamejaya, the Pauravā/Kuru king** — son of **Parīkṣit**, grandson of **Abhimanyu**, great-grandson of **Arjuna**; the reigning Kuru emperor at **Hāstinapura/Takṣaśilā** in the opening Kali Yuga. The genealogical tail of the lunar spine: **…Pāṇḍu → Arjuna → Abhimanyu → Parīkṣit → Janamejaya** (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* I.16, IX.22). See the [lunar-line spine](suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md#2d-paurava--bhārata--kuru-branch--pāṇḍavas--kauravas).
- **His brothers** — Janamejaya rules with three brothers, **Śrutasena, Ugrasena, Bhīmasena** (Janamejayas in the plural in some lists), and performs the sacrifice as the eldest (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).
  - *Variant (multiple Janamejayas):* Purāṇic king-lists name more than one **Janamejaya** in the Paurava roll; the snake-sacrificer is specifically **Janamejaya Pārikṣita** ("son of Parīkṣit"), distinguished from earlier homonyms. [disputed]

## The succession crisis and the rite — summary (full treatment: [Āstīka & the Snake-Sacrifice](../04-deep-dives/astika-sarpa-satra.md))

- **The line reduced to one heir** — After Kurukṣetra the whole Kuru line rests on **Parīkṣit** (revived in the womb by Kṛṣṇa from Aśvatthāman's *brahmāstra*); his death by **Takṣaka's** bite — the fruit of sage Śṛṅgin's seven-day curse — orphans a boy-king and nearly severs the dynasty a second time (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva; *Bhāgavata* I.18–19). See [Parīkṣit](parikshit.md).
- **Janamejaya's vow and the sarpa-satra** — Crowned young, Janamejaya learns the circumstance of his father's death and vows to **exterminate the serpent race** in a great fire-sacrifice at **Takṣaśilā** (the hotṛ **Caṇḍabhārgava** officiating); nāgas burn by the thousands, Takṣaka flees to **Indra**, and the rite is halted at its peak by the boy-sage **Āstīka's** boon — every stage, with its variants (Kadrū's curse, the Bhāgavata's reframing, the Nāga-pañcamī etiology), is catalogued in [the canonical deep-dive](../04-deep-dives/astika-sarpa-satra.md) (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Āstīka Parva).
  - *Variant (Uttaṅka's goad):* in the **Pauṣya** telling, the sage **Uttaṅka** — robbed of celestial earrings by Takṣaka — arrives at court and inflames Janamejaya's resolve, supplying the proximate trigger for the rite (Mahābhārata, Ādi, Pauṣya Parva). See [Uttaṅka](../04-deep-dives/uttanka.md). [scripture]

## The Mahābhārata's own frame

- **Vaiśampāyana recites the epic** — During (or just after) the snake-sacrifice, **Vyāsa** arrives at Janamejaya's court; at his bidding his disciple **Vaiśampāyana** narrates the entire *Mahābhārata* — the history of Janamejaya's own ancestors — to the king as the rite proceeds; the recitation is in turn re-told by **Ugraśravas Sauti** at the **Naimiṣa forest**, giving the epic its three-layer frame (Mahābhārata, Ādi 1; Pauṣya/Āstīka/Aṃśāvataraṇa Parvas). Full frame-analysis: [Āstīka deep-dive](../04-deep-dives/astika-sarpa-satra.md) and [Ādi Parva — the Frame & 19 sub-parvas](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md#the-frame--the-19-sub-parvas).

## Aftermath & afterlives

- **The horse-sacrifice expiation** — Some traditions hold Janamejaya later performs an **Aśvamedha** and incurs/expiates brahminicide-adjacent guilt for the mass slaughter of nāgas; he is counselled by sages on the violence of the rite (Mahābhārata, late strata; Purāṇic notices). [disputed]
- **Bhāgavata compression** — The *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* mentions the sarpa-satra only briefly (Skandha XII), its narrative weight thrown instead onto Parīkṣit's seven-day liberation by Śuka; the snake-sacrifice serves there mainly to date the recitation and confirm the Kali-Yuga setting (*Bhāgavata* XII.6).
- **Historical "two-clock" note** — Takṣaśilā (Taxila) is a real historical city; later tradition and some modern writers tried to anchor Janamejaya in post-Bhārata-war regnal lists (the Purāṇic Paurava king-roll continuing to the Nandas). The repo keeps the **scriptural clock** (late-Dvāpara/early-Kali Vaivasvata Manvantara) distinct from any **historical clock** placement of a Kuru king at Taxila. [scholarly]
