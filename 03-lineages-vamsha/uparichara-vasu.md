# Uparichara Vasu — The Sky-Faring Cedi King

> *Up to: [Lineages (Vaṃśa)](./README.md) · a Pauravā king of the [Candravaṃśa](./suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) and the genealogical hinge behind the [Mahābhārata](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md) Kuru line.*

**Vasu Uparicara** (Vasu "the Upward-Mover / Sky-Goer"), king of **Cedi**, sits in the Lunar (Candra) dynasty's **Pūru/Pauravā** branch. He is a small but structurally crucial node: through the curious circumstances of his fallen seed he begets **Satyavatī**, and Satyavatī — apparently a lowborn fisher-maiden — is in fact royal Lunar blood. She becomes the mother of **Vyāsa** and the grandmother-by-marriage of the whole Kuru tragedy. Vasu is thus the *upstream* ancestor who quietly re-legitimises the entire Kaurava-Pāṇḍava genealogy, and the institutor of the **Indra-dhvaja** royal festival.

---

## 1. Who he is — placement in the Lunar tree

- **A Pauravā king of Cedi** — Vasu is a descendant of **Pūru** (eldest line of Yayāti's youngest son), seated on the throne of **Cedi** (the Cedi country, roughly Bundelkhand). He is sometimes counted in the line leading toward the **Kuru** kings, with his own sons founding regional dynasties. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva, Sambhava-parvan ~63; *Harivaṃśa*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV)
- **The name "Uparicara."** *Upari-cara* = "moving above / going aloft." He earns the epithet from the boon that lets him travel through the sky in Indra's crystal car — he literally over-passes the earth. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63)
- **Cedi–Magadha continuity.** In the Purāṇic genealogies Vasu's sons seed several eastern kingdoms; his son **Bṛhadratha** is made the founder of the **Magadha** royal house and father (in the next generations) of **Jarāsandha**, while another son holds Cedi. So the same Cedi root that produces Satyavatī also produces the Bārhadratha line of Magadha. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63; *Harivaṃśa*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.19) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (king-order):* the exact roster and ordering of Vasu's five named sons (commonly **Bṛhadratha** of Magadha, **Pratyagraha**, **Kuśāmba/Maṇivāhana**, **Mavella/Macchilla**, and **Yadu** of the Cedis) differs between the *Mahābhārata* and the Purāṇas; never harmonise the lists. [disputed]

## 2. Tapas, retirement, and Indra's boons

- **The renunciation that Indra interrupts.** Vasu, a devout king fond of hunting, withdraws to practise severe **tapas** with the aim of higher worlds. The gods, led by **Indra**, fear he will out-austere them and lure him back to active kingship with gifts — the classic Purāṇic move of co-opting a too-powerful ascetic. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63)
- **The crystal aerial car (*ākāśa-/sphaṭika vimāna*).** Indra grants Vasu a wondrous **crystal flying chariot** (a *vimāna*) in which he alone among mortals coursed the sky — the source of his title **Uparicara**. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63)
- **The Vaijayantī garland.** Indra gives him an unfading **Vaijayantī** garland of lotuses (the "garland of victory"), which protected its wearer in battle and never withered. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63)
- **The bamboo staff (veṇu-yaṣṭi).** Indra also gifts a bamboo **staff/pole** for the protection of the righteous, which Vasu, grateful, plants in the earth at year's end to honour Indra — the seed of the festival below. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63)
- **Two clocks note.** All of this sits on the *scriptural* clock in the late Dvāpara of the 28th Mahāyuga, generations before the Bhārata war. There is no historical-clock anchor for Vasu himself; the Indra-dhvaja festival he founds, however, is attested as a real **historical** royal rite (below), so the figure is a charter-ancestor for a living institution. [scholarly]

## 3. The Indra-dhvaja / Indra-maha festival

- **Institution of the Indra-maha.** Out of gratitude for the boons, Vasu **inaugurates the annual festival of Indra** — the raising and worship of **Indra's banner-pole (Indra-dhvaja / Indra-ketu)** at the close of the year, with the bamboo staff decked and erected, then ceremonially lowered into the water. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63)
- **Bequeathed to all kings.** The epic frames Vasu as the *first* royal celebrant, after whom **other kings adopt the rite** — so the Mahābhārata uses Vasu as the mythic charter for the **Indra-dhvaja festival** practised by historical Indian monarchs (described in the *Bṛhat-saṃhitā* of Varāhamihira and in Kauṭilya-era civic ritual). [scholarly]
- **Promised prosperity.** Indra declares that kings who keep the festival will have flourishing, victorious realms; those who neglect it suffer. The story thus does double duty as dynastic legend and ritual prescription. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63)

## 4. The Girikā / Adrikā episode — birth of Satyavatī and Matsya

This is the node that makes Vasu genealogically load-bearing. The thread is consistently told in the Ādi Parva; catalogue the names carefully because the wife and the apsarā are easily confused.

- **Queen Girikā and the hunt.** Vasu's wife is **Girikā**. On the day she is ready to conceive (after her ritual bath), Girikā sends word, but Vasu's ancestors require him to go hunt for the **śrāddha** (ancestral rite). Torn, he goes to the forest. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63)
- **The fallen seed.** In the forest, thinking of the beautiful Girikā, Vasu's seed is involuntarily discharged. Unwilling to waste seed meant for his fertile queen, he catches it in a leaf and dispatches it to Girikā by a swift **falcon/hawk**, instructing the bird to carry it to the palace. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63)
- **The fish in the Yamunā.** A second hawk, mistaking the parcel for food, attacks the carrier; in the aerial fight the seed drops into the **Yamunā**. There it is swallowed by **Adrikā** — an **apsarā cursed by a brāhmaṇa to live as a fish** in the river. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63)
  - *Variant (name):* some recensions and retellings call the fish-apsarā **Adrikā**, others conflate her name with the queen's; the queen is **Girikā**, the fish is **Adrikā**. Keep them distinct. [disputed]
- **The twins from the fish.** In the tenth month the fish is caught by fishermen; cutting it open they find **a human boy and girl** in its belly. The fish, freed of the curse by bearing them, regains her apsarā form and returns to heaven. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63)
- **The boy — Matsya.** The male twin is given to Vasu, who keeps him; he becomes the virtuous, righteous king **Matsya**, ancestor of the **Matsya** royal line (and, in the tradition, tied to the Cedi/Matsya kṣatriya stock — the country whose king Virāṭa later shelters the Pāṇḍavas). (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63)
- **The girl — Satyavatī / Matsyagandhā.** The female twin, smelling of fish, is given by Vasu to the **fisherman-chief (dāśarāja)** to raise. She grows up as **Satyavatī**, nicknamed **Matsyagandhā** ("fish-scented") and **Kālī**; later, after **Parāśara's** boon, she becomes **Yojanagandhā** ("fragrant for a league") and **Gandhavatī**. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63) [folk: the fishy-to-fragrant transformation is the favourite detail of regional retellings]

## 5. Why Vasu is the genealogical hinge

- **Satyavatī is secretly royal.** Because she is **Vasu's own daughter** by his seed, the "fisher-maiden" Satyavatī carries **Pauravā Lunar blood** — the narrative makes her marriage to **Śāntanu** of the Kurus a *re-union* of the dynasty with itself, not a misalliance. The lowborn appearance is a disguise the Ādi Parva deliberately lifts. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63; cf. Ādi 100) [scholarly]
- **The downstream chain.** Satyavatī's line carries the whole epic:
  - **Satyavatī + Parāśara → Vyāsa** (premarital, the author-sage). (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63, 100)
  - **Satyavatī + Śāntanu → Citrāṅgada and Vicitravīrya.** (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 101)
  - **Vyāsa's niyoga → Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Pāṇḍu, Vidura** — and thence the **Kauravas and Pāṇḍavas**. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 105–106)
- **Great-grandfather of the tragedy.** Tracing upward, Vasu Uparicara stands behind the Kuru catastrophe as the originating ancestor of Satyavatī → Vyāsa → the disputed succession. He is the quiet root of the legitimacy that **Bhīṣma's vow** is sworn to protect. See the [Ādi Parva deep-dive](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md) for the Śāntanu–Satyavatī–Bhīṣma sequence, and the [Harivaṃśa](../04-deep-dives/harivamsha.md) for the parallel Lunar-line framing.
- **The Cedi/Magadha mirror.** Through his son **Bṛhadratha**, Vasu is equally the ancestral root of **Jarāsandha** of Magadha — so one Cedi king fathers *both* the maternal ancestry of the Pāṇḍavas (via Satyavatī) and the great antagonist dynasty of Magadha. The tradition treats this symmetry as deliberate. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi 63; Sabhā Parva for Jarāsandha) [scholarly]

## Sources

- **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva**, Sambhava-parvan / Ādivaṃśāvataraṇa (esp. adhyāya ~63): Cedirāja Vasu Uparicara, Indra's boons and the crystal car, the Indra-dhvaja festival, queen Girikā, the apsarā-fish Adrikā, and the twin birth of Matsya and Satyavatī; (adhyāya ~100–106) the Śāntanu–Satyavatī marriage, Bhīṣma's vow, and the niyoga of Vyāsa. (Critical Edition; cf. Ganguli tr.)
- **Harivaṃśa** (khila of the Mahābhārata): Lunar-dynasty genealogy, Vasu's sons and the Cedi/Magadha (Bārhadratha) branch.
- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** IV (esp. IV.19): Pauravā/Cedi genealogy, Vasu's descendants, the Magadha line.
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** IX: Candravaṃśa genealogies (Pūru line) framing Vasu's placement.
- **Varāhamihira, Bṛhat-saṃhitā** (ch. on Indra-dhvaja): the historical royal festival for which the Mahābhārata's Vasu episode is the mythic charter. [scholarly, comparative]
