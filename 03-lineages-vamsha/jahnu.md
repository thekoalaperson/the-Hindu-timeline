# Jahnu — Sage-King and Eponym of Jāhnavī (Gaṅgā)

> *Up to: [Lineages (Vaṃśa)](./README.md) · a Pauravā sage-king of the [Candravaṃśa](./suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md), listed in some rosters as a son of [Kuru](./kuru-the-eponym.md), whose swallowing-and-releasing of the descending river fixes one of [Gaṅgā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganga.md)'s great names — **Jāhnavī**, "daughter of Jahnu."*

**Jahnu** is a small but load-bearing node in the Lunar genealogy: he is at once a **king**, a **ṛṣi** mid-sacrifice, and an **eponym**. His single famous deed — drinking up the whole of **Gaṅgā** when her flood drowns his rite, then releasing her so she counts as his daughter — is the etiology behind the river's epithet **Jāhnavī**, one of her most-used names in stotra and epic alike. Genealogically he is a junction: the standard Pauravā throne-line passes *near* him, and the **Bhāgavata** routes the **Kuśika** branch (→ Gādhi → Viśvāmitra) *through* him, making Jahnu the shared root of two clans that the other Purāṇas keep separate.

---

## 1. Placement in the Lunar (Pauravā) genealogical spine

- **Listed as a son of Kuru (one roster).** Several Purāṇic recensions name Jahnu among the sons of **Kuru** — commonly **Sudhanvan, Jahnu, Pariṣadaśva (Parīkṣit the elder), and Pravara/Arava** — with the throne-line continuing through one brother down to **Pratīpa → Śāntanu**. The son-lists and spellings vary; the rosters are never to be harmonised. See the variant note in [Kuru the eponym](./kuru-the-eponym.md). (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.19; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.22) [disputed]
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata IX.15 placement):* the **Bhāgavata** treats Jahnu not merely as a leaf under Kuru but as a *channel* — the **Kuśika/Kānyakubja** line is threaded **through Jahnu's descendants** down to **Gādhi**, so that Jahnu becomes the genealogical hinge linking the Kuru side and the Kuśika side. See [Gādhi and the caru-swap](./gadhi-and-the-caru-swap.md). [disputed: differs from Viṣṇu Purāṇa's branch placement]
  - *Variant (Ajamīḍha-stem rosters):* some Purāṇic strands attach Jahnu to the **Ajamīḍha** stem of the Pauravā tree rather than directly to Kuru's sons, reflecting the general instability of the kings between Hastin and Śāntanu. [disputed]
- **A Pauravā, not a Solar king.** Across all the variants Jahnu sits firmly in the **Candravaṃśa** (Lunar line) of **Pūru → Pauravā → Bhārata**, never in the Sūryavaṃśa; the Ikṣvāku Bhagīratha who *leads* Gaṅgā down is Solar, so the eponym-episode is one of the rare places where a Solar king's quest crosses a Lunar sage's ground. See [Sūrya- & Candra-vaṃśa](./suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md). (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.19) [scholarly]
- **King and ṛṣi at once.** Tradition holds Jahnu as a **rājarṣi** — a kṣatriya king who is also a sage performing yajña — which is exactly what the eponym-story requires: he must be powerful enough to swallow a river and sanctified enough to be mid-sacrifice when she floods him. (*Rāmāyaṇa* Bāla Kāṇḍa 44)

## 2. The eponym episode — Gaṅgā swallowed and released

This is Jahnu's defining deed, told most fully in the **Bāla Kāṇḍa** of Vālmīki's *Rāmāyaṇa* as part of the great **Gaṅgāvataraṇa** (descent of the Ganges) that **Bhagīratha** secures for his ancestors.

- **The descent reaches Jahnu's rite.** As **Bhagīratha** leads the newly-descended **Gaṅgā** across the plains toward the ashes of **Sagara**'s sixty thousand sons, her torrent sweeps over the **sacrificial enclosure (yajña-vāṭa)** of the sage **Jahnu**, who is in the midst of a great sacrifice. (*Rāmāyaṇa* Bāla Kāṇḍa 44)
- **Jahnu drinks the river.** Enraged that the flood has drowned and scattered his rite, Jahnu — by his ascetic power — **drinks up the entire Gaṅgā in one draught**, and the river vanishes from the world. Bhagīratha's whole effort is undone in an instant. (*Rāmāyaṇa* Bāla Kāṇḍa 44)
- **The gods and Bhagīratha propitiate him.** The **devas** and ṛṣis (and Bhagīratha) approach the angry sage, praise him, and entreat him to give the river back, explaining that she is bound for the salvation of Sagara's sons; in some tellings they ask him to **accept Gaṅgā as his own daughter** so that releasing her costs him no honour. (*Rāmāyaṇa* Bāla Kāṇḍa 44)
- **The release — and the name Jāhnavī.** Mollified, Jahnu lets the river out again, and **she is thereafter called Jāhnavī** — "**daughter of Jahnu**" — because she is, as it were, reborn from his body. The flow rejoins Bhagīratha's path and continues to the sea. (*Rāmāyaṇa* Bāla Kāṇḍa 44) See [Gaṅgā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganga.md).
  - *Variant (released from the ear):* the most widespread version has Jahnu release Gaṅgā **through his ear (karṇa)** — hence the river is the sage's "ear-born" daughter. [scholarly: dominant reading]
  - *Variant (released from the thigh/side):* other strands have him let her out by **splitting his thigh** or his side, paralleling other "born-from-the-body" motifs in the lineage literature. [regional]
  - *Variant (a slit, not the ear):* still other retellings say he simply **cut open a passage** and let the waters flow, without specifying ear or thigh. [folk]
- **Why the epithet matters.** **Jāhnavī** is among Gaṅgā's commonest names in the *Mahābhārata*, the Purāṇas, and devotional hymnody — used, for instance, of the river-goddess who is Bhīṣma's mother. The eponym-story is the charter that lets a single sage's name ride permanently on India's holiest river. (*Mahābhārata*, Ādi & Anuśāsana Parvans, passim) [scholarly]

## 3. Significance — junction, not just eponym

- **A genealogical hinge.** Jahnu's chief structural role is as a **branch-junction**: the *Bhāgavata* routes the **Kuśika** kings (Gādhi → Viśvāmitra; and via Ṛcīka the Bhārgava graft) back through Jahnu, so that the warrior-sage **Viśvāmitra** and the Kuru kings share a Jahnu-ancestor. Other Purāṇas keep the Kuśika branch on the **Amāvasu** side and do *not* pass it through Jahnu — a genuine cross-tradition disagreement, not a copyist's slip. See [Gādhi and the caru-swap](./gadhi-and-the-caru-swap.md). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.15 vs *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.7) [disputed]
- **The river-as-daughter motif.** By being "swallowed and reborn," Gaṅgā joins the set of rivers and goddesses who acquire **patronymic kinship** with a sage (cf. her also being **Bhāgīrathī** after Bhagīratha and **Viṣṇupadī** from Viṣṇu's foot). Jahnu supplies the *kṣatriya-ṛṣi* link in that chain. (*Rāmāyaṇa* Bāla Kāṇḍa 44; *Bhāgavata* IX.9) [scholarly]
- **Two clocks.** On the **scriptural clock** Jahnu belongs to the deep Pauravā past of the present (7th, Vaivasvata) Manvantara, generations above the *Mahābhārata* war and contemporary with the Ikṣvāku Bhagīratha. On the **historical clock** he leaves no independent residue: "Jahnu" survives chiefly as a *linguistic* fossil inside the living river-name **Jāhnavī** — the eponym outlasting the man. The two clocks should not be collapsed. [scholarly]
- **A name carried by descendants.** Some genealogies give Jahnu a son and successors (e.g. **Sunaha/Sumantu → Ajaka → Balākāśva → Kuśa**), tying the river-eponym forward into the Kuśika kings — which is exactly why the *Bhāgavata* can use him as the bridge to Gādhi. The successor-names vary by recension. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.15) [disputed]

## Sources

- **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa 44** (Gaṅgāvataraṇa): the eponym episode — Gaṅgā, led by Bhagīratha, floods Jahnu's sacrifice; the sage drinks the whole river, is propitiated by the gods, and releases her (from the ear / thigh in variant readings), after which she is called **Jāhnavī**, "daughter of Jahnu."
- **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva** (Sambhava-parvan / Ādivaṃśāvataraṇa): the Pauravā–Bhārata genealogy in which Jahnu is placed among the Lunar kings; the epithet **Jāhnavī** for Gaṅgā recurs through the Ādi and Anuśāsana Parvans. (Critical Edition; cf. Ganguli tr.)
- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** IV.19: Pauravā/Kuru genealogy listing Jahnu among Kuru's line, with king-order variants; IV.7 places the Kuśika branch on the Amāvasu side (contrast with the Bhāgavata).
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (Śrīmad Bhāgavatam) IX.15: threads the **Kuśika/Gādhi** line through **Jahnu**, making him the junction linking the Kuru and Kuśika branches; IX.22 frames the Pūru-line placement.
- River-epithet context: **Jāhnavī**, **Bhāgīrathī**, **Viṣṇupadī** as patronymic names of Gaṅgā across epic and Purāṇic hymnody. [scholarly]
