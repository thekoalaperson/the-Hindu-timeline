# Devāpi: The Renounced Prince, Rain-Bringing Purohita, and Immortal Restorer of the Lunar Dynasty

> *Up to: [Lineages (Vaṃśa)](./README.md) · the eldest son of Pratīpa and elder brother of [Śāntanu](./shantanu.md) in the Pauravā/[Candravaṃśa](./suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) line — a single figure read three ways: disqualified heir, Vedic rain-priest, and deathless restorer who outlives all of [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).*

**Devāpi** (Devāpi Ārṣṭiṣeṇa, "son/descendant of Ṛṣṭiṣeṇa") is one of the few names in the tradition that the *Ṛgveda*, the *Mahābhārata*, and the Purāṇas all carry — and they carry him in startlingly different keys. He is at once the **eldest son of King Pratīpa** of the Kuru line, born to rule and barred from ruling; the **purohita who breaks a twelve-year drought** in *Ṛgveda* X.98; and the **yogic immortal** who waits out the whole of Kali Yuga at Kalāpa-grāma to re-seed the kṣatriya dynasties at the dawn of the next Kṛta. His disqualification is the quiet hinge that hands Hastināpura to his younger brother [Śāntanu](./shantanu.md) and so sets up the entire Kuru succession crisis — see [Kuru, the eponym](./kuru-the-eponym.md). This file gathers all three Devāpis as one coherent figure and keeps the **scriptural** and **historical** clocks distinct.

---

## 1. The renounced heir — disqualification by skin-affliction

- **Eldest of Pratīpa's three sons.** Devāpi is the firstborn of **Pratīpa** (Kuru-line); his brothers are **Bāhlīka** (who departs to rule the Bāhlīka kingdom) and the youngest, **Śāntanu**. By primogeniture the Hastināpura throne is Devāpi's. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.20; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.22)
- **Beloved of the people.** The Purāṇas stress that Devāpi was dear to the citizens — virtuous, devoted to dharma — so his loss to the throne is felt as a wrong, not a relief. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.20; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.22)
- **The skin-affliction bars consecration.** As his consecration (rājyābhiṣeka) nears, Devāpi is found to suffer a **skin-disease (tvag-doṣa / kuṣṭha, "leprosy" or skin-blemish)**. Brāhmaṇas rule that a king with a bodily defect cannot be anointed, and the elders/people refuse him. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.20; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.22) [the Purāṇas make the blemish the disqualifier; cf. §3]
- **Withdrawal to the forest.** Renouncing his claim, Devāpi **retires to the forest as an ascetic (tapasvin / muni)** — in some tellings still living there as a renowned king-turned-sage. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.20; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.22)
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata emphasis):* the epic mainly notes the throne *passing* to Śāntanu past the elder brother and dwells less on the disease, sometimes framing the withdrawal as Devāpi's own ascetic choice. (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva) [disputed]
- **The throne falls to Śāntanu.** With Devāpi disqualified and Bāhlīka settled abroad, **Śāntanu is consecrated** at Hastināpura — the act that seeds the later Kuru succession crisis (Bhīṣma's vow, the Satyavatī match, the disputed line down to the Bhārata war). See [Śāntanu](./shantanu.md). (*Mahābhārata* Ādi Parva; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.20)

## 2. The rain-bringing purohita — Ṛgveda X.98 and the drought

- **The Vedic Devāpi.** In the oldest layer, **Devāpi Ārṣṭiṣeṇa** is the **purohita (royal chaplain)** of king **Śaṃtanu (Śantanu)** — the seer to whom *Ṛgveda* X.98, the **Devāpi–Śaṃtanu rain hymn**, is ascribed. (*Ṛgveda* X.98; *Nirukta* II.10) [scholarly]
- **A twelve-year drought.** Yāska's etymological commentary preserves the backstory: **Śaṃtanu's kingdom suffers a twelve-year drought** because rain has been withheld from it. (*Nirukta* II.10; *Bṛhaddevatā*) [scholarly]
- **The dharmic cause — usurpation over an elder.** The traditional gloss links the two Devāpis: because **Śaṃtanu took the throne over his rightful elder brother Devāpi**, the gods (or the offended dharma of seniority) **withheld the rain**; the wrong must be set right through Devāpi himself. (*Nirukta* II.10; *Bṛhaddevatā*) [this is the bridge between the priest and the disqualified heir]
- **Devāpi invokes Bṛhaspati and the rain.** Made **purohita** for his younger brother's sacrifice, Devāpi propitiates **Bṛhaspati** (lord of prayer/Parjanya) and, in the words of *Ṛgveda* X.98, prays for and **obtains rain (vṛṣṭi)** for Śaṃtanu — ending the drought. (*Ṛgveda* X.98; *Nirukta* II.10) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (the cause of the drought):* in some retellings the elder's renunciation is voluntary and pious, yet the *omission of the elder* from kingship still ritually "offends," so the priestly act of Devāpi serving the younger restores cosmic order rather than redressing a crime. (*Nirukta* II.10; *Bṛhaddevatā*) [disputed]
- **Reconciling Vedic and Purāṇic portraits.** The **Vedic Devāpi** is a *seer-priest* who actively serves Śaṃtanu; the **Purāṇic Devāpi** is a *disqualified leper-ascetic* who withdraws. Later harmonizing tradition fuses them: the skin-disease (or renunciation) explains *why* the elder yields the throne, and the rain-hymn explains *how* the resulting imbalance is healed — one man, two roles. Caution: the **Vedic and epic/Purāṇic Devāpi need not be flatly equated**; the name is genuinely early-Iron-Age Kuru material the later tradition has woven into dynastic legend. (*Ṛgveda* X.98; *Nirukta* II.10; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.20) [scholarly] [comparative]

## 3. The deathless restorer — Kalāpa-grāma and the close of Kali Yuga

- **Devāpi does not die.** The Purāṇas extend his story past all ordinary chronology: Devāpi **never abandons the body** but enters deep **yogic suspension (yoga / samādhi)** and survives, in concealment, through the whole of Kali Yuga. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.24; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* XII.2.37–38)
- **The hidden village Kalāpa.** He abides at the village **Kalāpa(-grāma)** (Kalāpagrāma), the legendary refuge where the immortals wait, sustained by yoga across the yugas. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.24; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* XII.2.37–38)
- **Re-establisher of the lunar (kṣatriya) line.** At the **end of Kali Yuga**, when the kṣatriya order has all but vanished, Devāpi **re-emerges to re-found the Candravaṃśa (lunar/Paurava kṣatriya dynasty)** and re-establish proper dharma for the coming age. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.24; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* XII.2.37–38) See [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).
- **Maru, the solar-line counterpart.** Devāpi is paired with **Maru (Moru)** of the **Ikṣvāku/solar line (Sūryavaṃśa)** — a descendant of Rāma's line — who likewise survives by yoga at Kalāpa and **re-establishes the kṣatriya solar dynasty** at Kali's close. The two immortals together reseed *both* royal lines at the dawn of the next **Kṛta/Satya Yuga**. See [Sūrya- & Candra-vaṃśa](./suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md). (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.24; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.12, XII.2.37–38)
  - *Variant (timing vs. Kalki):* sources differ on whether this restoration follows **Kalki's** destruction of adharma at Kali's end and inaugurates the new Kṛta, or runs parallel to it; the Purāṇas place the Devāpi/Maru re-foundation at the **transition into the next Kṛta**. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.24; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* XII.2) [disputed]

## 4. Two clocks

- **Scriptural clock.** Within the 28th Mahāyuga, the *prince* Devāpi sits in the late **Dvāpara-yuga**, one generation above [Śāntanu](./shantanu.md) and the war's principal cast; the *immortal* Devāpi then **spans the entirety of Kali Yuga** in suspension and acts at the **Kali→Kṛta junction** — making him a rare cross-yuga figure who personally bridges Dvāpara, all of Kali, and the next Satya. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.20, IV.24; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.22, XII.2) [scriptural]
- **Historical clock.** The name **Devāpi Ārṣṭiṣeṇa**, with his brother **Śaṃtanu**, is authentically **Vedic** — *Ṛgveda* X.98 and *Nirukta* II.10 attest an early Kuru rain-priest legend that long predates the epic-Purāṇic dynastic frame. The Vedic seer and the Purāṇic deathless prince are best read as the **same name carried across very different textual horizons**, not a single datable person. (*Ṛgveda* X.98; *Nirukta* II.10) [scholarly] [comparative]

## Sources

- **Ṛgveda** X.98 — the **Devāpi–Śaṃtanu** hymn; Devāpi Ārṣṭiṣeṇa as purohita praying to Bṛhaspati for rain on Śaṃtanu's drought-struck kingdom. [scholarly]
- **Yāska, Nirukta** II.10 — the explanatory backstory: the **twelve-year drought**, the wrong of the younger Śaṃtanu reigning over the elder Devāpi, and Devāpi's priestly remedy. [scholarly]
- **Bṛhaddevatā** — corroborating account of the Devāpi–Śaṃtanu drought and rain legend. [scholarly]
- **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva** (Saṃbhava-parvan, ~94–97 critical-edition numbering) — Pratīpa's three sons; Devāpi the eldest barred/renouncing, Bāhlīka abroad, the throne descending to Śāntanu.
- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** IV.20 — Devāpi's skin-affliction and forest withdrawal; the Pratīpa → Śāntanu succession. **IV.24** — Devāpi and **Maru** surviving by yoga at **Kalāpa** to re-establish the kṣatriya (lunar and solar) lines at the end of Kali.
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (Śrīmad Bhāgavatam) IX.22 — the Candravaṃśa framing of Pratīpa's sons and Devāpi's disqualification. **XII.2.37–38** — the two immortals, Devāpi and Maru, re-founding the dynasties at Kali's close. **IX.12** — Maru in the Ikṣvāku/solar genealogy.
- **Matsya / Vāyu Purāṇa** (lineage chapters) — parallel Paurava genealogies listing Pratīpa's sons and the Devāpi tradition.
