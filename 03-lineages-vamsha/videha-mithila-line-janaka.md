# The Videha Line of Mithilā — Nimi to Janaka the Philosopher-King

> *Hierarchy: [`03-lineages-vamsha/`](README.md) — the **Videha (Maithila) cadet branch** of the **Sūryavaṃśa** (Solar dynasty; see the [Sūryavaṃśa–Candravaṃśa overview](suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md)), sprung from Ikṣvāku's son **Nimi**. The one royal house tradition remembers not for conquest but for **ātma-vidyā** — a dynasty of self-knowers whose most famous title-holder, **Janaka**, is the canon's archetypal philosopher-king. This file is both the lineage record and the **Janaka character hub**. Scriptural clock: from the dawn of the Vaivasvata line (Satya Yuga) through Sīradhvaja Janaka in the Tretā. Academic clock: the historical Videha of the Upaniṣadic Janaka is c. 8th–6th century BCE — see §7. [scholarly]*

---

## 1. Nimi and Vasiṣṭha — the mutual curse that made a bodiless dynasty

- **Nimi's sacrifice and the scheduling quarrel.** **Nimi**, son of **Ikṣvāku**, undertakes a long sacrificial session and engages the family priest **Vasiṣṭha** to officiate; Vasiṣṭha, already retained by **Indra** for a rite, tells the king to wait. Nimi, reflecting that life is fleeting, says nothing and begins the sacrifice with other officiants (**Gautama** among them). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.13.1–5; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.5)
- **The curse and the counter-curse.** Returning to find himself supplanted, Vasiṣṭha curses Nimi's **body to fall** ("let the body of this Nimi, who thinks himself learned, drop away"); the dying king, holding the curse unjust, **curses his guru back** — and Vasiṣṭha's body falls too. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.13.4–6; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.5) — companion file on the priest: [Viśvāmitra–Vasiṣṭha](../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md).
  - *Variant (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara-kāṇḍa ~sargas 55–57):* the epic also carries the Nimi–Vasiṣṭha mutual curse as a told tale, with the same double bodilessness.
- **Vasiṣṭha reborn of Mitra-Varuṇa.** The bodiless Vasiṣṭha enters the seed of **Mitra-Varuṇa** (spilled at the sight of the apsaras **Urvaśī**) and is born again with a new body — the kumbha-birth that also underwrites his "Maitrāvaruṇi" patronymic. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.13.6; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.5)
- **Nimi refuses re-embodiment.** Nimi's corpse is preserved in fragrant unguents for the duration of the sattra; at its close the pleased gods offer to restore him to his body. **He declines** — the body is nothing but bondage and misery, and the sage-kings fear the separation it entails — asking instead to dwell where beings themselves dwell. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.13.8–11; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.5)
- **Nimi in the blink of every eye.** The gods grant that Nimi live **in the eyes of all creatures** — whence the **blink** (*nimeṣa*, the opening and closing of the eyelids) is his residence and his memorial etymology. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.13.12; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.5)

## 2. Mithi the churned son — the three etymologies of the house

- **The churning of the corpse.** Fearing anarchy (*arājaka*), the ṛṣis **churn** (*mathana*) Nimi's preserved body — and a son emerges. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.13.13; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.5)
- **One birth, three names, one memorial verse.** The Bhāgavata packs the dynasty's whole nomenclature into a single verse: **Janaka** because of his extraordinary birth (*janmanā janakaḥ*), **Vaideha** because born of the bodiless one (*videhajaḥ*), **Mithila** because born of churning (*mithilo mathanāj jātaḥ*) — "and the city he built is **Mithilā**." (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.13.13)
- **"Videha" cuts two ways.** The kingdom-name **Videha** ("the bodiless") and the royal style **Vaideha** carry, for the tradition, a deliberate doctrinal resonance: the line founded by a king who *refused a body* culminates in kings famed for knowing the Self beyond the body — and lends its very name to **videha-mukti**, "disembodied liberation" (§6).
- **Geography of the realm.** Videha-Mithilā straddles the Gangetic north-east (modern north Bihar and Nepal's Terai); its surviving sacred capital-town is treated at [Janakpur / Mithilā](../06-sacred-geography/janakpur-mithila-sita-janmabhumi.md).

## 3. The Mithilā king-roll — and the many-Janakas problem

- **Janaka recites his own vaṃśa.** At the wedding of his daughters, Sīradhvaja Janaka recites the full line to Vasiṣṭha and Daśaratha: **Nimi → Mithi → Janaka (the first) → Udāvasu → Nandivardhana → Suketu → Devarāta → Bṛhadratha → Mahāvīra → Sudhṛti → Dhṛṣṭaketu → Haryaśva → Maru → Pratīndhaka → Kīrtiratha → Devamīḍha → Vibudha → Mahīdhraka → Kīrtirāta → Mahāromā → Svarṇaromā → Hrasvaromā → Sīradhvaja** (with his younger brother **Kuśadhvaja**). (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Bāla-kāṇḍa sarga 71)
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata IX.13; Viṣṇu Purāṇa IV.5):* the Purāṇic rolls run the same trunk with spelling and ordering wobble, and continue **past** Sīradhvaja (see §6) — treat the middle of the list as an open/variant roster. [disputed]
- **Devarāta, keeper of Śiva's bow.** The colossal bow of **Śiva** (the Pināka) is deposited by the gods **as a trust (nyāsa) with king Devarāta**, sixth in the roll — the heirloom on which the whole Rāmāyaṇa marriage-plot will turn. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Bāla-kāṇḍa sarga 66)
- **"Janaka" is a throne-title, not one man.** Tradition itself distinguishes several bearers, and the corpus keeps them apart:
  1. **Janaka the first** — Mithi's son, the eponym. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Bāla 71)
  2. **Sīradhvaja Janaka** — Sītā's father, the Rāmāyaṇa king (§4).
  3. **Janaka Vaideha of the śruti** — patron and pupil of **Yājñavalkya** in the *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* and *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* (§5).
  4. **Janaka Janadeva** — pupil of the Sāṃkhya teacher **Pañcaśikha**. (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva 211–212 [Ganguli])
  5. **Janaka Dharmadhvaja** — the king tested by the yoginī **Sulabhā**. (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva 308 [Ganguli])
  6. **Mahājanaka** — the shipwrecked-and-renouncing Janaka of the Buddhist *Mahājanaka Jātaka* (no. 539). [Buddhist tradition]
  - *Harmonizing view (traditional):* all Maithila kings bear "Janaka" dynastically, so the dialogues attach to different generations of one office.
  - *Scholarly view:* the Upaniṣadic Janaka is a plausibly **historical king of c. 8th–7th century BCE**, distinct from (and possibly the seed of) the epic figure; the epic-Purāṇic Janakas are literary multiples of his fame. [scholarly] [disputed]

## 4. Sīradhvaja Janaka and Sītā — the Tretā-yuga king

Full narrative home: [Bāla-Kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md) and [Sītā](../04-deep-dives/characters/sita.md); the living pilgrimage geography at [Janakpur](../06-sacred-geography/janakpur-mithila-sita-janmabhumi.md).

- **The furrow-child.** Ploughing the sacrificial ground, Janaka uncovers an infant girl in the **furrow (sītā)** and raises her as his daughter — *ayonijā*, "not womb-born," daughter of the Earth; his own name **Sīradhvaja** means "he of the plough-banner." (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Bāla-kāṇḍa sarga 66)
- **Vīrya-śulka and the siege of Mithilā.** Janaka vows Sītā to whoever can string Śiva's bow — "the bride-price of valour." The suitor-kings, failing and furious, **besiege Mithilā for a year**; Janaka propitiates the gods by tapas, receives a four-fold army, and routs them. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Bāla-kāṇḍa sarga 66)
- **The bow breaks; four weddings.** Rāma, brought by Viśvāmitra, bends and **snaps** the bow; Daśaratha is summoned, both genealogies are recited, and the two Ikṣvāku branches are knit fourfold: **Rāma–Sītā, Lakṣmaṇa–Ūrmilā** (Janaka's daughters), **Bharata–Māṇḍavī, Śatrughna–Śrutakīrti** (daughters of Kuśadhvaja). (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Bāla-kāṇḍa sargas 67–73) — see [Bharata, Śatrughna & their wives](../04-deep-dives/characters/bharata-shatrughna-and-their-wives.md).
- **Kuśadhvaja of Sāṅkāśyā.** When **Sudhanvan** of Sāṅkāśyā had besieged Mithilā demanding the bow and Sītā, Janaka slew him in battle and installed his brother **Kuśadhvaja** on Sāṅkāśyā's throne. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Bāla-kāṇḍa sarga 71)
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata IX.13.19–20):* the Bhāgavata makes Kuśadhvaja Sīradhvaja's **son**, not brother — an irreconcilable placement the corpus keeps side by side. [disputed]
- **Sītā's mother.** Vālmīki leaves Janaka's queen unnamed; later Maithilī and Rāmcaritmānas tradition calls her **Sunayanā**. [regional] [late text]

## 5. The philosopher-king — Janaka's dialogues

- **"People run off to Janaka!"** The fame of the Videha court is attested by a rival's complaint: **Ajātaśatru of Kāśī**, offered a teaching, sighs that everyone runs about crying "Janaka, Janaka!" (*Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 2.1.1)
- **The agnihotra test (śruti's first meeting).** Janaka of Videha, meeting three travelling brāhmaṇas — **Śvetaketu Āruṇeya, Somaśuṣma Sātyayajñi, and Yājñavalkya** — quizzes them on the agnihotra and drives off unsatisfied; Yājñavalkya pursues him and the exchange ends with the king granted the boon of **asking whatever question he wishes** — "thenceforth Janaka was a brahman," the text says of the kṣatriya. (*Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* 11.6.2)
- **The bahudakṣiṇa debate — a thousand gold-horned cows.** At Janaka's lavish sacrifice, **Yājñavalkya** claims the thousand-cow prize before debating and defeats eight challengers — among them **Gārgī Vācaknavī**, whose two-arrow questioning draws out the **akṣara** (imperishable) teaching. (*Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 3.1–3.9) — canonical homes: [Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad](../90-literature-corpus/upanishads/brihadaranyaka-upanishad.md) and [Gārgī & Maitreyī](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/gargi-and-maitreyi.md).
- **"What light does a person have?"** In private dialogue Janaka drives Yājñavalkya from sun, moon, fire and speech down to **the Self as one's own light** (*ātmajyotis*), through waking, dream and deep sleep, to the karma doctrine and the state beyond fear; Yājñavalkya tells him **"you have attained the fearless, Janaka"** (*abhayaṃ vai janaka prāpto 'si*). (*Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 4.1–4.4, esp. 4.2.4, 4.3.2–6)
- **The kingdom given away.** At the teaching's climax the king answers with the tradition's most famous royal renunciation-in-place: **"I give you the Videhas, and myself with them, into servitude."** (*Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 4.4)
- **Aṣṭāvakra at the gate.** A crooked-bodied boy of twelve out-riddles the doorkeeper and the king himself, then destroys the court debater **Bandin**, who had drowned his father — Janaka's court as the arena where the warped vessel proves the straight Self. (*Mahābhārata*, Vana Parva 132–134) — canonical home: [Aṣṭāvakra](../04-deep-dives/characters/ashtavakra.md).
- **Sulabhā's test — is the king really free?** The mendicant yoginī **Sulabhā** enters Janaka (Dharmadhvaja)'s mind by yoga-power to test his claim of liberation-on-the-throne; his indignant rebuke — she is a woman, an intruder — itself proves he still identifies with body, gender and office, and her reply is the tradition's strongest statement that **the ātman is not gendered**. (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva 308 [Ganguli]) — canonical home: [Mokṣadharma dialogues](../04-deep-dives/mokshadharma-dialogues.md).
- **Yājñavalkya and Pañcaśikha in the epic.** The Mokṣadharma stages two more Janaka curricula: Yājñavalkya expounds the **24/25/26 tattva-ladder** to Janaka (*Śānti Parva* 310–311 [Ganguli]), and **Pañcaśikha**, third in the Kapila–Āsuri line, teaches Janaka Janadeva an austere proto-Sāṃkhya (*Śānti Parva* 211–212, 218–219 [Ganguli]). — see [Mokṣadharma dialogues](../04-deep-dives/mokshadharma-dialogues.md).
- **Śuka sent to Mithilā.** Vyāsa sends his born-perfect son **Śuka** to Janaka — a *king* — as the final examiner of his liberation-knowledge; Janaka, testing him with delay and luxury, confirms that renunciation is a matter of knowledge, not costume. (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva ~325–326 [Ganguli])
- **"When Mithilā burns, nothing of mine burns."** The epic quotes an old gāthā **sung by Janaka of Videha**: "Unlimited is my wealth, yet I have nothing; if Mithilā burned, nothing of mine would burn" (*mithilāyāṃ pradīptāyāṃ na me dahyati kiñcana*) — the dynasty's motto of unattached kingship. (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva 17; echoed in the Mokṣadharma)
  - *Variant (Buddhist):* the *Mahājanaka Jātaka* (no. 539) puts the same burning-Mithilā verse in the mouth of the renouncing Bodhisatta-king Mahājanaka. [Buddhist tradition]

## 6. Karma-yoga's exemplar, videha-mukti, and the end of the line

- **Gītā 3.20 — perfection through action.** Kṛṣṇa's standing proof that engagement and realization coexist: "**By action alone Janaka and the others attained perfection**" (*karmaṇaiva hi saṃsiddhim āsthitā janakādayaḥ*) — cited for **loka-saṃgraha**, acting for the world's cohesion. (*Bhagavad Gītā* 3.20) — see the [Karma-ṣaṭka](../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita/03-to-06-karma-shatka.md).
- **Janaka and the siddhas' song.** The *Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha* makes Janaka its first full portrait of the **jīvanmukta king**: overhearing siddhas sing of the Self, he passes through despair to poised knowledge and goes on ruling. (*Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha* V, Upaśama) [late text] — see the [Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha](../90-literature-corpus/yoga-vasishtha.md) and its [tales catalog](../04-deep-dives/yoga-vasishtha-tales-catalog.md).
- **The Aṣṭāvakra Gītā frame.** The radical Advaita dialogue is framed as **Aṣṭāvakra teaching Janaka**, who awakens almost instantly — a medieval composition (academic datings range from c. 8th to 14th century CE) trading on the pair's epic fame. (*Aṣṭāvakra Gītā*) [late text] [scholarly]
- **Videha-mukti — the kingdom that named a doctrine.** Later Vedānta distinguishes **jīvanmukti** (liberation while living) from **videha-mukti** (liberation at/beyond the fall of the body); tradition relishes that the *Videha* kings — heirs of the bodiless Nimi, ruled by the deha-transcending Janaka — embody the pun. (Advaita Vedānta usage; *Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha*) — concept home: [karma–saṃsāra–mokṣa](../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md).
- **The line after Sīradhvaja.** The Bhāgavata continues: **Kuśadhvaja → Dharmadhvaja → Kṛtadhvaja and Mitadhvaja**; Kṛtadhvaja's son is **Keśidhvaja**, "master of ātma-vidyā," and Mitadhvaja's son **Khāṇḍikya**, "master of karma-path" — Keśidhvaja drives his cousin from the kingdom, then humbly teaches him the yoga that dissolves avidyā when Khāṇḍikya asks knowledge rather than revenge as his teacher's fee. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.13.19–21; the full Keśidhvaja–Khāṇḍikya yoga-dialogue at *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* VI.6–7)
- **"Self-knowers even in their homes."** The roll runs down to **Bahulāśva** and ends with **Kṛti**; the Bhāgavata closes the dynasty with its epitaph: these Maithila kings were **all adepts of ātma-vidyā, freed from the dualities even while living in their households** (*ātmavidyā-viśāradāḥ … dvandvair muktā gṛheṣv api*). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.13.20–27)
- **Karāla the Vaideha — the remembered end.** The *Arthaśāstra*'s roster of kings destroyed by vice includes **Karāla the Vaideha**, who "perished with his kinsmen" through lust — political memory of the Videha monarchy's fall. (*Arthaśāstra* 1.6) [scholarly]

## 7. Two-clocks note

- **Scriptural clock.** Nimi stands at the second generation from Vaivasvata Manu, at the dawn of the current Mahāyuga's Satya (Kṛta) Yuga; Sīradhvaja Janaka belongs to the **Tretā Yuga** of Rāma; the Mokṣadharma dialogues are recited in the Dvāpara-end frame of the epic; the harmonizers' single "Janaka" is impossible on this clock, which is the corpus's internal reason for keeping the title-holders distinct.
- **Academic clock.** The historical **Videha** emerges in late-Vedic literature: the *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa*'s foundation-legend of **Videgha Māthava**, led east across the **Sadānīrā** river by Agni Vaiśvānara, is read as mythic memory of the eastward extension of Vedic culture (see [Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa](../90-literature-corpus/vedas/brahmanas/shatapatha-brahmana.md), ŚB 1.4.1). The Janaka–Yājñavalkya stratum is dated c. **8th–6th century BCE**; by the Buddha's time the Videha monarchy had given way to the **Vajji (Vṛji) confederacy** centred on Vaiśālī; the *Rāmāyaṇa* and Mokṣadharma texts are later literary layers (c. 5th c. BCE – 3rd c. CE), the *Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha* and *Aṣṭāvakra Gītā* medieval. [scholarly]
