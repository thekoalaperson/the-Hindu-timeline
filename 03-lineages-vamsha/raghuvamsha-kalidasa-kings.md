# The Raghuvaṃśa Kings — Dilīpa, Raghu, Aja

> *Up to: [Lineages (Vaṃśa)](./README.md) · the three celebrated Kālidāsa episodes of the senior [Sūryavaṃśa](./suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) (§1b) — Dilīpa's cow-service, Raghu's world-conquest and Kautsa's gold-rain, and Aja's grief for Indumatī — the generations immediately above [Daśaratha and Rāma](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md).*

These three kings — **Dilīpa**, his son **Raghu**, and Raghu's son **Aja** (father of Daśaratha, hence Rāma's great-great-grandfather, grandfather, and father in line) — open Kālidāsa's dynastic mahākāvya the ***Raghuvaṃśa*** and supply its most beloved set-pieces: the lion-test of a king's devotion to his guru's cow, the conqueror who gives away the whole earth, and the lyric apex of Sanskrit elegy. The senior Solar line is renamed **Raghuvaṃśa** ("House of Raghu") after the middle of the three; **Rāma** is *Rāghava*, "descendant of Raghu." In the Purāṇas (Viṣṇu IV.4, Bhāgavata IX.9) these are bare names in the king-list — **Dilīpa → Raghu → Aja → Daśaratha**; almost all the narrative below is **Kālidāsa's literary expansion** (5th c. CE) draped over that skeleton. [scholarly]

---

## 1. Dilīpa and the cow Nandinī — the touchstone of guru-sevā

*Raghuvaṃśa* cantos **1–2**. The poem opens not with conquest but with a childless king earning a son by humble service. (The full canonical deep-dive lives at [../04-deep-dives/dilipa-nandini.md](../04-deep-dives/dilipa-nandini.md).)

- **The childless king's penance.** **Dilīpa** of Kosala and his queen **Sudakṣiṇā**, sonless because the king once slighted the celestial wish-cow **Surabhi** (Kāmadhenu), are set an expiation by the purohita **Vasiṣṭha** (see [Vasiṣṭha & Viśvāmitra](../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md)): to serve Surabhi's daughter **Nandinī**, the sage's own *kāmadhenu*, with total devotion. (*Raghuvaṃśa* 1.31–95)
- **The lion-test and the body-offering.** On the twenty-first day of the service a **lion** claiming to be Śiva's servant seizes Nandinī; his arm frozen to his quiver, Dilīpa offers **his own flesh** in the cow's place — whereupon the lion dissolves, an illusion conjured by Nandinī herself, and the cow grants the boon of a son. (*Raghuvaṃśa* 2.26–74)
- **The birth of Raghu.** Sudakṣiṇā conceives and bears **Raghu**, named for the future "swiftness" of his conquests — the son won purely by devotion, with which the *vaṃśa* properly begins. (*Raghuvaṃśa* 3.1–21)
- The **which-cow** variant (Nandinī as Surabhi's daughter vs. a mere epithet of Kāmadhenu) and the **which-Dilīpa** variant (Dilīpa I of the Bhagīratha line vs. this Dilīpa, Raghu's father) are catalogued with attribution in [Dilīpa & Nandinī](../04-deep-dives/dilipa-nandini.md). [disputed]

## 2. Raghu — digvijaya, the Viśvajit sacrifice, and the Kautsa gold-rain

*Raghuvaṃśa* cantos **3–5**. The king after whom the whole senior line is renamed. (A fuller deep-dive lives at [../04-deep-dives/raghu.md](../04-deep-dives/raghu.md).)

- **The boy who duels Indra.** When Dilīpa undertakes his hundredth **Aśvamedha** — which would make him Indra's peer — **Indra** carries off the consecrated horse; the prince Raghu pursues, fights Indra himself, and though worsted wins the god's admiration and a boon. (*Raghuvaṃśa* 3.38–69)
- **Accession.** Dilīpa anoints Raghu king of Kosala and retires to the forest in the *vānaprastha* manner of the Ikṣvākus. (*Raghuvaṃśa* 3.70)
- **The digvijaya — four-quarter world-conquest.** As the centrepiece of his sovereignty Raghu looses the sacrificial horse and conquers in all four directions: **east** (Suhma, Vaṅga, the Gaṅgā-delta islands), **south** (Pāṇḍya, Kerala, the Malaya hills to the southern ocean), **west** (the **Pārasīkas** [Persians] and **Yavanas**), and **north** (the **Hūṇas**, **Kāmbojas**, and Himālayan tribes up to the Kailāsa region). (*Raghuvaṃśa* 4.24–88)
  - *Variant (geography, [late text]):* the conquest-list — Hūṇas, Pārasīkas, Yavanas, Kāmbojas — mirrors the political horizon of the **Gupta age** (c. 4th–5th c. CE) retrojected onto a Tretā king; read as literary geography, not a Purāṇic itinerary. [scholarly]
- **The Viśvajit ("all-conquering") sacrifice.** At the climax of conquest Raghu performs the **Viśvajit**, whose vow requires the sacrificer to **give away the whole of his accumulated wealth** as *dakṣiṇā*; Raghu distributes the entire tribute of the earth to brahmins and suppliants, keeping for himself only earthen vessels — the conqueror of the world left deliberately with nothing, glory lying in *tyāga* rather than hoarding. (*Raghuvaṃśa* 5.1–3)
- **Kautsa's request.** Just as the treasury stands empty, the young brahmin **Kautsa**, pupil of the sage **Varatantu**, comes begging the **fourteen crore (140 million) gold coins** he owes as *guru-dakṣiṇā* — one crore for each of the fourteen branches of learning he has mastered. (*Raghuvaṃśa* 5.1, 5.20–24)
- **Raghu's resolve and Kubera's gold-rain.** Mortified that a worthy suppliant should leave empty-handed, Raghu — left only with clay pots — resolves to **wrest the gold from Kubera**, lord of wealth, and readies his chariot; rather than face the invincible Solar king, **Kubera rains a shower of gold** into Raghu's treasury overnight. (*Raghuvaṃśa* 5.25–30)
  - *Variant (mechanism):* in Kālidāsa the gold falls on Kubera's own initiative, forestalling the attack — a literary set-piece dramatizing the king's boundless *dāna*, absent from the Purāṇic king-lists. [late text]
- **The double restraint.** Raghu offers Kautsa the whole gold-rain; Kautsa takes only the fourteen crore he asked, refusing the surplus, and blesses Raghu with the boon of a worthy son — fulfilled in **Aja**. (*Raghuvaṃśa* 5.31–34, 36)
- **The dynasty's name.** From Raghu the senior Solar line is permanently styled **Raghuvaṃśa**, and Rāma carries the patronymic *Rāghava* into the [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md). (*Raghuvaṃśa*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.4; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.9)

## 3. Aja and Indumatī — the svayaṃvara and the elegy

*Raghuvaṃśa* cantos **6–8**. The lyric apex of the poem; almost wholly Kālidāsa's invention atop the Purāṇic name "Aja." (The full canonical deep-dive lives at [../04-deep-dives/aja-indumati.md](../04-deep-dives/aja-indumati.md).)

- **The svayaṃvara and the suitors' battle.** **Indumatī**, sister of King **Bhoja** of Vidarbha, passes by every assembled king and garlands **Aja**, Raghu's son; the rejected suitors' ambush on the homeward road is routed with the **Saṃmohana missile** taught by the gandharva **Priyaṃvada**. (*Raghuvaṃśa* 6.1–83; 7.31–71)
- **Reign and the heir.** Raghu in old age abdicates to Aja and withdraws as a *yogin*; Indumatī bears **Daśaratha**, the future father of Rāma. (*Raghuvaṃśa* 8.1–29; lineage in [suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md](./suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md))
- **The falling garland and the Aja-vilāpa.** A celestial garland slipping from **Nārada's vīṇā** kills Indumatī instantly — she is revealed as the **apsarā Harīṇī**, released from the sage **Tṛṇabindu's** curse; Aja's prolonged elegy over her, the celebrated **Aja-vilāpa**, is the high-water mark of *karuṇa-rasa* in Sanskrit *kāvya*, and after eight years of sorrow he ends his life in the Gaṅgā–Sarayū waters, reunited with her in heaven. (*Raghuvaṃśa* 8.33–95; [scholarly])
- The curse-frame (Tṛṇabindu, Harīṇī) has **no Purāṇic counterpart** — Aja and Indumatī are bare succession-names in Viṣṇu IV.4 / Bhāgavata IX.9; the framing variants are catalogued in [Aja & Indumatī](../04-deep-dives/aja-indumati.md). [late text]

## 4. The "two clocks" — sacred history vs. a Gupta court poem

- **Scriptural clock.** On the traditional reckoning Dilīpa, Raghu, and Aja are **Tretā-Yuga** kings of the present (28th) Mahāyuga of the Vaivasvata Manvantara, the three generations immediately above Daśaratha and Rāma; the Purāṇas (Viṣṇu IV.4, Bhāgavata IX.9) record only their succession. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.4; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.9)
- **Historical clock.** The narratives that make these kings famous come from **Kālidāsa's *Raghuvaṃśa***, a **5th-century-CE** mahākāvya of the Gupta classical age; its geography (Hūṇas, Pārasīkas, Yavanas) and its psychology (the Aja-vilāpa) are court-poetry retrojected onto archaic king-list names. The two registers must be kept distinct — the Tretā ancestors of sacred genealogy vs. the heroes of a post-classical Sanskrit epic. [scholarly]
- **Why gathered here.** In [suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md §1b](./suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) these three sit as one-line entries (Raghu; Aja & Indumatī) beside a bare "Dilīpa II"; this file expands them as the connected Kālidāsa cycle they form — devotion (Dilīpa), liberality (Raghu), and grief (Aja) — that frames the dynasty just before the [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md) begins.
