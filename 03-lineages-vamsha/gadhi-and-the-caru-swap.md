# Gādhi and the Caru-Swap — Birth of the Kuśika and Bhārgava Lines

> *Hierarchy: [`03-lineages-vamsha/`](README.md) — a single genealogical hinge-node of the **Candravaṃśa** (Lunar, Amāvasu/Kuśika branch). Gādhi, king of **Kānyakubja**, is the father from whom two of Hindu tradition's most consequential clan-energies fork — the brāhmaṇa-turned-fierce **Bhārgava** line (→ Jamadagni → Paraśurāma) and the kṣatriya-turned-sage **Kuśika** line (→ Viśvāmitra). The whole cross-over is fixed by one ritual mishap: the swapped caru.*

---

## 0. Where Gādhi sits in the tree

- **Genealogical spine.** **Purūravas → Āyu → … → Kṣatravṛddha → … → Kuśa → Kuśanābha → Gādhi (Gādin)**, kings of **Kānyakubja** (Kanauj). Gādhi's house is the **Kuśika** line (named for the ancestor Kuśika/Kuśa), a Lunar-dynasty kṣatriya branch. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.7; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.15; *Mahābhārata* Ādi & Vana Parva) See the [Candravaṃśa overview](suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
  - *Variant (Rāmāyaṇa Bāla Kāṇḍa 32–34):* Vasiṣṭha recites the Kuśika descent as **Kuśa → Kuśanābha → Gādhi**, with Kuśa a "Prajāpati-born" / Brahmā-descended king — the *Rāmāyaṇa* compresses and slightly differs from the Purāṇic count. [disputed: king-order]
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata IX.15):* makes the Kuśika branch spring from **Jahnu**'s line (whence Gaṅgā = Jāhnavī), threading Gādhi's ancestry through the Jahnu episode.
- **Gādhi himself.** A righteous Lunar king; remembered almost entirely as a **father-node** — through his two children the kṣatriya and brāhmaṇa orders are dramatically crossed. He has no major deed of his own; his significance is purely genealogical and ritual. (*Mahābhārata* Vana Parva 115–117; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.7)
  - *Variant [folk/regional]:* some Purāṇic and folk strands name Gādhi an incarnation of **Indra** himself, born to King Kuśanābha by Indra's grace — heightening the "divine seed in a mortal king" motif.

## 1. Gādhi's two children — the fork

- **Son: Kauśika → Viśvāmitra.** Gādhi's son, born a kṣatriya prince of Kānyakubja, named **Kauśika** ("of the Kuśika clan"); he renounces kingship and through extreme tapas rises through ṛṣi grades to become the **brahmarṣi Viśvāmitra** — the line's brāhmaṇa-ward thrust. (*Rāmāyaṇa* Bāla Kāṇḍa; *Mahābhārata* Ādi & Vana Parva) Full arc in [Viśvāmitra & Vasiṣṭha](../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md) and the [Bāla Kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md).
- **Daughter: Satyavatī.** Gādhi's daughter, given in marriage to the Bhārgava sage **Ṛcīka**. She is the literal genetic conduit by which kṣatriya nature enters the Bhārgava (brāhmaṇa) clan. (*Mahābhārata* Vana Parva 115; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.7; *Bhāgavata* IX.15)
  - *Note — name collision [disputed]:* this **Satyavatī** (daughter of Gādhi, wife of Ṛcīka, grandmother of Paraśurāma) is **distinct** from the Cedi-born **Satyavatī**/Matsyagandhā (wife of Śāntanu, mother of Vyāsa) in the [Kuru line](suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md). The texts share only the name. After her death this Satyavatī is said to become the river **Kauśikī** (Kosi). (*Mahābhārata* Vana Parva; *Devī-Bhāgavata*)

## 2. The Caru-Swap — the central myth

Ṛcīka, a sage of the **Bhārgava** (Bhṛgu-descended) line, marries Satyavatī. To beget sons, he prepares consecrated oblation-rice (**caru**) charged with specific qualities — and a swap inverts the destinies.

- **The bride-price horses.** Ṛcīka wins Satyavatī's hand from Gādhi by furnishing the bride-price the king demands — **one thousand white horses, each with one black ear** — obtained from Varuṇa. (*Mahābhārata* Vana Parva 115; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.7)
- **Two charus prepared.** Wishing both his wife and her mother (Gādhi's queen) to bear sons, Ṛcīka cooks **two consecrated charus**:
  - one charged with **brāhmaṇa-tejas** (brahminical, serene, learned nature) — intended for **Satyavatī**, so the Bhārgava line gets a true brahmin heir;
  - one charged with **kṣatriya-tejas** (martial, fierce nature) — intended for the **queen-mother**, so Gādhi's kṣatriya house gets a mighty warrior heir. (*Mahābhārata* Vana Parva 115–116; *Bhāgavata* IX.15)
- **Embracing trees added.** In the fuller telling Ṛcīka also charges two **trees** (or has each woman embrace a designated tree — a peepal/uḍumbara pairing) to reinforce the caru's effect; the women are instructed which caru to eat and which tree to embrace. (*Mahābhārata* Vana Parva 116)
- **The swap.** The mother, coveting the superior offspring she imagines her daughter is to have, **persuades Satyavatī to exchange** charus (and trees) — or the two simply eat the wrong portions. The brāhmaṇa-caru goes to the queen-mother; the kṣatriya-caru goes to Satyavatī. (*Mahābhārata* Vana Parva 116; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.7)
  - *Variant (who initiates) [disputed]:* Most strands blame the **mother's greed** for a finer son; some make it an innocent mix-up; the *Bhāgavata* (IX.15) keeps Ṛcīka's design but stresses the women ate "each the other's."
- **Ṛcīka perceives it by tapas.** The sage, returning, instantly knows by his yogic sight that the charus were swapped and laments the inversion now lodged in both wombs. (*Mahābhārata* Vana Parva 116)
- **The deferral boon.** At Satyavatī's anguished plea — she does not want a savage son in a brahmin house — Ṛcīka relents and **defers the fierceness by one generation**: her own son will be calm and brahminical, but the kṣatriya ferocity will surface in her **grandson**. (*Mahābhārata* Vana Parva 116; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.7)

## 3. The consequences — two lineages seeded

- **Bhārgava line gets the warrior-seed.** Satyavatī (eating the kṣatriya-caru) bears **Jamadagni** — outwardly a brahmin sage, inwardly carrying the kṣatriya fire; per the boon it is deferred to his son. Jamadagni's son by **Reṇukā** is **Paraśurāma** (6th avatāra of Viṣṇu) — a *brāhmaṇa by birth, kṣatriya by temperament*, who exterminates the kṣatriyas twenty-one times. The fierceness Ṛcīka's caru was never meant to give the Bhārgavas thus becomes the line's defining trait. (*Mahābhārata* Vana Parva 115–117; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.7, IV.11; *Bhāgavata* IX.15–16) See [Paraśurāma in the Vana Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md).
- **Kuśika line gets the sage-seed.** The queen-mother (eating the brāhmaṇa-caru) bears **Viśvāmitra** (Kauśika) — *kṣatriya by birth, brāhmaṇa by temperament and destiny*, who alone among kings wins **brahmarṣi** status by tapas. The brāhmaṇa-tejas misplaced into Gādhi's house powers Viśvāmitra's lifelong drive to transcend his warrior birth. (*Mahābhārata* Vana Parva 116–117; *Rāmāyaṇa* Bāla Kāṇḍa) See [Viśvāmitra & Vasiṣṭha](../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md).
- **The mirror-symmetry.** Gādhi is the node where two great clans receive each other's essence: the **Bhārgava** brahmins gain a warrior-saint (Paraśurāma), and the **Kuśika** kṣatriyas gain a king-turned-seer (Viśvāmitra). The caru-swap is tradition's etiology for *why* these two clans cross caste in opposite directions. (*Mahābhārata* Vana Parva; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.7)
  - *Variant (one-generation vs. immediate) [late text]:* texts that drop the deferral-boon make **Jamadagni himself** the fierce one; the mainstream Vana Parva version pushes the ferocity to Paraśurāma and keeps Jamadagni serene (until Kārtavīrya provokes him). [scholarly: the boon harmonizes the two readings]

---

## 4. Two-clocks note

- **Scriptural clock.** All of this is fixed in **Tretā Yuga** of the current (28th) Mahāyuga — Viśvāmitra survives into Rāma's Tretā era, and Paraśurāma bridges Tretā into the next age (he meets both Rāma and, later, Bhīṣma/Karṇa in Dvāpara as a *cirañjīvī*). (*Rāmāyaṇa*; *Mahābhārata*)
- **Historical clock.** No historical anchoring; **Kānyakubja/Kanauj** as a real city is far later. The Gādhi node is genealogical-mythic, not datable. [scholarly]
