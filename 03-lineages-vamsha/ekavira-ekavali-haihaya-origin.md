# Ekavīra and Ekāvalī — the origin of the Haihaya dynasty

> *Hierarchy: [`03-lineages-vamsha/`](README.md) — a Śākta origin-legend grafted onto the **Haihaya** sub-branch of the [Yādava-Candravaṃśa](yadava-vamsha.md). The **Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (Book VI) rewrites the birth of the Haihaya eponym as a divine-descent tale: **Lakṣmī cursed to a mare's body** bears **Ekavīra (= Haihaya)**, who — armed with a Devī-mantra from **[Dattātreya](../04-deep-dives/dattatreya.md)** — slays the daitya **Kālaketu** and wins **Ekāvalī**, seeding the line that culminates in **Kārtavīrya Arjuna**. Scriptural clock: deep antiquity of the current Mahāyuga, opening onto the Tretā Haihaya–Bhārgava cycle. Academic clock: a late Śākta upapurāṇa — see §6. [late text]*

---

## 0. Where this sits — a divine birth grafted onto the Yādava tree

- **The standard genealogy the DBP is overwriting.** In the mainstream Purāṇic vaṃśa-lists the Haihaya eponym is a purely human link: **Yadu → Sahasrajit → Śatajit → Haihaya** (with brothers Haya and Veṇuhaya), founder of the Haihayas of **Māhiṣmatī** on the Narmadā, ancestor of Kārtavīrya Arjuna. Haihaya there has no birth-miracle at all. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.11; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IX.23) See the [Yādava-vaṃśa overview](yadava-vamsha.md).
- **What the Devī-Bhāgavata adds.** The DBP (Book VI) makes the childless king **Turvasu — son of Yayāti, alias Harivarman** — the father-of-record, and his "son" a *foundling of divine descent*: the incarnate **Lakṣmī**-born colt-child **Ekavīra**, identified with **Haihaya**. This choice is itself the genealogical seam: Turvasu is Yadu's **brother** (both sons of Yayāti) and the eponym of a *separate* line, so the DBP grafts the Haihaya founder onto **Turvasu's branch** — a *different* descent from the smooth **Yadu → Sahasrajit → Śatajit → Haihaya** continuity of the Viṣṇu/Bhāgavata lists, not a seamless agreement with it. The dynasty's very name (*Haihaya* ← *haya*, "horse") is meanwhile re-etymologized from the mare-birth rather than from a plain eponym. This is a characteristic Śākta move: a royal line's charter is rewritten so its founder owes everything to the **Devī** and her mantra. [scholarly]
- **Disambiguation — two Kālaketus.** The abductor here is a **daitya/dānava Kālaketu** of Pātāla. He is *not* **Kālaketu the hunter (vyādha)** of the Bengali *[Caṇḍīmaṅgal](../90-literature-corpus/regional-and-oral-epics.md)* (Mukundarāma, 16th c.), a pious devotee-hero of Caṇḍī. The corpus keeps these two apart — same name, opposite characters. [folk: the Caṇḍīmaṅgal figure]
- **Disambiguation — Ekavīra / Ekāvalī.** *Ekavīra* ("the sole hero") is the mare-born king = Haihaya; *Ekāvalī* ("single-strand necklace") is the rescued princess, daughter of King **Raibhya**. The near-identical names are distinct persons — hero and bride — not variants of one figure.

## 1. The curse — Lakṣmī born as a mare

- **The distraction.** While Lakṣmī dwelt with **Viṣṇu**, **Revanta** — son of **Sūrya**, born of the mare-formed **Saṃjñā** — arrived mounted on the divine horse **Uccaiḥśravas**. Lakṣmī became so absorbed gazing at the wondrous horse that she failed to answer Viṣṇu when he spoke. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI, mare-birth chapters ~16–19) See [Revanta and the Sūrya family](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/surya-and-his-family.md) and [Lakṣmī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/lakshmi.md).
- **Viṣṇu's curse.** Offended by her abstraction, Viṣṇu curses Lakṣmī to be **born on earth as a mare**. She descends and performs severe tapas on the bank of the Kālindī/at a sacred confluence, longing to be reunited with her lord. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI ~16–19) [late text]
- **Śiva's boon — the son as ransom.** Moved by her penance, **Śiva** (in some tellings with Brahmā) grants that **Viṣṇu will come to her in the form of a horse**, that she will bear a son, and that upon the son's birth she will be released from the mare's body and restored to Vaikuṇṭha. Viṣṇu assumes an equine form and unites with the mare-Lakṣmī; from this union a boy is born. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI ~16–19)
  - *Structural parallel:* the horse-form conception deliberately mirrors the **Saṃjñā–Sūrya** episode that produced the **[Aśvins](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ashvins.md)** and Revanta — the same "mare and stallion on earth" motif that set the whole chain in motion. [scholarly]
- **The foundling king.** Lakṣmī, freed, returns to heaven, leaving the infant behind. The **childless king Turvasu — son of Yayāti, alias Harivarman** (Yadu's *brother*, and so not a Yādava but the head of a separate line) — coming to the river, discovers the abandoned divine child and — overjoyed — takes him as his own, naming him **Ekavīra**, "the one hero"; he is also called **Haihaya**. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI)

---

## 2. Ekavīra / Haihaya — the mare-born king and his mantra

- **A born devotee of the Devī.** Raised as crown prince, Ekavīra grows into an unmatched warrior and a devout worshipper of the **Goddess** — the trait on which the whole rescue-plot turns. His piety, not merely his valour, is what qualifies him to be the Devī's chosen instrument. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI)
- **The gift of Dattātreya.** The sage **[Dattātreya](../04-deep-dives/dattatreya.md)** initiates Ekavīra into the **Yogeśvarī-mahāmantra** called **Triloki-tilaka** ("ornament of the three worlds"), by whose power he can travel unobstructed anywhere in an instant. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI ~20–21)
  - *The mantra as transmitted (DBP):* **"Hrīṃ Gaurī Rudradayite Yogeśvarī Hūṃ Phaṭ Svāhā."** [late text]
  - *Note — the same Dattātreya patronage recurs down the line:* it is **Dattātreya** who later grants **[Kārtavīrya Arjuna](../04-deep-dives/jamadagni-parashurama.md)** his thousand arms and near-invincibility — the DBP thus makes the Haihaya house a *Dattātreya-favoured dynasty from its founder onward*. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI; cf. *Bhāgavata* IX.15)

## 3. Ekāvalī and the daitya Kālaketu

- **The princess.** **Ekāvalī**, daughter of King **Raibhya**, is a maiden of surpassing beauty. She is also — like her destined husband — a **worshipper of the Devī**, having received a **Devī-mantra from a brāhmaṇa** and worshipped the Goddess (Caṇḍikā/Bhagavatī) with great devotion for **three months**. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI.22) [chapter: "the stealing away of Ekāvalī"]
- **The abduction.** The **daitya Kālaketu**, smitten on seeing Ekāvalī (in some tellings by the Gaṅgā's bank), seizes her by force and carries her off to his city in **Pātāla**, overpowering her escort. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI.22)
- **The Goddess's intervention.** Ekāvalī's companion **Yaśovatī**, herself an adept of the **Bhagavatī-mantra**, worships **Caṇḍikā**; the Goddess appears to her in a dream and names the rescuer — **King Ekavīra/Haihaya**, the Devī's devotee — directing that word be carried to him. The rescue is thus engineered entirely through the Devī's grace and her mantra, on both sides of the match. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI.22)
  - *Emphasis (Śākta frame):* every hinge of the plot — the hero's power, the heroine's protection, the intelligence that unites them — is a **mantra-gift of the Goddess**. The martial climax is almost incidental to the devotional charter. [scholarly]

---

## 4. The battle and the rescue

- **Ekavīra descends to Pātāla.** Using the **Triloki-tilaka** mantra's power of unobstructed passage, Ekavīra reaches Kālaketu's stronghold. Kālaketu marches out with a host of **rākṣasas/dānavas** bearing every weapon. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI.23) [chapter: "the battle of Haihaya and Kālaketu"]
- **The death of Kālaketu.** In fierce single combat Ekavīra/Haihaya strikes the daitya-king with his **mace (gadā)**; Kālaketu **falls like a mountain split by lightning and dies**. His army broken, Ekavīra frees Ekāvalī. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI.23)
- **Restoration.** Ekavīra returns the princess to her father Raibhya, his mission accomplished by the Goddess's power throughout. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI.23)

## 5. Marriage — and the seed of the Haihayas

- **The wedding.** King Raibhya, in gratitude, gives **Ekāvalī** to **Ekavīra/Haihaya** in marriage with full rites and lavish gifts, and sends his daughter away with the Haihaya king. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI.23)
- **The son — and the road to Kārtavīrya.** Ekāvalī bears Ekavīra a son, **Kṛtavīrya**; from Kṛtavīrya is born **Kārtavīrya Arjuna** (Sahasrārjuna), the thousand-armed Haihaya emperor of **Māhiṣmatī** whose collision with **Jamadagni** and **Paraśurāma** drives the great Bhārgava–Haihaya cycle. The DBP's origin-tale is, in effect, the *prequel* to that vengeance-epic. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI.23; cf. *Bhāgavata* IX.15–16) See [Jamadagni & Paraśurāma](../04-deep-dives/jamadagni-parashurama.md) and [Paraśurāma](../04-deep-dives/parashurama.md).
  - *Variant — telescoped vs. long genealogy [disputed]:* the DBP makes **Ekavīra → Kṛtavīrya → Kārtavīrya** an almost immediate descent, whereas the *Viṣṇu/Bhāgavata* king-lists interpose several reigns (**Haihaya → Dharmanetra → … → Kṛtavīrya → Kārtavīrya**). Some retellings of the DBP tale even name the founder's son **Dharmanetra** (the standard next-generation Haihaya) rather than Kṛtavīrya — the two heir-names mark the seam where the Śākta origin-legend was stitched onto the received vaṃśa. Do not merge them; both counts are attested.
- **Why it matters to the line.** By this account the entire Haihaya house — the marauding **Tālajaṅghas** and **Vītihotras**, the over-mighty Kārtavīrya, the clan that murders Jamadagni and is 21 times purged by Paraśurāma — springs from a **Devī-devotee founder born of Lakṣmī**. It supplies the Haihayas a Śākta pedigree the older kṣatriya-vs-brāhmaṇa epic never gave them. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* VI) See also the parallel Haihaya-war material in the [Kāśī line](kashi-line-divodasa.md).

## 6. Two-clocks note

- **Scriptural clock.** Ekavīra's founding sits in the **deep antiquity of the current (28th) Mahāyuga**, among the primordial Lunar/Yādava reigns, only a few generations upstream of the **Tretā-Yuga** Haihaya–Bhārgava conflict (Kārtavīrya, Jamadagni, Paraśurāma). The mare-birth and the Pātāla battle float in mythic pre-history, outside any fixed yuga-date; the corpus files the founding as **late-primordial, opening onto Tretā**.
- **Academic clock.** The tale is exclusive to the **Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa**, a **Śākta upapurāṇa** dated by scholars to **[scholarly] c. 11th–13th century CE** (some place its final form as late as the 15th c.) — far later than the *Viṣṇu*/*Bhāgavata* genealogies it reworks. The divine-birth of Ekavīra is therefore best read as a **late Śākta re-charter** of an old dynastic eponym, not an independent early tradition. [scholarly] [late text]
