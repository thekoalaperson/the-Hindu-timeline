# Sources — The Hindu Timeline

A light, curated bibliography for the whole project. This is **not** an exhaustive
URL dump — every individual file carries its own `## Sources` section with the
specific citations (verse numbers, chapter references, page-specific links) used
there. This page lists the **canonical anchors** the project leans on most, with
one line each on what each source anchors in the cosmic timeline.

For the project map and the "you are here" cosmic address, see [README.md](README.md);
for the full textual taxonomy see [90-literature-corpus/](90-literature-corpus/README.md).

## 1. Primary Scriptural Sources

### Shruti — Veda & Upanishad
- **Ṛgveda Saṃhitā** — the oldest layer; anchors the cosmogony (Nāsadīya 10.129, Puruṣa 10.90), the Devī/Vāk Sūkta (10.125), and the earliest Vedic-period dating horizon.
- **Yajur / Sāma / Atharva Saṃhitās** — ritual, chant, and folk-magical strata; supply the four-Veda frame the timeline's literature corpus is built on.
- **Brāhmaṇas & Āraṇyakas** (esp. *Śatapatha* 1.8.1) — the Manu-and-the-flood seed of the Manvantara system.
- **The principal (Mukhya) Upaniṣads** (Aitareya, Bṛhadāraṇyaka, Chāndogya, Kaṭha, Muṇḍaka, Māṇḍūkya, Taittirīya, Praśna, Īśa, Kena, etc.) — anchor the *concepts-and-dharma* layer: ātman/brahman, the four Mahāvākyas, the koshas, and Nachiketa (Kaṭha).

### Smṛti — Itihāsa (Epics)
- **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa** (7 kāṇḍas; Baroda Critical Edition, Oriental Institute 1960–75) — primary anchor for the Treta-yuga Rāma narrative, kāṇḍa-by-kāṇḍa.
- **Mahābhārata** (18 parvas + Harivaṃśa; BORI Critical Edition, 1919–66) — primary anchor for the Dvāpara/Kali pivot, parva-by-parva, and the great frame stories (Nala, Sāvitrī, Yayāti, etc.).
- **Bhagavad Gītā** (Bhīṣma Parva, 18 adhyāyas) — anchor for the dharma/yoga doctrines, read through the Śaṅkara / Rāmānuja / Madhva bhāṣyas.

### Smṛti — Purāṇa (the 18 Mahāpurāṇas)
- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** — the project's backbone for the cosmic-time chain (units of time, kalpa, manvantara) and the Kali-yuga prophecy.
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa (Śrīmad Bhāgavatam)** — the daśāvatāra sequence, Kṛṣṇa-līlā, Gajendra/Ajāmila/Ambarīṣa episodes, and Kalki.
- **Matsya, Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇas** — the dynastic king-lists (vaṃśa) and the pañca-lakṣaṇa template.
- **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa** — houses the **Devī Māhātmya** (Durgā Saptaśatī, ch. 81–93), anchor for the Great Goddess cycle.
- **Śiva, Liṅga, Skanda Purāṇas** — the Śiva myth-cycle, the 12 Jyotirliṅgas, and sacred geography.
- (Plus Garuḍa, Kūrma, Padma, Agni, Nārada, Bhaviṣya, Brahma, Brahmavaivarta, Vāmana, Varāha, and the Devī-Bhāgavata reckoned in some Vaiṣṇava/Śākta lists — see [smriti-puranas.md](90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md) for the full 18 + the variant enumerations.)

### Smṛti — Āgama, Tantra, Dharmaśāstra
- **Pāñcarātra & Vaikhānasa Saṃhitās; the 28 Śaiva-Siddhānta + 64 Bhairava Āgamas; Śākta Tantras** (Kulārṇava, Mahānirvāṇa, Śrī Vidyā) — the sectarian ritual traditions underlying temple practice and the Daśa Mahāvidyā.
- **Manusmṛti** (esp. 1.64–80) — anchor for the time-unit ladder (nimeṣa→kalpa) and the varṇa/āśrama framework.
- **Yājñavalkya, Nārada, Parāśara Smṛtis; Dharma-sūtras** (Āpastamba, Gautama, Baudhāyana, Vasiṣṭha) — the dharma/saṃskāra layer.
- **Sūrya Siddhānta** (jyotiṣa) — the astronomical figures behind the yuga lengths and the Kali epoch (3102 BCE).

## 2. Key Online Reference Repositories

- **[Wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/)** — the project's most-used repository: full translated Purāṇas, epics, and Sanskrit-term glosses with chapter/verse granularity.
- **[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/)** — first-line orientation, version-comparison, and academic-dating summaries (cited throughout for overviews and variant catalogs).
- **[Sacred-Texts](https://www.sacred-texts.com/)** — public-domain classic translations (Ganguli Mahābhārata, Griffith Vedas, Wilson Viṣṇu Purāṇa).
- **[Vedabase](https://vedabase.io/)** — ISKCON/Gauḍīya Bhāgavatam and Gītā (Prabhupāda) for the Vaiṣṇava reading.
- **[GRETIL](http://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/)** — Göttingen e-text archive of the Sanskrit originals, for verse verification.
- **[Valmiki Ramayana — IIT Kanpur](https://www.valmiki.iitk.ac.in/)** — searchable critical Rāmāyaṇa with translation.
- **[BORI](https://bori.ac.in/department/mahabharata/)** — Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute, home of the Mahābhārata Critical Edition.
- **[DrikPanchang](https://www.drikpanchang.com/)** — practical Hindu time-keeping, calendars, and panchanga calculations.
- **Specialist references** — [IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/) (philosophy/darśanas), [Dharmawiki](https://dharmawiki.org/), [TemplePurohit](https://www.templepurohit.com/) and [HinduPad](https://hindupad.com/) (lists), [Indica Today](https://www.indica.today/) (myth-cycle deep reads).

## 3. Note on Dating & Variants

The timeline runs on **two clocks held in parallel**. The *traditional* clock is the
scriptural cosmic reckoning — Brahmā's life of 100 divine years, Kali-yuga beginning
in **3102 BCE** (the *Sūrya Siddhānta* / *Āryabhaṭīya* epoch), with mahāyuga and
manvantara spans taken literally from the Purāṇas. The *academic* clock is the
text-critical and archaeological consensus (Ṛgveda c. 1500–1200 BCE, the epics
reaching their critical-edition form across roughly 400 BCE–400 CE, most Purāṇas
500–1500 CE). Where these diverge, both figures are given and labeled; neither is
collapsed into the other.

**Variants are cataloged, never silently resolved.** When the Purāṇas, or the
Vaiṣṇava / Śaiva / Śākta / regional-folk traditions, disagree — on a unit of time
(15- vs 18-nimeṣa kāṣṭhā), an avatāra count, a king-list, a Jyotirliṅga's identity,
or the membership of the 18 Purāṇas — each version is recorded and attributed to its
source text. The per-file `## Sources` sections are the authority for any specific
claim; this page is only the curated map of where it all comes from.
