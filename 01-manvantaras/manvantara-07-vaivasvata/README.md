# Manvantara 7 — Vaivasvata Manu (Current)

> Hierarchy address: Shveta-Varaha Kalpa (Day 1 of Brahma's 51st year) → **7th of the 14 Manvantaras**, presided over by Vaivasvata Manu. Six manvantaras have elapsed; eight remain. We are presently in its **28th of 71 Mahayugas**, in **Kali Yuga**. This is the "you are here" manvantara — the cosmic age in which essentially all of recorded Hindu history, the great epics, and the well-known avatars unfold.

A **manvantara** = 71 Mahayugas + 1 sandhya (= one Satya Yuga, 1,728,000 yrs) ≈ **306,720,000 human years**. Each manvantara has its own Manu, set of seven sages (Saptarishi), an Indra, a class of gods, and the avatars Vishnu manifests within it. See [../README.md](../README.md) for the full list of 14 Manus and [the 28th Mahayuga](mahayuga-28-current/) for current-age events.

## The Manu: Vaivasvata (Shraddhadeva / Satyavrata)

- **Identity & names** — The 7th Manu, progenitor of present humanity. Called **Vaivasvata** ("son of Vivasvan"), **Shraddhadeva** (after his wife Shraddha), and **Satyavrata** (his name as a king before the flood, esp. in Bhagavata/Matsya accounts). (Vishnu Purana; Bhagavata Purana; Matsya Purana)
- **Parentage** — Son of **Vivasvan / Surya** (the Sun) and **Saranyu / Sanjna**; lineage traces Brahma → Marichi → Kashyapa → Vivasvan → Manu. (Vishnu Purana; Vaivasvata Manu — Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (naming):* his mother is given as **Saranyu** in Vedic sources and **Sanjna** in later Puranic retellings — same figure, divergent spellings/traditions.
- **Wife & offspring** — Married **Shraddha**; through a son-yielding sacrifice (or her own austerity) he begot ten children, founders of the two great royal lines. (Bhagavata Purana; Vishnu Purana)

## The Matsya Flood Myth (Manu's Survival)

The defining event of this manvantara's opening: Vishnu's **Matsya (Fish) avatar** warns Manu of a world-dissolving deluge and ferries him to safety, preserving the seeds of life into the new age.

- **The growing fish** — A tiny carp (*shaphari*) comes into Manu's hands as he offers water-libation in a river; it begs protection. He moves it jar → pitcher → well → tank → river → ocean as it miraculously outgrows each. Recognizing divinity, Manu submits. (Matsya Purana; Bhagavata Purana)
- **The warning & the boat** — Matsya foretells an all-destroying flood (pralaya). Manu builds a great boat and, per the fish's instruction, loads it with **the Saptarishi (seven sages), all kinds of seeds, and (in later versions) animals**. (Mahabharata, Vana Parva 3.186; Matsya Purana)
- **The towing** — The boat is tied to a **horn on the fish's head**; the fish drags it through the cataclysmic storm and moors it on a Himalayan peak (*Naubandhana*, "boat-binding") as the waters recede. Manu then recreates progeny. (Mahabharata, Vana Parva 3.186; Matsya Purana)
- *Variant (Shatapatha Brahmana, oldest version):* The earliest telling — the fish is **not yet identified with Vishnu** (no avatar framing). Manu reared the fish, was warned of the flood, built a ship; the fish towed it north and Manu alone survived, repopulating via a daughter, **Ida/Ila**, born from his offerings. (Shatapatha Brahmana 1.8.1)
- *Variant (Mahabharata):* The fish is identified with **Brahma** (not Vishnu); emphasizes the *matsya-nyaya* ("law of the fishes," might-makes-right) and the fish requesting sages + grains aboard. (Mahabharata, Vana Parva)
- *Variant (Matsya / later Puranas):* The fish is explicitly **Vishnu**; the towing-rope is the cosmic serpent — named **Vasuki** in some texts, **Adi-Shesha/Shesha** in others. Matsya later also slays the demon **Hayagriva** and recovers the stolen Vedas (a strand absent from the Brahmana account).
- *Variant (king-vs-Manu framing):* Bhagavata/Matsya frame him as **King Satyavrata** at the time of the flood, *becoming* Vaivasvata Manu afterward; the Vedic Shatapatha simply calls him **Manu**. [reconciliation: same individual across the textual layers]

## Offspring — Founders of the Two Great Dynasties (Vamshas)

Manu's ten children seed the entire genealogical spine of Hindu kingship. (Bhagavata Purana 9; Vishnu Purana 4)

- **The ten children (standard Bhagavata list):** Ikshvaku, Nabhaga, Dhrishta, Sharyati, Narishyanta, Nabhaga-Dishta (Nabhanedishta), Karusha, Prishadhra, Vasuman (Pramshu), and **Ila / Sudyumna**.
- **Ikshvaku — founder of the Surya Vamsha (Solar Dynasty).** Eldest son; his line runs through Mandhata, Harishchandra, Sagara, Dilipa, Raghu, Dasharatha, and **Rama**. (Vishnu Purana; Ramayana)
- **Ila / Sudyumna — root of the Chandra Vamsha (Lunar Dynasty).** Via Ila's child **Pururavas** (by Budha, son of Soma/Chandra) the Lunar line descends to Nahusha, Yayati, the Kurus and Pandavas, and **Krishna's** kin. (Vishnu Purana; Bhagavata Purana)
  - *Variant (Ila's sex):* Ila is variously **born female then turned male (Sudyumna)**, or born male and cursed to alternate sexes monthly, after entering a grove sacred to Shiva/Parvati. Because Ila is reckoned both "mother" and "father," the Lunar line is sometimes traced through Ila and sometimes through Sudyumna's sons. (Bhagavata Purana; Mahabharata)
  - *Variant (count):* Manu is also said to have had **fifty (or other) additional sons who perished quarrelling among themselves**, leaving the ten as the surviving progenitors. (Vaivasvata Manu — Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (Ikshvaku's primacy):* some lists make Ikshvaku arise from Manu's sneeze (*kshava*) rather than the standard birth, a folk etymology of the name. [late/folk gloss]

## The Officers of This Manvantara

Each manvantara is staffed by a cohort that holds office for its full ~306.7-million-year span. For Vaivasvata:

- **Manu:** Vaivasvata (Shraddhadeva). (Vishnu Purana)
- **Indra (king of the gods):** **Purandara**. ["Indra" is a title/office, not a personal name; Purandara holds it this manvantara.] (Vishnu Purana; Manvantara — Wikipedia)
- **Saptarishi (Seven Sages):** **Kashyapa, Atri, Vasishtha, Vishvamitra, Gautama, Jamadagni, Bharadvaja.** (Vishnu Purana; Brahmanda Purana)
  - *Variant (ordering differs):* texts list these same seven in different sequences — e.g. **Vasishtha, Kashyapa, Atri, Jamadagni, Gautama, Vishvamitra, Bharadvaja** (a reordering of the identical membership, not a name substitution). (Manvantara — Wikipedia; Dharmawiki)
  - *Variant (membership differs):* a few older enumerations substitute or add names — e.g. the Jaiminiya Brahmana list opens with **Agastya** (Agastya, Atri, Bharadvaja, Gautama, Jamadagni, Vasishtha, Vishvamitra — Kashyapa absent), and the Gopatha Brahmana adds **Gungu** alongside Agastya. The seven sages also differ from those of past manvantaras. (Saptarshi — Wikipedia; Dharmawiki)
- **Class of gods (devas):** named variously as the **Adityas, Vasus, and Rudras** (and in some texts the **Sadhyas/Vishvedevas**) for this manvantara. (Vishnu Purana; Bhagavata Purana)
  - *Variant:* Puranic enumerations of the deva-classes per manvantara do not fully agree; counts and group-names vary by text.

## Avatars Appearing Within Vaivasvata Manvantara

Vishnu manifests across this manvantara's mahayugas; the famous ones fall in the **28th (current) Mahayuga**:

- **Matsya (Fish)** — at the manvantara's flood-threshold, saving Manu (see above). (Matsya Purana)
- **Other early Dashavatara forms** — Kurma, Varaha, Narasimha, Vamana, Parashurama are placed within this manvantara's earlier/ongoing cycles by Puranic reckoning. [yuga-placements vary by text]
- **Rama** — in the **Treta Yuga** of the 28th (or, by some reckonings, an earlier) Mahayuga. (Ramayana; Vishnu Purana) — see [mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga](mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)
- **Krishna** — at the junction of **Dvapara → Kali** of the current 28th Mahayuga; his departure (3102 BCE) marks Kali's onset. (Mahabharata; Bhagavata Purana) — see [mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga](mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)
- **Buddha** — counted as an avatar of the current age in many Dashavatara lists. [included in standard lists; status debated across sects]
- **Kalki** — the *future* avatar, prophesied to end the current Kali Yuga and restore Satya, still ~426,800+ years off. (Kalki Purana; Bhagavata Purana) — see [mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga](mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)
  - *Variant:* sects disagree on Buddha vs. Balarama as the count's 9th avatar, and on whether avatars recur identically each Mahayuga or only in select ones.

## Position Within the Manvantara

- **Mahayugas elapsed:** 27 complete (each Satya→Treta→Dvapara→Kali, 4,320,000 yrs).
- **Current:** **28th Mahayuga**, **Kali Yuga**; ~5,127–5,128 years elapsed (as of 2026 CE) of Kali's 432,000.
- **Remaining in manvantara:** rest of this Kali Yuga (~426,873 yrs) + 43 further Mahayugas + the closing sandhya.
- After Vaivasvata comes the **8th Manu, Savarni** (Manvantara 8). (Vishnu Purana) — see [../manvantara-08-savarni](../manvantara-08-savarni/) if cataloged.
