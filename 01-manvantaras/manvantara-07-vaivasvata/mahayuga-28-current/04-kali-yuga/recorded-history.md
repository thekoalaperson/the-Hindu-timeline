# Kali Yuga — Documented History

> Hierarchy: Brahma's current life → 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara → 28th Mahayuga → **Kali Yuga** → the *historical/academic* branch.
> This file maps the **archaeologically and textually recorded history of the Hindu tradition** onto the Kali Yuga, distinguishing **scriptural/traditional dating** from **mainstream scholarly dating**. For the scriptural-event branch of this same yuga see `./events.md` (Kali narratives, Kalki) and `./00-overview.md`.

---

## Orientation: two clocks running at once

- **Traditional clock:** Kali Yuga began **3102 BCE** (17/18 Feb, midnight), reckoned from Krishna's departure / the close of the Mahabharata war. As of 2026 CE = **Kali year ~5,127–5,128**; ~426,873 years remain ([see constants](../../../../00-time-and-cosmology/) ). Almost all of "recorded history" thus sits inside the *first ~1.2%* of Kali Yuga.
- **Scholarly clock:** the same span is reconstructed from archaeology, epigraphy, palaeography, and text-criticism, yielding dates that frequently **diverge sharply** from the traditional/matha chronologies. This file flags every major divergence with `[traditional]` vs `[scholarly]`.
- **Method note:** dates below labelled `[scholarly]` are mainstream academic consensus; `[traditional]` are dates internal to Hindu tradition (Puranic, matha-succession, or hagiographic). Where a date is genuinely open it is marked `[disputed]`.

---

## I. Proto-history & the Indus link [disputed]

- **Indus Valley / Harappan Civilization** — Mature urban phase **c. 2600–1900 BCE** `[scholarly]`; earlier Mehrgarh roots c. 7000 BCE. Planned cities (Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Dholavira, Rakhigarhi), standardized weights, undeciphered script. (Wikipedia: *History of India*; archaeology consensus)
  - *Continuity claim [disputed]:* Some Indian scholars and the "Indigenous Aryans" school identify the Indus civilization **with** the Vedic culture (Saraswati-Sindhu civilization, citing the dried Ghaggar-Hakra as the Rigvedic Sarasvatī), pushing Vedic origins back into the 3rd millennium BCE. Mainstream scholarship treats the Indus script as undeciphered and the identification as **unproven**. Mark `[disputed]`.
  - *Pashupati seal [disputed]:* A Mohenjo-daro seal of a seated horned figure is often read as a "proto-Shiva / Pashupati." The identification is contested (could be a bull/buffalo deity or unrelated). `[disputed]`

- **Aryan question** — Mainstream model: **Indo-Aryan migrations** into the subcontinent c. 1900–1500 BCE bringing the Indo-European language that became Vedic Sanskrit `[scholarly]`. Opposing **Out-of-India / Indigenous Aryans** model holds Indo-Aryans were autochthonous. Genetic studies (Reich et al.) support steppe-ancestry inflow. `[disputed in Indian public discourse, but scholarly consensus favours migration]`

## II. Vedic period (the śruti is composed)

- **Rigveda composed** — c. **1500–1200 BCE** `[scholarly]`; the oldest layer of the four Vedas, hymns to Agni, Indra, Soma, Varuna, Ushas. *Traditional view:* the Vedas are **apauruṣeya** (authorless, eternal, "heard" not composed) and beginningless — there is no "date of composition" `[traditional]`.
- **Later Vedic corpus** — Sama, Yajur, Atharva Vedas; **Brahmanas, Aranyakas, principal Upanishads** c. 1200–500 BCE `[scholarly]`. Shift from sacrificial ritualism to **Ātman–Brahman** metaphysics in the Upanishads (Brihadaranyaka, Chandogya the oldest). (Wikipedia: *Vedic period*)
- **Vedanga, Sutras, Shrauta/Grihya ritual literature** — c. 800–300 BCE; codification of grammar (proto-Pāṇini), phonetics, ritual procedure.
- **Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī** — Sanskrit grammar, c. **5th–4th century BCE** `[scholarly]`.

---

## III. Second urbanization, Mahajanapadas & the śramaṇa challenge

- **Sixteen Mahajanapadas** — c. **600–321 BCE** `[scholarly]`; 16 kingdoms/oligarchies (Magadha, Kosala, Vatsa, Avanti, etc.); rise of cities, coinage, Magadhan ascendancy. (Wikipedia: *History of India*)
- **Heterodox movements** — **Mahavira** (Jainism) and **Gautama Buddha** flourish c. 6th–5th c. BCE `[scholarly]`; their critique of Vedic ritual and caste reshapes the religious field that "Hinduism" later defines itself against.
- **Composition of the great epics & smṛti (long redaction windows):**
  - **Mahabharata** — redacted c. **400 BCE – 400 CE** `[scholarly]`; *traditional:* composed by **Vyasa** at the cusp of Dvapara/Kali, recording events of ~3102 BCE `[traditional]`. (see `../03-dvapara-yuga/` for the war as scriptural event)
  - **Ramayana** (Valmiki) — core c. **500 BCE – 200 CE** `[scholarly]`; *traditional:* events occur in **Treta Yuga**, far earlier `[traditional]`. (see `../01-treta-yuga/`)
  - **Manusmṛti** (Dharmaśāstra) — c. 200 BCE – 200 CE `[scholarly]`.

---

## IV. Empires & the consolidation of classical Hinduism

- **Maurya Empire** — **c. 322–185 BCE** `[scholarly]`; Chandragupta, Bindusara, **Ashoka** (patronized Buddhism, rock/pillar edicts). First pan-Indian empire. (Wikipedia: *History of India*)
- **Post-Mauryan / Shunga, Indo-Greek, Kushana** — c. 185 BCE – 3rd c. CE; **Shunga** revival of Brahmanical/Vedic sacrifice; Kushana (Kanishka) syncretism.
- **Composition of the major Puranas** — c. **300–1000 CE** (with older oral cores) `[scholarly]`; Vishnu, Bhagavata, Shiva, Markandeya, Devi-Bhagavata, etc. — the texts that encode the **yuga/manvantara/kalpa cosmology** this whole repo uses, plus avatar theology and the Kali-Yuga decline narrative. (see `../../../../../90-literature-corpus/`)
- **Gupta Empire ("Golden Age")** — **c. 320–550 CE** `[scholarly]`; classical Sanskrit literature (**Kalidasa**), decimal/zero (**Aryabhata**, *Aryabhatiya* 499 CE — which fixes the **3102 BCE** Kali epoch astronomically), temple architecture, Vishnu/Shiva iconography crystallize. Birth of "classical Hinduism."
- **Six Darshanas (āstika philosophical systems) systematized** — Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Mimamsa, Vedanta; foundational sutras and commentaries 200 BCE – 500 CE. **Patanjali's Yoga Sutras** c. 2nd–4th c. CE.

---

## V. Classical temple & dynasty era; the rise of devotional South India

- **Tamil Bhakti — Alvars (Vaishnava) & Nayanars (Shaiva)** — **5th–9th c. CE** `[scholarly]`; 12 Alvars (incl. **Andal**, the only woman; **Nammalvar**) and 63 Nayanars (incl. Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar, Manikkavacakar) sing Tamil hymns to Vishnu/Shiva — the **earliest organized Bhakti movement**, later spreading north. (Wikipedia: *Bhakti movement*)
- **Great temple dynasties** — Pallavas (Mahabalipuram), **Cholas** (Brihadeeswarar, Thanjavur, 11th c.; Nataraja bronze tradition), Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas (Ellora Kailasa), Pandyas; temple Hinduism as state religion.
- **Tantra & Shakta/Shaiva traditions** — Kashmir Shaivism (**Abhinavagupta**, c. 950–1020 CE), Shrividya, Tantric ritual systematized.

---

## VI. The Acharyas (Vedanta founders) — note the traditional/scholarly date gap

- **Adi Shankara** (Advaita / non-dualism) — `[scholarly]` **c. 700–750 CE** (often cited 788–820 CE); founded the four mathas, *Brahmasutra-bhashya*, *Vivekachudamani* [authorship of some works disputed].
  - *Variant [traditional]:* Kanchi and some matha records date him to **509–477 BCE** — over a millennium earlier. Scholars reject this (Shankara cites the Buddhist logician **Dharmakirti**, 7th c.). Mark the early date `[disputed]`. (Wikipedia: *Adi Shankara*; Advaita-Vedanta.org dating page)
- **Ramanuja** (Vishishtadvaita / qualified non-dualism) — traditionally **1017–1137 CE** (120-year hagiographic lifespan); Sri Vaishnava theologian, *Sri Bhashya*, prapatti (surrender). `[scholarly accepts ~11th–12th c., the 120-yr span is hagiographic]`
- **Madhva (Ananda Tirtha)** (Dvaita / dualism) — **1238–1317 CE** `[traditional & broadly scholarly]`; Dvaita Vedanta, Udupi Krishna matha; held an irreducible difference between jiva and Brahman.
- **Other Vedanta acharyas:**
  - **Nimbarka** (Dvaitadvaita) — c. 12th–13th c. `[disputed, some place 7th c.]`
  - **Vallabha** (Shuddhadvaita / Pushtimarg) — **1479–1531 CE**.
  - **Chaitanya / Baladeva** (Achintya Bheda Abheda) — see Bhakti below.

---

## VII. The Bhakti & Sant movement (north India, 14th–17th c.)

> Devotional, often anti-ritual and anti-caste, in vernacular languages. Two broad streams: **saguṇa** (God with form — Rama/Krishna) and **nirguṇa** (formless God — Sant tradition). `[scholarly]` dates below; most are securely datable from manuscripts.

- **Ramananda** — c. 14th–15th c.; north-Indian bridge from Tamil Bhakti to Hindi devotion; teacher-figure linked to many later sants.
- **Kabir** — c. **1440–1518 CE**; weaver-poet, *nirguṇa* Sant, fierce critic of both Hindu ritualism and Islamic legalism; Bijak, dohas; claimed by Hindus, Muslims, and Sikhs.
- **Guru Nanak** — **1469–1539 CE**; founder of Sikhism, *nirguṇa* devotion (overlaps the Sant milieu).
- **Mirabai** — c. **1498–1547 CE** `[traditional/hagiographic]`; Rajput princess-saint, ecstatic Krishna-bhakti poetry in Rajasthani/Braj.
- **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** — **1486–1534 CE**; Bengal, ecstatic Krishna sankirtana, founder-inspiration of **Gaudiya Vaishnavism** (Achintya Bheda Abheda). (Wikipedia: *Bhakti movement*)
- **Tulsidas** — c. **1532–1623 CE**; author of **Ramcharitmanas** (Awadhi Ramayana), defining Rama-bhakti in the Hindi belt.
- **Surdas** — c. 1478–1583 CE; blind poet of Krishna's childhood (Sur Sagar), Pushtimarg.
- **Other sants:** **Namdev** & **Jnaneshwar** (Maharashtra, 13th c., Varkari tradition, *Jnaneshwari*), **Eknath**, **Tukaram** (1608–1649), **Ravidas** (15th c.), **Dadu Dayal**, **Tukaram**; **Shankaradeva** (Assam, 1449–1568, Ekasarana Dharma).
  - *Note:* The Varkari pilgrimage tradition to **Pandharpur (Vithoba)** predates and underlies the Marathi sants.

---

## VIII. Late-medieval & early modern (16th–18th c.)

- **Mughal-era Hinduism** — temple destruction and patronage both occur; **Akbar's** syncretism vs **Aurangzeb's** orthodoxy; Rajput, Maratha, and Vijayanagara (1336–1646, last great Hindu empire of the south) polities sustain temple Hinduism.
- **Sikh Gurus** — lineage from Nanak to Gobind Singh (Khalsa, 1699); Adi Granth compiled 1604.
- **Maratha resurgence** — **Shivaji** (1630–1680) frames Hindu sovereignty (Hindavi Swarajya).
- **Dharma/Smarta consolidation, Dashanami akharas, Vaishnava sampradayas** institutionalize the monastic orders founded by the acharyas.

---

## IX. Colonial-era reform & the "Hindu Renaissance" (19th–early 20th c.)

- **Brahmo Samaj** — founded **20 Aug 1828**, Calcutta, by **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** (with Dwarkanath Tagore); monotheist, anti-idolatry, anti-sati, anti-caste; "no scripture infallible," Enlightenment-rationalist reading. Trust deed 1830. (Wikipedia: *Brahmo Samaj*; Britannica)
  - Later led by **Debendranath Tagore** and **Keshab Chandra Sen** (who linked to Ramakrishna).
- **Arya Samaj** — founded **1875**, Bombay, by **Swami Dayananda Saraswati**; slogan **"Back to the Vedas"**, Vedas treated as infallible, rejection of Puranic ritual/idolatry, *shuddhi* (reconversion); strong in Punjab/UP. *Satyarth Prakash* (1875). (Wikipedia / Arya Samaj sources)
- **Ramakrishna Paramahamsa** (1836–1886) — Bengali mystic, Kali devotee, experiential universalism ("as many faiths, so many paths").
- **Swami Vivekananda** (1863–1902) — Ramakrishna's disciple; **1893 Parliament of the World's Religions, Chicago** address; founded **Ramakrishna Mission, 1 May 1897**; carried Vedanta to the West and energized modern Hindu self-confidence.
- **Theosophical Society** (Blavatsky/Olcott, India HQ Adyar 1882; **Annie Besant**) — Western-Hindu synthesis, revival interest in Sanskrit texts.
- **Other reformers:** **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar** (widow remarriage), **Jyotirao Phule** (anti-caste), **Narayana Guru** (Kerala, social equality), **Sri Aurobindo** (1872–1950, integral yoga, *Life Divine*), **Ramana Maharshi** (1879–1950, Advaita self-enquiry).

---

## X. Modern & contemporary Hinduism (20th–21st c.)

- **Gandhi** (1869–1948) — *satyagraha*, reinterpretation of Gita/ahimsa, Harijan uplift.
- **Global gurus & movements** — **Paramahansa Yogananda** (*Autobiography of a Yogi*, 1946; disciple of Yukteswar), **ISKCON** (A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, 1966, Gaudiya Vaishnavism worldwide), **Transcendental Meditation** (Maharishi Mahesh Yogi), **Sathya Sai Baba**, **Chinmayananda** (Chinmaya Mission), modern **yoga** as global export.
- **Sri Yukteswar variant [non-mainstream]** — held Kali Yuga **ended ~1700 CE** and we are now in **ascending Dvapara Yuga** (24,000-yr precessional cycle, *The Holy Science*, 1894). This reframes the entire "we are in Kali" premise; cataloged as a major variant. (see [constants §2/§4](../../../../00-time-and-cosmology/))
- **Late 20th–21st c.** — Hindu nationalism (Hindutva: Savarkar 1923; RSS 1925; VHP 1964), diaspora temple-building, digital Hinduism, ongoing temple/heritage debates (e.g. Ayodhya), and scholarly–traditional dating disputes that remain politically live.

## Key traditional-vs-scholarly divergences (summary table)

| Item | Traditional dating | Scholarly dating |
|------|------|------|
| Vedas | Eternal / authorless (apauruṣeya) | Rigveda c. 1500–1200 BCE |
| Mahabharata war / Kali start | 3102 BCE | War redaction 400 BCE–400 CE; epoch is astronomical (Aryabhata) |
| Ramayana events | Treta Yuga (millions of yrs ago) | Text c. 500 BCE–200 CE |
| Adi Shankara | 509–477 BCE (Kanchi) `[disputed]` | c. 700–750 CE |
| Indus = Vedic culture | Asserted by some `[disputed]` | Rejected (script undeciphered) |
| Current yuga | Kali (since 3102 BCE) | n/a (Yukteswar variant: ascending Dvapara) |
