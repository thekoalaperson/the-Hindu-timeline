# Dvāpara Yuga — Events

> Hierarchy: Brahma's current life → Shveta-Varaha Kalpa → 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara → 28th Mahayuga → **Dvāpara Yuga** (3rd of 4 yugas). Duration: **864,000 human years** (2,400 deva-years × 360). Dharma stands on **2 legs** (50% virtue). The third quarter of our Mahayuga, ending at Krishna's departure and the dawn of [Kali Yuga](04-kali-yuga/README.md) in **3102 BCE**. Preceded by [Treta Yuga](02-treta-yuga.md).

The defining events of this yuga cluster at its very end: the descent of **Krishna** (8th avatar of Vishnu), the **Mahābhārata** war, and **Vyasa's** literary work. Note the framework: Vishnu Purana (3.3) holds that *in every Dvapara* a Vyasa divides the Vedas — so "Vyasa" is a recurring office; the Vyasa of *our* Dvapara is Krishna Dvaipayana.

---

## 1. Vyāsa & the Ordering of Scripture (the yuga's literary axis)

- **Birth of Krishna Dvaipāyana Vyāsa** — Born on an island (*dvīpa*, hence Dvaipāyana) in the Yamuna to sage Parāśara and Satyavatī (a fisherwoman); dark-complexioned (*krishna*). Regarded as a partial *amsha* incarnation of Vishnu. (Mahabharata Adi Parva; Vishnu Purana 3.3–3.4)
- **Division of the one Veda into four** — Vyasa splits the primordial Veda into Rig, Yajur, Sāma, Atharva for the short-lived, weak-minded people of the declining age; teaches them to four disciples: **Paila** (Rig), **Vaiśampāyana** (Yajur), **Jaimini** (Sāma), **Sumantu** (Atharva). (Vishnu Purana 3.4; Bhagavata Purana 1.4, 12.6)
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 3.3):* This is the **28th** division of the Veda in this Manvantara — each Dvapara has had its own Vyasa (28 to date), the present being the 28th; a long list of the 28 Vyasas is given.
- **Composition of the Mahābhārata** — Vyasa authors the 100,000-verse epic ("the fifth Veda"), narrating his own grandsons' war; dictated to **Gaṇeśa** as scribe on condition Vyasa never pause and Ganesha never write without understanding. (Mahabharata Adi Parva 1)
- **Composition of the 18 Mahāpurāṇas (+ Upapuranas)** — Vyasa traditionally credited as compiler/author of the Puranas and the Brahma Sutras. (Bhagavata Purana 1.4; Vishnu Purana)
  - *Note [scholarly]:* Academically the Vedas predate any single redactor and the Mahabharata/Puranas grew over centuries (c. 400 BCE–400 CE+); "Vyasa" functions as a traditional attributive author. Distinguish scripture from historical text-dating.

## 2. The Kuru Lineage — Setup for the Mahābhārata

- **Śāntanu & the Ganga / Bhīṣma's vow** — King Shantanu of Hastinapura weds the river-goddess Gangā, who bears Devavrata (later **Bhīṣma**). To enable Shantanu's later marriage to **Satyavatī**, Bhishma takes the terrible vow of lifelong celibacy and renunciation of the throne — earning the boon of *ichchhā-mṛtyu* (death at will). (Mahabharata Adi Parva)
- **Vichitravīrya & niyoga** — Satyavati's sons Chitrāngada and Vichitravīrya die heirless; Satyavati summons her pre-marital son **Vyasa** to father heirs by niyoga on the widowed queens. (Adi Parva)
- **Birth of Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Pāṇḍu, Vidura** — By Vyasa: **Dhritarashtra** (born blind — queen Ambika shut her eyes), **Pandu** (born pale — queen Ambalika paled), and **Vidura** (born wise/healthy, by a maid; an incarnation of Dharma). (Adi Parva)
- **Birth of the Pāṇḍavas** — Pandu, cursed to die if he embraces a wife, leaves heirs via Kuntī's mantra (boon of sage Durvāsa) summoning gods: **Yudhiṣṭhira** (Dharma), **Bhīma** (Vāyu), **Arjuna** (Indra); and via Mādrī: twins **Nakula & Sahadeva** (Aśvins). Kunti's earlier secret son by Sūrya = **Karṇa**. (Adi Parva)
- **Birth of the Kauravas** — **Gāndhārī** (who blindfolds herself for her blind husband) bears, from a mass of flesh divided into 100 jars, the hundred Kauravas led by **Duryodhana**, plus a daughter Duḥśalā. (Adi Parva)
- **Droṇa, Kṛpa & the training of the princes** — Guru Drona teaches archery; Arjuna becomes peerless; Drona demands **Ekalavya's** thumb as guru-dakṣiṇā; the **Drupada** humiliation seeds Drona's vendetta. (Adi Parva)

## 3. KRISHNA — Descent of Vishnu (8th Avatar)

> Primary source: **Bhāgavata Purāṇa Books X–XI** (the most elaborate), plus the **Harivaṃśa** (Mahabharata appendix), **Vishnu Purana** (Book 5), and **Brahmavaivarta / Garga Saṃhitā** for devotional/regional expansion.

### Birth & rescue
- **Prophecy & imprisonment** — A voice warns **Kaṃsa**, tyrant of Mathura, that the 8th child of his sister **Devakī** (wife of **Vasudeva**) will kill him; Kamsa imprisons them and slays their first six infants. (Bhagavata 10.1–10.4)
- **Birth of Krishna** — Born at midnight, Bhādra-pada (festival = **Janmāṣṭamī**), as the 8th child; Vishnu appears four-armed then assumes infant form. (Bhagavata 10.3)
- **The crossing of the Yamunā** — Prison chains fall, guards sleep; Vasudeva carries baby Krishna across the flooded Yamuna (waters part; Śeṣa shields with his hood) to **Gokula/Vraja**, exchanging him for the newborn daughter of **Nanda** and **Yaśodā**. The girl (Yogamāyā) slips from Kamsa's grasp and foretells his doom. (Bhagavata 10.3–10.4)
  - *Variant (7th child, Balarāma):* Devaki's 7th conception (Śeṣa) is transferred to **Rohiṇī's** womb in Gokula — born as **Balarāma** (Saṅkarṣaṇa). (Bhagavata 10.2)

### Childhood līlās in Vraja (Gokula / Vṛndāvana)
- **Pūtanā** — The breast-poisoning demoness sent by Kamsa; infant Krishna sucks out her life. (Bhagavata 10.6)
- **Śakaṭāsura & Tṛṇāvarta** — Krishna kicks over the cart-demon; kills the whirlwind-demon who abducts him. (Bhagavata 10.7)
- **Damodara / butter-thief & the mortar** — Yashoda binds the butter-stealing toddler to a mortar; he uproots the twin **Yamala-Arjuna** trees, freeing two cursed Yakshas. The vision of the **universe in his mouth** is shown to Yashoda. (Bhagavata 10.8–10.10)
- **Serpent Kāliya** — Krishna subdues and dances on the hood of the poison-snake Kaliya in the Yamuna, banishing him. (Bhagavata 10.16)
- **Govardhana-līlā** — Krishna persuades the cowherds to stop Indra-worship; the enraged **Indra** sends torrential rain; Krishna **lifts Mount Govardhana** on his little finger for 7 days as an umbrella, protecting Vraja. Indra submits (festival = Govardhan Puja / Annakut). (Bhagavata 10.24–10.27)
- **Rāsa-līlā** — The circular moonlit dance with the **gopīs**; the supreme devotional (bhakti) episode; **Rādhā** is the foremost beloved. (Bhagavata 10.29–10.33)
  - *Variant:* **Rādhā is essentially absent from the Bhagavata Purana**; she is central in later texts — **Brahmavaivarta Purana**, **Gīta Govinda** of Jayadeva (12th c.), and Gaudiya/Nimbarka theology. [later/devotional development]
- **Slaying of Vraja demons** — Vatsāsura (calf), Bakāsura (crane), Aghāsura (serpent), Dhenukāsura (donkey, killed by Balarama), Pralambāsura, Keśī (horse), Vyomāsura — all Kamsa's agents. (Bhagavata 10.11–10.37)

### Mathura, Kamsa & the move to Dvārakā
- **Killing of Kaṃsa** — Summoned to Mathura by Akrūra; Krishna & Balarama break Kamsa's sacrificial bow, kill the elephant **Kuvalayāpīḍa** and wrestlers **Chāṇūra & Muṣṭika**, then Krishna drags Kamsa from his throne and kills him; restores Kamsa's father **Ugrasena**. (Bhagavata 10.42–10.44)
- **Schooling under Sāndīpani** — Krishna & Balarama study; as guru-dakshina recover the guru's dead son from Yama. (Bhagavata 10.45)
- **Jarāsandha & exodus to Dvārakā** — Kamsa's father-in-law **Jarāsandha** (king of Magadha) attacks Mathura 17 times; to spare the people Krishna builds and relocates the Yadavas to the sea-fortress **Dvārakā** (Gujarat coast); earns the epithet **Raṇacchoḍa** ("who quit the battle"). (Bhagavata 10.50–10.52; Vishnu Purana 5)
- **Marriage to Rukmiṇī** — Princess of Vidarbha, an incarnation of Lakshmi; betrothed against her will to Śiśupāla, she sends Krishna a letter; he **abducts/elopes** with her from the temple, defeating her brother Rukmī. Their son: **Pradyumna** (Kāmadeva reborn). (Bhagavata 10.52–10.54)
- **The other principal queens (Aṣṭabhāryā)** — Rukmini, Satyabhāmā, Jāmbavatī, Kālindī, Mitravindā, Satyā (Nāgnajitī), Bhadrā, Lakṣmaṇā — eight chief wives; plus the **16,100** women freed from the demon **Narakāsura** (whom Krishna slays with Satyabhama; festival = Naraka Chaturdashi/Diwali). (Bhagavata 10.58–10.59)
- **Syamantaka jewel & Jāmbavatī** — Krishna clears himself of stealing the gem; wins **Jāmbavatī** (daughter of the bear-king Jāmbavān) and Satyabhama. (Bhagavata 10.56–10.57; Vishnu Purana 4)
- **Other deeds** — Slays **Śiśupāla** (with the Sudarśana chakra after 100 insults, at Yudhishthira's Rājasūya), **Pauṇḍraka** (the false Vasudeva), **Dantavakra**, **Śālva** (and his flying city Saubha); burns Kāśī. (Mahabharata Sabha Parva; Bhagavata 10.66, 10.74, 10.76–10.77)

## 4. Balarāma (the 7th/elder; Śeṣa avatar)

- **Identity** — Elder brother of Krishna; incarnation of **Śeṣa-nāga**; wielder of the plough (**Halāyudha**) and mace; teacher of mace-fighting to both Bhima and Duryodhana. (Bhagavata 10; Mahabharata)
  - *Variant (Vaishnava lists):* Counted by some as an **avatar of Vishnu in his own right** (replacing Buddha in certain dashavatara lists, esp. in Jagannath/Odia and some Vaishnava traditions). [variant tradition]
- **Deeds** — Drags the Yamuna with his plough; kills Dhenukasura, Pralamba, and (in a drunken rage) **Rukmī** at Aniruddha's wedding; goes on pilgrimage during the Kurukshetra war, **remaining neutral**. (Bhagavata 10; Mahabharata Shalya Parva)

## 5. The MAHĀBHĀRATA — Full Arc

> Sources: **Mahābhārata** (18 parvas), with the **Bhagavad Gītā** embedded in the Bhīṣma Parva. Setting: Hastinapura/Kurukshetra; the cousins' war for the Kuru throne.

### Rise of the rivalry
- **Lākṣāgṛha (House of Lac)** — Duryodhana's plot to burn the Pandavas alive in a lac palace at Vāraṇāvata; they escape via a tunnel (Vidura's warning), faking their deaths. (Adi Parva)
- **Bhīma & the rākṣasas** — In the forest Bhima kills **Hiḍimba** (marries Hiḍimbā → son **Ghaṭotkaca**) and **Baka**. (Adi Parva)
- **Draupadī's svayaṃvara** — In Brahmin disguise Arjuna wins **Draupadī** (Pāñcālī, daughter of Drupada) by stringing the bow and hitting the rotating fish-eye target. By Kunti's inadvertent word + a past boon, Draupadi becomes the **common wife of all five Pandavas**. (Adi Parva)
- **Partition & Indraprastha** — The kingdom is split; Pandavas build the magnificent capital **Indraprastha** (the demon-architect **Maya** builds the Maya Sabhā). (Adi/Sabha Parva)
- **Rājasūya & the insult** — Yudhishthira's imperial sacrifice; Duryodhana, humiliated in the illusory hall ("blind son of a blind father" — Draupadi's reported jibe), nurses revenge. (Sabha Parva)

### The dice game & exile
- **The game of dice (Dyūta)** — **Śakuni** plays for Duryodhana; Yudhishthira gambles away wealth, kingdom, brothers, himself, and finally **Draupadī**. (Sabha Parva)
- **Draupadī's disrobing (Vastraharaṇa)** — Duḥśāsana drags Draupadi by the hair and attempts to strip her; **Krishna miraculously extends her sari** infinitely. Bhima vows to break Duryodhana's thigh and drink Duhshasana's blood. (Sabha Parva)
  - *Variant [textual]:* The explicit "Krishna supplies endless cloth" passage is regarded by many scholars as a **later interpolation**; the critical edition's account is more restrained (her garments are simply replenished / she is saved by dharma). [disputed/late]
- **13-year exile** — 12 years in the forest (Vana/Āraṇyaka Parva) + 1 year **incognito** (Virāṭa Parva) at King Virata's court (Yudhishthira=Kanka, Bhima=cook, Arjuna=eunuch dance-teacher Bṛhannalā, Draupadi=Sairandhrī). (Vana, Virata Parva)
- **Forest episodes** — Arjuna's penance and the **Pāśupatāstra** from Śiva (Kirātārjunīya); celestial weapons from the gods; the **Yakṣa Praśna** (Yudhishthira answers Dharma's riddles to revive his brothers); **Nala-Damayanti** & other inset tales. (Vana Parva)

### Prelude to war
- **Failed peace & Krishna's embassy** — Krishna goes as peace envoy; Duryodhana refuses to yield even "five villages"; tries to seize Krishna, who reveals his **Viśvarūpa** (cosmic form). War becomes inevitable. (Udyoga Parva)
- **Choosing sides** — Duryodhana picks Krishna's **Nārāyaṇī army**; Arjuna picks **unarmed Krishna as charioteer (Pārtha-sārathi)**. (Udyoga Parva)
- **Karṇa's loyalty** — Kunti reveals to Karna he is her firstborn and the Pandavas' brother; he stays loyal to Duryodhana but vows to fight only Arjuna and spare the other four. (Udyoga Parva)

### The Bhagavad Gītā
- **The Gītā (700 verses, 18 chapters)** — On the battlefield, Arjuna collapses in despair (*viṣāda*) at fighting kin; **Krishna delivers the Bhagavad Gītā** within the **Bhīṣma Parva** — teaching karma-yoga, bhakti-yoga, jñāna-yoga, the immortal Self, and revealing his universal form (ch. 11). Arjuna resolves to fight. (Mahabharata, Bhishma Parva 23–40)

### The 18-day Kurukṣetra War & key deaths
> 11 akṣauhiṇī (Kaurava) vs 7 (Pandava). Four successive Kaurava commanders: **Bhīṣma → Droṇa → Karṇa → Śalya** (then Duryodhana himself).
- **Days 1–10 — Bhīṣma commands.** First casualties (Uttara, Śveta); Bhima kills many Kaurava brothers; on **Day 10**, with Śikhaṇḍī (reborn Ambā) shielding Arjuna, **Bhīṣma falls** on a bed of arrows (śara-śayyā), choosing to wait for the winter solstice to die. (Bhishma Parva)
- **Days 11–15 — Droṇa commands.**
  - **Day 13 — Abhimanyu's death:** Arjuna's son penetrates the **Chakravyūha** but cannot exit; six maharathis (Drona, Karna, Duryodhana, Duḥśāsana, Kṛpa, Aśvatthāmā) corner and kill the unarmed boy (Jayadratha meanwhile blocks the other Pandavas at the vyūha entrance). (Drona Parva)
  - **Day 14 — Jayadratha's death:** Arjuna vows to kill **Jayadratha** by sunset or self-immolate; Krishna conceals the sun, draws Jayadratha out, then reveals it — Arjuna beheads him. (Drona Parva)
  - **Day 14–15 (night battle) — Ghaṭotkaca's death:** Bhima's rakshasa son rampages by night; **Karṇa** spends his infallible **Śakti** spear (reserved for Arjuna) to kill him — a decisive turn. (Drona Parva)
  - **Day 15 — Droṇa's death:** Krishna's stratagem — Bhima kills an elephant named "Aśvatthāmā" and Yudhishthira ambiguously confirms "Aśvatthāmā is dead (the elephant)"; the grieving Drona lays down arms and is beheaded by **Dhṛṣṭadyumna**. (Drona Parva)
- **Days 16–17 — Karṇa commands.**
  - **Karṇa's death (Day 17):** His chariot wheel sinks in the mud (sage Paraśurāma's & a Brahmin's curses take effect); his armor/earrings gone (given to Indra) and his Brahmāstra knowledge failing, Karna is killed by Arjuna on Krishna's urging while disarmed. (Karna Parva)
- **Day 18 — Śalya & the end.**
  - **Śalya** (commander) killed by Yudhishthira; **Śakuni** killed by Sahadeva; the last Kaurava brothers fall. (Shalya Parva)
  - **Duryodhana's fall:** Hides in a lake (Dvaipāyana); drawn out, fights **Bhīma** with maces; Bhima smashes his **thigh** (an unfair blow, on Krishna's hint, fulfilling his vow) — Duryodhana dies. (Shalya Parva)
  - **Sauptika (the night massacre):** **Aśvatthāmā**, Kṛpa & Kṛtavarmā raid the sleeping Pandava camp by night, slaughtering Dhrishtadyumna, **Śikhaṇḍī**, and the five sons of Draupadi (Upapāṇḍavas). Ashvatthama looses the **Brahmaśiras** at Uttarā's womb (the unborn **Parīkṣit**); Krishna revives the child and curses Ashvatthama to wander 3,000 years with a festering wound. (Sauptika Parva)

### Aftermath
- **Strī Parva & Gāndhārī's curse** — The widows lament; **Gāndhārī**, grieving, curses **Krishna** that he and the Yādavas will perish in mutual slaughter within 36 years. (Stri Parva)
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's reign & the Aśvamedha** — Coronation at Hastinapura; Bhishma (still on the arrow-bed) delivers the vast **Śānti** and **Anuśāsana Parvas** (dharma, statecraft, the **Viṣṇu Sahasranāma**) before dying at the solstice; horse-sacrifice performed. (Shanti, Anushasana, Ashvamedhika Parvas)

## 6. The Close of Dvāpara — Yādava Destruction & Krishna's Departure

> Source: **Mausala Parva** (Mahabharata Book 16); Bhagavata Purana Book 11.
- **The sages' curse on Sāmba** — In Dvaraka, Krishna's son **Sāmba** is dressed as a pregnant woman to mock visiting sages (Viśvāmitra, Durvāsa, Nārada et al.); cursed to bear an **iron club (musala)** that will doom the clan. The iron pestle, ground to powder and cast into the sea, regrows as deadly reed-grass (eraka). (Mausala Parva)
- **The Yādava civil war at Prabhāsa** — On pilgrimage, the drunken Yadavas quarrel; the reeds turn to iron clubs in their hands; **the entire clan slaughters itself** — Sātyaki, Kṛtavarmā, Pradyumna, and all the sons die. (Mausala Parva)
- **Death of Balarāma** — Balarama withdraws into yoga at the seashore; **Śeṣa-nāga** issues from his mouth into the ocean — he departs the world. (Mausala Parva; Bhagavata 11.30)
- **Death of Krishna** — Krishna sits in meditation under a pīpal tree, one foot raised; the hunter **Jara** ("Old Age"), mistaking the reddish sole for a deer, shoots an arrow piercing it; Krishna ascends to his abode, ending the avatar. (Mausala Parva; Bhagavata 11.30–11.31)
  - *Variant:* The arrow is forged from the **last remaining fragment** of the cursed iron pestle (the bit a fish swallowed and Jara recovered) — closing the curse-loop.
- **Submergence of Dvārakā** — As Krishna leaves, the sea **swallows Dvārakā**; Arjuna evacuates the survivors (mostly women and children), but is robbed en route — his powers fading with the age. (Mausala Parva)
- **The Pandavas' Great Departure (Mahāprasthāna)** — Hearing of Krishna's death, the Pandavas crown **Parīkṣit** (Abhimanyu's son) and walk north toward the Himalayas; all die on the way except Yudhishthira, who ascends to heaven with a dog (Dharma in disguise) (Mahaprasthanika & Svargarohana Parvas) — *though this departure technically occurs in early Kali.*

### Transition to Kali Yuga
- **End of Dvāpara = Krishna's departure.** Puranic tradition fixes the start of Kali Yuga at **midnight, 17/18 February 3102 BCE** (Julian), the moment of Krishna's death. (Surya Siddhanta; Vishnu Purana; Bhagavata 12.2)
  - Krishna is said to have lived **125 years** (Bhagavata) / popularly **89 years** at death; the war preceding it by ~36 years.
  - *Variant (Aryabhata):* Same 3102 BCE epoch but Kali begins at the Mahabharata war / Krishna's reckoning; sub-yugas treated as equal quarters.
  - *Variant (Sri Yukteswar):* Rejects the date framework entirely; Kali is far shorter and already past. [non-mainstream]
- See [Kali Yuga](04-kali-yuga/README.md) for what follows; the **Kuru/Pāṇḍava lineage** continues there through Parīkṣit and Janamejaya (to whom the Mahabharata is recited).

## 7. Variants & Recensions (catalog)

- **Mahābhārata recensions** — Two great manuscript families: the **Northern** and **Southern** recensions, differing in episode inclusion and length; the **Critical Edition** (BORI, Pune, 1919–1966) reconstructs a constituted text, treating much (e.g. some Draupadi-disrobing details, parts of the Anushasana Parva) as later accretion. [textual]
- **Harivaṃśa** — Appendix to the Mahabharata; principal *early* source for Krishna's childhood, parallel to/feeding the Bhagavata. (Harivamsha)
- **Bhāgavata vs. Vishnu vs. Brahmavaivarta Purana** — Krishna's biography is most fully devotional in the **Bhagavata** (Book X); **Vishnu Purana** (Book 5) is older and terser; **Brahmavaivarta** and **Garga Saṃhitā** vastly expand **Rādhā** and Goloka theology. [devotional development]
- **Rādhā** — Marginal/absent in the Bhagavata and Vishnu Purana; elevated to consort-goddess only in later texts (Gīta Govinda, Brahmavaivarta, Gaudiya theology). [later tradition]
- **Regional/folk Mahābhāratas** — Tamil (**Villiputtūrār's Pāratam**, Draupadi/Aravan cults), Telugu (**Nannaya/Tikkana/Errana** Andhra Mahabharatam), Kannada (**Pampa's Vikramārjuna Vijaya**, pro-Arjuna), Malayalam, Bengali, Indonesian (**Kakawin Bhāratayuddha**) — each with distinctive emphases and added episodes (e.g. **Barbarika/Khatu Shyam**, **Iravan/Aravan's** self-sacrifice in folk tradition). [regional/folk]
- **Balarāma as avatar** — Counted among the ten avatars in Jagannath/Odia & some Vaishnava lists (displacing Buddha). [variant tradition]
- **Date of Kali's onset** — 3102 BCE is Puranic/astronomical consensus; archaeo-astronomy debates (Mahabharata war dates proposed from ~3139 BCE to ~1000 BCE) are **modern scholarly conjecture**, not scriptural. [scholarly/disputed]
