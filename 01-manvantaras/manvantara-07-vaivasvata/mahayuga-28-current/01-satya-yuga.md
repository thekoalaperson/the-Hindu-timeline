# Satya (Kṛta) Yuga — Events

> Hierarchy: Brahma's current life → Śveta-Varāha Kalpa → **7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara** → **28th Mahāyuga** → **1st quarter: Satya (Kṛta) Yuga**.
> The golden age of THIS mahāyuga: dharma stands on all four legs (100%), the Bull of Dharma whole. Duration **1,728,000 human years** (4,800 deva-years). First yuga; precedes [Tretā](02-treta-yuga.md) → [Dvāpara](03-dvapara-yuga.md) → [Kali](04-kali-yuga/README.md).

---

## Orientation & a caveat on yuga-placement

The Purāṇas record events but rarely pin a divine episode to a *specific numbered* mahāyuga; the convention (followed here and by most digests) is to gather the four early **Daśāvatāra** appearances and the primordial dynasty/origin stories under "Satya Yuga." Treat the *current 28th-mahāyuga* placement as the timeline's organizing convention, not a hard scriptural claim — many events (e.g. Dhruva, Daksha's yajña) are tied in the texts to the **Svāyambhuva Manvantara** (the 1st), not strictly to this Satya Yuga. Variants are flagged throughout.

- *Variant (avatar count per yuga):* Standard tradition assigns the first **four** Daśāvatāra — Matsya, Kūrma, Varāha, Narasiṃha — to Satya Yuga. (Avatars of Satya Yuga; Dashavatara, Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (Narasiṃha placement):* some lists put Narasiṃha (with Vāmana, Paraśurāma) in **Tretā**, not Satya. [disputed] (Vedicmatters; Dashavatara, Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (24 avatars):* the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** counts 24 avatāras, not 10; many of the Satya-Yuga figures below (Sanaka-kumāras, Kapila, Nara-Nārāyaṇa, Dhanvantari) are among them. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.3)

## The Daśāvatāra appearances of Satya Yuga

### Matsya (the Fish) — the great deluge
- **Matsya saves the Vedas and the seed of life.** Viṣṇu as a fish warns **Vaivasvata Manu** (Satyavrata / Śraddhādeva) of a world-ending flood (pralaya); Manu loads the seven sages (Saptarṣi), seeds, and creatures onto a boat tied by the serpent Vāsuki to Matsya's horn, and is towed to safety. Matsya also slays the demon **Hayagrīva**, who had stolen the Vedas from a sleeping Brahmā, and restores them. (Matsya Purāṇa; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.24; Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa — earliest flood form)
  - *Variant (who is saved):* In the **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa** (oldest version) the fish saves Manu alone; the Saptarṣi + seed-bank detail is the later Purāṇic elaboration.
  - *Variant (placement):* Often framed as the *end-of-kalpa / pralaya* event rather than mid-Satya; the flood ushers in the new creation that Vaivasvata Manu then governs. The Matsya episode is what makes Vaivasvata Manu the Manu of THIS manvantara — so it is foundational to the whole node. (Matsya Purāṇa)

### Kūrma (the Tortoise) — Samudra Manthana (Churning of the Ocean of Milk)
The signature epic of Satya Yuga; narrated chiefly in the **Bhāgavata** (8.5–8.12), **Viṣṇu** and **Mahābhārata (Ādi Parva)**. Devas, weakened by Durvāsā's curse, ally with the Asuras to churn the Kṣīra Sāgara for **amṛta** (the nectar of immortality).

- **The curse that triggers it.** Sage **Durvāsā** gives Indra a divine garland; Indra's elephant **Airāvata** tramples it; Durvāsā curses the devas to lose their śrī (fortune/strength). Lakṣmī withdraws into the ocean. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.5; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.9)
- **The apparatus.** **Mount Mandara** = churning rod; serpent **Vāsuki** = churning rope; Asuras hold the head, Devas the tail (Asuras suffer Vāsuki's venom-breath). (Bhāgavata 8.7; MBh Ādi Parva)
- **Kūrma supports the mountain.** As Mandara sinks, Viṣṇu becomes the giant tortoise **Kūrma** and bears the mountain on his back as a pivot. (Bhāgavata 8.7; Viṣṇu Purāṇa)
- **The fourteen ratnas (treasures) emerge** during the churn (lists vary by text; the common set):
  - **Hālāhala / Kālakūṭa** (world-destroying poison) — first to rise.
  - **Kāmadhenu** (wish-cow), **Uccaiḥśravas** (white horse), **Airāvata** (white elephant), **Kaustubha** (gem, worn by Viṣṇu), **Pārijāta** (celestial tree), the **Apsarās** (incl. Rambhā), **Lakṣmī / Śrī** (returns and chooses Viṣṇu), **Vāruṇī / Surā** (goddess of wine — taken by the Asuras), **Candra** (moon), **Śaṅkha** (conch), **Dhanvantari with the amṛta-kalaśa**.
  - *Variant:* the **count and identity of the ratnas differ** across Bhāgavata, Viṣṇu, Padma, and MBh recensions; some lists include Tulasī, the goddess Jyeṣṭhā/Alakṣmī, or omit certain items. (Samudra Manthana, Wikipedia; Rudra Centre summary of Viṣṇu Purāṇa)
- **Hālāhala and Nīlakaṇṭha.** To save the cosmos, **Śiva drinks the Hālāhala poison**; **Pārvatī** holds his throat so it stays lodged there, turning it blue — hence **Nīlakaṇṭha** ("blue-throated"). (Bhāgavata 8.7; Śiva Purāṇa)
  - *Variant (Vaiṣṇava vs Śaiva framing):* Śaiva texts foreground Śiva's self-sacrifice as the supreme act; Vaiṣṇava texts foreground Viṣṇu's orchestration. A drop falls and is consumed by scorpions/serpents, explaining their venom [folk detail].
- **Dhanvantari and the amṛta.** The physician of the gods **Dhanvantari** rises holding the pot of nectar; Asuras seize it. (Bhāgavata 8.8) Dhanvantari is counted as an avatāra of Viṣṇu and progenitor of Āyurveda.
- **Mohinī.** Viṣṇu takes the form of the enchantress **Mohinī**, beguiles the Asuras, and distributes the amṛta only to the Devas. (Bhāgavata 8.8–8.9)
- **Rāhu / Svarbhānu → Rāhu & Ketu.** The Asura **Svarbhānu** disguises himself among the Devas and drinks a sip; Sūrya and Candra expose him; Mohinī/Viṣṇu beheads him with the **Sudarśana Cakra**. As he had tasted amṛta, head and body live on — the head becomes **Rāhu**, the body (with serpent tail) **Ketu**; their grudge against Sun and Moon causes **eclipses**. (Bhāgavata 8.9; MBh Ādi Parva)
  - *Variant:* some texts speak only of **Rāhu** (the head) as the eclipse-demon; the head/body → Rāhu/Ketu split is the astronomical (jyotiṣa) elaboration. (Exotic India, "Rahu Ketu")
- **War over the nectar.** Devas and Asuras battle; with strength restored the Devas prevail and Indra is reinstated. (Bhāgavata 8.10–8.11)
- *Amṛta-spill / Kumbh Mela tie-in:* later tradition holds drops fell at **Prayāga, Haridwar, Ujjain, Nashik**, sanctifying them — origin myth of the **Kumbh Melā**. [late/folk geographic elaboration] (Britannica; Samudra Manthana, Wikipedia)

### Varāha (the Boar) — Hiraṇyākṣa & the lifting of the Earth
- **Varāha rescues the Earth.** The Asura **Hiraṇyākṣa** drags **Bhūmi/Pṛthivī** (Earth) to the bottom of the cosmic ocean. Viṣṇu becomes the colossal boar **Varāha**, dives in, slays Hiraṇyākṣa in a thousand-year duel, and lifts the Earth on his tusks back to her place. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.13, 3.17–3.19; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.4)
  - *Variant (cosmogonic Varāha):* In some texts (and the **Śveta-Varāha Kalpa** naming itself) Varāha is the *creation-dawn* boar who raises the earth at the kalpa's start — i.e. a cosmological act, distinct from the Hiraṇyākṣa-slaying narrative. The two are often merged. (Wisdomlib, Śveta-Varāha Kalpa)
- **The Hiraṇyākṣa–Hiraṇyakaśipu pair: who they were.** The two Asura brothers are the reborn **Jaya and Vijaya**, Viṣṇu's gatekeepers, cursed by the **Sanaka-kumāras** to three demonic births; this is birth #1. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.15–3.16, 7.1)

### Narasiṃha (the Man-Lion) — Hiraṇyakaśipu & Prahlāda
- **Hiraṇyakaśipu's boon.** Enraged at brother Hiraṇyākṣa's death, **Hiraṇyakaśipu** performs extreme tapas and wins from **Brahmā** a boon of near-invulnerability: not slain by man **or** beast, neither day **nor** night, neither indoors **nor** outdoors, on neither earth nor sky, by no weapon, by no deva/asura/human. He then tyrannizes the three worlds. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.3–7.4)
  - *Variant (austerity duration):* **Harivaṃśa** specifies 11,500 years of tapas, standing in water under a vow of silence. (Hiranyakashipu, Wikipedia)
- **Prahlāda's bhakti.** His son **Prahlāda**, instructed in Viṣṇu-devotion by **Nārada** while still in the womb, becomes a supreme devotee. Hiraṇyakaśipu tries repeatedly to kill him — poison, elephants, weapons, fire — all fail. (Bhāgavata 7.5)
  - **Holikā.** Prahlāda's aunt **Holikā**, boon-proof against fire, sits with him in flames to burn him; she perishes, he survives — origin myth of **Holi**. (Bhāgavata; folk/festival tradition)
- **Narasiṃha appears.** Challenged "Is your Viṣṇu in this pillar?", Hiraṇyakaśipu smashes it; Viṣṇu bursts out as **Narasiṃha** (man-lion — neither man nor beast), seizes the Asura **at twilight** (neither day nor night), on the **threshold** (neither in nor out), on his **lap/thighs** (neither earth nor sky), and disembowels him with his **claws** (no weapon) — voiding every clause of the boon. (Bhāgavata 7.8)
- **Aftermath.** Narasiṃha's fury is finally pacified by Prahlāda's prayers; Prahlāda is enthroned as a righteous Asura king and ancestor of **Bali** (later of the Vāmana episode in [Tretā](02-treta-yuga.md)). (Bhāgavata 7.9–7.10)
  - *Variant (who calms him):* texts variously credit **Prahlāda**, **Lakṣmī**, **Śiva (as Śarabha)** [Śaiva text], or the **Goddess (Pratyaṅgirā / Devī)** [Śākta/Tantric] with subduing Narasiṃha. (Tales of Sanatan; Śarabha tradition) [tradition-dependent]

## Origin & dynasty events of Satya Yuga

### Dhruva — the boy who became the Pole Star
- **Dhruva's penance.** **Dhruva**, young son of King **Uttānapāda** (son of Svāyambhuva Manu) and queen **Sunīti**, is rebuffed from his father's lap by stepmother **Suruci**. Guided by **Nārada** and the mantra *Oṃ Namo Bhagavate Vāsudevāya*, he performs intense tapas; Viṣṇu appears and fixes him as the immovable **Dhruva Nakṣatra (Pole Star)**, the still point around whom the Saptarṣi revolve. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.11–1.12; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.8–4.12)
  - *Note on placement:* canonically set in the **Svāyambhuva (1st) Manvantara**, not this Satya Yuga proper; included here as a primordial origin story. [manvantara mismatch]

### Sati & the Daksha Yajña
- **Daksha's sacrifice and Satī's self-immolation.** Prajāpati **Dakṣa** (son of Brahmā) holds a grand yajña, deliberately snubbing his daughter **Satī** and her husband **Śiva**. Satī attends uninvited, is insulted, and **immolates herself** in the sacrificial fire (or by yogic fire). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.2–4.4; Śiva Purāṇa; Liṅga Purāṇa; Kālidāsa, *Kumārasambhava*)
- **Vīrabhadra's rampage.** Enraged, Śiva tears a lock of hair and creates **Vīrabhadra** and **Bhadrakālī**, who destroy the yajña and **behead Dakṣa**. (Bhāgavata 4.5; Śiva Purāṇa)
- **Restoration.** Pacified, Śiva revives the slain and restores Dakṣa with a **goat's head**. (Bhāgavata 4.7)
- **Śākta sequel — the Śakti Pīṭhas.** Śiva wanders bearing Satī's corpse; Viṣṇu's Sudarśana cuts it into pieces that fall across the land, founding the **51 (var. 52, 108) Śakti Pīṭhas**. Satī reincarnates as **Pārvatī**. (Devī Bhāgavata; Kālikā Purāṇa; Tantra literature)
  - *Variant (placement):* the Dakṣa yajña is generally a **Svāyambhuva-era / primordial** episode; tied to Satya Yuga only by convention. [manvantara mismatch]

### The founding of the royal lineages
- **Vaivasvata Manu's children.** Manu (Śraddhādeva) has **ten sons** — Ikṣvāku, Nṛga, Dhṛṣṭa, Śaryāti, Nariṣyanta, Prāṃśu, Nābhāga, Nediṣṭa, Karūṣa, Pṛṣadhra — and the figure **Ilā/Sudyumna** (gender-shifting), from whom the **Lunar dynasty (Candravaṃśa)** springs via Budha. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.1; Bhāgavata 9.1) — see [../../../../03-lineages-vamsha](../../../03-lineages-vamsha/) (lineages folder).
  - *Variant (Ilā/Sudyumna):* the texts differ on the daughter-becomes-son episode and Śiva/Pārvatī's forest curse; Bhāgavata vs Viṣṇu Purāṇa differ in detail. [variant]
- **Ikṣvāku founds the Solar dynasty (Sūryavaṃśa).** Eldest son **Ikṣvāku**, granted Āryāvarta/Kosala, is progenitor of the **Sūryavaṃśa**; his line includes Vikukṣi, Māndhātā, Hariścandra, Sagara, Bhagīratha, Raghu, Daśaratha, **Rāma** (latter in [Tretā](02-treta-yuga.md)). His son **Nimi** founds the Videha (Janaka) line. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.2–4.4; Harivaṃśa; List of Ikṣvāku kings, Wikipedia)
  - *Cross-tradition:* Jain tradition makes Ikṣvāku = the first Tīrthaṅkara **Ṛṣabhanātha**'s line; Buddhist tradition descends the Buddha from this dynasty. (Solar dynasty, Wikipedia) [other-tradition]

### Early sages, mind-born sons & other Satya-Yuga origins
- **The Sanaka-kumāras.** Brahmā's four perpetually-child mind-born sages (Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanātana, Sanatkumāra); their curse on **Jaya & Vijaya** sets off the Hiraṇyākṣa/Hiraṇyakaśipu/Rāvaṇa-Kumbhakarṇa demonic births. (Bhāgavata 3.12, 3.15–3.16)
- **Kapila & the burning of Sagara's sons.** Sage **Kapila** (Sāṃkhya founder, a Viṣṇu avatāra in the Bhāgavata's 24) incinerates the **60,000 sons of King Sagara** for accusing him of stealing the aśvamedha horse — the curse that later drives **Bhagīratha's** bringing-down of the **Gaṅgā**. (Bhāgavata 9.8; Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa) [some traditions place the Gaṅgāvataraṇa in Tretā]
- **Nara-Nārāyaṇa.** The twin sage-avatāras of Viṣṇu performing eternal tapas at Badarī; counted among the Bhāgavata's 24 avatāras. (Bhāgavata 4.1)
- **Churning participants (named cast), for reference:** Devas — Indra, the Ādityas, Sūrya, Candra, Vāyu, Varuṇa; Asuras — Bali (grandson of Prahlāda), Vṛṣaparvā, Svarbhānu; serpent Vāsuki; tortoise Kūrma; physician Dhanvantari; Mohinī (Viṣṇu). (Bhāgavata 8.6–8.10)

## Yuga character (for placement consistency)
- **Dharma:** 4 legs / 100% — the Bull of Dharma whole; no vice, no disease, lifespans of ~100,000 years (traditional). (Framework constants; Manu-smṛti 1.81–86)
- **Duration:** 1,728,000 human years = 4,800 deva-years (4,000 main + 400 sandhyā + 400 sandhyāṃśa). (Framework constants; Sūrya Siddhānta / Viṣṇu Purāṇa)
- **Mode of merit:** meditation/tapas is the prescribed dharma of Satya (vs yajña in Tretā, worship in Dvāpara, nāma-saṃkīrtana in Kali). (Bhāgavata 12.3)
