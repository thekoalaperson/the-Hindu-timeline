# Manvantara 11 — Dharma-Sāvarṇi (Future)

> Cosmic address: Shveta-Varaha Kalpa → 11th of the 14 Manvantaras (a *future* manvantara; we are currently in the 7th, [Vaivasvata](manvantara-07-vaivasvata/)). One of the cluster of "Sāvarṇi" Manus prophesied for the latter half of this Kalpa (8th–12th). See [the 14-Manvantara index](README.md) and [the cosmic clock](../00-time-and-cosmology/).

- A Manvantara = 71 Mahāyugas + a sandhyā ≈ **306,720,000 human years**. Each installs a fresh Manu, a set of seven Ṛṣis, an Indra, classes of devas, and (in the Bhāgavata scheme) an avatāra of Viṣṇu who upholds dharma through the cycle (Vishnu Purana III.1–2; Bhagavata Purana VIII.13). The Dharma-Sāvarṇi manvantara lies far in the future — after the present Vaivasvata and the intervening Sāvarṇi (8th), Dakṣa-Sāvarṇi (9th), and Brahma-Sāvarṇi / Rudra-Sāvarṇi manvantaras. The Puranic record for it is **brief and formulaic** (essentially a roster), and the two principal sources **disagree on names** — both rosters are catalogued below.

## The Manu

- **Dharma-Sāvarṇi** — the 11th Manu, eponymous ruler of the Dharma-sāvarṇi Manvantara. The name marks him as a "Sāvarṇi" (of the Sāvarṇa / Sūrya-related lineage that supplies several future Manus) distinguished by the epithet *Dharma* ("the law/righteous one") (Vishnu Purana III.2; Bhagavata Purana VIII.13).
  - *Parentage (Bhagavata Purana VIII.13):* Dharma-sāvarṇi is the **son of Ruci** (a Prajāpati); in this scheme the Manu himself is identified as a prior manifestation of the avatar **Dharmasetu** "come as the Manu." The Vishnu Purana gives the name without parentage [late text].
  - *Sons (Bhagavata Purana VIII.13):* he has **ten sons headed by Satyadharma** — son-lists are sparsely attested and vary by recension; treat the full enumeration as [late/uncertain].

## Saptarishi (Seven Sages)

The two great sources give **markedly different sage-rosters** for this manvantara; catalog both rather than harmonizing.

- *Vishnu Purana III.2 roster:* **Nischara, Agnitejas, Vapuṣmān, Viṣṇu, Aruṇi, Haviṣmān, Anagha** — the seven Ṛṣis of the Dharma-sāvarṇi Manvantara. (Variant transliterations in derived sources: Niḥsvara/Nihsvar, Agniteja, Vāyuṣmān/Vapushmán, Ghṛṇi, Aruṇa, Haviṣmān, Anagha.)
- *Bhagavata Purana VIII.13 roster:* the seven sages are **"Aruṇa and others"** — the Bhāgavata names the head-sage Aruṇa and leaves the remainder formulaic; it does **not** reproduce the Vishnu Purana's full seven [disputed].
  - *Note:* the recurrence of **Aruṇi/Aruṇa** and **Haviṣmān** across both lists is the only stable point of contact; the rest diverge. See the [Saptarishi cross-manvantara index](README.md) for the rotating-sage pattern.

## Indra & Classes of Devas

The sources **disagree on the name of Indra** for this manvantara — a genuine textual split, not a scribal variant.

- *Indra (Vishnu Purana III.2):* **Vṛṣa** (also given as Vṛṣabha) is the king of the gods.
- *Indra (Bhagavata Purana VIII.13):* **Vaidhṛta** is the Indra. (Some derived retellings of the Vishnu Purana also read "Vish/Vṛṣa"; the Vṛṣa ↔ Vaidhṛta split is the principal [disputed] point of this entry.)

- **Classes of devas (Vishnu Purana III.2):** the gods of this manvantara are the **Vihaṅgamas, Kāmagamas, and Nirmāṇaratis**, each class **thirty in number**.
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana VIII.13):* foregrounds only the **Vihaṅgamas** as the demigod class, without the triad-of-thirty schema [disputed].
  - *Variant (recensional):* deva-class names and counts for the Sāvarṇi cluster of manvantaras drift across Puranic recensions; the "three classes of thirty" formula is a Vishnu-Purana stylization [uncertain].

## Avatāra of Viṣṇu

- **Dharmasetu** ("the bridge/dyke of dharma") — the avatar of Viṣṇu (Hari) for the Dharma-sāvarṇi Manvantara, **born of Vaidhṛta and Āryaka** (Bhagavata Purana VIII.13). His role, like the other manvantara-avatars in the Bhāgavata scheme, is to sustain cosmic order across the age. The Vishnu Purana's terser manvantara list does **not** assign this avatar, so Dharmasetu is specifically a **Bhāgavata** contribution to the 11th-manvantara dossier.
  - *Note:* the name **Dharmasetu** doubles as a back-reference to the Manu's own identity in the Bhāgavata (the avatar "comes as Dharma-sāvarṇi Manu"), tying avatar and Manu together — a feature of the Bhāgavata's theology, not the Vishnu Purana's [late text].

## How sparse is the record?

- Beyond the rosters above, **no narrative events, conflicts, or stories are attributed to this manvantara** in the major Puranas — it is part of the *prophetic* (anāgata) future half of the Kalpa, sketched only as a list of office-holders. This is normal for the future manvantaras (8th–14th), which the texts enumerate rather than narrate. Be skeptical of any detailed "story" of Dharma-Sāvarṇi in late or popular retellings; the primary record is the roster. Compare the equally terse [Manvantara 9 — Dakṣa-Sāvarṇi](manvantara-09-daksha-savarni.md), [Manvantara 12 — Sāvarṇa/Rudra-Sāvarṇi](manvantara-12-rudra-savarni.md), and the better-attested [Manvantara 8 — Sāvarṇi](manvantara-08-savarni.md), which alone among the future Manus carries developed myth.
