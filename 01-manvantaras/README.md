# The 14 Manvantaras of the Current Kalpa

> Hierarchy location: `01-manvantaras/` — one level below the current Kalpa (Shveta-Varaha, 1st day of Brahma's 51st year). A Kalpa = 14 Manvantaras + 15 sandhya-junctions. We are in the **7th, the Vaivasvata Manvantara**. Six are past, this one is current, seven are future.
> See also: [the cosmic clock](../00-time-and-cosmology/) · current node → [`manvantara-07-vaivasvata/`](manvantara-07-vaivasvata/)

---

## What a Manvantara is

- **Manvantara ("Manu-interval")** — the reign of one **Manu**, the progenitor of humankind for that age, and the administrative epoch he presides over. (Vishnu Purana 3.1–2; Bhagavata Purana 8.1, 8.5, 8.13)
- **Duration** — the manvantara proper = **71 Mahayugas = 306,720,000 human years** (= 852,000 deva-years). The 15 inter-Manvantara sandhya-junctions (each = 1 Satya Yuga, 1,728,000 yrs) are counted **separately**: 14 × 306.72M + 15 × 1.728M = the full **4.32-billion-year Kalpa** (exactly 1,000 Mahayugas). (see [`units-of-time.md`](../00-time-and-cosmology/units-of-time.md) and [`yugas-and-mahayuga.md`](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md))
- **What is recreated each Manvantara** — "In each Manvantara, Manu, the seven Rishis, Indra, the gods, and the kings are created afresh." Each epoch therefore has its own **Manu**, its own **Indra** (king of the devas), its own **Saptarishi** (seven sages who transmit the Veda), its own **classes of gods (gana-devatas)**, and a presiding **Manvantara-avatara of Vishnu** who upholds dharma for that age. (Vishnu Purana 3.2)
- **Manvantara-avatara** — distinct from the more famous Dashavatara; these are the incarnations Vishnu takes once per Manvantara specifically to sustain the Manu's rule. The canonical list of 14 is from **Bhagavata Purana 8.1.** Note: the Vishnu Purana does not enumerate all 14 avataras; that scheme is principally Bhagavata.

## The Saptarishi for each Manvantara

The seven sages rotate completely each Manvantara. Lists below follow Vishnu Purana 3.1–2; major variants noted.

### Past Manvantaras

- **1 · Svayambhuva** — Marichi, Atri, Angiras, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu, Vasishtha (the seven mind-born sons of Brahma). (Vishnu Purana 3.1)
- **2 · Svarochisha** — Urja, Stambha, Prana, Dattoli, Rishabha, Nishchara, Arvarivat. (Vishnu Purana 3.1)
  - *Variant:* Urja, Stambha, Prana, **Vata, Prishava, Niraya, Parivan** (an alternate sage-list circulating in Puranic compilations).
- **3 · Uttama / Auttami** — the seven Rishis are given simply as **the seven sons of Vasishtha**. (Vishnu Purana 3.1)
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana 8.1):* the seven, headed by Pramada, are named Kaukundihi, Kurundi, Dalaya, Shankha, Pravahita, Mita, Sammita.
- **4 · Tamasa** — Jyotirdhama, Prithu, Kavya, Chaitra, Agni, Vanaka, Pivara. (Vishnu Purana 3.1)
- **5 · Raivata** — Hiranyaroma, Vedashri, Urdhvabahu, Vedabahu, Sudhaman, Parjanya, Mahamuni. (Vishnu Purana 3.1)
- **6 · Chakshusha** — Sumedhas, Virajas, Havishmat, Uttama, Madhu, Abhinaman, Sahishnu. (Vishnu Purana 3.1)
  - *Variant spelling:* Sumedha, Viraja, Havishman, Uttara, Madhu, Sahishnu, Atinama.

### Current Manvantara

- **7 · Vaivasvata (CURRENT)** — **Kashyapa, Atri, Vasishtha, Vishvamitra, Gautama, Jamadagni, Bharadvaja.** (Vishnu Purana 3.1; Mahabharata; Bhagavata Purana 8.13) — These are the Saptarishis "of the present age," fixed in the sky as the stars of Ursa Major (the Great Bear).
  - *Variant (older/astronomical list, Shatapatha Brahmana 2.1.2.4 lineage):* **Marichi, Atri, Angiras, Pulaha, Kratu, Pulastya, Vasishtha** — i.e. the same primordial seven as Svayambhuva's set are sometimes named as the current sages.
  - *Variant (some Puranic recensions):* substitute **Bhrigu** and **Marichi** into the seven, replacing Vishvamitra/Jamadagni — yielding Kashyapa, Atri, Bharadvaja, Gautama, Vasishtha, **Bhrigu/Marichi**, Vishvamitra.

### Future Manvantaras

- **8 · Savarni** — Galava, Diptimat, Parashurama (Rama Jamadagnya), Kripa, Drauni (Ashvatthama), Vyasa, Rishyashringa. (Vishnu Purana 3.2; Bhagavata Purana) — notable because these are the **seven Chiranjivis / long-lived ones** who survive into the next Manvantara to become its sages.
- **9 · Daksha-savarni** — Savana, Dyutimat, Bhavya, Vasu, Medhatithi, Jyotishman, Satya.
- **10 · Brahma-savarni** — Havishman, Sukriti, Satya, Apammurti, Nabhaga, Apratimaujas, Satyaketu.
- **11 · Dharma-savarni** — Nishchara, Agnitejas, Vapushman, Vishnu, Aruni, Havishman, Anagha.
- **12 · Rudra-savarni** — Tapasvi, Sutapas, Tapomurti, Taporati, Tapodhriti, Tapodyuti, Tapodhana. (note: all names built on *tapas*, "austerity")
- **13 · Deva-savarni / Raucya** — Nirmoha, Tattvadarshin, Nishprakampa, Nirutsuka, Dhritimat, Avyaya, Sutapas.
- **14 · Indra-savarni / Bhautya** — Agnibahu, Shuchi, Shukra, Magadha, Gridhra, Yukta, Ajita.

## Classes of gods (gana-devatas) per Manvantara

Each Manvantara has its own troops of devas. From Vishnu Purana 3.1–2:

- **1 · Svayambhuva** — the Yamas (and associated primal deities). (VP 3.1)
- **2 · Svarochisha** — Paravatas and Tushitas. (VP 3.1)
- **3 · Uttama** — Sudhamas, Satyas, Shivas, Pradarshanas, Vashavartis (five classes, twelve each). (VP 3.1)
- **4 · Tamasa** — Surupas, Haris, Satyas, Sudhis. (VP 3.1)
- **5 · Raivata** — Amitabhas, Abhutarajas, Vaikunthas, Sumedhas. (VP 3.1)
- **6 · Chakshusha** — Adyas, Prasutas, Bhavyas, Prithugas, Lekhas (eight classes of eight). (VP 3.1)
- **7 · Vaivasvata (CURRENT)** — **Adityas, Vasus, Rudras** (with the Sadhyas, Vishvadevas, Maruts, Ashvins per the wider list). (VP 3.1; Bhagavata 8.13)
- **8 · Savarni** — Sutapas, Amitabhas, Mukhyas. (VP 3.2)
- **9 · Daksha-savarni** — Paras, Marichigarbhas, Sudharmas. (VP 3.2)
- **10 · Brahma-savarni** — Sudhamas, Viruddhas, Shatasankhyas. (VP 3.2)
- **11 · Dharma-savarni** — Vihangamas, Kamagamas, Nirmanaratis. (VP 3.2)
- **12 · Rudra-savarni** — Haritas, Lohitas, Sumanasas, Sukarmas. (VP 3.2)
- **13 · Deva-savarni / Raucya** — Sudhamanas, Sudharmans, Sukarmanas. (VP 3.2)
- **14 · Indra-savarni / Bhautya** — Chakshushas, Pavitras, Kanishthas, Bhrajiras. (VP 3.2)

> *Note:* the gana-deva lists vary considerably across Purana recensions (Vishnu, Vayu, Brahmanda, Markandeya, Bhagavata each give partly different troop-names and counts). Treat the above as the Vishnu-Purana baseline, not a fixed canon.

## Notable events / stories anchored to each Manvantara

- **Svayambhuva (1st)** — **The first ordering of the world.** Svayambhuva Manu and his wife Shatarupa are the first human couple; their lineage (Priyavrata, Uttanapada → Dhruva) begins dynastic history. The **Manusmriti** is framed as Svayambhuva Manu's discourse. Avatar **Yajna** (the personified sacrifice) defends this Manu. (Vishnu Purana 1; Manusmriti 1; Bhagavata 3–4, 8.1)
- **Svarochisha (2nd)** — Reign of the son of Svarochis; the **Devi-Mahatmya frame story** ([Markandeya Purana]) is set partly in this Manvantara, where King Suratha and the merchant Samadhi worship the Goddess and Suratha is reborn as the next Manu (the Savarni-to-be). (Markandeya Purana / Devi-Mahatmya)
  - *Variant (Indra):* named **Vipashchit** in Vishnu Purana, **Rochana** in Bhagavata.
- **Uttama / Auttami (3rd)** — Son of Priyavrata; little narrative survives beyond the lists; some Puranas merge his sage-list into "the sons of Vasishtha." (Vishnu Purana 3.1)
- **Tamasa (4th)** — **Gajendra-moksha [variant placement].** The Bhagavata's avatar of this Manvantara is **Hari**, who in some retellings rescues the elephant-king Gajendra from the crocodile — though the better-known telling places Gajendra-moksha differently. (Bhagavata Purana 8.2–4, 8.1)
- **Raivata (5th)** — Avatar **Vaikuntha**, born of Vikuntha; said to have manifested the realm **Vaikuntha** (Vishnu's abode) for the pleasure of the goddess Ramaa. (Bhagavata Purana 8.5, 8.1)
- **Chakshusha (6th)** — Avatar **Ajita**; during his epoch occurs the **Samudra Manthana (churning of the ocean of milk)** in the Bhagavata's reckoning — Ajita takes the **Kurma (tortoise)** form to bear Mount Mandara, and later **Mohini** to distribute the amrita. (Bhagavata Purana 8.5–12, 8.1)
  - *Variant:* the Kurma/Manthana episode is also commonly assigned to the broader Dashavatara sequence rather than fixed to this single Manvantara.
- **Vaivasvata (7th, CURRENT)** — **The great flood / Matsya rescue.** Vaivasvata Manu (Shraddhadeva) is warned by the **Matsya (fish) avatar** of the coming praluvial flood; he preserves the Saptarishis, seeds, and creatures in a boat tied to the fish's horn, and so reseeds the world for our Manvantara. The avatar of record per Bhagavata 8.1 is **Vamana** (the dwarf who subdues Bali). This is the Manvantara of Rama, Krishna, the Mahabharata war, and all recorded history. (Matsya Purana; Shatapatha Brahmana 1.8.1; Bhagavata 8.24, 8.1) — **Deep-dive →** [`manvantara-07-vaivasvata/`](manvantara-07-vaivasvata/)
  - *Variant (avatar attribution):* popular tradition lists **Rama, Krishna, and Buddha** as "the avatars of the Vaivasvata Manvantara," whereas Bhagavata 8.1 names **Vamana** as the formal Manvantara-avatara. Both are recorded.
- **Savarni (8th)** — **The Manvantara to come.** Savarni Manu is identified with King **Suratha** (or with the son of Surya and Chhaya). The demon-king **Bali**, pushed to the netherworld by Vamana but promised Indra-hood, becomes the **Indra of this future age.** Its seven sages are the seven Chiranjivis (immortals). Avatar: **Sarvabhauma.** (Markandeya Purana; Vishnu Purana 3.2; Bhagavata 8.13)
- **Daksha-savarni (9th)** — Manu son of Varuna; Indra **Adbhuta**; avatar **Rishabha.** (Bhagavata 8.13)
- **Brahma-savarni (10th)** — Manu son of Upashloka; Indra **Shambhu**; avatar **Vishvaksena.** (Bhagavata 8.13)
- **Dharma-savarni (11th)** — Manu son of Ruchi; Indra **Vaidhrita**; avatar **Dharmasetu.** (Bhagavata 8.13)
- **Rudra-savarni (12th)** — Manu son of Rudra; Indra **Ritadhama**; avatar **Sudhama.** (Bhagavata 8.13)
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 3.2):* this 12th Manu is **Savarna**, not "Rudra-savarni."
- **Deva-savarni / Raucya (13th)** — Manu son of Satyasaha; Indra **Divaspati**; avatar **Yogeshvara.** (Bhagavata 8.13)
- **Indra-savarni / Bhautya (14th)** — **The last Manvantara of the Kalpa.** Manu son of Bhuti; Indra **Shuchi**; avatar **Brihadbhanu.** At its close comes the Kalpa-ending night of Brahma (naimittika pralaya). (Bhagavata 8.13; Vishnu Purana 3.2)

## Where we are, precisely

- **6 Manvantaras complete** (Svayambhuva → Chakshusha) = ~1.84 billion years elapsed this Kalpa, plus their 6 sandhya-junctions.
- **We are in the 7th, Vaivasvata** — and within it, the **28th of its 71 Mahayugas**, in the **Kali Yuga**, year ~5127 (2026 CE).
- **7 Manvantaras remain** (Savarni → Indra-savarni/Bhautya), after which the Day of Brahma ends.
