# Manvantara 8 — Sāvarṇi (Future)

> Where this sits: Brahma's current life → Shveta-Varaha Kalpa → the 14 Manvantaras → the **8th**, the first of the *future* Manus, ruling the age that opens after our own [7th, Vaivasvata](./manvantara-07-vaivasvata/). [Future — not yet begun.]
> Parent: [the-14-manvantaras](README.md) · Up to the master index: [README](../README.md)

The next Manu after Vaivasvata, and the **pivot of the whole second half of the kalpa**: the next six Manus (9th–14th) all bear the name "Sāvarṇi" and are reckoned as kin or namesakes of this one. His manvantara begins only after the present 28-mahayuga Vaivasvata cycle completes — roughly **426,000+ human (solar) years remain** in the current [Kali Yuga](manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) alone (Kali Yuga = 432,000 human years = 1,200 divine years; ~1,185 divine years remain), after which the remaining mahayugas of Vaivasvata must run their course. So this age is genuinely remote; the Purāṇas catalog its officers (Manu, Indra, sages, gods, avatar) as a fixed roster rather than as narrated events. Where the record gives *stories* (chiefly the birth-by-Devī's-boon thread) they are noted below; where it gives only *lists*, the lists are catalogued with their variants.

## The Manu

- **Sāvarṇi Manu (8th Manu)** — son of **Sūrya/Vivasvān** by **Chhāyā** ("Shadow"), the doppelgänger-wife whom **Saṃjñā/Saraṇyū** left behind in her place when she fled the Sun's heat. Because Chhāyā was of "like complexion" to Saṃjñā, her son is **Sāvarṇi** ("of like colour/complexion") — distinguishing him from Saṃjñā's own son **Vaivasvata Manu**, his elder half-brother and our present Manu (Vishnu Purana 3.2; Bhagavata Purana 8.13.1). Also titled **Sūrya-Sāvarṇi** in some lists.
  - *Birth & boon thread [scholarly/late text]:* In the **Devī Māhātmya** (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa 81–93, the *Durgā Saptaśatī*) and the **Devī Bhāgavata**, the future Sāvarṇi is identified with **King Suratha**, who in a past life lost his kingdom, retired to a forest hermitage, and — alongside the merchant **Samādhi** — worshipped the Goddess **Mahāmāyā/Durgā**. By Her boon Suratha is reborn as **Sāvarṇi, son of Sūrya**, to become Manu of the eighth age. This is the framing narrative for which the entire *Devī Māhātmya* is recited (Mārkaṇḍeya Purana; cf. [../../04-deep-dives/markandeya.md](../04-deep-dives/markandeya.md) for the Mārkaṇḍeya frame-story tradition).
  - *Note on the six "Sāvarṇi" successors:* The chief Sāvarṇi here is the **Sūrya-/Sun-Sāvarṇi**; the next several Manus are also "Sāvarṇi" types — [Dakṣa-Sāvarṇi (9th)](manvantara-09-daksha-savarni.md), [Brahma-Sāvarṇi (10th)](manvantara-10-brahma-savarni.md), [Dharma-Sāvarṇi (11th)](manvantara-11-dharma-savarni.md), [Rudra-Sāvarṇi (12th)](manvantara-12-rudra-savarni.md), [Deva-Sāvarṇi (13th)](manvantara-13-deva-savarni.md), and [Indra-Sāvarṇi (14th)](manvantara-14-indra-savarni.md) — each named for the deity who fathered or favoured him (Bhagavata Purana 8.13).

## Indra of the Age

- **Bali becomes Indra** — the supreme event marking this manvantara. The **dāitya king Bali**, sinless and supremely generous son of **Virochana** (grandson of [Prahlāda](../../04-deep-dives/) ), was pushed down to **Pātāla/Sutala** in our present age by Vishnu's [Vāmana](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) avatar, who in three strides reclaimed the three worlds Bali had won. As recompense for Bali's unbroken truthfulness and devotion, Vishnu granted that **in the Sāvarṇi manvantara Bali will ascend to the throne of Indra**, king of the devas (Vishnu Purana 3.2; Bhagavata Purana 8.13.15; the boon is given at Bhagavata 8.22–23, the [Vāmana](../../04-deep-dives/) episode). Bali is himself a **chiranjivi** — kept alive across the ages precisely so he may take up Indra-hood when this age dawns.

## Saptarishis (Seven Sages)

The seven sages of the Sāvarṇi age are unusually loaded with **chiranjivis** ("long-lived" immortals carried over from our own age) — fittingly, since the entire roster of the second half of the kalpa is staffed in advance. The lists vary by source:

- *Vishnu Purana 3.2 / Bhagavata Purana 8.13:* **Dīptimat (Dīptimān)**, **Gālava**, **Paraśurāma** (Rāma Jāmadagnya), **Kṛpa** (Kṛpācārya), **Drauṇi / Aśvatthāmā** (son of Droṇa), **Vyāsa** (Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana / "Bādarāyaṇa"), and **Ṛṣyaśṛṅga**.
  - *Variant (Wikipedia composite, after Bhagavata):* the same seven given as **Gālava, Dīptimān, Aśvatthāmā, Kṛpa, Ṛṣyaśṛṅga, "Vadrāyaṇa" (= Bādarāyaṇa/Vyāsa), and Paraśurāma** — "Vadrāyaṇa" being a corruption of Bādarāyaṇa, an epithet of Vyāsa.
  - *Chiranjivi overlap:* at least **four of the seven — Paraśurāma, Aśvatthāmā, Kṛpa, and Vyāsa — are among the canonical eight cirañjīvis** (see [../../04-deep-dives/the-chiranjivis.md](../04-deep-dives/the-chiranjivis.md)); **Ṛṣyaśṛṅga** and **Gālava** are also extremely long-lived sages, so the council is essentially a gathering of immortals. This is the deepest concentration of chiranjivis on any single saptarishi roster.
- *Variant (Vishnu Purana, alternate "future Manu" list sometimes read here):* **Nirmoha, Tattvadarśin, Niṣprakampa, Nirutsuka, Dhṛtimat, Avyaya, and Sutapā** [disputed] — this set appears in some manuscript traditions but is more securely the saptarishi-list of a *later* Sāvarṇi manvantara (Brahma-Sāvarṇi); editors disagree on which Manu it belongs to.

## Gods (Deva Classes)

- **Three classes of gods, twenty-one in each:** the **Sutapās**, the **Amitābhas**, and the **Mukhyas** (Vishnu Purana 3.2; "twenty-one of each" per the standard Wilson translation — distinct from the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa's "twenty-fold group," a different text).
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana 8.13):* the demigods of this age are likewise grouped as **Sutapās, Amitābhas (Vāmas in some readings), and Mukhyas**, each a host of twenty.
  - *Observation [scholarly]:* "Sutapā" doubles as both a deva-class name here and a saptarishi name in the alternate Vishnu Purana list above — a sign the Purāṇic compilers reused a small stock of stereotyped names across the future manvantaras; treat overlaps as textual rather than substantive.

## Sons of Sāvarṇi (Heirs / Kings)

The princes through whom the royal line of the age descends:

- *Bhagavata Purana 8.13:* headed by **Nirmoka** and **Virajaska (Virojaksha)**, "and others."
  - *Variant (Wikipedia, after Bhagavata infobox):* **Nirmoka, Suṣeṇa, Virojakṣa**.
- *Variant (Harivamsha, ch. 7 — ten named sons):* **Varīyān, Avarīyān, Sammala (Saṃmati), Dhṛtimān, Vasu, Variṣṇa, Ārya, Dhṛṣṇu, Rāja, and Sumati.**
- *Note [disputed]:* the name **Nirmoka** also appears in some Vishnu Purana manuscripts as one of the *sages* of this age and as a son simultaneously — another instance of name-reuse across the future-Manu lists; the better-attested reading makes Nirmoka a son.

## Avatar of the Age

- **Sārvabhauma ("Sovereign of all the earth")** — in the eighth manvantara Vishnu incarnates as **Sārvabhauma**, son of **Devaguhya** (father) and **Sarasvatī** (mother). His defining act: he **wrests Svarga (heaven and the office of Indra) from the then-reigning Indra Purandara** — i.e. the present Indra — **and bestows it upon Bali Mahārāja**, fulfilling the Vāmana-boon (Bhagavata Purana 8.13.15–16).
  - *Function:* Sārvabhauma is thus the *enthroner* of Bali; the avatar of this age exists chiefly to transfer Indra-hood, mirroring how Vāmana once *removed* it from Bali. The age is bracketed by two Vishnu avatars working in opposite directions on the same king.
  - *Note [late text]:* Sārvabhauma is one of the lesser-known **future avatars** of Vishnu; he is not part of the popular [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) list but belongs to the fuller *manvantara-avatāra* sequence (one Vishnu-incarnation per age) enumerated in Bhagavata 8.

## What the Record Does and Doesn't Give

- **Honest note on sparseness:** Unlike the present [Vaivasvata](./manvantara-07-vaivasvata/) age — which carries the entire weight of the epics and most Purāṇic narrative — the Sāvarṇi manvantara is delivered almost entirely as an *officer-roster*: Manu, Indra, seven sages, deva-classes, sons, avatar. The one genuine *story* attached to it is the **Suratha → Sāvarṇi rebirth frame of the Devī Māhātmya**, which is really a story of *our* age explaining how the future Manu earns his post. Everything else is prophecy-as-catalogue, and the variant lists above exhaust what the major texts record.
