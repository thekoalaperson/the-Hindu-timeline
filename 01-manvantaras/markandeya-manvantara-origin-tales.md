# Markandeya's manvantara-origin legends — Uttama's banished queen, Svarāṣṭra/Tāmasa, Ṛtavāc and Revatī

> Parent: [The 14 Manvantaras](README.md). Cosmic address: Śveta-Vārāha Kalpa → the **3rd, 4th and 5th of the 14 Manvantaras** (Auttama, Tāmasa, Raivata) — all long past, all preceding our current [7th (Vaivasvata)](manvantara-07-vaivasvata/). The sibling office-holder pages ([3rd](manvantara-03-uttama.md) · [4th](manvantara-04-tamasa.md) · [5th](manvantara-05-raivata.md)) carry only roll-call lists; this file supplies the **origin narratives** the corpus was otherwise missing, told almost uniquely by the **[Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/markandeya-purana.md)** in F.E. Pargiter's translation.

Unlike the Vishnu/Bhagavata streams — which reduce Manus 3–5 to bare name-lists and derive them all from Priyavrata — the **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa (Cantos LXIX–LXXVI)** gives each of these three Manus a full birth-legend, staged in the frame-dialogue of the Wise Birds. In every case a **cursed woman, an exiled or wronged king, and a Manu-son** are the moving parts. These are among the very few surviving *how a Manu came to be born* stories, and they do not agree with the Priyavrata-brotherhood genealogy of the other Purāṇas. **Dating (two clocks):** cyclically these reigns lie millions of years before the present Kali Yuga (which began 3102 BCE); historically the Mārkaṇḍeya's Manvantara section is [scholarly] among the older Purāṇic strata, its core c. 3rd–6th century CE.

## Auttama Manvantara — King Uttama's banished queen (3rd Manu)

The birth-legend of the **3rd Manu, Auttama**, is told across four cantos as the story of his father, **King Uttama**. It braids two abduction-recoveries — a brahman's shrewish wife and the king's own banished queen — into a redemption that earns Uttama a Manu for a son.

- **King Uttama and the unloving queen Bahulā** — Uttama, son of **Uttānapāda** and **Surucī** (and thus of the [Dhruva](../04-deep-dives/dhruva.md) household of the Svāyambhuva line), is devoted to his queen **Bahulā**, "of Babhru's race," who persistently spurns his affection. When she refuses even to take a drinking-cup from him at a royal gathering, the enraged king orders his door-keeper to abandon her, alone, in the forest — then rules on, bereft. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXIX)
- **The brahman's abducted wife** — a brahman comes to Uttama: his wife was carried off in the night, and he demands the king's help to recover *her specifically*, refusing the king's offer of a replacement, on the dharma that a man's own wife and offspring cannot be substituted. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXIX)
- **The rishi's rebuke** — searching, king and brahman reach a hermit-sage (*muni*) who pointedly withholds the guest-welcome from Uttama and rebukes him: by abandoning his wife in the forest, "thou didst abandon all thy righteousness." The rishi discloses that a **Rākṣasa named Valāka** (Balāka) holds the brahman's wife in the **Utpalāvataka** forest. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXX)
- **The Rākṣasa who ate her bad temper** — recovered, the brahman's wife proves as ill-tempered as before — except the Rākṣasa Valāka, it emerges, had **consumed the evil/ill-nature** out of her; the sage-brahman then works a rite to change her disposition so she may return transformed. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXX–LXXI) [The shrew-cure is the mirror-image of Bahulā's own coldness — the two women's tempers are the tale's paired knots.]
- **The Mitravindā sacrifice, sevenfold** — to mend the estranged royal couple, the brahman performs the **Mitravindā** sacrifice **seven times** to generate mutual affection between Uttama and the recovered Bahulā. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXII)
- **Bahulā found in the serpent-world** — the queen, it turns out, had been carried down to **Pātāla / Rasātala**, the [nāga](../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md) underworld, where a **Nāga king** sheltered her; her companion there is the Nāga princess **Nandā**, and the sage **Garga** dwells in that Rasātala. A devoted **Rākṣasa** retrieves Bahulā from Pātāla at the brahman's bidding. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXII)
- **The mute Nāga princess and the boon of a Manu** — Nandā had been cursed to **muteness** by her own father the Nāga king; the brahman's sacrifice to **Sarasvatī** restores her speech. In gratitude she blesses King Uttama with a **son who shall be a Manu**, master of the sciences and of righteousness. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXII)
- **Birth of Auttama, the 3rd Manu** — accordingly a son is born to Uttama and Bahulā who becomes **Manu Auttama** (Uttama), presiding sovereign of the 3rd Manvantara; his nativity is marked by heavenly drums and a rain of flowers. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXII)
  - *Variant (genealogical, Vishnu/Bhagavata streams):* these Purāṇas ignore the Uttama-legend entirely and make Auttama simply a **son of Priyavrata**, brother of Tāmasa and Raivata — see the office-holder [3rd Manvantara page](manvantara-03-uttama.md). The Mārkaṇḍeya's descent-from-King-Uttama and the Priyavrata-brotherhood are irreconcilable; catalog both. [disputed]

## Tāmasa Manvantara — Svarāṣṭra's exile and the doe-queen (4th Manu)

The **4th Manu, Tāmasa**, is born of a doe in the Mārkaṇḍeya's telling — the reincarnated, cursed queen of a dethroned king. This origin has nothing to do with the "born from darkness (*tamas*)" etymology the other Purāṇas assign; here the name is glossed from the *animal* birth and the flood-darkness of the hour.

- **King Svarāṣṭra dethroned** — **Svarāṣṭra** (Svarāṣṭra), a righteous king, loses his wives and his kingdom to the rival king **Vimarda**, and withdraws to the forest as an ascetic performing severe penance. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXIII–LXXIV)
- **The doe who was his queen** — amid a catastrophic **flood** he meets a **doe** who reveals she was his beloved deceased queen **Utpalāvatī**, now in deer-shape by the curse of the sage **Sutapas** (son of Nirvṛti-cakṣus): in her youth she had disrupted the sage's union with a doe, and was cursed to become a doe and there bring forth a son destined to be a Manu. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXIV)
  - *Variant (secondary retellings, e.g. Kāmakoṭi / popular summaries):* name the former queen **Mahiṣī** and give the boy the pet-name **Lola** before he is called Tāmasa; some versions double the deer-birth across "two successive births." These paraphrase the same Pargiter narrative and are not independent traditions. [folk] [late text]
- **Conception in the flood, the doe's release** — the king's touch during the deluge quickens the embryo in the doe; at the birth all creatures rejoice, and **the doe is freed from the curse** and attains the sublime worlds. The *ṛṣis* name the child **Tāmasa** — "born in darkness" — for both his mother's beast-condition and the darkness at his birth. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXIV) [The *muni* **Siddhavīrya** figures among the sages who attend/name him.]
- **Tāmasa conquers the earth** — grown to manhood, Tāmasa masters celestial weapons, defeats his father Svarāṣṭra's enemies, and wins the whole earth as **Manu Tāmasa**. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXIV)
- **The Tāmasa office-holders (Mārkaṇḍeya recension)** — sons **Nara, Kṣānti, Śānta, Dānta, Jānu, Jaṅgha**; the four deva-classes (27 each) **Satyas, Sudhīs, Surūpas, Haris**; the **Indra Śikhi**; and the seven ṛṣis **Jyotirdhāman, Pṛthu, Kāvya, Caitra, Agni, Valaka, Pīvara**. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXIV) — cross-check the variant rosters on the [4th Manvantara page](manvantara-04-tamasa.md).
  - *Note on the sixth ṛṣi:* the Mārkaṇḍeya's **Valaka/Balāka** is the same slot the Vishnu Purāṇa fills with **Vanaka** — the one wobbling name in an otherwise stable Tāmasa sage-list.

## Raivata Manvantara — Ṛtavāc's curse and the fallen Revatī (5th Manu)

The **5th Manu, Raivata**, is born of a maiden who *is* the Revatī constellation, pulled out of the sky by an angry father's curse. This is the Mārkaṇḍeya's own aetiology for **Mount Raivataka** (Girnar) and is entirely distinct from the famous Kakudmi–Revatī time-dilation tale of the Bhāgavata (see disambiguation below).

- **Ṛtavāc's ill-starred son** — the ṛṣi **Ṛtavāc** (Ṛtavāk) has a son **born under the nakṣatra Revatī** (the 27th/last asterism) who turns out wicked and tormenting, afflicting his parents with disease and misery. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXV)
- **The curse that pulled a star from the sky** — blaming the birth-star, the grief-maddened Ṛtavāc **curses the constellation Revatī to fall from heaven**. It descends upon **Mount Kumuda**, whose woods, ravines and cascades it suddenly floods with light; by this fall the mountain is renamed **Raivataka**, thenceforth famous across the earth. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXV) [This is a rare Purāṇic "a nakṣatra came down and became a place" aetiology — see [nakṣatras](../00-time-and-cosmology/nakshatras.md) and [Girnar / Raivataka](../06-sacred-geography/girnar-raivataka-datta-tirtha.md).]
- **The maiden Revatī, fostered by Pramuca** — from the fallen star's radiance a luminous **girl** arises; the ṛṣi **Pramuca**, whose hermitage stands on the mountain, adopts and rears her, naming her **Revatī**. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXV)
- **Agni names the bridegroom** — grown beautiful, Revatī needs a husband; Pramuca consults **Agni**, who names **King Durgama**, of **Priyavrata's** lineage, as her destined spouse. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXV)
- **The star restored for the wedding** — Durgama, arriving on a hunt, is recognized by Pramuca as the foretold son-in-law. Revatī asks to be wed **under the Revatī nakṣatra**, so by his austerities Pramuca **restores the fallen constellation to the moon's path** to make the marriage-star possible. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXV)
- **Birth of Raivata, the 5th Manu** — as his wedding-boon Durgama asks for a **son who shall rule a Manvantara**; the couple's child becomes **Manu Raivata** — virtuous, Veda-learned, wise in dharma — and the era takes its name, like the mountain, from the fallen **Revatī**. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXV–LXXVI)
- **The Raivata office-holders (Mārkaṇḍeya recension)** — deva-class the **Sumedhas(es)**; the **Indra Vibhu** (performer of a hundred sacrifices); the seven ṛṣis **Hiraṇyaroman, Vedaśrī, Ūrdhvabāhu, Vedabāhu, Sudhāman, Parjanya, Vasiṣṭha**; kings **Vaikunta** and **Amitābha** (fourteen each). (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* LXXV) — compare the [5th Manvantara page](manvantara-05-raivata.md).

## Disambiguations & name-clashes

- **Two different Revatīs (do not merge).** The Mārkaṇḍeya's Revatī is a **star-born foundling**, fostered by Pramuca, wed to King Durgama, and **mother of Raivata Manu**. The Bhāgavata/Vishnu Revatī is the **daughter of King Kakudmi(-Raivata)** who marries **Balarāma** after the Brahmaloka time-dilation, in the current Vaivasvata Manvantara — a different woman, a different era, treated on the [5th Manvantara page](manvantara-05-raivata.md). Both attach the *name Raivata* to Mount **Raivataka** (Girnar), which is the root of the confusion, but the persons are unrelated. [disputed] [scholarly]
- **Two "Uttama" figures.** King **Uttama** (father, of the Uttānapāda/[Dhruva](../04-deep-dives/dhruva.md) household) is distinct from his son **Auttama/Uttama Manu** (the 3rd Manu). Both spellings recur; keep father and Manu-son separate.
- **Two "Utpala-" names, one manvantara apart.** The brahman's wife is held in the **Utpalāvataka** *forest* (Auttama tale, Canto LXX); the doe-queen is named **Utpalāvatī** (Tāmasa tale, Canto LXXIV). An echo, not the same referent.
- **Mārkaṇḍeya vs. the Priyavrata genealogy.** The Vishnu and Bhāgavata Purāṇas make Manus 3–5 **brothers, all sons of Priyavrata**; the Mārkaṇḍeya gives each an independent birth-legend (King Uttama's son; the doe's son; the star-maiden's son). These frameworks are mutually exclusive and are preserved side by side, not harmonized. [disputed]

## Sources

**Primary (scriptural).** All three legends are from the **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa**, in F.E. Pargiter's translation (1904), within the "Exposition of the Manvantaras":
- *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* **Cantos LXIX–LXXII** — the Auttama Manvantara: King Uttama, Queen Bahulā, the brahman's abducted wife, the Rākṣasa Valāka, the sevenfold Mitravindā sacrifice, Bahulā in Pātāla, the mute Nāga princess Nandā, and the birth of Manu Auttama.
- *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* **Cantos LXXIII–LXXIV** — the Tāmasa Manvantara: King Svarāṣṭra vs. Vimarda, the doe-queen Utpalāvatī, the curse of Sutapas, the flood-conception, and the birth of Manu Tāmasa (sons, deva-classes, Indra Śikhi, seven ṛṣis).
- *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* **Cantos LXXV–LXXVI** — the Raivata Manvantara: Ṛtavāc's curse, the fall of the Revatī nakṣatra onto Mount Kumuda (renamed Raivataka), the maiden Revatī fostered by Pramuca, Agni's naming of King Durgama, the star restored, and the birth of Manu Raivata.
