# Mahākālavana and the release of Brahmā's skull — the Avantya opening cycle

> How **Avantikā/Ujjain** charters its own supreme sanctity: the *Avantikṣetra-māhātmya* — the opening cycle of the **Skanda Purāṇa's Avantya-khaṇḍa** — tells its *own* version of the **Brahmā-decapitation** myth, in which Śiva (as **Kāla-Bhairava**), condemned to carry the skull of the severed head as a beggar's bowl and hounded by the demoness **Brahmahatyā**, wanders the earth until the **kapāla finally drops** at the forest of **Mahākāla** on the **Kṣiprā** — the *Kapālamocana* ("release of the skull") that makes this the ground where **Mahākāla stays forever**. The Avanti claim on a myth that Kāśī and Kurukṣetra also claim.

This node isolates **one story** that the [main Ujjain node](ujjain-avantika.md) does not carry: the *Avantikṣetra-māhātmya's* internal charter-myth, the **Brahmā-kapāla / Kapālamocana** cycle by which the forest of Mahākāla is made the holiest of grounds. It is distinct from the [Dūṣaṇa–Candrasena origin legend](ujjain-avantika.md#i-mahākāleśvara--the-south-facing-jyotirliṅga) of the jyotirliṅga (which answers "why is Mahākāla *here*?") — the kapāla cycle answers a larger question, "why is **this whole kṣetra** supreme, freeing even the Great God from the gravest of sins?" (*Skanda Purāṇa, Avantya-khaṇḍa, Avantikṣetra-māhātmya*, chs. 1–7)

---

## I. The frame — the *Avantikṣetra-māhātmya* opens the Avantya-khaṇḍa

- **The setting of the tale** — the **Avantya-khaṇḍa** (Book V of the *Skanda Purāṇa* in the standard Veṅkaṭeśvara arrangement) opens with the **Avantikṣetra-māhātmya**, framed as **Sūta** reciting to the sages of the Naimiṣa forest the greatness of **Avantī / Mahākāla-vana**. The māhātmya's earliest chapters establish *why* this forest outranks other tīrthas — and its answer is the kapāla-cycle. (*Avantya-khaṇḍa, Avantikṣetra-māhātmya*, ch. 1)
- **Mahākāla-vana as the eternal ground** — the māhātmya's premise is that the **forest of Mahākāla** is *nitya* (eternal), untouched by *pralaya* like [Kāśī on the triśūla](kashi-varanasi.md); the tale that follows is the demonstration of that premise — the God of gods himself is purified *here*. (*Avantikṣetra-māhātmya*, chs. 1–2) — cf. [Kalpa & Pralaya](../00-time-and-cosmology/kalpa-and-pralaya.md)
- **A localized version of a pan-Śaiva myth** — the Brahmā-decapitation (the origin of **Kāla-Bhairava**) is told across the *Śiva Purāṇa*, *Skanda Purāṇa* recensions, the *Kāśī-khaṇḍa*, and the *Vāmana* / *Matsya* / *Kūrma* Purāṇas; each pilgrimage-text bends the *ending* — the place where the skull falls — toward its own kṣetra. The Avantya-khaṇḍa is the recension that **brings the release to Mahākāla-vana**. [scholarly]

---

## II. The severing of Brahmā's fifth head

- **The offence of the fifth head** — the myth's engine is the pride of **Brahmā's fifth head**. In the mainstream Śaiva telling the four-faced creator grows a **fifth face** and with it speaks arrogantly, claiming lordship over Śiva (or lies about having seen the top of the infinite **liṅga of light**); the fifth mouth utters what the other four would not. Śiva, insulted, brings forth his punishing form. (*Śiva Purāṇa, Śatarudra-saṃhitā* / *Vidyeśvara-saṃhitā*; *Avantikṣetra-māhātmya*, ch. 2)
  - *Variant (the offence):* other recensions make the sin **lust, not pride** — the fifth head gazes incestuously on Brahmā's own daughter-consort (**Sandhyā / Śatarūpā / Sarasvatī**), the same transgression for which [Rudra pierces Prajāpati with an arrow](../04-deep-dives/prajapati-ushas-rudra-piercing.md) in the Vedic myth; here the arrow becomes a beheading. The Avantya text foregrounds Brahmā's **arrogance before Śiva**; the daughter-motif is the older stratum. [disputed]
- **The birth of Kāla-Bhairava** — from Śiva's wrath (from his brow, or from a drop of his anger) springs **Bhairava** — the terrible, black, dog-accompanied form, **Kāla-Bhairava** ("the Bhairava who is Time/Death"). It is *Bhairava*, not the placid Śiva, who does the deed; the God offloads the violence onto his own emanation. (*Śiva Purāṇa*; *Avantikṣetra-māhātmya*, ch. 2) — full form-node: [Vaṭuka/Kāla-Bhairava origin](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vatuka-bhairava-origin.md)
- **The nail that severs the head** — Bhairava shears off the offending **fifth head** with the **tip of his left thumbnail** (*nakha*) — the standard iconographic detail — leaving Brahmā **four-faced** as he is worshipped ever after. The creator survives; only the proud head is lost. (*Śiva Purāṇa*; iconographic tradition)

## III. The Kapāla-vrata — the skull that will not fall

- **The sin of Brahmahatyā clings** — to slay Brahmā, the **creator and supreme brāhmaṇa**, is the archetypal **Brahmahatyā** (brahminicide), gravest of the *mahāpātakas* (great sins). Its consequence fastens onto Bhairava physically: the **severed skull (*kapāla*) sticks fast to his palm** and cannot be shaken off — the sin made visible, welded to the hand that held the blade. (*Avantikṣetra-māhātmya*, ch. 3; *Kāśī-khaṇḍa*) — cf. [Pāpa, puṇya & prāyaścitta](../05-concepts-and-dharma/papa-punya-and-prayaschitta.md)
- **The Kāpālika vow imposed** — even the Great God must **expiate**. Śiva/Bhairava is bound to the **Kapāla-vrata (Mahāvrata)**: to wander as a naked **Kāpālika beggar**, using the clinging skull as his **alms-bowl**, subsisting on what falls into it, until the penance is complete. This is the mythic charter of the **Bhikṣāṭana** ("the wandering-for-alms") form of Śiva and of the whole **Kāpālika** ascetic order that imitates it. (*Avantikṣetra-māhātmya*, chs. 3–4) — see [Śiva avatars & forms](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-avatars-and-forms.md)
- **Brahmahatyā personified pursues him** — the sin also takes **female bodily form** — a fearsome **Brahmahatyā** (the murder personified as a *cāṇḍāla* woman / hag) — who **follows Bhairava** everywhere he goes, unable to be outrun, marking him as unclean at every tīrtha he enters. The penance is a chase across the sacred geography of Bhārata. (*Avantikṣetra-māhātmya*, chs. 3–5)
- **Viṣṇu feeds the bowl** — at one station of the wandering, tradition has **Viṣṇu** open a vein of his own brow/hand to pour blood-streams into the never-filling skull — a torrent that flows for ages without sating it — dramatizing that ordinary tīrthas cannot end this sin; only the destined ground can. [late text] [folk]

## IV. The release of the skull at Mahākāla-vana

- **The wandering ends on the Kṣiprā** — carried through countless holy places without relief, Bhairava at last enters the **forest of Mahākāla (Mahākāla-vana)** on the bank of the **Kṣiprā (Śiprā)**. There — and *only* there — the **kapāla loosens and drops** from his hand of its own accord, and the pursuing **Brahmahatyā can go no further** and is destroyed / dissolved at the boundary of the kṣetra. The God is **freed**. (*Avantikṣetra-māhātmya*, ch. 6)
- **Kapālamocana-tīrtha** — the exact spot where the skull fell is enshrined as **Kapālamocana** ("the releasing of the skull") — a **tīrtha within the Mahākāla-kṣetra**, held to wipe out even Brahmahatyā for the pilgrim who bathes and offers there, since it dissolved that very sin for Śiva himself. (*Avantikṣetra-māhātmya*, ch. 6)
- **Why here and nowhere else** — the māhātmya's whole rhetorical purpose lands in this beat: if the **gravest of all sins** (killing the creator) is annulled *here* when every other tīrtha failed, then **Mahākāla-vana is the supreme purifier** — the ground whose sanctity exceeds all others, capable of liberating even the Deity. The kapāla-cycle is the *proof-text* for Avantikā's rank among the [Sapta Purī](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md). (*Avantikṣetra-māhātmya*, chs. 6–7)

## V. The charter — why Mahākāla stays, and Bhairava becomes kotwāl

- **Śiva resolves to remain as Mahākāla** — freed of the skull on this ground, Śiva does not leave: he **abides in the forest as Mahākāla**, "the Great Time," binding his presence to the place that redeemed him. The kapāla-cycle thus supplies a *second* etiology for the [Mahākāleśvara jyotirliṅga](12-jyotirlingas.md) — Śiva stays because here his own worst sin was undone, complementing the [Dūṣaṇa/Candrasena legend](ujjain-avantika.md#i-mahākāleśvara--the-south-facing-jyotirliṅga). (*Avantikṣetra-māhātmya*, chs. 6–7)
  - *Variant (which legend seats Mahākāla):* the two charter-myths sit side by side in the Avanti corpus and are **not harmonized** — one has Mahākāla *burst forth* to kill Dūṣaṇa and stay; the other has him *arrive burdened* and stay once freed. Both are recorded; neither is treated as the "real" reason. [scholarly]
- **Kāla-Bhairava as the city's magistrate** — because it was **Bhairava** who was freed here, the fierce form is enshrined as the **kotwāl (police-chief) of Avantikā**, chief of the **Aṣṭa-Bhairavas** guarding the city — the same office he holds at [Kāśī](kashi-varanasi.md). The kapāla-cycle is the mythic root of Ujjain's celebrated [Kāl Bhairav shrine and its liquor-offering](ujjain-avantika.md#v-kāla-bhairava--the-kotwāl-and-the-liquor-offering). (*Avantikṣetra-māhātmya*; local tradition)
- **Bhairava-aṣṭamī** — the day of the beheading / Bhairava's manifestation is commemorated as **Kāla-Bhairava-aṣṭamī** (Mārgaśīrṣa kṛṣṇa aṣṭamī), observed pointedly at Ujjain and Kāśī, both of which house the release-story. (festival tradition) [regional]
- **The ash and the skull, one theology** — the kapāla-cycle deepens the city's death-theology already carried by the [Bhasma-āratī](ujjain-avantika.md#ii-the-bhasma-āratī--the-rite-of-cremation-ash): the lord of the kṣetra is he who wore the **skull** and wears the **ash** — Time robed in the remains of what it consumes, purity won *through* the instruments of death, not apart from them.

## VI. Competing Kapālamocana tīrthas — the disambiguation

The skull-fall is a **prize claimed by several kṣetras**; the Timeline catalogs the claimants without merging them. Each pilgrimage-text makes *its* ground the place of release — a classic instance of localized māhātmya rivalry. [scholarly]

- **Avantikā / Ujjain (this node)** — the *Avantya-khaṇḍa's* **Avantikṣetra-māhātmya** brings the release to **Mahākāla-vana**, chartering the city's supremacy. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Avantya-khaṇḍa*, ch. 6)
- **Kāśī / Vārāṇasī** — the *Kāśī-khaṇḍa* has the skull cling to Bhairava until it **drops at the Kapālamocana tīrtha in Kāśī** (near Manikarṇikā / the ghats), one root of **Kāla-Bhairava the kotwāl of Kāśī**. The Timeline's [Kāśī node](kashi-varanasi.md) records only this variant. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Kāśī-khaṇḍa*)
- **Kurukṣetra** — the *Vāmana Purāṇa* and Kurukṣetra māhātmyas place a **Kapālamocana tīrtha on the Sarasvatī** at Kurukṣetra. In the *Mahābhārata, Śalya-parva* tīrtha-yātrā, a **rākṣasa's skull** severed by **Rāma's** arrow coursed through the sky and fastened onto the thigh of the sage **Mahodara**, who was freed of it by bathing at the tīrtha. (*Vāmana Purāṇa*; *Mahābhārata, Śalya-parva* tīrtha-yātrā) — see [Kurukṣetra](kurukshetra-dharmakshetra.md)
  - *Variant (whose skull, Kurukṣetra):* the Kurukṣetra Kapālamocana is variously the skull of a **rākṣasa** slain by **Rāma's** arrow that lodged in the sage **Mahodara** (*Śalya-parva*), *or* the head of the demon **Rāhu** struck by Viṣṇu, *or* the Brahmā-kapāla; the tradition itself keeps more than one occupant for the same tīrtha-name. [disputed]
- **Reading the rivalry** — these are **not contradictions to reconcile** but parallel charters: a widely-shared pan-Śaiva myth (Bhairava + Brahmahatyā + the clinging skull) whose *dénouement* each major kṣetra localizes to license its own claim to annul the ultimate sin. The value is that all the claims are preserved and attributed. [scholarly]

## VII. The two clocks

- **Scripture-clock** — the kapāla-cycle sits in **primordial / cross-yuga time**: the beheading of **Brahmā** is a cosmogonic-era event (the creator is already at work), and the release is presented as a **timeless charter** for a kṣetra that stands *outside* the yuga-turn — Mahākāla-vana is *nitya*, and Mahākāla "has always" abided since the skull fell. Within the standard address (current 7th/**Vaivasvata Manvantara**, 28th Mahāyuga, [Kali from 3102 BCE](../00-time-and-cosmology/units-of-time.md)) the story functions as a **recurring / trans-yuga myth**, not a dated one. (*Avantikṣetra-māhātmya*)
- **Academic-clock** — the **Avantya-khaṇḍa** as a *text* is a **late Purāṇic composition**, its māhātmya layers generally placed **[scholarly] c. 9th–13th century CE**, codifying older Śaiva Bhairava-lore and pilgrimage practice into a systematic charter for Ujjain; the *Kāśī-khaṇḍa* parallel is likewise **[scholarly] c. 13th–14th c. CE**. The Bhairava/Brahmā-kapāla myth-complex itself is older, with roots in the Vedic **Rudra-pierces-Prajāpati** motif and in the historical **Kāpālika / Mahāvrata** ascetic movement (attested by **c. 6th–8th c. CE**), whose central vow the myth mythologizes. [scholarly] [late text]
- **Ujjain the documented city** — the mythic charter overlies a real early-historic capital: **Avantī / Ujjayinī** was a *mahājanapada* capital (Iron Age second-urbanization, **[scholarly] c. 6th c. BCE** onward), which is why the māhātmya invests so heavily in ranking it against Kāśī and Kurukṣetra. [scholarly]
