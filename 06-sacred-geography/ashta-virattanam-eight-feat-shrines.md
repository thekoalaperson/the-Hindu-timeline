# Ashta Virattanam — the eight shrines of Shiva's heroic feats

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](README.md) → **Aṭṭa-vīraṭṭāṉam**. Eight Tamil Shiva temples, each fixed by a *sthala-purāṇa* as the exact spot where **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** performed one of his fierce, cosmos-ordering deeds — the *vīra* ("heroic") *sthānam* ("place"). The deeds themselves belong to primordial deva-time (no yuga date); the shrines that localise them are a recorded **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** devotional geography, all eight sung in the **Tēvāram** and so [Paadal Petra Sthalams](275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md). The network logic sits in [temple-networks-and-circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md).
>
> Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md)

**Aṭṭa-vīraṭṭāṉam** (Tamil *aṭṭa* "eight" < Skt *aṣṭa* + *vīraṭṭāṉam* < *vīra-sthāna*, "place of the heroic feat") is a closed set of eight Śiva temples in the Kāverī delta and northern Tamil country. Where the pan-Indian Purāṇas narrate Śiva's *saṃhāra-mūrti* deeds — the destructions by which he restores dharma — as events of the deva-age set variously at Kailāsa, Vārāṇasī, or "the ocean," the Tamil tradition **grounds each single feat in one named town**, so that the eight fierce acts become eight walkable pilgrimage sites. The presiding liṅga at each is called **Vīraṭṭāṉeśvarar** ("lord of the heroic place"); the set is the Śaiva counterpart, in the register of *ferocity*, to gentler circuits like the [Pancha Bhūta Sthalams](temple-networks-and-circuits.md) or the [Pancha Sabhai dance-halls](pancha-sabhai-nataraja-dance-halls.md). Membership is defined **by legend mapped onto place**, not by a saint's hymn — though all eight happen also to be Tēvāram shrines. [regional]

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic frame** — Every one of the eight deeds is set in **primordial deva-time**, in [Brahmā's current life](../01-manvantaras/README.md) but before the present Manvantara's human history: the Tripura war, the Dakṣa-yajña, the Jalandhara and Andhaka wars are all "outside the yuga clock," undated deva–asura or Śaiva-primacy events. The temples inherit that timelessness in their *sthala-purāṇas*, which claim the site *is* the original ground of the feat. (Source: *Śiva Purāṇa*, Rudra-/Yuddha-saṃhitā; local *sthala-purāṇas*)
- **Academic / historical frame** — The shrines enter the record as a set through the **Tēvāram** hymns of the Nāyanār poet-saints **Appar, Sambandar and Sundarar**, dated **[scholarly] c. 6th–9th c. CE**; all eight are among the 275/276 [Paadal Petra Sthalams](275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md). The collective label *Aṭṭa-vīraṭṭāṉam* and the fixed roster are a **medieval Śaiva harmonisation** — the mapping of eight pan-Purāṇic deeds onto eight delta towns — crystallising with the Tēvāram canon and the *Periya Purāṇam* hagiography (12th c.). (Source: *Tēvāram*; *Periya Purāṇam* [Cēkkiḻār]; see [the-nalvar](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/the-nalvar-tamil-shaiva-saints.md)) [scholarly]

## The eight feats, shrine by shrine

- **1. Tiruvatikai — Tripura-dāhana (the burning of the three cities)** — The town of **Tiruvatikai (Tiruvadigai) Vīraṭṭāṉam**, on the Gadilam near Panruti, marks where Śiva as **Tripurāntaka** loosed the single arrow that incinerated the three flying fortresses of **Tārakākṣa, Vidyunmālī and Kamalākṣa** — the whole pantheon reduced to the parts of his war-chariot. The full deva–asura war is at [tripura-dahana](../04-deep-dives/tripura-dahana.md). The temple is also famous in Nāyanār hagiography as the shrine where **Appar (Tirunāvukkaracar)**, stricken with unbearable colic after leaving Jainism, was cured and reconverted to Śaivism — his first Tēvāram decade is sung here. (Source: *Tēvāram* [Appar]; *Periya Purāṇam*; *sthala-purāṇa*) [regional]

- **2. Pariyalūr — Dakṣa-yajña-saṃhāra (the wrecking of Dakṣa's sacrifice)** — At **Pariyalūr (Thiruppariyalur / Parasalur) Vīraṭṭāṉam** Śiva is held to have destroyed the arrogant fire-sacrifice of the Prajāpati **Dakṣa**, who had insulted Śiva and driven his own daughter **Satī** to immolate herself. In the canonical telling Śiva does not strike directly: from his fury (or from a lock of his matted hair dashed to the ground) springs the terrible **Vīrabhadra**, who beheads Dakṣa and scatters the assembly of gods; Dakṣa is later restored with a goat's head. The reborn Goddess as **Pārvatī** and the whole Satī cycle are the narrative home: [sati-daksha-and-parvati](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md). (Source: *Śiva Purāṇa*, Rudra-saṃhitā, Satī-khaṇḍa; *Bhāgavata* IV.2–7; *sthala-purāṇa*) [regional]

- **3. Tiruvirkuḍi — Jalandhara-vadha (the slaying of Jalandhara)** — **Tiruvirkuḍi Vīraṭṭāṉam** (near Thiruthuraipoondi) is the localised site of Śiva's killing of the asura **Jalandhara** — born of the fire of Śiva's third eye cast into the ocean, made near-invincible by the *pativratya* of his wife **Vṛndā**, and undone only when Viṣṇu breaks her vow by deception. Śiva is said to have decapitated him with the razor-edge of his own **cakra** drawn in the ground with his toe. The full myth, and its charter of the Tulsī plant, are at [jalandhara-vrinda](../04-deep-dives/jalandhara-vrinda.md). (Source: *Śiva Purāṇa*, Rudra-saṃhitā, Yuddha-khaṇḍa; *Padma Purāṇa*; *sthala-purāṇa*) [regional]

- **4. Vaḻuvūr — Gajāsura-saṃhāra (flaying the elephant-demon)** — **Vaḻuvūr Vīraṭṭāṉam** enshrines Śiva as **Gajasaṃhāra-mūrti**: the elephant-demon **Gajāsura** (in the Purāṇas a son of Mahiṣāsura, tormenting devotees at Vārāṇasī) is impaled on the trident and flayed, Śiva dancing the fierce dance within the upheld hide — hence his epithet **Kṛttivāsas**, "clad in the skin." The pan-Indian narrative sets the deed at Kāśī; the *sthala-purāṇa* relocates it to Vaḻuvūr, whose celebrated Cōḻa **Gajasaṃhāra-mūrti bronze** — Śiva with one leg raised high, the flayed elephant-head overhead — is among the masterpieces of the type. See [shiva-myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) §"Gajāsura-saṃhāra." (Source: *Śiva Purāṇa*, Yuddha-saṃhitā; *Liṅga Purāṇa*; *Kūrma Purāṇa*; *sthala-purāṇa*) [regional]

- **5. Kurukkai — Kāma-dahana (the burning of Kāma)** — **Tirukkurukkai Vīraṭṭāṉam** (near Nannilam) marks where Śiva, in deep meditation, opened his **third eye** and burnt the love-god **Kāmadeva** to ash when Kāma fired his flower-arrow to kindle desire for Pārvatī — a scheme of the gods to beget the future Tāraka-slayer **Skanda**. At the pleas of Kāma's widow **Rati**, Śiva later restored him **bodiless (Anaṅga)**, surviving only as felt love; here Śiva is **Kāmāntaka / Kāmāri**. See [shiva-myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) §"Kāmadahana." (Source: *Śiva Purāṇa*, Rudra-saṃhitā; *Matsya Purāṇa*; Kālidāsa, *Kumārasaṃbhava*; *sthala-purāṇa*) [regional]

- **6. Tirukkōvilūr — Andhakāsura-vadha (slaying the blind demon)** — **Tirukkōvilūr Vīraṭṭāṉam**, on the Teṉ Peṇṇai (South Pennar), localises the impaling of **Andhaka** — the "blind one" born of Śiva's own sweat-drop, who lusted after Pārvatī unknowing she was his mother. Śiva holds him aloft on the **triśūla** until his blood is drained and his pride burnt away; the **Saptamātṛkā** drink each falling drop (which else would spawn a clone), and the chastened Andhaka is redeemed as the gaṇa **Bhṛṅgi**. Full account: [andhakasura-vadha](../04-deep-dives/andhakasura-vadha.md). (Source: *Matsya*, *Kūrma*, *Liṅga*, *Śiva Purāṇa*; *sthala-purāṇa*) [regional]

- **7. Kandiyūr — Brahmaśiraś-cheda (severing Brahmā's fifth head)** — At **Tirukkaṇḍiyūr** (near Thiruvaiyaru) Śiva, as fierce **Bhairava** born of his own wrath, shears off the insolent **fifth head of Brahmā** with a fingernail, leaving the creator four-headed — the sin of **brahmahatyā** clinging to him as the skull (*kapāla*) fused to his palm. The presiding deity is **Brahmaśiraḥ-Kaṇḍeśvarar** ("lord who cut off Brahmā's head").
  - *Variant (local expiation):* the pan-Indian myth has the skull finally drop, and the sin expiated, only at **Kāśī** (Kapālamocana tīrtha) — see [shiva-myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) §"Bhairava." The Kandiyūr *sthala-purāṇa* instead has **Viṣṇu** relieve Śiva of the brahmahatyā here; the adjacent **Hara-śāpa-vimocana Perumāḷ** (Viṣṇu) temple commemorates precisely that release. The traditions are kept separate, not merged. (Source: *Śiva Purāṇa*, Śatarudra-saṃhitā; *Skanda Purāṇa*; local *sthala-purāṇa*) [regional] [disputed]

- **8. Tirukkaḍavūr — Kālāntaka (the conquest of Death for Mārkaṇḍeya)** — **Tirukkaḍavūr (Tirukkaḍaiyūr) Amṛtaghaṭeśvarar** temple marks where Śiva burst from the liṅga as **Kālāntaka** ("ender of Death/Time") to save the boy-devotee **Mārkaṇḍeya**, fated to die at sixteen: when **Yama** flung his noose to drag the boy clinging to the liṅga, the noose looped the liṅga itself, and Śiva kicked Death down, granting the boy deathless eternal youth. It is the theological seat of the **Mahā-Mṛtyuñjaya** mantra; the Mārkaṇḍeya connection extends to [markandeya-manvantara-origin-tales](../01-manvantaras/markandeya-manvantara-origin-tales.md). The shrine is today the great venue for **Ṣaṣṭi-abda-pūrti** (60th) and *Bhīma-ratha* (70th/80th) wedding-anniversary rites — worship of the death-conqueror for long life; consort **Abhirāmi** is celebrated in Abhirāmi Bhaṭṭar's *Abhirāmi Antāti*. See [shiva-myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) §"Kālāntaka." (Source: *Śiva Purāṇa*; *Liṅga Purāṇa*; *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* context; *sthala-purāṇa*) [regional]

## Reading the set as a whole

- **The saṃhāra-mūrtis grounded in place** — The eight are, iconographically, Śiva's principal *saṃhāra* (destroyer) and *anugraha* (grace-through-destruction) forms: Tripurāntaka, Gajasaṃhāra, Kālāntaka, Kāmāntaka, Andhakāntaka, Jalandharāntaka, and the Bhairava and Vīrabhadra deeds. The Vīraṭṭāṉam network is the geography that turns this iconographic *class* into a pilgrimage. (Source: Śaiva Āgama / śilpa tradition) [scholarly]
- **Localisation over relocation** — In several cases the pan-Purāṇic setting is elsewhere (Gajāsura at Vārāṇasī; the brahmahatyā expiated at Kāśī). The Tamil *sthala-purāṇas* do not deny those; they claim the *original* ground was the local town — the standard move by which regional temple-legend annexes a universal myth. Where this collides with the canon (as at Kandiyūr) the corpus catalogues both, attributed. [regional] [disputed]
- **Feat vs. saint** — Unlike the Divya Desam or Paadal Petra sets, which are *canonised by a saint singing them*, the Vīraṭṭāṉam set is *canonised by the deed said to have happened there*. That all eight are also Tēvāram shrines is a convergence, not the defining criterion. (Source: [temple-networks-and-circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)) [scholarly]
- **A ninth?** — Devotional lists occasionally float honorary additions or dispute the exact town for Pariyalūr/Parasalur, but the fixed, source-cited set is **eight**. Roster additions are non-canonical. [folk] [flag: open-ended roster]
