# Panchavati — Rāma's Dwelling on the Godāvarī at Nashik

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](README.md) → **Pañcavaṭī / Nāsik**. The forest-home of **Rāma, Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa** in the **Daṇḍaka** wilderness on the **Godāvarī**, identified with modern **Nāsik (Nashik), Maharashtra** — the pivot of the *Araṇya-kāṇḍa* where the exile turns from idyll to catastrophe: the mutilation of **Śūrpaṇakhā**, the slaughter of **Khara–Dūṣaṇa**, **Mārīca's** golden-deer ruse, **Jaṭāyu's** fall, and the **abduction of Sītā** by **Rāvaṇa**. Today a living *tīrtha* and one of the four **[Kumbh Mela](kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md)** cities.
>

## I. Identity, name & the "two clocks"

- **Pañcavaṭī = "the [grove] of five banyans"** — *pañca* ("five") + *vaṭa* ("banyan/fig tree"): a tract of the **Daṇḍaka forest (Daṇḍakāraṇya)** on the south bank of the **Godāvarī**, chosen by Rāma for its water, shade, flowers, fruit and *kuśa* grass as the ideal *parṇa-śālā* site. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 15)
- **Scriptural clock (Tretā-yuga)** — within the *Rāmāyaṇa* frame Pañcavaṭī belongs to the **Tretā-yuga** exile, the **last and most fateful** forest-residence before the abduction sends Rāma south toward Kiṣkindhā and Laṅkā; this is the mythic register of every episode below. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*) — see the **[two-clocks](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md)** scriptural-vs-historical distinction.
- **Historical clock** — as a *living* tīrtha **Nāsik / Pañcavaṭī** is a continuously worshipped landscape elaborated by the **Godāvarī-māhātmya** and **Nāsik sthala-mahātmya**, by medieval temple-building, and by the **Siṃhastha Kumbh** cycle; the present **ghāṭs**, caves and temples are medieval-to-modern overlays on the epic ground. [scholarly]
- **Nāsik = "nose"** — the city-name is popularly derived from *nāsikā* ("nose"), recalling the cutting of **Śūrpaṇakhā's** nose (and ears) here; the **Daṇḍaka** identification places Pañcavaṭī within or just beside the modern city, the river-side suburb still bearing the name **Pañcavaṭī**. [folk etymology / regional]

## II. Agastya directs Rāma to Pañcavaṭī

- **The southern hermitages and Agastya** — moving deeper into **Daṇḍaka**, Rāma visits the *āśrama* of **Agastya's brother** and then of **Agastya** himself, the sage who had tamed the south for human habitation; Agastya gifts Rāma the **bow of Viṣṇu**, an inexhaustible quiver, a sword and arrows of **Indra** and **Brahmā**. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 11–12) — see [Agastya](../04-deep-dives/agastya.md)
- **The choice of Pañcavaṭī** — asked where the party should settle, **Agastya names Pañcavaṭī** on the **Godāvarī**, two *yojanas* off, praising it as level, watered, flower-rich and pleasant for Sītā — the explicit scriptural anchor for the site. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 13)
- **Jaṭāyu on the road** — en route Rāma meets the aged vulture-king **Jaṭāyu**, a friend of his father **Daśaratha**, who pledges to guard **Sītā** while Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa hunt; the bird becomes the watchman of Pañcavaṭī. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 14)
- **The leaf-hut on the Godāvarī** — at Pañcavaṭī **Lakṣmaṇa builds a spacious *parṇa-śālā*** of bamboo and thatch by the river; the three settle into forest-domesticity through the seasons, Rāma describing the Godāvarī, **Hemanta** frosts and lotus-pools to Sītā. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 15–16)

## III. Śūrpaṇakhā and the spark of the war

- **Śūrpaṇakhā's approach** — the *rākṣasī* **Śūrpaṇakhā** (sister of **Rāvaṇa**), wandering Daṇḍaka, sees Rāma, is smitten, and demands him as husband; Rāma deflects her in jest toward Lakṣmaṇa, and Lakṣmaṇa back to Rāma. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 17)
- **The mutilation (nose & ears cut)** — enraged and lunging at **Sītā**, Śūrpaṇakhā is seized by Lakṣmaṇa who, at Rāma's word, **cuts off her nose and ears** — the act that gives **Nāsik** its name and lights the fuse of the whole war. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 18)
  - *Variant (regional sthala-tradition):* local Nāsik lore fixes the mutilation at a specific spot in the Pañcavaṭī tract, the city's very name standing as the topographic memorial of the wound. [regional / folk]

## IV. Khara, Dūṣaṇa and the battle of Janasthāna

- **Khara and Dūṣaṇa roused** — the bleeding Śūrpaṇakhā flees to her brothers **Khara, Dūṣaṇa and Triśiras**, rākṣasa-governors of **Janasthāna** (the Daṇḍaka district around Pañcavaṭī), inciting them to avenge her. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 19–20)
- **The fourteen, then the army** — Khara first sends **fourteen** rākṣasas, all slain by Rāma; he then marches with **fourteen thousand** under Dūṣaṇa and Triśiras against the lone archer. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 20–22)
- **Rāma's solitary victory** — sending Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa to a cave, **Rāma alone destroys the whole host**, killing **Dūṣaṇa**, **Triśiras** and finally **Khara**, clearing Janasthāna of its rākṣasa garrison. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 23–30)
- **Akampana's warning to Rāvaṇa** — the survivor **Akampana** (and the fleeing Śūrpaṇakhā) carry word of the slaughter to **Laṅkā**, goading **Rāvaṇa** — and steering him toward abduction rather than open battle. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 31–34)

## V. Mārīca's golden deer and the abduction of Sītā

- **The Rāvaṇa–Mārīca compact** — **Rāvaṇa** seeks out **Mārīca**, who twice warns him of Rāma's power; forced on pain of death, Mārīca agrees to become the lure. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 35–40)
- **The golden deer (māyā-mṛga)** — **Mārīca** takes the form of a **jewelled golden deer** that grazes before the hut; **Sītā**, enchanted, begs Rāma to capture it, and Rāma pursues, posting Lakṣmaṇa to guard her. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 42–43)
- **The dying cry & the Lakṣmaṇa-rekhā** — struck by Rāma's arrow, **Mārīca** cries out *"Hā Sītā! Hā Lakṣmaṇa!"* in Rāma's voice; Sītā, fearing for Rāma, drives Lakṣmaṇa to leave her against his will. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 44–45)
  - *Variant (the Lakṣmaṇa-rekhā):* the protective line Lakṣmaṇa draws around the hut, which Sītā crosses, is **absent from Vālmīki** and belongs to later retellings (Rāmcaritmānas-stream and regional Rāmāyaṇas / folk drama). (*Rāmcaritmānas* tradition) [late text / folk]
- **Rāvaṇa as mendicant** — disguised as a wandering **sannyāsin**, Rāvaṇa approaches the undefended Sītā, then reveals himself and seizes her, carrying her off in his aerial chariot (*puṣpaka*). (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 46–49)

## VI. Jaṭāyu's fall

- **The vulture's stand** — the old eagle-king **Jaṭāyu**, hearing Sītā's cries, **attacks Rāvaṇa in mid-air**, wrecking his chariot and bow in a fierce duel to defend her. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 50–51)
- **The severed wings** — Rāvaṇa **cuts off Jaṭāyu's wings** and the bird falls; he survives only to deliver his dying testimony — the direction of Sītā's flight — to Rāma, who cremates him with a son's rites. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 51; 67–68)
  - *Variant (Sarvatīrtha, regional):* Nāsik-district tradition localises the fall of **Jaṭāyu** and his cremation by Rāma at **Sarvatīrtha / Ṭākeḍ**, some distance from Pañcavaṭī, where a Jaṭāyu shrine stands. [regional / folk]

## VII. Pañcavaṭī & Nāsik as a living tīrtha

- **Sītā Guphā (Sītā's cave)** — a cramped rock-cut cave-shrine in the Pañcavaṭī quarter, entered by a narrow stair, held to be where **Sītā** hid (or was kept) during the Pañcavaṭī residence; it adjoins the **five banyans** said to mark the original grove. (Nāsik sthala-tradition) [regional]
- **Kālārām Mandir ("black-Rāma temple")** — the great **18th-century** (rebuilt ~1788 CE under the **Peśvās / Sardār Raṅgārāo Oḍhekar**) temple of black-stone **Rāma, Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa**, the devotional heart of Pañcavaṭī; site of **Dr. B. R. Ambedkar's 1930 satyāgraha** for temple entry. (sthala-tradition; historical record) [historical]
- **Tapovan** — the riverside grove downstream where, by local tradition, the *tapas*-residence of the exiles and the **Śūrpaṇakhā / Lakṣmaṇa** episode are seated; a cluster of small shrines on the Godāvarī. (Nāsik sthala-tradition) [regional]
- **Rāmkuṇḍ ("Rāma's pool")** — the principal bathing-tank on the **Godāvarī** in Pañcavaṭī, where Rāma is said to have bathed and performed his father's *śrāddha*; the central **asthi-vilaya** ("bone-dissolving") tīrtha of Nāsik for immersing ashes, and the focus of the Kumbh bathing. (Godāvarī-māhātmya; sthala-tradition)
- **Kapāleśvara & the river-temples** — Pañcavaṭī's Godāvarī banks carry **Kapāleśvara Mahādeva**, **Sundarnārāyaṇa**, **Nārośaṅkar** and other shrines, knitting the Rāma-tīrtha into a dense Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava sacred quarter. (sthala-tradition) [regional]

## VIII. Godāvarī-māhātmya & the Kumbh

- **Source of the Godāvarī** — the river rises at **Tryambakeśvar**, ~30 km from Nāsik, where the **[Tryambakeśvar Jyotirliṅga](12-jyotirlingas.md)** and the **Brahmagiri** mark the spring; the **Godāvarī-māhātmya** (Brahma Purāṇa stream) exalts the river as the **Dakṣiṇa Gaṅgā ("Gaṅgā of the south")** / **Gautamī**, brought down by sage **Gautama's** *tapas*. (*Brahma Purāṇa, Gautamī-māhātmya*) — see [Godāvarī / Gautamī — Dakṣiṇa Gaṅgā](godavari-gautami-dakshina-ganga.md)
- **The Siṃhastha Kumbh of Nāsik–Tryambak** — Nāsik is one of the **four Kumbh Mela** cities; its **Siṃhastha** fair falls when **Jupiter (Bṛhaspati) enters Siṃha (Leo)**, roughly every **twelve years**, with bathing split between **Rāmkuṇḍ (Nāsik)** and **Kuśāvarta (Tryambak)** by sectarian custom (Vaiṣṇava akhāḍās at Rāmkuṇḍ, Śaiva at Tryambak). (pilgrimage tradition) — see [Kumbh Mela & Pilgrimage Cycles](kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md)
- **Standing in the Rāma-network** — though not a Sapta-Purī, **Pañcavaṭī / Nāsik** ranks among the foremost **Rāma-tīrthas**, paired in pilgrim imagination with [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md), [Chitrakūṭa](chitrakoot-kamadgiri.md), [Prayāga](prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md) and [Rāmeśvaram](rameswaram-setu-bandha.md) as the **abduction-station** of the exile, the hinge between the forest-idyll and the southern war. [scholarly]
