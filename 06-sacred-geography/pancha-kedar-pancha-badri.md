# Pañcha Kedār & Pañcha Badrī — the Garhwal Twin Circuits

> Two interlocked Himalayan pilgrimage circuits of the Garhwal region (Uttarakhand), threaded along the Mandākinī and Alaknanda valleys: the **Pañcha Kedār** — five Shiva shrines marking the dismembered body of the bull-form Shiva — and the **Pañcha Badrī** — five Vishnu shrines clustered around Badrinath. The two share geography, a Pāṇḍava sub-text, and a closing devotional logic: the Kedār pilgrim ends by affirming at Badrinath.

Both circuits sit inside the **Kedār-khaṇḍa** and **Badari-kṣetra** as defined in the Skanda Purāṇa's Himalayan geography. They are not "small four" (*Chota Char Dhām*) sets but parallel **fives** (*pañchaka*) — a recurring Garhwal pattern of distributing a single deity's presence across five trekking-distance peaks. Neither "five" is perfectly fixed: alternate members circulate (Ardha Badri, Dhyan Badri, Narsingh Badri), which this file catalogs in full.

## I. Pañcha Kedār — the Five Shiva Shrines

The five temples enshrine the five **body-parts of the bull-form Shiva** (Nandi-bull / *vṛṣabha-rūpa*) who plunged into the earth at Guptakāśī and re-surfaced piecemeal across Garhwal. The fixed worship order runs **Kedarnath → Tungnath → Rudranath → Madhyamaheshwar → Kalpeshwar**, descending in body-symbolism from hump to hair. (Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*; Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa)

- **Kedarnath (the hump — *pṛṣṭha-bhāga*)** — 1st and supreme of the five; Mandākinī valley, Rudraprayag district, **3,583 m (11,755 ft)**. The conical/pyramidal rock here is the bull's **hump (back)**; also one of the **[12 Jyotirlingas](12-jyotirlingas.md)** (the northernmost, *"Kedāram tu Himālaye"*) — the only Panch Kedar shrine that is simultaneously a Jyotirlinga. Open ~late April/early May to Oct/Nov (Akṣaya-tṛtīyā to Bhai-dūj). (Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*; Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa)
  - *Winter abode:* the deity's *utsava-mūrti* (palanquin idol) moves to **Omkareshwar Temple, Ukhimath** during the snow months.
- **Tungnath (the arms — *bāhu*)** — 2nd; above Chopta, Rudraprayag district, **3,680 m (12,070 ft)** — **the highest Shiva temple in the world** and the highest of the Panch Kedar. Enshrines the bull's **arms**. Linked in some traditions to **Rama**, who is said to have meditated here. (Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*)
  - *Winter abode:* idol descends to **Makkumath (Markandeya)**, near Chopta.
- **Rudranath (the face — *mukha*)** — 3rd; reached by hard trek from Sagar/Gopeshwar, Chamoli district, **3,559 m (11,677 ft)**. The bull's **face / head** is worshipped here as **Nīlkaṇṭha Mahādeva**, a natural rock face in a cave/meadow setting. (Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*)
  - *Winter abode:* worship shifts to **Gopeshwar**.
- **Madhyamaheshwar (Madmaheshwar — the navel & belly — *nābhi / madhya*)** — 4th; Mansoona base, Garhwal, **3,490 m (11,450 ft)**. The bull's **navel and stomach (mid-section)** — hence *madhya* ("middle"). A black navel-shaped lingam. (Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*)
  - *Winter abode:* idol to **Ukhimath** (shared seat with Kedarnath).
- **Kalpeshwar (the hair / matted locks — *jaṭā / keśa*)** — 5th and final; Urgam valley near Helang, Chamoli district, **2,200 m (7,200 ft)** — the **lowest** of the five and the **only one open year-round** (no winter closure, no idol-shifting). The bull's **matted hair (jaṭā)** is worshipped here, reached through a rock cave. (Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*)

### The Pāṇḍava–Nandi-Bull Legend

- **The sin and the search** — after the Kurukṣetra war, the **Pāṇḍavas** sought to expiate *gotra-hatyā* and *brahma-hatyā* (the killing of kinsmen and Brahmins). **Shiva**, unwilling to absolve them while their guilt was fresh, evaded them — first eluding them at **Kāśī (Varanasi)**, then fleeing to the Garhwal Himalaya. (Mahābhārata tradition; Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa)
- **Bhīma and the disguised bull** — at **Guptakāśī** ("hidden Kashi"), **Bhīma** spotted a bull grazing and recognized it as Shiva in disguise. Standing astride two mountains, he seized the bull by its tail and hind legs; the bull-form Shiva **dove into the earth** to re-emerge in five parts across Garhwal. (Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*)
  - *Variant (Skanda Purāṇa):* the bull was **torn asunder into five parts** that surfaced at the five sites of the Kedār-khaṇḍa — a dismemberment framing rather than a voluntary re-emergence. [variant]
- **The five re-emergences** — pleased by the manifestation, the Pāṇḍavas raised temples where each part rose: **hump → Kedarnath, arms → Tungnath, face → Rudranath, navel/belly → Madhyamaheshwar, hair → Kalpeshwar.** Worshipping these, they were freed of sin and (in the epic frame) proceeded to their final ascent of the Himalaya (*Mahāprasthānika*). (Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*; Mahābhārata, Mahāprasthānika Parva tradition)

### The Badrinath Affirmation (closing rite)

- **The unwritten rule** — after completing all five Kedār shrines, the pilgrim is bound by an **unwritten religious rite** to visit **Vishnu at Badrinath** as a *final affirmatory proof* of having sought Shiva's blessing — a deliberate Shaiva–Vaiṣṇava sealing that ties the Pañcha Kedār circuit into the **Pañcha Badrī** and the **[Chota Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)**. (Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*)

### Pilgrimage geography (fixed order)

| # | Temple | Body-part | Altitude | District | Winter seat |
|---|--------|-----------|----------|----------|-------------|
| 1 | **Kedarnath** | Hump | 3,583 m | Rudraprayag | Ukhimath (Omkareshwar) |
| 2 | **Tungnath** | Arms | 3,680 m | Rudraprayag | Makkumath |
| 3 | **Rudranath** | Face | 3,559 m | Chamoli | Gopeshwar |
| 4 | **Madhyamaheshwar** | Navel/belly | 3,490 m | Garhwal | Ukhimath |
| 5 | **Kalpeshwar** | Hair | 2,200 m | Chamoli | (open all year) |

- Typical trek route: Rishikesh/Haridwar → Sonprayag → **Kedarnath** → Chopta → **Tungnath** → Gopeshwar → **Rudranath** → Ukhimath → **Madhyamaheshwar** → Helang → **Kalpeshwar** → (affirm at Badrinath).

## II. Pañcha Badrī — the Five Vishnu Shrines

Five Vishnu shrines strung through the **Badari-kṣetra** of the Alaknanda valley — from Satopanth (~24 km above Badrinath) down to the Nandprayag region. The canonical Pañcha Badrī is **Badrinath, Adi Badri, Vridha Badri, Yogadhyan Badri, Bhavishya Badri**; it is a sub-set of a larger **Sapta Badrī** ("seven Badris"). (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*)

- **Badrinath (Badrī Viśāl / Badrīnārāyaṇa)** — the supreme Badri; Alaknanda bank, Chamoli district, **3,133 m (10,279 ft)**. Vishnu worshipped as the self-manifest black *śālagrāma* image in his twin-sage ascetic form **Nara–Nārāyaṇa**. Also the **North pole of the Greater [Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)** and a node of the Chota Char Dhām; the present temple is credited to **Adi Śaṅkara** (8th c.). Open ~April/May–Oct/Nov by astrological dates. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*; Skanda Purāṇa, Badari-māhātmya)
  - *The Badri-berry / Lakṣmī-tree legend:* while Vishnu did austere *tapas* exposed to Himalayan cold, **Lakṣmī** transformed herself into a **badrī (Indian jujube / berry) tree** to shelter and shade him — giving the place its name *Badri-kā-āśrama* ("hermitage of the berry"). (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*)
  - *Nara–Nārāyaṇa penance:* Vishnu, incarnate as the twin sages **Nara** and **Nārāyaṇa**, performed eternal penance in the open at Badarikāśrama — the eponymous ascetics of the Nara–Nārāyaṇa peaks that flank the shrine. (Skanda Purāṇa; Mahābhārata tradition)
- **Yogadhyan Badri (Yoga Badri — Pandukeshwar winter seat)** — Pandukeshwar, near Govind Ghāṭ, **1,829 m (6,001 ft)**. Vishnu in a **meditative (dhyāna) posture**. The legend: **King Pāṇḍu** did penance here to be cleansed of killing two mating deer (ascetics in a former life); the **Pāṇḍavas were born here**, and Pāṇḍu attained salvation here. Critically, it is the **official winter abode of Badrinath**: when Badrinath closes, the *utsava-mūrti* (festival image) is carried down to Pandukeshwar for worship. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*)
- **Bhavishya Badri (the "future" Badri)** — Subhain village, ~17 km from Joshimath via Tapovan, **2,744 m (9,003 ft)**. Vishnu as **Narasiṃha** (lion-man). The prophetic shrine: when **adharma overwhelms the world**, the **Nara and Nārāyaṇa mountains will collapse and seal the route to Badrinath**, rendering it inaccessible — at which point **Badrinath will manifest here** and be worshipped at Bhavishya Badri instead. A future-clock shrine, awaiting the close of this age. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*) — see [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)
  - *Narsingh-Badri link:* the prophecy is tied to the **Narasiṃha (Narsingh) temple at Joshimath**, whose idol has a thinning arm; when the arm fully withers, Badrinath shuts permanently and shifts to Bhavishya Badri — read as a marker of the **end of Kali Yuga**. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*) [folk eschatology]
- **Vridha Badri (Vṛddha Badri — "old Badri")** — Animath village, ~7 km from Joshimath, **1,380 m (4,530 ft)**. Vishnu worshipped as an **old man (vṛddha)** — the form in which he appeared to **sage Nārada** during his penance here. Tradition holds the **current Badrinath idol was carved by Viśvakarmā and first worshipped at Vridha Badri** before its relocation; hence "old." Open year-round. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*)
- **Adi Badri ("first Badri")** — ~17 km beyond Karnaprayag on the Karnaprayag–Ranikhet road, Chamoli district, **~1,500 m**. A cluster of **sixteen small temples** of the late **Gupta period (5th–8th c.)**; the chief Nārāyaṇa shrine holds a ~1 m black-stone Vishnu. Called *Adi* ("first/original") as the **first temple complex of the Badri group**; also functions as a winter refuge in the lower valley. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*)

### From Sapta Badrī to Pañcha Badrī (membership variants)

The five-shrine Pañcha Badrī is carved out of a wider **Sapta Badrī** roster; the two extra members and the substitutions vary by source. [open roster]

- **Dhyan Badri** — Urgam valley near Kalpeshwar, **2,135 m (7,005 ft)**; four-armed meditating Vishnu in black stone, founded by **Urvarīṣi** (son of King Puraṇjaya). One of the Sapta Badrī; **sometimes substituted into the Pañcha Badrī in place of Vridha Badri**. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*) [variant]
- **Ardha Badri ("half Badri")** — a small, hard-to-reach shrine on the Joshimath–Tapovan track; the seventh Sapta-Badrī slot. **Omitted from the Pañcha Badrī** in the standard count. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*) [variant]
- **Narsingh (Narasiṃha) Badri** — the Narasiṃha temple at Joshimath; **sometimes listed in the Sapta Badrī in place of Ardha Badri**, and bound to the Bhavishya Badri prophecy (thinning-arm idol). (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*) [variant]
- *Standard Pañcha Badrī (most-cited):* Badrinath + Adi Badri + Yogadhyan Badri + Bhavishya Badri + Vridha Badri — **omitting Ardha Badri and (usually) Dhyan Badri**. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*)

### Winter-abode shifting (the moving seat of Badrīnārāyaṇa)

- **Badrinath → Yogadhyan Badri (Pandukeshwar):** the festival image of Badrīnārāyaṇa is carried down each winter to **Pandukeshwar**, the recognized cold-season seat, since the high shrine is snow-locked Oct/Nov–April/May. This parallels the Kedār system's idol-shifting (Kedarnath→Ukhimath, etc.), so that **neither circuit ever goes fully dark** — worship merely descends the valley.
- *Eschatological terminus:* the **final** shift is to **Bhavishya Badri**, when the route is destroyed — the permanent end-state of Badrinath worship at the close of the age. [folk]

## III. How the Two Circuits Interlock

- **Shared geography** — both run through Chamoli/Rudraprayag districts along the Mandākinī (Kedār) and Alaknanda (Badrī) tributaries; a single extended yātrā commonly chains Pañcha Kedār → Badrinath → Pañcha Badrī.
- **Shared Pāṇḍava sub-text** — the Pāṇḍavas build the Kedār shrines (expiation), and **King Pāṇḍu** (their father) does penance at **Yogadhyan Badri**, where they are born — knitting the Mahābhārata lineage across both fives. (Mahābhārata tradition)
- **Shaiva–Vaiṣṇava sealing** — the Kedār pilgrim's mandatory closing visit to Badrinath fuses the Shiva-five and the Vishnu-five into one devotional arc.
- **Two clocks** — *scripture clock:* the legends sit in the post-Kurukṣetra / early-Kali frame (Pāṇḍavas, Nara–Nārāyaṇa). *Academic clock:* the surviving temple fabric is far younger — Adi Badri is Gupta-era (5th–8th c.), Badrinath's present shrine 8th-c. (Śaṅkara), and the "Panch Kedar"/"Panch Badri" circuit labels are medieval-to-modern Purāṇic/devotional organizations of older individual shrines. [scholarly]
