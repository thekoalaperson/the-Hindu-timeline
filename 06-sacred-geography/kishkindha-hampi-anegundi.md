# Kishkindha — The Vanara Kingdom at Hampi-Anegundi

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](README.md) → **Kiṣkindhā / Hampi-Anegundi**. The southern *Rāmāyaṇa* memory-landscape on the **Tuṅgabhadrā** in **Karnataka**, traditionally identified with the **Vānara kingdom of Sugrīva** — Mount **Ṛṣyamūka** of the Rāma-Sugrīva alliance, **Pampā Sarovar** where **Śabarī** awaited Rāma, and **Añjanādri**, a claimed Hanumān birth-hill — all woven into the **Vijayanagara** *Pampā-Virūpākṣa kṣetra* (the Hampi World Heritage zone).

## I. Identity, the "two clocks" & terrain

- **Kiṣkindhā = the vānara capital of the *Rāmāyaṇa*** — the cavern-girt mountain-kingdom of the *vānaras* (forest-monkeys), ruled by **Vāli** and then **Sugrīva**, near which Rāma forges his decisive alliance; tradition locates it among the granite hills and the **Tuṅgabhadrā** river at **Hampi / Anegundi**, Bellary–Koppal districts, Karnataka. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa*) — see [Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md)
- **Scriptural clock (Tretā-yuga)** — within the epic frame these events belong to the **Tretā-yuga** of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara, during Rāma's fourteen-year exile, in the year following Sītā's abduction from [Pañcavaṭī / Daṇḍaka](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md). (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*) — the mythic register of every event below; keep the **two-clocks** scriptural-vs-historical distinction.
- **Historical clock** — as a *living* landscape this is a continuously elaborated **sthala-purāṇa** geography, fixed in its present form by the **Vijayanagara** empire (capital here 1336–1565 CE), which read its own city as Rāma's Kiṣkindhā and built the **Pampā-Virūpākṣa**, **Rāmacandra** (Hazara Rāma) and **Anjanadri** complexes onto the epic ground. [scholarly]
- **Anegundi vs. the southern-shore Hampi** — older tradition seats the *vānara* citadel at **Anegundi** ("elephant-pit"), on the **north bank** of the Tuṅgabhadrā, the elder settlement; the Vijayanagara royal capital and its great temples rose on the **south bank** as Hampi proper. [regional]
- **The disputed identification** — the Hampi-Anegundi siting rests on sthala-tradition and Vijayanagara-era reading, not on an unbroken textual fix; rival/alternate Kiṣkindhā locations are sometimes proposed, and the identification is a **devotional-geographic** rather than archaeological certainty. [disputed]

## II. Pampā Sarovar & Śabarī (Araṇya-kāṇḍa → Kiṣkindhā)

- **Pampā Saras = the lotus-lake of the south** — *Pampā* is the celebrated lotus-lake (*sarovara*) of the Kiṣkindhā country, counted among the **Pañca-sarovara** (the five holy lakes) of tradition; it is the freshwater lake/tank at Anegundi, **distinct from the Keralan Pampā river**. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya- & Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa*; Pampā-māhātmya) [regional]
- **Śabarī's wait and the tasted berries** — directed by **Kabandha**, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa reach the *āśrama* by **Pampā** where the aged tribal ascetic **Śabarī**, disciple of sage **Mataṅga**, has awaited Rāma's *darśana* for years; she offers fruit and — in the best-loved telling — **berries she has first tasted** to ensure their sweetness, which Rāma accepts as pure love before she attains the higher worlds. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 70–74) — see [Araṇya Kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md)
  - *Variant (the tasted/half-eaten berries) [folk]:* the pre-tasting of the *ber* (jujube) fruit is the famous popular and Bhakti-era amplification — emblem of devotion above ritual purity — foregrounded in regional retellings more than in the bare Vālmīki line.
- **From Pampā into Kiṣkindhā** — Śabarī points Rāma onward to **Ṛṣyamūka** and the *vānaras*; the lament at the blossoming **Pampā** in spring, which sharpens Rāma's grief for Sītā, opens the **Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa** proper. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa* 1)
- **Pampā Sarovar as living tīrtha** — the Anegundi lake is shown today with a **Śabarī shrine** and a **Pampā-sarovara** bathing-tank; pilgrims revere it as the very lake of the epic and as a *pañca-sarovara* member, paired with the Tuṅgabhadrā in the local circuit. (sthala-tradition) — see [Sacred Rivers & Descent-Myths](sacred-rivers-and-descent-myths.md)

## III. Mount Ṛṣyamūka & the Rāma-Sugrīva alliance

- **Ṛṣyamūka = the one hill Vāli cannot enter** — the refuge-hill of the exiled **Sugrīva**, **Hanumān** and their ministers, barred to **Vāli** by the **curse of sage Mataṅga** (Vāli would die if he set foot on it); from its height Sugrīva spies the two armed strangers and fears assassination. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa* 2–3)
- **Hanumān's embassy** — Sugrīva sends **Hanumān**, who approaches disguised as a courteous brāhmaṇa mendicant, wins Rāma's trust with flawless speech, and **carries both princes on his shoulders** up to Sugrīva — Hanumān's entry into the epic's main action. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa* 3–4)
- **The agni-sākṣī friendship-pact** — Hanumān kindles fire by friction; Rāma and Sugrīva circumambulate it and swear mutual aid (*sakhya*) with **Agni** as witness — Rāma to slay Vāli and restore Sugrīva's kingdom and wife, Sugrīva to send the *vānaras* to find Sītā. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa* 5) — the dharmic ground Rāma later cites for the Vāli-vadha.
- **Sītā's ornaments dropped over Ṛṣyamūka** — Sugrīva produces the bundle of jewels Sītā had cast down from Rāvaṇa's flying chariot as it passed overhead; Rāma recognizes only her **foot-ornaments** and weeps — the first physical trace of the abduction's route. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa* 6)
- **The slaying of Vāli** — at the second challenge, with **Tārā's** warning ignored by the proud Vāli, **Rāma shoots Vāli from concealment**; the dying Vāli's dharma-indictment and Rāma's tenfold justification make this the epic's most-debated act. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa* 16–18) — full ethics-debate and variants in [Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md)
- **The local toponyms** — Anegundi tradition shows a hill as **Ṛṣyamūka**, a cave as the **Ṛkṣabila / Kiṣkindhā** cavern (the Māyāvī-pursuit cave), and a knoll as **Sugrīva's cave**, with rock-marks read as the scattered ornaments; these are sthala-identifications, not textual measurements. (sthala-tradition) [folk]

## IV. The launch of the search for Sītā

- **Sugrīva's lapse and Lakṣmaṇa's wrath** — enthroned at Kiṣkindhā (with **Aṅgada** made *yuvarāja*), Sugrīva sinks into post-monsoon pleasure and forgets his vow; **Lakṣmaṇa's** furious entry into the city recalls him to duty. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa* 30–33)
- **The four-direction muster** — Sugrīva summons the *vānara* hosts and dispatches search-parties to the **four quarters** to find Sītā within a month, detailing the cosmographic geography of each direction in long set-pieces. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa* 37–43)
- **The southern party and Sampāti** — the crucial **southern** party — under **Aṅgada**, **Hanumān** and the bear-king **Jāmbavān** — is despaired and starving in a cave when the dying vulture **Sampāti** (brother of Jaṭāyu) tells them Sītā is held by Rāvaṇa across the sea in Laṅkā, setting the stage for Hanumān's leap. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa* 56–66)

## V. Añjanādri — the claimed Hanumān birth-hill

- **Añjanādri = "the hill of Añjanā"** — a granite hill across the Tuṅgabhadrā (north of Hampi, near Anegundi) claimed in regional and Vijayanagara-era tradition as the **birthplace of Hanumān**, son of **Añjanā** (and of **Vāyu**); crowned today by a **Hanumān temple** reached by a long stair. (sthala-tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant (rival Hanumān birthplaces) [disputed]:* **Anjaneri** near Nashik (Maharashtra), **Hampi's Añjanādri** (Karnataka), and sites in Andhra/Telangana and elsewhere all claim Hanumān's *janma-sthāna*; the Hampi claim leans on the Kiṣkindhā identification, making the *vānara* land itself his cradle — but no single birthplace is textually fixed.
- **Hanumān as the kṣetra's native son** — the Hampi reading binds Hanumān's birth, his service to Sugrīva, and his meeting with Rāma into one landscape, so that the *vānara* hero is "of" this very ground. (devotional tradition) [folk]

## VI. The Vijayanagara *Pampā-Virūpākṣa kṣetra* — the woven sacred geography

- **Pampā / Pampāvatī weds Śiva as Virūpākṣa** — beneath the *Rāmāyaṇa* layer lies an older **Śaiva-Śākta** myth: the river-goddess **Pampā (Pampāvatī)**, daughter of Brahmā, performs *tapas* on **Hemakūṭa hill** and wins **Śiva** as her husband in his form **Virūpākṣa** ("the oddly/three-eyed lord"); from her the place is named **Pampā-kṣetra**, whence **"Hampi."** (Pampā-māhātmya; sthala-purāṇa) [regional]
- **The Virūpākṣa temple** — the **Virūpākṣa (Pampāpati) temple** at Hampi, an unbroken living shrine since at least the 7th c. CE and the imperial tutelary of Vijayanagara, anchors the Śaiva *kṣetra*; its annual **Pampā-Virūpākṣa kalyāṇotsava** (the divine wedding) is the great local festival. (temple tradition; epigraphy) [scholarly] — see [Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)
- **Hemakūṭa hill** — the low granite hill above the Virūpākṣa temple, strewn with early shrines, where Pampā's penance and the Śiva-Pampā union are localized; the mythic stage of the *kṣetra*'s name-story. (sthala-tradition) [regional]
- **The Rāmacandra / Hazāra Rāma temple** — the Vijayanagara kings' **palace-chapel of Rāma** (the "Hazāra Rāma," ~15th c. CE), its walls carved with the **Rāmāyaṇa** in continuous relief; it made Rāma the imperial *iṣṭa-devatā* and read the capital itself as **Kiṣkindhā**, fusing the *vānara*-epic and the Pampā-Virūpākṣa myth into one royal geography. (Vijayanagara temple tradition) [scholarly]
- **The imperial Kiṣkindhā idea** — Vijayanagara's self-presentation as the seat of Rāma's allies (Sugrīva, Hanumān) lent the empire epic legitimacy; the Hampi-Anegundi identification of Kiṣkindhā is in large part the durable legacy of this **medieval royal reading** of the landscape. [scholarly]
