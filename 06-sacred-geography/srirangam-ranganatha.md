# Śrīraṅgam — Raṅganātha (Bhūloka Vaikuṇṭha)

> The foremost of the [108 Divya Desams](108-divya-desams.md), on a Kāverī island at **Tiruvaraṅgam** (Tiruchirappalli, Tamil Nadu) — the **largest functioning Hindu temple complex on earth** (~156 acres, seven concentric *prākāras*), head shrine of the **Śrī Vaiṣṇava (Rāmānuja) sampradāya**, and the only Divya Desam sung by **all twelve Āḻvārs**. Here Viṣṇu reclines as **Raṅganātha** on Ādiśeṣa, facing south.
>

Śrīraṅgam is the **first and greatest** of the Divya Desams — called **Bhūloka Vaikuṇṭha** ("Vaikuṇṭha on earth"), the terrestrial mirror of Viṣṇu's supreme abode. The deity **Raṅganātha** (Tamil **Aḻagiya Maṇavāḷan**; the festival icon **Nam Perumāḷ**) reclines on the serpent **Ādiśeṣa** in *bhujaṅga-śayana*, with consort **Raṅganāyakī (Śrī Raṅga-Nācciyār)**, under the **Praṇavākāra (Oṃ-shaped) vimāna**. As with all *māhātmya* temples, the **legend-layer** (Śrīraṅga-māhātmya time, the "scriptural clock") and the **historical layer** (inscriptions, the *Koil Oḻugu* chronicle, the "academic clock") often part — separated below.

---

## I. The Raṅga-vimāna — From Brahmā to Ikṣvāku to Vibhīṣaṇa

The foundation legend, the **Śrīraṅga-māhātmya** (with parallels drawn from *Garuḍa* and *Nāradīya* Purāṇa references), traces the self-manifest **Raṅga-vimāna** (the shrine-tower enclosing the reclining mūrti) through a chain of divine custodians.

- **The svayambhū vimāna in the Ocean of Milk** — the **Raṅga-vimāna** with Raṅganātha reclining is held to be **self-manifest (svayam-vyakta)**, worshipped first by **Brahmā** at the *Kṣīra-sāgara* (Ocean of Milk), the cosmic source of the reclining form. (Śrīraṅga-māhātmya)
- **Passed to the Sūryavaṃśa** — Brahmā grants the vimāna to **Ikṣvāku**, founder of the [Solar dynasty](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md); it is enshrined and worshipped at **Ayodhyā** down the royal line, reaching **Rāma** himself. (Śrīraṅga-māhātmya)
- **Rāma gifts it to Vibhīṣaṇa** — after the [Laṅkā war](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/README.md), **Rāma** rewards the loyal rākṣasa **Vibhīṣaṇa** — newly crowned king of Laṅkā — with the family deity, the Raṅga-vimāna, to carry south for worship in his island kingdom. (Śrīraṅga-māhātmya)

## II. The Vibhīṣaṇa Episode — Why Raṅganātha Stays on the Kāverī

This is the heart of the sthala-purāṇa: the deity *chooses* the Kāverī island and refuses to move further south.

- **The condition of carrying** — Vibhīṣaṇa bears the vimāna toward Laṅkā under a divine stipulation: **wherever he sets it down on the ground, it will root there permanently** and cannot be lifted again. (Śrīraṅga-māhātmya)
- **The halt at Tiruvaraṅgam** — pausing on the **island between the Kāverī and Koḷḷiḍam (Coleroon)** rivers — drawn by its beauty, or (in tellings) at the time for his *sandhyā* worship — Vibhīṣaṇa **sets the vimāna down**, and Raṅganātha, charmed by the Kāverī isle, **refuses to rise again**. (Śrīraṅga-māhātmya)
  - *Variant (the Gaṇeśa trick) [folk]:* in a popular telling **Gaṇeśa**, disguised as a cowherd boy, is asked to hold the vimāna while Vibhīṣaṇa bathes; the boy sets it on the ground and vanishes — when Vibhīṣaṇa chases and strikes him on the head, the boy reveals himself as Gaṇeśa, and a hilltop **Ucchi Piḷḷaiyār** (Rock Fort Gaṇeśa) shrine in Tiruchirappalli commemorates the spot. [regional]
- **Facing south toward Laṅkā** — to console the departing Vibhīṣaṇa, Raṅganātha agrees to remain but **reclines facing south**, gazing perpetually toward **Laṅkā** so that his blessing always reaches Vibhīṣaṇa's kingdom — the iconographic explanation for the sanctum's unusual southward orientation. (Śrīraṅga-māhātmya) [regional]
- **Vibhīṣaṇa the cira-jīvī** — Vibhīṣaṇa departs for Laṅkā as one of the **immortals (cira-jīvī)**, returning periodically; the legend thus binds Śrīraṅgam to the *Rāmāyaṇa* afterlife and the Tretā–Kali continuity. [folk]

## III. The Seven Prākāras — A Cosmic Mandala in Stone

- **Sapta-prākāra (seven enclosures)** — the temple is built as **seven concentric rectangular walls (*prākāra*)** around the sanctum, the largest single temple enclosure-system in the world, read as a **mandala** descending from the gross outer world to the subtle divine center. The outermost is a residential town; the innermost holds the reclining Lord. (temple tradition; *Koil Oḻugu*)
- **The named enclosures (innermost → outermost)** — **Dharma-varma**, **Rāja-mahendran**, **Kulaśekharan**, **Āḻināḍan**, **Akalanka**, **Tiruviḍai (Tirumaṅgai-Āḻvār)**, and the outermost **Cittirai** *prākāra* — each named for a royal patron or saint who is said to have raised it. (*Koil Oḻugu*) [regional]
- **Twenty-one gopurams and the Rājagopuram** — the enclosures are pierced by **21 gopuram (tower-gateways)**; the southern **Rājagopuram**, at ~73 m (236 ft), is among the **tallest temple towers in Asia**, its base medieval but its full height **completed only in 1987** by the Ahobila Maṭha. (academic/historical) [scholarly]
- **The Thousand-Pillar Hall** — the **Āyiram-kāl maṇḍapam** (granite "1000-pillar" hall) on the temple's east side dates to the **Vijayanagara** period (c. 15th–16th c.), site of the annual **Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī** processions. [scholarly]

## IV. The Āḻvārs — Sung by All Twelve

- **The unique twelvefold mangaḷāśāsanam** — Śrīraṅgam is the **only Divya Desam glorified by all twelve [Āḻvārs](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/alvars-and-nayanars.md)** in the **Nālāyira Divya Prabandham**, making it the supreme node of the entire 108-temple grid. (Nālāyira Divya Prabandham)
- **Toṇḍaraḍippoḍi Āḻvār** — the *Tirumālai* and *Tiruppaḷḷiyeḻucci* (the "wake-up" hymn to Raṅganātha) are composed **for this shrine**; the Āḻvār is the temple's garland-maker, his very name meaning "dust of the devotees' feet." (Nālāyira Divya Prabandham)
- **Āṇḍāḷ's bridal vow** — the goddess-poet **[Āṇḍāḷ (Goda)](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/alvars-and-nayanars.md)**, in her *Nācciyār Tirumoḻi* and *Tiruppāvai*, vows to wed only Raṅganātha; tradition holds she **merged into the deity at Śrīraṅgam**, fulfilling her bridal devotion. (Nālāyira Divya Prabandham; hagiography) [folk]
- **Tiruppāṇ Āḻvār carried to the sanctum** — the low-caste musician-saint **Tiruppāṇ Āḻvār**, who would not tread the temple soil, is carried on the shoulders of the brahmin **Lokasāraṅga Muni** (by the Lord's own command) into the sanctum, where he sings the *Amalanādipirān* on Raṅganātha head-to-foot and merges into the deity — a foundational Śrī Vaiṣṇava parable of **bhakti over caste**. (Nālāyira Divya Prabandham; hagiography) [folk]

## V. Rāmānuja and the Ācāryas — The Living Headquarters

- **Rāmānuja's seat** — the great **Viśiṣṭādvaita** ācārya **[Rāmānuja](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/alvars-and-nayanars.md)** (traditionally 1017–1137 CE) made Śrīraṅgam the **administrative and theological capital** of Śrī Vaiṣṇavism, reorganizing the temple's worship, festivals, and personnel (the **74 simhāsanādhipatis** lineage). (hagiography; *Koil Oḻugu*) [historical]
- **The tomb-shrine (the embalmed body)** — uniquely, Rāmānuja's **mortal body is enshrined within the temple**, in a separate sannidhi in the fifth *prākāra*; tradition holds it is the **preserved (not cremated) body**, periodically re-coated with camphor/saffron, seated in *yoga* posture — a rare relic-shrine in Hinduism. (*Koil Oḻugu*; temple tradition) [regional]
- **The charama resting-place of the ācāryas** — Śrīraṅgam is the **charama** (final) home of the Śrī Vaiṣṇava lineage: **Periya Nambi, Kūrattāḻvān, Parāśara Bhaṭṭar, Piḷḷai Lokācārya, Vedānta Deśika**, and others are tied to the site, and the **Maṇavāḷa Māmunigaḷ** maṭha tradition continues here. [historical]

## VI. The Koil Oḻugu and the Muslim Raid

- **The *Koil Oḻugu* chronicle** — the **Koil Oḻugu** ("Temple Record/Ledger") is the Tamil **prose chronicle of Śrīraṅgam**, a centuries-long temple-administrative history blending sthala-purāṇa, the Vibhīṣaṇa legend, Rāmānuja's reforms, donor records, and the medieval crises — the single richest indigenous source for the temple's institutional past. (*Koil Oḻugu*) [scholarly]
- **The 1311 raid and the icon's flight** — when the armies of the **Delhi Sultanate (Malik Kāfūr, c. 1311 CE)** sacked the south, the festival icon **Nam Perumāḷ was hidden and carried into exile** (north to Tirupati and beyond) for decades; the *Koil Oḻugu* records that priests gave their lives and a wall was raised to disguise the sanctum. (*Koil Oḻugu*) [historical]
  - *Variant (the Muslim princess Sūratāṇi) [regional]:* a beloved legend holds that the processional icon was taken to **Delhi**, where the Sultan's daughter (**Sūratāṇi / Tulukka Nācciyār**) fell in love with it; recovered by the devotees, the icon's consort-shrine for "the Turkish lady" is worshipped to this day, where Raṅganātha is offered **roṭi and butter**. [folk]
- **Restoration** — the icon was reinstalled in the later 14th c. under **Vijayanagara** protection (the general **Goppaṇa Uḍaiyār**), after which the empire and the **Nāyaka** governors rebuilt and expanded the complex into its present scale. (academic/historical) [scholarly]

## VII. Two Clocks — Legend vs History

- **Scriptural clock** — the svayambhū vimāna, the Brahmā→Ikṣvāku→Rāma→Vibhīṣaṇa transmission, the southward gaze toward Laṅkā, and the Bhūloka-Vaikuṇṭha status sit in **māhātmya time**, undatable on any calendar. The Vibhīṣaṇa episode places the shrine's "arrival" at the **Tretā–Kali hinge** of the *Rāmāyaṇa* afterlife. [legend]
- **Academic clock** — the site is attested from the **early medieval** period: praised by the Āḻvārs (c. 6th–9th c. CE), endowed by **Cōḻa, Pāṇḍya, Hoysaḷa, Vijayanagara and Nāyaka** dynasties; the present fabric is largely **12th–17th c. CE**, with the Rājagopuram finished in **1987**. The temple is a **UNESCO Asia-Pacific heritage award** site and a candidate for World Heritage listing. [scholarly]
