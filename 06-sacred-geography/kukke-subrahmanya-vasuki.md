# Kukke Subrahmanya — Vasuki sheltered from Garuḍa

> **Kukke Subrahmanya** — the premier **sarpa-kṣetra** (serpent-shrine) of coastal Karnataka, on the **Kumāradhārā** river in the foothills of the Western Ghāṭs (**Sullia taluk, Dakṣiṇa Kannada**). Here **Kumāra-Skanda**, after the slaying of **Tārakāsura**, washed his weapons in the river — whence its name *Kumāra-dhārā* ("Kumāra's torrent") — and granted refuge to the serpent-king **Vāsuki**, who was performing penance in the **Biladvāra** cave to escape his enemy **[Garuḍa](../08-beings-and-bestiary/garuda.md)**. The two are worshipped together, making this the charter shrine for the remedy of **sarpa-doṣa** and the home of the **Āśleṣā-bali** and **Sarpa-saṃskāra** rites.

**Names & extent** — the *kṣetra* lies at the modern village of **Subrahmanya (Kukke Subrahmanya)**, on the west bank of the **Kumāradhārā** where it emerges from the **Kumāra-parvata** (Pushpagiri) massif of the Sahyādri. The presiding deity is **Subrahmaṇya** — the Sanskrit-Purāṇic name of the war-god **Skanda-Kārttikeya / Murugan** — venerated **inseparably with the nāga-rāja Vāsuki**, so that a single *darśana* is of "Subrahmaṇya-cum-Vāsuki." *Kukke* is a Tuḷu/Kannada word glossed variously as "casket / small basket," from the tradition that the image was found in (or is kept as) a *kukke*; the kṣetra sits within the wider **Parashurāma-created coastal land** of the **Sahyādri-khaṇḍa**. The two clocks are kept apart throughout: in **scriptural** time the founding acts (Skanda's weapon-washing, Vāsuki's refuge) belong to primordial deva-time after the **Tāraka-vadha** [conventionally Satya/Tretā]; in **terrestrial / historical** time Kukke is a continuously-worshipped Dakṣiṇa Kannada temple of the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)**. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Sahyādri-khaṇḍa* — *Subrahmaṇya-kṣetra-māhātmya*; temple sthala tradition) [regional]

---

## I. Skanda comes to the Kumāradhārā after the Tāraka-vadha

- **The war-god arrives with his host** — having slain the daitya **[Tārakāsura](../04-deep-dives/tarakasura-vadha.md)** — whose boon of death "only by a son of Śiva" was fulfilled by the six-day-old **Skanda** — the victorious **Kumāra** with **Indra** and the deva-army descended along the western Sahyādri to the mountain later named **Kumāra-parvata**. (*Sahyādri-khaṇḍa*, Subrahmaṇya-kṣetra-māhātmya) [regional]
- **Washing the weapons — the naming of the river** — Kumāra **bathed and washed his blood-stained lance (the Śakti/Vēl) and weapons** in the swift hill-stream, sanctifying its waters; from this act the river is called the **Kumāradhārā** ("Kumāra's stream / torrent"), and its source-pool on Kumāra-parvata is reckoned a first-rank *tīrtha*. (*Sahyādri-khaṇḍa*; sthala tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant (Śūrapadma cycle):* in the broader Tamil-Skanda tradition (the *Kanda Purāṇam*) the weapon-cleansing after the demon-war is localised at the sea-shore of **[Tiruchendūr](arupadai-veedu-murugan.md)**; the Sahyādri telling transposes the same "victorious Kumāra purifies his arms" mytheme to the **Kumāradhārā**. The traditions are parallel localisations, not to be merged. (*Kanda Purāṇam*; cf. [Ārupaḍai Vīḍu](arupadai-veedu-murugan.md)) [regional]
- **Kumāra halts and holds court** — pleased with the mountain and river, Skanda is said to have **taken up residence** on the Kumāra-parvata, receiving the homage of *devas* and *ṛṣis* — establishing the site as his southern seat before the events with Vāsuki. (*Sahyādri-khaṇḍa*) [regional]

## II. Vāsuki hidden in the Biladvāra cave, sheltered from Garuḍa

- **The nāga-king in flight** — **Vāsuki**, king of the *nāgas* (the same serpent who served as the churning-rope at the **[Samudra-manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)**), lived in dread of **[Garuḍa](../08-beings-and-bestiary/garuda.md)**, the eagle-mount of Viṣṇu and hereditary devourer of snakes — the enmity born of the **Kadrū–Vinatā** wager and Garuḍa's theft of the *amṛta*. (*Sahyādri-khaṇḍa*; cf. [Nāgas & Serpents](../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md)) [regional]
- **Penance in the Biladvāra** — seeking sanctuary, Vāsuki entered the deep rock-cave **Biladvāra** (*bila-dvāra*, "the door of the hole/cavern," a few km from the temple on the way to Kumāra-parvata) and there performed prolonged **tapas to Subrahmaṇya**, praying for protection from Garuḍa. (*Sahyādri-khaṇḍa*; sthala tradition) [regional]
- **Skanda grants refuge and joint worship** — Kumāra, pleased, appeared and **granted Vāsuki asylum**, promising that within the *kṣetra* Garuḍa could not seize him, and that **Vāsuki would abide there and be worshipped together with Subrahmaṇya** for all time — the charter that fuses the war-god and the serpent-king into one object of worship. (*Sahyādri-khaṇḍa*, Subrahmaṇya-kṣetra-māhātmya) [regional]
  - *Variant (dwelling-place):* some tellings keep **Vāsuki permanently in the Biladvāra cave**, believed still to reside there in penance; others hold that Vāsuki is present **within the sanctum itself**, coiled behind the image of Subrahmaṇya — the two readings underlie the shrine's twin foci (temple + cave). (sthala tradition) [folk]
- **Garuḍa restrained — the silver pillar** — because Vāsuki, sheltered in the *garbha-gṛha*, is held to emit **venomous flame/breath**, a **Garuḍa-kambha** (a pillar bearing Garuḍa, sheathed in silver) stands before the sanctum; tradition says it **absorbs and screens the poison-fire**, and devotees stand behind it for *darśana*, so that Garuḍa here **guards** rather than hunts the serpent. (temple sthala tradition) [regional] [folk]

## III. The shrine's serpent-theology — sarpa-doṣa and its remedies

- **The charter sarpa-kṣetra** — because Skanda here **reconciled the nāga to the divine order** and made serpent-worship one with Subrahmaṇya-worship, Kukke became the foremost South-Indian shrine for the **removal of sarpa-doṣa** — the astrological/karmic affliction incurred by killing a snake, disrespecting *nāgas*, or destroying anthills (*valmīka*), and by the **Kāla-sarpa** horoscopic combination. (temple tradition; cf. [Nāgas & Serpents](../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md)) [regional]
- **Āśleṣā-bali** — the signature rite: an offering (*bali*) made on the **Āśleṣā (Āyilyam) nakṣatra** — the lunar mansion **ruled by the Sarpas / Nāgas** (see [Nakṣatras](../00-time-and-cosmology/nakshatras.md)) — to propitiate the serpent-deities and cancel serpent-anger; it is performed on the Āśleṣā day of each month at the shrine. (temple ritual manual) [regional]
- **Sarpa-saṃskāra & Nāga-pratiṣṭhā** — the major expiatory ceremony **Sarpa-saṃskāra** (a "purificatory / funerary" rite for the wronged serpent-spirit, restoring it and lifting the *doṣa* from a lineage) and the installation of **nāga-stones (nāga-pratiṣṭhā)** are performed here, framed as **prāyaścitta** (expiation) within the karma–*doṣa* logic of the *dharma-śāstra*. (temple tradition; cf. [Pāpa, Puṇya & Prāyaścitta](../05-concepts-and-dharma/papa-punya-and-prayaschitta.md)) [regional]
- **Ādi-Subrahmaṇya — the anthill** — at the base of the *kṣetra* the serpent is also venerated in aniconic form as the **valmīka (anthill)** and *nāga* images of **Ādi-Subrahmaṇya**, expressing the identity of Subrahmaṇya with the primordial serpent worshipped in the earth. (sthala tradition) [regional] [folk]

## IV. The temple, its rites, and the festival calendar

- **The mūrti and orientation** — the presiding **Subrahmaṇya** is worshipped **facing east**, the serpent Vāsuki associated behind/with the image; the *garbha-gṛha*, the **Garuḍa-kambha**, and the **Kumāradhārā** bathing-ghāṭ form the ritual core, pilgrims first bathing in the river before *darśana*. (temple tradition) [regional]
- **Sarpa-saṃskāra sequence** — devotees typically **bathe in the Kumāradhārā**, take *darśana* of the Biladvāra cave and the anthill, and undergo the *āśleṣā-bali* / *sarpa-saṃskāra* over prescribed days — a fixed pilgrimage grammar that has made Kukke a mass sarpa-doṣa destination across South India. (temple administration) [regional]
- **Campā-Ṣaṣṭhī (Skanda-Ṣaṣṭhī)** — the principal festival, **Campā-Ṣaṣṭhī**, celebrates Skanda's **victory over Tārakāsura**; the deity is taken in procession, tying the temple's yearly cycle back to the founding *Tāraka-vadha* myth. (temple calendar) [regional]
- **The Madhva association** — in **historical** time the *kṣetra* is served by **Śivaḷḷi** and other Brahmin communities and is linked in tradition to the **Mādhva (Dvaita) maṭha** network of coastal Karnataka; Kukke is among the temples reckoned within that regional custodial orbit — the *recorded-history* layer, distinct from the Purāṇic charter. (temple administration; local history) [scholarly] [regional]
  - *Variant (Śaṅkara tradition):* a regional claim holds that **Ādi Śaṅkara** visited Kukke and composed a Subrahmaṇya hymn here; the celebrated *Subrahmaṇya-bhujaṅga* is more securely tied by tradition to **Tiruchendūr**, so the Kukke attribution is best held as local devotional memory. (regional tradition) [disputed] [folk]

## V. Timeline placement (the two clocks)

- **Scriptural clock** — the founding events (Skanda's post-Tāraka weapon-washing; Vāsuki's flight from Garuḍa and grant of refuge) are narrated as acts of **primordial deva-time**, conventionally anchored to the **[Satya/Tretā-Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/01-satya-yuga.md)** horizon of the Śaiva-Skanda cycle to which the *Tāraka-vadha* belongs; the *kṣetra* is thereby a **standing power-center** active through the present [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md). [regional]
- **Historical clock** — the **Sahyādri-khaṇḍa** carrying the *Subrahmaṇya-kṣetra-māhātmya* is widely judged a **late, much-expanded appendix** to the *Skanda Purāṇa*, compiled/reworked in the medieval-to-early-modern period to glorify the Konkan–Karnataka coast [scholarly]; the **temple's documented worship, the Mādhva custodial links, and the mass sarpa-doṣa rites** are a continuous **medieval-to-modern** institution — the standard sthala-purāṇa "two clocks" distinction between the *legend's* timelessness and the *institution's* datable history. [scholarly]
