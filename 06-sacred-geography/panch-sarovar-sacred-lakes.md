# Pañca-sarovara — The Five Sacred Lakes

> *The canonical roster of the **five holiest lakes** of Hindu tradition — **Mānasarovara**, **Puṣkara**, **Bindu-sarovar**, **Pampā-sarovar**, and **Nārāyaṇa-sarovar** — bound together as one interlocking set in the Purāṇic and *sthala-māhātmya* corpus. This node catalogs the roster as a whole and gives focused treatment to the two members that lack a home file of their own: **Nārāyaṇa-sarovar** (Kutch) and the roster-aspect of **Bindu-sarovar** (Siddhpur).*
>

**Why a roster node.** Hindu tradition fixes a closed list of **five supreme lakes** (*pañca-sarovara* / *pañca-saras*), named together the way the *sapta-purī* fix the seven *mokṣa*-cities or the *dvādaśa-jyotirliṅga* fix the twelve liṅgas. Three of the five already have dedicated files in this corpus; the other two — **Nārāyaṇa-sarovar** and the lake-aspect of **Bindu-sarovar** — are gathered here so the set is complete and cross-walkable. The list is widely cited as scriptural, though the *exact* fifth member is the one point on which tellings diverge (see roster-variants below).

---

## I. The canonical five (the roster)

- **The standard pañca-sarovara** — the five holy lakes most commonly enumerated are: **Mānasa-sarovara** (Tibet/Kailāsa), **Bindu-sarovara** (Siddhpur, Gujarat), **Nārāyaṇa-sarovara** (Kutch, Gujarat), **Puṣkara-sarovara** (Ajmer, Rajasthan), and **Pampā-sarovara** (Hampi/Anegundi, Karnataka). (*Skanda Purāṇa* and regional *sthala-māhātmya* lists; widely cited) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (the fifth slot):* the first four are stable across nearly all lists; the **fifth** is most often **Pampā**, but some regional rosters substitute **Acchoda** (a Himālayan lake of the *Purāṇas*) or simply name "five great lakes" without fixing the last. Treat **Pampā** as the standard fifth and the rest as a closed core. [disputed]
  - *Note — "sarovara" vs. "saras":* the set is called both *pañca-sarovara* and *pañca-saras*; the lakes are *sarovara* ("great lake / tank") rather than *kuṇḍa* (small temple-tank), marking them as the senior tier of sacred water-bodies. [scholarly]
- **Each lake is a Purāṇic charter, not merely water** — every member carries a primordial origin-myth tying it to a deity or sage: Mānasa to **Brahmā's mind**, Puṣkara to **Brahmā's lotus-weapon**, Bindu to **Viṣṇu's tear-drops**, Pampā to the **Rāmāyaṇa** (Śabarī, Hanumān), and Nārāyaṇa to **Viṣṇu's self-manifestation** at the sea's edge. The five together span the **Trimūrti** and the **two great epics**.

## II. The three members with their own files (cross-walk)

- **Mānasa-sarovara** — the supreme high-Himalayan lake, "born of Brahmā's mind" (*mānas*), paired with the **Kailāsa parikramā** and the dark twin **Rākṣas-tāl**; a four-faith *axis mundi* and hydrographic hub. Full treatment: **[Mānasarovara & the Kailāsa Parikramā](manasarovar-kailash-yatra.md)**. (*Skanda Purāṇa*, *Mānasakhaṇḍa*; *Matsya Purāṇa*)
- **Puṣkara-sarovara** — the crescent lake at Ajmer welling up where **Brahmā's lotus** struck the earth; the *Tīrtharāja* of the west and the one living temple-tīrtha of **Brahmā**, by reason of **Sāvitrī's curse**. Full treatment: **[Puṣkara — the Brahmā Tīrtha](pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md)**. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Pushkar-māhātmya; *Mahābhārata*, Tīrtha-yātrā Parva)
- **Pampā-sarovara** — the lake in the **Kiṣkindhā** country (Tuṅgabhadrā valley, Anegundi/Hampi), where **Rāma** met the devotee **Śabarī** and was guided toward **Sugrīva** and **Hanumān** in his search for **Sītā**; named for the goddess **Pampā** (Pārvatī), consort of **Virūpākṣa** at Hampi. Full treatment: **[Kiṣkindhā / Hampi-Anegundi](kishkindha-hampi-anegundi.md)**. (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Āraṇya/Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍas)

## III. Nārāyaṇa-sarovar (Kutch) — the Vaiṣṇava lake where Sarasvatī meets the sea

> The westernmost member: a Vaiṣṇava *tīrtha* on the **Kori Creek** coast of **Kutch** (Lakhpat *taluka*, Gujarat), where the lost river **Sarasvatī** is held to reach the **sea** — paired with the **Koṭeśvar Mahādev** shrine a short distance away. One of the *pañca-sarovara* and counted among the holiest Vaiṣṇava sites of the west.

- **The name and the charter** — **Nārāyaṇa-sarovar** ("the lake of [Viṣṇu-]Nārāyaṇa") is so called because **Viṣṇu / Nārāyaṇa is said to have manifested there**; the lake is reckoned one of the five most sacred lakes named in the *Bhāgavata*-tradition lists of holy waters. (Kutch *sthala-māhātmya*; Wikipedia, *Narayan Sarovar*) [regional]
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata association):* devotional tradition links the lake to the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa's* roster of holy *sarovaras*, citing it as a place where bathing yields the merit of the great fords; the specific *māhātmya* verses are regional rather than from the main critical text. [disputed]
- **Where Sarasvatī meets the sea** — the site's defining geographic claim: the **Sarasvatī**, the vanished/sacred river of the west, is held to complete its course and **merge into the Arabian Sea** here at the Kutch coast — making Nārāyaṇa-sarovar the river's *saṅgama* with the ocean, a confluence-sanctity like a western, oceanic Prayāga. (Kutch *sthala-tradition*; cf. [Sacred rivers & descent-myths](sacred-rivers-and-descent-myths.md)) [regional]
- **Koṭeśvar Mahādev — the paired Śaiva shrine** — a few km away stands **Koṭeśvar** ("lord of a crore [of liṅgas]"), a **Śiva** temple at the very edge of the land before the sea; legend ties it to **Rāvaṇa**, who is said to have received a powerful liṅga (*ātma-liṅga*) from Śiva and, through trickery, left a "crore" of replica liṅgas here — the Śaiva pole that completes the Vaiṣṇava lake, a Hari-Hara pairing at India's western threshold. (Koṭeśvar *sthala-purāṇa*; Wikipedia, *Koteshwar, Gujarat*) [folk] [regional]
- **The temple and the five-shrine complex** — the lake is ringed by a temple group (the principal **Trikamrai / Nārāyaṇa** shrines, with images of Viṣṇu's forms), whose visible fabric is attributed to medieval and early-modern patronage, including the **Vāghela / Jadeja** rulers of Kutch and a noted endowment by **Mahārāo** patrons; the present complex is largely **17th–19th c.** in fabric on an older sacred footprint. (Wikipedia, *Narayan Sarovar*) [regional]
- **Pilgrimage place** — Nārāyaṇa-sarovar is a major Vaiṣṇava pilgrim-stop of Saurāṣṭra/Kutch, often paired in circuit with **[Dvārakā](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md)** and the coastal Kṛṣṇa-kṣetras; bathing in the lake and *darśana* at Koṭeśvar form the standard two-shrine visit. [regional]

## IV. Bindu-sarovar (Siddhpur) — the roster's mātṛ-tīrtha pole

> The fourth member, the lake on the **Sarasvatī** at **Siddhpur** (north Gujarat) — the foremost site of the **mother's death-rite**. Treated in full at **[Bindu-sarovar (Siddhpur)](bindu-sarovar-siddhpur.md)**; summarized here for its place in the five.

- **Mātṛ-tīrtha — the Gayā of the mother** — Bindu-sarovara is the supreme site of **mātṛ-śrāddha** (*piṇḍa-dāna* and *tarpaṇa* for the **mother** and the maternal line), the explicit counterpart to **[Gayā](gaya-pitru-tirtha.md)**, the *pitṛ*-tīrtha of the father — encapsulated as "**the father's rite at Gayā, the mother's rite at Siddhpur**." (Siddhpur *sthala-purāṇa*; *Skanda Purāṇa*) [regional]
- **The Bhāgavata charter** — the lake is named *Bindu-sarovara* ("lake of the drop") because **Viṣṇu's tears (*bindu*) of compassion** fell there when he appeared to the Prajāpati **Kardama**; here Kardama wed **Devahūti**, and here their son **Kapila** — Viṣṇu incarnate — was born and expounded the **Sāṅkhya** *tattva*-system to his mother (the **Kapilopadeśa / Devahūti-putra Gītā**), liberating her. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.21–33)
  - *Note — Kapila's mother-teaching grounds the mother-rite:* the lake where the **avatāra-son liberated his own mother** becomes the natural place for any son to release his mother's soul — the *mātṛ-tarpaṇa* re-enacting Kapila's gift to Devahūti. [folk]

## V. The two clocks — scripture vs. terrain

- **Scriptural clock** — every lake in the roster is *primordial* in myth-time: Mānasa and Puṣkara tied to **Brahmā's** creative activity; Bindu to the **Svāyambhuva Manvantara** (the first *manvantara*, dawn of the present *kalpa*) via Kardama–Devahūti–Kapila; Nārāyaṇa to **Viṣṇu's self-manifestation**; Pampā to the **Tretā-yuga** events of the *Rāmāyaṇa*. The five are thus a **cross-yuga** set — no single yuga owns them; they are charters projected from the dawn of cosmic time and from the epic ages alike. [scholarly]
- **Historical clock** — the *datable* temple-and-tīrtha fabric of each is far younger: Puṣkara's Brahmā temple ~**14th c.**; Siddhpur's developed kuṇḍa and Rudra-mahālaya **Caulukya/Solanki-era** (11th–12th c.); Nārāyaṇa-sarovar's complex largely **17th–19th c.**; Hampi/Pampā's monumental fabric **Vijayanagara** (14th–16th c.); Mānasarovar's pilgrim infrastructure modern. The *māhātmyas* project the cosmic charter onto medieval and later sites. [scholarly]
- **Roster as a closed set** — like the *sapta-purī* and *jyotirliṅga* lists, the *pañca-sarovara* function as a **mnemonic circuit** binding far-flung sites (Tibet, Rajasthan, two ends of Gujarat, the Deccan) into one nationwide sacred geography; the binding itself is the tradition's work, not any single text's. [scholarly]
