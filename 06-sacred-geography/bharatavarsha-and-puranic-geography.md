# Bhāratavarṣa & Purāṇic Geography

> The horizontal map of the Hindu cosmos: the lotus-disc **Bhū-maṇḍala** of **sapta-dvīpa** (seven ring-continents) and **sapta-samudra** (seven oceans), the **nine varṣas** of central **Jambudvīpa** walled off by eight boundary-mountains around **[Meru](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md)**, and — at the disc's southern rim — **Bhārata-varṣa**, the unique *karma-bhūmi*, with its seven **kula-parvata** spines, its catalogue of holy rivers, and its lists of *janapadas*. This node catalogs the rival **Viṣṇu-Purāṇa** vs **Bhāgavata-Purāṇa** cosmographies side by side.
>

Purāṇic geography is **flat, concentric and mythic**, not surveyed. The earth-plane (*bhū-maṇḍala*) is a vast lotus-disc of **seven ring-shaped island-continents** (*dvīpas*), each girdled by a **ring-ocean** of a different liquid, each continent and ocean **double the breadth** of the one inside it. At the bull's-eye sits **Jambudvīpa**, partitioned into **nine varṣas** ("countries") by **eight boundary mountains**, with golden **[Meru](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md)** as the central pericarp. The southernmost varṣa, **Bhārata-varṣa**, is the only *karma-bhūmi* ("land of action") where dharma earns liberation. The framing chapters are **Viṣṇu Purāṇa book 2 (ch. 1–4)** and **Bhāgavata Purāṇa canto 5 (ch. 16–20)**, with parallels in *Matsya*, *Vāyu*, *Mārkaṇḍeya* and *Mahābhārata* (Bhīṣma-parva); the two main Purāṇas **disagree systematically** on names, order and presiding deities, catalogued throughout below.

## I. The Sapta-Dvīpa — seven ring-continents

The seven *dvīpas* radiate outward from Meru, each ruled originally by one of the **seven sons of Priyavrata** (grandson of [Manu Svāyambhuva](../README.md)), who furrowed the disc with his chariot-wheels to make the oceans. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.1; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.1)

- **Jambudvīpa** — the innermost, central continent (~100,000 yojanas across), named for the colossal **Jambū (rose-apple) tree** on Meru's slopes whose falling fruit forms the river **Jambū-nadī** (its juice becoming gold, *jāmbūnada*); contains the nine varṣas and Meru itself. Ruled by **Āgnīdhra**. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.2; *Bhāgavata* 5.16)
- **Plakṣadvīpa** — second ring, named for the *plakṣa* (fig/holy-fig) tree; ruled by **Idhmajihva**, divided into seven sub-varṣas. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.4; *Bhāgavata* 5.20)
- **Śālmaladvīpa** — third, named for the *śālmali* (silk-cotton) tree; ruled by **Yajñabāhu** (*Bhāgavata*) / **Vapuṣmat** (*Viṣṇu*).
  - *Variant (ruler, Viṣṇu Purāṇa):* names **Vapuṣmat** as king; the *Bhāgavata* gives **Yajñabāhu**. [disputed]
- **Kuśadvīpa** — fourth, named for sacred *kuśa* grass; ruled by **Hiraṇyaretas**. (*Bhāgavata* 5.20)
- **Krauñcadvīpa** — fifth, named for **Mount Krauñca**; ruled by **Ghṛtapṛṣṭha**.
- **Śākadvīpa** — sixth, named for the *śāka* (teak) tree; home in some texts to sun-worshipping **Maga** brāhmaṇas; ruled by **Medhātithi**.
- **Puṣkaradvīpa** — seventh and outermost, named for a giant lotus (*puṣkara*); ruled by **Vītihotra**; bounded by **Mānasottara** mountain and beyond it the *Lokāloka* wall. (*Bhāgavata* 5.20; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.4)
- *Variant (continent list — broad agreement):* both Purāṇas and the *Mahābhārata* give the same seven names; **Plakṣa, Śālmala, Kuśa, Krauñca, Śāka, Puṣkara** in that order outward, an unusually stable list. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.4; *Bhāgavata* 5.20)

## II. The Sapta-Samudra — seven ring-oceans

Each *dvīpa* is encircled by an ocean of a distinct liquid, in fixed order outward from Jambudvīpa. The names below follow the *Bhāgavata*/*Viṣṇu* concordance:

- **Lavaṇoda** — ocean of **salt water**, girdling Jambudvīpa.
- **Ikṣurasoda** — ocean of **sugarcane juice**, girdling Plakṣadvīpa.
- **Suroda** — ocean of **wine / spirituous liquor**, girdling Śālmaladvīpa.
- **Ghṛtoda** — ocean of **clarified butter (ghee)**, girdling Kuśadvīpa.
- **Kṣīroda** — ocean of **milk** (the *Kṣīra-sāgara* churned at [Samudra Manthana](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)), girdling Krauñcadvīpa.
- **Dadhi-maṇḍoda** — ocean of **curds / whey**, girdling Śākadvīpa.
- **Suddhoda / Jaloda** — ocean of **fresh (pure) water**, girdling Puṣkaradvīpa.
  - *Variant (milk/curds order):* sequences placing **milk** before **curds** vs after appear across texts; the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* (2.4) and *Bhāgavata* (5.20) agree on salt→sugarcane→wine→ghee→milk→curds→fresh-water, but *Mahābhārata* recensions vary the inner pairs. [disputed]

## III. The Nine Varṣas of Jambudvīpa

Jambudvīpa is partitioned into **nine varṣas** ("regions," each ~9,000 yojanas long) by **eight boundary mountains** — **six east–west ranges** and **two north–south flanking ranges** — with Meru and the central **Ilāvṛta** at the hub. **This is the point of sharpest divergence between the two Purāṇas.**

### Bhāgavata Purāṇa scheme (canto 5.16–18) — the standard nine

Arranged with Ilāvṛta (Meru) at center; three to the north, three to the south, one each east and west, plus Bhārata at the south rim:

- **Ilāvṛta-varṣa** — the central square containing **[Meru](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md)**; ruled/peopled by **Śiva (Saṅkarṣaṇa)** worship; women-only land per some verses. *Presiding deity: Saṅkarṣaṇa.*
- **Bhadrāśva-varṣa** — **east** of Ilāvṛta, beyond Mt. **Gandhamādana**. *Presiding deity:* **Hayagrīva** (the horse-headed [avatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)), worshipped by **Bhadraśravā**.
- **Ketumāla-varṣa** — **west** of Ilāvṛta, beyond Mt. **Mālyavān**. *Presiding deity:* **Kāmadeva / Pradyumna** form, with **Lakṣmī** and **Prajāpati's** daughters.
- **Ramyaka-varṣa** — **north**; ruled by **Manu Vaivasvata**. *Presiding deity:* **Matsya** (the fish [avatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)).
- **Hiraṇmaya-varṣa** — far **north**. *Presiding deity:* **Kūrma** (the tortoise [avatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)), worshipped by **Aryamā**/the Pitṛs.
- **Kuru-varṣa (Uttarakuru)** — **northernmost**, beyond Mt. **Śṛṅgavān**. *Presiding deity:* **Varāha**/**Yajña** (the boar [avatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)), worshipped by Bhūmi/Earth.
- **Hari-varṣa** — **south** of Ilāvṛta, between Niṣadha and Hemakūṭa. *Presiding deity:* **Nṛsiṃha** (the man-lion [avatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)), worshipped by **Prahlāda**.
- **Kimpuruṣa-varṣa (Kinnara)** — between Hemakūṭa and Himālaya. *Presiding deity:* **Rāma** (with **Hanumān** as eternal devotee). (See [Rāmāyaṇa](../90-literature-corpus/README.md).)
- **Bhārata-varṣa** — **southernmost**, the *karma-bhūmi*; this terrestrial India. *Presiding deity:* **Nara-Nārāyaṇa** (worshipped by **Nārada**). (*Bhāgavata* 5.19)
- *Variant (Kimpuruṣa vs Kinnara):* the *Bhāgavata* names it **Kimpuruṣa**; later glosses read **Kinnara**. [disputed]

### Eight boundary mountains (Bhāgavata 5.16)

- **East–west ranges** (north→south): **Śṛṅgavān**, **Śveta**, **Nīla** (north of Meru); **Niṣadha**, **Hemakūṭa**, **Himālaya** (south of Meru).
- **North–south flanking ranges**: **Mālyavān** (west) and **Gandhamādana** (east), the "tusks" boxing Ilāvṛta.

### Viṣṇu Purāṇa scheme (2.2) — same nine, reordered enumeration

The *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* lists the **same nine names** but enumerates them in a different grouping and stresses the **boundary-ranges** explicitly:

- The nine: **Bhārata, Kimpuruṣa, Hari, Ilāvṛta** (center), **Ramyaka, Hiraṇmaya, (Uttara)Kuru** (north chain), with **Bhadrāśva** (east) and **Ketumāla** (west).
- The seven (sometimes "six") east–west **maryādā-parvata** boundaries given as **Himavān (Himālaya), Hemakūṭa, Niṣadha** (south of Meru) and **Nīla, Śveta, Śṛṅgavān** (north), framing the varṣas. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.2)
  - *Variant (count of boundary mountains — 6 vs 8):* the *Viṣṇu* typically counts **six** transverse ranges; the *Bhāgavata* counts **eight** by adding the two flanking ranges (Mālyavān, Gandhamādana). [disputed]
  - *Variant (presiding deities):* the elaborate per-varṣa avatāra worship (Hayagrīva, Matsya, Kūrma, Nṛsiṃha…) is a **distinctively Bhāgavata** feature; the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* describes the varṣas more geographically and does not assign the same deity-roster. [scholarly]

## IV. Bhārata-varṣa — the karma-bhūmi (terrestrial India)

Bhārata, named after **King [Bharata](../README.md)** son of Ṛṣabha (*Bhāgavata*) or son of Duḥṣanta–Śakuntalā (*Mahābhārata*/epic tradition), is the **only land of action and merit** — the one varṣa where *yajña*, *dāna* and the four *yugas* operate and where even the gods wish to be born for the chance at liberation. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.3; *Bhāgavata* 5.19) It is shaped like a bow / triangle, 9,000 yojanas across, bounded by the **Himālaya** in the north and the ocean on three sides.

### The Sapta Kula-parvata — seven principal mountain ranges

The "family mountains" (*kula-parvata*) that bone Bhārata-varṣa (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.3; *Mahābhārata* Bhīṣma-parva):

- **Mahendra** — the Eastern Ghats / Odisha–Andhra hill belt.
- **Malaya** — the southern Western Ghats (Kerala/Tamil sandalwood hills).
- **Sahya** — the main **Western Ghats** (Sahyādri).
- **Śuktimat / Śuktimān** — eastern hill tract (often Rajmahal/Meghalaya region). [disputed location]
- **Ṛkṣa (Ṛkṣavat)** — the Vindhya–Satpura / Siwalik belt of central India.
- **Vindhya** — the Vindhya range dividing north (Āryāvarta) from south (Dakṣiṇāpatha).
- **Pāripātra / Pāriyātra** — the Aravalli / western Vindhya extension.
  - *Variant (spelling/identification):* **Pāripātra ↔ Pāriyātra**, **Ṛkṣa ↔ Riksha/Rikshavat**, **Śuktimat ↔ Śuktimān**; modern geographic equations are scholarly conjecture, not Purāṇic. [scholarly]

### Major rivers, grouped by their source-mountain

The Purāṇa catalogues scores of rivers (*nadī*) "the mothers of the world," sorted by the range they spring from (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.3):

- **From the Himālaya** — **Gaṅgā**, **Sindhu (Indus)**, **Sarasvatī**, **Śatadru (Sutlej)**, **Candrabhāgā (Chenab)**, **Yamunā**, **Vitastā (Jhelum)**, **Vipāśā (Beas)**, **Devikā**, **Sarayū**, **Gomatī**. (See [sacred rivers](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md).)
- **From Pāripātra** — **Vedasmṛti**, **Vedavatī**, **Vṛtraghnī**, **Carmaṇvatī (Chambal)**.
- **From Ṛkṣa** — **Śoṇa (Son)**, **Mahānadī**, **Narmadā** (in some lists), **Mandākinī**.
- **From Vindhya** — **Narmadā (Revā)**, **Surasā**, **Kṛṣṇā**, **Tāpī (Tāptī)**, **Payoṣṇī**, **Nirvindhyā**.
- **From Sahya** — **Godāvarī**, **Bhīmarathī**, **Kṛṣṇaveṇī (Kṛṣṇā)**, **Tuṅgabhadrā**, **Kāverī**.
- **From Malaya** — **Kṛtamālā (Vaigai)**, **Tāmraparṇī**.
- **From Mahendra** — **Trisāmā**, **Ṛṣikulyā**, **Ikṣulā**, **Lāṅgūlinī**.
- **From Śuktimat** — **Ṛṣikulyā**, **Kumārī**, **Mandagā**, **Palāśinī**.
  - *Variant (river ↔ mountain assignment):* sources disagree which range "owns" **Narmadā** (Vindhya vs Ṛkṣa) and **Kṛṣṇā** (Vindhya vs Sahya). [disputed] The list is **open-ended** — texts append "and hundreds and thousands of others." [folk]

### The Nine sub-divisions (upadvīpas / khaṇḍas) of Bhārata

Within Bhārata-varṣa the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* (2.3) names **nine regional divisions**, the ninth being the peninsular mainland:

- **Indra-dvīpa** — **Kaserumat (Kaseru)** — **Tāmravarṇa** — **Gabhastimat** — **Nāga-dvīpa** — **Saumya** — **Gandharva** — **Vāruṇa** — and the **ninth, the central mainland** (sometimes itself called **Kumāra / Bhārata proper**, "surrounded by the sea, 1,000 yojanas N–S").
  - *Variant (ninth name):* the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* leaves the ninth **unnamed**; later texts supply **Kumārikā-khaṇḍa** as the peninsula. [late text]

### The Janapadas — peoples & kingdoms of Bhārata

The Purāṇa enumerates the *janapadas* by quarter (*Madhyadeśa* center, *Udīcya* north, *Prācya* east, *Dakṣiṇāpatha* south, *Aparānta* west). A representative catalogue (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.3; *Mahābhārata* Bhīṣma-parva; *Matsya* 114):

- **Central (Madhyadeśa)** — **Kurus**, **Pāñcālas**, **Matsyas**, **Śūrasenas**, **Śālvas**.
- **North/Northwest (Udīcya)** — **Madras**, **Gāndhāras**, **Kāmbojas**, **Sindhu-Sauvīras**, **Saindhavas**, **Hūṇas**, **Bāhlīkas**, **Pāradas**.
- **East (Prācya)** — **Aṅgas**, **Vaṅgas**, **Magadhas**, **Puṇḍras**, **Kaliṅgas**, **Prāgjyotiṣas (Kāmarūpa/Assam)**.
- **South (Dakṣiṇāpatha)** — **Andhras**, **Pulindas**, **Vidarbhas**, **Daṇḍakas**, **Kuntalas**, **Cholas**, **Kerala/Keraḷas**, **Pāṇḍyas**, **Drāviḍas**, **Karṇāṭas**.
- **West (Aparānta)** — **Saurāṣṭras**, **Śūras**, **Ābhīras**, **Mālavas**, **Lāṭas**, **Pārasīkas (Persians)**.
- *Variant (open roster):* the janapada lists differ between *Viṣṇu*, *Vāyu*, *Matsya* and *Mārkaṇḍeya* (which adds the famous five-fold directional scheme); names of foreign peoples (Hūṇas, Pārasīkas, Cīnas) mark **late-text** layering reflecting historical contact. [late text] The roster is **open-ended** and was expanded by each recension. [scholarly]

## V. Why the Two Cosmographies Matter

- The **Bhāgavata** treats Jambudvīpa as a **theological mandala** — each varṣa an eternal heaven where a specific [avatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) is worshipped (Hayagrīva, Matsya, Kūrma, Nṛsiṃha, Varāha, Rāma, Nara-Nārāyaṇa) — making cosmography a map of *bhakti*.
- The **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** treats it as **proto-geography** — boundary ranges, rivers, peoples — closer to a gazetteer of Bhārata, and is the principal source for the *kula-parvata* and *janapada* lists.
- Both insist Bhārata alone is **karma-bhūmi**: the other eight varṣas are *bhoga-bhūmi* (lands of enjoyment without spiritual progress), so birth in Bhārata — this very land — is the rarest fortune. This anchors the entire [tīrtha-pilgrimage](README.md) economy of the tradition.
- The flat *bhū-maṇḍala* of these chapters stands in tension with the **spherical *bhū-gola*** of the *Sūrya-Siddhānta* and the astronomical Purāṇic strata — the two were never fully reconciled (see [Meru & cosmic mountains §two geographies](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md)). [scholarly]
