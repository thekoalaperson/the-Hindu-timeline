# Nandigrāma — Bharata's Regency Seat

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](README.md) → **Nandigrāma**. The village outside [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md) from which **Bharata** governed Kosala for fourteen years in bark and matted hair, with **Rāma's sandals** on the throne under the state canopy and every royal order reported to them — the capital of a kingdom whose king was elsewhere. On the historical clock, **Bharatkuṇḍ** (Nandigram) near **Bhadarsā** in Sohāwal tehsil, Ayodhya district, Uttar Pradesh — one of the five sites of the ASI's *Archaeology of the Rāmāyaṇa Sites* project.

## I. Identity & the "two clocks"

- **Nandigrāma = "the village of rejoicing"** — a settlement a short way outside **Ayodhyā** to which **Bharata** withdraws rather than take up residence in the capital, and from which he administers the kingdom of **Kosala** for the remaining years of Rāma's exile. Vālmīki names it and fixes what happens there; he does not measure the distance or describe the place. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 115)
- **Scriptural clock (Tretā-yuga)** — the regency belongs to the **Tretā-yuga** of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara, running the full span between Bharata's return from [Citrakūṭa](chitrakoot-kamadgiri.md) and Rāma's return from Laṅkā — the longest continuously-occupied stretch of the epic's timeline outside the forest itself. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*) — the mythic register; keep the **two-clocks** scriptural-vs-historical distinction.
- **Historical clock** — as a living site, **Bharatkuṇḍ** (also **Nandigram**), a village and pilgrimage place in **Sohāwal tehsil, Ayodhya (formerly Faizabad) district**, Uttar Pradesh, near the town of **Bhadarsā**, south-east of Ayodhyā on the Sultanpur road. [regional]
- **The name's irony** — a place called "village of joy" is where the epic's most sustained act of mourning is performed. The tradition has never smoothed this over; the local shrines are all shrines of waiting. [scholarly]

## II. Why Bharata goes there (*Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 112–115)

- **The sandals asked for at Citrakūṭa** — unable by any argument to move Rāma, Bharata asks instead for his **gold-adorned wooden sandals**. Rāma sets his feet in them and hands them over; Bharata carries them home **on the head of an elephant**, vowing bark and matted locks for fourteen years and **entry into fire** if Rāma is not there on the day after the fourteenth year ends. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 112) — see [Citrakūṭa](chitrakoot-kamadgiri.md)
- **The refusal to enter Ayodhyā** — arriving at the capital, Bharata tells Vasiṣṭha and the ministers plainly: "**I am going to Nandigrāma village** … there I shall be able to endure all this woe, occasioned by Rāma's absence." He will not live in the palace of a throne he has not been given. (*Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 115)
- **The kingdom held in trust** — his own formula is legal rather than sentimental: his brother "has given me this kingdom **as a trust**," and these sandals "diffuse gain and security to the people"; he will keep both until Rāma returns. (*Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 115)
- **The sandals enthroned** — at Nandigrāma the pādukā are set **on the royal throne** and the **state canopy held over them**, with the white parasol and the two golden-handled whisks used over the object and never over the man. Bharata **reports every royal order to the sandals** and refers all matters of consequence to them before acting. (*Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 115) — full treatment in [The Pādukā & the regency symbol](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/paduka-and-the-regency-symbol.md)
- **The regent's own discipline** — bark robes and matted hair "in the guise of an ascetic," the ground for a bed, plain fare, no royal dress and no royal pleasures; **Śatrughna** shares the whole vigil with him rather than the forest. (*Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 115) — see [Bharata & Śatrughna](../04-deep-dives/characters/bharata-shatrughna-and-their-wives.md)
- **A functioning administration, not a retreat** — the point the retellings blur: Kosala is **governed** from Nandigrāma for fourteen years. Ministers travel out to the village, the machinery of state runs, and the capital stands empty of its king by design. (*Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 115) [scholarly]

## III. The return (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 125–127)

- **Hanumān sent ahead** — from the returning Puṣpaka, Rāma sends **Hanumān** to Nandigrāma to reach Bharata **before** the fourteenth year expires, precisely because of the fire-vow; Hanumān recounts the whole forest years to him, and Bharata, overcome, offers him a hundred thousand cows, a hundred prime villages and **sixteen maidens of noble family** on the spot — the largest single grant Hanumān receives anywhere in the epic. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 125.43–45, 126) — see [Hanumān](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md)
- **How Bharata is found** — clad in **bark and black-antelope skin**, emaciated with fasting, carrying the **white parasol and the two whisks** that have gone unused for fourteen years, and joyful, the text says, for the first time in all that period. (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 127)
- **The sandals given back** — Bharata takes the wooden sandals and **places them himself beneath Rāma's feet**. The regency ends in the same gesture that began it, with no ceremony of abdication: the object goes back on the man. Rāma rises, takes him on his lap and embraces him. (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 127)
- **"You are a fifth brother"** — greeting Rāma's allies at Nandigrāma, Bharata says to **Sugrīva** that he is a fifth brother to the four of them — the epic's formal admission of the vānara king into the family, spoken by the one brother who never went to the war. (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 127)
- **The coronation is elsewhere** — Nandigrāma is the reunion; the **abhiṣeka** that begins Rāma-rājya is performed at [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md), which Rāma enters only after Bharata has handed the kingdom back. (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 127–128)

## IV. The living site — Bharatkuṇḍ

- **The kuṇḍ and the shrine** — the site is centred on a large tank, **Bharat Kuṇḍ**, with a temple complex to **Bharata**; local tradition holds that the *pādukā* were enthroned here and that Bharata performed Daśaratha's obsequies at the tank, which makes it a **śrāddha-tīrtha** as well as a Rāma-kṣetra. (sthala-tradition) [regional] [folk]
- **A pādukā-shrine rather than a Rāma-shrine** — what is venerated at Nandigrāma is characteristically the **sandals and the absence**, not an enthroned Rāma; the site's devotional temper is Bharata's, and it draws pilgrims for the ideal of *bhrātṛ-bhakti* (brother-devotion) and renunciant service more than for darśana of the Lord. [regional] — see [The Pādukā](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/paduka-and-the-regency-symbol.md)
- **On the Ayodhyā circuit** — Nandigrāma sits on the **pañca-kośī / caudah-kośī** pilgrimage orbit of the Ayodhyā region and is visited as an outer station of the Rāma-janmabhūmi complex rather than as an independent destination. [regional] — see [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md), [Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)
- **The exile-route sequence** — on the epic's own map Nandigrāma is the **northern anchor** of the route whose other stations are [Śṛṅgaverapura](sringaverapura-guha-ganga-crossing.md) on the Gaṅgā, Bharadvāja's āśrama at [Prayāga](prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md), and [Citrakūṭa](chitrakoot-kamadgiri.md): Bharata travels the whole of it out and back with an army, twice. (*Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 83–115)

## V. The archaeology — two clocks kept apart

- **The Rāmāyaṇa-sites project** — **Nandigrām** was one of the **five** sites (with **Ayodhyā**, **Bharadvāja āśrama / Prayāga**, **Śṛṅgaverapura** and **Citrakūṭa**) selected by **B. B. Lal** for the ASI-funded *Archaeology of the Rāmāyaṇa Sites* project begun in **1975** and excavated through the late 1970s, to test the epic's exile geography against the archaeological record. [scholarly] [historical]
- **What such digs can and cannot show** — the excavations bear on the **continuity of settlement** at places tradition names, not on the dating of the events; the *scriptural* clock places the regency in the Tretā-yuga, while the *historical* clock reads the mounds on their own stratigraphy. The two must not be collapsed into one another. [scholarly]
- **A contested project** — the Rāmāyaṇa-sites programme and its principal investigator have themselves been the subject of sustained historiographical dispute over method and inference; the corpus records the excavations as excavations and the identifications as identifications. [scholarly] [disputed]

## VI. Why the place matters

- **A capital of refusal** — Nandigrāma is the only seat of government in the epics that exists in order **not** to be a capital. Its whole significance is negative: it is where the throne is not sat on, the dress is not worn, and the city is not entered. [scholarly]
- **The doctrine it stages** — that sovereignty inheres in the rightful claim rather than in occupancy, and that the claim can be represented by a token, is asserted nowhere in the Rāmāyaṇa as a proposition; it is **enacted at Nandigrāma for fourteen years** and left for the reader to draw. [scholarly] — see [The Pādukā & the regency symbol](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/paduka-and-the-regency-symbol.md)
- **Rāma-rājya before Rāma** — later tradition reads the Nandigrāma years as the first instalment of **Rāma-rājya**: a kingdom well governed in the king's absence by a regent who claims nothing, held up in bhakti and in modern political rhetoric alike as the model of rule-as-service. [scholarly] [regional]
