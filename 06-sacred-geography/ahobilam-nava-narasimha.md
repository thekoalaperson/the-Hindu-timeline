# Ahobilam — The Nava Narasiṃha Kṣetra

> The "Lion-Hill" of **Tiru Siṅgavēḷ Kuṇḍram** in the Nallamala forest of Andhra Pradesh (Kurnool district): the prime **Narasiṃha kṣetra** of India, held to be the very hill on which **Vishnu burst from the pillar** as the Man-Lion to slay **Hiraṇyakaśipu**. It is at once a **[Divya Desam](./108-divya-desams.md)** sung by the Ālvārs, the asylum-seat of the **Ahobila Maṭha**, and a circuit of **nine** distinct Narasiṃha shrines (*Nava Narasiṃha*) strung across the hill.
>

**Ahobilam** (Telugu *Ahōbilaṃ*; Tamil *Tiru Siṅgavēḷ Kuṇḍram*, "the holy lion-precinct") lies in the **Nallamala hills** west of the Penna basin, about 150 km south of Kurnool. The *kṣetra* is split into two stations: **Eguva Ahobilam** ("Upper," the hill-top original shrine of the fierce Ugra Narasiṃha) and **Diguva Ahobilam** ("Lower," the valley town with the Prahlāda-Varada / Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha temple). Of the [108 Divya Desams](./108-divya-desams.md) this is the single one consecrated to **Narasiṃha**, sung by **Tirumaṅgai Ālvār**. Below, the *traditional* (Yuga / Purāṇic) frame is kept distinct from the *historical* (Vijayanagara-era) frame — the **"two clocks."** [scholarly]

## The sthala-purāṇa — why the lion-deed happened *here*

- **The pillar on the hill** — the *Ahobila-māhātmya* (set within the **Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa**) localizes the climax of the **[Prahlāda–Hiraṇyakaśipu](../04-deep-dives/prahlada-narasimha.md)** cycle to this exact peak: challenged "is your Vishnu in *this* pillar?", **Hiraṇyakaśipu** strikes the column, and **Narasiṃha** erupts from it on the hill-side to void the [Brahmā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)-given boon (neither day nor night, indoors nor out, man nor beast, on earth nor in sky). (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, *Ahobila-māhātmya*; *Bhāgavata* 7; *Vishnu Purāṇa* 1)
- **"Ahobalam / Ahobilam" — the naming** — two competing etymologies are given for the place-name in the *māhātmya* itself:
  - *Variant (Ahō-balam, "O the strength!"):* the **devas**, watching the Man-Lion tear the *daitya*, cry **"Ahō balam!"** ("Oh, what might!") — naming the hill for the Lord's overwhelming strength. [regional]
  - *Variant (Ahō-bilam, "O the cave!"):* the gods marvel instead **"Ahō bilam!"** ("Oh, what a cave!") at the great mountain cleft / *bila* (cavern) from which Narasiṃha sprang — tying the name to the natural cave-shrine of Ugra Narasiṃha. [regional]
- **Garuḍa's vision** — a strand of the māhātmya holds that **Garuḍa** performed tapas in the Nallamala and was granted a *darśana* of all nine Narasiṃha forms scattered across the hill — establishing the *Nava Narasiṃha* as a pre-existing, self-revealed (*svayam-vyakta*) set rather than a founded one. [traditional] [folk]

## The Nava Narasiṃha — the nine hill-shrines

The canonical circuit fixes **nine** Narasiṃha shrines around the *kṣetra*, each with its own posture (*kolam*), its own legend, and its own spring/tīrtha — enumerated exactly as the corpus enumerates the [Pañcha Kedār](./pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md) or the [Aṣṭavināyaka](./ashtavinayaka.md). Order and even membership shift slightly between recensions; the standard pilgrim sequence runs from the lower town up the gorge and back. (Ahobila-kṣetra-māhātmya; Ahobila Maṭha tradition)

- **Bhārgava Narasiṃha** — ~2 km from Lower Ahobilam beside the **Bhārgava-tīrtha** (Akṣaya-tīrtha) tank, where the sage **Bhārgava (Paraśurāma's line)** is said to have done penance and worshipped the Lord; a seated, relatively calm form. (Ahobila-māhātmya)
- **Yōgānanda Narasiṃha** — ~2 km from Lower Ahobilam; the Lord in **yōga-mudrā / yogic posture**, who (after the slaying, his fury cooled) taught **Prahlāda** the disciplines of *yoga* — hence *Yōga-ānanda*, "bliss of yoga." A teaching, pacified aspect. (Ahobila-māhātmya; Ahobila Maṭha tradition)
- **Chatravaṭa (Chenchu) Narasiṃha** — set under a sacred **banyan/peepal (*vaṭa*) canopy** ("the shaded one") amid thickets, where Narasiṃha is worshipped as a *Gandharva*-loving form of music and dance; a small shrine by the path. (Ahobila-māhātmya)
- **Ahōbila / Ugra Narasiṃha** — the **mūla-vīrya** (root-original) and supreme shrine, in a **natural cave (*bila*) at Upper Ahobilam**; the **fierce (*ugra*)** Man-Lion frozen in the very act of the kill, the *svayam-vyakta* heart of the whole kṣetra to which all the others are satellites. (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, *Ahobila-māhātmya*; Ahobila Maṭha)
- **Vārāha / Krōḍa (Krōḍākāra) Narasiṃha** — near the Ugra shrine; Narasiṃha with the **boar-face (*krōḍa* / Varāha aspect)**, worshipped beside **Bhū-devī / Lakṣmī**, fusing the third and fourth [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) much as at [Siṃhācalam](./simhachalam-varaha-narasimha.md). (Ahobila-māhātmya)
- **Mālōla Narasiṃha** — about 2 km uphill from the Ugra cave; the **gentle, consort-bearing** form — *Mā* (**Lakṣmī**) + *lōla* ("dear to / fond of"), Narasiṃha with the goddess on his lap, *saumya* (benign). The Ahobila Maṭha's **processional icon (*utsava-mūrti*)** is the Mālōla Narasiṃha, carried by the Jīyars on tour. (Ahobila-māhātmya; Ahobila Maṭha tradition)
- **Jvālā Narasiṃha** — high up the gorge near the **Rakta-kuṇḍa** ("blood-pool"); the **flaming (*jvālā*), most ferocious** form, marking the exact spot where the Lord is said to have **torn Hiraṇyakaśipu apart** — the demon's blood reddening the kuṇḍa below. (Ahobila-māhātmya)
- **Pāvana Narasiṃha** — the remotest shrine, ~6 km into the forest by the **Pāvana / Bhavanāśinī stream**; the **purifying (*pāvana*) and most peaceful** form, reckoned the *most merit-giving darśana* of the nine (sometimes called *Kṣīra* / Pāmuleti Narasiṃha). [regional] (Ahobila-māhātmya)
- **Kāraṇja / Kārañcha Narasiṃha** — between Lower and Upper Ahobilam under a **Kāraṇja (Indian-beech) tree**, **Ādiśeṣa** shading the Lord with his hood; a seated form on the climb. (Ahobila-māhātmya)
  - *Counting note:* recensions vary which ninth completes the set — most list **Kāraṇja**, while some traditions count a separate **Chenchu-Lakṣmī Narasiṃha** shrine (the tribal-bride form) or fold **Pāmuleti** in; the canonical number stays **nine**. [disputed]

## The Chenchu-Lakṣmī marriage legend

- **The tribal bride of the forest** — the most famous local legend has Narasiṃha, lingering on the hill after the slaying, **fall in love with Chenchu-Lakṣmī (Chenchitā)**, a maiden of the **Chenchu** forest tribe of the Nallamala — and **marry her** in tribal rite, so that the supreme Lord takes a *vana-vāsi* (forest-dweller) consort. (Ahobila-kṣetra-māhātmya; Chenchu oral tradition)
  - *Variant (Lakṣmī as the Chenchu girl):* the Chenchu maiden is **Lakṣmī herself**, descended in tribal form to **pacify the still-raging Ugra Narasiṃha** — the goddess cooling the wrath that even the slaying had not stilled. [regional]
- **Why the Chenchus claim the Lord** — the legend makes the **Chenchu people the Lord's in-laws (*bāva-maridi*)**; to this day Chenchu devotees hold hereditary ritual rights and an honored welcome at the festival, the rare case of a forest-tribe woven into a great Vaiṣṇava kṣetra's myth. [regional] [folk] (Wikipedia, *Ahobilam*; ethnographic accounts of the Chenchu)

## The Ahobila Maṭha (the institutional clock)

- **A wandering Śrīvaiṣṇava throne** — Ahobilam is the seat of the **Ahobila Maṭha**, a major **Vaḍagalai Śrīvaiṣṇava** monastic lineage founded **c. 1398 CE** whose pontiffs bear the title **Jīyar / Aḻagiyasiṅgar**; uniquely its head is a **perpetually itinerant** *saṃnyāsin* who tours with the **Mālōla Narasiṃha** *utsava-mūrti* rather than sitting at one seat. [scholarly] (Ahobila Maṭha tradition)
- **Foundation legend** — tradition says **Narasiṃha himself** appeared to the first Jīyar (**Ādivaṇ Śaṭhakōpa Yatīndra Mahādēśikan**) and handed over the Mālōla icon and the duty to propagate Vaiṣṇava *prapatti* (self-surrender) — directly tying the institution to the deity. [traditional]
- **Vijayanagara patronage — the standing temples** — the masonry temples at Upper and Lower Ahobilam are largely **Vijayanagara-era** (15th–16th c. CE); kings of the line, and the **Maṭha**, endowed and rebuilt the shrines — the *historical* clock under which continuous worship is firmly attested, against the *traditional* claim of a Yuga-old self-manifest hill. [scholarly] (epigraphy; Wikipedia, *Ahobilam*)

## Network & cross-links

- **A Narasiṃha Divya Desam** — as one of the [108 Divya Desams](./108-divya-desams.md), Ahobilam falls in the northern (Vaḍa-nāḍu) group alongside [Tirumala–Tirupati](./tirumala-tirupati-venkateshwara.md); **Tirumaṅgai Ālvār** sang its *maṅgalāśāsanam*, making it the canon's lone Man-Lion abode. (Nālāyira Divya Prabandham)
- **The Andhra Narasiṃha axis** — devotionally paired with **[Siṃhācalam](./simhachalam-varaha-narasimha.md)** (the Varāha-Narasiṃha of Visakhapatnam) and with **Mangalagiri** and **Yādagirigutta** in an informal pan-Andhra **Narasiṃha-kṣetra** circuit. [regional]
