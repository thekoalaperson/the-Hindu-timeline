# Nathdwārā — Śrīnāthajī, Chief Shrine of the Puṣṭimārga

> **Śrīnāthajī (Govardhana-nātha)** of **Nathdwārā** (Rajasthan, on the Banās, ~48 km NE of Udaipur) — the self-manifest **Govardhana-dhāraṇa** icon of Kṛṣṇa lifting the hill, supreme deity and **head shrine (nija-mandir / havelī)** of the **Vallabha Puṣṭimārga** (Śuddhādvaita Vaiṣṇavism). Worshipped as a living child-king through the eight-fold daily **sevā**, the deity was carried from **Braj to Mewār in 1672 CE** to escape Aurangzeb — the historical pivot of this node. As with all *māhātmya* sites, the **scriptural clock** (the Govardhana-līlā of the Dvāpara Yuga) and the **historical clock** (Vallabha's manifestation legend and the documented 17th-c. relocation) run separately and are kept apart below.

## I. The deity — Govardhana-nātha, the hill-lifting child

- **Śrīnāthajī's form** — a ~1.4 m black-stone (or basalt) icon of the boy **Kṛṣṇa with his left arm raised overhead** (lifting **Govardhana** hill on his upraised hand) and the right fist on the waist, lips pursed as if speaking, lotus-eyed, with a serpent, lion, parrot, two cows, two peacocks and sages carved on the slab. The raised-arm posture *is* the **Govardhana-dhāraṇa** mudrā — the deity is Kṛṣṇa frozen in the act of sheltering Braj. (Puṣṭimārga tradition)
- **Svayam-vyakta (self-manifest)** — the icon is held to be **not man-made but self-revealed (*svayambhū / prākaṭya*)**, emerging from **Govardhana hill** itself; the arm appeared first, then over time the full body, the deity rising of its own accord from the sacred hill that is Kṛṣṇa's own body. (Puṣṭimārga *vārtā* literature) [folk]
- **Names** — **Devadamana / Devadmana** ("subduer of the gods," i.e. Indra) is the older Braj name of the icon; **Gopāla / Govardhana-dhara**; renamed **Śrī Nāthajī** ("the Lord") by **Vallabhācārya**. He is treated as **Ṭhākurjī**, a living royal child served, not a static mūrti. (Puṣṭimārga tradition)
- **Theological status** — in **Śuddhādvaita** ("pure non-dualism") and the **Puṣṭimārga** ("path of grace/nourishment") of [Vallabhācārya](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md), Śrīnāthajī is **Pūrṇa Puruṣottama**, the complete Kṛṣṇa — supreme Brahman descended in grace, not merely an avatāra-vyūha. (Śuddhādvaita doctrine)

## II. Scriptural backdrop — the Govardhana-līlā (Dvāpara Yuga)

The icon's posture is the visual quotation of a **[Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md)** episode set in the **Dvāpara Yuga** — the "two clocks" begin here, in mythic time.

- **The Indra-yajña stopped** — at **Vṛndāvana/Govardhana** the boy Kṛṣṇa persuades **Nanda** and the cowherds to abandon the annual sacrifice to **Indra** and instead worship **Govardhana hill** and the **cows** — the real sustainers of pastoral life. (*Bhāgavata* 10.24)
- **Indra's storm** — the affronted **Indra** looses the **Sāṃvartaka** doomsday clouds, deluging Braj for seven days. (*Bhāgavata* 10.25)
- **Govardhana-dhāraṇa** — Kṛṣṇa **lifts Govardhana on the little finger of his left hand** and holds it as an umbrella for **seven days and nights**, sheltering all the cowherds, cows and Braj beneath — the act the Nathdwārā icon eternally portrays. Indra submits; the hill is thereafter worshipped as Kṛṣṇa himself (**Girirāja-jī**). (*Bhāgavata* 10.25–27)
- **Annakūṭa / Govardhana Pūjā** — the "mountain of food" festival (day after Dīpāvalī) re-enacts the cowherds' offering to the hill; at Nathdwārā it is the **chief annual utsava**, a literal hill of cooked rice and sweets heaped before Śrīnāthajī. (Puṣṭimārga calendar; *Bhāgavata* 10.24)
- **Link to the avatāra-scheme** — Kṛṣṇa as the **pūrṇāvatāra** of [Viṣṇu's ten descents](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md); Puṣṭimārga reads him as the source (*avatārī*) rather than one item in the list. (Śuddhādvaita)

## III. Vallabhācārya and the manifestation legend (late 15th–early 16th c.)

- **Prākaṭya on Govardhana** — tradition holds the raised **arm of the deity** had emerged from **Govardhana hill** (near **Āniyor / Jatipura** village, west of Mathurā) and was locally worshipped; the deity revealed his full identity to **Vallabhācārya (1479–1531)** on **Govardhana**, who recognised him as **Devadamana / Gopāla** and established formal **sevā**. The traditional date of Vallabha's encounter is **Saṃvat 1549 / c. 1493 CE**. (Puṣṭimārga *vārtā*; *Caurāsī Baiṭhak* tradition) [folk]
- **Sthāpana of worship** — Vallabha builds the first shrine on Govardhana and institutes the ritual order; his **84 and 252 Vaiṣṇavas (Caurāsī / Bāvan Vaiṣṇavan kī Vārtā)** hagiographies record the early devotee-community. (Puṣṭimārga hagiography) [late text]
- **Viṭṭhalanātha (Gusāīṃjī, 1515–1585/86)** — Vallabha's second son **systematises the sevā** into the **aṣṭa-yāma** (eightfold daily darśana) and the **Puṣṭimārga aesthetic** of song (kīrtana of the **Aṣṭachāp** poets), cuisine, dress and ornament; he divides the deities and seats among his **seven sons (Sāt Gharānā / seven houses / nidhis)**, of which Śrīnāthajī's house is the senior. (Puṣṭimārga tradition)
- **The Aṣṭachāp** — the eight poet-singers (notably **Sūrdās**, **Kumbhandās**, **Paramānandadās**, **Govindasvāmī**) whose Braj-bhāṣā padas are sung before the deity at each darśana — devotional verse woven directly into the liturgy. (Puṣṭimārga; Sūr-sāgara) [scholarly]
- **Vallabha's *Subodhinī*** — Vallabha's incomplete Sanskrit commentary on the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa**, the doctrinal charter reading the Govardhana- and rāsa-līlās as the supreme play of grace; with the *Ṣoḍaśa-grantha* and *Aṇubhāṣya* it grounds Śuddhādvaita. (Vallabha, *Subodhinī*) [scholarly]

## IV. The 1672 relocation — flight from Aurangzeb to Mewār

The defining **historical-clock** event of this node: the icon's journey **from Braj to Rajasthan**, well documented in Puṣṭimārga chronicles.

- **The threat** — under **Aurangzeb's** temple-destruction campaign in **Braj** (the Keśavadeva/Mathurā temple was demolished **1670**), the Govardhana deity was endangered, and the **Tilakāyat** (head priest, then **Dāmodaradāsa / Goswāmī Dāūjī** line) resolved to move Śrīnāthajī to safety. (Nathdwārā temple history) [scholarly]
- **The journey (1669–1672)** — the icon is carried out of Braj **c. 1669–70**, travelling by **bullock-cart** through Āgrā, Gwalior, Kota and Bundi region into **Mewār**, seeking a Rājput protector. (Nathdwārā Temple Board history)
- **The cart that would not move** — at the village of **Sīhāḍ / Siṅhāḍ** (Mewār), the **cart-wheels sank and would not move further**, taken as the deity's own choice to settle there — the foundation-miracle of the new shrine (echoing the Śrīraṅgam "deity refuses to rise" motif). (Nathdwārā tradition) [folk]
- **Rāṇā Rāj Siṃh's protection** — **Mahārāṇā Rāj Siṃh I of Mewār (r. 1652–1680)** grants asylum and patronage; the shrine is built at Siṅhāḍ, renamed **Nāthdvāra** ("gateway of the Lord"). The icon is **installed Saṃvat 1728 / 1672 CE**. (Mewār–Nathdwārā history) [scholarly]
- **The havelī, not a temple** — the shrine is built as a **havelī (mansion)**, the **nanda-bhavan / Nanda's house**, so the deity lives as a **royal child in a home**, not a god in a temple — central to Puṣṭimārga's domestic, intimate mood (*vātsalya / dāsya-bhāva*). (Puṣṭimārga tradition)
  - *Variant (dating) [disputed]:* sources place departure from Braj variously **1669, 1670 or 1671**, with installation at Nathdwārā fixed at **1672 CE (V.S. 1728/29)**; the en-route halts and years differ across chronicles.
  - *Variant (other Braj deities) [regional]:* the **same Aurangzeb-era flight** scattered the **Seven Houses** — e.g. **Mathureśajī to Kota, Dvārkādhīśa to Kankroli, Viṭṭhalanāthajī to Nathdwārā/Kota, Gokulnāthajī, Bālakṛṣṇa** — across Rajasthan; Śrīnāthajī's removal is the most celebrated. See [Braj rediscovery & relocations](mathura-vrindavan-braj.md).

## V. Sevā & darśana — the eightfold day

- **Aṣṭa-yāma sevā (eight services)** — Śrīnāthajī is served through **eight daily darśanas** marking the deity's day as a living child: **Maṅgalā** (dawn waking), **Śṛṅgāra** (adornment), **Gvāla** (cowherd departure), **Rājabhoga** (midday feast), **Utthāpana** (afternoon rousing), **Bhoga** (evening snack), **Sandhyā / Ārtī** (dusk), **Śayana** (putting to bed). Each opens briefly, with its own **pada**, dress and offering. (Puṣṭimārga sevā-paddhati)
- **Sevā not pūjā** — Puṣṭimārga insists the deity is **served (sevā)** out of love like a family child, **not worshipped (pūjā)** for boons; ritual is bhāva-led intimacy (*lālana*), the heart of the "path of grace." (Śuddhādvaita doctrine)
- **The Tilakāyat** — the **hereditary head priest-mahārāja** of the senior house, a direct **descendant of Vallabhācārya**, is the sole custodian who performs the central sevā; the Nathdwārā **Tilakāyat** is the spiritual head of the whole Puṣṭimārga. (Puṣṭimārga institution)
- **Śṛṅgāra & seasonal sevā** — costume, jewels, backdrop-cloth (**piccavāī**), flowers, food and rāga **change with the season, festival and time of day** — yielding the celebrated **Nathdwārā piccavāī painting** and **Nāthadvāra school** of art. (art-historical) [scholarly]
- **No festival of birth here** — uniquely, the deity's great festivals are the **līlā-events** — **Annakūṭa (Govardhana Pūjā)**, **Janmāṣṭamī**, **Holī / Ḍolotsava**, **Naukā-vihāra (boat-līlā)**, **Hindolā (swing, monsoon)** — re-enacting Braj within the havelī. (Puṣṭimārga calendar)
- **Pilgrimage circuit** — Nathdwārā anchors a Rajasthani Puṣṭimārga circuit with **Kankroli (Dvārkādhīśa)**, **Kota (Mathureśa)** and the **Caurāsī Baiṭhak** seats of Vallabha across India; theologically it remains tethered to **Govardhana in Braj**, its true origin-hill. (Puṣṭimārga tradition) — see [Sthala-purāṇas of Major Temples](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md).
