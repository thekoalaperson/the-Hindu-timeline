# Sthala-Purāṇas — Major Temple Origin Legends

> Origin legends (*sthala-purāṇa* / *kṣetra-māhātmya*) of major living temples **not** covered by the [12 Jyotirlingas](12-jyotirlingas.md) or [51 Śakti Pīṭhas](51-shakti-peethas.md) — each a self-glorifying "deity-came-to-this-spot" narrative that fixes the site on the cosmic timeline (which Yuga, which avatar, which sage). Per-temple: the legend, its scriptural/datable framing, and all source-attributed variants.
>

A **sthala-purāṇa** (also *kṣetra-māhātmya*, *talapurāṇam* in Tamil) is the local "scripture of a place" — a text or oral cycle explaining how a particular deity chose a particular spot, usually framing it against a Purāṇic timeframe (a named Yuga, an avatar's afterlife, a sage's *tapas*). Such texts are typically **medieval to early-modern** in datable form even when they narrate **Satya/Tretā/Dvāpara-Yuga** events; the **CATALOG-ALL-VARIANTS** policy is acute here, since rival sects, dynasties, and *maṭhas* each preserve their own recension. Below, *traditional* (Yuga / Purāṇa-internal) framing is distinguished from *academic* (manuscript / inscription / archaeology) dating throughout. [scholarly]

## I. Vaiṣṇava Kṣetras

### Tirumala–Tirupati — Veṅkaṭeśvara / Śrīnivāsa (the Kali-Yuga refuge)

- **Veṅkaṭeśvara / Bālājī of the Seven Hills (Veṅkaṭācala / Śeṣācala)** — Andhra Pradesh; the form of **[Vishnu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** held to be the *self-manifest (svayambhū)* refuge of dharma in the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** — the Purāṇas declare the temple Vishnu's own home in this age, the best place to obtain his grace as the four Yugas decline. Source-text is the **Veṅkaṭācala-Māhātmya** (drawn on the *Varāha*, *Bhaviṣyottara*, *Padma*, *Brahmāṇḍa*, *Skanda*, *Mārkaṇḍeya* Purāṇas). Academically the temple is attested from **c. 8th–9th c. CE** (Pallava/Chola/Vijayanagara patronage). (Veṅkaṭācala-Māhātmya; Britannica, *Venkateswara*; Wikipedia, *Venkateswara*)
- **The Bhṛgu-kick and Lakṣmī's departure** — Sage **Bhṛgu**, sent to test which of the Trimūrti is supreme, kicks Vishnu on the chest (the seat of **[Lakṣmī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)**); Vishnu humbly massages the foot, crushing the extra eye of ego in Bhṛgu's sole. Offended that her abode was kicked, Lakṣmī leaves Vaikuṇṭha for earth — the trigger for Vishnu to descend after her as **Śrīnivāsa** ("abode of Śrī"). (Veṅkaṭācala-Māhātmya; AstroVed)
- **Śrīnivāsa, the termite-mound, and Padmāvatī** — On Veṅkaṭa hill Vishnu-Śrīnivāsa dwells in a *valmīka* (termite-mound); while hunting an elephant he meets **Padmāvatī**, princess of the Cōḷa/Tondaman king (a re-incarnation of Lakṣmī born of the earth/lotus), and weds her. (Britannica; ExoticIndia, *Goddess Padmavati*)
- **Kubera's wedding-loan** — To pay the bride-price/wedding costs Śrīnivāsa borrows immense wealth from **Kubera**, the treasurer-god; devotees' offerings at Tirumala are traditionally framed as **repaying this perpetual divine debt** — a folk theology directly tied to the temple's legendary wealth. (Wikipedia, *Venkateswara*; jayanthdev.com)
  - *Variant (kṣetra-pālaka / Varāha priority):* the hill is **Varāha-kṣetra** — the **[Varāha avatar](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** stayed here after lifting the earth and slaying **Hiraṇyākṣa**, becoming guardian of the field; Śrīnivāsa must obtain Varāha's permission to settle, and pilgrims are enjoined to take *darśana* of **Varāhasvāmī first**. (radhakrishnatemple.net, *Sthala Purana of Tirumala*)
  - *Variant (which goddess):* the consort enshrined at hilltop is **Alamelumaṅgā / Padmāvatī** at nearby **Tiruchanur**, distinguished from the **Lakṣmī on Veṅkaṭeśvara's chest** — some traditions treat them as one Lakṣmī, others as two distinct manifestations. [disputed]
  - *Variant (Śaiva claim):* a minority older stratum read the *svayambhū* mūrti as **Śiva/Subrahmaṇya/Devī** before its firm Vaiṣṇava identification — a much-debated sectarian-history question, not part of the mainstream sthala-purāṇa. [disputed] [scholarly]

### Jagannātha Purī — Indradyumna & the unfinished images

- **Jagannātha, Balabhadra & Subhadrā (Puruṣottama-kṣetra)** — Odisha; a triad of **abstract, armless–legless wooden** images of **[Krishna/Vishnu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** with brother Balabhadra and sister Subhadrā, in the **Char Dhām** (East — see [char-dham](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)). The icon is **Dāru-Brahman**, "the Absolute in wood." Legend recorded in the **Madala Pañji** (temple chronicle), *Skanda Purāṇa* (Puruṣottama-kṣetra-māhātmya), *Brahma* & *Narada* Purāṇas. Academically the present temple dates to the **Ganga king Anantavarman Coḍagaṅga, 12th c. CE**. (Wikipedia, *Jagannath Temple, Puri*; visitodisha.net)
- **King Indradyumna of Satya Yuga** — a **Mālava** king (named in the *Mahābhārata* and Purāṇas) longs to worship Vishnu in his form as **Nīla-Mādhava**, a sapphire image secretly worshipped by the tribal **Śabara** chief **Viśvāvasu** in the forest. The king's brahmin emissary **Vidyāpati** marries the chief's daughter **Lalitā** to find it; but Nīla-Mādhava vanishes, telling the king to instead worship a **dāru** (sacred log) that will float ashore at **Bāṅki-Muhāna**. (visitodisha.net; shreekhetra.com)
- **The unfinished carving** — Vishnu (in some tellings **Viśvakarmā**, the gods' architect) comes disguised as an aged carpenter, agreeing to carve the images only if left **undisturbed inside a sealed chamber** for **21 days** (var. 15). Anxious at the silence, **Queen Guṇḍicā / King Indradyumna opens the door early**; the carpenter vanishes, leaving the images **without fully-formed hands and feet** — held ever since to be the deity's chosen, deliberately incomplete form. **Brahmā himself** is then brought from Brahmaloka to consecrate the temple. (Wikipedia, *Jagannath*; jkyog.in)
  - *Variant (carpenter's identity):* the divine sculptor is named as **Viśvakarmā** (gods' architect), or as **Vishnu/Ananta in disguise**, depending on the recension. [folk]
  - *Variant (origin of the wood):* the *dāru* is glossed as the bone/remains of **Krishna** after his cremation at the close of the **[Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md)** — linking the log to Krishna's mortal departure and the Yuga-turn into Kali. [folk]
  - *Variant (number of days):* sealed-chamber span given as **21 days** or **15 days** before the premature opening. [disputed]

### Guruvāyūr — Krishna installed by Guru & Vāyu

- **Guruvāyūrappan ("the Dwārakā of the South")** — Kerala (Thrissur); a standing four-armed **[Krishna](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** mūrti in his form of *Mahāviṣṇu*, the very image **Krishna himself worshipped at Dwārakā**. The name = **Guru** (Bṛhaspati, preceptor of the gods) + **Vāyu** (wind-god) + *ūr* (place). Academically attested from **c. 16th–17th c.** literature (the *Nārāyaṇīyam* of Melputtūr, 1586 CE); claimed far older in tradition. (Wikipedia, *Guruvayur Temple*; templepurohit.com)
- **The icon that survived Dwārakā's flood** — before his departure Krishna tells minister **Uddhava** that, as the sea will engulf **[Dwārakā](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md)** at the **Dvāpara→Kali** hinge, his worship-image will float free, to be installed by **Bṛhaspati (Guru)**. Guru and **Vāyu** carry the recovered mūrti south, meet **[Paraśurāma](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** (who had reclaimed Kerala from the sea), and install it by a lotus-lake — hence the spot is named for Guru and Vāyu, and the date is fixed at the **dawn of Kali Yuga**. (guruvayoorlive.in; hindu-blog.com)
  - *Variant (prior worshippers):* the same mūrti is said to have been worshipped successively by **Brahmā, Sutapas & Pṛśni / Kaśyapa & Aditi, then Vasudeva & Devakī** across cosmic ages before reaching Krishna — a lineage of divine owners stretching back to creation. [folk]

### Thiruvananthapuram — Anantapadmanābhasvāmī

- **Padmanābhasvāmī (Ananta-śayana Vishnu)** — Kerala; **[Vishnu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** reclining on the serpent **[Ananta-Śeṣa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md)**, viewed through three doors (face, navel-lotus with Brahmā, feet). Source: **Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa**; the *kṣetra* is **Ananta-kāḍu** ("Ananta's forest"). The reclining iconography ties it to the cosmic **yoga-nidrā** between dissolutions (see [00-time-and-cosmology](../00-time-and-cosmology/README.md)). Academically the great temple is **medieval**, monumentally rebuilt under **Mārtāṇḍa Varma (18th c.)**. (Wikipedia, *Padmanabhaswamy Temple*; Britannica)
- **Divākara Muni and the mischievous boy** — the sage Divākara Muni, devotee of Vishnu, is visited by a divine **little boy** who defiles his *śālagrāma* by putting it in his mouth; rebuked, the boy vanishes, saying he may be found in **Ananta-kāḍu** forest. The sage pursues him into a giant **ilappa tree** which falls and **transforms into Vishnu reclining on Ananta** — so vast that head, navel and feet stand miles apart (Thiruvallam to Trippāppūr); the sage prays the colossal form shrink to be enshrined. (gayatriheritage.com; medium.com/@starkworld)
  - *Variant (traditional dating):* some defenders date the temple via inscriptions and the **Saptarṣi calendar** to **c. 3176 BCE** (i.e. just after the Kali-Yuga start) — a *traditional/disputed* claim rejected by academic historians. [disputed] [traditional]

### Pandharpur — Viṭhobā / Viṭṭhala & Puṇḍalīka

- **Viṭṭhala / Pāṇḍuraṅga (the brick-standing god)** — Maharashtra, on the **Bhīmā (Chandrabhāgā)**; a black, arms-akimbo standing **[Krishna/Vishnu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)**, the heart-deity of the **Vārkarī** *bhakti* movement (Jñāneśvar, Nāmdev, Tukārām). Name *Viṭhobā* glossed as "he who stands on a brick (*viṭ*)." Praised in the **Skanda** & **Padma** Purāṇa *Pāṇḍuraṅga-māhātmyas*; academically a **medieval** (c. 12th–13th c.) Vārkarī cult center. (Wikipedia, *Vithoba*; *Pundalik*)
- **Puṇḍalīka's filial devotion** — the once-neglectful son **Puṇḍalīka**, reformed into the model of *mātṛ-pitṛ-bhakti* (service to parents), is serving his parents when **Vishnu/Krishna arrives** at his door. Unwilling to interrupt his duty, Puṇḍalīka tosses out a **brick** for the Lord to wait upon — and so pleased is Vishnu that he **stays, standing on the brick**, as Viṭṭhala. The legend exalts service-to-parents *above* even receiving God. (hindujagruti.org; pandharpurtemple.com)
  - *Variant (which deity stands):* Viṭhobā is identified as **Vishnu/Krishna** by Vaiṣṇavas, but some traditions and the akimbo iconography are read as **Krishna-with-Rukmiṇī (Rakhumāī)**, or syncretically blended with **Śiva** and even pastoral/folk hero-stones — a genuinely composite deity. [scholarly] [folk]
  - *Variant (Puṇḍalīka's parents' pilgrimage):* a fuller telling has Puṇḍalīka shamed into reform at sage **Kukkuṭasvāmī's** hermitage, where he sees the river-goddesses **Gaṅgā, Yamunā** et al. arrive at dawn to wash off pilgrims' sins. [folk]

### Prabhādevī (Mumbai) — Shree Siddhivināyaka

- **Siddhivināyaka of Prabhādevī** — Maharashtra (Mumbai); a small **[Gaṇeśa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** shrine grown into one of India's **most-visited and wealthiest** temples, its mūrti a black-stone **right-curled-trunk (*ujavyā sondechā*)** Gaṇeśa flanked by Ṛddhi and Siddhi. Unlike the older sites here, its "sthala-purāṇa" is **purely modern**: founded **19 November 1801 CE** by the contractor **Lakṣmaṇ Viṭhu Pāṭīl** with funds from the devout, childless **Deubāī Pāṭīl** (who wished that other barren women obtain children by the deity). Academically and traditionally an **early-modern foundation**, with its pan-Indian fame a **20th–21st c.** phenomenon. (Wikipedia, *Siddhivinayak Temple, Mumbai*) [modern]
  - *Caution (not the Aṣṭavināyaka Siddhatek):* this Mumbai Siddhivināyaka is **distinct from the Aṣṭavināyaka "Siddhivinayak" at Siddhatek** on the Bhīmā river (Ahmadnagar dist.) — the *self-manifest* Aṣṭavināyaka node where **Viṣṇu gained *siddhi* to defeat Madhu–Kaiṭabha**, the only Aṣṭavināyaka mūrti with a right-curled trunk (see [Aṣṭavināyaka §2](ashtavinayaka.md)). The two share the name **Siddhivināyaka** but are **different temples**; popular sources sometimes conflate them. [disputed]

## II. Śaiva & Śaiva-Śākta Kṣetras

### Sabarimala — Ayyappa / Hariharaputra

- **Ayyappa / Dharma-Śāstā / Maṇikaṇṭha** — Kerala (Western Ghats); **Hariharaputra**, the son born of **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** and **[Vishnu-as-Mohinī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** — uniquely a deity uniting the two great sects. Worshipped after a **41-day vrata** and the *Irumudi* trek; the *Bhūtanātha-gītā* / oral *Śāstā* tradition supplies the legend. The hill-shrine cult is documented from the **medieval** period; the Pandalam dynasty link is **early-modern**. (Wikipedia, *Ayyappan*; hariharakshethram.com)
- **Born to slay Mahiṣī** — the buffalo-demoness **Mahiṣī** (sister of **[Mahiṣāsura](51-shakti-peethas.md)**) holds a boon that only a child of **both Hari and Hara** can kill her. So Vishnu takes the female **Mohinī** form (cf. the **[Samudra Manthana](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md)**), unites with Śiva, and **Hariharaputra** is born to fulfill the impossible condition. (utsavapp.in; hindu-blog.com)
- **Maṇikaṇṭha of Pandalam** — the divine infant, a bell (*maṇi*) at his throat (*kaṇṭha*), is found on the **Pampā** river-bank and raised by the childless king **Rājaśekhara of Pandalam**; he fetches **tiger's milk** (riding the disguised Mahiṣī/leopard), defeats her, and reveals his divinity, asking that a shrine be built where his arrow lands — **Sabarimala**, named for the *Rāmāyaṇa* devotee **Śabarī**. (utsavapp.in; drikpanchang.com)
  - *Variant (Vāvar):* the Muslim warrior-companion **Vāvar** is honored with a mosque-shrine on the pilgrimage route — a syncretic layer of the legend. [folk]
  - *Variant (historicization):* some read Ayyappa as a **deified historical chieftain/warrior** of Pandalam who suppressed a bandit named **Udayanan**, later merged with the Śāstā deity. [scholarly]

### Madurai — Mīnākṣī-Sundareśvara

- **Mīnākṣī ("fish-eyed") & Sundareśvara** — Tamil Nadu; the warrior-queen goddess (a form of **[Pārvatī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)**) and her consort **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** as "the handsome one." Source: the Tamil **Tiruviḷaiyāḍal Purāṇam** (Śiva's 64 "sacred games/sports" in Madurai), redacted by **Parañcōti Munivar (c. 16th–17th c.)**; the temple complex is largely **Nāyaka-period (17th c.)** over older Pāṇḍya foundations. (Wikipedia, *Meenakshi Temple*; mythicremembering.com)
- **The three-breasted princess** — born of a *yajña* to the childless Pāṇḍya king **Malayadhvaja**, the girl **Taḍātakai/Mīnākṣī** has **three breasts**; a prophecy holds the third will vanish when she meets her destined husband. As warrior-queen she conquers the directions and storms **Kailāsa**; facing **Śiva**, her third breast vanishes — recognizing her lord. Śiva comes to Madurai as **Sundareśvara** and weds her, the wedding (*Tirukalyāṇam*) re-enacted as the temple's great festival. (Wikipedia; tamilnadutourism.tn.gov.in)
  - *Variant (the 64 līlās):* the **Tiruviḷaiyāḍal** cycle frames the *whole city* as the stage of Śiva's games — carrying sand for *piṭṭu*, the contest of the poet **Nakkīrar** vs. Śiva, etc. — so Madurai itself is the *kṣetra*, not just the shrine (enumerated below). [late text]

#### The Tiruviḷaiyāḍal — Śiva's sixty-four sacred games

- **The sixty-four *tiruviḷaiyāḍal* ("holy sports") of Sundareśvara** — the Tamil **Tiruviḷaiyāḍal Purāṇam** (and its Sanskrit counterpart the **Hālāsya Māhātmya**, a *Skanda-Purāṇa* khaṇḍa) narrates **64 līlās** by which Śiva-Sundareśvara sported in and around Madurai (the old *kṣetra*-name **Hālāsya** / *Kūḍal*) — so the entire city, its tank, streets and Vaigai river become the theatre of the god's play, with the temple only its centre. The canonical Tamil redaction is **Parañcōti Munivar's (c. 16th–17th c.)**, expanding an earlier 64-game list of **Perumpaṟṟappuliyūr Nampi**; both recensions agree on the number **64** but differ in the order and naming of individual games. (Tiruviḷaiyāḍal Purāṇam; Hālāsya Māhātmya; Wikipedia, *Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam*) [late text]
- **Sample līlās (a selection of the 64)** — among the most-told episodes:
  - **Carrying earth for the *piṭṭu* (rice-cake)** — to obtain a sweet *piṭṭu* cake, Śiva himself joins the labourers raising the Vaigai flood-embankment as a coolie working for the old woman **Vandhi/Pittu-vāṇicci**, is found idling and **struck on the back** by the Pāṇḍya king — the blow felt by every being in creation, revealing the labourer as the Lord.
  - **The Nakkīrar poetry-contest** — Śiva issues a verse to settle a poet's prize; the **Caṅkam** critic **Nakkīrar** alone dares fault the god's poem; Śiva opens his **third (forehead) eye** to scorch him, but Nakkīrar stands firm on scholarly truth, and is forgiven — a charter-myth of the Madurai *Caṅkam* academy.
  - **Selling bangles / the bangle-seller** — Śiva walks the streets as a **bangle-seller (vaḷaiyal viṟpavar)** to grace the women of Madurai.
  - **The stone elephant that ate sugarcane** — Śiva animates a **carved stone elephant** so that it eats the sugarcane (var. devours a Jain/rival devotee), proving his lordship over the inert.
  - **Other games** include turning **jackals into horses** (and back) for the king, the **kicking of the cosmic boundary**, the **fish-eyed goddess's birth and wedding (Tirukalyāṇam)**, and the granting of the *Caṅkam* "board" (*paḷḷakai*) that judged Tamil poetry — together fixing **Tamil letters, kingship and the river** all to Śiva's sport.
  (Tiruviḷaiyāḍal Purāṇam; tamilnadutourism.tn.gov.in; mythicremembering.com) [folk] [late text]
  - *Variant (count and ordering):* the games are uniformly counted as **64**, but the **Hālāsya Māhātmya** (Sanskrit) and the two Tamil redactions (**Nampi** vs. **Parañcōti**) disagree on which episodes occupy which slots and on a few titles — the **number** is canonical, the **inventory** is recension-specific. [disputed]

### Gokarṇa — Mahābaleśvara & the Ātma-liṅga

- **Mahābaleśvara of Gokarṇa (the Prāṇa-liṅga / Ātma-liṅga)** — Karnataka (Uttara Kannada coast), at the river-mouth shaped like a **cow's ear (*go-karṇa*)** — held to be the spot from which **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** re-emerged through the ear of the earth-cow / of Pṛthivī, whence the name. The liṅga is the **Ātma-liṅga (Prāṇa-liṅga)**, the very "soul-liṅga" of Śiva, making Gokarṇa a *mahā-kṣetra* styled **Dakṣiṇa Kāśī** ("the Kāśī of the South"). Source: *Skanda Purāṇa* (Sahyādri-khaṇḍa) and the local *Gokarṇa-māhātmya*; academically a **medieval** coastal Śaiva centre with later **Vijayanagara/Keḷadi** patronage. (Wikipedia, *Mahabaleshwar Temple, Gokarna*; *Skanda Purāṇa*) [late text]
- **Rāvaṇa, the boon, and Gaṇeśa's trick** — **[Rāvaṇa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)**, to make Laṅkā invincible, performs *tapas* and wins from Śiva the **Ātma-liṅga**, on the strict condition that **it must never be set on the ground** before reaching Laṅkā, or it will root forever. The gods, alarmed, send **[Gaṇeśa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** disguised as a brahmin **cowherd-boy**; **[Vishnu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** veils the sun to fake dusk so Rāvaṇa must stop for *sandhyā* prayers. Rāvaṇa hands the liṅga to the boy to hold — who, by prior pact, sets it down the moment Rāvaṇa is late, planting it at Gokarṇa. Enraged, Rāvaṇa cannot lift it (it is "Mahā-bala," of great strength — hence **Mahābaleśvara**) and strikes the boy on the head. (Gokarṇa-māhātmya; bharatonline.com; templepurohit.com)
  - *Variant (the scattered wrappings — the coastal liṅga chain):* in his fury Rāvaṇa hurls away the **casket and wrappings (packing-cloth and lid)** of the Ātma-liṅga; where each piece falls, a liṅga springs up — traditionally **Sajjeśvara, Dhāreśvara, Guṇavanteśvara** and **Mūrudeśvara** (the lid landing at Mūrudeśvar/Mṛdeśa) — so a **chain of coastal Śiva-kṣetras** is etiologically tied to the one Gokarṇa episode. (templepurohit.com; karnatakatourism.org) [folk]
  - *Variant (shared Atma-liṅga / Vaidyanātha cross-claim):* the **same** Rāvaṇa–Gaṇeśa Ātma-liṅga episode is told at **[Vaidyanātha Jyotirliṅga](12-jyotirlingas.md)** (Deoghar) — the two sites preserve rival "where the liṅga rooted" recensions of one pan-Indian legend. [disputed]
  - *Variant (Gaṇeśa's dent):* the blow Rāvaṇa lands on the cowherd-boy's head explains an indentation on Gokarṇa's **Mahā-Gaṇapati** image, worshipped before Mahābaleśvara. [folk]

### Rāmeśvaram — the Setu-bandha complex & Dhanuṣkoḍi

- **Rāmanātha-svāmī & the Setu-bandha kṣetra** — Tamil Nadu, on **Rāmeśvaram (Pāmban) island**; the **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** liṅga that **[Rāma](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** worshipped to expiate the *brahma-hatyā* of slaying the brahmin **Rāvaṇa** — simultaneously a **[Jyotirliṅga](12-jyotirlingas.md)** (*Setubandhe tu Rāmeśam*), the **southern [Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)** point, and the head of the **Setu** *tīrtha*-complex. Source: *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* (Setu-bandha), *Skanda Purāṇa* (Setu-māhātmya); the present temple is **Pāṇḍya/Nāyaka-period** over older foundations. (Wikipedia, *Ramanathaswamy Temple*; *Skanda Purāṇa*, Setu-māhātmya) [late text]
- **The two liṅgas of the consecration** — to atone after Laṅkā, Rāma sends **[Hanumān](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** to Kailāsa to fetch a liṅga; when he is late, **Sītā fashions a liṅga of sand (*Rāmaliṅga*)** for the auspicious moment, so the shrine keeps **two liṅgas** — Hanumān's **Viśvaliṅga** (worshipped first, by Rāma's decree) and Sītā's sand-liṅga. (Setu-māhātmya; tamilnadutourism.tn.gov.in) [folk]

#### Dhanuṣkoḍi — where the bridge began

- **Dhanuṣ-koḍi ("bow's tip")** — the **sandspit at the south-eastern tip** of Rāmeśvaram island, the closest point of India to Śrī Laṅkā, named because **Rāma marked the start of the [Rāma Setu](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) (Nala-setu) here with the tip (*koḍi*) of his bow (*dhanuṣ*)** — fixing the bridge's mainland anchor. In the *Rāmāyaṇa* return-telling, Rāma, asked by **Vibhīṣaṇa** to undo the now-needless bridge, **breaks it with his bow-end** at this very point — a second gloss of the name. (Wikipedia, *Dhanushkodi*; *Vālmīki/Rāmāyaṇa* tradition)
- **The sankalpa-snāna and Kodaṇḍarāma shrine** — pilgrims to Rāmeśvaram are traditionally enjoined to **first bathe at Dhanuṣkoḍi** (the meeting of the Bay of Bengal and Indian Ocean) and take the *sankalpa* there, gathering **Setu** sand/water, **before** the *darśana* of Rāmanātha — and to visit the **Kodaṇḍarāma temple** (where Vibhīṣaṇa is said to have surrendered to Rāma and been crowned). The old town was **destroyed in the 1964 cyclone**, leaving the shrine-ruins on the spit. (Wikipedia, *Dhanushkodi*; tamilnadutourism.tn.gov.in) [regional]
  - *Variant (bow-tip vs. bridge-breaking):* the name **Dhanuṣ-koḍi** is glossed either as the place Rāma **drew the bridge's starting line** with his bow-tip, or as the place he **snapped the completed bridge** at Vibhīṣaṇa's request — both etiologies circulate. [folk]

### Srisailam — Mallikārjuna & Bhramarāmbā

- **Mallikārjuna (Jyotirliṅga) & Bhramarāmbā (Śakti Pīṭha)** — Andhra Pradesh, on the **Nallamala** hills above the **Kṛṣṇā** river; a rare site that is **both a [Jyotirliṅga](12-jyotirlingas.md) and a [Śakti Pīṭha](51-shakti-peethas.md)** (where Sati's neck/upper-lip is said to have fallen). Praised in the *Skanda Purāṇa* (Śrīśaila-khaṇḍa); academically attested from **Sātavāhana/early-medieval** inscriptions, with major **Vijayanagara/Reddi** patronage. (Wikipedia, *Mallikarjuna Temple, Srisailam*; srisailadevasthanam.org)
- **Śiva follows Kārtikeya** — when **[Kārtikeya/Skanda](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** sulks off to Srisailam after the "fruit/marriage" quarrel with **Gaṇeśa**, his parents **Śiva & Pārvatī** follow and settle on the hill as **Mallikārjuna** (Śiva, worshipped with jasmine, *mallikā*) and **Bhramarāmbā** to remain near their son. (gotirupati.com; simhastha.org.in)
- **Bhramarāmbā slays the bee-demon** — the demon **Aruṇāsura** holds a boon against death by any 2- or 4-legged being; the Goddess takes the form **Bhramarī/Bhramarāmbā** and emits swarms of **six-legged bees (bhramara)** that destroy him. (srisailamtourism.com)
  - *Variant (folk pastoral origin):* a *Chenchu* (forest-tribe) tradition has Mallikārjuna wed the tribal girl **Chenchu-Lakṣmī**, embedding the deity in local hunter-gatherer lineage. [folk]

### Dharmasthala — Mañjunātha & the Dharma Daivas

- **Mañjunātha (Śiva) under tribal–Jain–Vaiṣṇava custodianship** — Karnataka (Dakshina Kannada); a **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** liṅga worshipped at a uniquely **pluralist** shrine: the priests are **Vaiṣṇava brahmins**, the hereditary custodians (**Heggade**) are **Jain**, and the guardian **Dharma Daivas** (folk *daivas*/bhūtas) are honored alongside. Cult and the **Heggade** lineage are **medieval** (c. 16th c. and later); no high-Purāṇic source — its "sthala-purāṇa" is a local *daiva* legend. (Wikipedia, *Dharmasthala Temple*; shridharmasthala.org)
- **The Dharma Daivas at Pergaḍe's house** — four guardian spirits of Dharma (**Kālarāhu, Kālarkāyi, Kumārasvāmī, Kanyākumārī**), seeking a household where dharma is truly practiced, come in human form to the **Jain chieftain Birmaṇṇa Pergaḍe** of **Kuḍuma**; pleased by his hospitality, they appear in his dream, ask him to vacate his home for their worship and dedicate his life to dharma. He builds their shrines; later a **Śiva liṅga from Kadri** is installed and named **Mañjunātha** — fusing folk *daiva* worship, Jain stewardship and Śaiva temple. (shridharmasthala.org; mystreal.com)
  - *Variant (later Śaiva overlay):* the Śiva-liṅga and brahmin priesthood are a **later addition** (attributed to the influence of **Vādirāja Tīrtha**, 16th c.); the original cult was purely the **Dharma Daivas / bhūta** worship. [scholarly] [folk]

## III. Śākta Kṣetras (Devī-centred, outside the 51 Pīṭhas)

### Vaiṣṇo Devī — the Trikūṭa cave goddess

- **Mātā Vaiṣṇo Devī (Trikūṭā / Triple Goddess)** — Jammu & Kashmir, in a cave of the **Trikūṭa** hills; the Goddess venerated as the unified form of **Mahākālī, Mahālakṣmī & Mahāsarasvatī** (three *piṇḍī* rock-forms). Not among the classical 51 [Śakti Pīṭhas](51-shakti-peethas.md) in most lists (some include it as a *pīṭha*); its legend is **regional/folk** with no early-Purāṇic text — the present pilgrimage cult is **late-medieval to modern**. (BhaktiBharat; dollsofindia.com)
- **Vaiṣṇavī, Śrīdhar's feast & Bhairavnāth** — born as a divine girl **Vaiṣṇavī** (a *aṃśa* of **[Vishnu's](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** energy / Triple Devī) devoted to **[Rāma](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)**, she attends a feast hosted by the devotee **Śrīdhar**. The tantric **Bhairavnāth** (a disciple of **Gorakṣanāth**) lusts after her; she flees to the Trikūṭa hills, hides in a cave (**Ardha-kuvārī / Garbha-joon**), and finally **beheads him** at the cave-mouth — his repentant head flying to the ridge above. (kidsgen.com; astroswamig.com)
- **The boon to Bhairav** — dying repentant, Bhairavnāth is forgiven and granted that **no pilgrimage is complete** until the devotee also visits his shrine (**Bhairon Mandir**) above the cave. (bhaktibharat.com; astroswamig.com)
  - *Variant (which deity she awaits):* Vaiṣṇavī vows to wed only **Vishnu/Rāma** in his **[Kalki](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** form at the **end of Kali Yuga** — until then she remains in penance in the cave, linking the shrine to the future Yuga-turn. [folk]

### Kanyākumārī — Devī Kanyā Kumārī

- **Kanyā Kumārī ("the virgin maiden")** — Tamil Nadu, at the **subcontinent's southern tip** (confluence of the Bay of Bengal, Arabian Sea and Indian Ocean); a form of **[Pārvatī/Durgā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)** as an eternal unmarried girl, her diamond nose-ring famously brilliant. Counted by some among the **[Śakti Pīṭhas](51-shakti-peethas.md)** (Sati's back/spine). Source: *Bhāgavata* & *Skanda* references; consecration attributed to **[Paraśurāma](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)**. (Wikipedia, *Devi Kanya Kumari*; templepurohit.com)
- **Slayer of Bāṇāsura** — the demon **Bāṇāsura** holds a boon that only a **virgin girl** can kill him; the Goddess incarnates as **Kumārī** to do so. She is also betrothed to **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** (Śucīndram); the gods, needing her to stay virgin to fulfill the boon, **sabotage the dawn wedding** (Nārada crows like a cock so Śiva, thinking it sunrise, turns back) — so she remains *kumārī* forever, and slays Bāṇāsura with her *cakra*. (tamilnadutourism.tn.gov.in; manasee-godsay on Medium)
  - *Variant (the unused wedding feast):* the multicolored sands of the shore are said to be the **rice and spices** of the wedding-feast that was never cooked. [folk]

### Kollūr — Mūkāmbikā

- **Mūkāmbikā (Devī as Sarasvatī-Lakṣmī-Pārvatī united)** — Karnataka (Kollūr), at the foot of the **Kodachadri** hill; the Goddess in a **Jyotirliṅga-like svayambhū** stone bearing a golden line (*svarṇa-rekhā*) dividing her **Śiva-half from her Śakti-half**, worshipped as combined **Mahākālī-Mahālakṣmī-Mahāsarasvatī**. Strongly associated with **[Ādi Śaṅkara](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)**, who is said to have consecrated her here. Cult **medieval**; major Vijayanagara-era patronage. (Wikipedia, *Kollur Mookambika Temple*; pujanpujari.com)
- **The muting of Kaumāsura / Mūkāsura** — a demon does *tapas* to **Brahmā** for an invincibility boon; to thwart him **Devī robs him of speech**, rendering him **Mūka** ("the mute"); enraged, the dumb demon (now **Mūkāsura**) harasses sage **Kola Maharṣi** at his penance, until the Goddess **slays him** — and at Kola's request stays on as **Mūkāmbikā** ("mother of the mute one"). (mookambika.co; templepurohit.com)
  - *Variant (the golden line):* at Kola's wish a **jyotirliṅga with a golden line** manifests so that Śiva and Śakti are worshipped together in one stone — making Kollūr a Śaiva-Śākta hybrid like Srisailam. [folk]

### Kolhapur — Mahālakṣmī / Ambābāī (Karavīra-kṣetra)

- **Mahālakṣmī (Ambābāī) of Karavīra** — Maharashtra, on the **Pañcagaṅgā** river; the premier **Lakṣmī kṣetra** of the western Deccan, where **Śrī herself dwells permanently on earth**. She is the **seventh** of the **Aṣṭādaśa (18) Mahā Śakti Pīṭhas** (*"...Mahālakṣmī ca Kolhāpure..."*) and one of the **Sāḍe-tīn (three-and-a-half) Śakti Pīṭhas of Maharashtra**. Source: the **Karavīra-māhātmya** (an appendix of the *Padma Purāṇa*, in other recensions the *Skanda Purāṇa*); the standing temple is **Cālukya/Śilāhāra-era (c. 7th–11th c. CE)**. **See the dedicated page: [Kolhapur — Mahālakṣmī / Karavīra](kolhapur-mahalakshmi-karavira.md).** (Karavīra-māhātmya; Aṣṭādaśa Śakti Pīṭha Stotram; Wikipedia, *Mahalaxmi Temple, Kolhapur*) [late text]
- **Lakṣmī's residence after leaving Viṣṇu** — the framing legend has **Śrī-Lakṣmī depart Vaikuṇṭha** after estrangement from **[Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** and settle at Karavīra in her own right; in the southern recension the cause is the **Bhṛgu-kick** (the *same* trigger that sends Viṣṇu down as Śrīnivāsa to [Tirumala](tirumala-tirupati-venkateshwara.md) — see §I above), so Kolhapur and Tirupati are read as the **separated divine couple**, the goddess **waiting** (southern *tāyār* theology) for Viṣṇu to reconcile. (Karavīra-māhātmya; Veṅkaṭācala-Māhātmya) [regional] [folk]
- **Kolhāsura slain — the city's name-myth** — a parallel stratum names the city for the buffalo-/demon-king **Kolhāsura (Kolāsura)**, whom the **Goddess slays** here; dying, he begs the city bear his name and the Goddess remain — so she stays as **Mahālakṣmī of Kolhāpura**. This *Devī-Māhātmya*-type demon-slaying coexists with the Lakṣmī-Śrī legend. (Karavīra-māhātmya; Wikipedia, *Kolhapur*) [regional] [disputed]
- **The Kiraṇotsava (sun-ray festival)** — the **west-facing svayambhū mūrti** is the key to a built **architectural-astronomical alignment**: **three times a year** the **setting sun's rays pass straight through the western doorway to fall on the face/body of the Goddess** for a few minutes on successive evenings — celebrated as **Kiraṇotsava**, a *datable medieval engineering* feat distinct from the Purāṇic legend (the "two clocks"). (Wikipedia, *Mahalaxmi Temple, Kolhapur*) [regional] [scholarly]

## IV. Modern & Composite Kṣetras (saint-samādhi sites)

> Living pilgrimage *kṣetras* whose "sthala-purāṇa" is **modern and biographical** (a historical saint's *samādhi*) rather than Purāṇic — included because they now function as first-tier *tīrthas* with their own emergent place-legend and all-India networks.

### Shirdi — Sāī Bābā Samādhi Mandir

- **Shirdi Sāī Bābā (the *fakir*-saint of Shirdi)** — Maharashtra (Ahmadnagar dist.); the *samādhi* shrine of **Sāī Bābā of Shirdi** (d. **15 October 1918**), a deliberately **composite, supra-sectarian** saint who taught *"Sabkā mālik ek"* ("one God for all") and is venerated by Hindus and Muslims alike — his iconography blending **Śaiva, Vaiṣṇava and Sūfī** registers. Unlike the Purāṇic kṣetras above, the place-legend is a **20th-century hagiography** (the *Śrī Sāī Satcharita* of Govind Dabholkar/"Hemādpant," 1920s), making Shirdi a fully **modern** *kṣetra*. (Wikipedia, *Sai Baba of Shirdi*; *Shri Sai Satcharita*) [modern]
- **The Samādhi Mandir (Buṭī Wāḍā)** — Bābā's tomb stands in the **Buṭī Wāḍā**, the stone mansion the devotee **Gopālrāo Buṭī** was building (by Bābā's own wish, conveyed in a dream) as a Murlīdhar-Kṛṣṇa temple; at Bābā's passing his body was interred there instead, so the **Buṭī Wāḍā became the Samādhi Mandir**, now the shrine's centre with a white-marble Bābā mūrti above the tomb. (saibabasansthan official; Wikipedia, *Shirdi Sai Baba Temple*)
- **Dwārakāmāī and the ever-burning dhuni** — Bābā lived in a ruined mosque he named **Dwārakāmāī** ("mother Dwārakā" — a Kṛṣṇa name given to a mosque, epitomizing his syncretism), where he kept a perpetual fire, the **dhuni**, whose ash (**ūdī**) he gave as healing *prasāda*; the dhuni still burns and ūdī is distributed today. The **Chāvaḍī** (where he slept on alternate nights) and the **nīm tree** of his arrival complete the in-town pilgrim circuit. (Shri Sai Satcharita; saibabasansthan official) [modern]
- **The all-India Sāī network** — from Shirdi the cult has spread into a vast **all-India (and global) temple network** of replica Sāī shrines and Thursday (*guru-vār*) worship, making Shirdi a **modern pan-Indian pilgrimage hub** rivalling the ancient kṣetras in footfall — an emergent *kṣetra* whose "māhātmya" is still being written. (Wikipedia, *Sai Baba of Shirdi*) [modern]
  - *Variant (sectarian framing):* devotional traditions variously read Bābā as an **avatāra of [Dattātreya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)**, of **Śiva**, or as a **Sūfī *pīr*** — the composite identity is integral, not incidental, to the kṣetra. [folk] [disputed]
