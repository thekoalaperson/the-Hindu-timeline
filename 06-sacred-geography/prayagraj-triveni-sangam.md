# Prayāga / Prayagraj — Tīrtharāja & the Triveṇī Saṅgam

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](README.md) → **Prayāga / Triveṇī Saṅgam**. The **Tīrtharāja** ("King of Pilgrimage-Sites") — the *kṣetra* at the confluence (*saṅgama*) of [Gaṅgā, Yamunā](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md) and the invisible **Sarasvatī**, where the *Prayāga-māhātmya* declares the merit of ten billion *tīrthas* to be matched by half of Prayāga alone.

## Identity & names — the King of Tīrthas

- **Tīrtharāja Prayāga** - The single *tīrtha* held supreme in *smṛti*; the *Prayāga-māhātmya* of the Matsya Purāṇa enumerates the five paramount *kṣetras* of the universe — Prayāga, Kāśī (Vārāṇasī), Kurukṣetra, Gayā, Avantikā (Ujjain) — and names **Prayāga first and king (rāja) among them** (Matsya Purāṇa, Prayāga-māhātmya, adhyāyas ~103–112).
- **Prayāga (प्रयाग)** - Classical/scriptural name; from *pra-* + *√yaj* ("to sacrifice excellently / before all"), i.e. "the place of the great/first sacrifice." Used throughout Rāmāyaṇa, Mahābhārata and the Purāṇas.
- **Prayagraj** - Restored official Hindi name of the modern city (renamed 2018, reviving the *tīrtharāja* epithet); the colonial name was **Allahabad** (Mughal **Ilāhābād**, "abode of God," coined under Akbar c. 1583).
- **Pratiṣṭhāna-puri** - Ancient Puranic name for the city/region on the eastern (Yamunā) bank, seat of the early lunar-line (Chandravaṃśa) kings; the *Prayāga-māhātmya* fixes Pratiṣṭhāna as a boundary of the sacred precinct. See [Chandravaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
- **Extent of the kṣetra** - The Matsya text bounds the **Prayāga-maṇḍala** as roughly *five yojanas* in compass, with **Pratiṣṭhāna** to the north/east, **Vāsuki-hrada** to the south, and the **Saṅgama** at the centre — within which the whole zone is *tīrtha* (Matsya Purāṇa, Prayāga-māhātmya).

## Origin of the name — Brahmā's Prakṛṣṭa Yajña

- **Prakṛṣṭa Yajña** - The dominant Puranic etymology: at the start of creation **Brahmā** performed a *prakṛṣṭa* ("pre-eminent / first") *yajña* here; "Pra-yāga" = the site of that supreme sacrifice. Hence Prayāga is also called the *Brahma-kṣetra* and *yajña-vedi* of the cosmos (*Prayāga-māhātmya*; Matsya & Padma Purāṇas).
  - *Variant (folk/Puranic):* the *yajña* established the Sangam as the cosmic *vedi* (altar) of the earth, around which **Viṣṇu** stationed himself in twelve forms (see Dvādaśa Mādhava below) to guard it perpetually.
- **Vāc / firstness reading** - "Prayāga" as "before (*pra*) all other *yāga*/tīrthas," i.e. the *tīrtha* taking precedence — consonant with the *Tīrtharāja* title.

## The Triveṇī Saṅgam — confluence of three rivers

- **The saṅgama itself** - Meeting of three streams: **Gaṅgā** (descended via [Bhagīratha](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md)), **Yamunā** (Yamī, sister of Yama, daughter of Sūrya), and the **Sarasvatī**. *Triveṇī* = "triple braid." A bath here is held to wash away all sin and break the cycle of rebirth (*saṃsāra*) (Wikipedia, *Triveni Sangam*; *Prayāga-māhātmya*).
- **Visible two, invisible third** - At the physical confluence the **Gaṅgā runs pale/whitish** and the **Yamunā darker blue-green**; the **Sarasvatī is adṛśya** ("invisible"), held to flow underground (*antaḥsalilā* / *gupta*) and join from below — making the meeting a *triveṇī* though only two rivers are seen.
  - *Scriptural basis [disputed by academics]:* the Mahābhārata depicts the once-mighty Sarasvatī drying and going underground (the **vinaśana** / disappearance), re-emerging unseen at sacred confluences. The **Ṛgveda** verse cited at confluence rites: "those who bathe where the two rivers — pale and dark — flow together rise to heaven" (RV passage quoted in *Triveni Sangam*, Wikipedia).
  - *Scholarly note [scholarly]:* mainstream geology/hydrology finds no surface Sarasvatī reaching Prayāga; the "underground Sarasvatī" is read as a theological/mythic identification (paleo-channel debates concern the NW Ghaggar-Hakra, not Prayāga). Two-clocks: scripture treats the Sarasvatī as eternally present; academic dating treats the surface river as long-vanished.
- **The braided merit** - The three rivers are read symbolically as the three *nāḍī* / three *guṇa* / knowledge-action-devotion; the *veṇī* (braid) is personified in the presiding deity **Veṇī Mādhava** (below).

## The Akṣayavaṭ — the imperishable banyan

- **Akṣayavaṭ (अक्षयवट)** - The "imperishable / undecaying banyan," held to survive even the *pralaya* (cosmic dissolution). One of Hinduism's most sacred trees, identified with **Maheśvara/Śiva** residing as the tree, and a refuge of the cosmos between dissolutions (*Prayāga-māhātmya*).
- **Scriptural references** - Cited in both the **Rāmāyaṇa** (Rāma, Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa are said to have visited the Prayāga *vaṭa* during exile, in the *Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* at sage Bharadvāja's āśrama nearby) and the **Mahābhārata** (*Tīrtha-yātrā / Vana parva* praise of the Prayāga *vaṭa*). The Chinese pilgrim **Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang, 7th c. CE)** recorded a great tree at Prayāga beneath which devotees gave up their lives for liberation [historical].
- **Pātālpurī temple** - The Akṣayavaṭ stands within/over the underground **Pātālpurī** temple, today inside the **Allahabad Fort** (built by Akbar, 1583, which enclosed the tree); access historically restricted, opened more freely in recent decades.
  - *Variant (folk):* tradition of *vaṭa-pradakṣiṇā* and, in earlier centuries, ritual self-immolation from the tree for instant *mokṣa* — a practice noted by travelers and later suppressed.

## Bharadvāja's āśrama — the exile-route hermitage

- **Bharadvāja-āśrama (भरद्वाजाश्रम)** - The hermitage of the sage **Bharadvāja** at the Prayāga *saṅgama*, the canonical first waypoint on the *vana-yātrā* (forest-exile journey). Crossing the Yamunā from Śṛṅgaverapura, **Rāma, Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa** reach Bharadvāja's *āśrama* at the confluence of Gaṅgā and Yamunā; the sage receives them with honour and, rather than keeping them at his own hermitage (which would draw the curious of Ayodhyā), directs them onward to **Chitrakūṭa** (Citrakūṭa) as the ideal place of exile-dwelling (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, *Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* sargas ~50–56).
- **Bharata's return visit & the miraculous feast** - When **Bharata**, refusing the throne, marches with his army to bring Rāma back, his host halts at Bharadvāja's *āśrama*; the sage, by his ascetic power (and, in the telling, the divine architect / *Viśvakarmā* and the *deva*-world summoned to serve), conjures a vast miraculous **feast** (*ātithya*) — palaces, divine food, drink, music and apsaras — entertaining Bharata's whole army before he proceeds to Chitrakūṭa (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, *Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* sarga ~91).
- **Living tīrtha — Bharadwaj Ashram, Prayagraj** - The site is venerated today as the **Bharadwaj Ashram** in the Colonelganj area of Prayagraj, a living temple complex (with images of Bharadvāja and a *Bharadveśvara* Śiva *liṅga*) held to be the historical seat of the sage and a stop on the local Rāmāyaṇa-circuit *parikramā*; it anchors the Prayāga *vaṭa* tradition noted above (cross-ref the [Akṣayavaṭ](#the-akṣayavaṭ--the-imperishable-banyan) section).
- **ASI survey site [historical/archaeological]** - Bharadwaj Ashram, Prayagraj is one of the **five sites** taken up in the Archaeological Survey of India's *"Archaeology of the Rāmāyaṇa Sites"* project (alongside other places associated with the epic itinerary) — an excavation/exploration programme correlating the *Rāmāyaṇa* topography with the material record. *Two-clocks:* tradition treats the *āśrama* as the very hermitage of Bharadvāja in the *Tretā-yuga*; the ASI project is a modern historical-archaeological exercise and does not certify the epic chronology.

## Prayāga-māhātmya — scriptural praise & the supreme-merit acts

- **Where attested** - The *Prayāga-māhātmya* survives most fully in the **Matsya Purāṇa** (~adhyāyas 103–112), with closely parallel passages in the **Padma Purāṇa** (*Svarga / Uttara khaṇḍa*), the **Kūrma Purāṇa**, and echoes in the **Agni** and **Skanda** Purāṇas and the Mahābhārata *Tīrtha-yātrā parva*.
- **The Tīrtha-koṭi verse** - The signature claim: *"tīrtha-koṭi-sahasrāṇi prayāgārdhena tat-phalam"* — "the merit of a thousand crore (ten billion) tīrthas equals but **half** of Prayāga." This is the proof-text for the *Tīrtharāja* title (Matsya/Padma *Prayāga-māhātmya*).
- **Aśvamedha-equivalence** - Three days' residence at Prayāga with bathing, fasting and prayer is declared to yield merit equal to **ten Aśvamedha** (horse-sacrifice) *yajñas*; death at Prayāga grants liberation from rebirth (Matsya *Prayāga-māhātmya*).
- **Four categories of supreme-merit acts** - The *māhātmya* groups the meritorious works performed at Prayāga into four classes [as systematized from the Matsya/Padma text; rosters vary by recension — flagged open-ended]:
  - *Snāna (sacred bathing)* - the *saṅgama-snāna*, supremely in the month of **Māgha**; the foundational act yielding *pāpa-nāśa* (destruction of sin).
  - *Dāna (giving)* - charitable gifts (cows, gold, land, the **Veṇī-dāna**); gift here multiplied immeasurably.
  - *Tapas / Vrata (austerity & vows)* - fasting, vows, *kalpavāsa* and self-discipline within the *kṣetra*.
  - *Śrāddha / Piṇḍa-dāna (rites for the ancestors)* - *tarpaṇa* and offering of *piṇḍa* for the *pitṛs* — Prayāga held equal in this to **Gayā** (below).
  - *Variant ordering (folk/pandit tradition):* some local *prayāgvāl* (pilgrim-priest) traditions render the four as **snāna, dāna, śrāddha, and muṇḍana (tonsure)**, or count *japa/dhyāna* (remembrance) as a fifth.

## Ancestral & life-cycle rites — Veṇī-dāna, muṇḍana, piṇḍa-dāna

- **Veṇī-dāna (वेणी-दान)** - The signature Prayāga rite: married women offer a lock of hair (the *veṇī*, plaited at marriage) into the Sangam, symbolically "donating the braid" — echoing the *triveṇī* braid of the rivers and seeking marital auspiciousness/longevity for the husband; performed especially during the [Kumbh / Magh Mela](kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md).
- **Muṇḍana (tonsure)** - Head-shaving at the Sangam — for first-tonsure of children and as a *saṃskāra* of pilgrimage; the offered hair is given to the rivers as a *dāna*.
- **Piṇḍa-dāna & tarpaṇa** - Prayāga is one of the three great centres for *śrāddha* (with **Gayā** and **Kāśī**); the *māhātmya* declares the *pitṛs* released by *tarpaṇa* offered at the Sangam, **Prayāga held equal to Gayā** for ancestral liberation (so the classic *pitṛ-yātrā* couples Gayā + Prayāga + Kāśī). See [Sapta Purī & sacred rivers](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md).
- **Aṣṭhi-visarjana (immersion of ashes)** - The Sangam is the premier site for immersing cremation ashes; nationally, the ashes of **Mahatma Gandhi (1948–49)** and **Atal Bihari Vajpayee (2018)**, among others, were immersed here [historical].

## Kalpavāsa & the Māgha / Magh Mela

- **Kalpavāsa (कल्पवास)** - The month-long ascetic residence on the Sangam sands through **Māgha** (Jan–Feb): pilgrims (*kalpavāsīs*) live in tents, take the daily pre-dawn *snāna*, eat once, perform *japa*, *dāna* and listen to scripture — a vow held to grant the merit of a *kalpa* (aeon) of austerity, hence the name.
- **Māgha / Magh Mela** - The annual bathing assembly at the Sangam each Māgha; its great amplifications are the **Kumbh** (every ~12 yrs) and **Ardha-Kumbh** (every ~6 yrs) — see the dedicated [Kumbh Mela & Pilgrimage Cycles](kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md) node for the *jyotiṣa* timing, the four *amṛta*-drop cities and the *amṛta-yoga*.
- **Key bathing days (parva)** - Makara Saṅkrānti, Paush Pūrṇimā, Mauni Amāvasyā (the supreme *snāna* day), Vasanta Pañcamī, Māghī Pūrṇimā, Mahāśivarātri.

## The Dvādaśa Mādhava — twelve Viṣṇu shrines of Prayāga

- **Dvādaśa Mādhava (द्वादश माधव)** - Twelve forms of **Viṣṇu (as Mādhava)** stationed around the Prayāga *kṣetra*, said to have been established/expanded by Viṣṇu (at Brahmā's *yajña*) to perpetually guard the Sangam. Their circuit (*Dvādaśa-Mādhava parikramā*) is a core Prayāga pilgrimage. The *Prayāga-māhātmya* proof-text: *"yaḥ prayāge mādhavān dvādaśa saṃsevet sa vimukta-pāpaḥ"* — "who serves the twelve Mādhavas at Prayāga is freed from all sin."
- **Veṇī Mādhava** - The presiding deity (*kṣetra-pāla*) of Prayāga, at **Dāraganj**; named for the *veṇī* (river-braid), ranked the chief of the twelve in the Matsya, Padma and Agni Purāṇas; worship here is said to grant the combined fruit of all *tīrthas*.
- **The twelve (roster) [open-ended — orderings and a few names vary by source]:** Veṇī Mādhava, Ādi Mādhava, Manohar Mādhava, Bindu Mādhava, Gadā Mādhava, Chakra Mādhava, Śaṅkha Mādhava, Akṣayavaṭ Mādhava, Saṅkaṣṭhar (Saṅkaṣṭa-hara) Mādhava, Anant Mādhava, Asi Mādhava, and Padma Mādhava.
  - *Variant roster (pandit tradition):* some lists substitute **Triveṇī Mādhava** for Akṣayavaṭ/Ādi Mādhava, yielding alternate twelves — flagged as variant; the symbolic frame is Viṣṇu's four *āyudhas* (Śaṅkha, Cakra, Gadā, Padma) plus his cosmic and protective aspects.
