# Mount Abu — Arbuda-giri & Achaleshwar

> *Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up: [Master Index](../README.md) · Related: [Prabhāsa / Somnāth](prabhasa-somnath-tirtha.md) · [Girnar — Raivataka / Datta-tīrtha](girnar-raivataka-datta-tirtha.md) · [Puṣkara — Brahmā Tīrtha](pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md) · [Meru, Kailāsa & Cosmic Mountains](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md)*

**Mount Abu** (*Arbuda-giri*, *Arbudācala*; the forest **Arbudāraṇya**; in the *Skanda Purāṇa* the **Arbuda-khaṇḍa** material set within the **Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa**) is the sole high hill-tīrtha of the **Aravalli** range and of western Rajasthan in Purāṇic geography — an isolated granite massif rising above the Sirohi plains, crowned by **Guru Śikhar** (the Aravallis' highest peak, ~1722 m). It is celebrated as the seat where the sage **Vasiṣṭha** kept his hermitage and lit the world-changing **agni-kuṇḍa**, as **Achaleśvar** (Śiva worshipped not as a liṅga but as the god's **toe-print**), and as the cool retreat-tīrtha of the desert — "**the elder son of [Himālaya](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md)**" in local māhātmya. Like its Saurāṣṭra siblings [Prabhāsa](prabhasa-somnath-tirtha.md) and [Girnar](girnar-raivataka-datta-tirtha.md), its core scriptural charter sits in the *Skanda Purāṇa*'s western corpus; like [Puṣkara](pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md), it is a Rajasthan node whose myth fuses a Vasiṣṭha legend with a tribal-Rajput origin story.

**Naming & the two clocks** — In *scriptural* time the hill is **Arbuda**, named for the **serpent Arbuda** who saved Nandin (below) and for the **Arbudāraṇya** forest where ṛṣis tapas-ed; its charter is a *māhātmya*, not datable yuga-history. In *terrestrial / historical* time it is **Mount Abu**, Sirohi district, Rajasthan — a hill-station and Jain pilgrimage center (the **Dilwārā** temples, 11th–13th c. CE), the Aravallis being among the oldest mountain ranges on earth. The two layers are kept apart throughout: the **Agnikula** Rajput-origin legend below is a medieval bardic charter, not the secular history of those dynasties. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa* — *Arbuda-khaṇḍa*; bardic *Pṛthvīrāj Rāso* tradition; Wikipedia, "Mount Abu," "Agnivanshi") [late text][regional]

## I. The serpent Arbuda — the naming myth

- **The fall of Nandin and the cobra Arbuda** — In the *māhātmya*'s naming story, **Śiva's bull Nandin** (or, in variants, a cow) slipped and fell into a deep chasm in the earth; the great serpent **Arbuda**, a *nāga* son of the world-snake, raised himself up and **bore the falling bull on his hood** back to safety. As reward the place was named **Arbuda-giri / Arbudācala** after the serpent, and the hill became holy. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa* — *Arbuda-khaṇḍa*; Arbuda sthala tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant (folk etymology):* the name is glossed instead from **arbuda** ("a swelling, a mass, a myriad") — the hill as a "great mass" lifted out of the plain, or as ten-million (an *arbuda* is a numeral) of merit. (local tradition) [folk]
- **Arbudāraṇya, the ṛṣis' forest** — the wooded massif is the **Arbuda forest** where sages settled for tapas in the western wilderness, a green island in the desert; its springs and groves make it the cool counter-tīrtha to the arid Marudeśa. (*Skanda Purāṇa* — *Arbuda-khaṇḍa*) [regional]

## II. Vasiṣṭha's āśrama and the Gaumukh spring

- **Vasiṣṭha on Arbuda** — the ṛṣi **Vasiṣṭha** (one of the [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md)) is held to have kept his hermitage on Arbuda after settling in the west; his **Gaumukh** ("cow's-mouth") shrine, lower on the hill, marks a perennial spring that issues from a carved stone cow's head and feeds a sacred tank. (*Skanda Purāṇa* — *Arbuda-khaṇḍa*; Gaumukh sthala tradition) [regional]
- **Nandinī / Kāmadhenu at Gaumukh** — the spring is linked to **Nandinī**, the wish-cow (daughter of **Kāmadhenu**) whom Vasiṣṭha kept; the same cow whose theft by **Viśvāmitra** drove the Vasiṣṭha–Viśvāmitra feud is, in the local telling, watered here — knitting Arbuda into the great ṛṣi-rivalry cycle. (Gaumukh tradition; cf. *Rāmāyaṇa* Bāla-kāṇḍa) [folk]
- **The marble bull and footprints** — the Gaumukh complex preserves a marble **Nandin**, images of **Vasiṣṭha, Rāma and Kṛṣṇa**, and footprint-stones, framing the spring as the original āśrama-tīrtha of the hill. (Gaumukh sthala tradition) [regional]

## III. The agni-kuṇḍa and the Agnikula origin

> Arbuda's most famous legend is the **Agnikula** ("fire-race") charter: the bardic origin-myth that derives four great Rajput clans from a fire-pit lit by Vasiṣṭha on this hill. It is a **medieval clan-genealogy**, kept on the *māhātmya* clock — not the recorded-history origin of those dynasties.

- **The fire-sacrifice on Arbuda** — robbed of his wish-cow (or, in the *Pṛthvīrāj Rāso* telling, beset by **demons/asuras** ravaging the western land), **Vasiṣṭha** kindled a great **agni-kuṇḍa** (fire-pit) atop Arbuda and performed a homa to bring forth defenders of dharma. (*Pṛthvīrāj Rāso*; Agnikula bardic tradition) [late text][disputed]
- **The four fire-born clans** — from the flames sprang armed heroes, the ancestors of the **Agnivaṃśa** ("fire-lineage") Rajputs — most commonly the **Pratīhāra (Pratihar/Parihar)**, **Cāhamāna (Chauhan)**, **Caulukya (Solanki)**, and **Paramāra (Parmar/Pawar)** clans — created to guard the earth and the sacrifice. (*Pṛthvīrāj Rāso*; bardic genealogies) [late text][disputed]
  - *Variant (Paramāra charter):* an older, narrower version makes the fire-pit produce a single hero, **Paramāra** ("slayer of foes"), founder of the Paramāra dynasty alone — Vasiṣṭha creating him to recover the wish-cow stolen by Viśvāmitra. This is the form found on Paramāra inscriptions (e.g. the *Nava-sāhasāṅka-carita* milieu); the four-clan list is the later, expanded *Rāso* version. (Paramāra court tradition) [regional][disputed]
  - *Variant (number & names):* bardic lists vary — some give **three** clans, some **four**, occasionally substituting **Tomar/Tuar** or others; the canon is unstable, a mark of its legendary character. (bardic tradition) [folk]
- **Two-clocks caveat** — scholarship treats the Agnikula story as a **post-facto legitimation myth** (Vaiṣṇava/Brāhmaṇical absorption of warrior clans), **not** the historical origin of the Pratihāras, Chauhans, Solankis or Paramāras, who are attested independently in inscriptions. The fire-birth belongs to the *māhātmya* layer of Arbuda's lore. (scholarly consensus) [scholarly][disputed]

## IV. Achaleśvar — Śiva's toe-print

- **The toe-print liṅga** — at **Achaleśvar Mahādev** (within Achalgarh) Śiva is worshipped **not as a carved liṅga but as a natural toe-print / impression** (*aṅguṣṭha*) in the rock, held to be the toe of Śiva that **holds up the earth** — the deity *Achala*, "the immovable / steadfast," who keeps the hill (and the world) from sinking. A deep *brahma-khāī* pit before the shrine is said to be unfathomable, descending to the netherworld. (Achaleśvar sthala tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant (Arbuda link):* the immovability theme twins with the serpent myth — Arbuda steadies the bull, Achala-Śiva steadies the earth; both make the hill a *pillar* fixing the western land in place. (local tradition) [folk]
- **The brass Nandin and Mandākinī tank** — the complex holds a large **pañca-dhātu (five-metal) Nandin** and the **Mandākinī kuṇḍa**; bardic lore tells of buffalo-demons slain at the tank by a Paramāra king, again linking Arbuda's Śaiva and Agnikula strands. (Achalgarh tradition) [folk]
- **Achalgarh** — the hilltop **fort of Achalgarh**, rebuilt by **Rāṇā Kumbha** of Mewar (15th c. CE), guards the shrine; this is the **historical clock**, distinct from the toe-print māhātmya. (Wikipedia, "Achalgarh") [scholarly]

## V. The summits, springs and lakes

- **Guru Śikhar** — the **highest peak of the Aravalli range** (~1722 m), crowned by a shrine to **Dattātreya** (and, in some accounts, to his mother **Anasūyā**) — echoing the Datta summit-cult of nearby [Girnar](girnar-raivataka-datta-tirtha.md); a bell-tower and the "Guru's peak" name mark it as the spiritual high point of western India. (Guru Śikhar sthala tradition) [regional]
- **Gaumukh spring** (see §II) — Vasiṣṭha's perennial cow's-mouth source, the original water-tīrtha of the hill. (Gaumukh tradition) [regional]
- **Nakki Lake** — the heart-shaped lake of Mount Abu, said in folk etymology to have been **dug out by the gods with their fingernails (*nakh*)** in a single night — to imprison or escape the demon **Bāskali / Rakṣa** — making it a god-made sacred tank; modern Abu's central lake. (Nakki sthala tradition) [folk]
- **The cool refuge** — across the māhātmya Arbuda is praised as the **green, watered, cool hill** amid the desert, a refuge for ṛṣis, gods and pilgrims — its springs (Gaumukh, Mandākinī, Nakki, the Trevor's/Toad-rock tanks) defining it as a water-rich oasis-tīrtha. (*Skanda Purāṇa* — *Arbuda-khaṇḍa*) [regional]

## VI. Place in the western tīrtha-network

- **The Aravalli pair with Puṣkara** — Arbuda and **[Puṣkara](pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md)** are the two great Rajasthan hill/lake tīrthas of the Aravallis: Puṣkara the **Brahmā** lake of the eastern Aravallis, Arbuda the **Śiva/Vasiṣṭha** hill of the western Aravallis. (Purāṇic tīrtha geography) [scholarly]
- **The Saurāṣṭra–Marudeśa māhātmya arc** — its charter in the *Skanda Purāṇa*'s **Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa** binds Arbuda to the same western corpus as **[Prabhāsa / Somnāth](prabhasa-somnath-tirtha.md)** and the **[Vastrāpatha / Girnar](girnar-raivataka-datta-tirtha.md)** māhātmyas — the holy nodes of Gujarat–Rajasthan. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa*) [scholarly]
- **A cosmic-mountain echo, in miniature** — as the "son of Himālaya" and earth-steadying pillar (Achala), Arbuda is the western lowland's local stand-in for the [cosmic mountains](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md) — not Meru's peer, but the Aravallis' one sacred summit. (local māhātmya) [folk]
- **The Jain overlay (historical clock)** — Mount Abu is also a premier **Śvetāmbara Jain** tīrtha — the marble **Dilwārā** temples (Vimal Vasahi, 1031 CE; Lūṇa Vasahi, 1230 CE) — a parallel sacred tradition of the same hill, on the recorded-history layer. (Wikipedia, "Dilwara Temples") [scholarly]
