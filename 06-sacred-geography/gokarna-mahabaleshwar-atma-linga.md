# Gokarṇa Mahābaleśvara & the Ātma-liṅga

> **Gokarṇa** ("cow's ear") — the great coastal **Śiva kṣetra** of the Karnataka **Kanara** shore, where the **Mahābaleśvara** liṅga (the **Prāṇaliṅga / Ātma-liṅga**, "Śiva's own self-liṅga") is rooted in the shape of a **cow's ear**. It is the canonical South-Indian site of the **[Rāvaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana)–Ātmaliṅga** episode (where **Gaṇeśa** as a brahmin boy tricks Rāvaṇa into setting the liṅga down), a famed **mukti-kṣetra** styled the **"Kāśī of the South,"** and a **[Tīrtha-yātrā Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md)** ford on the western Sahyādri coast.
>

**Naming & extent** — *Go-karṇa* ("cow's ear") is glossed both from the **ear-of-the-cow shape** of the Mahābaleśvara liṅga and from the tradition that **Śiva re-emerged here through the ear of Pṛthvī (the cow-Earth)** at the close of a *pralaya*. The kṣetra sits on the **Karwar / Uttara Kannada coast** of Karnataka, where the **Gaṅgāvali** and **Aghanāśinī** rivers meet the Arabian Sea, anchored by the **Mahābaleśvara** temple, the **Koṭi-tīrtha** tank, and the **ocean ghāṭ**. The two clocks are kept apart: in *scriptural* time Gokarṇa is a primordial mukti-tīrtha and the stage of the Rāvaṇa episode (placed in the **[Tretā Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the Rāmāyaṇa); in *terrestrial / historical* time it is the temple-town of **Gokarna, Uttara Kannada**, whose worship runs through the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)**. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Sahyādri-khaṇḍa*; *Gokarṇa-māhātmya*; *Śiva Purāṇa*)

## I. The Ātma-liṅga won by Rāvaṇa

> The dominant **South-Indian recension** of the liṅga-relocation legend localises it at Gokarṇa with the **Mahābaleśvara / Prāṇaliṅga** as its prize — to be read against the **North-Indian Vaidyanāth recension** (Deoghar / Parli) carried in the [12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md) file. Both share the same Rāvaṇa–Gaṇeśa–set-down skeleton; they differ only in *where* the liṅga roots.

- **Kaikasī's earthen liṅga** — Rāvaṇa's mother **Kaikasī** worshipped a daily **sand/earth liṅga** of Śiva at the seashore; when a tide washed it away, she lamented that she had never worshipped a true, indestructible liṅga. Rāvaṇa vowed to fetch Śiva's **own Ātma-liṅga** from Kailāsa for her. (*Gokarṇa-māhātmya*; Sahyādri tradition) [regional]
- **Tapas for the Ātma-liṅga** — **Rāvaṇa**, the rākṣasa king of Laṅkā, performed extreme austerities to **Śiva** (in the southern telling, on the slopes of Kailāsa), offering his **heads** one by one into the fire until Śiva, pleased, appeared. (*Śiva Purāṇa*; *Gokarṇa-māhātmya*)
- **The boon and its peril** — Śiva granted Rāvaṇa the **Ātma-liṅga** (the *Prāṇaliṅga*, the very core of Śiva's power), warning that **once set on the ground it would root immovably** wherever it touched earth. (*Śiva Purāṇa*; *Gokarṇa-māhātmya*)
  - *Variant (Pārvatī instead of the liṅga):* in some southern tellings Rāvaṇa first sought **Pārvatī** herself and was deceived with a substitute; chastened, he asked for the Ātma-liṅga as the true boon. (*Gokarṇa-māhātmya*; folk Rāmāyaṇa) [folk]

## II. The gods' alarm and Gaṇeśa's trick

- **The devas appeal to Viṣṇu and Gaṇeśa** — alarmed that an invincible Rāvaṇa armed with the Ātma-liṅga would be unstoppable, the gods begged **Viṣṇu** and **Gaṇeśa** to prevent the liṅga reaching Laṅkā. (*Gokarṇa-māhātmya*; Sahyādri tradition)
- **Viṣṇu hides the sun** — to force Rāvaṇa to halt for his **sandhyā** (evening prayer), Viṣṇu concealed the sun with his **Sudarśana** (a false dusk), so that the punctilious Rāvaṇa, unable to skip his rites, needed someone to hold the liṅga. (*Gokarṇa-māhātmya*) [regional]
- **Gaṇeśa as the brahmin boy** — **Gaṇeśa** took the form of a small **brahmin cowherd boy** and agreed to hold the Ātma-liṅga, but on the condition that he would **set it down and call out three times**; if Rāvaṇa did not return by the third call, he would place it on the ground. (*Gokarṇa-māhātmya*; *Śiva Purāṇa*)
- **The liṅga set down at Gokarṇa** — Rāvaṇa's prayers ran long; the boy called thrice and **planted the liṅga on the earth** at Gokarṇa, where it rooted at once. Returning, Rāvaṇa could not budge it. (*Gokarṇa-māhātmya*; *Śiva Purāṇa*)
- **Mahā-bala — "the great strength"** — Rāvaṇa heaved at the fixed liṅga with all his might and failed; from this *mahā-bala* ("great force") exerted in vain the liṅga is named **Mahābaleśvara**. (*Gokarṇa-māhātmya*; Sahyādri tradition)

## III. The cow's ear and the scattered casings

- **Go-karṇa — the cow's-ear shape** — in pulling, Rāvaṇa is said to have **twisted the liṅga**, leaving it in the form of a **cow's ear (go-karṇa)** — the aetiology of both the liṅga's shape and the place-name. (*Gokarṇa-māhātmya*) [regional]
  - *Variant (Earth's ear):* an older gloss derives *Gokarṇa* from Śiva's emergence **through the ear of Pṛthvī (the cow-Earth)** after a deluge — a primordial naming independent of the Rāvaṇa episode. (Sahyādri tradition) [regional]
- **The discarded coverings flung away** — in frustration Rāvaṇa **hurled away** the casket, lid, string, and cloth that had wrapped the liṅga; where each fell, a subsidiary Śiva shrine arose — the **Dhāreśvara, Guṇavanteśvara, Murudeśvara, and Śejjeśvara** liṅgas — together with Gokarṇa forming a regional five-shrine set. (*Gokarṇa-māhātmya*; coastal Karnataka tradition) [regional][folk]
- **Murudeśvara** — the cloth (or covering) flung to **Murudeśvara** (Bhaṭkal taluk) became its famous seaside liṅga, the best-known of the cast-off shrines. (Sahyādri / coastal tradition) [folk]

## IV. Gokarṇa as mukti-kṣetra — the "Kāśī of the South"

- **A liberation-tīrtha rivalling Kāśī** — Gokarṇa is praised as a **mukti-sthala** where death or even a single bath grants liberation, drawing the epithet **"Kāśī of the South"** (Dakṣiṇa-Kāśī) and **"Gaṅgā of the South."** (*Gokarṇa-māhātmya*; Sahyādri-khaṇḍa) — compare [Kāśī Viśvanātha](kashi-varanasi.md). [regional]
- **The Koṭi-tīrtha** — the temple's sacred tank, the **Koṭi-tīrtha** ("ten-million fords"), is the prime bathing-place; rites for the dead (*śrāddha*, *nārāyaṇa-bali*, *tripiṇḍī*) performed here are held especially potent — a southern parallel to [Gayā](gaya-pitru-tirtha.md). (*Gokarṇa-māhātmya*; sthala tradition) [regional]
- **The story of the sinner Mitrasaha / Dhīra** — the *māhātmya* tells of a grievous sinner liberated merely by **dying within Gokarṇa**, illustrating its power to dissolve even the worst karma. (*Gokarṇa-māhātmya*) [folk]
- **Sparśa-darśana of the Ātma-liṅga** — uniquely, devotees are permitted to **touch** the Mahābaleśvara liṅga directly (reaching into the *yoni-pīṭha*), since it is Śiva's very *prāṇa* — a privilege rare among major liṅgas. (sthala tradition) [regional]

## V. Gokarṇa in the Mahābhārata and Rāmāyaṇa

- **A western Tīrtha-yātrā ford** — the **[Tīrtha-yātrā Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md)** of the *Vana Parva* names **Gokarṇa** among the holy sites of the **western / southern coast**, praising it as a tīrtha frequented by the gods and sages where bathing yields great merit. (*Mbh* Vana Parva, *Tīrtha-yātrā Parva*)
- **Vālmīki's mention** — the **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa** notes **Gokarṇa** among renowned tīrthas and as a place of austerity (associated in tradition with the sage who, near Gokarṇa, became **Vālmīki**); the kṣetra thus predates the epic's narrative frame. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*) [disputed]
- **Tretā-Yuga anchoring** — because the Rāvaṇa episode belongs to the Rāmāyaṇa world, the Ātma-liṅga's rooting is fixed in **scriptural** [Tretā-Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) time, while the temple's worship is a continuous **Kali-Yuga** institution — the two-clocks distinction held throughout the *māhātmyas*.

## VI. Tīrtha geography & the temple circuit

- **A composite coastal kṣetra** — the modern circuit binds the **Mahābaleśvara** temple, the **Koṭi-tīrtha** tank, the **Gokarṇanātha / Bhadrakālī** and **Tāmra-Gaurī** shrines, the ocean ghāṭ, and the five cast-off liṅgas (Dhāreśvara–Murudeśvara) along the Kanara coast. (Gokarṇa sthala tradition)
- **Place in the structured sets** — Gokarṇa is **not** counted among the canonical **[12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md)** nor the **[Pañcha Bhūta Sthalams](pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md)**, yet it is reckoned a *svayambhu*-grade Ātma-liṅga of the first rank and a fixed node in the **western-coast pilgrimage circuit** ([Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)). (Purāṇic tīrtha-lists)
- **Recension contrast (the "set-down" twins)** — the same Rāvaṇa–Gaṇeśa legend roots the liṅga at **Gokarṇa (south)** in the Sahyādri tradition and at **Vaidyanāth / Baidyanāth (north — Deoghar / Parli)** in the Śiva-Purāṇa jyotirliṅga tradition; the traditions are **never merged** but represent parallel localisations of one mytheme. (compare [12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md)) [scholarly]
- **The historical clock** — the present **Mahābaleśvara** temple is a stone structure of the Kanara coast, patronised under successive dynasties (Kadambas onward) and venerated as a living mukti-tīrtha into the present day — the *recorded-history* layer, distinct from the scriptural events above.
