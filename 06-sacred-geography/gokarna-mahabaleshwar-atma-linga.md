# Gokarṇa Mahābaleśvara & the Ātma-liṅga

> **Gokarṇa** ("cow's ear") is the great coastal **Śiva kṣetra** of the Karnataka **Kanara** shore. Its liṅga, **Mahābala / Mahābaleśvara**, is called the **Ātma-liṅga / Prāṇaliṅga** ("Śiva's own self-liṅga") in local tradition. The Purāṇic chapters on Gokarṇa say only that **[Rāvaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana)** won this liṅga by tapas and that **Gaṇanāyaka** installed it here. The fuller story, in which **Gaṇeśa**, disguised as a boy, tricks Rāvaṇa into letting the liṅga touch the ground, belongs to the local *māhātmya* and to popular retellings. The site is famed as a **mukti-kṣetra**, popularly styled the **"Kāśī of the South,"** and it is a ford of the **[Tīrtha-yātrā Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md)** on the western coast.
>
> Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md) · Related: [12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md) · [Pañcha Bhūta Sthalams](pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md) · [Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md) · [Rāmāyaṇa deep-dive](../04-deep-dives/ramayana)

**Naming & extent.** Two glosses of *Go-karṇa* ("cow's ear") circulate. One takes it from the **ear-of-the-cow shape** of the Mahābaleśvara liṅga. The other holds that **Rudra emerged here through the ear of Pṛthvī, the Earth in the form of a cow**. Neither gloss appears in the Purāṇic Gokarṇa chapters checked for this page (ŚP 4.8–10; SkP 3.3.2–3), and both are treated here as local tradition [regional]. The texts do agree on the setting: the kṣetra lies **on the shore of the western ocean** (ŚP 4.8.1–4, 4.10.44; SkP 3.3.2.115–122), and Pulastya's tīrtha list in the Mahābhārata calls it *samudramadhye*, "in the midst of the sea" (Mbh 3.83.22). Today it is the temple-town of **Gokarna, Uttara Kannada**, between the mouths of the **Gaṅgāvali** and **Aghanāśinī** rivers on the Arabian Sea. Its anchors are the **Mahābaleśvara** temple, the **Koṭi-tīrtha** tank and the ocean ghāṭ.

The two clocks are kept apart here. In *scriptural* time, Gokarṇa is a primordial tīrtha, and the Rāmāyaṇa world ([Tretā Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)) already knows it as a place of tapas. In *historical* time, its worship runs through the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)**.

*Not to be confused with* other places of the same name. J. L. Shastri's note on ŚP 4.8 separates this Kanara Gokarṇa from a **Gokarṇa in Nepal**, a town on the Bāgmatī river. The **Tirugokarṇam** temple at Pudukkottai in Tamil Nadu is also a separate shrine. In Dhaumya's list of southern tīrthas, the Mahābhārata names a *Gokarṇa* lake in a Pāṇḍya / Tāmraparṇī setting (Mbh 3.86.11–13); that passage is recorded in §V but is not assumed to describe the Kanara site.

## I. The Ātma-liṅga won by Rāvaṇa

> **Three layers** must be kept apart:
>
> 1. **The Purāṇic Gokarṇa core.** Two parallel passages, ŚP 4.8.14 and SkP 3.3.2.88–93, say only that the liṅga was won by Rāvaṇa through severe tapas and installed at Gokarṇa by **Gaṇanāyaka**.
> 2. **The Śiva Purāṇa's Vaidyanātha chapter (ŚP 4.28).** This chapter tells a fuller relocation story: tapas, a boon, a liṅga that roots wherever it is set down, and an **unnamed cowherd** who sets it down. In that chapter the liṅga becomes **Vaidyanātha**; it never mentions Gokarṇa or Gaṇeśa. That telling is covered in the [12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md) file.
> 3. **The local *Gokarṇa-māhātmya* and popular telling.** This layer adds Kaikasī's sand-liṅga, Viṣṇu's false dusk, Gaṇeśa disguised as a boy who calls three times, the twisting into a cow's ear, and the scattered coverings.
>
> In the two Purāṇic accounts the liṅga is first carried off and then roots where it is set down. The two accounts disagree about where this happens and who sets the liṅga down.

- **The Purāṇic statement** — Rāvaṇa, a rākṣasa, obtained the liṅga *by severe penance*, and **Gaṇanāyaka** installed it at Gokarṇa. Gaṇanāyaka is conventionally read as Gaṇeśa. No trick is described, and the passage gives no reason for the installation. (ŚP Koṭirudra-saṃhitā 4.8.14; SkP Brahma-khaṇḍa, Brahmottara-khaṇḍa 3.3.2.88–93)
- **Kaikasī's earthen liṅga** — Rāvaṇa's mother **Kaikasī** worshipped a daily **sand/earth liṅga** of Śiva at the seashore. When it was lost, she lamented that she had never worshipped an indestructible liṅga, and Rāvaṇa vowed to fetch Śiva's **own Ātma-liṅga** from Kailāsa for her. (popular Gokarṇa sthala tradition) [regional][folk]
  - *Variant (how the liṅga was lost):* some tellings say the tide washed it away; others say **Indra**, jealous of her worship, stole it and threw it into the sea. (popular retellings) [folk]
  - *Canonical counterpart (Vālmīki):* in the Rāmāyaṇa, Kaikasī also sets her sons on the path of tapas, but for a different reason. Seeing **Vaiśravaṇa** (Kubera) arrive in his Puṣpaka, she urges Daśagrīva to become his equal. Daśagrīva vows to do so and goes with his brothers to **the hermitage of Gokarṇa** to perform tapas. (*VR* 7.9.31–37, Baroda CE)
- **Tapas and the head-offering** — in the popular Gokarṇa telling, **Rāvaṇa** performs extreme austerities to **Śiva** on Kailāsa and offers his **heads** one by one into the fire until Śiva, pleased, appears. (popular Gokarṇa tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant (Vālmīki: at Gokarṇa, to Brahmā):* at Gokarṇa, Daśānana fasts for 10,000 years and offers one head into the fire at the end of each thousand years. As he is about to cut the tenth, **Brahmā** (Pitāmaha) appears. The boon is invulnerability to gods, dānavas, nāgas, yakṣas and others; men are excluded because Rāvaṇa scorns them. No liṅga is involved. (*VR* 7.10.10–20)
  - *Variant (Śiva Purāṇa, Vaidyanātha recension):* Rāvaṇa first propitiates Śiva on **Kailāsa**, then in a grove on the southern side of **Himavat**, offering nine heads before Śiva appears and restores them. In this version he asks for unequalled strength. (ŚP 4.28.1–10, 4.28.48–54)
- **The boon and its peril** — Śiva gives Rāvaṇa the liṅga and warns that **wherever it is set on the ground it will become fixed**. This condition is attested in the Śiva Purāṇa's *Vaidyanātha* chapter (ŚP 4.28.15). In the Gokarṇa telling, the liṅga is the **Ātma-liṅga / Prāṇaliṅga**, the core of Śiva's power. The Purāṇic Gokarṇa chapters checked here (in translation) call it simply the liṅga **Mahābala**. (ŚP 4.28.15; popular Gokarṇa tradition)
  - *Variant (Pārvatī instead of the liṅga):* in some southern tellings, Rāvaṇa first sought **Pārvatī** herself and was deceived with a substitute. Chastened, he then asked for the Ātma-liṅga. This variant was not located in the Purāṇic chapters checked. (folk Rāmāyaṇa tradition) [folk]

## II. The gods' alarm and Gaṇeśa's trick

- **The devas appeal to Viṣṇu and Gaṇeśa** — the gods fear that Rāvaṇa, armed with the Ātma-liṅga, would be unstoppable, so they beg **Viṣṇu** and **Gaṇeśa** to keep it from reaching Laṅkā. (popular Gokarṇa tradition) [regional]
  - *Compare (Śiva Purāṇa, Vaidyanātha):* there, Indra and the gods take counsel, and **Nārada** offers to help them. After the liṅga is fixed, Nārada goads Rāvaṇa into lifting Kailāsa, and Śiva then curses him. (ŚP 4.28.23–34, 4.28.61–72)
- **Viṣṇu hides the sun** — to force Rāvaṇa to halt for his **sandhyā** (evening rite), Viṣṇu hides the sun with his **Sudarśana** disc, creating a false dusk. The punctilious Rāvaṇa will not skip his rites and so needs someone to hold the liṅga. (popular Gokarṇa tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant (Śiva Purāṇa, Vaidyanātha):* there is no false dusk; Rāvaṇa stops because, through Śiva's māyā, he feels an urge to urinate that he cannot resist. (ŚP 4.28.17)
- **Gaṇeśa as the brahmin boy** — **Gaṇeśa** takes the form of a small **brahmin cowherd boy** and agrees to hold the Ātma-liṅga on one condition: he will **call out three times**, and if Rāvaṇa has not returned by the third call, he will set the liṅga down. (popular Gokarṇa tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant (Śiva Purāṇa, Vaidyanātha):* the holder is an **unnamed cowherd**, not Gaṇeśa. When Rāvaṇa fails to return, he finds the weight unbearable and sets the liṅga down himself. (ŚP 4.28.18–19)
- **The liṅga set down at Gokarṇa** — Rāvaṇa's rites run long, and the boy calls three times and **plants the liṅga on the earth** at Gokarṇa, where it roots at once. Rāvaṇa returns and cannot budge it. (popular Gokarṇa tradition) [regional]. The Purāṇic core says only that Gaṇanāyaka installed it (ŚP 4.8.14; SkP 3.3.2.88–93).
- **Mahā-bala ("the great strength")** — popular etymology says Rāvaṇa heaved at the fixed liṅga with all his might and failed, and that this *mahā-bala* ("great force") spent in vain gives the liṅga its name. (popular Gokarṇa tradition) [folk]. The Purāṇic chapters simply name the liṅga **Mahābala**, saying it pervades the nether worlds and *softens* in the Kali age (ŚP 4.8.9).
- **The blow on Gaṇeśa's head** — in anger, Rāvaṇa strikes the boy on the head. The standing, two-armed image in Gokarṇa's **Mahāgaṇapati** shrine bears a dent on the crown that is pointed out as the mark of this blow. (temple tradition) [folk]. The historian P. Gururaj Bhat (*Studies in Tuluva History and Culture*) dates the image to about the **6th century CE**, as reported by the *Deccan Chronicle* on 25 Jul 2026. [scholarly]

## III. The cow's ear and the scattered casings

- **Go-karṇa, the cow's-ear shape** — in pulling, Rāvaṇa is said to have **twisted the liṅga** into the form of a **cow's ear (go-karṇa)**. This explains both the liṅga's shape and the place-name. (popular Gokarṇa tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant (Earth's ear):* another gloss derives *Gokarṇa* from **Rudra's emergence through the ear of Pṛthvī (the cow-Earth)**. This naming does not depend on the Rāvaṇa episode, and the site is sometimes called *Rudra-yoni*. The gloss is found in secondary retellings; it was not located in the Purāṇic chapters checked. (local tradition) [regional]
- **The discarded coverings flung away** — in frustration, Rāvaṇa **hurls away** the casket, lid, string and cloth that had wrapped the liṅga. Where each falls, a Śiva shrine arises: **Dhāreśvara**, **Guṇavanteśvara**, **Murudeśvara** and **Sajjeśvara** (also spelled Śejjeśvara). With Gokarṇa they form the coastal *pañca-kṣetra*. Retellings disagree about which covering fell where. (coastal Karnataka tradition) [regional][folk]
- **Murudeśvara** — the cloth (or covering) that fell at **Murudeśvara** (Bhaṭkal taluk) became its famous seaside liṅga, the best known of the cast-off shrines. (coastal tradition) [folk]

## IV. Gokarṇa as mukti-kṣetra — the "Kāśī of the South"

- **The Purāṇic praise of Gokarṇa** — Gokarṇa destroys sins *such as brahmin-slaughter*. It holds "crores and crores" of liṅgas; everything there is a liṅga, and all its waters are tīrthas (ŚP 4.8.4–6). SkP 3.3.2 says the same: *every stone in Gokarṇa is a Śiva-liṅga*, and Mahābala is the *sole overlord* of all Śiva-liṅgas. The Mahābaleśvara image is a **doorway to salvation**, and worship on the dark fourteenth of Māgha (Śivarātri) frees even sinners (ŚP 4.8.24–25).
- **The epic merit** — after a three-night fast, worship of Īśāna here yields the merit of **ten aśvamedhas** and **gāṇapatya** (the rank of one of Śiva's gaṇas). A twelve-night stay purifies the self. (Mbh 3.83.25, Baroda CE; Ganguli, Vana Parva §LXXXV)
- **"Kāśī of the South"** — the popular epithet **Dakṣiṇa-Kāśī** ranks Gokarṇa beside Kāśī as a liberation-tīrtha. Compare [Kāśī Viśvanātha](kashi-varanasi.md). (popular usage) [regional]
- **The liṅga that changes colour** — Mahābala is said to be white in Kṛta, deep red in Tretā, yellow in Dvāpara and dark in Kali. (ŚP 4.8.8; SkP 3.3.2)
- **The guardians of the four gates** — Viṣṇu, Brahmā, Indra and the gods attend the **eastern** gate. Yama, Citragupta, Agni, the Pitṛs and the Rudras attend the **southern** gate. Varuṇa and the rivers, the Gaṅgā among them, attend the **western** gate. Vāyu, Kubera, **Bhadrakālī**, Caṇḍikā and the Mothers attend the **northern** gate. (ŚP 4.8.15–19)
- **The Koṭi-tīrtha** — the sacred tank, **Koṭi-tīrtha** ("ten-million fords"), is named in the Skanda Purāṇa's Gokarṇa chapter as the foremost of its tīrthas (SkP 3.3.2). Local practice holds rites for the dead performed here (*śrāddha*, *nārāyaṇa-bali*, *tripiṇḍī*) to be especially potent; compare [Gayā](gaya-pitru-tirtha.md). (sthala practice) [regional]
- **King Mitrasaha (Kalmāṣapāda) freed at Gokarṇa** — **Mitrasaha**, also called **Kalmāṣapāda** / Kalmāṣāṅghri, is an Ikṣvāku king. Vasiṣṭha cursed him to become a rākṣasa after a demon cook served human flesh at a śrāddha. When the curse ended, he was pursued by personified **Brahmahatyā**. Near **Mithilā** the sage **Gautama** directed him to Gokarṇa. There he **bathed in the sacred ponds and worshipped Mahābala**, and his sins were wiped away without trace. (ŚP 4.10.1–49; SkP 3.3.2.37–66; SkP 3.3.3.156–161, where Gautama's advice to the same king concludes)
- **The blind Caṇḍālī woman** — in a former life she was a brahmin girl who fell into sin: she lived with a śūdra, drank wine, and in drunkenness killed and ate a calf. Reborn blind among Caṇḍālas, she begged at Gokarṇa on **Śiva-caturdaśī**. A bunch of **bilva** leaves thrown into her hands fell from her onto a liṅga during the night. That unwitting offering and her night's vigil carried her to Śiva's realm. (ŚP 4.9; SkP 3.3.3)
  - *Variant (her former name):* she is **Sauminī** in the Śiva Purāṇa (ŚP 4.9.3) and **Sumitrā**, daughter of a brahmin of the Kekaya land, in the Skanda Purāṇa (SkP 3.3.3.32–33).
- **Sparśa-darśana of the Ātma-liṅga** — devotees may **touch** the Mahābaleśvara liṅga, reaching into the *yoni-pīṭha*, because it is held to be Śiva's very *prāṇa*. (sthala practice) [regional]

## V. Gokarṇa in the Mahābhārata and Rāmāyaṇa

- **Pulastya's tīrtha list** — after Kanyā-tīrtha, the pilgrim reaches **Gokarṇa**, *renowned in the three worlds, in the midst of the sea, honoured by all*. Brahmā and the gods, ṛṣis, bhūtas, yakṣas, piśācas, kinnaras, nāgas, siddhas, cāraṇas, gandharvas, humans, rivers, seas and mountains all worship **Umāpati** there. The merit of the place follows (see §IV). The *sthāna* of Gāyatrī, renowned in the three worlds, comes next in the list. (Mbh 3.83.22–26, Baroda CE; Ganguli, Vana Parva §LXXXV)
- **Dhaumya's southern list** — after the Pāṇḍya tīrthas, within its description of the Tāmraparṇī, the list names *the place renowned in the three worlds as Gokarṇa*, where the gods performed tapas. Ganguli renders it as the *lake of Gokarna*, cool, sacred and hard for the unpurified to reach. The Pāṇḍya setting makes it uncertain whether this is the Kanara site; the passage is recorded separately rather than merged. (Mbh 3.86.11–13; Ganguli, Vana Parva §LXXXVIII) [disputed]
- **Rāvaṇa at Gokarṇa in the Rāmopākhyāna** — resolved on his course against Rāma, Rāvaṇa flies over Trikūṭa and the Kāla mountains, crosses the ocean and comes to **Gokarṇa**, *the beloved, tranquil place of the trident-bearer* (Śiva). There he meets his former minister **Mārīca**, who had become an ascetic out of fear of Rāma. (Mbh 3.261.52–55, Baroda CE; Ganguli, Vana Parva §CCLXXV)
  - *Interpolation:* a passage that appears only in some manuscripts and is relegated to the CE apparatus (3.259.19 \*1236) has Daśagrīva and Vibhīṣaṇa perform tapas for thousands of years *in this Gokarṇa forest of austerity*. [interpolation]
- **Other epic notices** — the epic places other figures at Gokarṇa as well:
  - **Śeṣa** performs tapas at Gokarṇa among other tīrthas (Mbh 1.32.3).
  - **Arjuna**, during his forest exile, goes to Gokarṇa after Maṇalūra and the birth of Babhruvāhana (Mbh 1.209.24).
  - Gokarṇa is named as a haunt of ascetics (*tapodhanāḥ*) (Mbh 6.7.48).
  - The sage **Ālambāyana** performs tapas at Gokarṇa for a hundred years and receives a hundred sons from **Śarva** (Mbh 13.18.5–6).
  - The aśvamedha horse, with Arjuna following, passes Gokarṇa on its way to Prabhāsa (Mbh 14.84.12). (Baroda CE)
- **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa** — Gokarṇa is a place of tapas in the Rāmāyaṇa itself:
  - The childless **Bhagīratha** performs a long tapas at Gokarṇa, arms raised, amid five fires, eating once a month, until Brahmā is pleased (*VR* 1.41.11–13).
  - Sītā is told that **Kesarī**, Hanumān's father, went to Gokarṇa mountain and, at the holy tīrtha of the ocean, slew Śambasādana (*VR* 5.33.73–74).
  - **Daśagrīva**, **Kumbhakarṇa** and **Vibhīṣaṇa** perform their great tapas at the **Gokarṇa hermitage**, where Brahmā grants their boons (*VR* 7.9.37–7.10.40; see §I).

  All are cited from the Baroda CE. The kṣetra therefore has a canonical link to Rāvaṇa in Vālmīki, independent of the liṅga story.
- **Tretā-Yuga anchoring** — the Ātma-liṅga's rooting is placed in **scriptural** [Tretā-Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) time because the Rāvaṇa episode belongs to the Rāmāyaṇa world, while the temple's worship is a continuous **Kali-Yuga** institution. The two clocks are kept apart.

### Other Purāṇic and classical notices

- **Paraśurāma recovers Gokarṇa from the sea** — the sages who lived at Gokarṇa tell **Paraśurāma** that the holy place, with its sacred ponds, sank into the ocean when the sons of Sagara were digging the earth. Paraśurāma goes to the western shore and threatens **Varuṇa** with a fiery missile, and Varuṇa submits. Paraśurāma then hurls his **sruva** (sacrificial ladle), which lands 200 yojanas away at **Śūrpāraka**. The sea withdraws that far, and the holy spot *regains its original state*. (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa 2.3.57.12–75, 2.3.58.1–31, tr. Tagare)
- **Balarāma's pilgrimage** — after the Keralas and Trigartas, **Balarāma** visits *the Śiva-kṣetra called Gokarṇa, where Dhūrjaṭi is ever present*, and then goes on to the Āryā Dvaipāyanī and Śūrpāraka. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.79.19–20)
- **Kālidāsa** — **Nārada** travels to play his vīṇā before *the Lord whose abode is Gokarṇa, on the shore of the southern ocean*. His garland then falls on Indumatī. (*Raghuvaṃśa* 8.33) This is an early classical attestation of the shrine.

## VI. Tīrtha geography & the temple circuit

- **A composite coastal kṣetra** — the circuit links the **Mahābaleśvara** temple, the **Mahāgaṇapati** shrine, the **Bhadrakālī** and **Tāmra-Gaurī** shrines, the **Koṭi-tīrtha** tank and the ocean ghāṭ. It also takes in the four outlying cast-off liṅgas (Dhāreśvara, Guṇavanteśvara, Murudeśvara, Sajjeśvara) along the Kanara coast. Bhadrakālī already appears among Mahābala's gate-guardians in ŚP 4.8.19. (Gokarṇa sthala tradition)
- **Place in the structured sets** — Gokarṇa is **not** one of the canonical **[12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md)** or the **[Pañcha Bhūta Sthalams](pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md)**. The Skanda Purāṇa's Gokarṇa chapter nevertheless calls Mahābala the overlord of all Śiva-liṅgas (SkP 3.3.2), and Gokarṇa is a fixed node in the **western-coast pilgrimage circuit** ([Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)). Tamil Śaiva enumerations count Gokarṇa among the 275 *pāḍal peṟṟa* talams of the Tēvāram; the specific hymns were not verified for this page. [regional]
- **Recension contrast (the "set-down" twins)** — one mytheme, a liṅga won by Rāvaṇa and fixed where it was set down en route, is localised in two places:
  - **Gokarṇa (south)**, installed by **Gaṇanāyaka** (ŚP 4.8.14; SkP 3.3.2.88–93).
  - **Vaidyanātha (north; claimed by Deoghar and by Parli)**, set down by an **unnamed cowherd** (ŚP 4.28.18–25).

  The Śiva Purāṇa carries both localisations without reconciling them. ŚP 4.28 names only a cowherd, so where popular Vaidyanāth retellings give the role to Gaṇeśa, that detail does not come from this chapter. The two traditions are **not merged** here. (compare [12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md)) [regional]
- **The historical clock** — the shrine is attested in Kālidāsa's *Raghuvaṃśa* (8.33). A standing two-armed Mahāgaṇapati image is dated to about the 6th century CE by P. Gururaj Bhat [scholarly]. Tradition, repeated in popular sources but not verified here, credits the first temple to the **Kadamba** founder Mayūraśarman / Mayūravarman (4th century CE) [unverified]. Gokarṇa remains a living mukti-tīrtha today. This *recorded-history* layer is distinct from the scriptural events above.
