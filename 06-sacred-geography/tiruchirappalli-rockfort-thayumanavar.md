# Thāyumānavar of Tiruchirāppaḷḷi Rock Fort — Śiva as the mother-midwife

> On the ancient granite outcrop (**Malaikōṭṭai**, "the rock fort") rising above the **Kāverī** at **Tiruchirāppaḷḷi** (Trichy, Tamil Nadu) — a **[Pāḍal Petṟa Sthalam](275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md)** sung in the Tēvāram, where Śiva as **Thāyumānavar / Mātṛbhūtēśvara** ("he who became even the mother") once midwifed a devotee's childbirth, and where, at the summit, **Ucchi Piḷḷaiyār** (Gaṇeśa) fled the wrath of Vibhīṣaṇa after grounding the [Raṅganātha vimāna at nearby Śrīraṅgam](srirangam-ranganatha.md).
>

The **Rock Fort** of Tiruchirāppaḷḷi is a single mass of Archaean granite carrying **two ancient shrines on one hill**: on its lower southern flank the Śaiva cave-temple of **Thāyumānaswāmi** (Sanskrit **Mātṛbhūtēśvara**), and at its very apex the small Gaṇeśa shrine of **Ucchi Piḷḷaiyār**. The two enshrine two of the most beloved sthala-legends of the Kāverī delta — one Śaiva, one bound to the Vaiṣṇava epic of [Śrīraṅgam](srirangam-ranganatha.md) — and both turn on the same river in flood. As with every *māhātmya* site, the **legend-layer** (undatable "scriptural clock") and the **historical layer** (Pallava rock-cut caves, Tēvāram, Nāyaka fort — the "academic clock") diverge sharply; they are kept apart below.

---

## I. Thāyumānavar — "He Who Became Even the Mother"

- **The devotee Ratnāvatī in labour** — the sthala-purāṇa tells of **Ratnāvatī** (also given as **Ratnāvalī**), a pious young wife heavy with child on the town's bank of the Kāverī, who as her hour came longed for her **own mother** — the only one who could attend a first birth — living across the river. (Tiruchirāppaḷḷi / Sirappaḷḷi sthala-purāṇa) [regional]
- **The Kāverī in spate** — the river rose in sudden **flood**, and the mother, hurrying to her daughter, was **stranded on the far bank**, unable to cross the torrent while the girl's pains grew. (Sirappaḷḷi sthala-purāṇa) [folk]
- **Śiva takes the mother's form** — moved by Ratnāvatī's devotion and helplessness, **Śiva of the rock assumed the exact form of the absent mother**, came to the girl's side, **comforted her and delivered the child**, acting as nurse and midwife through the birth — the singular grace that names the shrine. (Sirappaḷḷi sthala-purāṇa) [regional]
- **The vanishing when the true mother comes** — when the flood at last subsided and the **real mother arrived**, the Śiva-form **disappeared**; the two women, comparing accounts, realized the Lord himself had stood in for the mother, and the deity was thereafter called **Thāyumānavar** — Tamil *tāy* ("mother") + *ān(a)* ("became") + *avar* ("he who") = "**he who became even the mother**." (Sirappaḷḷi sthala-purāṇa) [regional]
- **Mātṛbhūtēśvara — the Sanskrit name** — the Sanskrit dedication renders the Tamil precisely: **Mātṛ-bhūta-īśvara**, "the Lord who became (took the being of) the mother," making explicit that here the *linga* is worshipped not as father-god but as **the maternal principle incarnate**. (Sthala-purāṇa; temple dedication) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (the debt / the merchant's daughter) [folk]:* some tellings frame the girl as a merchant's daughter whose **husband and household were away**, so that no woman at all could reach her but the Lord — sharpening the point that Śiva supplies precisely the care that human kinship fails to deliver.
- **The svayambhū liṅga on the rock** — the object of worship is a **self-manifest (svayambhū) liṅga** on the living granite of the hill's face, not an installed stone; the cave-shrine is cut around it. (Sthala-purāṇa; temple tradition) [regional]
- **The consort Mattuvār Kuḻali** — the goddess of the shrine is **Mattuvār Kuḻali Ammai** (Sanskrit **Sugandha Kuntaḷāmbikā**), "she of the honey-fragrant tresses," worshipped in her own sannidhi. (Temple tradition) [regional]

## II. The Rock Itself — an Element and a Mother

- **A hill that is worshipped as body, not seat** — like [Aruṇācala](arunachala-tiruvannamalai-fire-hill.md) and the [Pañca Bhūta Sthalams](pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md), the Rock Fort is a site where the **mountain-mass itself is sacred**; the Thāyumānavar cave does not enshrine a mountain-god so much as sanctify the rock as the standing form on which the maternal grace was shown. (Sthala tradition) [regional]
- **The Kāverī as the pivot of both legends** — both the Thāyumānavar birth-tale and the Ucchi Piḷḷaiyār episode turn on the **Kāverī in flood** and on **crossing (or failing to cross) the river** — the same delta geography that makes nearby [Śrīraṅgam](srirangam-ranganatha.md) an island; see [Sacred Rivers & Descent-Myths](sacred-rivers-and-descent-myths.md). (Sthala-purāṇas of the Kāverī delta) [scholarly]

## III. Ucchi Piḷḷaiyār — Gaṇeśa Flees Vibhīṣaṇa to the Summit

The Rock Fort's second legend is the **hill-sequel to the [Śrīraṅga-māhātmya](srirangam-ranganatha.md)**: it explains how the Gaṇeśa who tricked Vibhīṣaṇa on the Kāverī island came to sit atop the Trichy rock. (This is the local completion of the episode summarized at the Śrīraṅgam node.)

- **The vimāna set down at Tiruvaraṅgam** — carrying the **Raṅganātha vimāna** south toward Laṅkā under the rule that *wherever set on the ground it roots forever*, **Vibhīṣaṇa** halts on the Kāverī island for his **sandhyā** worship and entrusts the shrine to a **cowherd boy** — **Gaṇeśa in disguise** — who promptly **sets it on the earth**, fixing Raṅganātha at [Śrīraṅgam](srirangam-ranganatha.md) for all time. (Śrīraṅga-māhātmya; Sirappaḷḷi sthala-purāṇa) [regional]
- **The chase up the rock** — the enraged Vibhīṣaṇa pursues the fleeing boy, who **runs to the top of the Tiruchirāppaḷḷi rock**; catching him at the summit, Vibhīṣaṇa **strikes him a blow on the crown of the head (Tamil *ucci*)**, at which the boy reveals himself as **Gaṇeśa**. (Sirappaḷḷi sthala-purāṇa) [regional]
- **"Ucchi Piḷḷaiyār" — the double meaning of *ucci*** — Gaṇeśa remains enshrined at the **apex**, named **Ucchi Piḷḷaiyār**, where *ucci* means both the **summit** of the hill and the **crown of the head** he was struck upon — the pun fixing both the deity's throne and the wound of the tale. (Sthala-purāṇa; temple tradition) [folk]
- **Cross-link and disagreement** — the [Śrīraṅgam node](srirangam-ranganatha.md) carries this same episode as a **[folk] variant** (the cowherd trick) attached to the Vibhīṣaṇa legend; the *canonical Śrīraṅga-māhātmya* does not require Gaṇeśa at all — the deity there simply refuses to rise once set down. The Rock Fort tradition is the telling that **needs** Gaṇeśa, because it must explain the summit shrine. Both are preserved, not merged. (Comparative sthala tradition) [regional]

## IV. Trichy — the Name and the Three-Headed Demon

- **Trisirapuram — the asura's penance** — one sthala-etymology derives **Tiruchirāppaḷḷi** from **Trisira-puram**, "the town of the three-headed one," after a demon **Trisiras (Triśiras)** who performed severe **tapas** to Śiva on this rock and won his boon here — hence the sanctity of the hill. (Sthala-purāṇa) [disputed][regional]
  - *Variant (Tamil etymology) [scholarly]:* philologists more cautiously read the name from Tamil elements (e.g. *tiru-ciṟu-paḷḷi*, "holy little town/shrine," or an older *Cirā-paḷḷi*), the "three-headed demon" being a later back-formation onto a pre-existing place-name. The derivation is **genuinely contested** and should not be stated as settled.
- **A Śaiva, not Rāvaṇa's, Triśiras** — this Triśiras is a **local Śaiva penitent-asura** of the sthala-purāṇa and should **not** be conflated with the *Rāmāyaṇa* Triśiras (a rākṣasa general in Khara's army slain by Rāma in the Daṇḍaka forest), a distinct character. (Disambiguation) [scholarly]

## V. The Tēvāram Layer — a Pāḍal Petṟa Sthalam

- **Sung by the Nāyanmār** — Tiruchirāppaḷḷi (Sirappaḷḷi) is counted among the **275 [Pāḍal Petṟa Sthalams](275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md)**, the Śiva-shrines glorified in the Tamil **Tēvāram**; hymns to the Rock Fort Lord are ascribed to **Tiru-jñāna-Sambandar** and **Appar (Tirunāvukkaracar)**, anchoring the cult in the 7th-century Tamil Śaiva-bhakti canon. (Tēvāram, Tirumuṟai 1–7) [scholarly]
- **Part of the Tamil Śaiva map** — the shrine belongs to the **Cōḻa-nāḍu** cluster of the Tēvāram temples and to the wider [Tamil bhakti canon](../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md); its inclusion is the earliest firm literary witness to the deity's worship. (Tēvāram; canonical geography) [scholarly]

## VI. Two Clocks — Legend vs History

- **Scriptural clock** — the **Thāyumānavar midwife-grace** is a Śaiva *bhakti* miracle of no fixed date, told in **māhātmya time**; the **Ucchi Piḷḷaiyār** episode is pinned to the **[Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/README.md) afterlife** (Vibhīṣaṇa the *cira-jīvī* bearing the vimāna south after the Laṅkā war), placing it at the **Tretā–Kali hinge**, exactly where [Śrīraṅgam](srirangam-ranganatha.md) sits; the **Triśiras penance** is placed in undatable asura-*tapas* time. None of these fall on any calendar. [regional]
- **Academic clock** — the Rock Fort is a mass of **very ancient Archaean granite/charnockite** (~2.5–3.8 Ga). Its worship is attested from the **Pallava period**: the hill carries **rock-cut cave-temples of Mahendravarman I (early 7th c. CE)** with important inscriptions, is praised in the **7th-c. Tēvāram**, elaborated under **Cōḻa, Pāṇḍya, Nāyaka** patronage, and the fortress itself was raised by the **Madurai Nāyakas (16th–17th c.)**, later contested in the **Carnatic Wars (18th c.)**. [scholarly]
- **The saint who took the deity's name** — the Śaiva-Siddhānta poet-philosopher **Tāyumāṉavar (c. 1705–1742 CE)**, author of a celebrated body of Tamil devotional-philosophical verse and once a court official under the Nāyaka administration at Trichy, is **named after this Rock Fort deity** — a distinct historical person who must not be confused with the god Thāyumānavar himself. (Literary history) [scholarly]
