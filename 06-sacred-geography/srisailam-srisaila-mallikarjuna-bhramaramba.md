# Srisailam — Śrīśaila, the Dakṣiṇa Kailāsa

> Sacred geography of the Goddess *and* the Lord at one summit: **Śrīśaila** ("the auspicious hill") on the **Nallamala** range above the **Kṛṣṇā** river is the rare *kṣetra* that is **simultaneously a [Jyotirliṅga](12-jyotirlingas.md) (Mallikārjuna) and a [Śakti Pīṭha](51-shakti-peethas.md) (Bhramarāmbā)** — Śiva and Śakti enshrined on the same rock. Its origin myth belongs to the deep cross-yuga past (Skanda-Purāṇa *Śrīśaila-khaṇḍa*); its standing temple to the historical centuries — the **two clocks** held apart below.
>

**Śrīśaila / Śrīparvata** is a forested mountain in the **Nallamala** hills of Andhra Pradesh, ringed by the **Kṛṣṇā** as it cuts a gorge through the range. In the sacred imagination it is the **Dakṣiṇa Kailāsa** — the "Kailāsa of the South" — a southern double of [Śiva's northern mountain](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md) where the divine couple actually *dwells*, not merely visits. The hill carries an unusual doubling: the **Mallikārjuna** liṅga (Śiva) and the **Bhramarāmbā** shrine (the Goddess) sit on the same plateau, fusing the [Śaiva jyotirliṅga circuit](12-jyotirlingas.md) and the [Śākta pīṭha network](51-shakti-peethas.md) at a single point. [scholarly]

## I. Śrīśaila — the Hill and Its Geography

- **Śrīśaila / Śrīparvata** — "the blessed hill," on the **Nallamala** ("black hills") spur of the Eastern Ghats; the **Kṛṣṇā** river horseshoes around its base, the *kṣetra* sitting on the south bank of the gorge. The river-bank *tīrtha* below the hill is **Pātāḷa-gaṅgā**, descended to by a long flight of steps for ritual bathing. (Skanda-Purāṇa, *Śrīśaila-khaṇḍa*; sthala-purāṇa.) [scholarly]
- **Dakṣiṇa Kailāsa — "the Kailāsa of the South"** — the hill's master-title: where [Mount Kailāsa](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md) is Śiva's transcendent northern seat, Śrīśaila is his *abiding* southern residence, so that pilgrimage here is held to equal the inaccessible northern yātrā. The two are read as poles of one axis. (Śrīśaila-khaṇḍa.) [regional]
- **The Śrīśaila-khaṇḍa** — a major *khaṇḍa* (section) of the **Skanda Purāṇa** is devoted wholly to this hill: its *māhātmya* (glorification), its *tīrthas*, the Mallikārjuna and Bhramarāmbā legends, and the merit of *darśana*. It is the single richest textual charter for the site. (Skanda-Purāṇa, *Śrīśaila-khaṇḍa*.) [scriptural]
- **A Mahābhārata ford** — the **Tīrtha-yātrā-parvan** of the *Mahābhārata*, cataloguing the holy fords of Bhārata, knows **Śrīparvata** as a southern *tīrtha* sacred to Śiva — an early, pre-temple witness to the hill's sanctity. (Mahābhārata, Vana-parvan, *Tīrtha-yātrā*.) [scholarly]

## II. Mallikārjuna — the Jyotirliṅga (cross-yuga)

- **Second of the twelve** — In the *Dvādaśa Jyotirliṅga Stotra* (attrib. Ādi Śaṅkara) Mallikārjuna stands **second**, after Somnāth: *"Śrīśaile Mallikārjunam."* The liṅga is one of the twelve supreme *svayambhu* light-pillars of [the jyotirliṅga roster](12-jyotirlingas.md). (Śiva Purāṇa, Koṭi-Rudra Saṃhitā; *Dvādaśa Jyotirliṅga Stotra*.) [scriptural]
- **The Kārttikeya / Kumāra quarrel** — the founding legend: the boy **Kārttikeya (Skanda / Kumāra)**, slighted in the contest with **Gaṇeśa** over marriage-precedence (Gaṇeśa winning the bride-prize by circumambulating his parents), departs in pique from Kailāsa and settles on a southern hill (**Krauñca / Śrīśaila**) as an ascetic. (Skanda-Purāṇa, *Śrīśaila-khaṇḍa*; Śiva Purāṇa.)
- **The parents follow their son** — grieving the absence of their child, **Śiva and Pārvatī follow Kumāra** to the hill to remain near him, and there manifest as the abiding liṅga — the parental love that anchors the Lord to Śrīśaila. (Śrīśaila-khaṇḍa; Śiva Purāṇa.)
- **Mallikā + Arjuna = Mallikārjuna** — the etymology of the liṅga's name: **Mallikā** ("jasmine," the flower Pārvatī offered / her form) joined to **Arjuna** (a name of Śiva, also the white *arjuna* tree); the compound enshrines Goddess-and-God in one word. The liṅga is worshipped with jasmine and bilva. (Sthala-purāṇa; Śrīśaila-khaṇḍa.) [regional]
  - *Variant (Candravatī / the herder-cow — sthala-purāṇa):* a local recension tells of a princess **Candravatī** who, retreating to the hill, sees a *kapila* cow daily emptying its milk over an anthill; digging, she finds the self-manifest liṅga, which she worships under jasmine creepers — hence *Mallika-arjuna*. [folk / regional]

## III. Bhramarāmbā — the Śakti Pīṭha (cross-yuga)

- **The bee-goddess** — beside the liṅga stands **Bhramarāmbā** ("the Mother of bees," *bhramara* = bee), one of the principal forms of the Goddess and a node of [the Śakti-pīṭha network](51-shakti-peethas.md). The dual enshrinement makes Śrīśaila one of the very few sites *both* jyotirliṅga *and* pīṭha. (Skanda-Purāṇa, *Śrīśaila-khaṇḍa*; pīṭha lists.) [scholarly]
- **The fallen body-part** — as a *śakti pīṭha*, the site marks where a fragment of **[Satī's](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)** dismembered body fell during [Śiva's grief-wandering](51-shakti-peethas.md). The texts disagree on which part:
  - *Variant (Aṣṭādaśa Pīṭha Stotram, attrib. Ādi Śaṅkara):* Śrīśaila is named among the **eighteen Mahā Śakti Pīṭhas** — *"Śrīśaile Bhramarāmbikā"* — the most-recited devotional list, without specifying the *aṅga*. [scriptural]
  - *Variant (Tantra Cūḍāmaṇi / Pīṭhanirṇaya):* the fallen part is the **right anklet (nūpura)**; the resident Śakti **Bhramarāmbā (Śrīsundarī)** and her Bhairava **Sundarānanda**. [tantra]
  - *Variant (folk / regional):* a popular telling assigns the **neck / upper lip** of Satī to Śrīśaila — a placement not concordant with the *Pīṭhanirṇaya* anklet-tradition; the *aṅga* of Śrīśaila is among the **disputed** pīṭha attributions. [folk][disputed]
- **The bee-demon etiology** — a *māhātmya* tale grounds the name: the Goddess, to slay the demon **Aruṇāsura** (or a *mahiṣa*-class asura) immune to ordinary weapons, generates a swarm of black **bees (bhramara)** from her body that destroy him — whence **Bhramarāmbā**, she who fought as bees; the humming of bees is heard as her presence. (Devī-māhātmya / Śākta lore; sthala tradition.) [regional]
- **Coupled with Mallikārjuna** — uniquely, the Goddess here is not a distant consort but shares the plateau with the liṅga, so the pilgrim takes the *darśana* of **Śiva-and-Śakti together** — the doctrinal fusion the hill is famous for. [scholarly]

## IV. Devotional & Sectarian Layers

- **Ādi Śaṅkara and the Śivānanda-laharī** — tradition holds that **Ādi Śaṅkara** visited Śrīśaila and composed (or completed) his **Śivānanda-laharī** ("wave of the bliss of Śiva"), a hundred-verse hymn of Śaiva *bhakti*, before the Mallikārjuna liṅga; the **Śrīśaila** sojourn is woven into his hagiography (the *Śaṅkara-vijaya* lives), including a brush with a Kāpālika who sought his head. (Śaṅkara-vijaya tradition; Śivānanda-laharī colophon-lore.) [regional][disputed]
- **Vīraśaiva / Liṅgāyata associations** — Śrīśaila is a cardinal seat of the **Vīraśaiva–Liṅgāyata** tradition; the 12th-c. *śaraṇa* poets sang it, and the hill is bound to the **Pañcapīṭha / pañca-ācārya** Vīraśaiva monastic geography, with the *jaṅgama* devotion centered on the Mallikārjuna liṅga. (Vīraśaiva *vacana* and maṭha tradition.) [regional]
- **Resort of Siddhas and Rasa-siddhas** — as **Śrīparvata**, the hill is celebrated across Tantric, Nātha, and Buddhist-Vajrayāna lore as a haunt of **Siddhas, alchemists (rasa-siddhas), and yoginīs**; **Nāgārjuna** the alchemist is traditionally linked to the Śrīparvata region (nearby **Nāgārjunakoṇḍa**). (Tantric / Siddha tradition.) [scholarly]

## V. The Two Clocks

- **Scriptural clock (cross-yuga):** the *manifestation* of Mallikārjuna and the fall of Satī's *aṅga* belong to the deep mythic past — the Liṅgodbhava/jyotirliṅga age and the [Dakṣa-yajña dismemberment](51-shakti-peethas.md) cycle — *before* structured historical time; the **Skanda-Purāṇa** *Śrīśaila-khaṇḍa* presents them as eternally-present realities of the hill. [scriptural]
- **Historical clock:** the *attested* cult runs through the **Sātavāhana–Ikṣvāku** era of nearby Nāgārjunakoṇḍa (early centuries CE), with the standing temple complex elaborated under the **Reḍḍi, Kākatīya, and Vijayanagara** dynasties (13th–16th c.) — the great *prākāra* wall, *gopuras*, and sculpted galleries belong to these centuries. The mountain bridges both clocks. [scholarly]

## Sources

- **Skanda Purāṇa, *Śrīśaila-khaṇḍa*** — the foundational *māhātmya*: the Dakṣiṇa-Kailāsa identity, the Kumāra-quarrel origin of Mallikārjuna, the *Mallikā+Arjuna* etymology, Bhramarāmbā, the *tīrthas* and Pātāḷa-gaṅgā [scriptural]
- **Śiva Purāṇa** (Koṭi-Rudra / Śatarudra Saṃhitā) — Mallikārjuna as the second of the twelve [jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md); Śiva-Pārvatī following Kārttikeya to the hill [scriptural]
- **Dvādaśa Jyotirliṅga Stotra** (attrib. Ādi Śaṅkara) — *"Śrīśaile Mallikārjunam,"* fixing the liṅga in the canonical twelve [scriptural]
- **Aṣṭādaśa Śakti Pīṭha Stotram** (attrib. Ādi Śaṅkara) — *"Śrīśaile Bhramarāmbikā,"* Śrīśaila among the eighteen Mahā Pīṭhas; **Tantra Cūḍāmaṇi / Pīṭhanirṇaya** for the right-anklet *aṅga*, Śakti Śrīsundarī and Bhairava Sundarānanda [scriptural][tantra]
- **Mahābhārata, Vana-parvan (Tīrtha-yātrā-parvan)** — Śrīparvata as a southern Śaiva *tīrtha*, an early pre-temple witness [scholarly]
- **Śivānanda-laharī** and **Śaṅkara-vijaya** hagiographies — Ādi Śaṅkara's Śrīśaila sojourn and hymn [regional][disputed]
- **Vīraśaiva–Liṅgāyata vacana & maṭha tradition; Siddha / Tantric lore** — Śrīśaila as a Vīraśaiva seat and a Śrīparvata resort of Siddhas and rasa-siddhas [regional]
