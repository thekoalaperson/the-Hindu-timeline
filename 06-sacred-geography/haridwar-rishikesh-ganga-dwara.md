# Haridwar & Rishikesh — Gaṅgā-dvāra, Gateway to the Char Dhām

> Section: [Sacred Geography](./README.md) · Master index: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md)
> The twin Gaṅgā-gateway towns of the upper Doab: **Haridwar** (Māyāpurī / Gaṅgā-dvāra), one of the seven *mokṣa-purī* and a Kumbh city where *amṛta* fell; and **Rishikesh**, threshold of the Himalayan *yātrā* to the Chota Char Dhām.

## The City of Many Names

- **Gaṅgā-dvāra ("Gate of the Ganges")** - the oldest descriptive name: the Gaṅgā, after ~253 km from Gaumukh at the Gangotri glacier, debouches from the Himalaya onto the Gangetic plain (the Doab) for the first time here, hence the "gateway/door" by which the goddess enters the world of men. The *Mahābhārata* (Vana Parva / Tīrtha-yātrā section) names "the spot where Gaṅgā rusheth past" as **Gaṅgādvāra**, a great *tīrtha*, with adjacent Kankhal equally sacred (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva; Wikipedia, Haridwar).
- **Māyāpurī ("City of Māyā")** - civic/Puranic name honoring the goddess **Māyā Devī**, whose shrine here is the city's tutelary Śakti seat; the name persisted as an alternative official designation into modern times (Wikipedia, Maya Devi Temple, Haridwar).
- **Kapila-sthāna / Kapilā** - named for sage **Kapila**, whose *āśrama* tradition is localized here; ties the town into the Bhagīratha/Sagara cycle (Kapila's wrath burned Sagara's 60,000 sons, prompting the descent of Gaṅgā) (Wikipedia, Haridwar).
- **Mokṣa-dvāra / Mokṣa-purī** - "doorway of liberation," marking Haridwar's status as a *mukti*-conferring city (Wikipedia, Haridwar).

- **Hari-dvāra vs Hara-dvāra (the sectarian variant)** - the modern name is read two ways depending on lineage. [disputed]
  - *Variant — Hari-dvāra (Vaiṣṇava):* "Gateway to **Hari** (Viṣṇu)" — the road up to **Badrinath** (Viṣṇu's abode); Vaiṣṇavas spell and pronounce it *Haridwar* (Wikipedia, Haridwar; Har Ki Pauri).
  - *Variant — Hara-dvāra (Śaiva):* "Gateway to **Hara** (Śiva)" — the road up to **Kedarnath** (Śiva's abode); Śaivas spell and say *Hardwar*. Both routes begin from this single trailhead, so both readings are traditionally accepted (Wikipedia, Haridwar).

## Har-ki-Pauri & the Brahmakund (the *amṛta*-fall site)

- **Har-ki-Pauri ("Footsteps of Hari/Hara")** - the principal bathing *ghāṭ* on the Gaṅgā, the ritual heart of Haridwar; the name is itself read as "the feet of the Lord," because a divine footprint is venerated here (Wikipedia, Har Ki Pauri).
  - *Variant (Vaiṣṇava):* the name preserves **Viṣṇu's footprint** — Viṣṇu is said to have appeared here in the Vedic age and left his footprints in the stone set in the upper wall of the *ghāṭ*, kept perpetually touched by the Gaṅgā (Wikipedia, Har Ki Pauri).
  - *Variant (Śaiva):* read as the footstep of **Hara (Śiva)**, consistent with the Hara-dvāra etymology.
- **Brahmakund** - the most sacred precinct within Har-ki-Pauri, the pool where the evening *ārtī* is performed; tradition holds this is the exact spot where a drop of **amṛta** fell from the celestial *kumbha* during the Samudra Manthana, making it the holiest bathing point of the *ghāṭ* (Wikipedia, Har Ki Pauri; Kumbh sources).
- **Mode of the spill** - sources differ on the carrier of the pitcher.
  - *Variant (common):* **Garuḍa**, Viṣṇu's mount, carried the *kumbha* and let drops fall at four spots (Wikipedia, Har Ki Pauri).
  - *Variant:* the carrier was **Jayanta** (Indra's son) / the **Moon (Soma)** guarding the pot, the pursuit lasting twelve divine days = twelve human years (basis of the Kumbh's 12-year cycle) (Kumbh sources). [variants]

## The Samudra Manthana Link → A Kumbh City

- **Why Haridwar is a Kumbh site** - in the Purāṇic account, after the **Churning of the Ocean of Milk** the pot (*kumbha*) of *amṛta* was seized to keep it from the *asuras*; in flight, drops spilled at **four** earthly sites — **Haridwar, Prayāg (Allahabad), Ujjain, and Nashik** — which became the four Kumbh Mela cities (Wikipedia, Haridwar Kumbh Mela; Kumbh sources). See [Kumbh Mela & Pilgrimage Cycles](./kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md).
- **Haridwar's astronomical trigger** - the Haridwar Kumbh falls when **Jupiter (Bṛhaspati) is in Aquarius (Kumbha rāśi)** and the **Sun enters Aries (Meṣa)**; the river-bath at Har-ki-Pauri during this window is held to confer the *amṛta*-equivalent of immortality/liberation (Wikipedia, Haridwar Kumbh Mela).
- **Cycle** - a **Pūrṇa (full) Kumbh** every 12 years at Haridwar, with an **Ardh (half) Kumbh** at the 6-year midpoint (shared with Prayāg). Major modern melas: 1998, 2010, 2021 (Pūrṇa) (Wikipedia, Haridwar Kumbh Mela).
- **Theological reading** - the Manthana churned *amṛta* once; the Kumbh re-actualizes it, the Gaṅgā at Brahmakund standing in for the elixir itself — drinking/bathing = partaking of immortality. [folk-theological]

## Māyā Devī — the Śakti Pīṭha that Named the City

- **Māyā Devī Temple** - tutelary goddess of Haridwar, three-headed and four-armed, an *avatāra* of Śakti/Ādi-Māyā; her presence gives the city the name **Māyāpurī**. The shrine groups idols of Māyā with **Kālī** and **Kāmākhyā** and two further forms of the Goddess. Reckoned an 11th-century structure and one of Haridwar's three surviving ancient temples (with Nārāyaṇa-śilā and the Bhairava temple) (Wikipedia, Maya Devi Temple).
- **Body-part of Satī** - in the Śakti Pīṭha geography Māyāpurī is the site where Satī's **heart and navel** fell (some accounts say *nābhi*/navel) when Viṣṇu dismembered her corpse to end Śiva's grief-dance; this anchors Haridwar among the great *pīṭhas*. See [51 Shakti Pīṭhas](./51-shakti-peethas.md).
  - *Variant (local tradition):* the temple is called "the source of all Śakti Pīṭhas," a *māhātmya* claim of primacy rather than a standard list entry (tirthayatra.org; tourmyindia). [folk]
- **Satī's self-immolation locale** - one local telling places Satī's yogic self-immolation not (only) at Daksha's *yajña* at Kankhal but at Māyāpurī itself, where she sat in *dhyāna* and consumed her body by inner fire (Wikipedia / temple tradition). Linked to the **Daksha Mahādev Temple, Kankhal** (3 km away), site of Daksha's spoiled sacrifice — see [Sthala-Purāṇas of Major Temples](./sthala-puranas-major-temples.md).

### Chaṇḍī Devī & Mansā Devī — the Flanking-Hill Siddha Pīṭhas

- **Triad of Pīṭhas** - Haridwar is ringed by **three** goddess seats forming a *Siddha Pīṭha* triangle: **Māyā Devī** (in town), **Chaṇḍī Devī** (east hill), **Mansā Devī** (west hill) (Wikipedia, Maya Devi Temple).
- **Chaṇḍī Devī** - on **Nīl Parvat** across the Gaṅgā; the goddess **Chaṇḍī/Chaṇḍikā** who, in the *Devī Māhātmya* cycle, slew the *asuras* **Śumbha–Niśumbha** (and their generals **Chaṇḍa–Muṇḍa**); the main image is said to be installed by **Ādi Śaṅkarācārya** (8th c.). A **Siddha Pīṭha** — *darśana* "fulfills wishes." (tourmyindia; Wikipedia.)
- **Mansā Devī** - on **Bilva Parvat (Bilwa/Shivalik hill)**, the wish-fulfilling goddess **Mansā** ("the mind/desire," a form of Śakti, sister of the serpent-king Vāsuki in some lore); pilgrims tie threads to a temple tree for boons. Also a **Siddha Pīṭha**, reached by ropeway (Wikipedia; tourmyindia).
- *Note:* "Siddha Pīṭha" here = a seat where austerities/vows bear sure fruit; distinct from the 51 *Śakti Pīṭhas* body-part schema, though Māyā Devī belongs to both. [terminological]

## The Genealogy-Register (Panda Bahī) Tradition

- **Pandas as hereditary genealogists** - at Har-ki-Pauri, Brahmin priests called **paṇḍās** maintain handwritten family registers — **bahī** (also *vahī* / *pothī*) — recording each pilgrim family's lineage; a family's records are held by a specific *paṇḍā* lineage by region/clan (Wikipedia, Hindu genealogy registers at Haridwar).
- **What is recorded** - entries are made when descendants come to **immerse a relative's ashes** or take a ritual dip; the pilgrim writes/signs the family's *bahī*, updating births, deaths, and marriages, often signed by multiple kin as witnesses (Wikipedia, Bahi genealogy registers).
- **Depth** - many registers trace family histories **20+ generations**, making Haridwar the most comprehensive surviving repository of Hindu genealogical records; the *bahīs* have been admitted as **evidence in inheritance/property litigation** (Wikipedia, Hindu genealogy registers at Haridwar). [scholarly]
- **Function** - links the living to ancestors (*pitṛ*) the way [Gaya](./gaya-pitru-tirtha.md) and [Prayāg](./prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md) do; Haridwar's *asthi-visarjana* (ash-immersion) and *piṇḍa-dāna* serve the *pitṛ-yajña*.

## Rishikesh — Threshold of the Himalayan Yātrā

- **Ṛṣikeśa ("Lord of the Senses / of the ṛṣis")** - ~25 km upstream of Haridwar where the Gaṅgā runs in a Himalayan gorge; the name is a title of **Viṣṇu** (Hṛṣīkeśa, "master of the senses"). Functionally the **gateway to the Garhwal Himalaya** and the **starting point of the Chota Char Dhām yātrā** (Wikipedia / tourism sources).
- **Chota Char Dhām trailhead** - the Himalayan *yātrā* to **Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath, Badrinath** is traditionally begun from Rishikesh/Haridwar; see [Char Dhām & Chota Char Dhām](./char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) and [Pancha Kedar / Pancha Badri](./pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md).
- **The four ancient temples of Rishikesh** - tradition counts **Bharat Mandir, Raghunath Temple, Lakshmaṇa Temple, Śatrughna Temple** — the city as a Rāma-family shrine cluster (tirthayatra.org). [open-ended roster — sources vary on exact four]

### Bharat Mandir

- **Bharat Mandir** - the oldest/central temple of Rishikesh, dedicated to **Hṛṣīkeśa Nārāyaṇa (Viṣṇu)**; its re-consecration is traditionally credited to **Ādi Śaṅkarācārya** (c. 789 CE, Basant Panchami). Glorified in the **Kedar-Khaṇḍa of the Skanda Purāṇa** (Skanda Purāṇa, Kedar-Khaṇḍa; tirthayatra.org).

### The Ṛṣi-Tapas Legends

- **Raibhya Ṛṣi (the name-giving sage)** - **Raibhya** performed severe *tapasyā* on the Gaṅgā bank here to please **Viṣṇu**; pleased, **Nārāyaṇa** appeared to him seated on the branch of a **mango tree** and boon: He would remain here as **Hṛṣīkeśa Nārāyaṇa**, and the place would bear that name — hence **Rishikesh** (tirthayatra.org; Skanda Purāṇa tradition).
- **Raghunāth (Rāma) link** - the **Raghunath Temple** near **Triveṇī Ghāṭ** honors **Rāma and Sītā**; folk tradition ties Rishikesh to **Rāma's penance** for slaying Rāvaṇa (a *brahmahatyā* expiation), with **Lakṣmaṇa** crossing the Gaṅgā here on a jute-rope bridge — the legend behind **Lakṣmaṇ Jhūlā** (tourism sources; local *māhātmya*). [folk]
- **Triveṇī Ghāṭ** - principal Rishikesh *ghāṭ*, named for the confluence-symbolism of Gaṅgā–Yamunā–Sarasvatī; site of the evening **Gaṅgā Ārtī** and *piṇḍa-dāna*.

## The Gaṅgā Ārtī

- **Sandhyā Ārtī at Har-ki-Pauri / Brahmakund** - the signature evening rite: lamps, conch, bells and the hymn to **Gaṅgā Mātā** offered at dusk as floating *dīpa* (leaf-boats of flowers and flame) are set on the current; the most attended *ārtī* on the upper Gaṅgā, drawing pilgrims daily and in vast numbers at Kumbh (Wikipedia, Har Ki Pauri; tourism sources).
- **Rishikesh counterpart** - a parallel **Gaṅgā Ārtī at Triveṇī Ghāṭ** (and at Parmarth Niketan), more ashram-led/musical in character. [contemporary practice]

## Why Haridwar Ranks Among the Mokṣa-Purīs

- **Sapta Purī membership** - the **Garuḍa Purāṇa** lists the seven liberation-cities as **Ayodhyā, Mathurā, Māyā (=Haridwar), Kāśī, Kāñcī, Avantikā (Ujjain), Dvārāvatī (Dwarka)** — "Māyā" here = Māyāpurī/Haridwar. See [Sapta Purī & Sacred Cities](./sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md) (Garuḍa Purāṇa; Wikipedia, Haridwar).
- **Confluence of cult-streams** - Haridwar uniquely binds **Vaiṣṇava** (Viṣṇu's footprint, Hari-dvāra → Badrinath), **Śaiva** (Hara-dvāra, Daksha-Kankhal → Kedarnath), and **Śākta** (the three Pīṭhas) currents at one *tīrtha* — a microcosm of pan-Hindu pilgrimage.

## Two Clocks — Scripture vs. Academic Dating

- **Scriptural / mythic clock** - the *amṛta*-fall belongs to the Samudra Manthana, a primordial event of an early Manvantara (current = 7th, Vaivasvata; 28th Mahāyuga); the Gaṅgā's descent (Bhagīratha) is set in **Tretā/Dvāpara** ancestral time; Viṣṇu's footprint at Har-ki-Pauri is placed in the "Vedic age."
- **Academic / historical clock** - extant temples (Māyā Devī, Bharat Mandir) are **early-medieval (8th–11th c. CE)**; Śaṅkarācārya consecrations are **8th–9th c. CE**; the Kumbh as a fixed 12-year astronomical institution is attested in the **medieval–early-modern** record, with the European description by Thomas Hardwicke in **1796**. [scholarly]
