# Sākṣi-Gopāla — the Witness Gopāla who Walked from Vṛndāvana

> A **Kṛṣṇa-kṣetra** in the Purī district of Odisha (the town of **Sākṣigopāl / Satyabāḍī**, c. 20 km from Purī on the Purī–Bhubaneswar road) — where the Vṛndāvana **Gopāla** Deity is said to have **walked south behind a young brāhmaṇa** to testify in court to a marriage-promise, becoming fixed at the very spot where the boy looked back. A signature Gauḍīya narrative and a fixed halt on the Jagannātha-Purī pilgrimage.
>

Sākṣi-Gopāla is one of the rare Vaiṣṇava kṣetras whose central miracle is a **legal deposition** — a mūrti bearing witness in a human assembly. The story's canonical home is the **Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya-līlā, chapter 5** ("The Activities of Sākṣi-gopāla"), where **Nityānanda Prabhu** narrates it to the Bengali devotees travelling to Purī, at the temple itself. Two clocks run through it: the **scriptural clock** of the Vṛndāvana Gopāla (an ancient self-manifest Kṛṣṇa image, the walk undatable on any calendar), and the **historical clock** of the Gajapati king **Puruṣottama Deva** (r. c. 1467–1497 CE), whose southern campaign is said to have carried the Deity to Odisha. The two are kept distinct below.

---

## I. The two brāhmaṇas and the Vṛndāvana pilgrimage

- **The elder and the younger** — in the city of **Vidyānagara** (in the south) lived two brāhmaṇas, one **old and wealthy**, one **young and poor**, who set out together on a long pilgrimage of the holy places (*tīrtha-yātrā*). No personal names are given in the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*; they are simply the *vṛddha* and the *yuvā* brāhmaṇa. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5)
- **Faithful service** — throughout the journey the **young brāhmaṇa served the old one** as a servant serves a master — carrying his baggage, cooking, tending him — with such devotion that the elder was deeply moved. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5)
- **Reaching Vṛndāvana** — after visiting Gayā, Kāśī, Prayāga and other tīrthas, the pair came at last to **Mathurā and Vṛndāvana**, to the temple of **Gopāla** — Kṛṣṇa in his cowherd-boy form. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5; cf. [Mathurā–Vṛndāvana–Braj](mathura-vrindavan-braj.md))
- **The promise before Gopāla** — overwhelmed by the young man's service, the old brāhmaṇa **vowed, standing before the Gopāla Deity, to give his daughter in marriage to the youth** — making Gopāla himself the **witness (sākṣī)** to the pledge. The young man protested that the gulf in their wealth and family standing made it impossible; the elder insisted, invoking the Deity as guarantor. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5)

---

## II. The broken word and the assembly

- **Homecoming and objection** — back in Vidyānagara the old brāhmaṇa's **kinsmen — above all his own son — refused the match**, unwilling to marry a daughter of a rich house to a poor and lowly-connected boy. Under family pressure the elder grew silent and evasive, and at last began to deny the promise. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5)
- **"Gopāla is my witness"** — the young brāhmaṇa held firm: the vow had been made **before Gopāla in Vṛndāvana**, and could not be revoked. The dispute was carried to a **public assembly (sabhā)** of learned men. When the youth declared that the Deity himself would come to testify, the assembly **laughed** — how could a carved image walk and speak? (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5)
  - *Variant (the elder's guilt):* in the telling, the old brāhmaṇa is not painted as simply wicked but as **torn** — inwardly certain the vow was true, outwardly bent by his son and relatives; some devotional expansions stress that he secretly hoped the youth would win. [folk]
- **The challenge accepted** — the assembly agreed that **if Gopāla came and bore witness, the marriage would stand**. The young brāhmaṇa undertook to fetch the Lord himself, and set out again — alone — on the long road north to Vṛndāvana. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5)

## III. The walking Deity and the look-back

- **The petition to Gopāla** — reaching Vṛndāvana, the youth stood before Gopāla and **begged the Deity to come south and speak the truth** in the assembly. Gopāla answered that **a Deity does not walk** — such a thing had never been heard. The brāhmaṇa pleaded that his word, and the honour of the promise, hung on it. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5)
- **Gopāla's condition** — moved by the devotee, **Gopāla consented to follow** — but on one condition: the brāhmaṇa **must walk ahead and never look back**. So long as he could hear the **tinkling of the Deity's ankle-bells (nūpura)**, he would know Gopāla was behind him; the Lord would also require only **cooked rice offered daily** as he travelled. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5)
  - *Variant (the daily offering):* devotional retellings specify that each day the brāhmaṇa cooked and offered rice, which the invisible Gopāla consumed — a proof, along with the bells, that the Deity truly walked behind. [folk]
- **The long road south** — day after day the young brāhmaṇa walked toward Vidyānagara, **the ankle-bells sounding steadily at his back**, never once turning to look. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5)
- **The look-back that roots the Deity** — on reaching the outskirts of Vidyānagara, the brāhmaṇa went ahead to the assembly and announced that **Gopāla had come**. To make the sceptics believe, he **turned to point the Deity out — and looked back**. At that instant **Gopāla halted and became fixed, immovable, at the very spot**, unable to proceed further. The condition broken, the walking stopped — and the place of the look-back became the Deity's seat. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5)
- **The testimony** — though now stationary, **Gopāla spoke, confirming before the whole assembly** that the old brāhmaṇa had indeed vowed his daughter to the youth. The assembly, awestruck, upheld the promise; **the marriage was performed**, and a **temple was raised over the spot** where the Witness-Gopāla stood. The old brāhmaṇa's son, chastened, became a devotee. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5)
  - *Variant (the son's turn):* some expansions add that the erring son fell at the Deity's feet and that both brāhmaṇas thereafter served Gopāla together at the new shrine. [folk]
- **The name Sākṣi-Gopāla** — from this deposition the Deity took his abiding title: **Sākṣi-Gopāla**, "Gopāla the Witness" (*sākṣī* = witness) — Kṛṣṇa who left Vṛndāvana to keep a devotee's word. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5)

## IV. Puruṣottama Deva and the journey to Odisha

The second half of *Madhya-līlā* 5 carries the Deity from the south into Odisha, and joins the Sākṣi-Gopāla legend to the Gajapati house of Purī.

- **The Gajapati and the southern king** — **Puruṣottama Deva**, king of Odisha (Utkala), sought the hand of the **daughter of the king of Vidyānagara**. But when the southern king saw Puruṣottama **sweeping the road before Jagannātha's chariot** during the Ratha-yātrā (the *Cherā Paṅharā* rite of ritual humility), he scorned him as a **"sweeper" (menial)** and broke off the betrothal. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5)
- **War and divine aid** — insulted, Puruṣottama marched south, was **first defeated**, then prayed to **Jagannātha and Balabhadra**, who are said to have ridden out as **two warriors** and turned the battle; Puruṣottama **conquered Vidyānagara**. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5) [folk]
  - *Variant (the revenge-marriage):* in the well-known twist, Puruṣottama ordered his minister to give the captured princess in marriage **to an actual sweeper** as retaliation; the minister instead judged her worthy and **married her to the king himself** during the next Ratha-yātrā, when the Gajapati was again "a sweeper" before the Lord. [folk]
- **The Deity carried north** — during or after this campaign the **Sākṣi-Gopāla Deity was brought from Vidyānagara to Odisha** by Puruṣottama Deva and installed at his capital, **Kaṭaka (Cuttack)**. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5)
  - *Variant (later relocation):* temple tradition holds that a **later Gajapati** moved the Deity from Cuttack to his present seat at **Sākṣigopāl / Satyabāḍī near Purī**, where he now stands on the pilgrim road to Jagannātha; the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* records the Cuttack installation, the relocation being a later, sthala-tradition layer. [regional]
- **Nityānanda's narration** — Nityānanda Prabhu recounts the entire history to the Bengali devotees **at the Sākṣi-Gopāla temple itself**, as they journey toward Purī and Caitanya Mahāprabhu; the retelling is the reason the tale became a **signature Gauḍīya narrative**. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5)

## V. Two clocks — legend and history

- **Scriptural clock** — the **Vṛndāvana Gopāla** is an ancient, self-manifest cowherd Kṛṣṇa; the promise, the walk, the bells and the look-back sit in **undatable devotional time**, with no calendar anchor. The tale is theological before it is chronological: its point is that Kṛṣṇa **subordinates his own Deity-nature to a devotee's honour**. [folk]
- **Academic clock** — **Puruṣottama Deva** is a firmly historical **Sūryavaṃśī Gajapati** of Odisha, r. **c. 1467–1497 CE**, whose **southern campaigns** (against Kāñcī and the Vijayanagara frontier) are attested; the transfer of a Deity from the conquered south to Odisha is thus dated to the **late 15th century CE**. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (identifying Vidyānagara):* "Vidyānagara" of the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* is read by some scholars as **Vijayanagara** (or a town within its orbit), consistent with Puruṣottama's Kāñcī war; the identification is **not certain**. [disputed] [scholarly]
- **Text date** — the **Caitanya-caritāmṛta** was composed by **Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja** c. **1600 CE**, roughly a century after Caitanya (1486–1534 CE) and Puruṣottama Deva — a late-16th/early-17th-century Gauḍīya redaction of an already-current Odia legend. [scholarly]

## VI. Cult, pilgrimage and significance

- **A fixed halt on the Purī yātrā** — the **Sākṣigopāl temple** stands on the direct road between Bhubaneswar/Cuttack and **Jagannātha-Purī**, and darśana of the Witness-Gopāla is a **customary stop** for pilgrims approaching the *dhāma* — the walking Kṛṣṇa greeting those who come to the fixed Lord of the Universe. (temple tradition; cf. [Jagannāth Purī](jagannath-puri-purushottama-kshetra.md)) [regional]
- **Guarantor of promises** — because Gopāla here **kept a human vow**, the Deity is invoked as a **witness and guarantor of oaths and agreements**; devotees bring disputes and pledges to him, and the town-name **Satyabāḍī** ("abode of truth") carries the same sense. [regional]
- **Gauḍīya theology** — for the **Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava** tradition the episode is a favourite proof of *bhakta-vātsalya* — the Lord's tenderness toward his devotees, going so far as to **walk out of his own temple** to defend a servant's word. It is read alongside Caitanya's Purī period and the *Cherā Paṅharā* humility of the Gajapati. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya 5; see [Nimbārka–Vallabha–Caitanya](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md)) [scholarly]
- **The "deity that moves / chooses its place" motif** — Sākṣi-Gopāla belongs to a wider Vaiṣṇava pattern of Deities who **move, turn, or fix themselves** for a devotee (cf. Uḍupi's westward-turning Kṛṣṇa, Ḍākor's Raṇchoḍrāī who followed Bodāṇā, Purī's floating *dāru*). Here uniquely the miracle is **forensic** — testimony in court. See [Ḍākor Raṇchoḍrāī & Bodāṇā](dakor-ranchhodrai-bodana.md). [regional]
