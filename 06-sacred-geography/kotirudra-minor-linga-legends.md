# Koṭirudra minor liṅga legends — Atrīśvara, Nandikeśvara, Mahābala

> **The three sthala-legends that open the Śiva Purāṇa's liṅga-book** — before the *Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* narrates the twelve jyotirliṅgas proper, it establishes the power of the *upaliṅga* / lesser liṅga through three exempla: **Atrīśvara** in the Kāmada forest by Citrakūṭa (an act of drought-penance), **Nandikeśvara** on the **Revā (Narmadā)** (a widow saved posthumously), and **Mahābala** at **Gokarṇa** on the Kanara coast (an outcaste woman's good goal).
> Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up: [Master Index](../README.md) · Related: [12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md) · [Gokarṇa Mahābaleśvara & the Ātma-liṅga](gokarna-mahabaleshwar-atma-linga.md) · [Atri & Anasūyā](../04-deep-dives/atri-anasuya.md)

The **Koṭirudra-saṃhitā** ("collection of the ten-million Rudras") is the sixth book of the **Śiva Purāṇa**, dedicated to the liṅga in all its forms. Its opening chapters (1–2) praise the jyotirliṅgas and their **upaliṅgas** (subsidiary liṅgas) and the śiva-liṅga as such; then, before the great jyotirliṅga catalogue, chapters **3–11** plant three sthala-māhātmyas whose common thesis is that Śiva's grace through the liṅga overrides drought, death, sin, and birth alike. The **two clocks** run apart here: on the *scriptural* clock the Atri–Anasūyā episode belongs to the saptarṣi household of the current **[Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)** (early in this Mahāyuga), while the Nandikeśvara and Mahābala human-tales are undated Purāṇic exempla; on the *academic* clock the received Śiva Purāṇa, and the Koṭirudra-saṃhitā among its later strata, is dated **[scholarly] c. 10th–14th century CE**.

## I. Atrīśvara — the drought-penance of Atri and Anasūyā (chs. 3–4)

- **The hundred-year drought in the Kāmada forest** — a drought lasting a hundred years afflicts the **forest of Kāmada** near **[Citrakūṭa](chitrakoot-kamadgiri.md)**; trees dry without sprout or fruit, no water remains even for daily need, and living beings face dissolution. Into this the sage **Atri** and his wife **Anasūyā** turn their penance toward relief. (Śiva Purāṇa, *Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 3)
- **Atri's yogic trance** — Atri enters deep meditation, practising **prāṇāyāma** repeatedly to invoke Śiva's aid, and remains immobilised in trance for **fifty-four years**, unaware of the world. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 3)
- **Anasūyā's clay liṅga and her superior service** — while her husband is stilled in samādhi, Anasūyā **fashions an earthen (clay) liṅga by mantra** and worships Śiva with mind, speech, and body without pause. The assembled gods and sages judge that **Anasūyā's service surpasses even Atri's penance** — the *pativratā*'s active devotion outweighing the yogin's stillness. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 3)
  - *Note:* this **drought-episode is proper to the Śiva Purāṇa** and is not carried in the [Atri & Anasūyā deep-dive](../04-deep-dives/atri-anasuya.md) (which centres the Mārkaṇḍeya/Bhāgavata Trimūrti chastity-test and the Rāmāyaṇa counsel to Sītā); the river-halting *satya-saṃkalpa* tales there are a separate, kindred motif. [regional]
- **Śiva takes root as Atrīśvara** — drawn by both the trance and the worship, **Śiva manifests from the earthen liṅga** (4.50) and, rather than returning to Kailāsa, **stations himself there permanently**. Granted a boon (4.51), the couple ask only that he "stay in this penance-grove and be the bestower of happiness to the worlds" (4.54); consenting, Śiva "the destroyer of miseries, was known as **Atrīśvara**" — named after the sage Atri (4.56). (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 4.50–56)
- **Gaṅgā's condition and the sacred pit** — attracted by Anasūyā's devotion, **Gaṅgā** arrives and offers her aid (4.9–10); when Anasūyā begs her to remain, Gaṅgā consents **on the condition that Anasūyā transfer to her one year's merit** of Śiva-worship (4.43), and thereafter Gaṅgā abides in the **sacred kuṇḍa (pit)** at the site (4.55) — the drought answered by a permanent water-source beside a permanent liṅga. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 4.43, 4.55)
- **The fruit of worship** — the māhātmya closes that worship of Atrīśvara is "conducive to worldly pleasures and salvation… yields all desires and enhances devotion" (4.61) — the standard *bhukti-and-mukti* (enjoyment-and-liberation) promise attached to an upaliṅga. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 4.61)

## II. Nandikeśvara — the brahmin widow saved on the Narmadā (chs. 5–7)

- **The devout widow of Karṇikī** — in the city of **Karṇikī** on the **western bank of the Revā (Narmadā)** lived a noble brahmin family. The father journeyed to **[Kāśī](kashi-varanasi.md)** and died there; his sons performed his obsequies. The widowed mother, giving pious gifts and raising her **two sons** — the elder named **Suvāda** — is depicted as **virtuous, not sinful**: her one unfulfilled longing was to reach Kāśī herself. (Śiva Purāṇa, *Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 5)
  - *Disambiguation:* although this tale is often summarised as a "sinful lady saved," the text portrays her as **pious with an unfulfilled desire**, not as a great transgressor — the sharp contrast is with the genuinely fallen outcaste of the **Mahābala** legend (§III), where actual grievous sin is dissolved. Keep the two cases distinct. [scholarly]
- **The dying wish** — on her deathbed she tells Suvāda, "I had a great desire to go to Kāśī. That was not to be. Now I am dying here," and asks that after death her **bones be immersed in the Gaṅgā**. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 5)
- **Nandikeśvara's power** — the chapter frames the coming miracle with the liṅga's glory: **"Lord Nandika prevents the sins of a crore of murders,"** being "the bestower of the fruits of desire and salvation too" (5.11) — a claim that sets the Narmadā liṅga on a par with the Gaṅgā at Kāśī. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 5.11)
- **The journey and Gaṅgā as a maiden** — Suvāda sets out bearing his mother's bones, with a servant, and halts at the village of **Viṃśatigrāma** after the first day. There **Gaṅgā appears as a beautiful bejewelled maiden** and directs him: on the **seventh lunar day of Vaiśākha** Gaṅgā is herself present at the **Nandikeśvara** liṅga on the Narmadā — cast the bones there rather than carrying them further. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 6)
- **The bones cast into the Narmadā** — Suvāda **casts half the bones from his bundle into the holy river** at Nandikeśvara; at once his **mother appears endowed with a divine form**, blesses her son, and ascends. She "attained the excellent goal, thanks to the grace of Śiva," enjoying long happiness in that world. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 6)
- **The thesis of the shrine** — the legend's whole point is that the **Narmadā liṅga delivers the Kāśī-fruit**: the unfulfilled pilgrim need not reach Vārāṇasī, for immersion at Nandikeśvara on Vaiśākha-śukla-saptamī grants the same posthumous liberation — the tīrtha comes to the devotee. Chapter 7 rounds this out with a further **greatness of Nandikeśvara**. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 7)

## III. Mahābala at Gokarṇa — the outcaste woman's good goal (chs. 8–10)

- **Mahābala on the western ocean** — the **Mahābala** liṅga stands at **[Gokarṇa](gokarna-mahabaleshwar-atma-linga.md)** on the shore of the western ocean in the **Kanara** country. The Koṭirudra-saṃhitā ties it to the **Ātma-liṅga** cycle: the image "was installed by **Gaṇanāyaka (Gaṇeśa)** at Gokarṇa" after **Rāvaṇa** had won it from Śiva by severe penance — the set-down that roots the liṅga and gives it the name *Mahā-bala*, "great strength." Its power "pervades the regions as far below as the seven nether worlds." (Śiva Purāṇa, *Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 8)
  - *Cross-reference:* the full Rāvaṇa–Gaṇeśa "set-down" narrative and the cow's-ear aetiology live in [Gokarṇa Mahābaleśvara & the Ātma-liṅga](gokarna-mahabaleshwar-atma-linga.md); that file carries the sinner-**Mitrasaha** exemplum but **not** the Cāṇḍālī tale below, which is this file's complement. (see [12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md) for the northern Vaidyanāth recension)
- **The setup** — chapter 8 closes by announcing the exemplum: **"It is by the power of Mahābala that a Cāṇḍālī attained Śivaloka after worshipping that image of Śiva"** — grace that reaches even the lowest by birth. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 8)
- **Sauminī, the fallen brahmin girl** — the outcaste woman was, in a former birth, **Sauminī**, a brahmin girl. Widowed young, she **transgressed the bounds of decency through lust**; cast off by her family, she was taken to wife by a **Śūdra** and so fell to outcaste (**cāṇḍāla**) status. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 9)
- **The chain of sin** — in that degraded life she **ate meat and drank wine**, and while intoxicated **killed a calf, mistaking it for a goat** — adding go-hatyā-like slaughter to her transgressions. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 9)
- **Rebirth in suffering** — after death **Yama** had her reborn as the **congenitally blind, leprous daughter of a Cāṇḍāla woman**, enduring severe suffering throughout that life — the karmic wages of the former sins. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 9)
- **Inadvertent worship at Gokarṇa** — during a **Śiva festival (Śivarātri)** at Gokarṇa a traveller placed **bilva leaves** in her hands; the leaves **fell upon the Mahābala liṅga**, so that "unwittingly she observed the rites," and she **kept vigil (jāgaraṇa) through the night**. By this unintended worship and night-long fast she accrued merit. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 9)
- **Mahābala grants Śivaloka** — Mahābala, "destructive of sins and the bestower of the highest bliss instantaneously," **carried her to Śiva's abode** — the outcaste sinner attaining the *śreyas* (good goal) denied to the twice-born by mere birth. Chapter 10 extends the **greatness and glory of Mahābala**. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 9–10)
  - *Doctrinal note:* the tale is a charter for **ajñāna-kṛta pūjā** (worship done unknowingly) and **anāyāsa-mukti** — that the liṅga's grace is efficacious even without intention, caste, or knowledge — a recurring Śaiva bhakti motif (cf. the [Śivarātri hunter legend](../04-deep-dives/shivaratri-hunter-legend.md), where a fowler's unwitting bilva-drops win Śiva). [scholarly]

## IV. Reading the three legends together

- **A graded triad of grace** — the sequence is deliberately ascending in the depth of what is redeemed: Atrīśvara answers **natural calamity** (drought) for great sages; Nandikeśvara answers an **ordinary devotee's unfulfilled longing** at death; Mahābala answers **grievous sin and low birth** by inadvertent worship. Together they argue that no obstacle — element, mortality, or karma — is beyond the liṅga. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 3–10)
- **The upaliṅga frame** — placed **before** the twelve-jyotirliṅga catalogue, the three function as *upaliṅga* / subsidiary-liṅga proofs, establishing the theology of liṅga-grace with vivid human cases before the geography of the great liṅgas is unrolled. (*Koṭirudra-saṃhitā* 1–2) — see [12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md).
- **Tīrtha substitution** — both the Nandikeśvara and Mahābala tales encode **tīrtha substitution**: the Narmadā liṅga yields the Kāśī-fruit; the Gokarṇa liṅga yields Śivaloka to one who never sought it. The merit-economy of pilgrimage is thereby democratised — a pointed contrast to the strict *prāyaścitta* (expiation) calculus. See [Pāpa, Puṇya & Prāyaścitta](../05-concepts-and-dharma/papa-punya-and-prayaschitta.md) and [Gayā Pitṛ-tīrtha](gaya-pitru-tirtha.md).
- **The two clocks, restated** — the Atrīśvara episode is fixed on the *scriptural* clock in the saptarṣi age of this Mahāyuga; the Nandikeśvara and Mahābala human-tales are **undated exempla** (Cross-Yuga in the corpus's reckoning). On the *academic* clock the whole samhitā is a **[scholarly] c. 10th–14th century CE** compilation, its Gokarṇa material overlapping the coastal Sahyādri-khaṇḍa tradition. (see [Gokarṇa](gokarna-mahabaleshwar-atma-linga.md))
