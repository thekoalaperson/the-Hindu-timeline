# Banke Bihari — Vrindavan & Nidhivan

> **Bāṅke Bihārī jī** of **Vṛndāvana** — the most-visited shrine of [Braj](mathura-vrindavan-braj.md) and the heart-temple of the **Haridāsī Sampradāya**. A combined **Rādhā–Kṛṣṇa** icon in *tribhaṅga* ("thrice-bent") pose, manifested from the grove of **Nidhivana** by the singer-saint **Svāmī Haridāsa** (16th c.), worshipped not with bells and ārtī-blaze but with a **drawn curtain (jhāṅkī) darśana** of intermittent veiling. As with every *māhātmya* node the **scriptural clock** (the eternal Vṛndāvana *rāsa*) and the **historical clock** (Haridāsa's *prākaṭya* and the 1864 temple) run separately and are kept apart below.

## I. The deity — Bāṅke Bihārī, the thrice-bent one

- **The name** — *Bāṅke* ("bent, crooked, dandyish") + *Bihārī* ("one who sports/wanders," i.e. the playful Kṛṣṇa of the *vihāra* / *līlā*); together "the bewitching bent-bodied wanderer." The bends are the three flexions of the **tribhaṅga** stance — neck, waist and knee curved — the classic flute-playing Kṛṣṇa posture. (Haridāsī tradition)
- **A combined Rādhā–Kṛṣṇa form** — the black icon is held to be **Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa fused in a single body (*yugala-svarūpa*)**, the union of the divine pair rather than Kṛṣṇa alone; the devotee sees both the lover and the beloved in one mūrti. (Haridāsī Sampradāya doctrine)
  - *Variant (folk):* the eyes of Bāṅke Bihārī are said to be so charged that prolonged eye-contact can "draw the soul out" or cause a devotee to faint/follow the deity — the stated reason for the curtain custom. (Vṛndāvana folk tradition) [folk]
- **Svayam-prākaṭya (self-manifest)** — the icon is **not carved but self-revealed**, having emerged from the soil of **Nidhivana** at Svāmī Haridāsa's song; it is therefore treated as a living, breathing deity (*sākṣāt Ṭhākurjī*), not a fashioned image. (Haridāsī *prākaṭya* legend) [folk]

## II. Scriptural backdrop — the eternal Vṛndāvana rāsa (Dvāpara / Goloka)

The shrine's theology rests on the **[Bhāgavata](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md)** Vṛndāvana *līlā* — but unlike most Braj sites it does not point to a single past event so much as to a **continuing, present-tense play**.

- **The rāsa that never ended** — Haridāsī (and broader Braj) tradition holds that the **nightly *rāsa-līlā*** of Kṛṣṇa and the *gopīs* described in *Bhāgavata* 10.29–33 **still continues unseen** in the groves of [Nidhivana / Sevā-kuñja](mathura-vrindavan-braj.md); the manifested icon is a window onto that ongoing eternity, not a memorial of a finished act. (Braj *sthala* tradition) [folk]
- **Goloka made visible** — in line with the Braj-wide claim, Vṛndāvana is **Goloka descended to earth**, so Bāṅke Bihārī is the supreme Yugala *dhāma* shown in form. (Vaiṣṇava theology — cf. [Braj §I](mathura-vrindavan-braj.md))

## III. Svāmī Haridāsa & the manifestation (16th c.)

The defining **historical-clock** node — Haridāsa's life and the *prākaṭya* of the icon, dated by tradition to the mid-16th century.

- **Svāmī Haridāsa (c. 1480–1573, dates disputed)** — the **rasika** singer-saint of Vṛndāvana, ascetic of **Nidhivana**, regarded in his lineage as an **incarnation of the *sakhī* Lalitā** (one of Rādhā's eight chief companions); founder of the **Haridāsī Sampradāya** and one of the towering figures of North-Indian devotional music. (Haridāsī hagiography) [late text]
  - *Variant (dating) [disputed]:* his birth is placed variously **c. 1478, 1480 or 1512 CE**; the *prākaṭya* of the icon is traditionally fixed at **V.S. ~1542 / c. 1485 CE** in some accounts and mid-16th c. in others — the chronology is hagiographic, not documentary.
- **The song that called the deity** — tradition: at Nidhivana, Haridāsa sang in longing for the divine couple; the pair **appeared before him as the dazzling Śyāmā–Śyāma (Rādhā–Kṛṣṇa)**, and at his prayer that the world could not bear such radiance they **merged into the single dark icon of Bāṅke Bihārī**, which he then enshrined. (Haridāsī *prākaṭya* legend) [folk]
- **Tānsen and Akbar** — a celebrated (and disputed) tradition makes **Tānsen**, the Mughal court-musician of **Akbar**, a disciple of Haridāsa; Akbar is said to have come incognito to Nidhivana to hear the master sing — a story illustrating Haridāsa's musical eminence more than verifiable history. (folk/biographical tradition) [disputed]
- **The *goswāmī* custodians** — worship of the manifested icon descended through Haridāsa's family/disciple line (the **Goswāmīs of the Haridāsī Sampradāya**), who remain the hereditary servitors of Bāṅke Bihārī to this day. (Haridāsī institution)

## IV. The Haridāsī Sampradāya & the dhrupad lineage

- **A music-born sampradāya** — alone among the major Braj traditions, the **Haridāsī Sampradāya** is founded on **devotional song (saṃkīrtana / dhrupad)** as its central *sevā*; the deity is served above all through **music**, in the *rasika-bhāva* of the *sakhīs* who attend the divine couple. (Haridāsī tradition) [scholarly]
- **Dhrupad of the Haridāsī gharānā** — Haridāsa stands at the fountainhead of the **Vṛndāvana / Haridāsī dhrupad** stream of Hindustani classical music; his repertoire and the practice of singing before the deity shaped a living temple-music lineage distinct from the [Aṣṭachāp kīrtana](nathdwara-shrinathji.md) of the Vallabha Puṣṭimārga. (music-historical) [scholarly]
- **The *Kelimālā*** — Haridāsa's first body of songs, the **"Garland of Play" (*Kelimālā*)**: ~110+ *padas* in Braj-bhāṣā describing the eternal *vihāra* of Rādhā–Kṛṣṇa in the Vṛndāvana groves — the celebratory, *līlā*-immersed register. (Svāmī Haridāsa, *Kelimālā*)
- **The *Siddhānta-ke-pad*** — his doctrinal songs, the **"Verses of Doctrine" (*Siddhānta ke pada*)**: a smaller set (~18 padas) of teaching-verses on detachment, the *rasika* path and the supremacy of the Yugala, forming the philosophical charter of the lineage. (Svāmī Haridāsa, *Siddhānta-ke-pad*)
- **Nikuñja-upāsanā** — the worship-mood is **groves-of-love (*nikuñja*)**: the devotee identifies as a *sakhī* serving the play of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa within the inner grove, the same Nidhivana made interior. (Haridāsī doctrine)

## V. Nidhivana — the sthala legend

- **The "treasure-grove" (*Nidhi-vana*)** — the dense tangled grove in Vṛndāvana where Haridāsa lived and where the icon manifested; the *līlā-sthal* held to be a perpetual site of the **nightly rāsa**. (Braj *sthala* tradition) — see [Braj §III](mathura-vrindavan-braj.md). [folk]
- **The trees that are gopīs** — folk legend: the **twisted, low, intertwined tulsī-like trees of Nidhivana are the *gopīs* themselves**, who take tree-form by day and resume their bodies at night to dance the rāsa with Kṛṣṇa. (Nidhivana sthala legend) [folk]
- **The forbidden night** — **no human may remain in Nidhivana after the evening ārtī**; it is sealed at dusk, for the rāsa resumes and any mortal who watches is said to lose sanity, sight or life — and the **Rang Mahal** within is laid each night with bed, betel and toiletries found "used" by morning. (Vṛndāvana folk tradition) [folk]
- **Haridāsa's samādhi** — the **samādhi (resting-shrine) of Svāmī Haridāsa** stands within the Nidhivana precinct, a pilgrimage focus of the Haridāsī lineage. (Haridāsī tradition)

## VI. Darśana, sevā & the 1864 temple

- **The curtain (jhāṅkī) darśana** — uniquely, Bāṅke Bihārī is **never shown continuously**: a **curtain is drawn across the deity every few minutes**, opening only in brief flashes (*jhāṅkī*) so the icon's overpowering gaze cannot be held too long — a rhythm of veiling and unveiling found at no other major Vaiṣṇava shrine. (Haridāsī sevā tradition) [regional]
- **No bells, no conch** — in the temple there is **no ringing of bells and no conch-blowing**, lest the "sleeping" or sporting deity be disturbed; worship is hushed, mood-led intimacy rather than loud ritual — a deliberate contrast to standard temple ārtī. (Haridāsī sevā tradition) [regional]
- **Maṅgalā darśana once a year** — the **Maṅgalā (dawn) darśana** is opened **only one day a year**, on **Akṣaya Tṛtīyā**; on this day alone the deity's lotus **feet are revealed (*caraṇa-darśana*)**, otherwise kept covered. (Haridāsī temple custom) [regional]
- **Festivals** — distinctive *utsavas* include the **Hariyālī Tīj** (monsoon swing — the deity placed on a **golden hindolā**), **Jhulan**, **Holī**, **Janmāṣṭamī**, and the rare **Maṅgalā** and feet-darśana days; the mood throughout is the *līlā*-play of the Yugala. (Haridāsī calendar)
- **The 1864 temple foundation** — the present **Bāṅke Bihārī Mandir** in the **Rāmana-reti / Vidyāpīṭha quarter** of Vṛndāvana was **built in 1864 CE** by the Haridāsī Goswāmīs, the icon brought from Nidhivana to the new shrine; built in the **Rajasthani-arched** style, it is today the most-thronged temple of Braj. (Banke Bihari Temple history) [scholarly]
- **A first-tier Kṛṣṇa kṣetra** — within Braj the shrine stands alongside Govinda-deva, Rādhāramaṇa, Madana-mohana and Rādhāvallabha as one of the **chief *Ṭhākur* temples of the Vṛndāvana rediscovery**, yet doctrinally and musically it is its own node — the Haridāsī heart-shrine, distinct from the broader [Braj](mathura-vrindavan-braj.md) and [Puṣṭimārga](nathdwara-shrinathji.md) currents. (Vṛndāvana temple history)
