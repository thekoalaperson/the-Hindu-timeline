# Pandharpur — Viṭṭhala Kṣetra & the Wārī Pilgrimage

> The **bhū-Vaikuṇṭha** ("Vaikuṇṭha on earth") of Maharashtra: on the **Bhīmā (Candrabhāgā)** river stands **[Viṭṭhala / Viṭhobā / Pāṇḍuraṅga](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vithoba-vitthal.md)** — the hands-on-hips form of **[Kṛṣṇa/Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** who has waited on **Puṇḍalīka's brick** since the devotee tossed it out for him — and to this standing god the **Vārkarī** walking pilgrimage (**Wārī**) converges twice a year in the largest recurring foot-pilgrimage of the Deccan. Where [Kolhapur](kolhapur-mahalakshmi-karavira.md) holds the consort-Lakṣmī, Pandharpur holds the waiting Lord; the two anchor the Marathi Vaiṣṇava map.
>

**Names of the kṣetra** — **Pandharpur** (Marathi **Paṇḍharī / Paṇḍharpūr**), Sanskritized as **Pāṇḍuraṅga-kṣetra** ("field of the pale-limbed one" — a name genuinely puzzling for a black-stone deity, see the [etymology discussion](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vithoba-vitthal.md)) and, after its founding devotee, **Puṇḍarīka-kṣetra / Puṇḍalīka-kṣetra**. Vārkarī song calls it **bhū-Vaikuṇṭha** and "the Kāśī of the South" of the Marathi country. The river frontage is the **Candrabhāgā** — the Bhīmā, renamed at Pandharpur for its crescent-moon (*candra*) bend. (*Pāṇḍuraṅga-māhātmya*; Vārkarī abhaṅga corpus) [regional]

*This file is the canonical home for the **place and pilgrimage**; the deity's iconography, names, and theology live in [vithoba-vitthal.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vithoba-vitthal.md).*

---

## I. The Puṇḍalīka legend — why the Lord stands here

- **Puṇḍalīka's brick** — the once-neglectful son **Puṇḍalīka (Puṇḍarīka)**, reformed into the very model of *mātṛ-pitṛ-bhakti* (service to parents), is tending his aged mother and father when **Kṛṣṇa/Viṣṇu himself arrives** at his door. Unwilling to break off his service even for God, Puṇḍalīka **tosses out a brick (*vīṭ*)** for the Lord to stand on and wait — and the Lord, delighted that filial duty outranked even his own *darśan*, **remains standing on that brick**, hands on hips, as **Viṭṭhala** of Pandharpur. (*Pāṇḍuraṅga-māhātmya*, Skanda Purāṇa / Padma Purāṇa recensions; Vārkarī hagiography)
  - *Variant (Puṇḍalīka's reform):* a fuller telling routes the wayward Puṇḍalīka through the hermitage of sage **Kukkuṭasvāmī**, where at dawn he sees the river-goddesses **Gaṅgā, Yamunā** and their sisters arrive in soiled clothes to wash off the sins pilgrims have left in them — the shock that converts him to a life of service. [folk]
  - *Variant (the Dvārakā frame):* in another cycle the Lord comes to the Pandharpur region **from Dvārakā, seeking Rukmiṇī**, who has withdrawn in anger to the grove of **Diṇḍīravana (Diṇḍīrvan)** near Pandharpur; the Puṇḍalīka episode is woven into that quest, and the couple's estrangement is invoked to explain the **separate shrines of Viṭṭhala and Rakhumāī** in the temple complex. Traditions are cataloged side by side, not merged. (*Pāṇḍuraṅga-māhātmya*; Marathi tradition) [regional]
- **Puṇḍalīka's shrine on the river** — Puṇḍalīka is honored with his own **samādhi-shrine on the Candrabhāgā sands**, and Vārkarī practice is to take his *darśan* together with the Lord's — fixed in the universal Vārkarī cry **"Puṇḍalīka-varadā Hari Viṭṭhal!"** ("Hail Hari Viṭṭhal, boon-giver to Puṇḍalīka!"). The devotee is invoked *before* the god he detained. (Pandharpur temple tradition; Vārkarī practice) [regional]

## II. Candrabhāgā — the Bhīmā tīrtha

- **Bhīmā becomes Candrabhāgā** — the **Bhīmā** (a major tributary of the Kṛṣṇā, rising at **Bhīmāśaṅkar** in the Sahyādris — one of the 12 [jyotirliṅga](12-jyotirlingas.md) sites) takes the name **Candrabhāgā** at Pandharpur, where its course curves like the **crescent moon**; the *māhātmya* glorifies bathing in this bend as destroying sins and granting the fruit of great *tīrthas*. (*Pāṇḍuraṅga-māhātmya*) [late text]
  - *Caution:* this Deccan Candrabhāgā is **distinct from the Punjab/Himalayan Candrabhāgā (the Chenab)** of northern river-lists (see [Sacred Rivers & Descent Myths](sacred-rivers-and-descent-myths.md)); the shared name is a deliberate sanctifying transfer, and popular sources sometimes conflate the two. [scholarly]
- **The riverfront rite** — the Vārkarī sequence at the goal: **snāna in the Candrabhāgā**, *darśan* of **Puṇḍalīka's shrine** on the sands, then the queue to Viṭṭhala for **pad-sparśa darśan** — Pandharpur is famous as a shrine where ordinary pilgrims may **touch the deity's feet**, an unusually intimate access central to the *kṣetra*'s egalitarian reputation. (Pandharpur temple tradition) [regional]
- **Sites around the bend** — the sacred perimeter includes the **Viṣṇupada** (stone bearing footprints of the Lord across the river), the grove **Diṇḍīrvan** of the Rukmiṇī cycle, and **Gopāḷpūr** with its Gopāḷkṛṣṇa shrine, visited in the pilgrims' circumambulatory customs (*pradakṣiṇā* of the town and riverside). (Marathi pilgrim tradition) [regional] [folk]

## III. The Pāṇḍuraṅga-māhātmya — the sthala-purāṇa

- **The glorification text** — the **Pāṇḍuraṅga-māhātmya** ("Greatness of Pāṇḍuraṅga") is the Sanskrit *kṣetra-māhātmya* of Pandharpur, transmitted as a section of the **Skanda Purāṇa** and, in a distinct recension, of the **Padma Purāṇa**; it narrates the Puṇḍalīka legend, praises the Candrabhāgā, and proclaims the merit of *darśan* and of the recurring pilgrimage. Like other [sthala-purāṇas](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md) it retro-fits a living cult into a Purāṇic frame. (*Pāṇḍuraṅga-māhātmya*) [late text]
  - *Variant (recensions):* the Skanda and Padma versions differ in details, and later Marathi *māhātmya* retellings multiply the variants; there is no single critical text. [scholarly] [late text]
- **The Marathi scripture layer** — alongside the Sanskrit *māhātmya*, the effective scripture of the *kṣetra* is the **abhaṅga corpus** of the Vārkarī saints — [Jñāneśvar](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/jnaneshwar.md), [Nāmdev](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/namdev.md), [Eknāth](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/eknath.md), [Tukārām](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/tukaram.md), [Janābāī](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/janabai.md), [Cokhāmeḷā](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/chokhamela.md) and the rest — which sings Pandharpur itself ("my mother-house Paṇḍharī") as much as the god. [regional] [scholarly]

## IV. Temple, image, saints' marks — the documented shrine

- **The temple in history** — the standing Viṭṭhala temple is academically a **medieval Deccan foundation**: inscriptional evidence attests the shrine by the **late 12th century CE** (a Pandharpur inscription of **1189 CE** is commonly cited as the earliest secure reference to the god's shrine), with major **13th-century expansion under Yādava-era patronage**; the cult and its sant-lineage are documented from roughly the 11th–13th c. onward. (epigraphic and historical scholarship, e.g. R. C. Dhere, *Rise of a Folk God*; G. A. Deleury, *The Cult of Vithoba*) [scholarly]
- **Nāmdev's step (Nāmdev pāyrī)** — the **first step of the temple's great gate (Mahādvāra)** is named for [Nāmdev](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/namdev.md), who wished to remain forever at the threshold **under the feet of the saints** entering for *darśan*; his memorial is set into that step. (Vārkarī hagiography; Pandharpur temple tradition) [regional]
- **Cokhāmeḷā at the door** — the samādhi of the Mahār saint [Cokhāmeḷā](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/chokhamela.md), who in his lifetime was barred from entry by caste, stands **at the foot of the Mahādvāra** — placed there, tradition says, at Nāmdev's initiative; it dramatizes both the movement's anti-caste ethic and the historical exclusions it fought (temple entry for "untouchables" was won only in **1947**, after the campaign of Sane Guruji). (Vārkarī hagiography; documented history) [regional] [scholarly]
- **Kānhopātrā's tree** — the dancing-girl saint [Kānhopātrā](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/kanhopatra.md), refusing the summons of the Bīdar king, died at Viṭṭhala's feet and was buried in the temple precinct, where a **tarāṭī tree** rose over her grave — she is the rare saint memorialized *inside* the complex. (Mahīpati, *Bhaktavijaya*) [regional] [folk]
- **The image carried off and brought back** — Vārkarī hagiography (Mahīpati's *Bhaktavijaya*) tells that the Viṭṭhala image was **taken to Vijayanagara** (by the emperor, out of devotion) and **restored to Pandharpur by Bhānudās** (great-grandfather of [Eknāth](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/eknath.md), 16th c.); scholars read in this and related traditions memories of the image's **removals and concealments** during medieval invasions, with some arguing the present image is a reinstallation. (Mahīpati, *Bhaktavijaya*; Dhere) [folk] [disputed] [scholarly]

## V. The Wārī — Āṣāḍhī & Kārttikī pilgrimage

- **The vow of the walk** — a **Vārkarī** ("one who makes the *vārī*") vows the recurring walking pilgrimage to Pandharpur, wears the **tulasī-mālā**, and keeps a simple ethical rule (vegetarianism, no liquor, daily nāma and Ekādaśī fasting); the *sampradāya* is named for the pilgrimage itself, not for a founder-ācārya. (Vārkarī tradition; see [Vithoba deep-dive](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vithoba-vitthal.md)) [regional]
- **The four yātrās, two great ones** — Pandharpur observes pilgrimage festivals at **Āṣāḍhī Ekādaśī** (Jun–Jul, the greatest), **Kārttikī Ekādaśī** (Oct–Nov), and lesser gatherings at **Māghī** and **Caitrī** Ekādaśīs. The two great dates lock the *kṣetra* to Viṣṇu's cosmic calendar: **Āṣāḍhī = Śayanī Ekādaśī**, when Viṣṇu lies down for his four-month *yoga-nidrā* (Cāturmāsya), and **Kārttikī = Prabodhinī**, when he wakes — the pilgrims arrive precisely at the hinges of the god's sleep and waking (see [Ekādaśī Māhātmyas](../04-deep-dives/ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md)). (*Ekādaśī-māhātmya* cycle; Vārkarī calendar) [regional]
- **Diṇḍī and pālkhī — the moving geography** — pilgrims walk in **diṇḍīs** (registered singing companies, each with its flag, *vīṇā*-bearer, mṛdaṅga and cymbal players), organized around **pālkhīs** — palanquins bearing not images but the **pādukās (sandals) of the saints**, so that the saints themselves are held to walk each year to their Lord. Scores of pālkhīs set out from saints' samādhi-towns across Maharashtra and converge on Pandharpur for Āṣāḍhī. (Vārkarī practice; Deleury) [regional] [scholarly]
  - **Jñāneśvar's pālkhī (Āḷandī → Pandharpur)** — the senior procession: the pādukās of [Jñāneśvar](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/jnaneshwar.md) leave his samādhi at **Āḷandī**, pass through **Pune** and **Sāsvaḍ**, then by **Jejurī, Lonand, Phaltan, Nātepute, Māḷśiras and Veḷāpur** toward Wākhrī on Pandharpur's edge — a march of roughly 250 km over about three weeks. (Vārkarī practice) [regional]
  - **Tukārām's pālkhī (Dehu → Pandharpur)** — the pādukās of [Tukārām](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/tukaram.md) set out from **Dehu**, travel via **Pune**, then take the eastern route through **Loṇī Kāḷbhor, Yavat, Baramati, Indāpur and Akluj**; the two great pālkhīs converge near **Wākhrī** and enter Pandharpur together on the eve of Āṣāḍhī Ekādaśī. (Vārkarī practice) [regional]
  - **The wider garland** — further pālkhīs join from the other saints' seats: **[Nivṛttināth](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/nivritti-sopan-muktabai.md)** from Tryambakeśvar, **Sopān** from Sāsvaḍ, **Muktābāī** from Muktāīnagar, **[Eknāth](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/eknath.md)** from Paiṭhaṇ, **Nāmdev** from Pandharpur itself, and many more — the map of the Wārī is a living index of the sant-geography of Maharashtra. (Vārkarī practice) [regional]
- **Riṅgaṇ and the rites of the road** — at fixed halts the marching column performs the **riṅgaṇ**: pilgrims form a great ring through which the pālkhī's sacred horse (held to carry the saint's presence) **gallops**, and dust from its hooves is taken as blessing; there are likewise communal games (*bhāruḍ*, *phugaḍī*) and nightly **kīrtan** — the Wārī functioning as a mobile festival of the abhaṅga corpus. (Vārkarī practice) [regional] [folk]
- **How the pālkhī form arose (documented clock)** — carrying the saints' pādukās to Pandharpur is attested from the generation after Tukārām: tradition credits **Nārāyaṇ Mahārāj, Tukārām's youngest son (late 17th c.)**, with instituting a joint procession of Tukārām's and Jñāneśvar's pādukās; the present **separate, militarily-ordered pālkhī system** is credited to **Haibatrāvbābā (Haibatbābā) Ārphaḷkar**, a courtier of the Scindias, who reorganized the Jñāneśvar pālkhī with its diṇḍī-register, horses and staged halts (dated by different authorities to the **late 18th–early 19th c.**). (Deleury; Vārkarī tradition) [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Scale** — the modern Āṣāḍhī Wārī draws **hundreds of thousands to over a million** pilgrims into Pandharpur, among the largest regularly recurring pilgrimages in India after the [Kumbh cycle](kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md); women have always walked it, and its saint-roster spans every caste — the point Vārkarī tradition itself insists on (cf. [Bahiṇābāī](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/bahinabai.md), [Gorā Kumbhār](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/gora-kumbhar.md), [Janābāī](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/janabai.md)). (documented practice) [scholarly] [regional]
- **The cries of the road** — the Wārī's soundscape is fixed: **"Viṭṭhal Viṭṭhal!"**, **"Puṇḍalīka-varadā Hari Viṭṭhal!"**, and **"Jñānobā-Tukārām!"** — the last yoking the first and last great sants into a single mantra of the walking column. (Vārkarī practice) [regional]

## VI. Timeline placement (the two clocks)

- **Scriptural clock** — Viṭṭhala is **Kṛṣṇa**, so the deity's root is **Dvāpara**; the Dvārakā-to-Diṇḍīrvan quest variant explicitly bridges Dvāpara Kṛṣṇa to the Deccan site. The **Puṇḍalīka episode and the Lord's standing wait** are narrated in the *māhātmya* as an undated, still-continuing act — a power fixed for the whole present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)**, with the annual Wārī keyed to Viṣṇu's cosmic sleep and waking. [late text]
- **Historical clock** — the shrine is inscriptionally secure from the **late 12th c. CE** (1189 CE) with **13th-c. Yādava-era** expansion; the sant-lineage runs **c. 13th–17th c.** (Jñāneśvar d. 1296 to Tukārām d. c. 1650); the pādukā-processions date from the **late 17th c.** (Nārāyaṇ Mahārāj) and the organized pālkhī system from the **late 18th–early 19th c.** (Haibatrāvbābā); **temple entry for all castes came in 1947**. Never collapse the two clocks. [scholarly]
