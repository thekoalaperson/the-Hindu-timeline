# Gayā — the Supreme Pitṛ-Tīrtha (Ancestral Liberation)

> The foremost **pitṛ-tīrtha** ("ford of the ancestors") of the Hindu world: the *kṣetra* in Bihar where a single offering of *piṇḍa* (rice-and-sesame balls) is held to liberate **fourteen generations** of forebears on both the paternal and maternal lines and to deliver wandering *pretas* into the world of the *pitṛs*. Built on the body of the slain demon **Gayāsura**, pinned by Viṣṇu's footprint, and watered by the curse-dried **Phalgu** — Gayā is the death-rite counterpart to [Kāśī's](kashi-varanasi.md) death-liberation. The dedicated ancestor-rite node: the Gayā Māhātmya, the Viṣṇupada, the vedī-circuit, the Akṣayavaṭa, and Pitṛ Pakṣa.

**Why Gayā is supreme** — among all *tīrthas*, Gayā alone is the *pitṛ-tīrtha par excellence*: where ordinary śrāddha *sustains* the ancestors, Gayā-śrāddha is held to *finish* the obligation forever, releasing the dead from the *preta*-state into *pitṛ-loka* or beyond, and absolving the descendant of the recurring annual debt. The act is called **Gayā-śrāddha** or **piṇḍa-dāna**; one who performs it earns the title **Gayāli / Gayāwāl** (the patrons) and is said to make his line *putra* in the true sense — "*pun-nāmnaḥ narakāt trāyate*," "he who saves from the hell named *Put*." (*Vāyu Purāṇa, Gayā-māhātmya*; *Garuḍa Purāṇa, Preta-khaṇḍa*)

## I. The Gayā Māhātmya (Vāyu Purāṇa) — the textual charter

- **The Gayā-māhātmya** - the principal scriptural eulogy of the site, embedded in the **Vāyu Purāṇa** (and recapitulated/expanded in the *Agni*, *Garuḍa*, *Nāradīya*, *Vāmana* and *Padma* Purāṇas). It frames the whole *kṣetra* as a *yajña-bhūmi* — a permanent sacrificial ground sanctified by the devas — and lays down the vedī-circuit and its phala-śruti (promised fruits). (*Vāyu Purāṇa, Gayā-māhātmya*)
- **The promised fruit (phala-śruti)** - the Māhātmya promises that piṇḍa offered at Gayā secures the ancestors' passage out of *preta-yoni*, even those who died ignoble or unrites deaths, and that the merit is **inexhaustible** (*akṣaya*) — hence the central role of the *Akṣaya-vaṭa* (§VI). [late text]
- **Tri-tīrtha framing** - later pilgrim manuals organise Gayā as a triad of *śilā* (rock-altars): **Pretaśilā** (the "rock of the *pretas*"), **Rāmaśilā / Rāmkuṇḍ**, and the **Viṣṇupada** group, often with **Brahmayoni** hill as the fourth pole — the pilgrim circuit threads them. [scholarly]
- **Two clocks — note** - the Purāṇic charter is *mythic-eternal* (the site is sanctified "from the beginning"); **academic dating** places the Vāyu-Purāṇa Gayā-māhātmya in the mid-first-millennium CE strata, with the *Vishnupada* temple in its present form rebuilt **1787 CE** by the Maratha queen **Ahalyābāī Holkar** of Indore. [scholarly]

## II. The Gayāsura legend — the demon as the ground itself

- **Gayāsura's penance** - a colossal *asura* named **Gaya** (Gayāsura) — by the Vāyu account **125 yojanas tall and 60 yojanas in girth** — performed terrible austerities (on **Kolāhala** hill, var. by the gods' design) until his body became so purified that the mere *sight or touch* of him sent beings straight to Vaikuṇṭha, emptying the heavens of merit-economy and the hells of sinners. (*Vāyu Purāṇa, Gayā-māhātmya*)
- **The crisis of an over-holy body** - because everyone who saw Gayāsura attained liberation regardless of karma, **Yama's hells emptied** and the cosmic order of reward-and-punishment collapsed; the devas, led by **Brahmā**, sought to fix him in one spot. (*Vāyu Purāṇa*; *Agni Purāṇa*)
- **The yajña on his body** - Gayāsura, asked for a boon, offered his own body as the **altar** for a great sacrifice; he lay down (head to the north), and Brahmā/the devas performed the *yajña* upon him, making his body the holiest ground on earth. (*Vāyu Purāṇa, Gayā-māhātmya*)
- **The gods stand on him; Viṣṇu pins him** - when Gayāsura's body kept quaking and would not stay still, the gods stood upon him and **Viṣṇu placed a great stone slab (the Dharmaśilā / Gayāśila) on his head/chest**, pressing it down with his own foot — the **Viṣṇupada footprint** (§III). Even pinned, the body would not be quiet until Viṣṇu boon-granted him. (*Vāyu Purāṇa*; *Agni Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (who weights the rock):* in one telling the slab is **Dharmavrata's** / **Dharma's** body turned to stone (the rock named *Dharmaśilā*), and the *devas plus Viṣṇu* stand upon it together; in another Viṣṇu alone subdues the demon. [disputed]
  - *Variant (Yama-vs-Brahmā motive):* some recensions foreground **Yama's** complaint (his hells emptied) rather than a generic deva-crisis as the trigger for fixing Gayāsura. [folk]
- **The boon — Gayā the eternal tīrtha** - in exchange Viṣṇu/the gods granted that the demon's name would mark the spot forever (**Gayā**) and that **any ancestral rite performed there would liberate the dead**; the gods promised to remain present on his body. Thus the demon's self-sacrifice *becomes* the sacred geography. (*Vāyu Purāṇa, Gayā-māhātmya*)
- **The span of the kṣetra** - because his body is the ground, *Gayā-kṣetra* is extensive (the Māhātmya gives it a *yojana*-measured boundary, hence the **360+ vedīs** scattered across the region, §V) — the demon's whole prone form, not a single shrine. (*Vāyu Purāṇa*)

## III. Vishnupada — Viṣṇu's footprint

- **The Viṣṇupada (Dharmaśilā)** - the central shrine: a ~**40 cm footprint of Viṣṇu** impressed in solid black basalt (the *Gayāśila / Dharmaśilā*), held to be the very foot with which Viṣṇu pressed Gayāsura down. The footprint, ringed in silver, is the single most meritorious spot for piṇḍa-dāna in Gayā. (*Vāyu Purāṇa*; prayagpandits.com; tirthayatra.org)
- **The Vishnupad temple** - the octagonal *śikhara* shrine enclosing the footprint, rebuilt in its present form in **1787 CE by Ahalyābāī Holkar**. Non-Hindus are traditionally barred from the inner sanctum. (Wikipedia, *Vishnupad Temple*; prayagpandits.com) [scholarly]
- **"Where the piṇḍas never stop"** - the temple is described as the place where offerings are made continuously day and night through the season; the footprint is the focal *vedī* where the line's whole obligation can be discharged at once. (tirthayatra.org)

## IV. The Phalgu / Falgu — the curse-dried river

- **The Phalgu river** - the sacred river of Gayā, on whose bank the *kṣetra* stands; ritual bathing here precedes piṇḍa-dāna. Famously it runs **dry on the surface** at Gayā, its water flowing **beneath the sandy bed** — so piṇḍas are often shaped from the **sand of the riverbed** itself. (Wikipedia, *Phalgu River*; prayagpandits.com)
- **Sītā's curse (the etiology of the dry river)** - in the *Rāmāyaṇa*-derived local legend, **Rāma, Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa** came to Gayā to perform śrāddha for **Daśaratha**. While Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa bathed, the ancestor's hand appeared demanding the piṇḍa; **Sītā offered a piṇḍa of sand** and called **five witnesses** — the **Phalgu river, a cow, the Akṣayavaṭa (banyan), a Ketakī flower, and a Brāhmaṇa**. (folk *sthala-purāṇa*; prayagpandits.com) [folk]
  - When Rāma returned and doubted, **four witnesses lied** (the river, cow, flower, Brāhmaṇa), and only the **Akṣayavaṭa told the truth**. Sītā then **cursed** the false witnesses: the **Phalgu to flow underground / lose its surface water**, the cow's mouth to be impure, the Ketakī to be barred from Śiva-worship, the Brāhmaṇa-line to remain ever-needy; and she **blessed the Akṣayavaṭa with imperishability**. (folk; prayagpandits.com) [folk]
  - *Variant (witness-roster):* the list of cursed witnesses varies regionally (some omit the cow or substitute a *tulsī*/other plant); the constant pair is **false Phalgu** + **truthful Akṣayavaṭa**. [folk]
- **Distinct from the Buddhist Phalgu** - the same river flows past **Bodh Gayā** (§VIII); the Buddhist tradition remembers it as the river by whose Bodhi-tree the Buddha sat — the river bridges the two religious geographies. (Wikipedia, *Phalgu River*)

## V. The vedīs — the piṇḍa-altar circuit

- **The vedī-network** - Gayā-śrāddha is not one offering but a **circuit of *vedīs* (ritual platforms)** spread across the *kṣetra*, at each of which a piṇḍa is offered for a specified class of the dead; completing the prescribed set discharges the rite. (*Vāyu Purāṇa, Gayā-māhātmya*; prayagpandits.com)
- **The number of vedīs [disputed]** - the count is given variously by source and era — flag as an **open-ended, source-divergent roster**:
  - *Variant (45):* the most-cited classical figure — **45 vedīs** in the core Gayā-śrāddha circuit. (prayagpandits.com) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (48 / 54):* pilgrim manuals also give **48** or **54** principal piṇḍa-vedīs as the "classical list." [disputed]
  - *Variant (360+):* the whole *kṣetra* is said to contain **360-odd vedīs / sthals** (the demon's full body), of which the 45/54 are the actively-visited core. (prayagpandits.com) [folk]
- **Principal vedīs (representative, not exhaustive)** - **Viṣṇupada** (the footprint, §III); **Pretaśilā** (hill-rock for the *pretas*, esp. unrited/violent deaths); **Rāmaśilā / Rāmkuṇḍ** (associated with Rāma's visit); **Brahmayoni hill** (the "womb of Brahmā," a liberation summit); **Akṣayavaṭa** (the closing offering, §VI); the **Phalgu bank**; **Mātaṅga-vāpī**, **Gayāśiras**, **Uttara-mānasa / Dakṣiṇa-mānasa**, and **Bhīmgayā / Gadālola** among others. [scholarly]
- **Three-day standard rite** - the full traditional Gayā-śrāddha runs over **(commonly) three days** of bathing, *saṅkalpa*, and offerings at the successive vedīs, concluding at the Akṣayavaṭa; abbreviated one-day versions are also performed. (prayagpandits.com) [scholarly]

## VI. The Akṣayavaṭa of Gayā — the imperishable banyan

- **The Akṣayavaṭa ("undecaying banyan")** - the immortal banyan inside the Vishnupad complex, the **final stop** of the Gayā-śrāddha; offerings made under it are *akṣaya* (inexhaustible in merit). Held to be the tree blessed by Sītā for its truthfulness (§IV). (prayagpandits.com)
- **Site of the gayāwāl's blessing** - the *Gayāwāl/Gayāli* priests issue the formal acknowledgement (a *sankalpa*/receipt) of completed rites here, closing the pilgrim's obligation. [folk]
- **Not the only Akṣayavaṭa** - distinguish from the *Akṣayavaṭa* of **Prayāga (Allahabad)** and of **Kurukṣetra**; each is a separate "undying banyan" in its own *māhātmya*. [scholarly]

## VII. The fourteen generations & the theology of release

- **Fourteen generations, both lines** - the governing claim: Gayā-piṇḍa liberates **seven generations of ancestors above and (or, in another reckoning, fourteen total)** on **both the paternal and maternal lines** — releasing them from rebirth into *pitṛ-loka* or *mokṣa*. (*Garuḍa Purāṇa, Preta-khaṇḍa*; prayagpandits.com)
  - *Variant (count):* commonly stated as **14 generations** (7 ancestral + 7 descendant, or 14 ancestral); some texts say **21**; sources differ — flag the number as roster-divergent. [disputed]
- **Garuḍa Purāṇa, Preta-khaṇḍa** - the canonical death-text describes the *preta*'s post-mortem journey and prescribes śrāddha; it singles out **Gayā** as the place where even a *preta* trapped by an improper death can be delivered, and where the descendant's recurring debt is finally cancelled. (*Garuḍa Purāṇa, Preta-khaṇḍa*)
- **Sapiṇḍī-karaṇa & the end of the debt** - normal śrāddha (*sapiṇḍīkaraṇa*, the rite joining the newly dead to the *pitṛs*) must be repeated annually; the special power of **Gayā** is that it is held to make the offering *once-for-all*, freeing the line from the cyclical obligation — a *mokṣa* for the dead paralleling [Kāśī's mokṣa for the dying](kashi-varanasi.md). (*Garuḍa Purāṇa*; cf. [saṃskāras](../05-concepts-and-dharma/varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md))
- **For the un-rited & the heirless** - Gayā-śrāddha is prescribed for those who died **without proper funeral rites, by violence, or without descendants** (the *pretaśilā* class) — the site that can save the otherwise-unsavable. (*Garuḍa Purāṇa, Preta-khaṇḍa*; prayagpandits.com)

## VIII. Gayā vs. Bodh Gayā — two cities, one river

- **Gayā (Hindu pitṛ-tīrtha)** - the Hindu ancestor-rite city of the **Vishnupad / Phalgu / vedī-circuit**, the subject of this file. (Wikipedia; gaya.nic.in)
- **Bodh Gayā (Buddhist enlightenment site)** - a **distinct town ~12 km south**, the place where **Gautama Buddha attained enlightenment** under the **Bodhi tree** beside the same **Phalgu**; site of the **Mahābodhi Temple** (UNESCO World Heritage). (Wikipedia, *Bodh Gaya*; Britannica)
- **"Twin spiritual cities"** - the two are often paired as the Hindu and Buddhist poles of the **Gayā district**, linked by the Phalgu; do **not** conflate the Vishnupad pitṛ-tīrtha with the Mahābodhi enlightenment site. [scholarly]

## IX. Pitṛ Pakṣa — the ancestral fortnight & the Gayā mela

- **Pitṛ Pakṣa (Mahālaya Pakṣa)** - the **16-lunar-day** dark fortnight (*kṛṣṇa-pakṣa* of Bhādrapada/Āśvina, Sept–Oct) dedicated to the ancestors, when śrāddha and *tarpaṇa* are owed to the *pitṛs*; the most meritorious season to perform piṇḍa-dāna. Closes on **Mahālaya Amāvasyā / Sarvapitṛ Amāvasyā**. (Wikipedia, *Pitru Paksha*; see [festivals & vratas](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md))
- **The Gayā Pitṛ-Pakṣa Melā** - the annual mass pilgrimage when lakhs converge on Gayā for piṇḍa-dāna across the vedī-circuit; the Bihar government coordinates a **~17-day administrative melā** spanning the 16-day fortnight plus margins. (makemyvisit.com; namoastro.com) [scholarly]
- **Tithi-specific offerings** - within the fortnight, the day matching the ancestor's death-*tithi* (or the appropriate general day — e.g. *Mahā-bharaṇī*, *Avidhavā navamī* for married women, *Ghāyala caturdaśī* for the violently-dead) is chosen; Gayā collapses these into its vedī-rite. (Wikipedia, *Pitru Paksha*) [scholarly]

## X. Gayā among the heptads — the Sapta-Purī variant

- **The Māyā = Gayā substitution** - in the standard **Sapta Purī** (seven mokṣa-cities), the seventh after Kāśī/Kāñcī etc. is **Māyā = Māyāpurī = Haridwar**; a **minority/regional reading glosses *Māyā* as *Gayā***, substituting the great pitṛ-tīrtha for the Gaṅgā-gateway. Most authorities reject this and fix Māyā = Haridwar. (*Garuḍa Purāṇa*; see [Sapta Purī](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md#māyā--haridwar)) [disputed]
- **Why the slip is intelligible** - both are Gaṅgā-region liberation cities and both bear a "**footprint of Viṣṇu**" (Har-ki-Pauri at Haridwar; the Viṣṇupada at Gayā), making the names easy to swap in recitation. [folk]
- **Functional place in the network** - whether or not it enters the heptad, Gayā is the unrivalled **death-rite pole** of the [tīrtha network](temple-networks-and-circuits.md), complementing Kāśī (death-liberation), Prayāga (confluence-bathing) and Haridwar (Gaṅgā-gateway). [scholarly]
