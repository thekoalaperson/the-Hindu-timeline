# The 275/276 Paadal Petra Sthalams — Shiva Temples of the Tevaram

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](./README.md) → **Paadal Petra Sthalams**. The Shaiva twin of the 108 [Divya Desams](./temple-networks-and-circuits.md#the-108-divya-desams--abodes-sung-by-the-alvars): the closed set of Shiva temples *sung* into canon by the Nayanar saint-poets in the first seven *Tirumurai*. For the saints themselves see [alvars-and-nayanars](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/alvars-and-nayanars.md); for the wider circuit logic see [temple-networks-and-circuits](./temple-networks-and-circuits.md).

A **Paadal Petra Sthalam** (Tamil *pāḍal peṟṟa talam*, "place that has received a song") is a Shiva shrine glorified in the **Tevaram** — the Shaiva *bhakti* hymn-corpus of the **Moovar** ("the three"): **Appar, Sambandar, and Sundarar**. The act of one of the three composing a full **pathigam** (decad of verses) on a temple is what *canonises* it — exactly parallel to *mangalasasanam* on the Vaishnava side. These are the 6th–9th c. CE devotional clock; the temples' own origin-legends (*sthala-puranas*) reach back to creation, but their entry into this network is firmly a **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** event of recorded Tamil history. [scholarly]

## The count — 275 vs 276

- **275 (commonly cited) / 276 (full canon)** - The figure quoted in older literature and most tour lists is **275**; the standardised modern enumeration (aanmeegam.org, dharisanam) is **276**, after late additions/disambiguations (e.g. **Thiruvidaivai**, **Kiliyanur**) made on the strength of *Devara pathigam* attributions and temple inscriptions (Source: [Wikipedia, *Paadal Petra Sthalam*]; [aanmeegam.org]).
  - *Variant (275 vs 276):* the discrepancy turns on how the non-terrestrial-Tamil "ideal" nodes (**Tirukkayilai**/Kailash, **Tirupparuppatham**/Srisailam) and a few merged or rediscovered shrines are counted; **276** is the full roster, of which the bulk are concretely locatable temples and a handful are mythic/distant (Source: [aanmeegam.org]; [Wikipedia]). [disputed]
- **271 in Tamil Nadu** - The overwhelming majority. The remaining ~5 lie outside the modern state: 2 in Andhra Pradesh, 1 Karnataka, 1 Kerala, 2 in Uttarakhand, 2 in Sri Lanka, 1 Nepal, plus **Tirukkayilai on Mount Kailash** (Tibet) — the exact outside-TN tally shifts with the 275/276 question (Source: [Wikipedia, *Paadal Petra Sthalam*]; [aanmeegam.org]). [disputed]
- **Qualification rule** - At least one of the Moovar must have sung a dedicated *pathigam*; temples merely *named in passing* fall to the secondary **Vaippu Sthalam** tier (see below) — the boundary is doctrinally policed, exactly as **Abhimana Sthalams** sit below the 108 Divya Desams (Source: [Wikipedia]).

## The Moovar and the Tevaram

- **The Tevaram** - "Garland of divine songs"; the first **seven of the twelve Tirumurai** (the Shaiva canon), comprising the hymns of the three. *Tirumurai* 1–3 = Sambandar, 4–6 = Appar, 7 = Sundarar (Source: [Wikipedia, *Tevaram*]; [cultureandheritage.org]).
- **Sambandar** (Tirugnana Sambandar, 7th c.) - Child-prodigy saint of *Tirumurai* 1–3; the largest single contributor of pathigams to the network (Source: [Wikipedia, *Tevaram*]). See [Nayanars](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/alvars-and-nayanars.md).
- **Appar** (Tirunavukkarasar, "king of the tongue", 7th c.) - Former Jain (*Dharmasena*) reconverted to Shaivism; *Tirumurai* 4–6; famed for *uzhavarappadai* (hoe-service) temple cleaning (Source: [Wikipedia, *Appar*]).
- **Sundarar** (Sundaramurti Nayanar, 8th c.) - *Tirumurai* 7; the "friend of Shiva" (*tozhan*); his **Tiruttondar Tokai** ("list of the holy servants") is the seed-list later expanded by Nambiyandar Nambi and Sekkizhar into the **63 Nayanars** (Source: [Wikipedia, *Sundarar*]).
- **Periya Puranam connection** - The hagiography of all 63 Nayanars (incl. the Moovar) is **Sekkizhar's Periya Puranam** (12th c.), itself the **12th Tirumurai**; it narrates many of these temple-visits as miracle events (Source: [Wikipedia, *Periya Puranam*]).

## Moovar ranking — sung by three, two, or one

The hierarchy *within* the set is by how many of the Moovar sang a given temple.

- **Moovar Paadal Petra Sthalam** - Sung by **all three** — the highest prestige. Examples: **Sri Kalahasti** (AP), **Thyagaraja temple, Tiruvotriyur** (Chennai), and the great Chola-Nadu shrines (Source: [srikalahastitemple.org.in]; [Wikipedia, *Thiruvotriyur Temple*]).
- **Two-singer temples** - Sung by two of the three; a recognised middle band.
- **One-singer temples** - A single Moovar's pathigam suffices for entry; the most numerous category, and the source of many of the obscure rural shrines.
- **Padikasu significance** - At **Tiruvarur** (Thyagaraja temple), Sundarar's intimate "friend-of-Shiva" status is dramatised by the *padikasu* (the daily gold coin Shiva is said to have granted him); the Tiruvarur–Sundarar bond marks it as the affective heart of the Sundarar corpus (Source: [Wikipedia, *Sundarar*]; Periya Puranam). [folk]

## Region-wise — the Tevara Nadus

The canon is classified by the medieval **Nadu** (territorial) divisions, not modern districts. Counts below follow the aanmeegam.org/dharisanam 276-tabulation and vary by ±1–2 across lists. [scholarly]

### Chola Nadu — Vadakarai (north of the Kaveri) — ~63

- **Densest non-southern band** - The temples on the **north bank** of the Kaveri, the Chola heartland's upper half (Source: [aanmeegam.org]; [dharisanam.com]).
- **Tiruvaiyaru, Tiruppananthal, Tiruvidaimarudur** - Among the major Vadakarai shrines, with full Moovar attention.

### Chola Nadu — Thenkarai (south of the Kaveri) — ~128

- **The largest single region** - Nearly half the entire canon sits on the **south bank** of the Kaveri, the temple-saturated Thanjavur–Mayiladuthurai–Nagapattinam belt (Source: [aanmeegam.org]; [dharisanam.com]).
- **Chidambaram (Thillai)** - The *akasha* (ether) Pancha Bhuta Sthalam and Shaiva-Siddhanta epicentre; sung by all three (Source: [Wikipedia]). See [Pancha Bhuta Sthalams](./temple-networks-and-circuits.md).
- **Tiruvarur** - Thyagaraja temple, Sundarar's home shrine and *padikasu* site (above).
- **Sirkazhi (Brahmapureeswarar)** - Birthplace of **Sambandar**; a foundational node of the corpus (Source: [Wikipedia, *Sirkazhi*]).
- **Vaitheeswaran Koil, Mayiladuthurai, Vedaranyam** - Other major Thenkarai shrines, each with multiple pathigams.

### Thondai Nadu — ~33

- **The northern Tamil region** (Kanchipuram–Chennai–Tiruvallur belt) (Source: [aanmeegam.org]).
- **Ekambareswarar, Kanchipuram** - The *prithvi* (earth) Pancha Bhuta Sthalam.
- **Tiruvotriyur (Thyagaraja)** - Moovar Paadal Petra Sthalam in north Chennai (Source: [Wikipedia]).
- **Tiruvanmiyur, Tiruvalitayam, Marundeeswarar (Thiruvanmiyur)** - Further Thondai-Nadu shrines.

### Nadu Nadu — ~24

- **The "middle country"** between Chola and Thondai Nadu (Cuddalore–Villupuram belt) (Source: [aanmeegam.org]).
- **Tiruvennainallur, Tirukkovilur, Tirupathiripuliyur (Padaleeswarar, Cuddalore)** - Principal Nadu-Nadu temples; Tiruvennainallur is where Sundarar was "claimed" by Shiva as bondsman (the *tozhan* episode) (Source: Periya Puranam). [folk]

### Pandya Nadu — ~14

- **The deep south** (Madurai–Tirunelveli) (Source: [aanmeegam.org]).
- **Meenakshi Amman / Madurai (Sundareswarar)** - The Pandya capital shrine, focus of the Sambandar–Jain confrontation legends (Source: [Wikipedia, *Madurai*]). [folk]
- **Tirunelveli (Nellaiappar), Tiruchendur, Tirupparankunram** - Other Pandya-Nadu Paadal Petra temples.

### Kongu Nadu — ~7

- **The western uplands** (Coimbatore–Erode–Karur) (Source: [aanmeegam.org]).
- **Avinashi, Tiruchengode, Karur Pasupatheeswarar** - The handful of Kongu shrines that received pathigams.

### Malai Nadu (Kerala) — 1

- **Tiruvanchikkulam (Kerala)** - The lone Kerala node, near Kodungallur; associated with **Cheraman Perumal Nayanar** (the Chera king-saint and Sundarar's companion) (Source: [aanmeegam.org]; [Wikipedia]).

## Vada Nadu and Ila Nadu — the non-Tamil nodes

The "northern lands" (*Vada Nadu*) and "the Ila/Eelam land" (Sri Lanka) hold the ideal and far-flung sites — several sung *without the saints physically travelling there*, on report or in vision. These are the geography-stretching boundary of the canon. [disputed]

- **Tirukkayilai (Mount Kailash, Tibet)** - Shiva's own abode (Kailasanathar); the cosmic apex node, sung as the source rather than a visited temple — central to the 275-vs-276 dispute (Source: [Wikipedia]; [lordshiva276templepaadalpetrasthalam.blogspot.com]). See [Meru, Kailasa & cosmic mountains](./meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md).
- **Tirukkedaram (Kedarnath, Uttarakhand)** - The Himalayan *jyotirlinga*; counted in Vada Nadu, often paired with nearby **Gaurikund** as a second Uttarakhand node (Source: [aanmeegam.org]). See [12 Jyotirlingas](./12-jyotirlingas.md).
- **Tirupparuppatham / Tiruppariyalur — Srisailam (Mallikarjuna, AP)** - The *jyotirlinga* of the Nallamala hills, in the Tamil hymns under the name *Tirupparuppatam* (Source: [Wikipedia]; [aanmeegam.org]). See [12 Jyotirlingas](./12-jyotirlingas.md).
- **Tirukkachi-mel-thali / Kashi (Varanasi)** - The pan-Indian Shiva city (Vishwanath) is invoked in the Tevaram as the supreme *kshetra*; its precise enumeration in the 276 is list-dependent (Source: [aanmeegam.org]). See [Sapta Puri](./sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md). [disputed]
- **Tirukkalahasti / Srikalahasti (AP)** - The *vayu* (air/wind) Pancha Bhuta Sthalam; a celebrated **Moovar** temple (all three sang it) in the Tamil-adjacent Rayalaseema region (Source: [srikalahastitemple.org.in]; [Wikipedia]).
- **Thiruketheeswaram (Mannar, Sri Lanka)** - One of the **Pancha Ishwarams** of Sri Lanka; the chief Ila-Nadu node, sung by Sambandar and Sundarar (Source: [Wikipedia, *Ketheeswaram*]; [aanmeegam.org]).
  - *Variant:* some 276-lists add **Thiruppanthurai / Koneswaram (Trincomalee, Sri Lanka)** as the second Eelam shrine, giving two Sri Lankan nodes (Source: [aanmeegam.org]; [dharisanam.com]). [disputed]
- **Nepal node** - One Wikipedia tabulation places a single Nepal temple in the wider count; identification is contested and absent from many lists (Source: [Wikipedia, *Paadal Petra Sthalam*]). [disputed][late text]

## Vaippu Sthalams — the secondary tier

- **Vaippu Sthalam** ("placed/mentioned site") - Shiva shrines **named in passing** in the Tevaram but lacking a dedicated pathigam — the explicit "not-quite-Paadal-Petra" band, the Shaiva analogue of the Vaishnava **Abhimana Sthalams**. Estimates run to ~270+ such mentioned places, partly overlapping the main list (Source: [Bharatpedia, *Vaippu Sthalam*]; [Wikipedia]). [scholarly]
- **Function** - They show the network's edge is patrolled: being *remembered* in a hymn is not the same as being *sung*; only a full decad confers Paadal Petra status (Source: [Wikipedia]).

## Festival and living-cult significance

- **Pancha Bhuta cluster overlap** - Four of the five **[Pancha Bhuta Sthalams](./temple-networks-and-circuits.md)** (earth-Kanchipuram, fire-Tiruvannamalai, water-Tiruvanaikaval, ether-Chidambaram) are Paadal Petra temples; the fifth, air-Kalahasti, is the AP Moovar node — embedding the elemental scheme inside the song-canon (Source: [Wikipedia, *Pancha Bhuta Sthalam*]).
- **Tirumurai recitation (Oduvar tradition)** - In Paadal Petra temples the original Tevaram pathigam for *that* shrine is chanted daily/at festivals by hereditary **Oduvars**, keeping the 7th–9th c. text liturgically live (Source: [Wikipedia, *Tevaram*]).
- **Padikasu and processional honour** - Temples with strong Sundarar/Sambandar association (Tiruvarur, Sirkazhi) foreground that saint in the *brahmotsavam* iconography and procession order; the saint who *sang* the temple is honoured within it (Source: Periya Puranam; [Wikipedia]). [folk]
