# Śṛṅgaverapura — Guha & the Gaṅgā Crossing

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](README.md) → **Śṛṅgaverapura / Guha's Crossing**. The Niṣāda riverside town on the **Gaṅgā** just upstream of [Prayāga](prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md), where the tribal boatman-king **Guha** ferried **Rāma, Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa** across the river on the first night of the *vana-vāsa* (exile) — the tradition's foremost shrine to a tribal/low-status devotee and a touchstone of inclusive *bhakti*.

## Identity & names

- **Śṛṅgaverapura (शृङ्गवेरपुर)** — the Niṣāda capital-town on the bank of the **Gaṅgā**, named in the *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* as the first major halt of Rāma's exile after leaving [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md); the name is glossed as "the city of the *śṛṅgavera*" (ginger / horn-shaped ornament). (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 50)
- **Singraur / Singhraur** — the surviving toponym of the modern village (Singraur, ~35–40 km northwest of [Prayagraj](prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md) on the Gaṅgā), continuous with the ancient Sanskrit name and the locus of the archaeological site. [scholarly]
- **A Niṣāda kṣetra on the exile route** — Śṛṅgaverapura is the canonical second stage of the *vana-yātrā* (Ayodhyā → Tamasā → Gomatī/Sandhya → **Śṛṅgaverapura on the Gaṅgā** → across to the Prayāga side → **Bharadvāja's āśrama at Prayāga** → Citrakūṭa). (*Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 50–54)

## Guha — the Niṣāda king and Rāma's friend

- **Guha, lord of the Niṣādas** — **Guha** (Guhaka) is the chief/king of the **Niṣādas** — a forest/fisher-boatman people, ranked outside the four *varṇas* — and an old, loving friend (*sakhā*, *priya*) of Rāma; learning of Rāma's arrival he comes out with his kinsmen and gifts. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 50)
- **"You are my fifth brother"** — Rāma greets Guha warmly and Guha offers his whole kingdom, food and lodging; in Tulsīdās's retelling Rāma embraces him as a brother, and Guha is counted in devotional tradition among Rāma's intimate companions (with **Sugrīva, Vibhīṣaṇa, Hanumān**). (*Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 50; *Rāmcaritmānas, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa, Kevaṭ/Guha prasaṅg*)
- **The vow of the forester & the night-watch** — Rāma, holding to ascetic discipline, declines cooked food and beds, taking only water and the horses' fodder; through that first night **Lakṣmaṇa keeps vigil** with Guha by the sleeping Rāma and Sītā, the two conversing in grief over Rāma's banishment. (*Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 50–52)

## Maṭṭing the hair — the vaṭa-kṣīra rite

- **The jaṭā made with banyan-sap** — at Śṛṅgaverapura Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa renounce princely grooming: they call for the milky sap (*kṣīra*) of the **nyagrodha / vaṭa** (banyan) and with it mat their hair into the ascetic's **jaṭā** (matted locks), formally assuming the *tāpasa* (forest-hermit) form for the exile. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 52)
- **The threshold of vana-vāsa** — this *jaṭā-bandhana* marks the visual and ritual transition from prince to forest-dweller; Śṛṅgaverapura is thus the place where Rāma "becomes" the exiled ascetic of the rest of the epic. [scholarly]

## The Gaṅgā crossing — the ferrying

- **The crossing of the Gaṅgā** — at dawn Rāma asks Guha to summon a sturdy boat; Guha calls his kinsmen and a stout ferry-boat, and the three — Rāma, Sītā, Lakṣmaṇa — are rowed across the **Gaṅgā** to the southern (Prayāga-ward) bank, whence they proceed to **Bharadvāja's āśrama** near the [Saṅgam](prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md). (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 52)
- **Sītā's prayer to Gaṅgā** — midstream, **Sītā** salutes the river-goddess **Gaṅgā**, vowing offerings on the safe return of her lord from exile — a vow recalled when the family does return. (*Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 52)
- **Sumantra turned back** — the charioteer **Sumantra**, who had driven the exiles this far, is here sent back to Ayodhyā with the chariot; the crossing of the Gaṅgā is the point of no return, beyond which Rāma travels on foot. (*Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 52)

### The Kevaṭ-prasaṅg — Tulsīdās's boatman

- **The boatman who washed Rāma's feet** — in the **Rāmcaritmānas**, the ferrying is dramatized as the famous **Kevaṭ** (boatman) episode: the *kevaṭ*, knowing the dust of Rāma's feet had once turned the stone Ahalyā back into a woman, refuses to launch his boat until he has **washed Rāma's feet**, lest his wooden boat too be transformed into a person — half wit, half utter devotion. (*Rāmcaritmānas, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa, Kevaṭ-prasaṅg*) [late text]
  - *Variant (relation of Kevaṭ and Guha):* Tulsīdās's **Kevaṭ** (the humble boatman) and Vālmīki's **Guha** (the Niṣāda king) are treated in some retellings as **one figure** (Guha himself rowing the boat) and in others as **distinct** (Guha the chief, the *kevaṭ* his ferryman); the corpus keeps both readings rather than collapsing them. [folk] [disputed]
- **Foot-washing as the bhakti climax** — the *kevaṭ* drinks the foot-wash water (*caraṇāmṛta*) and asks no fare but Rāma's grace; the episode is recited as a model of *dāsya/prema-bhakti*, the lowliest devotee winning the Lord by love. (*Rāmcaritmānas*) [folk]
- **The return passage** — on Rāma's victorious return from Laṅkā the *Rāmcaritmānas* and folk tradition have the boatman/Guha again receive the party at the Gaṅgā, completing the friendship arc. (*Rāmcaritmānas, Uttara-kāṇḍa*; folk) [folk]

## Bharata at Śṛṅgaverapura — Guha's later role

- **Guha meets the grieving Bharata** — when **Bharata** marches to the forest to bring Rāma home, his army halts at Śṛṅgaverapura; Guha, ever loyal, first **suspects Bharata of pursuing Rāma with hostile intent**, arming his Niṣādas, then is reassured by Bharata's evident grief and devotion. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 84–85)
- **Guha shows the place of Rāma's night** — Guha guides Bharata, points out the spot beneath the tree where Rāma and Sītā had slept on grass, and shows the blades of *kuśa* and the marks of their bed — moving Bharata to fresh lament; Guha then helps ferry Bharata's host across the Gaṅgā toward Bharadvāja. (*Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 85–86)
- **The Niṣāda as keeper of the tīrtha** — in these chapters Guha functions as the **custodian of the crossing**, the local lord who knows and guards the river-passage of the *Rāmāyaṇa* — fixing Śṛṅgaverapura in the epic's memory as *Guha's* place. (*Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 84–86)

## Inclusive bhakti — the foremost shrine to a tribal devotee

- **Honoring the outcaste devotee** — Śṛṅgaverapura is celebrated as the tradition's premier site venerating a **tribal / low-status (Niṣāda) bhakta**: Rāma's open friendship with Guha, an *a-varṇa* forester, is read across Vaiṣṇava and reform traditions as scriptural warrant that **devotion overrides birth** — paired with Rāma's acceptance of **Śabarī's** berries and the *kevaṭ*'s love. [scholarly]
- **A pilgrimage of equality** — the *ghāṭ* at Singraur, with its **Rāma–Guha** and **Śṛṅgi Ṛṣi** associations, draws Niṣāda/fisher communities especially, who claim Guha as ancestor-king and patron; the site anchors a self-conscious "**Nishad**" Rāma-bhakti identity. [regional] [folk]
- **The Śṛṅgi Ṛṣi layer** — a parallel etymology ties the town to the sage **Ṛṣyaśṛṅga (Śṛṅgi Ṛṣi)**, the "horn-born" ṛṣi who performed Daśaratha's **putra-kāmeṣṭi/aśvamedha** that brought about Rāma's birth — so local tradition links the place both to Rāma's *birth* (via the ṛṣi) and his *exile* (via Guha). [regional] [disputed]

## The archaeological site — two clocks

- **Singraur excavations** — Śṛṅgaverapura survives as a real, excavated mound at **Singraur** on the Gaṅgā in Prayagraj district; excavations (notably by **B. B. Lal / ASI, 1977–1986**) on the *Archaeology of the Rāmāyaṇa Sites* project exposed continuous occupation and a large **brick water-tank / hydraulic complex** dated to roughly the **1st century BCE–1st century CE (Śuṅga–Kuṣāṇa horizon)**. [scholarly] [historical]
- **The Rāmāyaṇa-sites project** — Singraur was one of the five *Rāmāyaṇa*-associated sites (with Ayodhyā, Bharadvāja-āśrama/Prayāga, Nandigrāma, Citrakūṭa) examined to test the geography of the epic's exile route against the archaeological record. [scholarly]
- **Two-clocks distinction** — *scripturally*, Śṛṅgaverapura belongs to the **Tretā-yuga** events of Rāma's exile, on the sacred clock of the *yugas*; *historically*, the excavated settlement and its tank belong to the last centuries BCE / early CE — the two clocks should not be conflated, the dig dating the **continuity of a sacred place**, not the dating of the avatāra. [scholarly]
