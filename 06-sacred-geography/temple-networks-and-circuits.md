# Temple Networks & Pilgrimage Circuits

> Sacred geography of the cosmos: the *structured* temple sets — closed numbered circuits (108, 275, 12, 8, 6, 5…) sung by saint-poets or codified in *sthala-puranas*, each a complete pilgrimage geometry to be walked as a whole. They map the deities of [02-deities](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md) onto the *tirtha* layer of [Bharata as punya-bhumi](README.md), most of them crystallised in the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** even where their origin myths reach back to creation.
>
> Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md) · Related: [12 Jyotirlingas](12-jyotirlingas.md) · [51 Shakti Peethas](51-shakti-peethas.md) · [Char Dham](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) · [Sapta Puri](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md)

A *circuit* (*yatra*) differs from a loose holy site: it is a **fixed, numbered set** treated as one organism — the pilgrimage is incomplete until all nodes are visited, often in a prescribed order returning to the start. The numbering is canon (108, 275, 12…) and itself sacred. The two great Tamil canons — the Vaishnava **Naalayira Divya Prabandham** (Alvars) and the Shaiva **Tevaram** (Nayanars) — between them define the densest temple networks on earth (5th–9th c. CE). North-Indian circuits (Pancha Kedar/Badri, Ashtavinayaka) rest instead on *sthala-purana* legend mapped onto the Himalaya and the Deccan.

## Vaishnava Networks

### The 108 Divya Desams — abodes sung by the Alvars

The *Divya Desam* ("divine/holy place") is a Vishnu (with consort Lakshmi/Sri) shrine **glorified in the hymns of the twelve [Alvars](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md)** (poet-saints, c. 5th–9th c. CE) collected in the *Naalayira Divya Prabandham* (4,000 verses). To qualify, the main shrine must be Vishnu and an adjoining shrine Lakshmi; the act of an Alvar singing a temple is *mangalasasanam* ("auspicious benediction") and is what canonises it (Source: [Wikipedia, *Divya Desam*]; [divyadesam.com]).

- **The count: 108** - 106 terrestrial + 2 non-earthly. Of the 106 earthly, **105 in India, 1 in Nepal** (Muktinath/Saligramam) (Source: [Wikipedia, *Divya Desam*]).
- **The 2 celestial Desams** - **Tiruparkadal** (*Kshira Sagara*, the Ocean of Milk where Vishnu reclines on Shesha) and **Vaikuntha** (*Paramapadam*, the supreme abode) — not on earth, reached only after liberation (Source: [Wikipedia; iskconbangalore.co.in]). [traditional]
- **Indian distribution** - Tamil Nadu ~84, Kerala 11, Andhra Pradesh 2, Uttar Pradesh 4, Uttarakhand 3, Gujarat 1 (Source: [Wikipedia, *Divya Desam*]).
  - *Variant (counting):* exact per-state tallies vary by 1–2 across lists depending on how border/merged shrines (e.g. the Tirunaraiyur cluster) are counted; the total 108 is fixed.
- **Srirangam (Tiruvarangam)** - The foremost (*Bhuloka Vaikuntham*), largest functioning temple complex in the world, Ranganatha reclining; head of the [Srivaishnava](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md) tradition of Ramanuja (Source: [templepurohit.com]).
- **Tirumala–Tirupati (Venkateshwara)** - The single most-visited Divya Desam (and temple worldwide by footfall); Andhra Pradesh (Source: [oneindiaonline.com]).
- **Northern Divya Desams** - **Badrinath**, **Naimisharanya**, **Ayodhya**, **Mathura**, **Dwarka**, **Salagrama/Muktinath (Nepal)** — the circuit reaches the Himalaya and the pan-Indian *yatra* axis, overlapping the [Char Dham](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) and [Sapta Puri](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md) (Source: [Wikipedia, *Divya Desam*]).
- **The Alvars** - Twelve saint-poets (Poigai, Bhutham, Pey, Tirumazhisai, Nammalvar, Madhurakavi, Kulasekhara, Periyalvar, Andal [the one woman], Tondaradippodi, Tiruppan, Tirumangai); **Tirumangai Alvar** sang the most Desams (Source: [Wikipedia; radhakrishnatemple.net]).

### Abhimana Sthalams & 108 vs wider sets [scholarly]

- **Abhimana Sthalams** - Important Vishnu temples *not* in the canonical 108 (not sung by an Alvar, or only obliquely) yet held in high esteem — a recognised secondary tier showing the network's boundary is doctrinally policed (Source: [Wikipedia, *Divya Desam*]).

## Shaiva Networks

### The 275 Paadal Petra Sthalams — temples praised in the Tevaram

The Shaiva counterpart to the Divya Desams: Shiva temples **sung in the *Tevaram*** (the first seven *Tirumurai*) by the Nayanar saint-poets **Appar, Sambandar, and Sundarar** (the *Moovar*, "the three"), 6th–9th c. CE. A temple qualifies when at least one of the three composed a full *pathigam* (decad) on it (Source: [Wikipedia, *Paadal Petra Sthalam*]; [aanmeegam.org]).

- **The count: 275 (commonly) / 276 (full)** - The widely-cited figure is **275**; the complete enumeration including one additional/contested shrine gives **276** (Source: [Wikipedia; hindudevotionalblog.com]).
  - *Variant (275 vs 276):* the discrepancy turns on whether *Tirukkayilai* (Kailash) and *Tirupparuppatham* (Srisailam) — non-terrestrial-Tamil sites — are both counted, and on one merged shrine; the canonical figure is usually given as **276** Paadal Petra, of which 275 are concretely locatable (Source: [aanmeegam.org]).
- **Distribution** - 271 in Tamil Nadu, the remaining ~5 outside it — including **Tirukkayilai (Mount Kailash)** and **Tirukkedaram (Kedarnath)**, **Tiruppariyalur/Srisailam**, and sites in the north (Source: [Wikipedia, *Paadal Petra Sthalam*]).
- **The Moovar** - **Appar** (Tirunavukkarasar, 7th c.), **Sambandar** (Tirugnana Sambandar, 7th c.), **Sundarar** (Sundaramurthi, 8th c.) — three of the **63 [Nayanars](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md)** whose hagiography is the *Periya Puranam* (Source: [Wikipedia; cultureandheritage.org]).
- **Moovar / two / one** - Temples sung by all three (*Moovar pathigam*) rank highest; many have only one or two singers (Source: [tirikala.com]).
- **Vaippu Sthalams** - The secondary tier: shrines merely *mentioned* in the Tevaram without a dedicated *pathigam* — the explicit "not-quite-275" category, parallel to the Vaishnava Abhimana Sthalams (Source: [Bharatpedia, *Vaippu Sthalam*]). [scholarly]

### The Pancha Bhuta Sthalams — the five elements

Five Shiva temples, each enshrining the *linga* as one of the **five great elements (*pancha mahabhuta*)**; all are also Paadal Petra Sthalams. Four in Tamil Nadu, one in Andhra, clustered between 78°–79°E (Source: [Wikipedia, *Pancha Bhuta Sthalam*]; [artofliving.org]).

- **Prithvi (Earth)** - **Ekambareswarar**, Kanchipuram (TN) — a *prithvi linga* of sand (Source: [Wikipedia, *Pancha Bhuta Sthalam*]).
- **Appu / Jala (Water)** - **Jambukeswarar**, Thiruvanaikaval / Tiruvanaikoil (TN) — an *appu linga* with a perennial underground spring in the sanctum (Source: same).
- **Agni (Fire)** - **Arunachaleswarar**, Tiruvannamalai (TN) — the fire-hill; site of *Karthigai Deepam* and the [Lingodbhava](12-jyotirlingas.md) fire-pillar legend (Source: same).
- **Vayu (Air)** - **Srikalahasti**, Andhra Pradesh — the *vayu linga*, lamp-flame ever-flickering (the one non-TN node) (Source: same).
- **Akasha (Ether/Sky)** - **Thillai Nataraja (Chidambaram)** (TN) — the *akasha linga*, the formless "Chidambara Rahasyam" empty space, home of the cosmic dance of Nataraja (Source: [Wikipedia; 99pandit.com]).

### Arupadai Veedu — the six abodes of Murugan

Six hill/shore temples of **Murugan (Kartikeya/Skanda)**, the six *padai veedu* ("war camps/abodes"), eulogised in the Sangam-era *Thirumurugatrupadai* (Nakkeerar) and the *Thiruppugazh* (Arunagirinathar). Their order is fixed in tradition (Source: [Simple Wikipedia, *Arupadai Veedu*]; [arupadai.org]).

- **Thiruparankundram** (near Madurai) - 1st abode; Murugan's marriage to **Deivanai** after slaying **Surapadman** (Source: [arupadai.org]).
- **Tiruchendur** (shore, Bay of Bengal) - The only seaside abode; the battlefield where Surapadman fell (Source: same).
- **Palani (Pazhani)** - Murugan as **Dandayudhapani**, the renunciate who relinquished the cosmic mango/jnana-pazham in pique — the "pride-shedding" abode (Source: [tarakesh.com]).
- **Swamimalai** (near Kumbakonam) - Where the child Murugan taught the meaning of **Om (Pranava)** to his father Shiva (*Swaminatha*) (Source: [arupadai.org]).
- **Thiruthani** (near Chennai) - 5th abode; the peace after the wars (Source: same).
- **Pazhamudircholai** (near Madurai) - Forest abode; Murugan's riddle-play blessing the poetess **Avvaiyar** (Source: same).
  - *Variant (folk extension):* popular Tamil devotion adds further "abodes" (e.g. **Kundrathur**, **Marudhamalai**, the Sri Lankan **Kataragama**) as honorary *padai veedu*, but the canonical Sangam set is strictly **six** (Source: [murugan.org]). [folk]

### The Navagraha Temples — the nine planets (Kumbakonam cluster)

Nine Shiva temples around **Kumbakonam–Mayiladuthurai (Thanjavur–Mayiladuthurai belt), TN**, each with a sub-shrine to one of the **Navagraha** (nine "graspers"/planetary deities); pilgrims circuit all nine to remedy astrological afflictions (*dosha*). The presiding deity is usually Shiva, the graha a secondary shrine (Source: [Wikipedia, *Navagraha temples in Tamil Nadu*]).

- **Surya (Sun)** - **Suryanar Kovil** (Suryanar Koil, near Kumbakonam) — the rare temple where the planet itself is the *main* deity (Source: [Wikipedia]).
- **Chandra (Moon)** - **Kailasanathar**, Thingalur (Source: same).
- **Angaraka / Chevvai (Mars)** - **Vaitheeswaran Koil** (Vaitheeswaran, the "divine healer") (Source: same).
- **Budha (Mercury)** - **Swetharanyeswarar**, Thiruvenkadu (Source: same).
- **Guru / Brihaspati (Jupiter)** - **Apatsahayesvarar**, Alangudi (Source: same).
- **Shukra (Venus)** - **Agneeswarar**, Kanjanur (Source: same).
- **Shani (Saturn)** - **Dharbaranyeswarar**, Tirunallar (Karaikal/Puducherry) — the most-thronged, for Saturn-remedy (Source: [Wikipedia; godigit.com]).
- **Rahu (N. lunar node)** - **Naganathar**, Tirunageswaram (Source: [Wikipedia]).
- **Ketu (S. lunar node)** - **Naganathaswamy**, Keezhaperumpallam (Source: same).
  - *Variant (Rahu/Ketu mythic origin):* the nodes are the severed head/body of the asura **Svarbhanu** from the [Samudra Manthana](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md), not true planets — a key distinction the circuit preserves (Source: [Wikipedia, *Navagraha*]). [traditional]
- **Navagraha order** - Circuit is walked in *graha* sequence (Surya → Ketu); some traditions prescribe visiting Suryanar Kovil first and returning there (Source: [navagrahatemple.com]).

## Ganesha Network — the Ashtavinayaka

The **eight self-manifest (*swayambhu*) Ganesha shrines of Maharashtra** (around Pune, Raigad, Ahmednagar), each with a distinct idol, trunk-curl, and *sthala-purana*; the *Ashtavinayaka Yatra* begins and ends at Morgaon (Source: [Wikipedia, *Ashtavinayaka*]; [ashtavinayakyatra.in]).

- **1. Moreshwar / Mayureshwar**, Morgaon - The *adi* and final node; Ganesha as peacock-rider who slew the demon Sindhu (Source: [myoksha.com]).
- **2. Siddhivinayak**, Siddhatek - Where Vishnu gained *siddhi* (perfection) to defeat Madhu-Kaitabha; the only one with a right-curled trunk (Source: same).
- **3. Ballaleshwar**, Pali - Named for the boy-devotee **Ballal**; the only Ashtavinayak named after a devotee (Source: [rudraksha-ratna.com]).
- **4. Varadvinayak**, Mahad - The boon-giver (*varada*); a perpetually-lit lamp (Source: [myoksha.com]).
- **5. Chintamani**, Theur - Recovered the wish-gem *Chintamani*; Ganesha who removes anxiety (*chinta*) (Source: same).
- **6. Girijatmaj**, Lenyadri - The only cave-temple (Buddhist-era rock-cut caves); Ganesha as son of **Girija (Parvati)** (Source: same).
- **7. Vighnahar / Vigneshwar**, Ozar (Ojhar) - Slayer of the demon **Vighnasura**; gold-domed (Source: same).
- **8. Mahaganapati**, Ranjangaon - The most powerful form, ten/twenty-armed; where Shiva worshipped Ganesha before fighting Tripurasura (Source: [Wikipedia, *Ashtavinayaka*]).
  - *Variant (yatra order):* the standard sequence is Morgaon → Siddhatek → Pali → Mahad → Theur → Lenyadri → Ozar → Ranjangaon → back to Morgaon; some itineraries reorder by geography for convenience, but liturgical completion requires the return to Morgaon (Source: [ashtavinayakyatra.in; myoksha.com]).

## Himalayan Shaiva–Vaishnava Twin Circuits (Uttarakhand)

### Pancha Kedar — five fragments of Shiva's bull-body

Five high Garhwal-Himalaya Shiva temples; the **Pandavas**, seeking absolution after Kurukshetra, pursued Shiva, who fled as a bull (*Nandi*) and dove into the earth, re-emerging in five parts (Source: [Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*]).

- **Kedarnath** (3,583 m) - The **hump** (*pitha*); also one of the [12 Jyotirlingas](12-jyotirlingas.md) and a [Chota Char Dham](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) node — the supreme Kedar (Source: [Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*]).
- **Tungnath** (3,680 m) - The **arms/bahu**; highest Shiva temple in the world (Source: same).
- **Rudranath** (3,559 m) - The **face/mukha**; worshipped as Nilkanth (Source: same).
- **Madhyamaheshwar (Madmaheshwar)** (3,490 m) - The **navel and stomach (*nabhi*)** (Source: same).
- **Kalpeshwar** (2,200 m) - The **hair/jata**; the only one accessible year-round (Source: same).
- **Pilgrimage order** - Strict sequence Kedarnath → Tungnath → Rudranath → Madhyamaheshwar → Kalpeshwar; capped by a closing visit to Badrinath (Vishnu) as affirmation (Source: [Wikipedia; chardham-pilgrimage-tour.com]).

### Pancha Badri — five abodes of Vishnu (Badri)

The Vaishnava twin to Pancha Kedar; five Vishnu shrines extending the **Badrinath** dimension across the Alaknanda/Garhwal region (Source: [Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*]; [mountainiax.com]).

- **Badrinath (Badri Vishal)** - The chief; a [Char Dham](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) and [Chota Char Dham](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) node and a [Divya Desam](#the-108-divya-desams--abodes-sung-by-the-alvars) (Source: [mountainiax.com]).
- **Adi Badri** - The "original" Badri, a cluster of ancient (Gupta-era attributed) shrines near Karnaprayag (Source: same).
- **Vridha Badri** - The "old/aged" Badri, where Vishnu is said to have first appeared (Source: same).
- **Yogadhyan (Yoga-Dhyan) Badri**, Pandukeshwar - Vishnu in meditative pose; winter seat where Badrinath's worship moves off-season (Source: same).
- **Bhavishya Badri** - The "future" Badri; tradition holds it will become the principal Badri shrine when the present route to Badrinath is destroyed at the end of the age (Source: [mountainiax.com]). [traditional]
  - *Variant (membership):* lists occasionally substitute or add **Ardha Badri** among the five; the core Badrinath–Adi–Vridha–Yogadhyan–Bhavishya set is standard (Source: [Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*]).

## How the Networks Interlock

- **Multi-membership nodes** - Single temples belong to several circuits at once: **Rameshwaram** = Char Dham + Jyotirlinga + (Setu) tirtha; **Kedarnath** = Pancha Kedar + Jyotirlinga + Chota Char Dham; **Badrinath** = Char Dham + Pancha Badri + Divya Desam; **Tiruvannamalai** = Pancha Bhuta + Paadal Petra. These overlaps are deliberate — they bind the sub-networks into one mesh.
- **Canon by saint vs canon by legend** - The southern sets (Divya Desam, Paadal Petra) are defined *textually* (a saint sang it); the northern/Deccan sets (Pancha Kedar, Ashtavinayaka, Navagraha) are defined by *sthala-purana legend* mapped onto a region.
- **Secondary tiers are policed** - Each major network names its "almost-in" class — **Abhimana Sthalam** (Vaishnava), **Vaippu Sthalam** (Shaiva) — proving the numbered count is doctrinally guarded, not casual. [scholarly]
