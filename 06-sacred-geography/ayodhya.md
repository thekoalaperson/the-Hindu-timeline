# Ayodhyā — Birthplace of Rāma (Sākēta Kṣetra)

> The eldest **mokṣa-purī**: the "unconquerable" (*a-yodhyā*, "not to be warred against") capital of the **Sūryavaṃśa / Ikṣvāku** dynasty on the **Sarayū**, founded by **Manu Vaivasvata**, birthplace of **Rāma** the seventh [avatāra of Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md), and the city that walked into the river behind its king at the end of the *Rāmāyaṇa*. The dedicated *kṣetra* node: founding & etymology, the descent of the Sarayū, the *Rāmāyaṇa* city-plan, Rāma's *jala-samādhi*, the **Skanda Purāṇa Ayodhyā-māhātmya** and its *parikrama* circuits, and Ayodhya as **Divya Deśam** and **sapta-purī** seat.

## I. Founding — Manu Vaivasvata & the Sūryavaṃśa capital

- **Founded by Manu Vaivasvata** - the city is held to have been laid out by **Vaivasvata Manu** (son of **Sūrya/Vivasvat**, the progenitor of the current [7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara](../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md)) on the south bank of the **Sarayū**, as the capital of the **Kosala** kingdom. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 1.5; *Skanda Purāṇa, Ayodhyā-māhātmya*)
  - *Variant (Brahmā / Vishnu pre-foundation):* in the *Ayodhyā-māhātmya* the *kṣetra* itself is primordial — laid out by **Brahmā** or resting on **Viṣṇu's discus (Cakra-tīrtha)** before Manu's terrestrial city — so Manu *settles* an already-sacred ground rather than creating it ex nihilo. [late text]
- **Seat of the Ikṣvāku / Sūryavaṃśa line** - capital of the **Solar dynasty** descending Manu → Ikṣvāku → ... → Raghu (whence *Rāghava*) → Aja → Daśaratha → **Rāma**; the city's prestige is the prestige of the *raghu-vaṃśa* kings who upheld *dharma* through Vedic sacrifice. (*Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa*; *Raghuvaṃśa* of Kālidāsa) — see [Rāmāyaṇa lineage](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/README.md)
- **Kosala province** - Ayodhya is the *rāja-dhānī* of **Kosala**, the great trans-Sarayū kingdom of the eastern Gangetic plain; "Ayodhya" names the city, "Kosala" the realm. (*Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 1.5)

## II. The name — "unconquerable," and the parallel names

- **Ayodhyā = "not to be warred against"** - from *a-* (negative) + *yodhya* ("to be fought / assailable," from √*yudh*, "to fight"): the **impregnable** city no enemy can take — a fortress-name encoding its towering gates, moats and standing garrisons in the *Rāmāyaṇa*'s description. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa*; Wikipedia, *Ayodhya (Ramayana)*)
- **Sākēta** - the classical / Sanskrit-Pali twin-name of the city (used in Buddhist and Jain sources and by Kālidāsa); scholars equate **Sākēta** with Ayodhya, and Vaiṣṇava devotion uses **Sākēta** especially for the **eternal, transcendent Ayodhya** in Vaikuṇṭha-like heaven — Rāma's celestial *dhāman* of which the earthly city is the reflection. (Pāṇini; Buddhist sources; Vaiṣṇava theology)
- **Viśākhā** - an ancient alternative name attested for the Sākēta/Ayodhya site, sometimes treated as a quarter or earlier name of the town. (epigraphic / textual tradition) [scholarly]
- **Kosala** (the realm), **Avadh / Oudh** (the later regional name surviving in "Awadh"), and **Rāma-janmabhūmi** (the devotional name of the birth-precinct itself). (Wikipedia)

## III. The Sarayū — river of descent and of return

- **Sarayū / Saryū** - the river on whose southern bank the city stands; in the *Ayodhyā-māhātmya* it issues divinely — **created from Viṣṇu's left foot-thumb** as the **Gaṅgā issued from his right** — making the Sarayū a *pāda-tīrtha* of Viṣṇu, not merely a Himalayan stream. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Ayodhyā-māhātmya*; kamakoti.org)
  - *Variant (Mānasa origin):* a parallel tradition traces the Sarayū from **Lake Mānasa(rovara)** / the *Mānasa* of Brahmā — literally "flowing from the (Brahmā's) mind-lake," a sister-descent to the Mānasarovara source-myth of other sacred rivers. [folk]
- **The river as life-purifier and death-liberator** - bathing in the Sarayū at Ayodhya's *ghāṭs* destroys sin; dying within the *kṣetra* on its bank confers liberation — the standard *mokṣa-purī* logic (river = purification of life, city = liberation in death) applied here. (*Ayodhyā-māhātmya*) — cf. [sapta-purī river-city pairing](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md)

## IV. The city in the Rāmāyaṇa (Bāla-kāṇḍa city-plan)

- **A planned imperial metropolis** - Vālmīki opens the epic with a lavish description (*Bāla-kāṇḍa*, sarga 5): a city laid out along great avenues, **measured at twelve yojanas long by three broad** in one reckoning, fortified with deep moats, towering gates and unbreachable ramparts, lined with mansions, gardens, *sabhā*-halls, water-tanks and markets, and garrisoned so it is literally *a-yodhyā*. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 1.5–6)
- **Daśaratha's court** - the city is ruled at the epic's outset by **Daśaratha**, attended by ministers (Sumantra) and the family-priest **Vasiṣṭha**; the **Rāma-janma** (birth of Rāma and his brothers after the *putra-kāmeṣṭi* sacrifice) occurs here, on **Rāma Navamī** in *Caitra*. (*Bāla-kāṇḍa*) — see [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/README.md)
- **Pivot of the epic's grief and joy** - Ayodhya is the stage of Rāma's *abhiṣeka* preparation, **Kaikeyī's two boons** and the **14-year exile**, **Bharata's regency** governing from nearby **Nandigrāma** with Rāma's sandals (*pādukā*) on the throne, and finally the triumphant **return (the prototype of Dīpāvalī / Dīwālī)**. (*Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa*; *Yuddha-kāṇḍa*)

## V. Rāma's jala-samādhi — the city's exit from the world (Uttara-kāṇḍa)

- **Mahāprasthāna into the Sarayū** - at the close of his reign, having appointed his sons **Kuśa and Lava** to Kosala, Rāma (with Lakṣmaṇa already departed) walks into the **Sarayū** and, taking his **Vaiṣṇava form**, ascends to Vaikuṇṭha — a *jala-samādhi* (water-final-entry) rather than a burning or burial. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara-kāṇḍa*)
- **The whole city follows** - the citizens of Ayodhya — its people, even animals — enter the river behind their king and attain the higher worlds, an unparalleled image of a city dissolving en masse into liberation. (*Uttara-kāṇḍa*)
- **Goprātāra / Guptār Ghāṭ** - the exact spot of the ascent is named **Gopratāra Tīrtha** ("the ford where the cattle crossed"), surviving as **Guptār Ghāṭ** downstream of the city; bathing there is held especially liberating because it is the *door by which Rāma left the earth*. (*Uttara-kāṇḍa*; *Ayodhyā-māhātmya*; Wikipedia)

## VI. The Skanda Purāṇa Ayodhyā-māhātmya & the parikrama circuits

The principal glorification-text is the **Ayodhyā-māhātmya**, one of the *upa-khaṇḍas* of the **Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa** of the **[Skanda Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md)** — **10 chapters, ~990 ślokas**, a tīrtha-by-tīrtha handbook of the city. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa, Ayodhyā-māhātmya*; wisdomlib; kamakoti.org)

- **The Pañcakośī Parikramā (~5 kos / ~15 km)** - the inner circumambulation girdling the urban *kṣetra*, threading the city's chief ghāṭs and temples (Svargadvāra, Nāgeśvaranātha, Hanumān Garhī, Kanak Bhawan and the Sarayū front); walked especially on **Kārtika Pūrṇimā / Ekādaśī**. (*Ayodhyā-māhātmya*; pilgrimage tradition)
- **The Caudah-kośī Parikramā (~14 kos / ~42 km)** - the great outer circuit encompassing the wider sacred field of Ayodhya (the *kṣetra* boundary), drawing immense Kārtika crowds; the larger of the two prescribed *parikramas*. (pilgrimage tradition) [flag: route-roster of intermediate stations varies between guides]
- **The eight (and more) tīrthas** - the *māhātmya* catalogues the holy fords, among them **Cakra-tīrtha** (sprung from Viṣṇu's *cakra*, granting long life & mokṣa), **Brahma-kuṇḍa** (made by Brahmā), **Ṛṇa-vimocana** (debt-release) and **Pāpa-vimocana** (sin-release) tīrthas, **Sahasradhārā** (from the jewels of **Śeṣa-nāga**), **Svargadvāra**, **Candrahari**, **Sītā-kuṇḍa** and **Gayā-kūpa** (the local **[Gayā](gaya-pitru-tirtha.md)** for *pitṛ*-rites). (*Ayodhyā-māhātmya*; kamakoti.org)

### Key tīrthas, ghāṭs & temples

- **Svargadvāra ("gate of heaven")** - the riverfront precinct held to be the **nearest access to heaven**, identified with the place of Rāma's cremation/ascent in local tradition; its ghāṭ and the cluster of saints' *samādhis* anchor the *māhātmya*'s opening tīrtha-list. (*Ayodhyā-māhātmya* ch. 3)
- **Guptār Ghāṭ (Gopratāra Tīrtha)** - the downstream ghāṭ marking Rāma's *jala-samādhi* (§V above); a primary mokṣa-conferring bathing-spot. (*Uttara-kāṇḍa*; *Ayodhyā-māhātmya*)
- **Nāgeśvaranātha** - one of Ayodhya's **oldest temples**, a **Śiva** shrine traditionally founded by **Kuśa**, Rāma's son, after he recovered an armlet from a *nāga*-maiden of the Sarayū; a chief node of the Śaiva layer within the Vaiṣṇava city. (sthala-tradition; Wikipedia) — see [Śiva myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)
- **Hanumān Garhī** - the hilltop fort-temple of **Hanumān**, conceived as the **guardian-commandant of the city** who dwells in Ayodhya watching over Rāma's seat; the conventional *first darśana* before approaching the Janmabhūmi. (devotional tradition) — see [Hanuman](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md)
- **Kanak Bhawan ("the golden palace")** - the temple on the site of the palace said to have been gifted to **Sītā** by **Kaikeyī** as a wedding present; enshrines **Sītā-Rāma** in golden-crowned form, the devotional heart of the *yugala* (divine-couple) worship. (sthala-tradition)
- **Mani Parvat / Sītā-kuṇḍa & the lesser shrines** - the mound said to be a fragment of Hanumān's mountain (the *droṇa-/sañjīvanī*-parvata flight), with the *Daśaratha-* and *Sītā-kuṇḍa* tanks rounding out the inner circuit. (local tradition) [folk]

### Variant — Śiva seated as kṣetra-guardian

- **Skanda/Śiva as protector of the Vaiṣṇava city** - in keeping with the *Skanda Purāṇa*'s Śaiva voice, the *māhātmya* frame **seats Śiva (and the war-god Skanda/Kārttikeya) as guardian of the kṣetra**, so that even Rāma's Vaiṣṇava capital has a **Rudra/kṣetra-pāla** watching its bounds — the *Nāgeśvaranātha* liṅga being the standing terrestrial sign of this Śaiva guardianship over the city of Viṣṇu's avatāra. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Ayodhyā-māhātmya* frame) [late text]
  - *Variant (mutual indwelling):* devotional readings make this **reciprocal** — Śiva is the supreme *Rāma-bhakta* who dwells in Ayodhya to gaze on Rāma, mirroring Rāma's worship of Śiva at [Rāmeśvaram](12-jyotirlingas.md); the guardianship is love, not mere watch-keeping. [folk]

## VII. Ayodhya among the great pan-Indian networks

- **A Sapta-Purī (mokṣa-purī)** - the **first-listed** of the seven liberation-cities of the *Garuḍa Purāṇa* verse (Ayodhya, Mathurā, Māyā/Haridwar, Kāśī, Kāñcī, Avantikā/Ujjain, Dvārakā), and one of the three **Vaiṣṇava** seats among them; dying in Ayodhya confers *mokṣa*. (*Garuḍa Purāṇa*) — see [Sapta Purī](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md)
- **A Divya Deśam — "Tiru Ayodhi"** - one of the **108 [Divya Deśams](108-divya-desams.md)** sung by the **Āḻvārs**, the only major North-Indian Rāma-Janmabhūmi seat in the Tamil Śrī-Vaiṣṇava canon; the presiding form is **Rāma** (with Sītā), grouped in the northern (*Vaḍa Nāḍu*) cluster. (Āḻvār *Mangaḷāśāsanam*; greenmesg.org)
- **Janmabhūmi of the avatāra** - the precinct of Rāma's birth (*Rāma-janmabhūmi*), centre of the modern temple, makes Ayodhya the terrestrial anchor of the **Rāma-avatāra** narrative — the *daśāvatāra*'s seventh descent born into Sūryavaṃśa flesh here. (devotional tradition) — see [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)
- **A cross-traditional tīrtha** - Ayodhya is also revered in **Jainism** as the birthplace of **five Tīrthaṅkaras** (Ṛṣabhanātha, Ajitanātha, Abhinandananātha, Sumatinātha, Anantanātha) and figured in Buddhist Sākēta accounts — a sacred ground claimed across the dharmic traditions. (Jain tradition; Wikipedia) [scholarly]

## VIII. The Tulsīdās / Rāmcaritmānas devotional layer

- **Tulsīdās at Ayodhya** - **Gosvāmī Tulsīdās** (1532–1623 CE) **began composing the *Rāmcaritmānas*** at Ayodhya in **1574 CE** (Vikrama 1631, Caitra/Rāma-Navamī), recasting Vālmīki's Sanskrit epic into Avadhī *caupāī*-and-*dohā* for the people of the very land the epic describes. (Wikipedia; tradition) — see [Rāmāyaṇa retellings](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/README.md)
- **Ayodhya as the Mānasa's living stage** - the *Rāmcaritmānas* turns Ayodhya into a **bhakti-landscape**: the city's return-festival, Bharata's *prema*, and the *sākēta-dhāman* ideal feed directly into the **Rāmlīlā** performance tradition and the *Sītā-Rāma yugala* devotion centred at Kanak Bhawan. (Rāmlīlā tradition) [folk]
- **Saint-corridor of the Sarayū front** - the Svargadvāra and ghāṭ quarter became, post-Tulsīdās, a dense corridor of **Rāmānandī** *akhāṛās*, maṭhas and saints' *samādhis*, embedding the medieval Rāma-bhakti revival into the city's sacred topography. (sectarian history) [scholarly]
