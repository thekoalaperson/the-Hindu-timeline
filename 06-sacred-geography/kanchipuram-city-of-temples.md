# Kāñcīpuram — the Golden City (Banaras of the South)

> The only **southern mokṣa-purī**: of the seven sacred cities (*Sapta Purī*) that grant liberation, **Kāñcī** alone lies in the Tamil South, the rest strung across the Gangetic north and Saurāṣṭra. Called the **"city of a thousand temples"** and the **golden city** (*Kāñcī* = a woman's golden waist-girdle), it is uniquely **bipartite** — **Śiva-Kāñcī** (Big Kanchi, west) and **Viṣṇu-Kāñcī** (Little Kanchi, east) — with the goddess **Kāmākṣī** holding the centre. It is the rare node that is at once a *mokṣa-purī*, a [Śakti-pīṭha](51-shakti-peethas.md), a [Pañca-Bhūta-Sthala](pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md), the densest single cluster of [Divya Deśams](108-divya-desams.md) (~14–15), and the seat of the **Kāñcī Kāmakoṭi Pīṭha**.
>

**Names of the city** — each encodes a layer of the *kṣetra*. **Kāñcī / Kāñcīpuram** ("the city of the golden girdle," *kāñcī*; or after the *kāñci* flowering plant). **Satyavrata-kṣetra** ("the field of the true vow"), its Purāṇic name, the ground of Brahmā's vow and sacrifice. **Toṇḍai-maṇḍala / Tuṇḍīra-deśa capital** (the regional name of the Pallava heartland). **Kacci** / **Kacchi** (the classical Tamil form, used by the Āḻvārs and Nāyaṉmārs). **Conjeeveram** (the colonial English rendering). **"Banaras of the South" / Dakṣiṇa-Kāśī** (the epithet pairing it with [Kāśī](kashi-varanasi.md) as the southern liberation-city). (Wikipedia, *Kanchipuram*; Garuḍa Purāṇa Sapta-Purī list)

## I. The mokṣa-purī of the South

- **The seventh of the Sapta Purī** - the *Garuḍa Purāṇa* śloka *"Ayodhyā Mathurā Māyā Kāśī Kāñcī Avantikā / Purī Dvārāvatī caiva saptaitā mokṣadāyikāḥ"* names **Kāñcī** as one of the seven cities that *of themselves* grant *mokṣa*. Six lie north/west (Ayodhyā, Mathurā, Haridvāra/Māyā, Kāśī, Avantikā/Ujjain, Dvārakā); **Kāñcī is the sole southern member**, the only Dravidian-country *mokṣa-purī*. (Garuḍa Purāṇa; see [Sapta Purī](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md))
- **The "navel" of the goddess in the South** - just as Kāśī is the *kṣetra* on Śiva's trident, Kāñcī is held to be the **nābhi-sthāna** (navel-point) of the earth-goddess and the seat where the goddess *does tapas* to win Śiva — the city is feminine, the golden girdle (*kāñcī*) that cinches the sacred body of Bhārata. (sthala-purāṇa tradition; kamakoti.org)
- **Bestower of liberation through the goddess** - in Śākta reading Kāñcī grants *mukti* because **Kāmākṣī** (the eye-of-desire goddess) here turns the soul's desire toward release; the city is *Kāmakoṭi*, "the myriad/ultimate of desire transmuted." (Lalitā / Kāmākṣī tradition) [late text]
- **Pairing with Kāśī (two liberation-poles)** - tradition explicitly mirrors the two: **Kāśī in the north, Kāñcī in the south**, the two ends of the axis of *mokṣa-kṣetras*, hence "Banaras of the South." Where Kāśī liberates by *death-in-the-city*, Kāñcī liberates by *darśana of the goddess and the two great gods together*. (popular tradition; greenmesg.org)

## II. The bipartite city — Śiva-Kāñcī and Viṣṇu-Kāñcī

The defining feature of Kāñcī among all sacred cities: it is split into two confessional halves around a single goddess-centre, so that a Śaiva, a Vaiṣṇava and a Śākta pilgrim each find their supreme *kṣetra* in one town. (Wikipedia, *Kanchipuram*)

- **Śiva-Kāñcī (Big Kanchi / Periya Kāñci, west)** - the **western** half, the older Śaiva city, dominated by **Ekāmbareśvara** and the cluster of Śiva temples; the Pallava temple-quarter and the silk-weaving heart of the modern town. Houses the goddess **Kāmākṣī** at its core. (Wikipedia)
- **Viṣṇu-Kāñcī (Little Kanchi / Cinna Kāñci, east)** - the **eastern** half, the Vaiṣṇava city, dominated by **Varadarāja Perumāḷ** on Hastagiri (Atthigiri) and the dense knot of Divya Deśams; the Sri Vaiṣṇava ācārya-quarter. (Wikipedia)
- **The two halves, one mokṣa-kṣetra** - the bipartition is not sectarian rivalry but a *complementary whole*: the *sthala-purāṇa* frames Kāñcī as the place where **Śiva (as Ekāmbara) does penance for the goddess** in the west while **Viṣṇu (as Varadarāja) grants boons** in the east, the goddess **Kāmākṣī** at the meeting-point uniting both. (sthala-purāṇa; kamakoti.org)

## III. Śiva-Kāñcī — the western Śaiva city

- **Ekāmbareśvara (Ekāmbaranāthar)** - the supreme Śiva temple of the city and the **Pṛthivī-liṅga (earth element) of the [Pañca-Bhūta-Sthalams](pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md)** — the five temples mapping Śiva to the five elements (earth-Kāñcī, water-Tiruvāṉaikkāval, fire-Tiruvaṇṇāmalai, air-Kālahasti, ether-Cidambaram). One of the largest temples in India (~25 acres), with a **59 m rājagopuram**; the liṅga is a *svayambhū* of sand (*pṛthvī*) that may not be bathed, worshipped instead with jasmine and a perpetual lamp. (Wikipedia, *Ekambareswarar Temple*)
  - **The mango-tree (Ekāmra) legend** - the name *Eka-āmra* ("one mango tree"): beneath an ancient sacred mango, the goddess **Kāmākṣī**, doing penance, fashioned a Śiva-liṅga of sand and embraced it as the river **Vegavati** flooded to test her; she shielded the sand-liṅga with her own body and Śiva, won, took her as bride. The temple's mango tree, with four branches yielding four tastes (the four Vedas), still stands. (sthala-purāṇa; Wikipedia) [folk]
  - This is the great **earth-element penance** node — see [Pañca-Bhūta-Sthalams](pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md) and the Śiva-Pārvatī marriage cycle in [Shiva myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md).
- **Kailāsanāthar temple** - the **oldest standing temple in Kāñcī** and one of the oldest in South India, built **c. 685–705 CE by the Pallava king Rājasiṃha (Narasiṃhavarman II)** with completion by his son Mahendravarman III, in sandstone. The earliest Pallava structural Śiva temple, with 58 cells around the *vimāna*, foundational to the **Dravidian temple style** later elaborated by the Cholas. An ASI-protected monument. (Wikipedia, *Kailasanathar Temple*) [scholarly]
- **Kacchapeśvara (Kachabeswarar)** - a Śiva temple commemorating **Viṣṇu as the tortoise (Kūrma / kacchapa)** worshipping Śiva — the rare shrine where the Vaiṣṇava avatāra venerates the Śaiva liṅga, a deliberate *samarasya* (harmony) emblem binding the two halves of the city. (sthala-purāṇa; templesinindiainfo)
- **Other Śaiva nodes** - **Onakāntheśvara**, **Mātṛkeśvara**, and numerous liṅga-shrines fill Big Kāñci; the city's **108 Śiva-liṅgas** of tradition. Kāñcī's Śaiva temples are part of the **[Pāḍal Petra Sthalams](275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md)** sung by the Nāyaṉmār saints. [flag: full Śaiva roster open-ended]

## IV. Viṣṇu-Kāñcī — the eastern Vaiṣṇava city & Divya Deśam cluster

Kāñcī holds the **densest concentration of [Divya Deśams](108-divya-desams.md)** of any city — the 108 Vaiṣṇava abodes sung by the twelve **Āḻvārs** in the *Nālāyira Divya Prabandham*. Counts vary **14 or 15** depending on whether sub-shrines are counted separately. (Wikipedia; divyadesam.com)

- **Varadarāja Perumāḷ (Tiru-Attiyūr / Hastagiri / Atthigiri)** - the supreme Viṣṇu temple of Kāñcī, on the **elephant-hill (Hasti-giri / Atthigiri)**; a ~23-acre complex with ~350 inscriptions across Pallava, Chola and Vijayanagara reigns. (Wikipedia, *Varadharaja Perumal Temple*)
  - **Brahmā's yajña legend** - Brahmā performed the great **aśvamedha** (some say a fire-sacrifice) here at **Satyavrata-kṣetra**; when the river **Vegavati** rose to wash it away (sent, in one telling, by an offended Sarasvatī), **Viṣṇu lay across the flood to stop it** and then rose from the *yajña*-fire / an **athi (fig) tree** as **Varadarāja** ("king of boon-givers") to dwell forever. (sthala-purāṇa; Wikipedia)
  - **Athi-Varadar (the submerged fig-wood deity)** - the original ~10 ft **fig-wood (athi) image** lies submerged in the temple tank **Anantasaras**, raised for public worship only **once in 40 years** over 48 days (24 reclining, 24 standing); last shown **2019**, next due **2059**. (Wikipedia)
  - **Tirukkacci Nambi** - the Vaiṣṇava saint who performed *ālavaṭṭa-kaiṅkarya* (fanning the deity) and composed the Sanskrit **Devarāja-aṣṭaka**; held to converse directly with Varadarāja. See §VII below for his pivotal link to Rāmānuja. (Wikipedia; Sri Vaiṣṇava tradition)
- **Ulagaḷanta Perumāḷ (Tiru-Ūragam)** - "the one who measured the worlds," the colossal **Trivikrama / Vāmana** form striding the cosmos (left foot raised to the sky). Uniquely, this single temple **contains three further Divya Deśams within its precincts — Nīragam, Kāragam and Kārvānam** — so one shrine yields four of the 108 abodes. (divyadesam.com; hindustudentscouncil.org)
- **Pāṇḍava Dūta / Pāṇḍava Thūthar (Tiru-Pāḍakam)** - Viṣṇu as **Kṛṣṇa the envoy (dūta)** of the Pāṇḍavas, seated in the *viśvarūpa* (cosmic-form) *darśana* he gave to king **Janamejaya**, commemorating Kṛṣṇa's embassy to the Kaurava court before [Kurukṣetra](kurukshetra-dharmakshetra.md). (divyadesam.com)
- **Aṣṭabhuja Perumāḷ (Aṣṭabhujakaram)** - the **eight-armed** Viṣṇu bearing eight weapons for universal protection; linked to a Sarasvatī–Brahmā contest legend. (hindustudentscouncil.org)
- **Yatōktakāri / Yathothakari (Tiru-Veḷḷakkuḷam, "Sonna-vaṇṇam-Seyda Perumāḷ")** - "he who did exactly as told," the reclining Viṣṇu who obeyed the Āḻvār **Tirumaḻicai Āḻvār's** command to rise and leave (and return) with him — the deity literally following the devotee's word. (divyadesam.com)
- **Aḻagiya Singar / Yoga-Narasiṃha (Tiru-Veḷukkai)** - **Narasiṃha** who *chose to stay* here for his devotees (Tamil *vēṇḍi irukkai* → Veḷukkai); a large seated man-lion form. (hindustudentscouncil.org)
- **Dīpa-Prakāśar (Tiru-Taṇkā, "Viḷakkoḷi Perumāḷ")** - Viṣṇu as the **light that dispels darkness** (lamp-radiance), tied to a Sarasvatī-flood legend; a standing west-facing form. (divyadesam.com)
- **Vaikuṇṭha-Perumāḷ (Tiru-Param-Eccūr / Paramapadanāthan)** - Viṣṇu as **lord of Vaikuṇṭha**; the Pallava-era Vaikuṇṭha Perumāḷ temple is also famed for sculpted panels narrating Pallava dynastic history. (divyadesam.com)
- **Further Kāñcī Divya Deśams** - **Pavaḷavaṇṇam** (coral-hued Viṣṇu), **Pacchai-vaṇṇam** (emerald-hued), **Nīragam, Kāragam, Kārvānam** (the three inside Ulagaḷanta), plus **Tiru-Niraga/Tiru-Kaḷvanūr** — together the cluster reaches **14–15** of the 108. [flag: exact enumeration varies by list]

## V. Kāmākṣī Ammaṉ — the goddess at the centre (Śakti Pīṭha)

- **Kāmākṣī ("the eye of desire / love-eyed")** - the **reigning deity of all Kāñcī**, seated at the precise centre between the Śaiva west and Vaiṣṇava east; the city has **no other functioning Ambāḷ shrine in temple form** by tradition, so that the whole city *is* her temple and the lesser goddess-shrines stay as latent forms — a deliberate *ekāmbikā* ("one mother") arrangement. (Wikipedia, *Kamakshi Amman Temple*) [folk]
- **A Śakti Pīṭha** - Kāñcī Kāmākṣī is counted among the **[Śakti Pīṭhas](51-shakti-peethas.md)** — the site where, in the Satī dismemberment myth, the goddess's **navel (nābhi)** (some lists: her *back/skeleton*) fell; this anchors Kāñcī as the "navel of the goddess." (Devī-Bhāgavata / pīṭha lists) — see [51 Śakti Pīṭhas](51-shakti-peethas.md)
  - *Variant (which body-part):* lists variously assign **navel** or the **back/upper-frame (pṛṣṭha)** of Satī to Kāñcī; the Bhairava is **Ruru/Devarāja-Bhairava** and the Devī **Devagarbha / Kāmākṣī**. [disputed]
- **The Śrī-Cakra and Ādi Śaṅkara** - **Ādi Śaṅkara** is held to have *pacified and seated* the fiercely powerful Kāmākṣī by **consecrating a Śrī-Cakra (Śrī-Yantra) before her** in the *bilākāśa* (cavity) under the central shrine, so the goddess sits in *padmāsana / yogic* calm rather than wild Ugra form — the temple is thereby a living **Śrī-Vidyā** seat. (kamakoti.org; Kamakshi tradition)
- **Gāyatrī-maṇḍapa & the seated Devī** - Kāmākṣī sits in *padmāsana* in the *Gāyatrī-maṇḍapa*, holding sugarcane bow, flower-arrows, *pāśa* and *aṅkuśa* — the iconography of **Lalitā-Tripurasundarī** of the *Lalitā-Sahasranāma*. (Śrī-Vidyā tradition)

## VI. The Kāñcī Kāmakoṭi Pīṭha & the Ādi Śaṅkara link

- **Kāñcī Kāmakoṭi Pīṭha (Kāñcī Maṭha)** - a **Śaṅkarācārya monastic seat** beside the Kāmākṣī temple, styling itself **Mūlāmnāya Sarvajña Pīṭha** ("the original, all-knowing seat"); a centre of Vedic learning and Śrī-Vidyā Advaita. Its lineage claims an **unbroken line of ~70 ācāryas** (the *Jagadgurus*, titled *Śaṅkarācārya*). (Wikipedia, *Kanchi Kamakoti Peetham*; kamakoti.org)
- **The Ādi Śaṅkara claim** - the Maṭha's tradition holds that **Ādi Śaṅkara himself founded it at Kāñcī and attained *siddhi* (his final resting) here** (placing his founding in the matha's own reckoning at *482 BCE*), making Kāñcī (not Kedārnāth) his last seat in this tradition. (kamakoti.org) [disputed — see below]
  - *Variant (the four-maṭha tradition):* the mainstream account (the Śṛṅgeri / *Maṭhāmnāya* tradition) holds Ādi Śaṅkara founded **four cardinal maṭhas** — **Śṛṅgeri (south), Dvārakā (west), Purī (east), Jyotir/Badrī (north)** — and that he *attained siddhi at Kedārnāth*, with Kāñcī's independent foundation and its early dating **historically contested by scholars**. [scholarly] [disputed] — see [Ācāryas & Sampradāyas](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md)
- **Significance regardless of dating** - whatever its historical origin, the Kāñcī Pīṭha is among the most influential living Śaṅkara-seats, its 20th-c. ācārya **Candraśekharendra Sarasvatī ("the Paramācārya," Mahāperiyava)** a major modern spiritual figure. (kamakoti.org)

## VII. Tirukkacci Nambi & Rāmānuja — the Vaiṣṇava nerve-centre

Viṣṇu-Kāñcī is the cradle of the **Śrī Vaiṣṇava** ācārya tradition and the formative ground of **Rāmānuja**, the great Viśiṣṭādvaita teacher. (Sri Vaiṣṇava tradition; Wikipedia)

- **Tirukkacci Nambi** - a senior Śrī Vaiṣṇava devotee at Varadarāja Perumāḷ, of non-brahmin birth yet revered as a teacher; he performed daily *ālavaṭṭa-kaiṅkarya* (fanning the Lord) and was believed to **converse face-to-face with Varadarāja**. (Wikipedia, *Varadharaja Perumal Temple*)
- **The six words (āru vārttai) from Varadarāja** - the famed episode: the young **Rāmānuja**, troubled by doctrinal doubts, asked Tirukkacci Nambi to put his questions to the deity. The Lord answered with **six sentences** — declaring **(1) I am the Supreme** (*paratattva* is Nārāyaṇa); **(2) the distinction between soul and God is real** (*bhedābheda* / *cit*–*Īśvara* distinction); **(3) take refuge (prapatti/śaraṇāgati)** as the means; **(4) remembrance of God at the last moment is not required** (for the surrendered); **(5) liberation comes at the end of this body**; **(6) take refuge in Periya Nambi (Mahāpūrṇa)** as your ācārya. These six became foundational axioms of **Viśiṣṭādvaita Śrī Vaiṣṇavism**. (Sri Vaiṣṇava hagiography, *Guruparamparā*) [folk/tradition]
- **Rāmānuja's Kāñcī years** - Rāmānuja was born nearby (Śrīperumbudūr) and first studied at Kāñcī; his break with the Advaitin **Yādava-Prakāśa**, his service of carrying water for the deity, and his eventual initiation under **Periya Nambi (Mahāpūrṇa)** all centre on Viṣṇu-Kāñcī before he moved to Śrīraṅgam. (Sri Vaiṣṇava tradition) — see [Ācāryas & Sampradāyas](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md)
- **The Āḻvār presence** - Kāñcī is sung by multiple **Āḻvārs**; **Tirumaḻicai Āḻvār** and **Pēyāḻvār** are strongly tied to its shrines (the Yatōktakāri legend belongs to Tirumaḻicai), embedding the city in the **[Nālāyira Divya Prabandham](108-divya-desams.md)**.

## VIII. The convergence — one city, four sacred networks

What makes Kāñcī singular is that **four distinct pan-Indian sacred geographies intersect here at once**:

- **Mokṣa-purī** - one of the **Sapta Purī**, the only southern liberation-city. (Garuḍa Purāṇa) — [Sapta Purī](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md)
- **Pañca-Bhūta-Sthala node** - Ekāmbareśvara is the **earth (pṛthvī) liṅga** of the five-element Śiva temples. — [Pañca-Bhūta-Sthalams](pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md)
- **Śakti Pīṭha** - Kāmākṣī marks the **navel** of Satī, a living Śrī-Vidyā seat. — [51 Śakti Pīṭhas](51-shakti-peethas.md)
- **Divya Deśam super-cluster** - **~14–15 of the 108** Vaiṣṇava abodes, the densest in any city. — [108 Divya Deśams](108-divya-desams.md)
- **Pāḍal Petra cluster + Śaṅkara Pīṭha** - Śaiva temples in the **275 Pāḍal Petra Sthalams** plus the **Kāñcī Kāmakoṭi** Śaṅkara seat — so Śaiva, Vaiṣṇava, Śākta and Smārta-Advaita traditions each crown Kāñcī. — [275 Pāḍal Petra Sthalams](275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md)
- **Toṇḍai-maṇḍala / Pallava capital** - capital of **Tuṇḍīra-deśa (Toṇḍaimaṇḍalam)** and of the **Pallava dynasty (4th–9th c. CE)**, later under Cholas and Vijayanagara — the political patronage that built this temple density. (Wikipedia) [scholarly]
