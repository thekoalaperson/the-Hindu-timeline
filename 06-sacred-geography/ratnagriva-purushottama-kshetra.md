# Ratnagrīva's Pilgrimage to Puruṣottama-kṣetra — the Padma's Jagannātha Māhātmya

> A second, independent charter of **Purī / Puruṣottama-kṣetra** — not the famous Skanda-Purāṇa Indradyumna legend (for that, see [Jagannāth Purī](jagannath-puri-purushottama-kshetra.md)) but the **Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 17–22**: the tale of **King Ratnagrīva of Kāñcī**, told *inside* the story of **Rāma's Aśvamedha** when the sacrificial horse nears the **Nīla mountain**. Scriptural clock: **Tretā Yuga** frame (the aśvamedha of Rāma).
>
> Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md) · Canonical Purī file: [Jagannāth Purī — Puruṣottama Kṣetra](jagannath-puri-purushottama-kshetra.md) · Text map: [Padma Purāṇa khaṇḍa map](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/padma-purana-khanda-map.md)

The Padma Purāṇa's **Pātāla-khaṇḍa** devotes six chapters of its Rāma-aśvamedha epic to the glory of **Puruṣottama** and the **Nīla mountain** (Nīlācala — the blue hill of Purī). Its charter of the kṣetra is strikingly **different in cast and plot** from the Skanda Purāṇa's Indradyumna cycle: no Indradyumna, no Nīla Mādhava, no floating log, no wooden triad — instead a South-Indian pilgrim-king, a hidden golden temple worshipped by **Brahmā** himself, a transforming morsel of the Lord's rice, and **Viṣṇu walking out to meet his devotee disguised as an ascetic**. Both māhātmyas are cataloged in this corpus, unmerged, per house rule.

---

## I. The Frame — Rāma's Horse Reaches the Nīla Mountain (Tretā Yuga)

- **The Śeṣa–Vātsyāyana frame** — the whole Pātāla-khaṇḍa aśvamedha narrative is told by the serpent **Śeṣa** to the sage **Vātsyāyana**; within it, Rāma releases the horse-sacrifice steed and appoints **Śatrughna** its protector. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 1–11; see the [khaṇḍa map](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/padma-purana-khanda-map.md) and [Uttara-Rāmāyaṇa / Lava-Kuśa](../04-deep-dives/uttara-ramayana-lava-kusha.md))
- **The horse nears Nīlācala** — wandering onward from Ahicchatrā and Cyavana's hermitage (Pātāla-khaṇḍa 12–16), the horse and Śatrughna's army approach the region of the **Nīla mountain**; the minister-companion **Sumati** explains the place's greatness to Śatrughna by recounting an old story — the tale of **Ratnagrīva, king of Kāñcī**. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 17)
- **The narrative return** — after the tale, Sumati announces "now your horse has reached the vicinity of the Nīla mountain"; the army bathes and praises Viṣṇu before the digvijaya rolls on toward **Damana** and **Subāhu** (Pātāla-khaṇḍa 23 ff.). The Purī māhātmya is thus a deliberate *tīrtha-station* set into the horse's itinerary. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 22–23)

---

## II. The Ascetic at Ratnagrīva's Court in Kāñcī

- **Ratnagrīva of Kāñcī** — Sumati's tale opens in **Kāñcī** (the southern temple city; cf. [Kāñcīpuram](kanchipuram-city-of-temples.md)), ruled by the righteous **Ratnagrīva**, under whom all four varṇas keep their dharma and the city prospers. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 17)
- **The wandering brāhmaṇa** — an ascetic brāhmaṇa — emaciated, with matted hair, bark garments and a staff — suddenly appears in the king's assembly. Ratnagrīva honors him and asks the two questions that drive the whole cycle: **which god ends the round of dwelling in the womb**, and **which holy place can bestow liberation**? (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 17)
- **The answer is Puruṣottama** — the ascetic, a veteran of many tīrthas, answers with the eastern seaboard: the confluence of **Gaṅgā and the ocean** and, above all, the **Nīla mountain of Puruṣottama** — which he glorifies through a wonder he has witnessed. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 17–19)

---

## III. The Kirāta Miracle — the Lord's Rice on Mount Nīla

- **The kirāta child and the piṇḍa** — the ascetic recounts: on an eastern peak of **Mount Nīla** stands a divine temple of **Viṣṇu**. A **kirāta** (forest-tribe) child found it and ate a particle of **rice offered to the Lord** (*piṇḍa*) — and was instantly transfigured, gaining a **four-armed** divine body bearing Viṣṇu's own emblems (conch, disc). (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 18)
- **The tribe transformed** — seeing the marvel, the other kirātas came to the temple, ate the sanctified food, and likewise became four-armed — "the wonderful importance of *piṇḍa*." (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 18)
  - *Comparison (Purī tradition):* the motif — the Lord's offered food transfiguring even forest-dwellers, with no rule of eligibility — reads as a māhātmya-charter for what Purī's living cult knows as **Mahāprasād**, the caste-dissolving temple food; and the kirātas here echo the tribal (Śabara) substrate that the Skanda's Indradyumna cycle and modern scholarship both attach to Jagannātha. See [Jagannāth Purī §VI](jagannath-puri-purushottama-kshetra.md). [scholarly]
- **The ascetic's own vision** — the brāhmaṇa adds his own testimony: bathing at the **Gaṅgā–ocean confluence** and climbing the gem-studded mountain, he **saw Viṣṇu** and won freedom from rebirth. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 19; cf. [Gaṅgāsāgara](gangasagar-sagara-dvipa-kapila-ashram.md))

## IV. The Pilgrimage Proclaimed — a Whole Kingdom Sets Out

- **Ratnagrīva resolves on yātrā** — fired by the account, the king asks for full pilgrimage instruction; the ascetic teaches the pilgrim's discipline — detachment, right conduct, and devotion to **Hari-Puruṣottama** — before the journey. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 19)
- **The minister Uttama's proclamation** — Ratnagrīva has his minister **Uttama** announce the pilgrimage throughout Kāñcī, and the procession that forms is deliberately universal: **brāhmaṇas, kṣatriyas, vaiśyas, śūdras** and the occupational groups all join the march to Puruṣottama — the māhātmya's second statement (after the kirātas) that this kṣetra is open to all. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 19)
- **The road of hymns** — the royal party travels toward **Nīlagiri** hearing songs of **Kṛṣṇa and Viṣṇu**, visiting holy places and distributing gifts along the way. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 20)

## V. The Gaṇḍakī Interlude — Śālagrāma Glory on the Road

- **The Gaṇḍakī from Brahmā's tears** — encountering the sacred **Gaṇḍakī** river, the king asks a learned brāhmaṇa about it and is told the river was produced by **Brahmā from teardrops**, to destroy sins; in it lie the stones marked with the **disc (cakra)** — the **śālagrāma** stones that embody Viṣṇu's forms. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 20; canonical śālagrāma file: [Muktinath — Gaṇḍakī śālagrāma-kṣetra](muktinath-gandaki-shalagrama-kshetra.md))
  - *Note (geography):* a Gaṇḍakī crossing on a Kāñcī-to-Purī route is devotional itinerary, not cartography — the māhātmya threads the great Vaiṣṇava tīrthas (Gaṇḍakī, Gaṅgā-sāgara, Nīlācala) into one pilgrim's road. [scholarly]
- **Śālagrāma dharma** — daily śālagrāma worship frees from rebirth (provided the worshipper keeps good conduct and shuns greed); the **water that has bathed a śālagrāma purifies even a thousand sins**; tulasī leaves, conch, bell, disc-stone, copper vessel and the names of Viṣṇu are counted its companions. The text also states its own harsh exclusion — that **women must not touch the śālagrāma**, on pain of long hells ("fourteen Indras' periods") — recorded here as the Pātāla-khaṇḍa's claim, contested by other śālagrāma traditions. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 20) [disputed]
- **The Śabara hunter saved** — exemplum: a brutal hunter, a **Śabara of the Kīkaṭa country**, who killed beasts and robbed pilgrims, is seized at death by **Yama's** messengers; a Vaiṣṇava lays a **śālagrāma on his chest**, gives him tulasī-water, and utters **Rāma's name** — Viṣṇu's attendants free him, declaring that the śālagrāma's touch burns in a moment even the sin of brahmin-slaying; the Śabara reaches liberation. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 20)

---

## VI. Puruṣottama in the Guise of an Ascetic

- **The invisible mountain** — Ratnagrīva worships **twenty-four śālagrāma stones**, reaches the **Gaṅgā–ocean confluence** — and finds he **cannot see the Nīla mountain**, though his brāhmaṇa guide can perceive its region: the kṣetra veils itself from eyes not yet ripened by devotion. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 21)
- **The five-day vigil** — at the brāhmaṇa's instruction the king **fasts and sings Puruṣottama's praises**, holding the vow for five days. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 21)
- **The Lord comes disguised** — moved by compassion for his devotee, **Puruṣottama himself approaches wearing the apparel of an ascetic**. The "ascetic" promises that Viṣṇu will appear **at noon the next day**, and names **five** who will attain the highest place: **the king, his minister, his wife, the brāhmaṇa, and Karamba the weaver** — then vanishes, revealing who he was. The camp erupts in music and song. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 21)
  - *Motif note:* the Lord of Purī meeting his pilgrim incognito — and a weaver named among the five saved — anticipates the leveling, servant-Lord theology of the later Jagannātha cult (cf. the Chera Paṅharā at [Purī](jagannath-puri-purushottama-kshetra.md)); the Padma text itself simply narrates it. [scholarly]

## VII. The Theophany on Nīla Mountain — Five Go to Vaikuṇṭha

- **The dream of four arms** — that night Ratnagrīva dreams of himself **four-armed, dancing before the Lord** beside Śiva and the gods; the brāhmaṇa reads it as the divine promise confirmed. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 22)
- **The mountain unveiled** — after the ritual sea-bath at the confluence, the gods' **kettledrums** sound in the sky, a **shower of flowers** falls, and the **Nīla mountain appears** — blazing brighter than a crore of suns, with peaks of silver and gold. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 22)
- **The temple where Brahmā worships** — ascending, the five find a **golden temple in which Brahmā perpetually worships Viṣṇu**; Ratnagrīva bathes the image and offers garments, incense and food, then utters a long eulogy of Viṣṇu as the source of Brahmā and Rudra and lord of the avatāras — including the **fish form that overcame the demon Śaṅkha**. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 22; cf. the Matsya–Śaṅkhāsura variants in the corpus's avatāra files)
- **The ascent** — Viṣṇu grants the king and his four companions **four-armed celestial bodies**; a bell-hung **vimāna** descends and carries all five to **Vaikuṇṭha**. A sixth man — a devoted brāhmaṇa left heartbroken on the shore — also wins the four-armed form through sheer devotion. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 22)
- **Phala-śruti of Nīlācala** — the chapter closes on the kṣetra's charter-claim: the Nīla mountain is honoured by Viṣṇu himself; **on merely seeing it one goes to Vaikuṇṭha**, and even hearing or telling its greatness bears merit. (Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 22)

## VIII. Two Charters, One Kṣetra — Padma vs Skanda

- **Different cast, different miracle** — the Skanda Purāṇa's Puruṣottama-kṣetra Māhātmya (and Brahma Purāṇa) builds Purī on **King Indradyumna**, the vanished **Nīla Mādhava**, the floating *dāru* and the **unfinished wooden triad**; the Padma's Pātāla-khaṇḍa knows none of this — its Purī is a **self-hidden golden temple** with no image-origin story, revealed to a **southern king** through fasting and song. The two are independent māhātmyas of the same kṣetra and are kept separate in this corpus. (Skanda Purāṇa, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa vs Padma Purāṇa, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 17–22; full Skanda cycle at [Jagannāth Purī §II](jagannath-puri-purushottama-kshetra.md))
- **Shared bedrock** — what the two charters *do* share: the **Nīla mountain** (Nīlācala) as the holy site, the **sea-bath**, the sanctity of the Lord's **food**, and a **tribal presence** at the heart of the story (Śabara Viśvāvasu there; the transfigured kirātas and the saved Śabara hunter here) — convergences scholars read as memory of the shrine's non-Brahmanical substrate. [scholarly]
- **Academic clock** — the Pātāla-khaṇḍa's Rāma-aśvamedha (with this māhātmya inside it) is a **late devotional stratum** of the Padma Purāṇa, akin to the Jaimini-style aśvamedha epics; the Purāṇa as a whole is dated c. **4th–15th c. CE** with core layers c. **750–1000 CE**, and the māhātmya presupposes an already-celebrated Purī — the surviving temple being a 12th-c. Eastern Gaṅga foundation. (see [Padma khaṇḍa map](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/padma-purana-khanda-map.md)) [late text] [scholarly]
- **Scriptural clock** — the frame sits in **Tretā Yuga** (Rāma's aśvamedha; spine: [Tretā Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)); Ratnagrīva's tale is told *within* it as a story already old, its own internal date left unstated by the text.
