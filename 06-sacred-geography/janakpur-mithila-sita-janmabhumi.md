# Janakpur and Mithilā — Sītā's Birthplace

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](README.md) → **Janakpur / Mithilā**. The first-tier living *Rāmāyaṇa* pilgrimage city in the **Madhesh / Terai of southern Nepal**, held to be the capital of **Janaka's Videha-Mithilā** and the birthplace of **Sītā (Jānakī, Maithilī)** — the *kṣetra* of the **Dhanuṣa-yajña** where [Rāma](ayodhya.md) broke **Śiva's bow (Pināka)** and won her hand; the home of the great **Jānakī Mandir** and the heart of **Maithilī / Sītā devotion**; and the terrestrial counterweight to [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md) in the *Sītā-Rāma* couple-cult.

## I. Identity, name & the "two clocks"

- **Mithilā = the realm; Janakpur(dhām) = the town** — *Mithilā* is the ancient **Videha** kingdom of the Gangetic north-east straddling the modern **Nepal–Bihar** border; **Janakpur** (Janakpurdhām) is its surviving sacred capital-town in **Dhanusha district, Province 2 (Madhesh), Nepal**, named for King **Janaka**. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 66; regional Maithilī tradition)
- **Videha = "the bodiless" line** — the dynasty traces to **Nimi**, cursed to lose his body, whose churned-out son **Mithi** (whence *Mithilā*) and **Janaka** (a dynastic title, "the begetter," not only a personal name) founded the realm; the philosopher-king **Janaka** of the *Rāmāyaṇa* is **Sīradhvaja Janaka**. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 70–71; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*) — see the Videha lineage in [Bāla-Kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md)
- **Scriptural clock (Tretā-yuga)** — within the *Rāmāyaṇa* frame Sītā's birth, the bow-sacrifice and the four-fold wedding belong to the **Tretā-yuga** of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara, the mythic register of every event below. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*) — see the **two-clocks** distinction at [Bāla-Kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md).
- **Historical clock** — as a *living* tīrtha Janakpur is a continuously worshipped landscape whose temples, *kuṇḍas* and festivals are **medieval-to-modern** overlays; the present **Jānakī Mandir** dates only to **1910–1911 CE**, the devotional Maithilī cult to the post-Vidyāpati (15th c.) era — the epic ground and the standing shrines belong to different clocks. [scholarly]
- **Cross-border kṣetra** — the *Sītā-janmabhūmi* claim is shared across the line: **Janakpur (Nepal)** holds the temple-centre, while **Sitamarhi (Bihar)** asserts the actual ploughing-furrow birth-spot at **Punaura-dhām**; pilgrims and the Maithilī cultural region span both states. [regional] [disputed — birth-spot localisation]

## II. Sītā's earth-birth — the furrow of Mithilā (Bāla-kāṇḍa 66)

- **The ploughing of the sacrificial field** — preparing ground for a *yajña* (in one telling, to break a drought), **Janaka** drives the plough and uncovers an infant girl in the **furrow (sītā)**; he takes her as his daughter. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 66) — see [Bāla-Kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md)
- **Ayonijā — "not womb-born"** — Sītā is *ayonijā*, sprung from **Bhūmi (Earth)** herself, hence her epithets **Bhūmijā / Pārthivī** ("Earth-daughter"); her names **Sītā** ("furrow"), **Jānakī** ("Janaka's") and **Maithilī** ("of Mithilā") all fix her to this soil. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 66)
  - *Variant (Lakṣmī incarnate):* Sītā is **Lakṣmī** descended to partner Viṣṇu's Rāma-avatāra — the standard Vaiṣṇava reading that makes Mithilā the birth-place of the Goddess of fortune. (*Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa*; Vaiṣṇava theology)
  - *Variant (Vedavatī):* she is the reborn **Vedavatī**, the chaste woman who cursed Rāvaṇa — linking her birth to the doom of Laṅkā. [scholarly / Purāṇic]
  - *Variant (Rāvaṇa's daughter):* in the **Adbhuta Rāmāyaṇa** and several Jaina tellings (Saṅghadāsa's *Vasudevahiṇḍī*, Guṇabhadra's *Uttarapurāṇa*) Sītā is the abandoned daughter of **Rāvaṇa and Mandodarī**, buried for a prophecy and recovered by Janaka in the furrow. [variant — non-Vālmīki]
- **Janaka the philosopher-king** — the foster-father is the *brahmavādin* sage-king of the Upaniṣadic dialogues (the *Bṛhadāraṇyaka* Janaka), so Mithilā is also remembered as a **seat of Vedic learning and jñāna** — the city where wisdom and the Goddess were both born. (*Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad*; tradition) [scholarly]

## III. The Dhanuṣa-yajña — the bow-breaking (Bāla-kāṇḍa 66–67)

- **The Śiva-dhanus (Pināka)** — Janaka's house holds the colossal bow of **Śiva**, given to his ancestor **Devarāta** by the gods; no king has even stirred it. Janaka vows Sītā to whoever can string it — *vīrya-śulka*, "the bride-price of valour." (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 66–67)
- **Arrival under Viśvāmitra** — **Rāma** and **Lakṣmaṇa**, brought by the sage **Viśvāmitra**, reach Mithilā and are welcomed by Janaka and his priest **Śatānanda** (Ahalyā's son); Viśvāmitra asks to show the boys the famous bow. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 66)
- **Breaking the bow** — at Viśvāmitra's bidding **Rāma lifts the Pināka effortlessly, bends it to string — and it snaps with a thunderclap** that fells the onlookers; Sītā is won. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 67)
  - *Variant (internal contradiction):* in the *Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* Sītā recalls the bow as **Varuṇa's**, while *Bāla-kāṇḍa* names it Śiva's Pināka — a noted textual discrepancy. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Tulsīdās, Rāmcaritmānas):* the Avadhī telling expands a full **dhanuṣa-yajña / svayaṃvara** — many kings fail, Sītā first sees Rāma in **Janaka's flower-garden (Phulwārī)**, Lakṣmaṇa's fiery taunts, then Rāma's effortless feat; the most popular North-Indian version, dramatized annually in Maithilī **Rāmlīlā**.
  - *Variant (Sītā's strength, folk):* as a child Sītā once lifted the bow's box while sweeping, proving she alone "matched" it. [folk]
- **Dhanuṣā-dhām** — the legend is anchored at **Dhanushadham**, ~18 km north-east of Janakpur, where a venerated **stone fragment** is shown as a piece of the shattered Pināka; it is a principal sub-tīrtha of the Janakpur circuit. (sthala-tradition) [regional]

## IV. The four-fold wedding — Vivāha Pañcamī (Bāla-kāṇḍa 70–73)

- **Daśaratha summoned** — Janaka sends word; **Daśaratha** arrives with **Vasiṣṭha**, and the genealogies of both the **Ikṣvāku** and **Videha** lines are recited before the rite. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 70–71) — see [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md)
- **Kuśadhvaja's daughters** — Janaka's brother **Kuśadhvaja** brings his two daughters so that all four sons of Daśaratha may wed together. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 71–72)
- **The quadruple marriage** — at Mithilā are solemnized: **Rāma–Sītā**, **Lakṣmaṇa–Ūrmilā** (Janaka's daughters), and **Bharata–Māṇḍavī**, **Śatrughna–Śrutakīrti** (Kuśadhvaja's daughters). (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 73)
- **Vivāha Pañcamī** — the wedding-day is fixed on **Mārgaśīrṣa Śukla Pañcamī** and celebrated as **Vivāha Pañcamī**, Janakpur's greatest festival: a re-enacted *barāt* (groom's procession) brings "Rāma" from Ayodhyā, drawing pilgrims from across the Maithilī world. (Maithilī tradition) [regional]
- **The Maithilī wedding-paintings** — the Janaka–Daśaratha marriage is the founding subject of **Mithilā / Madhubani painting**, the region's ritual folk art traditionally adorning wedding walls (*kohbar*); the *Sītā-Rāma vivāha* is its central icon. (regional folk tradition) [folk]

## V. The Jānakī Mandir & the sacred topography of Janakpur

- **Jānakī Mandir (Nau Lakhā Mandir)** — the great white **Rajput-Mughal-domed temple** at the city-centre marking Sītā's birth/abode, built **1910–1911 CE** by **Queen Vrishabhanu Kunwari of Tikamgarh** at a reputed cost of nine *lākh* (hence *Nau Lakhā*); it enshrines a **Sītā image said to have been found here by a sādhu (Śūrkiśordās) in 1657 CE**, the founding relic of the modern cult. (temple history; Wikipedia) [scholarly]
- **Rām Mandir & the older shrines** — the older **Rām Mandir** (Pagoda-style, 18th c., attrib. the Gorkhāli general **Amar Singh Thapa**) and the **Vivāha Maṇḍap** beside the Jānakī Mandir mark the wedding-pavilion; these pre-date the grand temple as the city's devotional core. (sthala-tradition) [regional]
- **The sacred ponds (sāgar/kuṇḍa)** — Janakpur is ringed with holy tanks — **Gaṅgāsāgar** and **Dhanuṣsāgar** chief among reputedly dozens — where pilgrims bathe; tradition holds the city once had a great network of *kuṇḍas* serving the *yajña* and the temple-economy. (pilgrimage tradition) [regional]
- **Parikramā circuits** — the city anchors a **Janakpur Parikramā** and the wider **Mithilā-mādhava / Bara-mādhava** circuit linking Janakpur, **Dhanushadham**, **Jaleshwar** and the Bihar sites (**Sitamarhi**, **Punaura-dhām**); walked especially at **Vivāha Pañcamī**, **Rāma Navamī** and **Sītā Navamī**. (pilgrimage tradition) [regional]
- **Rāmānandī devotional centre** — Janakpur is a stronghold of the **Rāmānandī** order and of **Sītā-upāsanā** (worship of Sītā as supreme, the *rasika* and *sakhī* devotion), making it the Goddess-side counterpart to the Rāma-centred [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md) and the exile-tīrtha [Chitrakūṭa](chitrakoot-kamadgiri.md). (sectarian tradition) [scholarly]

## VI. Mithilā as a Śakti Pīṭha

- **The left shoulder of Satī** — in the *śakti-pīṭha* network, **Mithilā** is counted among the seats where a part of **Satī's** body fell during Śiva's grief-wandering — here the **left shoulder (skandha)**, with the resident **Śakti as Umā (Mahādevī)** and her **Bhairava as Mahodara**. (*Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa*; *Tantra Cūḍāmaṇi / Pīṭhanirṇaya*) — see [51 Śakti Pīṭhas](51-shakti-peethas.md)
  - *Note [disputed]:* the **exact temple** answering to "Mithilā" is unfixed — candidates include sites near the **Nepal–Bihar border** (Janakpur area) and **Jaleshwar/Uchaith** in Bihar; the pīṭha-lists name the *region* more firmly than any single shrine. [disputed]
- **The Goddess doubled** — Mithilā thus carries **two distinct feminine sacralities**: the *Rāmāyaṇa* **Sītā-janmabhūmi** (Sītā as Lakṣmī, born of Earth) and the **Śākta pīṭha** (fragment of Satī/Pārvatī) — independent traditions the house style keeps separate rather than merging. [scholarly]

## VII. Standing in the network

- **A first-tier Rāmāyaṇa tīrtha** — though **not a Sapta-Purī**, Janakpur-Mithilā ranks with [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md) and [Chitrakūṭa](chitrakoot-kamadgiri.md) as a core node of the living *Rāmāyaṇa* pilgrimage map — the unique **bride-side** centre of the epic. (pilgrimage tradition) [scholarly]
- **Heart of Maithilī identity** — Janakpur is the cultural capital of the **Maithilī** language-region; the *Sītā-Rāma vivāha* underwrites Maithilī literature (from **Vidyāpati**'s padas onward), Madhubani art, and the *kanyādāna* self-image of Mithilā as **"the father of the bride" of all Hindustan**. (regional tradition) [regional]
- **Festival calendar** — **Vivāha Pañcamī** (the wedding), **Sītā Navamī / Jānakī Navamī** (Sītā's birth, *Vaiśākha Śukla Navamī*), **Rāma Navamī**, and the regional **Chhaṭh** anchor the year; *Rāmlīlā* and *Rāmāyaṇa* recitation saturate the calendar. [regional]
