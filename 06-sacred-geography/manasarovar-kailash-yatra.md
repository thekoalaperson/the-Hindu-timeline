# Mānasarovara & the Kailāsa Parikramā

> The supreme high-Himalayan **tīrtha**: the freshwater **Mānasa-sarovara** ("lake born of Brahmā's mind"), its dark twin **Rākṣas-tāl**, and the **parikramā** (clockwise circumambulation) of **Mount Kailāsa** — the one peak that is never climbed because it *is* Śiva's throne. A four-faith *axis mundi* (Śaiva, Bauddha, Jaina, Bön) and the hydrographic hub from which four great rivers fan out to the four quarters — a terrestrial echo of the Purāṇic four-rivers-from-Meru scheme.

This file zooms into the lake-and-mountain complex that the [cosmic-mountain file](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md) treats only in passing. The key distinction it carries throughout: **scripture vs. terrain.** In myth Kailāsa is Śiva's eternal abode and Mānasarovara is *manas* made water; on the ground they are a 6,638 m peak and a 4,600 m lake on the Tibetan plateau, sharing a watershed with the sources of the Indus, Sutlej, Brahmaputra and a Ganga-tributary. The tradition reads the geography *as* the theology.

---

## I. Mānasa-sarovara — the lake of the mind

- **Etymology & origin** — **Mānasa-sarovara** = *mānas* ("mind, consciousness") + *sarovara* ("lake"); the lake **created in / from the mind of [Brahmā](../00-time-and-cosmology/brahma-lifetime.md)** — Brahmā formed it out of his consciousness (*manas*) as the abode of his mount, the **haṃsa (swan / bar-headed goose)**, who descends to it each year. (*Skanda Purāṇa*, *Mānasakhaṇḍa*; *Matsya Purāṇa*; Wikipedia, *Lake Manasarovar*)
  - *Variant (descent myth):* in some retellings the lake first formed **in Brahmā's mind**, then **manifested on earth** so that his sons and the gods could perform austerities at Kailāsa's foot — myth-time precedes terrain-time. [late text]
- **The terrestrial lake** — a high freshwater lake on the Tibetan plateau (Ngari): **~4,600 m (15,100 ft)** elevation, surface **~320 km²**, circumference **~88 km**, max depth **~100 m** — among the highest freshwater lakes of its size on earth. (Wikipedia, *Lake Manasarovar*)
- **Roundness & purity** — its near-circular shape is read as a **solar / yang** disc and a symbol of **light, the mind, and liberation**; bathing here is held to wash sins of many births. (sthala tradition; *Mānasakhaṇḍa*)
- **The haṃsa (swan)** — the **paramahaṃsa** ("supreme swan") imagery: the swan that can separate milk from water becomes the lake's emblem of the discriminating, liberated mind; Mānasarovara is the swan's *mānasa* home, source of the recurring poetic motif "haṃsa flying to Mānasa." (poetic-Purāṇic topos; cf. [Sarasvatī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saraswati.md) whose vāhana is also the haṃsa)
- **Liturgical status** — circling the mountain *and* bathing in the lake together constitute the act of merit; the lake is one of the holiest waters in Hinduism, drunk and carried home as *tīrtha-jala*. (Wikipedia; pilgrimage practice)

## II. Rākṣas-tāl — the demon-lake, the dark twin

- **Rākṣas-tāl (Rāvaṇa-hrada / Rakshastal)** — the **saltwater, endorheic** lake immediately **west** of Mānasarovara; the paired "shadow" lake, crescent-shaped (read as **lunar / yin**), often called the **"ghost lake."** (Wikipedia, *Lake Manasarovar*; *Rakshastal* sources)
- **The Rāvaṇa connection** — named for the *rākṣasa* king **Rāvaṇa**, who is said to have performed severe austerities here (in some tellings standing in its waters, offering his ten heads to Śiva) to win boons; hence **Rāvaṇa-hrada**, "Rāvaṇa's lake." (folk / sthala tradition; cf. [Rāmāyaṇa](../01-itihasa-ramayana-mahabharata/ramayana-overview.md)) [folk]
- **The salt / sweet polarity** — the central symbolic pair: **Mānasarovara = fresh, round, solar, light, the divine mind; Rākṣas-tāl = salt, crescent, lunar, demonic, the unquiet ego.** Folk belief holds nothing grows in or beside Rākṣas-tāl and pilgrims do not bathe there. (popular tradition) [folk]
- **The Gaṅgā-chu channel** — the two lakes are physically linked: **Mānasarovara overflows into Rākṣas-tāl through the natural channel called Gaṅgā-chu (Ganga Chhu)** — when the sacred lake brims and pours into the demon-lake it is taken as an auspicious omen (and a meteorological one, marking good monsoon years). (Wikipedia, *Lake Manasarovar*)
  - *Variant (Gaurī Kuṇḍa beside Rākṣas-tāl):* some itineraries place a **Gaurī Kuṇḍa** in the lake-basin where **Rāvaṇa first glimpsed Pārvatī (Gaurī)** en route to Śiva — folding the lakes into the Pārvatī cycle (note: distinct from the Gaurī Kuṇḍa *on the parikramā*, §IV). [folk]

## III. Mount Kailāsa — the un-summited throne

- **Kailāsa (Tib. Kang Rinpoche / Gang Tise, "Precious Snow Jewel")** — the black, four-faced, ~**6,638 m** peak ~30 km north of the lakes; the **terrestrial abode of [Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) and Pārvatī**, with sons [Gaṇeśa and Kārttikeya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md) and the *gaṇa* host. (*Śiva*, *Liṅga*, *Matsya Purāṇas*; see [cosmic-mountain file](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md) §IV)
- **Never climbed — by design** — Kailāsa **has never been summited**; in Hindu, Bön, Buddhist and Jain belief, to set foot on the summit would desecrate the living seat of deity. Mountaineering permits have been refused; the only sanctioned act is to **walk around** it. The pilgrimage is therefore *parikramā* (Skt.) / *kora* (Tib.), never ascent. (Wikipedia, *Mount Kailash*)
  - *Variant (folk):* legends hold that those who attempted the climb died, vanished, or were turned back; the **Milarepa story** (Bön/Buddhist) instead has the yogi *fly* to the summit on a sunbeam, winning the mountain for Buddhism — a victory of devotion, not of climbing. [folk]
- **Kailāsa ≡ Meru?** — popular theology equates Kailāsa with cosmic **Meru** while older Purāṇic cosmography keeps them distinct; the parikramā treats the earthly peak as Meru's reflection. (see [meru-kailash](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md) §IV for the full variant-problem)

## IV. The Parikramā / Kora — the ~52 km circuit

The circumambulation is **clockwise** (*pradakṣiṇā*, keeping the sacred on the right) for Hindus, Buddhists and Jains; **anticlockwise** for **Bön** practitioners — the two streams of pilgrims passing each other on the same trail. Standard length **~52 km (some sources ~53 km / 33 mi)**, done in **3 days** on foot from **Darchen**; the devout do it in a single ~15-hour day, the supremely devout by full-body **prostration** (*aṅga-pradakṣiṇā*), taking ~2–3 weeks; one circuit is meritorious, **108** circuits the ideal lifetime goal. (Wikipedia; pilgrimage itineraries)

- **Darchen** — the base settlement (~4,600 m) at Kailāsa's southern foot where the circuit begins and ends.
- **Yam Dwār (Yama-dvāra, "Gate of Yama")** — the gateway **a short walk from Darchen** marking the formal entrance to the circuit; passing through it is held to be a symbolic **death and rebirth** — one crosses the threshold of **Yama** (lord of death) and is reborn purified on emerging. Pilgrims circle the small *torana* gate before starting. (parikramā tradition)
- **Day 1 — Darchen → Dīrāpuk (~12–14 km)** — along the **Lha-chu** valley to the gompa of **Dīrāpuk (~4,900 m)**, which faces the dramatic **north face** of Kailāsa across the valley — the classic first close view of the peak.
- **Day 2 — Dīrāpuk → Dolma La → Zuthul-puk (~22 km)** — the hardest stage, begun before dawn:
  - **Śiva-tsal** — a plateau strewn with cast-off clothing, hair and possessions; pilgrims **symbolically "die" here**, leaving something of the old self behind before the pass — a second death-and-rebirth station.
  - **Dolma La (Tārā Pass, ~5,630 m / 18,470 ft)** — the **highest point** of the circuit, sacred to **Dolma / [Tārā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-mahavidyas.md)** (the savioress goddess in Buddhism; assimilated to Pārvatī/Śakti by Hindus); festooned with prayer-flags. Crossing the pass completes the rebirth begun at Śiva-tsal.
  - **Gaurī Kuṇḍa (Pārvatī-sarovara, ~5,608 m)** — the emerald, often-frozen tarn just below Dolma La on the descent; named for **Gaurī (Pārvatī)**. Sthala tradition makes it the pool where **Pārvatī bathed**, and where (in the Gaṇeśa origin-myth) she fashioned a son from the **saffron/turmeric paste of her body** to guard her bath — the boy whose head Śiva struck off and replaced, becoming **[Gaṇeśa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md)**. (sthala-purāṇa; cf. Gaṇeśa birth-myth)
  - descent to **Zuthul-puk (~4,790 m)**, the "miracle cave" gompa associated with **Milarepa**.
- **Day 3 — Zuthul-puk → Darchen (~10–14 km)** — out through the Zhong-chu gorge back to Darchen, closing the circuit.
- **Inner kora (Nandi parikramā)** — a far harder **inner circuit** around the southern **Nandi** massif, traditionally permitted **only to those who have completed 13 outer koras** — the esoteric grade of the pilgrimage. [folk]

## V. The four great rivers — terrestrial echo of the four-from-Meru

The Kailāsa–Mānasarovara region is a genuine continental hydrographic node: **four of the great rivers of the subcontinent rise within ~100 km** of the lakes, draining to the four quarters — read by tradition as the earthly fulfilment of the Purāṇic scheme in which **four rivers fall from Meru / Brahmapurī to the four directions** (Sītā, Alakanandā, Cakṣus, Bhadrā — see [meru-kailash](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md) §I). Each river is mythically associated with an animal-mouth or face on the Kailāsa massif. (Wikipedia, *Lake Manasarovar*; *Matsya / Skanda Purāṇa* four-river tradition)

- **Brahmaputra (Yarlung Tsangpo)** — rises **east** of Kailāsa; the great trans-Himalayan river, son of **Brahmā** (rare male river-name). Traditionally the **"Horse-mouth" (Aśva-mukha)** source.
- **Indus (Sindhu)** — rises **north**; the river that named *Hindu* and India itself. Traditionally the **"Lion-mouth" (Siṃha-mukha)** source.
- **Sutlej (Śatadru / Langchen)** — rises **west**, draining **Rākṣas-tāl**; a chief tributary of the Indus. Traditionally the **"Elephant-mouth" (Hasti / Langchen)** source.
- **Karṇālī / Ghāghara (Mapcha / Mānasa-source)** — rises **south**; a major **tributary of the Ganga**, so this is the strand by which the **Gaṅgā** is linked to the region. Traditionally the **"Peacock-mouth" (Mayūra-mukha)** source. (Wikipedia, *Lake Manasarovar*)
  - *Variant (Ganga-source claim):* popular and some textual traditions call Mānasarovara/Kailāsa the **source of the Gaṅgā** outright (via the Karṇālī), and link it to the myth of **Śiva taming the descending Gaṅgā in his locks at Kailāsa**; geographically the Gaṅgā proper rises at Gaumukh/Gangotri, so this is a mythic, not hydrological, sourcing. [disputed] (cf. [Char Dhām / Gaṅgotrī](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md))
  - *Variant (four-mouths roster):* the animal-mouth assignments (horse/lion/elephant/peacock) vary between sources and are **not fixed** across all texts. [folk] [open roster]

## VI. Pan-dharmic status — one mountain, four faiths

- **Śaiva (Hindu)** — Kailāsa is **Śiva's throne**, Mānasarovara is *manas* made water, the lakes and circuit a supreme **mokṣa-tīrtha**; the parikramā is "the walking path to liberation." (*Śiva / Skanda Purāṇas*)
- **Bauddha (Buddhist)** — Kailāsa is **Mount Meru, the cosmic centre**, and the abode of **Demchok / Cakrasaṃvara** (the wrathful-blissful tantric deity) with consort **Dorje Phagmo (Vajravārāhī)**; Mānasarovara is **Anavatapta / Anotatta**, the lake at the centre of the world where **Queen Māyā conceived the Buddha** in Buddhist legend. The kora is *deva-kora*. (Buddhist tradition; Wikipedia)
- **Jaina** — Kailāsa is **Aṣṭāpada**, the mountain where the **first Tīrthaṅkara [Ṛṣabhanātha (Ādinātha)](../09-living-tradition-and-regional/jain-tradition-overview.md)** attained **nirvāṇa / mokṣa** — the foundational liberation-site of Jainism. (Jain tradition)
- **Bön** — the indigenous pre-Buddhist Tibetan tradition holds Kailāsa as **Yungdrung Gu-tseg ("nine-stacked swastika mountain")**, the *axis mundi* of the **Zhang-Zhung** empire and seat of the sky-goddess **Sipaimen**; Bön pilgrims circle **anticlockwise**. (Bön tradition; Wikipedia)
- **Net** — Kailāsa–Mānasarovara is among the very few sites on earth sacred to **four living religions at once**, each reading the same terrain through its own cosmology — a rare convergence of the [tīrtha](README.md) idea across traditions. [scholarly]

## VII. Rites, omens & the pilgrimage in practice

- **Bath + circuit** — the complete act of merit is **snāna (bathing) in Mānasarovara** *plus* the **parikramā of Kailāsa**; pilgrims also circumambulate the lake (~88 km, ~4 days), bathing at fixed ghāṭs/*chiu* points. (Wikipedia)
- **Carrying the water** — *Mānasa-jala* is bottled and carried home as the most potent *tīrtha-jala*, used in worship and at death-rites.
- **The overflow omen** — Mānasarovara **brimming over into Rākṣas-tāl via the Gaṅgā-chu** is read as a blessing and a sign of plenty; its failing to overflow, an ill omen. [folk]
- **Death-and-rebirth structure** — the circuit is explicitly a **journey through death**: **Yam Dwār** (gate of Yama) at the start, **Śiva-tsal** (the cremation-ground plateau where one "dies") before the pass, **Dolma La / Tārā** (the savioress who carries one across) as rebirth, and **Gaurī Kuṇḍa** (the goddess's regenerative pool) on the descent. The pilgrim emerges symbolically reborn.
- **The 108 ideal** — a single parikramā erases the sins of one life; **108 circuits** are said to assure **mokṣa** and the right to attempt the inner Nandi kora. [folk]
- **Access note** — the site lies in **Tibet (PRC)**; modern yātrā runs via Nepalese or Indian (Lipulekh / Nathu La) routes and is subject to permits and closures — a logistical, not scriptural, constraint. [scholarly]
