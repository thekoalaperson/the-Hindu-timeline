# Kolhapur — Mahālakṣmī / Ambābāī (Karavīra Kṣetra)

> The premier **Lakṣmī kṣetra** of the western Deccan and one of the most-recited of the **18 Mahā Śakti Pīṭhas**: on the **Pañcagaṅgā** river in Maharashtra, the Goddess **Mahālakṣmī (Ambābāī)** is enthroned at **Karavīra** as the seat where **Śrī herself dwells permanently on earth** — the spot the goddess of fortune chose after leaving Vaikuṇṭha, and where (in the southern *tāyār* / Śrī theology) she waits for **Viṣṇu** who has settled at nearby **[Tirumala-Tirupati](tirumala-tirupati-venkateshwara.md)**. Where Pandharpur holds the standing Viṭṭhala, Karavīra holds his consort-Lakṣmī; the two anchor the Deccan Vaiṣṇava-Śākta map.

**Names of the kṣetra** — **Karavīra** (Sanskrit *karavīra*, the oleander; the Purāṇic name of the city, whence *Kolhāpura* > Kolhapur) is the **sthala** named throughout the *māhātmya*; the city's other Purāṇic name **Kolhāsura-pura** ties it to the demon Kolhāsura slain here. The Goddess is **Mahālakṣmī** in Sanskrit/Purāṇic register and **Ambābāī** ("mother") in Marathi devotion; she is counted as the **Mahālakṣmī Devī of Kolhapur**, seventh of the **Aṣṭādaśa (18) Mahā Śakti Pīṭhas** in the *stotram* attributed to **[Ādi Śaṅkara](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)** (see [51 Śakti Pīṭhas §2](51-shakti-peethas.md)). The protected zone is **Karavīra-kṣetra**, glossed in tradition as a place so holy it is never destroyed even at the *pralaya* (dissolution). (*Karavīra-māhātmya*; *Wikipedia: Mahalaxmi Temple, Kolhapur*) [regional]

## I. The Karavīra-māhātmya — Śrī settles on earth

- **The Goddess chooses Karavīra** — the **Karavīra-māhātmya**, a *kṣetra-māhātmya* attached to the **Padma Purāṇa** (and, in other recensions, the **Skanda Purāṇa**), glorifies Karavīra as the spot where **Mahālakṣmī takes up permanent earthly residence** — declaring it the foremost of all *tīrthas*, a place where the merit of *darśana* exceeds that of every other holy field. (*Karavīra-māhātmya*, Padma Purāṇa) [late text]
- **Lakṣmī leaves Vaikuṇṭha** — the framing legend has **Śrī-Lakṣmī depart Vaikuṇṭha** after a quarrel/estrangement with **[Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)**, and descend to earth to dwell at Karavīra; the *kṣetra* is thus the seat of the goddess of fortune in her own right, not merely a consort-shrine. (*Karavīra-māhātmya*)
  - *Variant (the Bhṛgu-kick origin — Veṅkaṭācala link):* in the widely-told southern recension the cause of Lakṣmī's departure is sage **Bhṛgu kicking Viṣṇu's chest** (the seat of Śrī), after which the offended Lakṣmī quits Vaikuṇṭha — the **same trigger** that sends Viṣṇu down as **Śrīnivāsa** to [Tirumala](tirumala-tirupati-venkateshwara.md); in this telling Lakṣmī settles at **Kolhapur** while Viṣṇu settles at **Tirupati**, so the two shrines are read as the **separated divine couple**. (*Veṅkaṭācala-Māhātmya*; regional Śrī-Vaiṣṇava tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant (she waits for him):* the southern **tāyār** ("mother") theology makes Karavīra the place where **Lakṣmī abides waiting for Viṣṇu** to come and reconcile — pilgrims to Tirupati are traditionally enjoined to also visit Kolhapur, completing the couple's *darśana*. [folk]
- **Kolhāsura slain — the city's demon-myth** — a parallel/older stratum names the city for the *asura* **Kolhāsura** (var. **Kolāsura**), a buffalo-/demon-king whom the **Goddess slays** here after he terrorizes gods and sages; dying, he begs that the city bear his name and that the Goddess remain — so she stays as **Mahālakṣmī of Kolhāpura**. This *Devī-Māhātmya*-type demon-slaying myth (cf. [Devī Māhātmya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)) coexists with the Lakṣmī-Śrī legend rather than replacing it. (*Karavīra-māhātmya*; *Wikipedia: Kolhapur*) [regional] [disputed]
- **The Goddess as supreme Śakti, not only Śrī** — the *māhātmya* exalts Mahālakṣmī as the **Ādi Śakti** of the *Devī-Bhāgavata* threefold scheme (Mahākālī–Mahālakṣmī–Mahāsarasvatī), so Karavīra's goddess is simultaneously the **consort-Lakṣmī of Viṣṇu** and the **independent Great Goddess** — the dual identity that seats the shrine in both Vaiṣṇava and Śākta networks. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa*) [scholarly]

## II. The Śakti-Pīṭha identity — and a careful distinction

- **One of the 18 Mahā Śakti Pīṭhas** — Kolhapur's Mahālakṣmī is the **seventh** entry in the **Aṣṭādaśa Śakti Pīṭha Stotram** (*"...Mahālakṣmī ca Kolhāpure..."*), the most-recited devotional roster of pan-Indian "great" pīṭhas — its firmest pan-Indian Śākta credential. (Aṣṭādaśa Śakti Pīṭha Stotram; see [51 Śakti Pīṭhas §2](51-shakti-peethas.md))
- **Not the same as the 51-list "Mahālakṣmī" of Sylhet** — *caution:* the canonical **51 Śakti Pīṭha** list (*Tantra Cūḍāmaṇi*) assigns the Śakti named **Mahālakṣmī** to **Sylhet (Jaintapur), Bangladesh** (Satī's neck), **not** to Kolhapur — Kolhapur belongs to the **18 Mahā** scheme, a *distinct enumeration* that overlaps the 51 only partly (see [51 Śakti Pīṭhas §note](51-shakti-peethas.md)). Some popular sources wrongly conflate the two. [disputed] [scholarly]
- **A Śrī Pīṭha, not strictly a Satī-aṅga site** — because Kolhapur's primacy rests on the **Lakṣmī-settles-here** *māhātmya* rather than a fallen body-part of [Satī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md), some traditions count it as a **Śrī-pīṭha / Mahālakṣmī-pīṭha** distinct in character from the dismemberment pīṭhas — its membership in the "pīṭha" network is by the 18-Mahā devotional list, not by the Daksha-yajña myth. [scholarly]

## III. Distinctive features of the shrine

- **The west-facing svayambhū mūrti** — the **black-stone image of Mahālakṣmī** (c. 1 m, four-armed, bearing *mātuluṅga* citron, mace, shield and a *pānapātra*/vessel, a *liṅga* and *yoni* worked into the crown) faces **west** — an unusual orientation that is the key to the temple's signature solar festival. (*Wikipedia: Mahalaxmi Temple, Kolhapur*) [regional]
- **The Kiraṇotsava (sun-ray festival)** — the temple's most famous feature: **three times a year** (around late Jan/Feb and Nov, on fixed dates) the **setting sun's rays pass straight through the western doorway and windows to fall directly on the face/body of the mūrti**, illuminating the Goddess for a few minutes on successive evenings — an **architectural-astronomical alignment** built into the medieval temple, celebrated as **Kiraṇotsava** ("festival of rays"). The "two clocks" point: the *myth* of the Goddess belongs to Purāṇic time, but the *Kiraṇotsava* is a **datable medieval engineering** feat (the standing temple is Cālukya/Śilāhāra-era, **c. 7th–9th c. CE**, with later additions). (*Wikipedia: Mahalaxmi Temple*; temple administration) [regional] [scholarly]
- **Hemādpanti / Cālukya temple fabric** — the present **kāraṇa**-style stone temple is attributed in tradition to the **Cālukya** king **Karṇadeva (c. 7th c.)** and substantially built/renovated under the **Śilāhāra** dynasty (9th–11th c.), with **Maratha** (Chhatrapati / Sankeshwar-Kolhapur) restoration after a period of concealment of the image during medieval invasions — the *traditional* concealment-and-rediscovery story is told to explain a gap in continuous worship. (*Wikipedia: Mahalaxmi Temple, Kolhapur*) [scholarly] [regional]
- **Part of the Maharashtra Devī circuit (Sāḍe-tīn Śakti Pīṭha)** — in **Marathi regional** sacred geography Kolhapur's Ambābāī is one of the **"three-and-a-half" Śakti Pīṭhas of Maharashtra** — with **Tuljā Bhavānī** (Tuljapur), **Mahūr's Reṇukā / Ekavīrā** (also an 18-Mahā site, see [51 Śakti Pīṭhas §2](51-shakti-peethas.md)), and **Saptaśṛṅgī (Vaṇī)** as the "half" — a living **[pilgrimage circuit](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)** distinct from the pan-Indian lists. (*Wikipedia: Three and a half Shakti Peethas*) [regional] [folk]

## IV. Timeline placement (the two clocks)

- **Scriptural clock** — the *māhātmya* events (Lakṣmī's descent from Vaikuṇṭha; the slaying of Kolhāsura; the Goddess's permanent enthronement) are narrated as **Kali-Yuga-relevant** acts of the Goddess that **fix a persisting power-center** — like the [Śakti Pīṭhas](51-shakti-peethas.md) generally, Karavīra is read as a *standing* seat active through the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)**, its origin set vaguely in the deep Purāṇic past. [late text]
- **Historical clock** — the **Karavīra-māhātmya** survives as a **medieval/early-modern** Purāṇic appendix; the **temple fabric** is **c. 7th–11th c. CE**; the **Kiraṇotsava** alignment and the major festivals are documented **medieval-to-modern** practice. The distinction between the *legend's* timelessness and the *building's* datable history is the standard [sthala-purāṇa](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md) "two clocks." [scholarly]

## Sources

- *Karavīra-māhātmya* — the *kṣetra-māhātmya* of Karavīra/Kolhapur, transmitted as an appendix of the **Padma Purāṇa** (and, in other recensions, the **Skanda Purāṇa**); narrates Lakṣmī's descent and the glorification of the *kṣetra*.
- *Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* — the Mahākālī–Mahālakṣmī–Mahāsarasvatī threefold-Goddess theology underlying Mahālakṣmī's identity as Ādi Śakti.
- *Aṣṭādaśa Śakti Pīṭha Stotram* (attrib. Ādi Śaṅkarācārya) — lists Mahālakṣmī of Kolhapur as the 7th of the 18 Mahā Śakti Pīṭhas. (cf. Hindupedia, "Ashta Dasa Shakthi Peetha Stotram")
- *Veṅkaṭācala-Māhātmya* — the Bhṛgu-kick / Lakṣmī-leaves-Vaikuṇṭha frame shared with Tirumala, supplying the regional "separated couple" reading of Kolhapur vs. Tirupati.
- Wikipedia — *Mahalaxmi Temple, Kolhapur*; *Kolhapur*; *Three and a half Shakti Peethas* (en.wikipedia.org) — west-facing mūrti, Kiraṇotsava sun-alignment, Cālukya/Śilāhāra dating, Maratha restoration, the Maharashtra "sāḍe-tīn" circuit.
