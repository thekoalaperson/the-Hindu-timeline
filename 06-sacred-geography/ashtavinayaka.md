# Aṣṭavināyaka — the Eight Swayambhū Ganesha Shrines

> The *Aṣṭavināyaka* ("eight Vināyakas/Gaṇeśas") is a fixed pilgrimage circuit of eight temples around Pune in **Maharashtra**, each housing a **swayambhū** (self-manifest, *not* sculptor-carved) murti of Ganesha tied to a distinct *sthala-purāṇa*. Read here as Kali-Yuga-era *kṣetras* where Ganesha's obstacle-removing power settled on earth, framed by the **Gaṇeśa Purāṇa** and **Mudgala Purāṇa** (late-medieval, c. 13th–16th c. CE [scholarly]).
>

The term *swayambhū* ("self-born") marks these murtis as having appeared on their own — emerged from rock, earth, or divine will — rather than being consecrated artisan-icons; this is the structural cousin of the Shaiva **[jyotirlinga](12-jyotirlingas.md)** doctrine of self-arisen Shiva-pillars. The eight are spread across roughly a 600 km loop in **Pune, Ahmednagar, and Raigad districts**. Six lie in inland Pune/Ahmednagar (Deccan plateau); two (Pali, Mahad) lie in coastal Raigad (Konkan).

## Textual Frame — the Two Ganesha Puranas

- **Gaṇeśa Purāṇa** - one of the two *upapurāṇas* (minor Puranas) dedicated wholly to Ganesha; narrates **four incarnations** of Ganesha across the four yugas (Mahotkata-Vinayaka in Satya, Mayureshvara in Treta, Gajanana in Dvapara, Dhumraketu in Kali) and supplies the Ballaleshwar (Pali) legend. Scholarly dating: c. 1100–1400 CE [late text].
- **Mudgala Purāṇa** - the more philosophical Ganapatya text; narrates **eight incarnations** (*Aṣṭāvatāra*) of Ganesha, each slaying a demon personifying an inner vice. This eight-fold scheme is the doctrinal mirror behind the *eight* shrines, though the temple roster and the Purana's avatara-list are not a one-to-one map [scholarly].
  - **Vakratuṇḍa** ("curved trunk") - slays **Matsarāsura** (jealousy/envy); mount the lion.
  - **Ekadanta** ("one-tusk") - slays **Madāsura** (pride/arrogance/intoxication).
  - **Mahodara** ("big-belly") - slays **Mohāsura** (delusion/infatuation).
  - **Gajavaktra / Gajānana** ("elephant-face") - slays **Lobhāsura** (greed).
  - **Lambodara** ("pendulous-belly") - slays **Krodhāsura** (anger).
  - **Vikaṭa** ("misshapen/huge") - slays **Kāmāsura** (lust/desire); mount the peacock.
  - **Vighnarāja** ("lord of obstacles") - slays **Mamāsura** (attachment/possessiveness).
  - **Dhūmravarṇa** ("smoke-coloured") - slays **Ahaṃtāsura** (ego/self).
- **The 21 (Ekaviṃśati) frame** - Ganapatya worship layers the *Ekaviṃśati-nāmāvalī* (21 names of Ganesha) and the offering of **21 modakas / 21 dūrvā (durva grass) blades** in odd-numbered tufts. Pilgrims commonly offer 21 modakas as *naivedya* at each node. The "21 forms" rosters vary by recension and are **[folk]/open-ended** rather than a closed canonical list.
- **Pradakṣiṇā note** - several nodes prescribe a distinctive circumambulation count (Siddhatek famously requires a long **7-pradakshina** hill-circuit around the temple's rock).

## The Distinctive Common Markers

- **Trunk-curl direction (*sūṇḍ*)** - seven of the eight murtis have the trunk curled to the **left** (*vāmamukhī* / *ḍāvyā sondechā*), associated with the lunar *iḍā nāḍī*, accessibility, and householder prosperity. **Only Siddhivinayak (Siddhatek)** has the trunk curled to the **right** (*ujavyā sondechā* / *dakṣiṇābhimukha* power), aligned with the solar *piṅgalā nāḍī* — held to be more potent, more austere, and harder to propitiate.
- **Consorts** - most murtis are flanked by or seated with **Siddhi and Riddhi** (or Buddhi), the personified powers of accomplishment and prosperity.
- **Swayambhu form** - the idols are deliberately rough/un-anthropomorphic; several are little more than a vermilion-smeared rock-face with eyes and trunk marked, reinforcing the *self-arisen* claim against the polished carved icon.

## The Fixed Yatra Order — Begin and End at Morgaon

The traditional *śāstra*-prescribed sequence is a closed loop: it **begins at Moreshwar (Morgaon)** and, after the other seven, **returns to Morgaon** to complete the yatra (the return darshan is mandatory for the pilgrimage to "count"). The canonical order:

1. **Moreshwar / Mayureshwar** — Morgaon
2. **Siddhivinayak** — Siddhatek
3. **Ballaleshwar** — Pali
4. **Varadvinayak** — Mahad
5. **Chintamani** — Theur
6. **Girijatmaj** — Lenyadri
7. **Vighneshwar / Vighnahar** — Ozar
8. **Mahaganapati** — Ranjangaon
9. **→ back to Moreshwar (Morgaon)** to close the circuit.

- *Variant (practical routing):* modern 3-day tour operators run a geographically efficient loop and do not always honor the scriptural begin-and-end-at-Morgaon rule; the **devotional** prescription nonetheless fixes Morgaon as both *ādi* (first) and concluding node. The "first and foremost" status of Morgaon is itself part of the tradition's self-understanding.

## The Eight Nodes — Idol, Legend, History

- **1. Moreshwar / Mayureshvara — Morgaon** (Pune dist., on the **Karha river**; ~55–65 km from Pune). The *ādi* (first/chief) and concluding node of the yatra.
  - *Idol:* trunk curled **left**, three-eyed; a cobra (**Nāgarāja**) coils protectively over it; flanked by **Siddhi and Riddhi**. The temple is built of black stone with **four gates** (four yugas / four directions), corner minaret-like towers, and a large **Nandi** before the sanctum (unusual for a Ganesha temple — said to have been en route to a Shiva temple and halted here). (ashtavinayakyatra.in; Wikipedia)
  - *Sthala-purana:* King **Chakrapani** of Mithila and queen **Ugra** propitiated Sūrya and bore a son, **Sindhu**, who became the asura **Sindhurāsura**. Ganesha, riding his **peacock (*mayūra*)** vahana — hence *Mayūreśvara* — slew him; **Morgaon** ("peacock-village," *mor* = peacock) is where the demon's head fell. The murti is said to have been **consecrated twice by Brahma**, once before and once after Sindhura's depredations. (Gaṇeśa Purāṇa; Wikipedia)
  - *History:* fortified-looking temple ascribed to the **Bahamani** period, with defensive towers built to deter Mughal-era raids. The 13th–14th c. Ganapatya saint **Morya Gosavi** is deeply linked to this *kṣetra* (his samadhi is at nearby Chinchwad). [historic]

- **2. Siddhivinayak — Siddhatek** (Ahmednagar dist., on the **Bhima river**; near Daund). The **only** Ashtavinayaka idol with a **right-curled trunk**.
  - *Idol:* faces **north**, trunk to the **right** (*ujavī sondh*), ~3 ft tall and 2.5 ft wide; **Riddhi and Siddhi** seated on one thigh. The right trunk marks it as the *siddhi-pīṭha* — potent but demanding. Pilgrims perform a long **7-fold pradakshina** around the temple hill. (Wikipedia; ashtavinayakyatra.in)
  - *Sthala-purana:* at creation's edge the asuras **Madhu and Kaiṭabha** (sprung from Vishnu's ear-wax) seized the Vedas / threatened Brahma; **Vishnu** could not subdue them until he worshipped Ganesha **here** and obtained *siddhi* — then prevailed (in some tellings as **Hayagrīva**). The hillock is *Siddha-ṭeka*, "the mound of accomplishment." (Wikipedia; cf. Devī-Māhātmya frame for Madhu-Kaiṭabha)
  - *History:* inner sanctum built by **Ahilyabai Holkar** (18th c.); the access road and ghats attributed to the Peshwa general **Haripant Phadke**. Saints **Morya Gosavi** and **Narayan Maharaj** associated with realization here. [historic]

- **3. Ballaleshwar — Pali** (Raigad dist., Konkan; between **Sarasgad fort** and the **Amba river**; ~110–125 km from Mumbai). The only Ashtavinayaka named after a **devotee**, not the deity.
  - *Idol:* faces **east**, trunk to the **left**, with **diamonds set in the eyes and navel**; the murti leans against a stone backrest; a **Mūṣika** (mouse) with modaka sits before it. Temple is oriented so that at/after the winter solstice the **sun's rays fall directly on the murti**. (Gaṇeśa Purāṇa; Wikipedia)
  - *Sthala-purana:* in the **Treta Yuga**, the boy **Ballāl**, son of the merchant **Kalyāṇ-seth (Kalyani)**, gathered village children to worship a great stone as Ganesha in the forest. Enraged villagers complained; his father **beat him, scattered the worship, and left him bound to a tree**. The dying boy's devotion summoned Ganesha, who freed and blessed him, granting that **his name precede the Lord's** — *Ballāl-eśvara* — and merged into the worshipped stone (*Dhuṇḍi Vinayaka* is the original stone, worshipped first). (Gaṇeśa Purāṇa; Wikipedia)
  - *History:* wooden shrine rebuilt in **stone (1760, by Nana Phadnavis / Krishnaji Khedekar)**; the hall's **eight carved pillars** mark the eight directions; a large bell gifted by **Chimaji Appa** (brother of Bajirao I) hangs here. *Specialty:* prasad is **besan laddu**, not the usual modaka. [historic]

- **4. Varadvinayak — Mahad** (Raigad dist., Konkan; near **Khopoli**, off the Mumbai–Pune road). "Vināyaka who grants boons."
  - *Idol:* faces **east**, trunk to the **left**; a *nandādīp* (oil lamp) said to have **burned continuously since 1892**; four elephant murtis at the corners. *Specialty:* uniquely, **devotees may personally enter the sanctum vicinity** to offer worship directly to the murti. (Wikipedia; ashtavinayakyatra.in)
  - *Sthala-purana:* sage **Gṛtsamada** (Ṛgvedic ṛṣi of the *Gaṇānāṃ tvā* hymn, RV 2.23.1) worshipped Ganesha here after a curse / for liberation; Ganesha, pleased, granted boons (*varada*) and was enshrined as **Varada-Vināyaka**. (Wikipedia)
  - *History:* the murti was reportedly **found immersed in an adjoining lake in 1690 CE**; the temple built **1725 CE** by the Kalyan *subhedar* **Ramji Mahadev Biwalkar**. [historic]

- **5. Chintamani — Theur** (Pune dist.; at the **confluence (sangam) of the Mula, Mutha, and Bhima**; ~22–25 km from Pune). The Peshwa **family deity (*kuladaivata*)**.
  - *Idol:* faces **east**, trunk to the **left**, with a **carbuncle/precious gem and diamonds as eyes**; seated under (the legend of) the **Kadamba tree** — Theur's old name **Kadambanagara**. (Wikipedia)
  - *Sthala-purana:* the greedy prince **Guṇa** stole the wish-fulfilling **Chintāmaṇi** jewel from sage **Kapila**. Ganesha recovered it and slew Guṇa; when Kapila declined to keep the gem, Ganesha was adorned with it at the throat — hence **Chintāmaṇi Vināyaka**, the remover of mental anxiety (*chintā*). (Wikipedia)
  - *History:* shrine ascribed to **Dharanidhar Maharaj Dev**, ~a century before **Madhavrao I Peshwa** added the outer wooden *sabha-mandapa*. Madhavrao I, who venerated this as his *kuladaivata*, **died here (1772, of tuberculosis, aged ~27)**; his widow **Ramabai performed sati at Theur on 18 November 1772**. [historic]

- **6. Girijatmaj — Lenyadri** (Pune dist.; ~12 km from Narayangaon on the Pune–Nashik road; a **cave temple** in a Buddhist rock-cut complex). The only Ashtavinayaka set in a **cave**, and the only one in a hill of Buddhist *leṇas*.
  - *Idol:* faces **north**, trunk to the **left**; deliberately rough-hewn; **worshipped from the rear** of the cell, carved into the living rock. The hall (the 8th of ~18 caves) is **~53 ft long, 51 ft wide, 7 ft high with no supporting pillars**, reached by **~283–307 steps**. *Specialty:* **no electric lighting** — lit by daylight through the cave mouth. (Wikipedia; ashtavinayakyatra.in)
  - *Sthala-purana:* **Pārvatī (Girijā)** performed penance here desiring a son; **Girijā's ātmaja** ("Girijā's own son") = **Girijātmaja**, i.e. Ganesha as Parvati's child, born/installed at this spot. (Wikipedia)
  - *History:* the caves are originally **Buddhist (Hinayana, c. 1st–3rd c. CE)**, later repurposed for Ganesha worship. **Shivneri fort** (birthplace of Chhatrapati Shivaji) lies nearby. [historic]

- **7. Vighneshwar / Vighnahar — Ozar** (Pune dist.; on the banks of the **Kukadi river**, near Junnar). "Remover of obstacles."
  - *Idol:* faces **east**, trunk to the **left**, with **rubies in the eyes**, a **diamond on the forehead**, and a jewel in the navel; **Riddhi and Siddhi** at the sides. The temple has a golden *kalasha* and *shikhara* and is ringed by a **broad stone rampart wall** one can walk along the top of. (Wikipedia)
  - *Sthala-purana:* the demon **Vighnāsura** (created by **Indra** to wreck King **Abhinandana's** sacrifice) ravaged all ritual; Ganesha defeated him. Begging mercy, the demon was spared on condition he trouble none who worship Ganesha — and was granted that **his name be spoken before the Lord's**: hence **Vighna-hara / Vighneśvara** (he who *removes* obstacles, having been the obstacle-demon subdued). (Wikipedia)
  - *History:* the golden dome/spire and renovation attributed to **Chimaji Appa, c. 1785 CE**, after his victory over the Portuguese at Vasai. [historic]

- **8. Mahaganapati — Ranjangaon** (Pune dist.; ~50 km from Pune on the Pune–Ahmednagar/Shirur road). The most martial and "great" form, closing the outward leg before the return to Morgaon.
  - *Idol:* faces **east**, seated cross-legged on a **lotus** with a broad forehead, trunk to the **left**, flanked by **Siddhi and Riddhi**. *Specialty:* the temple is oriented so that during the **southward (dakṣiṇāyana) sun the rays fall directly on the murti**. A **[folk]/unverified** local belief holds a hidden original idol — *Mahotkaṭa* — with **ten trunks and twenty arms** lies vaulted beneath. (Wikipedia)
  - *Sthala-purana:* **Shiva himself worshipped Ganesha here** before marching to destroy **Tripurāsura** (the three flying demon-cities) — invoking the obstacle-remover to ensure victory. Shiva built the shrine at the worship-site; the town **Maṇipura** grew there, now **Ranjangaon**. This inverts the usual order: the great god Shiva propitiating his own son. (Wikipedia; cf. Tripurāntaka myth)
  - *History:* core temple dated to the **9th–10th c. CE**; the inner sanctum (*garbhagṛha*) built by **Madhavrao I Peshwa, 1790 CE**; worship rights vested in **Anyaba Dev**. [historic]

## Two Clocks — Scripture vs. Academic Dating

- **Scriptural time:** the legends place the manifestations across multiple yugas (Ballal in Treta; Madhu-Kaitabha at creation's edge; Shiva–Tripurasura in cosmic prehistory) — i.e. the kṣetras are framed as primordial, their *worship* enduring into the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** of the 28th Mahayuga.
- **Academic time:** the **Gaṇeśa and Mudgala Purāṇas are late-medieval (c. 13th–16th c. CE)**; the temples in their present stone form are overwhelmingly **17th–18th c. CE Maratha/Peshwa** constructions (Holkar, Phadke, Phadnavis, Chimaji Appa, Madhavrao I). The Ganapatya elevation of Ganesha to *supreme Brahman* that underwrites the circuit is itself a Deccan-medieval development. [scholarly]
