# Chitrakoot — Kamadgiri and the Forest-Tirtha of Rāma's Exile

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](README.md) → **Chitrakūṭa / Kāmadgiri**. The first-tier living *Rāmāyaṇa* pilgrimage town on the **Uttar Pradesh–Madhya Pradesh** border where **Rāma, Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa** spent the early **eleven of their fourteen** exile years; the site of the **Bharata-milāp**, where Bharata received Rāma's **pādukā** (sandals) to rule as regent; the home-tīrtha of **Tulsīdās**; and — after [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md) — the most-visited Rāma site in India.
>

## I. Identity, name & the "two clocks"

- **Citrakūṭa = "the hill of many wonders / the peak of marvels"** — *citra* ("variegated, wondrous, picture-like") + *kūṭa* ("peak, summit, mass"): the forested ridge-country of the **Vindhya** foothills along the **Mandākinī** river, praised by Vālmīki as a place where the very sight of the hill, river and groves drives away grief. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 56)
- **Scriptural clock (Tretā-yuga)** — within the *Rāmāyaṇa* frame Citrakūṭa belongs to the **Tretā-yuga** exile of Rāma, the early forest-home before the move deeper south to **Pañcavaṭī / Daṇḍaka**; this is the mythic register of every site below. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*) — see the **[two-clocks](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/2-ayodhya-kanda.md)** scriptural-vs-historical distinction.
- **Historical clock** — as a *living* tīrtha Citrakūṭa is a continuously worshipped pilgrimage landscape elaborated by the **Purāṇas**, the medieval **Rāmānandī** and **Nimbārka/Vaiṣṇava** establishments, and above all by **Tulsīdās** (16th c. CE); the temples, ghāṭs and the Kāmadgiri *parikrama* in their present form are medieval-to-modern overlays on the epic ground. [scholarly]
- **Twin-state geography** — the *kṣetra* straddles the modern border: **Karwi / Chitrakoot district (UP)** holds much of the town, while the core sacred hill **Kāmadgiri** and several tīrthas lie in **Satna district (MP)**; pilgrims cross the line without noticing it. [regional]

## II. Citrakūṭa in the Rāmāyaṇa (Ayodhyā-Kāṇḍa, sargas 50–56)

- **Choice of Citrakūṭa on Vālmīki's counsel** — after crossing the **[Gaṅgā at Śṛṅgaverapura](prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md)** with **Guha** the Niṣāda and the **Yamunā**, Rāma reaches the **[Prayāga](prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md)** hermitage of sage **Bharadvāja**, who directs the party to settle at **Citrakūṭa**, ten *krośa* off, as the ideal *tapovana*. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 54)
- **The leaf-hut (parṇa-śālā) on the Mandākinī** — at Citrakūṭa **Lakṣmaṇa builds a thatched *parṇa-kuṭī*** and Rāma performs the housewarming rites; the three live here in idyllic forest-domesticity, Rāma describing the hill's birds, deer, flowering trees and the **Mandākinī's** waters to Sītā. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 56)
- **Sage Vālmīki's āśrama (variant of the hermit-host)** — tradition also seats the hermitage near **Vālmīki's** own retreat, weaving the epic's author into its landscape.
  - *Variant (later residence):* in the *Uttara-kāṇḍa* it is **Vālmīki's āśrama** (commonly localised in this same forest belt) that shelters the **banished Sītā** and is the birthplace of **Lava and Kuśa** — a second, later Citrakūṭa-adjacent episode kept distinct from the exile-stay. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara-kāṇḍa*) [disputed — site identification varies]
- **Eleven of the fourteen years** — Citrakūṭa is the **longest single residence** of the exile, traditionally **eleven years and some months**, before disturbances by *rākṣasas* and the wish for deeper solitude drive the party to **Atri's āśrama** and on to **Daṇḍaka / Pañcavaṭī**. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 1; pilgrimage tradition)

## III. The Bharata-milāp — the heart-event of the kṣetra

- **Bharata's march to Citrakūṭa** — learning of his mother **Kaikeyī's** boons and his father **Daśaratha's** death, **Bharata** refuses the throne and leads the whole of Ayodhyā — mothers, ministers, **Vasiṣṭha**, the army — to Citrakūṭa to beg Rāma to return and reign. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 99–112) — see [Ayodhyā-Kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/2-ayodhya-kanda.md)
- **The reunion of the brothers (Bharata-milāp)** — Rāma and Bharata meet in grief and love; Rāma, bound by *dharma* to honour his father's word, **refuses to cut short the exile**, and the brothers debate kingship and duty before the assembled sages. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 100–111)
- **The pādukā (sandals) given as regent-token** — Rāma surrenders his **gold-bedecked sandals**; Bharata sets them on the throne and returns to rule **as their servant from Nandigrāma**, governing Kosala in Rāma's name for the remaining exile. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 112) — the **Bharat Milāp Mandir** at Citrakūṭa marks the meeting-spot, preserving stone "footprints" said to be sunk by the weight of the brothers' embrace. [folk]

## IV. Kāmadgiri (Kāmtānāth) — the wish-granting hill

- **Kāmadgiri = "the mountain that fulfils desire"** — *kāma* ("wish") + *da* ("granting") + *giri* ("hill"): the low, densely forested horseshoe-ridge held to be the **original Citrakūṭa** of the epic and the very form of **Rāma himself** (worshipped as **Kāmtānāth**, "lord who fulfils longing"). (*Citrakūṭa-māhātmya*; sthala-tradition)
- **The Mukhāravinda and the four faces** — the hill is venerated **as a deity, not climbed**; pilgrims revere the **Mukhāravinda** ("lotus-face") shrine at its foot, with four "face" shrines (*mukha*) marking the cardinal points of the living mountain-god. (sthala-tradition) [regional]
- **The Kāmadgiri Parikramā (~5 km)** — the **circumambulation of the hill** is the central ritual act of Citrakūṭa, a barefoot ~5 km circuit past hundreds of small shrines, the **Bharat Milāp**, **Mukhāravinda** and the *Sākṣī-Gopāl* / Lakṣmaṇa shrines; performed especially on **Amāvasyā** and on the great festival days below. (pilgrimage tradition)
- **Festival concentration** — the *parikrama* draws its largest crowds on **Amāvasyā** (especially **Somavatī Amāvasyā**), **Dīpāvalī**, **Makara Saṅkrānti**, **Rāma Navamī** and the autumn fairs; the **Dīpadāna** of lamps around the hill at Dīpāvalī recalls the lights of Rāma's forest-home. [folk]

## V. The tīrtha-circuit on the Mandākinī

- **Rāmghāṭ (Rāmaghāṭa)** — the principal bathing-ghāṭ on the **Mandākinī (Payasvinī)** river in the town, where Rāma and Sītā are said to have bathed and which Tulsīdās frequented; the evening **ārtī** and the *Mandākinī* boat-darśana centre here. (sthala-tradition; Tulsī tradition)
- **Sphaṭik Śilā ("the crystal rock")** — a riverside rock on the Mandākinī bearing impressions held to be **Rāma's and Sītā's footprints**; in *Rāmcaritmānas* tradition this is where **Jayanta** (Indra's son in crow-form) pecked Sītā's foot and was punished by Rāma's **brahmāstra**-reed, losing one eye. (*Rāmcaritmānas*, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa; *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara-kāṇḍa* 38 [Jayanta-crow]) [folk localisation]
- **Jānakī Kuṇḍ** — a bathing-pool on the Mandākinī upstream, the spot where **Sītā (Jānakī)** is said to have bathed during the residence; a quiet riverside shrine-cluster. (sthala-tradition)
- **Gupt Godāvarī ("the hidden Godāvarī")** — a pair of cave-shrines where a spring-fed stream flows out of the rock; tradition makes it a **secret outlet of the Godāvarī** and a chamber where Rāma held court, the inner cave's narrowing passage and natural "throne" shown as his audience-hall. (sthala-tradition) [folk]
- **Hanumān Dhārā** — a hillside shrine where a perennial water-trickle falls on a **Hanumān** image, held to be the stream Rāma directed onto Hanumān to cool the **fire of his tail** after the burning of Laṅkā. (sthala-tradition) — see [Hanumān](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md)
- **Bharatkūp ("Bharata's well")** — a well some distance off where **Bharata** is said to have poured the waters of all the tīrthas gathered for Rāma's (refused) coronation; **Atri** advised him to store them here, sanctifying the spot. (*Citrakūṭa-māhātmya*; local tradition) [regional]
- **Atri & Anasūyā āśrama (Anusuiyā)** — upstream on the Mandākinī, the hermitage of sage **Atri** and his wife **Anasūyā**, who counsels Sītā on *pati-vrata-dharma* and gifts her divine ornaments; the **descent of the Mandākinī** itself is credited to **Anasūyā's** *tapas* bringing the river down to relieve a drought. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 117–118; *Citrakūṭa-māhātmya*)

## VI. Sthala-māhātmya — Citrakūṭa's glorification

- **Purāṇic praise** — Citrakūṭa is glorified across the **Padma Purāṇa** and **Skanda Purāṇa** tīrtha-sections (a **Citrakūṭa-māhātmya** circulates in this stream), which exalt the **Mandākinī** bath and the **Kāmadgiri** circuit as destroyers of sin and granters of every *kāma*, the hill being **Rāma's own embodied presence**. (*Padma Purāṇa*; *Skanda Purāṇa*; *Citrakūṭa-māhātmya*) [late text]
- **The river that wipes grief** — the *māhātmya* logic echoes Vālmīki's own line that *seeing* Citrakūṭa banishes sorrow: bathing in the **Mandākinī / Payasvinī** and circling Kāmadgiri yields the merit of the great tīrthas without the journey. (*Citrakūṭa-māhātmya*; *Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* 56)
- **Standing in the network** — though **not a Sapta-Purī**, Citrakūṭa ranks among the foremost **Rāma-tīrthas**, paired in pilgrim imagination with [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md), [Prayāga](prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md) and Nāsik/Pañcavaṭī as stations of the exile; see the wider [Sapta Purī & Sacred Cities](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md) frame. [scholarly]

## VII. The Tulsīdās / Rāmcaritmānas layer

- **Tulsīdās's home-tīrtha** — **Gosvāmī Tulsīdās** (1532–1623 CE) lived and worshipped at Citrakūṭa, and parts of the **[Rāmcaritmānas](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/2-ayodhya-kanda.md)** are traditionally tied to it; his **Kavitāvalī** and **Vinaya-patrikā** voice intense Citrakūṭa-devotion. (Tulsī tradition; Wikipedia)
- **The vision of Rāma (the famous tilak-darśana)** — the best-loved legend has **Hanumān** (in parrot-form) lead Tulsīdās to Rāmghāṭ, where Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa appear as **young hunters on horseback**; Tulsīdās, lost in grinding sandal-paste, fails to recognise them until Hanumān reveals it — the origin of his couplet *"Citrakūṭ ke ghāṭ par bhai santan kī bhīr…"*. (Tulsī hagiography; *Bhaktamāl* tradition) [folk]
- **A centre of Rāmcaritmānas devotion** — Citrakūṭa is today a dense corridor of **Rāmāyaṇī** maṭhas, *Rāmlīlā* and *Mānas*-recitation, the **Tulsī Jayantī** and **Dīpāvalī** *Dīpadāna* anchoring its devotional calendar. [regional]
