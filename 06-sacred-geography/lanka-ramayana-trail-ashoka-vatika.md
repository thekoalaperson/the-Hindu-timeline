# Aśoka-vāṭikā & the Laṅkā Rāmāyaṇa Trail

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](README.md) → **Aśoka-vāṭikā & the Laṅkā Rāmāyaṇa Trail**. The Laṅkā-side memory-landscape of the *Rāmāyaṇa* — above all the **Aśoka-vāṭikā**, the grove where **Rāvaṇa** held **Sītā** captive and where **Hanumān** found her, delivered Rāma's signet-ring and took back her crest-jewel (*Sundara Kāṇḍa*) — read on the scriptural clock in **Tretā Yuga** of the 28th Mahāyuga, on the historical clock a living **Sri Lankan Rāmāyaṇa-trail** pilgrimage geography centred on the **Sītā Eliya / Seetha Amman temple** at Hakgala near Nuwara Eliya.

## I. Identity & the "two clocks"

- **Aśoka-vāṭikā = "the grove of the aśoka-trees"** — the pleasure-garden inside Rāvaṇa's island-city of **Laṅkā** where the abducted **Sītā** is confined among *aśoka*, *campaka* and *sāla* trees, guarded by hideous **rākṣasīs**, refusing Rāvaṇa and awaiting Rāma; also called **Aśoka-vana** / **Pramadā-vana** ("garden of the ladies"). (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍa*) — see [Sundara Kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md)
- **Scriptural clock (Tretā-yuga)** — within the epic frame every event below belongs to the **Tretā-yuga** of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara, in the final phase of Rāma's fourteen-year exile, in the months after Sītā's abduction from [Pañcavaṭī / Daṇḍaka](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md) and before the **Setu-bandha** crossing from [Rāmeśvaram](rameswaram-setu-bandha.md). (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*) — the mythic register; keep the **two-clocks** scriptural-vs-historical distinction.
- **Historical clock** — as a *living* landscape this is a continuously elaborated devotional geography, fixed in its present touristic-pilgrimage form by the **modern Sri Lankan "Rāmāyaṇa Trail"** (a state-tourism and Hindu-pilgrimage circuit promoted from the 2000s onward) onto sites in the central highlands and the west coast. [scholarly]

## II. The Aśoka-vāṭikā in the Sundara Kāṇḍa

- **Hanumān's leap and search of Laṅkā** — having crossed the ocean in a single bound, **Hanumān** enters Rāvaṇa's golden city by night, searches the palace and the inner apartments in vain, and at last comes upon the **Aśoka-vāṭikā**, reasoning that the grieving Sītā would be kept apart in a grove. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍa*, sargas 14–15)
- **Sītā discovered beneath the śiṃśapā** — Hanumān, shrunk small, hides in a **śiṃśapā (aśoka) tree** and beholds **Sītā** — gaunt, soiled, hemmed by **rākṣasī** wardresses, weeping among the flowering trees — recognising her by Rāma's described ornaments. (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* 15–16)
- **Rāvaṇa's wooing and Sītā's refusal** — Rāvaṇa comes to the grove to court Sītā with threats and blandishments; she repels him with a blade of grass between them (the *tṛṇa*), and he sets her a deadline on pain of death, the rākṣasīs menacing her after he leaves. (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* 18–23)
- **The signet-ring (aṅgulīya)** — to win Sītā's trust without alarming her, Hanumān drops **Rāma's signet-ring** engraved with his name before her; she takes it as proof the monkey is Rāma's true envoy — the emotional pivot of the *kāṇḍa*. (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* 36) — see [Sundara Kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md)
- **The cūḍāmaṇi (crest-jewel) token** — Sītā gives Hanumān her **cūḍāmaṇi** (hair-jewel) and recalls the private **kākāsura** (crow-demon) episode as a recognition-token only Rāma would know, to carry back as proof she lives. (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* 38)
- **The grove laid waste** — to provoke the rākṣasas, Hanumān **uproots and devastates the Aśoka-vāṭikā** (sparing the tree by Sītā), slays the warriors sent against him, and is finally bound by **Indrajit's** *brahmāstra*. (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* 41–48)
- **Laṅkā-dahana (the burning)** — captured and his tail set alight, Hanumān leaps roof to roof and **burns Laṅkā**, then quenches his tail in the sea and recrosses to report to Rāma — the act that gives the highlands their later "scorched" etiologies. (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* 52–55)

## III. The Sītā Eliya / Seetha Amman identification

- **Sītā Eliya = the trail's Aśoka-vāṭikā** — the modern Sri Lankan Rāmāyaṇa trail identifies the captivity-grove with **Sītā Eliya** ("Sītā's plain/light"), a hamlet near **Hakgala**, on the road between **Nuwara Eliya** and **Welimada** in the central highlands of Sri Lanka. [regional]
- **The Seetha Amman Kovil** — a small, brightly-painted **Hindu temple** stands at the site beside the **Sītā Eliya stream (Sītā Oya)**, enshrining **Sītā** together with **Rāma, Lakṣmaṇa and Hanumān**; it is held to be the only temple in the world dedicated to Sītā as captive, and is an active *pūjā* and pilgrimage site. [regional]
- **The stream where Sītā bathed** — devotees point to the **Sītā Oya** beside the kovil as the brook where Sītā bathed during her captivity; offerings are floated and the water treated as *tīrtha*. [folk]
- **Hanumān's footprints in the rock** — round depressions in the boulders of the streambed are shown as **Hanumān's footprints**, of varied sizes — explained as the marks of the shape-shifting monkey who could grow or shrink at will. [folk]
- **The Hakgala "scorch" etiology** — local tradition reads the nearby **Hakgala** rock and gardens as part of the grove, and a class of trail-legends explains patches of barren or darkened ground across the highlands as scorched by **Hanumān's burning tail** (the *Laṅkā-dahana*). [folk]

## IV. The wider Laṅkā trail (context nodes)

- **Rāvaṇa's other captivity-sites (rival groves)** — because the trail is a modern composite, **other locales also claim the Aśoka-vāṭikā / Sītā-confinement** memory beyond Sītā Eliya; the central-highland cluster (Nuwara Eliya district) is the dominant identification, but the roster of "where Sītā was kept" varies by guide. [disputed]
  - *Variant (multiple holding-places):* some trail-tellings have Rāvaṇa **move Sītā** between sites — a flying-palace (*Puṣpaka*) itinerary — to harmonise competing local claims into one captivity-journey. [folk]
- **Ravana-connected highland sites** — the trail strings the Aśoka-vāṭikā together with other Rāvaṇa-themed stops — caves and falls named for Rāvaṇa near **Ella** (the **Rāvaṇa Ella falls** and cave), and peaks read as his strongholds — forming a "where Sītā was held / where Rāvaṇa ruled" highland leg. [regional]
- **Divurumpola — the agni-parīkṣā site** — the trail also marks **Divurumpola** ("place of oath") as the spot of Sītā's **fire-ordeal (agni-parīkṣā)** after the war; though Vālmīki sets the ordeal on the Laṅkā battlefield, the trail localises it here. [regional/late identification]
- **Relation to the mainland Rāmāyaṇa kṣetras** — these Laṅkā nodes are the far terminus of a Rāmāyaṇa pilgrimage axis running [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md) → [Pañcavaṭī / Daṇḍaka](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md) → [Kiṣkindhā / Hampi](kishkindha-hampi-anegundi.md) → [Rāmeśvaram / Setu-bandha](rameswaram-setu-bandha.md) → **Laṅkā**, the southern reach of the [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/README.md) memory-geography. — see [Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)

## V. Status, authority & cautions

- **Vālmīki names the grove, not the modern map** — the *Sundara Kāṇḍa* fixes the **events** (discovery, ring, crest-jewel, devastation, burning) and the **name** Aśoka-vāṭikā; it does **not** geolocate them on any identifiable Sri Lankan place. The Sītā Eliya / Seetha Amman identification is a **modern devotional overlay**, not a textual datum. [scholarly]
- **A recent, tourism-shaped circuit** — unlike the anciently-attested *sthala-purāṇa* circuits of the mainland, the Sri Lankan Rāmāyaṇa Trail is a **contemporary construction** (promoted heavily from the early 2000s by Sri Lankan tourism and Hindu organisations) selecting and naming sites to materialise the epic; its node-roster is fluid. [scholarly]
- **The Laṅkā-=-Sri Lanka question** — whether Vālmīki's island Laṅkā *is* the historical island of Sri Lanka is itself **contested**; alternate hypotheses place the epic's Laṅkā elsewhere, so the entire trail rests on a prior, debated identification. [disputed] — cf. [Bhāratavarṣa & Purāṇic Geography](bharatavarsha-and-puranic-geography.md) on the gap between mythic and surveyed space.
