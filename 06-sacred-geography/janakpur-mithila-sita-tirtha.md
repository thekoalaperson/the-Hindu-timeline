# Janakpur / Mithilā — Sītā's Birthplace

> *Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](README.md) → **Janakpur / Mithilā (Sītā-tīrtha)**. The bride-side capital of the* Rāmāyaṇa *— King **Janaka's** city of **Videha-Mithilā**, birthplace of **Sītā (Jānakī, Maithilī)**, scene of the **Dhanuṣa-yajña** where [Rāma](ayodhya.md) strung and shattered **Śiva's bow (Pināka)** to win her, and home of the great **Jānakī Mandir** that is the living heart of Maithilī Sītā-bhakti.*
>

## I. The place & the "two clocks"

- **Mithilā = the realm, Janakpur = the city** — *Mithilā* is the ancient **Videha** kingdom of the north-eastern Gangetic plain, straddling the modern **Nepal–Bihar** border; **Janakpurdhām** (Dhanusha district, Madhesh Province, southern Nepal) is its surviving sacred capital-town, named for King **Janaka**. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 66; regional Maithilī tradition)
- **A Rāmāyaṇa tīrtha, not "merely" a pīṭha** — Janakpur is treated here primarily as a **living *Rāmāyaṇa* kṣetra** — the unique **bride-side** node of the epic pilgrimage map, terrestrial counterweight to [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md) — distinct from its separate identity as the **Mithilā [Śakti Pīṭha](51-shakti-peethas.md)** (Satī's left shoulder), which the house style keeps apart rather than merging. [scholarly]
- **Scriptural clock (Tretā-yuga)** — within the *Rāmāyaṇa* frame Sītā's birth, the bow-sacrifice and the four-fold wedding belong to the **Tretā-yuga** of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara — the mythic register of every narrative event below. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*) — see the **two-clocks** distinction at [Bāla-Kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md)
- **Historical clock** — as a *standing* pilgrimage town Janakpur is a medieval-to-modern overlay: the present **Jānakī Mandir** dates only to **1910–11 CE**, its founding relic to **1657 CE**, the literary Maithilī Sītā-cult to the post-**Vidyāpati** (15th c.) era — the epic ground and the visible shrines belong to different clocks. [scholarly]
- **Cross-border kṣetra** — the *Sītā-janmabhūmi* claim spans the line: **Janakpur (Nepal)** holds the temple-centre and the wedding-pavilion, while **Sitamarhi (Bihar)** asserts the actual furrow birth-spot at **Punaura-dhām**; pilgrims and the Maithilī cultural region cross both states. [regional] [disputed — birth-spot localisation]

## II. Sītā's earth-birth — the furrow of Mithilā (Bāla-kāṇḍa 66)

- **The plough uncovers the child** — preparing the sacrificial field for a *yajña* (in one telling, to break a drought), **Janaka (Sīradhvaja)** drives the plough and lifts an infant girl from the **furrow (sītā)**, taking her as his daughter. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 66)
- **Ayonijā — "not womb-born"** — Sītā is *ayonijā*, sprung from **Bhūmi (Earth)** herself; her names **Sītā** ("furrow"), **Jānakī** ("Janaka's") and **Maithilī** ("of Mithilā") all bind her to this soil. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 66)
  - *Variant (Lakṣmī incarnate):* Sītā is **Lakṣmī** descended to partner Viṣṇu's Rāma-avatāra, making Mithilā the birth-place of the Goddess of fortune. (*Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa*; Vaiṣṇava theology)
  - *Variant (Rāvaṇa's daughter):* in the **Adbhuta Rāmāyaṇa** and several Jaina tellings she is the abandoned daughter of **Rāvaṇa and Mandodarī**, buried for a prophecy and recovered in the furrow. [variant — non-Vālmīki]
- **Janaka the philosopher-king** — the foster-father is the *brahmavādin* sage-king of the Upaniṣadic dialogues (the *Bṛhadāraṇyaka* Janaka), so Mithilā is also a remembered **seat of Vedic *jñāna*** — the city where both wisdom and the Goddess were born. (*Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad*; tradition) [scholarly]

## III. The Dhanuṣa-yajña — Rāma breaks Śiva's bow (Bāla-kāṇḍa 66–67)

- **The Śiva-dhanus (Pināka)** — Janaka's house guards the colossal bow of **Śiva**, given to his ancestor **Devarāta** by the gods; no king has even stirred it. Janaka vows Sītā to whoever can string it — *vīrya-śulka*, "the bride-price of valour." (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 66–67)
- **Arrival under Viśvāmitra** — **Rāma** and **Lakṣmaṇa**, escorted by the sage **Viśvāmitra**, reach Mithilā and are received by Janaka and his priest **Śatānanda** (Ahalyā's son); Viśvāmitra asks that the boys be shown the famous bow. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 66)
- **Stringing & breaking the Pināka** — at Viśvāmitra's word **Rāma lifts the bow effortlessly, bends it to string — and it snaps with a thunderclap** that fells the onlookers; the feat wins Sītā's hand. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 67)
  - *Variant (internal discrepancy):* in the *Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa* Sītā recalls the bow as **Varuṇa's**, while *Bāla-kāṇḍa* names it Śiva's Pināka — a noted textual inconsistency. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Tulsīdās, [Rāmcaritmānas](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md)):* the Avadhī telling expands a full **dhanuṣa-yajña / svayaṃvara** — assembled kings fail, Sītā first sees Rāma in **Janaka's flower-garden (Phulwārī)**, Lakṣmaṇa's fiery taunts answer the scornful kings, then Rāma's effortless feat; this is the most popular North-Indian version, dramatized annually in Maithilī **Rāmlīlā**.
  - *Variant (Sītā's strength, folk):* as a child Sītā once lifted the bow's casket while sweeping, proving she alone "matched" the Pināka. [folk]
- **Dhanuṣā-dhām** — the bow-legend is anchored at **Dhanushadham**, ~18 km north-east of Janakpur, where a venerated **stone fragment** is shown as a shard of the shattered Pināka; it is a principal sub-tīrtha of the Janakpur circuit. (sthala-tradition) [regional]

## IV. The four-fold wedding — Vivāha Pañcamī (Bāla-kāṇḍa 70–73)

- **Daśaratha summoned** — Janaka sends word to Ayodhyā; **Daśaratha** arrives with **Vasiṣṭha**, and the genealogies of the **Ikṣvāku** and **Videha** lines are recited before the rite. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 70–71) — see [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md)
- **Kuśadhvaja's daughters** — Janaka's brother **Kuśadhvaja** brings his two daughters so that all four sons of Daśaratha may wed in one ceremony. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 71–72)
- **The quadruple marriage** — at Mithilā are solemnized **Rāma–Sītā** and **Lakṣmaṇa–Ūrmilā** (Janaka's daughters), with **Bharata–Māṇḍavī** and **Śatrughna–Śrutakīrti** (Kuśadhvaja's daughters). (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa* 73)
- **Vivāha Pañcamī** — the wedding-day is fixed on **Mārgaśīrṣa Śukla Pañcamī** and kept as **Vivāha Pañcamī**, Janakpur's greatest festival: a re-enacted *barāt* (groom's procession) brings "Rāma" from Ayodhyā to wed "Sītā," drawing pilgrims from across the Maithilī world. (Maithilī tradition) [regional]
- **The Mithilā wedding-paintings** — the Janaka–Daśaratha marriage is the founding subject of **Mithilā / Madhubani painting**, the region's ritual folk art adorning wedding walls (*kohbar*); the *Sītā-Rāma vivāha* is its central icon. (regional folk tradition) [folk]

## V. The Jānakī Mandir & the sacred topography

- **Jānakī Mandir (Nau Lakhā Mandir)** — the great white **Rajput–Mughal-domed** temple at the city-centre marking Sītā's birth/abode, built **1910–11 CE** by **Queen Vrishabhanu Kunwari of Tikamgarh** at a reputed cost of nine *lākh* (hence *Nau Lakhā*); it enshrines a **Sītā image said to have been found here by the sādhu Śūrkiśordās in 1657 CE**, the founding relic of the modern cult and the devotional heart of Maithilī Janakī-bhakti. (temple history; Wikipedia) [scholarly]
- **Vivāha Maṇḍap & the older Rām Mandir** — beside the Jānakī Mandir the **Vivāha Maṇḍap** marks the wedding-pavilion, and the older pagoda-style **Rām Mandir** (18th c., attrib. the Gorkhāli general **Amar Singh Thapa**) pre-dates the grand temple as the town's earlier core. (sthala-tradition) [regional]
- **The sacred ponds (sāgar / kuṇḍa)** — Janakpur is ringed with holy tanks — **Gaṅgāsāgar** and **Dhanuṣsāgar** chief among reputedly dozens — where pilgrims bathe; tradition holds the city once carried a great network of *kuṇḍas* serving the *yajña* and the temple-economy. (pilgrimage tradition) [regional]
- **Rāmānandī Sītā-upāsanā** — Janakpur is a stronghold of the **Rāmānandī** order and of *Sītā-upāsanā* (worship of Sītā as supreme, in the *rasika* and *sakhī* moods), making it the Goddess-side counterpart to Rāma-centred [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md). (sectarian tradition) [scholarly]

## VI. Pilgrimage — the cross-border Mithilā circuit

- **Janakpur Parikramā & the Mithilā-mādhava circuit** — the town anchors a local **parikramā** and the wider **Bara-mādhava / Mithilā** route linking Janakpur, **Dhanushadham** and **Jaleshwar (Nepal)** with the Bihar sites **Sitamarhi** and **Punaura-dhām**; walked especially at **Vivāha Pañcamī**, **Rāma Navamī** and **Sītā Navamī**. (pilgrimage tradition) — see [Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md) [regional]
- **Sitamarhi (Bihar) — the furrow birth-spot** — across the border, **Sitamarhi** (lit. "Sītā's mound") and **Punaura-dhām** claim the exact ploughing-furrow where Janaka found Sītā; the cross-state pair makes the *Sītā-janmabhūmi* a shared Nepal–Bihar tīrtha. [disputed]
- **A first-tier Rāmāyaṇa node** — though **not a Sapta-Purī**, Janakpur ranks with [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md) and [Chitrakūṭa](chitrakoot-kamadgiri.md) as a core station of the living *Rāmāyaṇa* pilgrimage map — the only major **bride-side** centre of the epic. (pilgrimage tradition) [scholarly]
- **Heart of Maithilī identity** — Janakpur is the cultural capital of the **Maithilī** language-region; the *Sītā-Rāma vivāha* underwrites Maithilī literature (from **Vidyāpati** onward), Madhubani art, and Mithilā's *kanyādāna* self-image as **"the bride's father" of all Hindustan**. (regional tradition) [regional]
- **Festival calendar** — **Vivāha Pañcamī** (the wedding), **Sītā Navamī / Jānakī Navamī** (Sītā's birth, *Vaiśākha Śukla Navamī*), **Rāma Navamī** and the regional **Chhaṭh** anchor the year, with *Rāmlīlā* and *Rāmāyaṇa* recitation saturating the calendar. [regional]
