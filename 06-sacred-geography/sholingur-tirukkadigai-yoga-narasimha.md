# Sholingur (Tirukkaḍigai) — the Twin-Hill Yoga Narasimha Kṣetra

> The hill-temple of **Ghaṭikācalam** at Sholingur (Tamil **Tirukkaḍigai**, "the place of the *ghaṭikā*") in Vellore district, Tamil Nadu — a [Divya Desam](108-divya-desams.md) where Viṣṇu stands on the **Periya-malai** ("big hill") as the meditating **Yoga Narasiṃha** with **Amṛtavalli Thāyār**, his fierce Man-Lion wrath cooled into yogic stillness; faced on the **Chinna-malai** ("small hill") by a rare hilltop shrine of **Yoga Anjaneya** (Hanumān). Famed across the Śrī Vaiṣṇava world for granting **clarity of mind and intellect** in a single *ghaṭikā* (~24 minutes) of worship.

**Sholingur** (also *Cōḷiṅghur*; Tamil **Tirukkaḍigai**, Sanskrit **Ghaṭikācalam** / **Ghaṭikādri**, "the hill of the *ghaṭikā* hour") lies in Vellore district between Kāñcīpuram and Tirupati. It is unique among the [108 Divya Desams](108-divya-desams.md) for being a **twin-hill** site: the taller **Periya-malai** (~750 ft, ~1,300 steps) bears the main shrine of **Bhaktavatsala Yoga Narasiṃha**, while the nearby **Chinna-malai** ("small hill," the *Takkaṇ Kuṉṟam*) carries a separate, much-visited shrine of **Yoga Anjaneya / Hanumān**. As elsewhere in this corpus the *traditional* (Yuga / Purāṇic) frame is kept distinct from the *historical* (epigraphic / sampradāya) frame — the **"two clocks."** [scholarly]

---

## The sthala-māhātmya — why the cooled Lion stands here

- **The pacified Narasiṃha** — the *Ghaṭikācala-māhātmya* (transmitted within the **Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa**'s *sthala* literature) holds that after the slaying of **Hiraṇyakaśipu** the **Ugra (fierce) Narasiṃha**, still burning, withdrew to this hill and settled into **yoga-mudrā** — the seated/standing meditative posture — so that the deity here is the *cooled* Man-Lion, **Yoga Narasiṃha**, rather than the killing form of [Ahobilam](ahobilam-nava-narasimha.md). (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, *Ghaṭikācala-māhātmya*)
- **The "ghaṭikā" efficacy** — the māhātmya's signature claim: worship offered here yields its fruit within a single **ghaṭikā** (one *ghaṭikā* = ~24 minutes, a sixtieth of a day) — hence *Ghaṭikācala* ("ghaṭikā-hill") and Tamil *Tirukkaḍigai*. The boon specifically named is **clarity of intellect, sound mind, and removal of mental affliction / madness** — the hill's enduring fame as a curer of confused or troubled minds. (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, *Ghaṭikācala-māhātmya*; Śrī Vaiṣṇava tradition)
  - *Variant (the golden hour, Ghaṭikācala):* one strand reads *ghaṭikā* as the **auspicious muhūrta** in which the devas' worship of the Lord here bore instant fruit, fixing the place-name to that "perfect hour." [regional]
  - *Variant (the alchemical / "pot" reading):* a folk gloss links *ghaṭi* to a **pot (*ghaṭa*)** of nectar (*amṛta*) tied to the consort **Amṛtavalli**, the hill as a vessel of life-giving grace. [folk]
- **Amṛtavalli Thāyār** — the goddess of the *kṣetra* is **Amṛtavalli** ("creeper of nectar"), Lakṣmī enthroned beside the meditating Lord — her name binding the hill's grace to *amṛta* (immortal nectar) and to the calming of the fierce form. (Sthala tradition; Nālāyira Divya Prabandham)
- **Brahmā's penance** — a strand of the māhātmya credits **Brahmā** with tapas on the hill, gaining *darśana* of the yogic Narasiṃha, by which the site is reckoned **svayam-vyakta**-tinged (divinely revealed) rather than merely founded. [traditional] [folk]

## The twin hills — the two shrines

- **Periya-malai — Yoga Narasiṃha (Bhaktavatsala)** — the great hill's summit shrine houses the *mūla* deity **Narasiṃha in yoga posture**, knees bound in the *yoga-paṭṭa* band, titled **Bhaktavatsala** ("loving to devotees"); the festival/consort goddess is **Amṛtavalli**, and the *vimāna* is named **Hema-koṭi (Kanaka) vimāna**. Reached by a long stepped climb, it is the Divya Desam proper. (Nālāyira Divya Prabandham; sthala tradition)
- **Chinna-malai — Yoga Anjaneya (Hanumān)** — the smaller hill carries one of South India's rare hilltop **Hanumān** shrines, **Yoga Anjaneya / Yoga Narasiṃha-Anjaneya**, shown four-armed in meditative posture bearing **śaṅkha and cakra** (a *sāttvika*, Viṣṇu-attribute Hanumān) — installed, by tradition, to **guard the cooled Narasiṃha** and to receive devotees first. He is invoked especially for **mental focus, courage, and intellect**, doubling the hill's "clarity of mind" fame. [regional] (sthala tradition)
  - *Variant (Doddācārya's installation):* tradition widely credits the **consecration of the Yoga Anjaneya icon** on the Chinna-malai to the ācārya **Doddācārya** (see below), who is said to have fixed Hanumān there to protect the kṣetra and its devotees from evil. [regional] [late text]
- **The pilgrim order** — custom is to climb the **Chinna-malai (Hanumān) first** and the **Periya-malai (Narasiṃha) after**, so that the guardian is honoured before the Lord — a sequencing echoing the satellite-then-summit logic of [Ahobilam](ahobilam-nava-narasimha.md)'s circuit. [regional]

## Place in the Nālāyira Divya Prabandham (the Āḻvār clock)

- **A Tondai-nāḍu Divya Desam** — Tirukkaḍigai is counted among the [108 Divya Desams](108-divya-desams.md), in the **Tondai-nāḍu** (Pallava north) group near Kāñcīpuram; it is one of the relatively few Divya Desams whose deity is a **Narasiṃha** form, alongside [Ahobilam](ahobilam-nava-narasimha.md). (Nālāyira Divya Prabandham)
- **Sung by Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār** — the *maṅgalāśāsanam* (benedictory verses) on Tirukkaḍigai are attributed to **Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār**, the same saint who sang [Ahobilam](ahobilam-nava-narasimha.md)'s Man-Lion — knitting the two Narasiṃha Divya Desams under one Āḻvār voice. (Nālāyira Divya Prabandham, *Periya Tirumoḻi*)
  - *Counting/attribution note:* the precise verse-count and whether additional Āḻvārs touched the hill vary across the standard rosters (*divyadesam.com*, *prapatti.com*); the canonical fact is its inclusion among the 108 and its association with Tirumaṅgai. [disputed]

## Doddācārya & the Śrī Vaiṣṇava sampradāya (the institutional clock)

- **Doddayācārya / Doddācārya** — the hill is intimately tied to **Doddācārya** (Doddayācārya), a revered post-Rāmānuja **Śrī Vaiṣṇava ācārya** associated with the Sholingur region (roughly the 16th–17th c. CE in the historical clock), remembered as a great devotee of the Yoga Narasiṃha and patron of the kṣetra's worship. [scholarly] (Śrī Vaiṣṇava sampradāya tradition)
  - *Variant (the protective Hanumān legend):* tradition holds that **Doddācārya**, troubled by hostile forces, **installed / propitiated the Yoga Anjaneya** of the Chinna-malai, who thereafter shielded the ācārya and the hill — the most popular local account of how the twin-hill pairing came to be. [regional] [folk]
- **A Rāmānuja-lineage abode** — as a Divya Desam, Sholingur sits inside the **Śrī Vaiṣṇava (Rāmānuja) sampradāya** grid headed by [Śrīraṅgam](srirangam-ranganatha.md); the daily *ārādhana* follows Pāñcarātra Vaiṣṇava usage, and the hill is woven into the ācārya-traditions that radiate from the delta heartland. [scholarly]

## Network & cross-links

- **The Narasiṃha Divya Desam pair** — devotionally Sholingur is read with [Ahobilam](ahobilam-nava-narasimha.md) as the **two Narasiṃha Divya Desams** of the Āḻvār canon — Ahobilam the *fierce, slaying* hill, Sholingur the *cooled, meditating* hill — the two poles of the Man-Lion's mood. [regional]
- **Beyond the canon** — it complements the non-canonical but great Narasiṃha kṣetra of [Siṃhācalam](simhachalam-varaha-narasimha.md) in the broader South-Indian Narasiṃha geography, while drawing its sampradāya authority from [Śrīraṅgam](srirangam-ranganatha.md). [regional]
- **The "clarity of mind" pilgrimage** — uniquely among Divya Desams, Sholingur is sought less for *mokṣa*-petition than for **intellectual lucidity, relief of mental illness, and success in study/examinations** — the living, distinctive draw of the *ghaṭikā* hill. [regional]
