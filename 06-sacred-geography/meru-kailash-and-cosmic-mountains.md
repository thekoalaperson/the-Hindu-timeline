# Mount Meru, Kailāsa & the Cosmic Mountains

> The vertical and horizontal architecture of the Hindu universe expressed in **stone**: **Meru/Sumeru** as the golden *axis mundi* that pins together the [fourteen lokas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md), the ring-system of **sapta-dvīpa** continents and **sapta-samudra** oceans of Purāṇic geography, **Kailāsa** as Śiva's terrestrial throne, the churning-rod **Mandara**, the winged fugitive **Maināka**, and the **kula-parvata** spines that bone *Bhārata-varṣa*. This node catalogs the rival cosmographies — the **Purāṇic flat sapta-dvīpa disc** versus the **Sūrya-Siddhānta spherical, polar Meru** — text by text.
>

A **cosmic mountain** in Hindu thought is rarely a surveyed peak: it is a *cosmographic device*, a pillar that holds apart sky and earth (*axis mundi*) or a boundary-wall (*maryādā-parvata*) that compartmentalises the world. The tradition runs **two parallel geographies** that were never fully reconciled: (1) the **Purāṇic** model — a vast flat lotus-disc (*bhū-maṇḍala*) of concentric ring-continents centred on Meru; and (2) the **astronomical / siddhāntic** model — a **spherical earth** (*bhū-gola*) with Meru as the polar axis. Pilgrimage tradition then *projects* these mythic mountains onto Himalayan peaks, treating earthly Kailāsa and the Himālaya as reflections of cosmic Meru.

## I. Mount Meru / Sumeru — the cosmic axis

- **Meru (Sumeru, Mahāmeru, Hemādri "golden mountain")** — the five-peaked golden mountain at the dead centre of *Jambudvīpa* and thus of the entire universe; the *axis mundi* around which the [fourteen lokas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) are stacked vertically and the dvīpas/oceans arrayed horizontally. Sun, moon and the stars are said to wheel around it. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.16; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.2; *Matsya Purāṇa*)
- **Dimensions** — given as **84,000 yojanas** tall (≈ 1,082,000 km / 672,000 mi at the common ~12.9 km/yojana, i.e. some 85× Earth's diameter), plunging **16,000 yojanas** below the earth's surface and rising 84,000 above; shaped like an inverted cone / lotus-pericarp, **broader at the summit (32,000 yojanas) than the base (16,000 yojanas)**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.16.7; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.2)
  - *Variant (yojana length):* because the **yojana** is itself unfixed (estimates from ~3 to ~13.5 km), every absolute figure for Meru is scale-elastic; the texts intend cosmic, not metric, magnitude. [disputed]
  - *Variant (Buddhist):* Buddhist cosmology gives **80,000 yojanas** wide and 80,000 high, with the gods of the *Cāturmahārājika* and *Trāyastriṃśa* heavens on its slopes and summit — a parallel, non-identical system (*Abhidharmakośa*). [scholarly]
- **Four faces / four materials** — the sides are made of precious substance, conventionally: **north = gold, east = crystal (silver), south = lapis-lazuli (sapphire-blue), west = ruby**; the blue southern face is said to tint the sky over India. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.16; *Mahābhārata*, Bhīṣma-parva)
  - *Variant (color↔direction mapping):* texts disagree on which jewel faces which quarter (e.g. gold-east vs gold-north). [disputed]
- **Brahmapurī at the summit** — atop Meru sits **Brahmapurī / Manovatī**, the square golden city of **[Brahmā](../00-time-and-cosmology/brahma-lifetime.md)** (the creator), the cosmic capital. Around it, on the summit plateau, stand the **cities of the eight Lokapālas** (the guardians of the directions — Indra's *Amarāvatī* in the east, Agni, Yama, etc.), so that Meru's top is a divine compass-rose. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.2; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.16)
- **The Gaṅgā's descent** — the celestial **Gaṅgā (Ākāśa-Gaṅgā / Mandākinī)** falls on Brahmapurī and divides into four (or more) cosmic rivers flowing to the four quarters off Meru's flanks (Sītā, Alakanandā, Cakṣus, Bhadrā). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.17) — see [sacred rivers](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md).
- **Function as world-axis** — Meru is the churning-pivot of cosmic events: it is the seat from which the *Manvantara* hierarchy is administered and the reference frame for the [devas vs asuras](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) (devas "above"/north, asuras "below"/south). [late text]

### The four buttress-mountains around Meru

Encircling Meru like the legs of a stool stand four **maryādā / viṣkambha** peaks, each bearing a colossal totem-tree and a sacred lake:

- **Mandara** — to the **east**, marked by a **mango (Devacūta)** tree; later the churning-rod of the [Samudra Manthana](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.16.8–11)
- **Merumandara / Gandhamādana** — to the **south**, bearing a **jambū (rose-apple)** tree whose fruit gives *Jambudvīpa* its name; the falling juice forms the river **Jambū-nadī** and the gold **jāmbūnada**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.16)
- **Supārśva** — to the **west**, bearing a giant **kadamba**; its flowing sap forms streams of honey. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.16)
- **Kumuda** — to the **north**, bearing a **banyan (vaṭa)** tree. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.16)
  - *Variant (Wikipedia/popular):* the four are sometimes listed as **Mandara (E), Supārśva (W), Kumuda (N) and Kailāsa (S)** — folding the Śaiva mountain into Meru's footstool. [folk]
  - *Variant (count):* some Purāṇas multiply these into rows of secondary peaks (*kesarācala*, "filament-mountains") radiating from Meru like stamens of a lotus. [late text]

## II. Jambudvīpa, its nine varṣas & the six varṣa-parvatas

Meru sits at the navel of **Jambudvīpa** ("rose-apple island"), the innermost continent — a disc **100,000 yojanas** across. Jambudvīpa is partitioned into **nine varṣas (regions)** by horizontal **varṣa-parvata** boundary-ranges running east–west, with **Ilāvṛta** (the Meru region) at the centre.

- **Ilāvṛta-varṣa** — the central square enclosing Meru itself; abode of Śiva and the gods, forbidden to ordinary mortals. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.17)
- **Bhārata-varṣa** — the **southernmost** region — *our* world, the unique **karma-bhūmi / puṇya-bhūmi** where dharma, action and liberation are possible and where the [yuga cycle](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md) actually turns; all other varṣas are *bhoga-bhūmi* (lands of mere enjoyment). (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.3; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.19)
- **The eight other varṣas** — going north: **Kimpuruṣa, Hari** (south of Meru); **Ramyaka, Hiraṇmaya, Kuru/Uttarakuru** (north of Meru); **Bhadrāśva** (east of Meru) and **Ketumāla** (west of Meru), flanking Ilāvṛta. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.16; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.2)
- **The six varṣa-parvatas (boundary-ranges)** — south to north: **Himavat, Hemakūṭa, Niṣadha** (south of Meru) and **Nīla, Śveta, Śṛṅgavān** (north of Meru) — the "ribs" that divide the varṣas; Bhārata lies south of **Himavat** (the Himālaya). (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.2; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.16)
  - *Variant (Markandeya Purāṇa):* describes Jambudvīpa as **depressed at north and south, elevated and broad in the middle**, the raised hump being Ilāvṛta / Meru-varṣa — a saddle-shaped, not flat, disc. [late text]

## III. The Sapta-dvīpa & Sapta-samudra (seven continents & seven oceans)

Beyond Jambudvīpa, the Purāṇic earth is a flat **bhū-maṇḍala** of **seven concentric ring-continents**, each **double** the width of the one inside it, each girdled by a ring-ocean of a **different liquid**:

- **Jambudvīpa** (rose-apple; Meru-centred) — encircled by the **ocean of salt-water (lavaṇa)**.
- **Plakṣadvīpa** (fig) — ocean of **sugarcane juice (ikṣu)**.
- **Śālmaladvīpa** (silk-cotton tree) — ocean of **wine / liquor (surā / madya)**.
- **Kuśadvīpa** (kuśa-grass) — ocean of **clarified butter (ghṛta / ghee)**.
- **Krauñcadvīpa** (curlew / heron) — ocean of **curds / yoghurt (dadhi)**.
- **Śākadvīpa** (teak / fig) — ocean of **milk (kṣīra)**.
- **Puṣkaradvīpa** (blue-lotus) — ocean of **sweet water (svādu-jala)**, the outermost.
(*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.20; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.4; *Matsya Purāṇa*; *Brahma Purāṇa* ch. 16)
  - *Variant (ordering of oceans):* the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* sequence (salt, sugarcane, wine, ghee, curd, milk, fresh water) differs in detail from other lists; the **third (wine) and fifth (curd)** are the most frequently swapped between recensions. [disputed]
  - *Variant (continent spelling/identity):* **Śālmala vs Śāmala**, and the tree-totems assigned to **Śāka** vs **Krauñca**, vary by Purāṇa. [late text]
- **Beyond the seventh ocean** — a ring of gold land (*Kāñcanī-bhūmi*), then the wall-mountain **Lokāloka** ("light-and-no-light") dividing the lit world from outer darkness, and finally the cosmic egg-shell. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.20.34–37)
- **Jambudvīpa = Sudarśanadvīpa** — in the *Bhāgavata* the central island is also called **Sudarśana**, viewed from above as a lotus. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.16)

## IV. Mount Kailāsa — Śiva's terrestrial abode

- **Kailāsa** — the snow-white Himalayan/Tibetan peak (the real **Mount Kailash**, ~6,638 m, in Tibet) revered as the **dwelling of [Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) and Pārvatī** with sons [Gaṇeśa and Kārttikeya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md); the divine assembly (*gaṇa*) court. (*Śiva Purāṇa*; *Liṅga Purāṇa*; *Matsya Purāṇa*)
- **Mānasarovar & Rākṣastāl** — the twin lakes at its foot: **Mānasa-sarovara** ("lake born of Brahmā's mind") and **Rākṣas-tāl**, the holiest of pilgrimage waters; the *parikramā* (circumambulation) of Kailāsa is a supreme [tīrtha-yātrā](README.md). (Britannica, *Mount Kailash*)
- **Abode of Kubera too** — Kailāsa is also the seat of **Kubera**, lord of wealth and of the **Yakṣas**, whose city **Alakā** sits on its slopes (the setting of Kālidāsa's *Meghadūta*). (*Mahābhārata*; *Meghadūta*)
- **Rāvaṇa lifts Kailāsa** — the *kailāsa-uddhāraṇa*: the demon-king **Rāvaṇa** tries to uproot the mountain; Śiva pins it with his toe, crushing Rāvaṇa, who then composes the *Śiva-tāṇḍava-stotra* — a favourite sculptural theme (Ellora, Kailāsa temple). (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara-kāṇḍa; *Śiva Purāṇa*)
- **Kailāsa ≡ Meru?** — *the central variant problem.* Popular and later theology **identifies Kailāsa with Meru** (or with the southern buttress of Meru), collapsing the terrestrial and cosmic mountains; but the older Purāṇic cosmography keeps them **distinct** — Meru is the trans-cosmographic golden axis, Kailāsa a varṣa-parvata peak in Bhārata's quarter that mortals can actually visit. (Wikipedia, *Mount Kailash*: "in later Hindu theology, Kailash came to be identified with Meru.")
  - *Variant (Jain):* Kailāsa = **Aṣṭāpada**, where the first Tīrthaṅkara **Ṛṣabhanātha** attained *mokṣa*. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Buddhist / Bön):* Kailāsa = **Mount Kailāsa / Tise / Kang Rinpoche**, abode of Cakrasaṃvara and the *axis mundi* of Bön. [scholarly]

## V. Other named mountains: Mandara, Maināka, Mahendragiri & Indrakīla

- **Mandara** — the buttress-peak (above, §I) requisitioned as the **churning-rod of the [Samudra Manthana](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** (Churning of the Ocean of Milk): devas and asuras wind the serpent **Vāsuki** around it as the rope; when it begins to sink, **Viṣṇu as Kūrma** (the tortoise, 2nd avatāra) bears it on his back. From the churning rise the fourteen *ratnas* (Lakṣmī, the moon, Airāvata, Ucchaiḥśravas, the *amṛta*, etc.). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.6–8; *Mahābhārata*, Ādi-parva; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 1.9)
- **Maināka** — son of **Himavat** (the Himālaya personified) and brother of **Gaṅgā** and **Pārvatī**; the one mountain that **kept its wings**. (*Rāmāyaṇa*; *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*; *Kathāsaritsāgara*)
  - **The winged mountains myth** — in primeval ages mountains **had wings** and flew about, crushing settlements when they landed; **Indra sheared off their wings** with his *vajra* to fix them in place. **Vāyu** (wind-god) spirited away his friend Maināka and hid him **in the ocean**, so Maināka alone retained his wings. (*Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*; *Kathāsaritsāgara*)
  - **Maināka & Hanumān** — when **Hanumān** leaps across the ocean to Laṅkā ([Sundara-kāṇḍa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md)), Maināka rises from the sea to offer him rest (a debt repaid to the wind-god, Hanumān's father); Hanumān touches it in courtesy but flies on. (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Sundara-kāṇḍa)
- **Gandhamādana, Niṣadha, Trikūṭa** — further named peaks: **Gandhamādana** (fragrant mountain near Badari, a tapas-site of the [Saptaṛṣis](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md) and of Hanumān's herb-quest); **Trikūṭa**, the three-peaked golden mountain on which **Laṅkā** is built. (*Mahābhārata*; *Rāmāyaṇa*)
- **Mahendragiri** — the great peak of the **Mahendra** kula-parvata (the Eastern Ghāts, in the Ganjam district of southern Odisha; cf. §VI), revered less as a range than as the **eternal abode of [Paraśurāma](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** (the immortal sixth avatāra, a *cirañjīvī*): after exterminating the kṣatriyas twenty-one times and then **ceding the conquered earth to Kaśyapa / to Rāma Dāśarathi**, Paraśurāma withdrew from worldly action and **retired to Mahendragiri to perform tapas until the end of the age** — making the mountain a living Paraśurāma-tīrtha rather than a mere geographic marker. (*Mahābhārata*, Vana-parva & Śānti-parva; *Rāmāyaṇa*, Bāla-kāṇḍa; *Bhārgava* legends)
  - **Hanumān's launch-point** — in the *Rāmāyaṇa* it is from the summit of **Mahendra(giri)** that **Hanumān makes his great leap across the ocean to Laṅkā**; he treads the peak down under the force of his spring at the opening of the [Sundara-kāṇḍa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md). (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Kiṣkindhā- & Sundara-kāṇḍa)
    - *Variant (which Mahendra):* the leap-mountain is sometimes localised to a southern (Malaya-region) Mahendra near the sea rather than the Odishan kula-parvata; the texts conflate **Mahendra the kula-parvata** and **Mahendra the coastal leap-peak**. [disputed]
- **Indrakīla** — a Himalayan peak (set near the **Gandhamādana** country) where, during the [Pāṇḍavas'](../04-deep-dives/characters/arjuna.md) forest exile, **Arjuna performed severe tapas** to win celestial weapons; there he fought **[Śiva] disguised as a Kirāta** (mountain-hunter) over a slain boar, and on being recognised was granted the **[Pāśupatāstra](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md)** — the episode forming the core of **Bhāravi's** Sanskrit *mahākāvya* the ***Kirātārjunīya***. (*Mahābhārata*, Vana-parva, Kairāta-parva; Bhāravi, *Kirātārjunīya*)

## VI. The Kula-parvatas — the seven spines of Bhārata-varṣa

Within Bhārata-varṣa itself the texts name **seven kula-parvatas** ("clan / principal mountains") — the major ranges that frame the regions of India proper, distinct from the cosmographic varṣa-parvatas:

- **Mahendra** — the Eastern Ghāts (Odisha–Andhra); its peak **Mahendragiri** is Paraśurāma's abode and Hanumān's leap-point — see §V above. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.3; *Matsya Purāṇa* 114)
- **Malaya** — the southern Western Ghāts / Kerala (sandalwood country).
- **Sahya** — the (northern) Western Ghāts (Sahyādri).
- **Śuktimat (Śuktimān)** — variously placed (eastern/central India).
- **Ṛkṣa (Ṛkṣavān)** — the Vindhya–Satpura belt (Maikal / Amarkantak, source of the Narmadā).
- **Vindhya** — the great east–west Vindhya range dividing north from south India.
- **Pāripātra (Pāriyātra)** — the western Aravalli / western Vindhya extension.
(*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.3; *Matsya Purāṇa* 114; *Mahābhārata*, Bhīṣma-parva)
  - *Variant (membership):* lists swap **Pāriyātra ↔ Pāripātra** spelling and occasionally substitute **Himavat** or a local range; the seven are a *type* more than a fixed roster. [disputed]
  - *Vindhya legend:* the Vindhya grows skyward in jealousy of Meru until the sage **Agastya** asks it to bow until he returns from the south — and never returns, fixing it low; the myth marks Agastya's "Aryanisation" of the Deccan. (*Mahābhārata*; *Padma Purāṇa*) [folk]
- **Each varṣa has its own seven** — the Purāṇas assert *every* varṣa of Jambudvīpa carries seven such kula-parvatas plus minor hills (*kṣudra-parvata*), making the scheme fractal. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.3)

## VII. Variant cosmographies — Purāṇic disc vs Siddhāntic globe

The single sharpest fault-line in Hindu cosmography is between the **devotional Purāṇic** model and the **mathematical astronomical (siddhāntic)** model. They use the *same vocabulary* (Meru, Jambudvīpa) for *incompatible* geometries.

- **Purāṇic model — flat bhū-maṇḍala** — Earth is a **horizontal disc** (*Bhāgavata* 5; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2): Meru a vertical pillar at the centre of Jambudvīpa, the **seven ring-continents and liquid oceans** spreading outward, the sun circling *over* the disc (so day/night are a matter of the sun's position relative to Meru, not a spinning globe). Distances are in **billions of yojanas**; the aim is theological and liturgical, not observational. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.16–26)
- **Siddhāntic model — spherical bhū-gola** — the **Sūrya-Siddhānta** (surviving form c. 4th–5th c. CE, revised ~800 CE) asserts **the Earth is a sphere (bhū-gola) hanging in space** — "this globe is in space where there is no above nor below." Here **Meru is the axis of the spherical Earth at the geographic North Pole**, with the **devas dwelling on the northern Meru** and a southern counter-pole (**Vaḍavāmukha / a southern Meru**) where the **asuras** dwell; the two pole-stars sit at the zeniths of the two Merus. (*Sūrya-Siddhānta* 12; Wikipedia, *Surya Siddhanta*)
  - **Four cardinal cities on the equator** — the siddhāntic Earth is gridded by four cities 90° apart on the equator: **Laṅkā (prime meridian, 0°), Yamakoṭi (east), Romaka / Romakapattana (west), Siddhapura (antipode)** — an early longitudinal coordinate system. (*Sūrya-Siddhānta*) [scholarly]
  - **Reconciliation attempts** — commentators and modern apologists variously read the Purāṇic *bhū-maṇḍala* as (a) a **polar-projection map** of the globe, (b) a description of the **solar system / planetary plane** rather than land, or (c) a different "plane of existence" altogether; traditionalists hold both as literally true on different scales. [disputed]
- **Net effect** — the **astronomers quietly accepted a round Earth** by the mid-first millennium CE while the **Purāṇic liturgical disc remained canonical for ritual and devotion** — the two were rarely forced to collide, and Hindu tradition carries both. [scholarly]
