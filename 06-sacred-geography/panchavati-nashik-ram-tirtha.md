# Pañcavaṭī — Nashik (Daṇḍaka Rāma-tīrtha)

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](README.md) → **Pañcavaṭī / Nāsik (Daṇḍaka Rāma-tīrtha)**. The Godāvarī-side tract of the **Daṇḍaka forest** where **Rāma, Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa** kept their last and most fateful exile-home — the *parṇa-śālā*, the mutilation of **Śūrpaṇakhā**, the **golden deer**, and the **Sītā-haraṇa** — now a living *tīrtha*-cluster (Sītā Gufā, Tapovan, Kapāleśvara) and one of the four **Kumbh Melā** cities.

## I. The site & the "two clocks"

- **Pañcavaṭī = "the grove of five banyans"** — *pañca* ("five") + *vaṭa* ("banyan"); a tract of **Daṇḍakāraṇya** on the south bank of the **Godāvarī**, chosen by Rāma for water, shade, fruit, flowers and *kuśa*-grass as the ideal *parṇa-śālā* ground. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 15)
- **Scriptural clock (Tretā-yuga)** — within the *Rāmāyaṇa* frame Pañcavaṭī is the **last forest-residence of the Tretā exile** before the abduction turns Rāma south toward Kiṣkindhā and Laṅkā; the mythic register of every episode below. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*) — see the [two-clocks](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md) scriptural-vs-historical distinction.
- **Historical clock** — as a *living* tīrtha, **Nāsik / Pañcavaṭī** is a continuously worshipped Godāvarī landscape elaborated by the **Gautamī-māhātmya**, by medieval-to-modern temple-building and ghāṭ-construction, and by the **Siṃhastha Kumbh** cycle; the present caves, tanks and shrines are later overlays on the epic ground. [scholarly]
- **Nāsik = "nose"** — the city-name is popularly derived from *nāsikā* ("nose"), memorialising the cutting of **Śūrpaṇakhā's** nose here; the riverside Pañcavaṭī quarter of the modern city still bears the epic name. [folk etymology / regional]

## II. The exiles' dwelling on the Godāvarī

- **Agastya names Pañcavaṭī** — pressing south through Daṇḍaka, Rāma reaches **Agastya's** āśrama; the sage who tamed the south gifts him **Viṣṇu's bow**, an inexhaustible quiver, the sword and arrows of **Indra** and **Brahmā**, and directs the party to settle at **Pañcavaṭī** on the Godāvarī, two *yojanas* off — the explicit scriptural anchor for the site. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 11–13) — see [Agastya](../04-deep-dives/agastya.md)
- **Jaṭāyu the watchman** — en route Rāma meets the aged vulture-king **Jaṭāyu**, a friend of **Daśaratha**, who pledges to guard **Sītā**; the bird becomes the guardian of Pañcavaṭī. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 14)
- **Lakṣmaṇa's leaf-hut** — at Pañcavaṭī **Lakṣmaṇa raises a spacious *parṇa-śālā*** of bamboo, thatch and clay by the river; the three settle into forest-domesticity through the seasons, Rāma describing the Godāvarī, the **Hemanta** frosts and the lotus-pools to Sītā. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 15–16)
  - *Variant (Nāsik sthala-tradition):* local lore fixes the hut-site at **Tapovan**, the riverside grove downstream where the *tapas*-residence and the Lakṣmaṇa episode are seated. [regional]

## III. Śūrpaṇakhā — the spark of the war

- **Śūrpaṇakhā's suit** — the *rākṣasī* **Śūrpaṇakhā** (sister of **Rāvaṇa**), wandering Daṇḍaka, is smitten with Rāma and demands him as husband; Rāma deflects her in jest toward Lakṣmaṇa, and Lakṣmaṇa back to Rāma. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 17)
- **The mutilation (nose & ears cut)** — lunging at **Sītā**, Śūrpaṇakhā is seized by Lakṣmaṇa who, at Rāma's word, **cuts off her nose and ears** — the act that gives **Nāsik** its name and lights the fuse of the whole war. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 18)
  - *Variant (regional):* Nāsik tradition fixes the wounding at a precise spot in the Pañcavaṭī tract, the city's very name standing as its topographic memorial. [regional / folk]
- **Khara, Dūṣaṇa & Janasthāna** — the bleeding Śūrpaṇakhā rouses her brothers **Khara, Dūṣaṇa and Triśiras**, rākṣasa-governors of **Janasthāna** (the Daṇḍaka district around Pañcavaṭī); **Rāma alone destroys the host of fourteen thousand**, clearing the district and goading **Rāvaṇa** toward abduction rather than open war. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 19–34)

## IV. The golden deer & the Sītā-haraṇa

- **The golden deer (māyā-mṛga)** — pressed on pain of death, **Mārīca** takes the form of a **jewelled golden deer** that grazes before the hut; **Sītā**, enchanted, begs Rāma to capture it, and Rāma pursues, posting Lakṣmaṇa to guard her. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 42–43)
- **The false cry** — struck by Rāma's arrow, **Mārīca** cries *"Hā Sītā! Hā Lakṣmaṇa!"* in Rāma's voice; Sītā, fearing for Rāma, drives Lakṣmaṇa to leave her against his will. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 44–45)
  - *Variant (Lakṣmaṇa-rekhā):* the protective line Lakṣmaṇa draws around the hut, which Sītā crosses, is **absent from Vālmīki** and belongs to later retellings (Rāmcaritmānas-stream, regional Rāmāyaṇas and folk drama). [late text / folk]
- **Rāvaṇa as mendicant** — disguised as a **sannyāsin**, Rāvaṇa approaches the undefended Sītā, reveals himself and **seizes her**, carrying her off in the aerial **puṣpaka**; the abduction is the pivot of the whole epic, seated at Pañcavaṭī. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 46–49)
- **Jaṭāyu's fall** — the old vulture-king **attacks Rāvaṇa in mid-air** and is felled when Rāvaṇa **cuts off his wings**; he survives only to give Rāma the direction of Sītā's flight and is cremated with a son's rites. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa* 50–51; 67–68)
  - *Variant (Sarvatīrtha / Ṭākeḍ):* Nāsik-district tradition localises Jaṭāyu's fall and cremation at **Sarvatīrtha**, some distance from Pañcavaṭī, where a Jaṭāyu shrine stands. [regional / folk]

## V. The living tīrtha-cluster

- **Sītā Gufā (Sītā's cave)** — a cramped rock-cut cave-shrine in the Pañcavaṭī quarter, entered by a narrow stair, held to be where **Sītā** hid or was kept (and where Rāvaṇa abducted her); it adjoins the **five banyans** said to mark the original grove, with Rāma–Sītā–Lakṣmaṇa and a Śiva-liṅga within. (Nāsik sthala-tradition) [regional]
- **Tapovan** — the riverside grove downstream where, by local tradition, the *tapas*-residence of the exiles and the **Śūrpaṇakhā / Lakṣmaṇa** episode are seated; a cluster of small shrines at the **Godāvarī–Nandini (Nāsardi)** confluence, with a "Lakṣmaṇa cut the nose here" spot. (Nāsik sthala-tradition) [regional]
- **Kapāleśvara Mahādeva** — the chief Śaiva shrine on the Pañcavaṭī ghāṭ, famed as the rare **Śiva temple without a Nandi**: local legend holds the bull stepped aside to become Śiva's *guru* in absolving him of *brahma-hatyā*, so no Nandi faces the liṅga. (Nāsik sthala-tradition) [regional / folk]
- **Rāmkuṇḍ ("Rāma's pool")** — the principal bathing-tank on the **Godāvarī**, where Rāma is said to have bathed and performed **Daśaratha's** *śrāddha*; the central **asthi-vilaya** ("bone-dissolving") tīrtha of Nāsik for immersing ashes, and the focus of the Kumbh bathing. (Gautamī-māhātmya; sthala-tradition)
- **Kālārām Mandir** — the great black-stone **Rāma–Sītā–Lakṣmaṇa** temple (rebuilt ~1788 CE under the Peśvā-era patron **Sardār Raṅgārāo Oḍhekar**), devotional heart of Pañcavaṭī and site of **B. R. Ambedkar's 1930 temple-entry satyāgraha**; paired with **Kapāleśvara, Sundarnārāyaṇa and Nārośaṅkar** in a dense Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava quarter. (sthala-tradition; historical record) [historical]

## VI. Nāsik in the Kumbh & the Rāma-network

- **Source of the Godāvarī** — the river rises at **Tryambakeśvar** (~30 km off) on the **Brahmagiri**, where the [Tryambakeśvar Jyotirliṅga](12-jyotirlingas.md) marks the spring; the **Gautamī-māhātmya** (Brahma Purāṇa stream) exalts it as the **Dakṣiṇa Gaṅgā ("Gaṅgā of the south") / Gautamī**, brought down by sage **Gautama's** *tapas*. (*Brahma Purāṇa, Gautamī-māhātmya*) — see [Godāvarī / Gautamī — Dakṣiṇa Gaṅgā](godavari-gautami-dakshina-ganga.md)
- **The Siṃhastha Kumbh** — Nāsik is one of the **four Kumbh Melā** cities; its **Siṃhastha** fair falls when **Jupiter (Bṛhaspati) enters Siṃha (Leo)**, roughly every twelve years, with bathing split between **Rāmkuṇḍ (Nāsik)** for the Vaiṣṇava akhāḍās and **Kuśāvarta (Tryambak)** for the Śaiva by sectarian custom. (pilgrimage tradition) — see [Kumbh Melā & Pilgrimage Cycles](kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md)
- **Node in the Rāma circuit** — though not a Sapta-Purī, **Pañcavaṭī / Nāsik** ranks among the foremost **Rāma-tīrthas**, the **abduction-station** paired in pilgrim imagination with [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md), Chitrakūṭa, Prayāga and Rāmeśvaram along the exile-route — the hinge between the forest-idyll and the southern war. (pilgrimage tradition) — see [Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md) [scholarly]
