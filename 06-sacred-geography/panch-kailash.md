# Pañch Kailāsh — The Five Kailāsa Pilgrimage Circuit

> The canonical **pentad of five Kailāsa peaks** of Śaiva pilgrimage — one supreme Tibetan throne plus four Indian-Himalayan "Kailāshes" of Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh — grouped as a single *pañchaka* circuit in the manner of the [Pañcha Kedār & Pañcha Badrī](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md) Garhwal fives. Each is a place where the mountain *is* Śiva: an un-summited peak, a natural rock-Śivaliṅga, or a *parikramā* whose terrain is read as theology.
>

Like the Garhwal fives, **Pañch Kailāsh is a devotional grouping, not a fixed scriptural list** — it distributes Śiva's mountain-presence across five Himalayan summits, of which only Tibet's Mount Kailāsa carries unambiguous pan-Purāṇic authority; the four Indian members are knit into the set by **regional sthala-purāṇas, mahātmyas and living yātrā tradition** rather than by a single canonical roster. The grouping is **medieval-to-modern in organization** even where the individual sites and their legends are older. [scholarly] [open roster]

- **The pentad (most-cited roster)** — (1) **Mount Kailāsa** (Tibet, ~6,638 m) — the supreme throne, treated in its own file; (2) **Ādi Kailāsh / Chhota Kailāsh** (Kumaon, Uttarakhand); (3) **Kinnaur Kailāsh** (Himachal); (4) **Shrīkhaṇḍ Mahādev** (Kullu, Himachal); (5) **Maṇimahesh Kailāsh** (Bharmour/Chamba, Himachal). (regional yātrā tradition; Wikipedia, *Panch Kailash*) [regional]
  - *Variant (membership):* alternate counts circulate — some lists drop one Himachal peak or substitute another local "Kailāsh"; the **four-Indian-plus-Tibet** form is the standard. [open roster]
- **The structural logic** — each member reproduces the Kailāsa template in miniature: an **unclimbable summit** (Maṇimahesh, Kinnaur Kailāsh's pinnacle), a **natural rock-liṅga** (Kinnaur, Shrīkhaṇḍ), a **paired sacred lake / kuṇḍa** (Mānasarovara → Pārvatī Sarovar, Maṇimahesh Lake), and a **circumambulatory or summit yātrā** that doubles as a death-and-rebirth passage — the Tibetan archetype echoed four times along the Indian Himalaya. [scholarly]

## II. Ādi Kailāsh / Chhota Kailāsh (Om Parvat) — Kumaon, Uttarakhand

- **Ādi Kailāsh ("first Kailāsa") / Chhota ("little") Kailāsh** — a ~**5,945 m** peak near the Indo-Tibetan border in the **Vyas valley, Pithoragarh district, Kumaon**, Uttarakhand; held to so resemble Mount Kailāsa that pilgrims unable to reach Tibet make their parikramā here as a **substitute Kailāsa**. (Wikipedia, *Adi Kailash*) [regional]
- **Pārvatī Sarovar / Gaurī Kuṇḍa** — the sacred lake at the mountain's foot, the local counterpart of **Mānasarovara**; named for **Pārvatī (Gaurī)**, with a small **Śiva–Pārvatī temple** on its shore — the Śiva-Pārvatī dyad reproduced beside its own "Mānasarovara." (sthala tradition) [regional]
- **Om Parvat (Ādi Kailāsh massif)** — a nearby peak (~**6,191 m**) on whose face perennial snow settles into the natural shape of the sacred syllable **"ॐ (Om)"** against the dark rock — read as Śiva's self-inscription on the mountain; one of the great natural-marvel darśanas of the region. (Wikipedia, *Om Parvat*) [regional]
  - *Variant (counting Om Parvat separately):* some itineraries treat **Om Parvat** as its own darśana distinct from the Ādi Kailāsh summit; the two stand close in the same Vyas-valley circuit and are usually pilgrimaged together. [regional]
- **Yātrā note** — the Ādi Kailāsh–Om Parvat yātrā runs from Dharchula via Gunji and is the Kumaoni answer to the Tibetan parikramā; a high-profile pilgrimage in the living tradition. [scholarly]

## III. Kinnaur Kailāsh — Kinnaur, Himachal Pradesh

- **Kinnaur Kailāsh (Kinner Kailash)** — a sacred peak (~**6,050 m**) in **Kinnaur district**, Himachal, one of the most arduous of the five; held to be a **winter abode of Śiva** where he sits in council with the gods. (Wikipedia, *Kinnaur Kailash*) [regional]
- **The ~79-ft natural Śivaliṅga** — near the summit stands a colossal **vertical rock formation ~79 feet (~24 m) high**, worshipped as a self-manifest (*svayambhū*) **Śivaliṅga**; it is said to **change colour through the day** with the shifting light — the goal-darśana of the yātrā. (Wikipedia, *Kinnaur Kailash*) [regional] [folk]
- **The Kinnaur Kailāsh parikramā** — a strenuous high-altitude circuit (often counted ~**3 days / multi-day**, the route varying) around the massif, distinct from the simpler liṅga-darśana trek from Tangling/Powari; one of the toughest Himachal yātrās. (yātrā tradition) [regional]

## IV. Shrīkhaṇḍ Mahādev — Kullu, Himachal Pradesh

- **Shrīkhaṇḍ Mahādev** — a summit of ~**5,155–5,227 m** in **Kullu district**, Himachal, crowned by a natural rock **Śivaliṅga ~75 feet (~23 m) tall** that is the yātrā's culminating darśana — among the highest and hardest pilgrimage treks in India. (Wikipedia, *Shrikhand Mahadev*) [regional]
- **The Bhasmāsura–Mohinī sthala-legend** — the demon **Bhasmāsura** won from Śiva a boon that **anyone whose head he touched with his hand would be reduced to ash**; he turned at once to test it on Śiva himself. **Viṣṇu took the form of the enchantress [Mohinī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)**, who lured the smitten demon into mirroring her dance until he laid **his own hand on his head** and was incinerated. Local tradition fixes this episode in the Shrīkhaṇḍ massif — the caves and ridges here are read as the ground of Bhasmāsura's destruction. (sthala-purāṇa; Bhāgavata / Śiva-purāṇic Bhasmāsura cycle) [regional]
  - *Variant (Bhīm Talāi / nearby stations):* the route's named halts (e.g. Bhīm Dwāri) and pools are woven into Pāṇḍava and Bhasmāsura sub-legends by local telling, with details varying between guides. [folk]
- **Yātrā note** — the Shrīkhaṇḍ yātrā (from Jaon/Bagipul via Sinhgad, Thachru, Bhīm Dwāri, Pārvatī Bāg) is an official multi-day high-risk pilgrimage held in mid-summer (Śrāvaṇ), regulated for altitude and weather. [scholarly]

## V. Maṇimahesh Kailāsh — Bharmour / Chamba, Himachal Pradesh

- **Maṇimahesh Kailāsh** — a ~**5,653 m** peak rising above **Maṇimahesh Lake** in the **Bharmour (Brahmaur) region of Chamba district**, Himachal; the abode of **Śiva as Maṇimaheśvara** ("the great lord of the jewel"), with Pārvatī as **Maṇimaheśvarī**. (Wikipedia, *Manimahesh Lake*; Skanda Purāṇa Himalayan khaṇḍa tradition) [regional]
- **The unclimbed "jewel on Śiva's forehead"** — the summit has **never been climbed**; tradition holds a **jewel (maṇi) shines on Śiva's forehead** atop the peak, visible only to the pure as a glint of light off the snow — and that the mountain repels any attempt to summit it, like Tibet's Kailāsa. (sthala tradition) [folk]
- **Maṇimahesh Lake (Ḍal)** — the high glacial lake (~**4,080 m**) at the mountain's foot, the local **Mānasarovara**; pilgrims bathe in its icy water before darśana of the peak, the bath being the core act of merit. (Wikipedia, *Manimahesh Lake*) [regional]
- **The Maṇimahesh Yātrā** — the major pilgrimage, drawing lakhs annually: a trek from **Hadsar** via **Dhancho** to the lake, climaxing in a holy dip and circumambulation. It is timed to the fortnight from **[Kṛṣṇa] Janmāṣṭamī to Rādhāṣṭamī** (Bhādrapada) — the **Maṇimahesh Chhaḍī (sacred mace) procession** from Bharmour/Chamba reaches the lake at the appointed night. (Wikipedia, *Manimahesh Lake*; Chamba Śaiva tradition) [regional]
- **Chamba / Markaṇḍeya frame** — the Maṇimaheśvara mahātmya belongs to the **Chamba Śaiva and Markaṇḍeya transmissions** of the western Himalaya, which localize Śiva's Kailāsa-abode and the *maṇi* legend in the Bharmour hills. (Markaṇḍeya / Chamba tradition; Skanda Purāṇa Himalayan khaṇḍa) [regional] [late text]

## VI. The pentad as a unit

| # | Kailāsh | Region | Height | Sacred object | Lake / kuṇḍa | Yātrā timing |
|---|---------|--------|--------|---------------|--------------|--------------|
| 1 | **Mount Kailāsa** | Tibet | ~6,638 m | the peak itself | Mānasarovara | summer (permits) |
| 2 | **Ādi Kailāsh** | Kumaon (UK) | ~5,945 m | Om Parvat ॐ snow-mark | Pārvatī Sarovar / Gaurī Kuṇḍ | summer |
| 3 | **Kinnaur Kailāsh** | Kinnaur (HP) | ~6,050 m | ~79-ft rock Śivaliṅga | — | Śrāvaṇ |
| 4 | **Shrīkhaṇḍ Mahādev** | Kullu (HP) | ~5,155–5,227 m | ~75-ft rock Śivaliṅga | (Pārvatī Bāg pools) | Śrāvaṇ |
| 5 | **Maṇimahesh Kailāsh** | Chamba (HP) | ~5,653 m | unclimbed "jewel" summit | Maṇimahesh Lake | Janmāṣṭamī–Rādhāṣṭamī |

- **Two clocks** — *scripture clock:* each peak is timeless Śiva-abode, the legends (Bhasmāsura–Mohinī, the Chamba *maṇi*, the Pārvatī lakes) sitting in mythic / early-Kali time. *Academic clock:* the **"Pañch Kailāsh" grouping is a regional-modern devotional construction**, assembling four Indian Himalayan yātrās (each with its own older sthala-purāṇa) around the one pan-Hindu Tibetan Kailāsa — paralleling how "Pañcha Kedār" and "Pañcha Badrī" are medieval-to-modern organizations of older shrines. [scholarly]
- **Substitute-Kailāsa logic** — because Tibet's Kailāsa lies across a closed border (permits, geopolitics), the four Indian members function as **accessible stand-ins**, each advertised as a Kailāsa one can actually reach — a geographic-devotional adaptation, not a scriptural demotion of the Tibetan peak. [scholarly]
