# The 12 Jyotirlingas of Shiva

> Sacred geography of the cosmos: the twelve *jyotirlingas* ("pillars/columns of light") are Shiva's self-manifest (*svayambhu*) abodes scattered across the Indian subcontinent. They are read here as fixed points where the formless Shiva pierced the manifest world during the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** of the 28th Mahayuga (yet whose origin myth predates creation itself).
>

The *jyotirlinga* (Sanskrit *jyotis* "radiance/light" + *linga* "sign/mark/symbol of Shiva") is a representation of Shiva not as carved icon but as a self-arisen column of fire/light. The **Shiva Purana** (Koti-Rudra Samhita / Shatarudra Samhita) names **64 original jyotirlingas**, of which **12 (dvadasha)** are held supreme. Reciting their names — the *Dvadasha Jyotirlinga Stotra* attributed to Adi Shankara — is said to erase the sins of seven lifetimes.

## The Origin Myth: The Pillar of Light (Lingodbhava)

- **The supremacy quarrel** - Brahma (creation) and Vishnu (preservation) dispute which of them is the supreme deity / the cause of the cosmos (Shiva Purana, Vidyeshvara Samhita; Linga Purana). The dispute frames the linga's appearance as occurring at/before manifest creation, outside ordinary yuga time — though its *worship* belongs to the cosmic ages.
- **The infinite column appears** - To settle it, Shiva manifests as a boundless flaming pillar (*jyotirlinga* / *stambha*) piercing the three worlds, with no visible top or bottom (Shiva Purana; Linga Purana — the *Lingodbhava* iconographic scene).
- **The contest** - The two agree to find its ends: Vishnu descends downward (as the boar **Varaha**, cf. [Dashavatara](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)) and Brahma ascends upward (riding his swan, *hamsa*). Neither reaches an end.
- **Brahma's lie and the ketaki flower** - Brahma, unwilling to admit defeat, produces a *ketaki* (screw-pine) flower as false witness that he reached the summit; Vishnu honestly confesses failure (Shiva Purana).
- **The curse and the boon** - Enraged at the falsehood, Shiva curses Brahma to have no worship/temples; the *ketaki* flower is barred from Shiva worship for bearing false witness. Vishnu is blessed to be eternally worshipped for his honesty (Shiva Purana).
  - *Variant (Linga Purana / Vayu Purana):* the emphasis is cosmological — the linga as the *prakriti*-pillar from which creation issues — more than the moralized curse-narrative of the Shiva Purana.
- **The 12 sites** - Where this light-pillar's power settled on earth, the twelve jyotirlingas arose, each tied to a local liberating legend (below).

## The Dvadasha Jyotirlinga Stotra — Canonical Ordering

The stotra (and most Puranic listings) enumerate the twelve in this traditional sequence — note it differs from any geographic order:

1. **Somnath** (Saurashtra, Gujarat) — *Saurashtre Somanatham*
2. **Mallikarjuna** (Srisailam) — *Shrishaile Mallikarjunam*
3. **Mahakaleshwar** (Ujjain) — *Ujjayinyam Mahakalam*
4. **Omkareshwar** (Mamleshwar, MP) — *Omkaramamaleshvaram*
5. **Vaidyanath/Kedarnath** [ordering varies — see notes]
6. **Bhimashankar** — *Dakinyam Bhimashankaram*
7. **Kashi Vishwanath** (Varanasi) — *Varanasyam tu Vishvesham*
8. **Trimbakeshwar** (on the Gautami/Godavari) — *Tryambakam Gautamitate*
9. **Vaidyanath** — *Vaidyanatham chitabhumau* / *Paralyam Vaidyanatham*
10. **Nageshwar** — *Nagesham Darukavane*
11. **Rameshwaram (Ramanathaswamy)** — *Setubandhe tu Ramesham*
12. **Grishneshwar** — *Ghushmesham cha Shivalaye*

- *Variant (ordering):* The stotra's middle places **Kedarnath** ("*Himalaye tu Kedaram*") and **Vaidyanath** at differing slots across recensions; the set of twelve is stable even where the recital order shifts.

## The Twelve, with Location and Presiding Legend

- **1. Somnath** - Prabhas Patan / Veraval, **Saurashtra coast, Gujarat**. The "first" and *adi* jyotirlinga. **Chandra (Soma, the Moon-god)**, cursed by Daksha to wane for neglecting his other wives, worshipped Shiva here; Shiva tempered the curse to periodic waxing/waning — hence *Soma-natha*, "Lord of the Moon" (Shiva Purana; Skanda Purana, Prabhasa Khanda). Historically the most-destroyed-and-rebuilt temple (notably sacked by Mahmud of Ghazni, 1025–26 CE; rebuilt repeatedly, last in 1951). [recorded history](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md)
- **2. Mallikarjuna** - Srisailam, on the Krishna river, **Andhra Pradesh** (Nallamala hills). Called the "Kailasa of the South." After the boy **Kartikeya/Skanda** went off in pique over Ganesha's marriage, **Shiva and Parvati** followed him to the hill to be near him, residing as *Mallika* (Parvati, jasmine) + *Arjuna* (Shiva) (Skanda Purana; Shiva Purana). One of few sites that is both a jyotirlinga and a **[Shakti Peetha](51-shakti-peethas.md)** (Bhramaramba Devi).
- **3. Mahakaleshwar** - **Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh**, on the Kshipra river. The only *south-facing* (*dakshinamukhi* / *dakshinamurti*) jyotirlinga among the twelve (upheld by the tantric tradition), with *Mahakala* as lord of time/death — its linga held to draw *shakti* from within itself rather than being mantra-installed (a property of all *svayambhu* jyotirlingas, not unique to it). Shiva emerged as Mahakala to slay the demon **Dushana** who tormented the devout brahmin **Vridhi/Vrishabha** and the city's pious king (Shiva Purana). Site of the famed *Bhasma Aarti* with cremation ash.
- **4. Omkareshwar** - Mandhata island in the **Narmada river, Khandwa district, MP**; the island is shaped like the syllable **Om (ॐ)**. Two shrines share the linga's worship: **Omkareshwar** (on the island) and **Mamleshwar/Amareshwar** (south bank). Legends: the **Vindhya mountain** worshipped Shiva here to gain prestige over Meru; and the **Devas vs. Danavas** were saved when Shiva split into Omkara and Amaleshwara (Shiva Purana).
- **5. Kedarnath** - **Garhwal Himalaya, Uttarakhand** (~3,580 m), near the Mandakini's source; the northernmost jyotirlinga, open only ~6 months/year. The **Pandavas**, seeking absolution for kin-slaying after the **[Mahabharata](../04-deep-dives/)** war, pursued Shiva (who fled as a bull, *Nandi/vrishabha*) into the Himalaya; he dove into the ground leaving his **hump** at Kedarnath (the other body-parts forming the *Panch Kedar*) (Skanda Purana, Kedara Khanda; Mahabharata-linked folk tradition). Also linked to **Nara-Narayana's** penance at Badari.
- **6. Bhimashankar** - **Sahyadri (Western Ghats), Pune district, Maharashtra**; source of the **Bhima river**. Shiva manifested as *Bhima* to destroy the demon **Tripurasura** (or **Bhima**, son of Kumbhakarna in some tellings) who tormented the Shiva-devotee king **Kamarupeshwar**; the sweat (*bhima*) Shiva shed in the fierce battle became the Bhima river (Shiva Purana).
  - *Variant (location, [disputed]):* a tradition identifies the jyotirlinga with **Bhimashankar near Guwahati, Assam** (or **Bhimashankar in Sakshi/ Kashipur**); the mainstream identification is the Sahyadri site in Maharashtra.
- **7. Kashi Vishwanath** - **Varanasi (Kashi/Banaras), Uttar Pradesh**, on the Ganga. The holiest, *Vishvanatha* "Lord of the Universe." Kashi is said to rest on Shiva's trident, untouched by *pralaya* (cosmic dissolution); dying here grants *moksha* via Shiva's *Taraka* mantra (Kashi Khanda of Skanda Purana; Shiva Purana). The Gyanvapi complex; the present temple rebuilt by Ahilyabai Holkar (1780) after Aurangzeb's demolition (1669). [recorded history](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md)
- **8. Trimbakeshwar** - **Nashik district, Maharashtra**, at the source of the **Godavari (Gautami Ganga)** below Brahmagiri hill. The linga uniquely has three faces (*tri-ambaka*) of Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva. Linked to sage **Gautama**: falsely accused of killing a cow (a *maya*-cow), he brought the Ganga down as the Godavari to purify the sin, and Shiva remained as Trimbakeshwar (Shiva Purana; Brahma Purana). Origin point of the **Sinhastha Kumbh Mela** ([Kumbh Mela](kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md)).
- **9. Vaidyanath (Baidyanath)** - presiding legend: **Ravana**, the rakshasa king of Lanka, did extreme tapas, offering his **ten heads** one by one into fire to win Shiva's favor and carry the **Atma-linga** to Lanka; Shiva appeared as the divine physician (*Vaidya/Baidya*) and reattached/healed Ravana's heads — hence *Vaidyanatha* (Shiva Purana). The linga was then tricked into being set down (via the Ganesha-as-cowherd / urination-pretext episode), rooting it at the spot.
  - *Variant (location — actively [disputed]):* The defining controversy among the twelve. The stotra reads ambiguously ("*Vaidyanatham chitabhumau*" vs. "*Paralyam Vaidyanatham*").
    - *Deoghar, Jharkhand (Baidyanath Dham):* identified with *chita-bhumi* (an old name **Chitabhumi/Chidabhumi** of Deoghar); endorsed by the Dwarka and Puri Shankaracharyas as the original. Houses the Ravana-Atmalinga tradition. (mainstream / most widely accepted)
    - *Parli, Maharashtra (Parli Vaijnath):* reading "*Paralyam*" = Parli; the older name of the area was also rendered *Parali*. Strong Maharashtrian/southern claim.
    - *Baijnath, Himachal Pradesh (Kangra):* a third claimant temple to the same epithet. [disputed]
  - The Government of India has not officially designated one; the **three-way claim (Deoghar / Parli / Baijnath)** is itself part of the tradition. [scholarly: unresolved]
- **10. Nageshwar (Nagesha)** - "Lord of Serpents," in **Darukavana** ("*Nagesham Darukavane*"). Shiva freed his devotee **Supriya** and fellow prisoners held by the demon **Daruka** and his demoness wife **Daruka** (a Parvati-devotee, for whom the forest *Darukavana* was named) in their undersea city; chanting the Shiva mantra summoned Shiva, who destroyed the demon and stayed as Nagesha (Shiva Purana).
  - *Variant (location — [disputed]):* three temples claim *Darukavana*:
    - *Nageshwar near Dwarka, Gujarat (Devbhoomi Dwarka):* coastal, "undersea-city" fits the Krishna/Dwarka belt; widely promoted as the jyotirlinga.
    - *Aundha Nagnath, Hingoli district, Maharashtra:* old Marathi tradition holds this the *first* of the twelve; linked to the Pandavas' exile.
    - *Jageshwar, Almora district, Uttarakhand:* Himalayan deodar (*daru*) forest reading of *Darukavana*. [disputed]
- **11. Rameshwaram (Ramanathaswamy)** - **Rameswaram island, Tamil Nadu**, at *Setubandha* (the bridge to Lanka). Before/after the Lanka war, **Rama** (cf. [Ramayana deep dives](../04-deep-dives/)) installed and worshipped a Shiva linga to expiate the *brahma-hatya* of killing Ravana (a brahmin). **Hanuman** was sent to fetch a linga from Kailasa; when delayed, **Sita** fashioned one from sand (the *Ramalinga*); Hanuman's linga (*Vishwalinga/Hanumalinga*) is venerated first by Shiva's decree (Ramayana-derived; Skanda Purana). The southernmost jyotirlinga; one of the **Char Dham** ([Char Dham](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)).
- **12. Grishneshwar (Ghushmeshwar)** - **Verul, near the Ellora caves, Aurangabad district, Maharashtra**; the "last"/smallest jyotirlinga. The devotee **Kusuma (Ghushma)**, whose son was murdered out of jealousy by her co-wife and thrown into the tank where she immersed her daily 101 lingas, kept her faith; Shiva revived the boy and manifested as *Ghushmeshwara/Grishneshwara* at her request (Shiva Purana).
  - *Variant (name):* spelled *Grishneshwar / Ghrishneshwar / Ghushmeshwar / Kusumeshwar* across texts after the devotee's names.

## Cosmic & Yuga Framing

- **Outside and inside time** - The *Lingodbhava* origin is staged at the threshold of creation (Brahma–Vishnu, the makers of manifest cosmos), placing the jyotirlingas conceptually *prior* to ordinary yuga reckoning — yet their earthly legends play out within the **[28th Mahayuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md)**: Somnath/Chandra in deep antiquity, Rameshwaram in **[Treta Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** (Rama), Kedarnath in **[Dvapara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** (Pandavas), and active pilgrimage worship through **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)**.
- **Kashi and pralaya** - Kashi Vishwanath is uniquely said to survive *pralaya* (dissolution at a Day of Brahma's end, cf. [kalpa and pralaya](../00-time-and-cosmology/kalpa-and-pralaya.md)), held aloft on Shiva's trident — a node of permanence across the cosmic cycle.
- **64 → 12** - The reduction from 64 jyotirlingas (Shiva Purana) to the supreme 12 is itself a Kali-Yuga-era devotional canonization; regional traditions still venerate additional "jyotirlingas" locally [folk tradition].
