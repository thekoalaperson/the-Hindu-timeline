# Siṃhācalam — Varāha-Narasiṃha Svāmī

> The "Lion-Hill" (*Siṃhācala*, "lion-mountain") above Visakhapatnam in Andhra Pradesh: a *svayam-vyakta* (self-manifest) hill-shrine of **Vishnu** in the rare composite **Varāha-Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha** form — the only major temple where the Boar (Varāha) and Man-Lion (Narasiṃha) avatāras fuse in one icon, kept perpetually buried under sandalwood paste except on one day a year. Tied directly to the **Prahlāda–Hiraṇyakaśipu** cycle.
>

**Siṃhācalam** (Telugu *Siṃhāchalam*, "lion-hill") is one of the foremost Vaiṣṇava *kṣetras* of the Andhra coast, about 16 km north of Visakhapatnam on a spur of the Eastern Ghats. Its deity, **Varāha-Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha Svāmī**, is a **composite avatāra-form** — the snout/face of the **[Varāha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** Boar joined to the **[Narasiṃha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** Man-Lion — that the *sthala-purāṇa* binds to the slaying of **Hiraṇyakaśipu** to protect the child-devotee **Prahlāda**. Like Tirumala it is held to be **svayam-vyakta** ("self-manifest," not consecrated by human hands); architecturally the standing temple is **Eastern Ganga / early-Kakatiya** work (13th c. CE) over an older Cōḷa-period core. Below, the *traditional* (Yuga / Purāṇa-internal) frame is kept distinct from the *academic* (inscriptional / architectural) frame throughout — the **"two clocks."** [scholarly]

---

## The deity & the composite form

- **Varāha-Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha Svāmī** — the presiding mūrti of Siṃhācalam: a *tribhaṅga* (thrice-bent) standing form with the **boar-head of Varāha** and the body/limbs of **Narasiṃha**, **Lakṣmī** on the chest/left — uniting the **third** ([Varāha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)) and **fourth** ([Narasiṃha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)) of the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) in a single icon — a fusion found at almost no other major temple. (Siṃhācala-kṣetra-māhātmya; Wikipedia, *Simhachalam*)
- **The two avatāras, one purpose** — both Varāha (slayer of **Hiraṇyākṣa**) and Narasiṃha (slayer of his brother **Hiraṇyakaśipu**) descend to destroy the **Diti-born brothers** who oppress the worlds; tradition reads the composite as Vishnu showing that **both** demon-brothers' menace ends at this hill. (Siṃhācala sthala-purāṇa)
- **Svayam-vyakta kṣetra** — the icon is one of the *self-manifest* abodes of Vishnu (in the class of Tirumala and the eight *svayam-vyakta* sites of Vaiṣṇava lore), not installed by a sculptor — establishing its sanctity as primordial rather than founded. [traditional] (Wikipedia, *Simhachalam*)

## The Prahlāda legend localized

- **Hiraṇyakaśipu and the boon** — the *daitya* king, having won near-invincibility from **[Brahmā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md)** (death neither by day nor night, indoors nor out, by man nor beast, on earth nor in sky), persecutes his own son **Prahlāda** for his unbroken devotion to **Vishnu** — the classic Narasiṃha cause (*Bhāgavata* 7; *Vishnu Purāṇa* 1). (Bhāgavata-Purāṇa, skandha 7; Vishnu Purāṇa)
- **Prahlāda cast from the hill** — the local *māhātmya* adds that the boy is **hurled into the sea** from the mountain on his father's order; **Vishnu rescues him**, and Prahlāda, devotion intact, builds the **first shrine** for the Lord on the hill — making Siṃhācalam the very site of Prahlāda's deliverance. (Siṃhācala-kṣetra-māhātmya; Narasiṃha-Purāṇa tradition)
- **Narasiṃha at the threshold** — Vishnu bursts from the **pillar** as the Man-Lion at twilight (*saṃdhyā* — neither day nor night), on the **threshold** (neither in nor out), and tears Hiraṇyakaśipu on his **lap** with his **claws** (neither man nor beast, weapon nor unarmed), voiding the boon — here the *kṣetra* claims the deed for the Lion-Hill itself. (Bhāgavata-Purāṇa 7; Siṃhācala sthala-purāṇa)
  - *Variant (Prahlāda as founder):* the māhātmya credits **Prahlāda** with the original temple, which then **decayed across the Yugas** and lay buried in an anthill until re-revealed — a standard *svayam-vyakta* "lost-and-refound" motif. [folk] [regional]
  - *Variant (Pururavā's refinding):* a later king — often named **Pururavā** of the lunar line — is said to have **rediscovered** the buried icon and re-established worship, receiving the divine command to keep it coated in sandal. [regional] [late text]

## The sandalwood (chandanam) covering & Chandanotsavam

- **The perpetual chandanam coat** — uniquely, the mūrti is kept **entirely encased in sandalwood paste (*chandanam*)** year-round, so that pilgrims normally see a smooth **liṅga-like sandal mound**, *not* the carved avatāra-form beneath. (Wikipedia, *Simhachalam*; templepurohit.com)
  - *Variant (why the coating — the heat motif):* tradition holds the **Narasiṃha form is too fierce/hot (*ugra*)**; the cooling sandal paste **pacifies** the deity and protects devotees from his terrible heat — the standard explanation for *ugra-Narasiṃha* shrines. [folk]
  - *Variant (Prahlāda's command):* the coating is said to fulfill **Prahlāda's own request** (or the Lord's instruction to Pururavā) that the awesome form be **veiled** except on a single day. [regional]
- **Chandanotsavam (Akṣaya Tṛtīyā)** — once a year, on **Vaiśākha Śukla Tṛtīyā (Akṣaya Tṛtīyā**, Apr–May), the old paste is removed and the **true *nija-rūpa* (original carved form) is revealed** for *darśana* (the *Nijarūpa Darśanam*), lasting only ~12 hours before fresh chandanam is re-applied. (Wikipedia, *Simhachalam*; templepurohit.com)
- **The annual re-coating in stages** — fresh sandal is applied in **four installments** through the year (on Akṣaya Tṛtīyā and three later occasions, e.g. on *Vaiśākha / Jyeṣṭha / Āṣāḍha* and *Nṛsiṃha-Jayantī*-linked dates), so the mound is rebuilt to its full thickness — a rare living ritual cycle. [regional] (Devasthanam tradition)

## Eastern Ganga / Kakatiya temple & history (the historical clock)

- **The standing temple — 13th c. CE** — the present stone complex (West-facing *vimāna*, *mukha-maṇḍapa*, sixteen-pillared *Naṭya-maṇḍapa*, *kalyāṇa-maṇḍapa*) is largely built/rebuilt under the **Eastern Ganga king Narasiṃhadeva I** (r. c. 1238–1264 CE, the builder of Konārka) and his line — a **Kaliṅga-Orissan style** monument on Andhra soil. (Wikipedia, *Simhachalam Temple*; ASI)
- **Dense inscriptional record** — the temple walls carry **hundreds of inscriptions** (Eastern Ganga, Kakatiya, Reddi, Gajapati, Vijayanagara) recording endowments — among the richest epigraphic corpora of any Andhra temple; **Śrī Kṛṣṇadevarāya** of Vijayanagara records gifts here after his Kaliṅga campaign (early 16th c.). (Epigraphia Indica; Wikipedia)
- **Two clocks** — *traditionally* the shrine is **timeless/svayam-vyakta**, founded by Prahlāda in a past Yuga; *academically* continuous worship is attested only from **c. 11th–13th c. CE** (Cōḷa core, Eastern Ganga expansion) into the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** historical period. [scholarly]

## Network & cross-links

- **Not a Divya Desam** — despite its rank, Siṃhācalam is **not** among the [108 Divya Desams](./108-divya-desams.md) (the Ālvārs did not sing it; Andhra has only **two** Divya Desams — Tirumala and Ahobilam-adjacent counts vary) — it belongs to the broader **[sthala-purāṇa](./sthala-puranas-major-temples.md)** tier of major living temples rather than the saint-canonised [temple networks](./temple-networks-and-circuits.md). [scholarly]
- **Narasiṃha circuit of Andhra** — devotionally paired with **Ahobilam** (the nine Narasiṃhas) and **Mangalagiri**/**Yādagiri** as part of an informal pan-Andhra **Narasiṃha-kṣetra** pilgrimage axis. [regional] (see [temple networks](./temple-networks-and-circuits.md))
