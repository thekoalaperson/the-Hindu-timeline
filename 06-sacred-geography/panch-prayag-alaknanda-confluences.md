# Pañch Prayāg — the Five Sacred Confluences of the Alaknandā

> The five holy *saṅgams* (river-junctions) of the Garhwal Himalaya (Uttarakhand), strung down the **Alaknandā** in descending order — **Vishnuprayag → Nandaprayag → Karnaprayag → Rudraprayag → Devprayag** — the last being where the Alaknandā meets the Bhāgīrathī and the united stream is first called **Gaṅgā**. A five-fold (*pañchaka*) network like its Garhwal siblings, but of *waters* rather than shrines.
>

*Prayāga* ("place of sacrifice/confluence") is the technical name for a junction of rivers where bathing carries multiplied merit — the archetype being the [Triveṇī Saṅgam at Prayagraj](prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md). Garhwal tradition, drawing on the **Kedāra-khaṇḍa** geography of the Skanda Purāṇa, distributes five such prayāgas along the Alaknandā, each with its own presiding deity, bathing *kuṇḍ*, and legend. Together with the [Pañcha Kedār / Pañcha Badrī](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md), [Pañch Kailash](panch-kailash.md), and [Pañch Sarovar](panch-sarovar-sacred-lakes.md), they complete the region's — and the tradition's — recurring habit of sacred fives. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*; Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa)

---

## I. The River Frame — Alaknandā, Bhāgīrathī, and the Making of the Gaṅgā

- **Two headstreams, one Gaṅgā** — the **Bhāgīrathī** (rising at Gaumukh, the snout of the Gangotri glacier) and the **Alaknandā** (rising from the Satopanth and Bhagirath-Kharak glaciers below Chaukhamba, above [Badrinath](badrinath-badarikashrama-kshetra.md)) are the two chief source-streams of the Gaṅgā. Hydrologically the Alaknandā is the larger; ritually the Bhāgīrathī is the "true" Gaṅgā, since it traces the descent-path won by **Bhagīratha's** austerities. The name **Gaṅgā** applies to the united river only from **Devprayag** downstream. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*, *Alaknanda River*) — full descent narrative in [Sacred Rivers & Descent Myths](sacred-rivers-and-descent-myths.md) and [Gaṅgā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganga.md); the Sagara-ancestor frame in [Sagara](../03-lineages-vamsha/sagara.md).
- **The descending ladder of confluences** — each major left- or right-bank tributary of the Alaknandā is absorbed at a named prayāga: **Dhauligaṅgā at Vishnuprayag, Nandākinī at Nandaprayag, Piṇḍar at Karnaprayag, Mandākinī at Rudraprayag, Bhāgīrathī at Devprayag**. Pilgrims on the [Chota Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) roads pass all five; bathing at each is held to carry Prayāga-like merit. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*)
- **Saṅgam theology** — as elsewhere in the corpus, sanctity concentrates at the *saṅgam*: the meeting of two visible streams (and, in belief, unseen ones) multiplies the purifying power of either alone. (Skanda Purāṇa tradition; see [Sacred Rivers & Descent Myths](sacred-rivers-and-descent-myths.md)) [scholarly framing]

## II. The Five Prayāgas, in Downstream Order

### 1. Vishnuprayag — Alaknandā + Dhauligaṅgā

- **The confluence** — first and highest of the five (~1,372 m), just below Joshimath in Chamoli district, where the **Dhauligaṅgā** (itself fed by the Niti-valley streams) joins the Alaknandā arriving from Badrinath. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*)
- **Nārada's penance** — the site's charter legend: the divine sage **Nārada** performed austerities here and **Vishnu appeared to him** at the junction, giving the prayāga its name. Bathing at the **Vishnu Kuṇḍ** beside the saṅgam is the site's rite. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*; Kedāra-khaṇḍa tradition)
- **The Vishnu temple** — an octagonal temple of Vishnu stands over the confluence, credited to the **Maharani of Indore (dated 1889)** — a reminder that the built fabric of the prayāgas is largely modern even where the legend is ancient. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*) [scholarly]
- **Gateway function** — Vishnuprayag is the ritual hinge between the Badrinath *dhām* above it and the valley circuit below; the [Pañcha Badrī](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md) shrines cluster around this stretch of the river.

### 2. Nandaprayag — Alaknandā + Nandākinī

- **The confluence** — second in order (~1,358 m), Chamoli district, where the **Nandākinī**, flowing off the **Nandā Devī** massif, meets the Alaknandā. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*)
- **King Nanda's sacrifice** — the common naming legend: a **King Nanda** performed a great *yajña* here and won the gods' favour, and the prayāga preserves his name. Tradition identifies him with **Nanda the Yādava**, foster-father of Krishna. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*) [regional]
  - *Variant (goddess-derivation):* the name is taken instead from the **Nandākinī river and the goddess Nandā (Nandā Devī)**, Garhwal's presiding goddess — a Devī rather than Yādava etymology. [regional] [variant]
- **Kaṇva's hermitage; Dushyanta & Śakuntalā** — local tradition places the **āśrama of sage Kaṇva** near Nandaprayag and holds that the *gāndharva* marriage of **King Dushyanta and Śakuntalā** — the union that produced **Bharata**, eponym of Bhāratavarṣa — took place in this region. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*; the base story is Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva / Kālidāsa's *Abhijñānaśākuntalam*; its Garhwal localization is [folk] [regional] — the plains tradition sites Kaṇva's āśrama on the Mālinī near Kotdwar.)
- **Gopāl temple** — the saṅgam's shrine is to Krishna as **Gopāl(jī)**, consonant with the Nanda-Yādava naming. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*)

### 3. Karnaprayag — Alaknandā + Piṇḍar

- **The confluence** — third (~788 m), Chamoli district, where the **Piṇḍar** (also called **Karṇa Gaṅgā** here), draining the Pindari glacier, joins the Alaknandā. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*)
- **Karṇa's tapas to Sūrya** — the charter legend: **Karṇa** of the Mahābhārata performed austerities here to his father **[Sūrya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/surya-and-his-family.md)**, and by this worship obtained his **golden armour and earrings (kavacha-kuṇḍala)** that made him nigh-invulnerable. A **Karṇa temple** marks the rock of his penance. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*) — Karṇa's full arc, including Indra's later begging-away of that same armour, in [Karṇa](../04-deep-dives/karna.md). *Note:* the Mahābhārata itself narrates Karṇa's birth with the armour and its surrender to Indra (Āraṇyaka/Vana Parva) without naming this site; the localization at Karnaprayag is Garhwal tradition. [regional]
  - *Variant (last-rites tradition):* a local tradition holds that after the Kurukṣetra war **Krishna performed Karṇa's funeral rites** in this region — extending the site's claim from Karṇa's penance to his obsequies. [folk] [disputed]
- **Umā Devī temple** — Karnaprayag's second shrine is to **Umā (Pārvatī)**, whom regional tradition treats as a daughter of this valley; her festival image travels the surrounding villages in a periodic procession. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*) [regional]
- **Documented history** — **Swami Vivekananda** is recorded to have stayed and meditated at Karnaprayag (1890s wanderings in Garhwal); the town is today the terminus of the under-construction Rishikesh–Karnaprayag rail line. (Wikipedia, *Karnaprayag*) [scholarly]

### 4. Rudraprayag — Alaknandā + Mandākinī

- **The confluence** — fourth (~610 m), seat of Rudraprayag district, where the **Mandākinī**, descending from [Kedarnath](kedarnath-kedara-kshetra.md), meets the Alaknandā — the junction of the Kedār and Badrī river-corridors. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*)
- **Shiva as Rudra; Nārada's music** — the charter legend: **Nārada**, humbled over his pride in his own vīṇā-playing, performed penance here, and **Shiva appeared in his Rudra form** to grace him — teaching/blessing him in music and, in tellings, granting him mastery of the vīṇā. The prayāga takes its name from this Rudra-appearance. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*; Kedāra-khaṇḍa tradition)
  - *Variant (tāṇḍava tradition):* some tellings add that **Shiva performed the tāṇḍava** (his cosmic dance) at this site, and/or played the **rudra-vīṇā** here, drawing Nārada to the spot — the dance-frame rather than the teaching-frame. [folk] [variant] — Shiva's dance-mythology at large in [Shiva myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md).
- **Rudranāth and Chamundā temples** — the confluence is overlooked by a **Rudranāth (Shiva) temple** with a shrine of the goddess **Chamundā** across the junction. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*) — distinguish this Rudranāth *temple* from the Rudranath *peak-shrine* of the [Pañcha Kedār](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md).
- **Documented history** — the town gave its name to the **man-eating leopard of Rudraprayag** (killed by Jim Corbett, 1926), which preyed on pilgrims of the Kedarnath road — a grim footnote of the pilgrimage's modern record; the 2013 Uttarakhand floods devastated this Mandākinī corridor. (Corbett, *The Man-Eating Leopard of Rudraprayag*, 1948) [scholarly]

### 5. Devprayag — Bhāgīrathī + Alaknandā = Gaṅgā

- **The confluence of confluences** — fifth and last (~475 m; Tehri Garhwal district): the **Bhāgīrathī**, arriving from Gangotri–Gaumukh, meets the **Alaknandā**, and from this point the united river is **the Gaṅgā by name**. In sanctity it is ranked with [Prayagraj's Triveṇī](prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md); Garhwal tradition treats it as the *first* Prayāga of the Gaṅgā's own body. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*, *Devprayag*)
- **Deva Śarmā's austerities** — the naming legend: the sage **Deva Śarmā** performed tapas here and was blessed with a divine vision, and the town preserves his name. (Wikipedia, *Devprayag*; Kedāra-khaṇḍa tradition) [regional]
- **Rāma's expiation** — Devprayag's great temple is the **Raghunāthjī (Rāma) temple**, among the oldest shrines of the region; tradition holds that **Rāma performed penance here to expiate the killing of Rāvaṇa** — a Brahmin by birth (grandson of Pulastya), so that even a righteous war left *brahma-hatyā* to be cleansed. (Wikipedia, *Devprayag*; regional sthala-purāṇa tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant:* some tellings bring **Rāma with Lakṣmaṇa**, or place **King Daśaratha's** earlier penance here as well — the site accumulates Ikṣvāku associations. [folk] [variant]
- **The Badrinath priests' town** — Devprayag is the hereditary home of the **paṇḍās (pilgrim-priests) of Badrinath**, binding the lowest prayāga to the highest dhām in one professional-ritual community. (Wikipedia, *Devprayag*) [regional]
- **Onward flow** — below Devprayag the Gaṅgā runs to [Rishikesh and Haridwar](haridwar-rishikesh-ganga-dwara.md), where she leaves the mountains at Gaṅgā-dwāra — and ultimately to [Gaṅgāsāgar](gangasagar-sagara-dvipa-kapila-ashram.md), completing the descent-geography that begins at these glaciers.

## III. Membership Notes & Variants

- **The canonical five** — Vishnuprayag, Nandaprayag, Karnaprayag, Rudraprayag, Devprayag is the fixed, universally cited set; unlike the [Pañcha Badrī](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md), the Pañch Prayāg roster does not circulate alternates. (Wikipedia, *Panch Prayag*)
- **Keshavprayag (a sixth?)** — some enumerations of Alaknandā confluences add **Keshavprayag**, near Mana above Badrinath, where the (Himalayan) **Sarasvatī** stream meets the Alaknandā — but it is *not* counted in the Pañch Prayāg. [regional] [variant]
- **Prayāga-jaṭā symbolism** — devotional tradition reads the five confluences as strands of **Shiva's matted hair** through which the descending Gaṅgā was filtered, extending the Gaṅgāvataraṇa image (Gaṅgā caught in Shiva's jaṭā) onto the map itself. [folk] — the descent narrative proper in [Sacred Rivers & Descent Myths](sacred-rivers-and-descent-myths.md).

## IV. Two Clocks

- **Scriptural/cyclic clock** — the legends span the yugas: **Bhagīratha's Gaṅgā-descent** and **Rāma's expiation at Devprayag** sit in the **Tretā Yuga** frame; **Karṇa's tapas**, **Nanda's yajña**, and the Dushyanta–Śakuntalā association belong to the **Dvāpara** (and earlier Lunar-dynasty) frame; **Nārada's** appearances at Vishnuprayag and Rudraprayag are cross-yuga, as befits the wandering sage.
- **Academic/historical clock** — the *prayāga* concept is Purāṇic (Kedāra-khaṇḍa's Garhwal geography; the Skanda Purāṇa's compilation is generally dated c. 7th–12th c. CE and later for regional khaṇḍas); the surviving temple fabric is largely early-modern to modern (e.g., the 1889 Vishnuprayag temple), and the grouping "Panch Prayag" as a named pilgrim circuit is a medieval-to-modern devotional organization of older individual saṅgam sanctities. [scholarly]
