# Girnar (Raivataka) — The Multi-Tradition Sacred Mountain

> **Girnar** (*Girinagara*, "the hill-city"; scripturally **Raivataka** / **Ujjayanta**; in the *Skanda Purāṇa* the **Vastrāpatha-kṣetra** / *Vastrāpada*) — the premier sacred peak of **Saurāṣṭra** (Kāṭhiāwāḍ, Gujarat), rising in five summits above Junāgaḍh. It is a rare mountain held holy by **four living traditions at once**: the summit *tapas*-peak and footprint-shrine of **Dattātreya**, a primal seat of the **Nāth / Gorakhnāth** yogīs, the **Ambā / Ambikā** śakti-shrine, and the holiest northern **Jain tīrtha** of the 22nd Tīrthaṅkara **Nemīnātha (Ariṣṭanemi)** — circled by the annual **Girnar Parikramā**.

**Naming & the two clocks** — In *scriptural* time the hill is **Raivataka** / **Ujjayanta**, named in the *Mahābhārata* and *Bhāgavata* as the festival-park beside [Dvārakā](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md) and, in the *Skanda Purāṇa*'s **Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa**, the **Vastrāpatha-kṣetra** sanctified by Dattātreya and the Goddess. In *terrestrial / historical* time it is **Girnar**, a volcanic massif near **Junāgaḍh** whose lower rock bears one of **Aśoka's rock-edicts** (3rd c. BCE) and centuries of inscriptions — the secular clock that must not be conflated with the *māhātmya* clock. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa* — *Vastrāpatha-kṣetra-māhātmya*; Wikipedia, "Girnar")

## I. Raivataka in the scriptural geography

- **Raivataka / Ujjayanta hill** — The peak named in epic-Purāṇic Dvārakā literature as the Yādava festival-ground; *Ujjayanta* is its older Sanskrit name (so called in the *Mahābhārata* tīrtha-lists). (*Mbh*; *Bhāgavata* 10; see [Dvārakā](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md))
- **The Raivataka festival & Subhadrā** — On the Raivataka slopes the Vṛṣṇi-Andhakas hold their great spring festival; it is from the **Raivataka procession** that **Arjuna**, on Kṛṣṇa's counsel, abducts **Subhadrā**, mother of Abhimanyu — anchoring the hill in the [Mahābhārata](../04-deep-dives/dattatreya.md) narrative. (*Mbh, Ādi Parva* ~218–223; *Bhāgavata* 10)
- **King Raivata / Revata** — Folk etymology ties the name to **Raivata Kakudmin**, the *Ānarta* king whose daughter **Revatī** married **Balarāma**; the hill is read as his old domain. (*Bhāgavata* 9.3; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*) [regional]
- **Vastrāpatha / Vastrāpada — "place of the garment/foot"** — The *Skanda Purāṇa*'s name for the kṣetra, glossed as where a divine **garment (vastra)** fell or the holy **foot (pada)** of Dattātreya / the Goddess is set — the seed of the summit footprint-cult. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa*) [late text]

## II. Dattātreya's tapas-peak & the summit footprint (Datta Pādukā)

- **The highest summit as Datta-śikhara** — The loftiest of Girnar's five peaks (**Guru Śikhar / Dattātreya peak**, ~3,666 ft, reached by ~9,000+ stone steps) is held to be where the avadhūta **[Dattātreya](../04-deep-dives/dattatreya.md)** — the triune Vishnu-form son of **Atri** and **Anasūyā** — performed and still performs eternal *tapas*. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa, Vastrāpatha-māhātmya*; Datta-sampradāya tradition)
- **The footprint shrine (Datta Pādukā)** — The summit enshrines not an image but **Dattātreya's footprints (pādukā)** in a small domed *dhām*, worshipped as his abiding bodily presence — the focal *darśana* of the climb. (Datta-sampradāya tradition; Wikipedia, "Girnar") [regional]
- **Eternal, ever-living guru** — In the Datta theology Dattātreya is **deathless (*cirajīvin*)** and physically present on Girnar; pilgrims hold that he still wanders the hill before dawn, making the peak a *jāgṛta* ("awake") tīrtha. (Datta-sampradāya / Mahānubhāva tradition) [folk]
- **Annual Datta Jayantī** — The hill is thronged on **Dattātreya Jayantī** (Mārgaśīrṣa pūrṇimā), the avadhūta's birth-festival, alongside Kārttika full-moon climbs. (regional tradition) [regional]

## III. The Nāth / Gorakhnāth yogī seat

- **A primal Nāth tīrtha** — Girnar is one of the oldest seats of the **[Nāth Sampradāya](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/nath-sampradaya-and-the-siddhas.md)**, the Śaiva Siddha-yogī order; Nāth tradition co-opts **Dattātreya** himself as a guru of the lineage, so the Datta-peak and the yogī cult overlap on the same summit. (Nāth hagiography; Mallinson, in [Dattātreya deep-dive](../04-deep-dives/dattatreya.md)) [disputed]
- **Gorakhnāth's peak (Gorakh Śikhar)** — A separate high summit, **Gorakhnāth Śikhar** (the highest point of the massif), is named for **Guru Gorakṣanātha (Gorakhnāth)**, organizer of the order and of haṭha-yoga, who is said to have done penance here; it bears his *dhūnī* and footprint. (Nāth tradition; Wikipedia, "Gorakhnath") [regional]
- **The Kānphaṭa connection** — Girnar long hosted **Kānphaṭa ("split-ear") yogīs** of the Gorakṣa order with *maṭhas* and *dhūnīs* about the hill, weaving it into the all-India Nāth network seated at [Gorakhpur](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/nath-sampradaya-and-the-siddhas.md). The historical Gorakhnāth is placed anywhere **9th–13th c. CE** [disputed], so this layer belongs to the *historical* clock, not the timeless Datta-tapas of the *māhātmya*. (Nāth tradition; Wikipedia, "Gorakhnath") [regional]

## IV. The Goddess — Ambā / Ambikā Mātā

- **Ambā-Mātā shrine on the ridge** — Below the Datta and Gorakh peaks stands the **Ambājī (Ambā-Mātā)** temple, where the Goddess as **Ambikā** is the presiding *kuladevī* of the hill; newlyweds traditionally climb to seek her blessing. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa* — Ambikā lore; regional tradition) [regional]
- **The kṣetra-devī of Vastrāpatha** — The *Vastrāpatha-māhātmya* pairs **Ambikā** with Dattātreya as the two great powers sanctifying the field, making her integral to the Purāṇic charter of the mountain. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa*)
- **Distinction from the Śakti-pīṭha network** — This Girnar **Ambikā** is the local hill-goddess and should **not** be confused with the Gujarat **[Śakti Pīṭha](51-shakti-peethas.md)** of **Ambājī (Gabbar hill, Banaskantha)**, the "heart"-pīṭha listed among the 51 — a different site and a different cult-charter. (see [51 Śakti Pīṭhas](51-shakti-peethas.md)) [disputed]
  - *Variant (folk conflation):* popular Gujarati devotion sometimes blurs the two Ambājīs into one Goddess; the texts keep their charters separate. [folk]

## V. The Jain tīrtha of Nemīnātha (Ariṣṭanemi)

- **Girnar as the holiest northern Jain peak** — For Śvetāmbara and Digambara Jains, Girnar (**Ujjayanta / Ujjinta-giri**) is among the five supreme *siddha-kṣetras*; it is the site of the **dīkṣā (renunciation), kevala-jñāna (omniscience) and nirvāṇa (liberation)** of the **22nd Tīrthaṅkara, Nemīnātha (Ariṣṭanemi)**. (Jain *Uttarapurāṇa*; *Kalpa-sūtra*; Jain tradition)
- **The Kṛṣṇa cousin-kinship** — Jain tradition makes **Nemīnātha a cousin of Kṛṣṇa** (a Yādava prince of Dvārakā/Saurāṣṭra) who renounced on his wedding day rather than cause the slaughter of animals penned for the feast — explicitly tying the Jain peak to the same Yādava Saurāṣṭra as the [Dvārakā](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md) cycle. (Jain *Hari-vaṃśa-purāṇa*; *Uttarapurāṇa*)
- **The Jain temple-city (Tuk)** — A walled cluster of marble **Jain temples (Neminātha temple, c. 12th c. and later)** stands on a lower plateau (the *deraseras* / *ṭūṅk*), one of the great medieval Jain pilgrimage complexes of western India. (Jain tradition; Wikipedia, "Girnar Jain temples") [scholarly]
- **A shared mountain, separate charters** — Hindu (Datta/Nāth/Ambā) and Jain (Nemi) cults occupy **different summits and plateaus** of the one massif; the traditions are catalogued side by side here and **never merged**. (multi-tradition site)

## VI. The Girnar Parikramā (Lili Parikramā)

- **The "green circumambulation"** — The annual **Girnar Parikramā** (*Lili Parikramā*, "lush circuit") rounds the base of the massif through ~36 km of forest over several days, beginning on **Kārttika Ekādaśī / Dev-Diwali** and ending on **Kārttika Pūrṇimā**. (regional tradition; Junāgaḍh / Gujarat Tourism) [regional]
- **Halts of the circuit** — The route threads forest shrines and *kuṇḍas* — **Jīna Bābā nī Madhī, Mālvelā, Bordevī** — drawing lakhs of pilgrims and sādhus through the **Gir** fringe forest. (regional tradition) [folk]
- **Sādhu congregation** — Like the great *melā*-tīrthas, the Parikramā and the Mahāśivarātri **Bhavnāth fair** at Girnar's foot gather **Nāgā sādhus, Nāths and avadhūtas**, reaffirming the hill's standing as a living renunciant centre. (regional tradition) [regional]

## VII. The historical clock [scholarly]

> Distinct from the *māhātmya*; secular data on the physical mountain — not to be conflated with scripture-time.

- **Aśokan & later inscriptions** — A boulder at Girnar's foot carries **Aśoka's fourteen Major Rock Edicts** (c. 257 BCE) and, on the same rock, the **Rudradāman** (150 CE) and **Skandagupta** (455–58 CE) inscriptions on the **Sudarśana lake** dam — among India's most important epigraphic records. (Wikipedia, "Junagadh rock inscription") [scholarly]
- **Temple chronology** — The surviving Jain marble shrines and Hindu summit structures are largely **medieval and later (12th c. onward)**, the worship-layer atop the far older scriptural sanctity. (Wikipedia, "Girnar") [scholarly]
