# Kedarnath — Kedāra Kṣetra

> The highest and northernmost of the **[12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md)**, head-shrine of the **[Pañcha Kedār](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md)** circuit and a **[Chota Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)** pole: a conical rock-liṅga at ~3,583 m on the Mandākinī, where the bull-form Śiva dived into the earth before the Pāṇḍavas and left his **hump** at the surface.

This file is the **standalone shrine treatment** of Kedarnath as a *kṣetra* — the bounded holy field of **Kedāra** in the upper Mandākinī valley, Rudraprayag district, Uttarakhand. It complements the group-file treatments (where Kedarnath figures as one of twelve Jyotirliṅgas, one of five Kedārs, one of four Chota Char Dhām poles) rather than repeating them. The governing distinction throughout is **two clocks** — the *scripture clock* seats the shrine's founding in the post-Kurukṣetra / Dvāpara–early-Kali frame (the Pāṇḍavas); the *academic clock* dates the surviving stone temple far younger (medieval Garhwal). [scholarly]

## The Kṣetra and its Name

- **Kedāra-kṣetra** — the sacred field is **Kedāra**, a name glossed as "marshy/boggy field" or "field of liberation"; the Śiva of the place is **Kedārnātha / Kedāreśvara**, "Lord of Kedāra." The whole upper Mandākinī basin between the Kedār peak and the Mandākinī's source is read as the *kṣetra*, with the temple as its bindu. (Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa)
- **The Skanda Purāṇa Kedāra-khaṇḍa** — Kedarnath's defining *sthala-purāṇa* sits inside the **Kedāra-khaṇḍa**, a Himalayan section of the encyclopedic Śaiva Skanda Purāṇa that maps the Garhwal Śiva-kṣetras (the same khaṇḍa frames the wider [Pañcha Kedār](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md)). It glorifies Kedāra as a place where Śiva is eternally present and where *darśana* erases the gravest sins. (Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa) [classical]
- **The Jyotirliṅga verse** — in the *Dvādaśa Jyotirliṅga Stotra* the shrine is named *"Himālaye tu Kedāram"* ("Kedāra in the Himālaya"), fixing it as the **northernmost** of the twelve self-manifest light-pillars and the only Himalayan member. (Śiva Purāṇa; Dvādaśa Jyotirliṅga Stotra, attrib. Ādi Śaṅkara) — see [12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md)

## The Pāṇḍava Bull-Form (Vṛṣabha-rūpa) Re-emergence

- **The sin and the pursuit** — after the **Kurukṣetra** war the **Pāṇḍavas** sought to expiate *gotra-hatyā* and *brahma-hatyā* (the slaying of kinsmen and Brahmins). **Śiva**, unwilling to absolve guilt still fresh, evaded them — eluding them first at **Kāśī (Varanasi)** and then fleeing into the Garhwal Himalaya. (Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa; Mahābhārata-derived sthala-purāṇa)
- **The disguised bull at Guptakāśī** — at **Guptakāśī** ("hidden Kashi") Śiva took the form of a **bull (*vṛṣabha* / Nandi-bull)** and grazed among a herd to escape detection. **Bhīma**, recognizing him, straddled two mountains and seized the bull; the **bull-form Śiva dived into the earth** (*antardhāna*), refusing to be caught. (Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa)
- **The hump surfaces at Kedarnath** — as Śiva plunged downward at Guptakāśī he **re-emerged piecemeal across Garhwal**, and the **hump / back (*pṛṣṭha-bhāga*)** of the bull rose at **Kedarnath** — the part venerated in the present conical rock. Pleased by the manifestation, the Pāṇḍavas raised the temple here and were freed of their sin. (Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa; Mahābhārata-derived tradition)
  - *Variant (re-emergence vs. dismemberment):* the dominant telling has Śiva *voluntarily* re-manifesting in five parts; a Skanda-Purāṇa-linked variant frames the bull as **torn asunder into five parts** that surfaced across the Kedār-khaṇḍa — a dismemberment rather than a re-emergence. [variant]
  - *Variant (Guptakāśī etymology):* the name **Gupta-kāśī** ("secret Kashi") is itself explained by this episode — the place where Śiva *hid* (*gupta*) after eluding the Pāṇḍavas at Kāśī. [regional]
- **The conical rock-liṅga** — Kedarnath's object of worship is **not a carved or cylindrical liṅga** but an irregular, roughly **conical / pyramidal rock outcrop** of dark stone, read as the bull's hump — a *svayambhu* (self-manifest) form rather than an installed image, consistent with its Jyotirliṅga status. (Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa; temple tradition)

## Head at Paśupatinātha — the Body-Distribution Tradition

- **Hump here, head in Nepal** — a widely held tradition extends the bull-body across the Himalaya: while the **hump** is worshipped at Kedarnath, the bull-form Śiva's **head / face** is said to have surfaced at **[Paśupatinātha](pashupatinath-kathmandu-nepal-kshetra.md)** on the Bāgmatī in Kathmandu, Nepal — knitting the two great Himalayan Śiva-kṣetras into a single dismembered-bull frame across the modern India–Nepal border. (folk/regional tradition) [folk]
  - *Variant (Pañcha Kedār internal mapping):* the more localized Garhwal scheme keeps the whole bull *within* Uttarakhand — hump → Kedarnath, arms → Tuṅganātha, **face → Rudranātha**, navel/belly → Madhyamaheśvara, hair → Kalpeśvara — and does **not** route the head to Nepal. The Paśupatinātha-head claim is a *separate*, trans-Himalayan tradition that should not be merged with the five-shrine body-map. (Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa; cf. [Pañcha Kedār](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md)) [variant — do not conflate]

## Pañcha Kedār — Head of the Circuit

- **First and supreme of the five** — Kedarnath is the **1st and head shrine** of the **[Pañcha Kedār](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md)**, the five Garhwal temples marking the bull's body-parts; the fixed worship order descends **Kedarnath (hump) → Tuṅganātha (arms) → Rudranātha (face) → Madhyamaheśvara (navel/belly) → Kalpeśvara (hair)**. It is the **only Pañcha Kedār shrine that is also a Jyotirliṅga**. (Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa; Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*)
- **Closing affirmation at Badrinath** — the Kedār pilgrim is bound by an unwritten rite to seal the circuit with a visit to **Vishnu at Badrinath**, tying the Śaiva five into the Vaiṣṇava [Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) arc. (Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*)

## High-Himalayan Pilgrimage & the Winter Idol-Shift

- **Altitude and season** — the temple stands at **~3,583 m (11,755 ft)** near the Mandākinī's headwaters below the **Kedār-dome / Kedārnāth peak**, reached on foot/pony from **Gaurīkuṇḍa / Sonprayag**. It opens only ~6 months, **Akṣaya-tṛtīyā (late April/May) to Bhai-dūj / Kārttika (Oct/Nov)**, and is snow-locked the rest of the year. (Wikipedia, *Kedarnath*; *Chota Char Dham*)
- **Winter abode — Omkareśvara at Ukhimaṭh** — when the high shrine closes, the deity's **utsava-mūrti** (palanquin/*ḍolī* image) is carried down to the **Omkareśvara Temple at Ukhimaṭh (Ukhīmaṭh)**, where Kedarnath is worshipped through the snow months; the seat is shared with Madhyamaheśvara. (Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*; *Chota Char Dham*)
- **Chota Char Dhām pole** — Kedarnath is the **Śaiva pole** of the Garhwal **[Chota Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)** (Yamunotrī → Gaṅgotrī → Kedarnath → Badrinath); in the abbreviated **Do Dhām** it pairs with Badrinath as the Śiva–Vishnu duo. (Wikipedia, *Chota Char Dham*)
- **The 2013 disaster** — the temple famously **survived the catastrophic June 2013 flash floods** that devastated the valley and killed thousands of pilgrims — a boulder (the *Bhīma-śilā*) lodged behind the shrine is credited in popular accounts with diverting the floodwater. [recorded history] (Wikipedia, *Chota Char Dham*)

## The Mahāprasthānika Terminus

- **The Pāṇḍavas' final ascent** — having built and worshipped the Kedār shrines and been absolved, the Pāṇḍavas (in the epic frame) proceeded to their **Mahāprasthāna** — the "great departure," the renunciatory northward walk into the Himālaya toward Svarga, narrated in the **Mahābhārata's Mahāprasthānika Parva**. The Garhwal pilgrimage tradition reads Kedarnath and the high Kedār country as lying on this final route, making the *kṣetra* the threshold of the Pāṇḍavas' ascent. (Mahābhārata, Mahāprasthānika Parva tradition; Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa) [regional]
  - *Note (two clocks):* in the *scripture clock* this seats Kedarnath's founding in the Dvāpara–early-Kali junction (the Pāṇḍavas). The *academic clock* dates the standing stone temple to medieval Garhwal, with the Pāṇḍava and Mahāprasthāna associations as the sthala-purāṇa overlay rather than archaeology. [scholarly]
