# Srivilliputhur — the Vaṭapatraśāyī Kṣetra and Birthplace of Āṇḍāḷ

> *Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md) · Related: [108 Divya Desams](108-divya-desams.md) · [Śrīraṅgam — Raṅganātha](srirangam-ranganatha.md) · [275 Paadal Petra Sthalams](275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md)*

**Srivilliputhur** (Tamil **Tiruvilliputtūr**, Virudhunagar district, Tamil Nadu) is the **Vaṭapatraśāyī Divya Desam** — where Viṣṇu reclines as a child on a **banyan leaf (*vaṭa-patra*)** floating on the cosmic waters — and, in Śrī Vaiṣṇava memory, the **birthplace of two Āḻvārs**: **Periyāḻvār** and his foster-daughter **Āṇḍāḷ (Gōdā Devī)**, the only woman among the twelve. It is the **origin-place of the *Tiruppāvai* and the *Nācciyār Tirumoḻi***, and its towering **rājagopuram** is the emblem on the **Government of Tamil Nadu state symbol**. As with all *māhātmya* sites, the **legend-layer** ("scriptural clock") and the **historical layer** ("academic clock") are kept apart below.

---

## I. The Vaṭapatraśāyī Mūrti — Viṣṇu on the Banyan Leaf

- **Vaṭapatraśāyī (Tamil Vaṭapatraśāyī Perumāḷ)** — the presiding deity reclines in *bhujaṅga-śayana* posture; the temple name evokes the **cosmic image of the infant Kṛṣṇa/Viṣṇu floating on a single banyan leaf** on the waters of dissolution (*pralaya*), the *Vaṭa-patra-śāyī* form celebrated by the poet-saints. (sthala-purāṇa; Āḻvār hymns)
- **The consort and the local name** — the goddess of the shrine is **Āṇḍāḷ** herself, enshrined as **Gōdā / Cūḍikkoḍutta Nācciyār** ("the lady who gave the garlands she had worn"); the kṣetra is therefore simultaneously a **Viṣṇu shrine and an Āṇḍāḷ shrine**, the only Divya Desam where a human poet-saint is worshipped as the temple goddess. (sthala-purāṇa; hagiography) [regional]
- **Divya Desam status** — Srivilliputhur is counted among the **[108 Divya Desams](108-divya-desams.md)**, the Vaiṣṇava temples hymned in the **Nālāyira Divya Prabandham**; this places it on the same sacred grid as **[Śrīraṅgam](srirangam-ranganatha.md)** and the other Āḻvār-sung abodes. (Nālāyira Divya Prabandham)
- **Not a Śaiva paadal petra sthalam** — as a Vaiṣṇava Divya Desam, Srivilliputhur stands **outside** the Śaiva **[275 Paadal Petra Sthalams](275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md)** network of the *Tēvāram*; the two pilgrimage grids run parallel and do not overlap here. [scholarly]

## II. Periyāḻvār — Viṣṇucitta, the Garland-Maker and Theologian

- **Viṣṇucitta of Srivilliputhur** — the Āḻvār later called **Periyāḻvār** ("the great Āḻvār") is born here as **Viṣṇucitta**, a brahmin devotee who tends a **tulasī and flower garden** and weaves **garlands (mālā)** daily for Vaṭapatraśāyī. (Periyāḻvār hagiography; *Guruparamparā*) [folk]
- **The Madurai debate** — summoned by the **Pāṇḍya king (Vallabhadeva / Śrīvallabha)**, Viṣṇucitta wins a court-debate establishing **Viṣṇu's supremacy (paratva)**; on his return Viṣṇu appears in the sky and he spontaneously sings the **mangaḷam *Pallāṇḍu* ("May you live long")** — fearing the very Lord might be harmed by the evil eye — earning the title **Periyāḻvār**. (Periyāḻvār hagiography) [folk]
- **His Prabandham** — Periyāḻvār's **Tiruppallāṇḍu** and **Periyāḻvār Tirumoḻi** (including the tender *piḷḷai-tamiḻ* baby-Kṛṣṇa verses) enter the **Nālāyira Divya Prabandham**; the *Tiruppallāṇḍu* opens the entire canon in recitation order. (Nālāyira Divya Prabandham) [scholarly]

## III. Āṇḍāḷ (Gōdā Devī) — Found in the Tulasī Garden

- **The foundling in the tulasī bed** — Periyāḻvār discovers an infant girl **beneath a tulasī plant (*tuḷaci*) in his temple garden**; he names her **Kōdai / Gōdai** ("garland of flowers") and raises her as his daughter — the future **Āṇḍāḷ** ("she who rules [over the Lord]"). (Āṇḍāḷ hagiography; *Guruparamparā*) [folk]
  - *Variant (Bhū-devī avatāra) [regional]:* tradition holds Āṇḍāḷ to be an **incarnation of Bhūmi-devī (the Earth goddess)**, born of the earth to demonstrate ideal *bhakti* and bridal love (*madhura-bhāva*) for human beings.
- **The worn-garland transgression** — the girl secretly **wears the flower-garlands meant for Vaṭapatraśāyī** before they are offered, admiring her reflection as the Lord's bride; discovered, Periyāḻvār is mortified — but Viṣṇu appears in a dream and declares he will **accept only the garlands Gōdai has first worn**, whence her epithet **Cūḍikkoḍutta Nācciyār** ("she who gave the garlands she wore"). (Āṇḍāḷ hagiography) [folk]
- **A girlhood at Srivilliputhur** — the **well** from which she drew water, the garden, and the shrine are still shown at the temple as the physical setting of her legend, anchoring the hagiography to the kṣetra's geography. [regional]

## IV. The Tiruppāvai and Nācciyār Tirumoḻi — Composed Here

- **The *Tiruppāvai*** — Āṇḍāḷ's **thirty-verse *pāvai* (vow) poem**, framed as Gōpīs of Vraja keeping the *mārgaḻi* (Mārgaśīrṣa) **Kāttiyāyani-vrata** to win Kṛṣṇa, is **composed at Srivilliputhur**; recited across Tamil Nadu through the whole month of **Mārgaḻi (Dec–Jan)**, it is among the most beloved texts of the Prabandham and is chanted in temples far beyond the Śrī Vaiṣṇava world. (Nālāyira Divya Prabandham — *Tiruppāvai*)
- **The *Nācciyār Tirumoḻi*** — her longer, more intensely **erotic-devotional (*madhura-bhāva*)** work of 143 verses, including the **Kāmadeva-vrata**, the dream-wedding (*Vāraṇam āyiram*), and the cloud-messenger verses — voicing a bride's longing to wed Viṣṇu alone. (Nālāyira Divya Prabandham — *Nācciyār Tirumoḻi*)
- **The vow to wed only Raṅganātha** — in these poems Āṇḍāḷ refuses all earthly marriage and vows to wed **Raṅganātha of [Śrīraṅgam](srirangam-ranganatha.md)**; the resolve drives the bridal-mysticism that defines her cult. (Nālāyira Divya Prabandham; hagiography) [folk]
- **The merging at Śrīraṅgam** — Viṣṇu commands Periyāḻvār to **bring Gōdai in bridal procession to Śrīraṅgam**, where she **enters the sanctum and merges into Raṅganātha**, completing her marriage to the Lord — narratively binding Srivilliputhur (her birth) to Śrīraṅgam (her union). (Āṇḍāḷ hagiography; *Guruparamparā*) [folk]

## V. The Rājagopuram — Emblem of Tamil Nadu

- **The towering gopuram** — the temple's main **rājagopuram**, rising in **eleven tiers to roughly 59 m (~192 ft)**, is one of the tallest temple towers in the region and dominates the Srivilliputhur skyline; it is traditionally linked to a Pāṇḍya/Nāyaka-era patron. (temple tradition; academic) [scholarly]
- **The state symbol** — this gopuram's silhouette is the **emblem on the official symbol of the Government of Tamil Nadu**, making Srivilliputhur's tower an icon of the entire state. (Government of Tamil Nadu) [scholarly]
- **Twin sanctums** — the complex holds **two principal shrines**: the **Vaṭapatraśāyī (Viṣṇu) temple** and the adjacent **Āṇḍāḷ temple**, treated as a single Divya Desam but architecturally a paired kṣetra of Lord and saint-goddess. [regional]

---

## VI. The Garland Tradition — Linking Srivilliputhur to Śrīraṅgam

- **The *tirukkaḻal sāṟṟu* / garland-offering link** — by the legend of the worn garlands and Āṇḍāḷ's union with Raṅganātha, Srivilliputhur and Śrīraṅgam are joined in a **living ritual relationship**: garlands (*mālā*) made at Āṇḍāḷ's natal shrine are sent to be offered to **Raṅganātha at Śrīraṅgam**, re-enacting the bride's gift to her Lord. (temple tradition; hagiography) [regional]
- **Mutual festival exchange** — the two temples honor the Āṇḍāḷ–Raṅganātha marriage in their festival calendars (notably **Āḍi Pūram**, Āṇḍāḷ's birth-asterism, and the **mārgaḻi** *Tiruppāvai* recitation), keeping the Srivilliputhur–Śrīraṅgam axis ritually alive. [regional]

---

## VII. Two Clocks — Legend vs History

- **Scriptural clock** — the Vaṭapatraśāyī form, Āṇḍāḷ's birth from the tulasī bed (or as Bhū-devī), the worn-garland miracle, and her merging into Raṅganātha sit in **māhātmya / hagiographical time**, undatable on any calendar; in sampradāya reckoning the Āḻvārs belong to the **Dvāpara–Kali hinge** and are placed by tradition in remote antiquity. [legend]
- **Academic clock** — historically the **Āḻvārs flourished c. 6th–9th century CE**; Periyāḻvār and Āṇḍāḷ are usually dated to the **8th–9th century CE** in the Pāṇḍya country. The present temple fabric — the rājagopuram and maṇḍapas — is largely **Pāṇḍya, Vijayanagara and Nāyaka** work of the **medieval period**, with the site continuously endowed thereafter. [scholarly]
