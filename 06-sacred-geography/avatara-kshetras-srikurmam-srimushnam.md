# Avatāra Kṣetras — Śrīkūrmam & Śrīmuṣṇam

> *Two living temples that complete the avatar-abode roster: the Kūrma (Tortoise) and the Bhū-Varāha (Boar). Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md) · Related: [Sthala-Purāṇas of Major Temples](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md) · [Tirumala–Tirupati](tirumala-tirupati-venkateshwara.md) · [Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md) · [Muktināth–Gaṇḍakī Śālagrāma Kṣetra](muktinath-gandaki-shalagrama-kshetra.md) · [Daśāvatāra / Vishnu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)*

Most of the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) forms anchor a premier living temple — Narasiṃha at Ahobilam/[Siṃhācalam](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md), Rāma at [Ayodhyā](ayodhya.md), Kṛṣṇa at [Mathurā](mathura-vrindavan-braj.md) and [Dvārakā](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md), Vāmana and Paraśurāma in Kerala-Tamil shrines. **Two avatāras are unusually thin on the ground**: the **Kūrma** (Tortoise, 2nd avatāra) has effectively *one* major temple anywhere — **Śrīkūrmam** in coastal Andhra; and the **Bhū-Varāha** (Boar lifting the Earth, 3rd avatāra) has its foremost southern *kṣetra* at **Śrīmuṣṇam** in Tamil Nadu. This file pairs the two abodes that "complete the roster." The *traditional* (Yuga / Purāṇa-internal) frame is kept distinct from the *academic* (inscriptional / architectural) frame throughout — the **"two clocks."** [scholarly]

---

## I. Śrīkūrmam — the Tortoise (Kūrma) Kṣetra

- **Śrī Kūrmanātha / Kūrma-svāmī** — the presiding deity at **Śrīkūrmam**, near Śrīkākulam in coastal northern Andhra Pradesh; **[Vishnu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** worshipped in his **Kūrma** (tortoise) form — held to be the **only major temple of the Kūrma avatāra** in India. (Wikipedia, *Srikurmam*; Śrīkūrma-māhātmya)
- **The horizontal tortoise-icon** — uniquely, the mūrti is not an anthropomorphic standing Vishnu but a **tortoise-shaped svayam-vyakta stone** laid out roughly horizontally; the deity is worshipped in two stones, a *Kūrma-rūpa* (tortoise-back/śilā) at the rear and a smaller front stone, an iconography found at virtually no other temple. (Wikipedia, *Srikurmam*) [regional]
- **The dual dhwaja-stambhas** — Śrīkūrmam is famous for having **two flag-staffs (dhwaja-stambha)** — one at the front and one at the rear of the shrine — reflecting that the tortoise-deity is worshipped from **both ends** (head and tail), a layout said to be unique among South Indian temples. (Wikipedia, *Srikurmam*; temple tradition) [regional]
- **The pillared maṇḍapa & sacred tank** — the complex has a celebrated **stone pillared maṇḍapa** (traditionally "thousand-pillared") and the tank **Śveta-puṣkariṇī / Kūrma-tīrtha**, where the *māhātmya* sites the avatāra's appearance. (Wikipedia, *Srikurmam*)

### The Kūrma sthala-purāṇa

- **The Samudra-manthana frame** — the governing myth is the **churning of the ocean of milk**: when Mount **Mandara** sinks while the devas and asuras churn for *amṛta*, Vishnu becomes the **Kūrma** (tortoise) and dives beneath to bear the mountain on his back as the churning-pivot — the cosmogonic deed the *kṣetra* localizes here. (Kūrma Purāṇa; *Bhāgavata* 8; *Mahābhārata* Ādi-parva)
- **Śveta-mahārṣi / the king's vision** — the local *Śrīkūrma-māhātmya* (transmitted with **Padma**/**Skanda**/**Brahmāṇḍa** material) holds that a sage or king (often **Śveta**) performed *tapas* and Vishnu manifested **self-revealed as the tortoise-stone** at this spot on the Bay-of-Bengal shore. (Śrīkūrma-māhātmya; temple tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant (svayam-vyakta refinding):* like other *self-manifest* shrines, the tortoise-icon is said to have been **lost / buried and re-discovered**, the worship re-established by a later devotee — the standard *svayam-vyakta* "lost-and-refound" motif. [folk]
- **Vaiṣṇava sectarian history** — Śrīkūrmam is notable in **Madhva / Gauḍīya** lore: it is counted among the temples **[Madhva](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/ramanuja-and-madhva.md)** and later **Caitanya** are said to have visited, and inscriptions record its conversion from **Śaiva to Vaiṣṇava** worship (attributed to the **Dvaita ācārya Narahari Tīrtha**, a disciple of Madhva, 13th–14th c.). (Wikipedia, *Srikurmam*; Dvaita tradition) [scholarly]

### Śrīkūrmam — the historical clock

- **The standing temple — Eastern Ganga work** — the present stone complex is largely **Eastern Ganga / Kaliṅga-style** (13th–14th c. CE), with later **Gajapati, Reddi and Vijayanagara** additions; its walls carry a **dense inscriptional record** including Sanskrit, Telugu and Odia epigraphs. (Wikipedia, *Srikurmam Temple*; ASI; *South Indian Inscriptions*)
- **Narahari Tīrtha's regency** — inscriptions tie the **Madhva** ascetic **Narahari Tīrtha** (regent of the Kaliṅga Gaṅga court, late 13th c.) to the temple's reorganization as a Vaiṣṇava centre — an epigraphically grounded link between a great ascetic and the shrine. (Epigraphia Indica; Dvaita tradition) [scholarly]
- **Two clocks** — *traditionally* the tortoise-icon is **timeless / svayam-vyakta**, manifest in the cosmogonic churning age; *academically* continuous worship is attested from **c. 11th–14th c. CE** into the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** historical period. [scholarly]

## II. Śrīmuṣṇam — the Bhū-Varāha (Boar) Kṣetra

- **Śrī Bhū-Varāha-svāmī** — the presiding deity at **Śrīmuṣṇam** (Cuddalore district, Tamil Nadu); **[Vishnu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** as the **Bhū-Varāha**, the Boar who lifts the Earth-goddess **Bhū-devī** on his tusk — held to be the **foremost Varāha-kṣetra of the South**. (Wikipedia, *Bhuvarahaswamy Temple, Srimushnam*; Śrīmuṣṇa-māhātmya)
- **The Earth-rescue iconography** — the mūrti shows Varāha **standing with Bhū-devī seated on his thigh/elbow**, the cosmic moment of the earth lifted from the *pralaya*-waters after the Boar slays the demon **Hiraṇyākṣa** who had rolled her to the ocean-floor. (Varāha Purāṇa; *Bhāgavata* 3; temple tradition)
- **One of the Aṣṭa svayam-vyakta kṣetras** — Śrīmuṣṇam is reckoned among the **eight self-manifest (*svayam-vyakta*) abodes of Vishnu**, the icons held to have arisen on their own rather than been carved. The classic list of eight is: **[Tirumala (Veṅkaṭādri)](tirumala-tirupati-venkateshwara.md), [Śrīraṅgam](srirangam-ranganatha.md), Śrīmuṣṇam, Naimiṣāraṇya, Puṣkara, Badari, [Śālagrāma](muktinath-gandaki-shalagrama-kshetra.md), and Toṭādri** (Vānamāmalai). (Vaiṣṇava tradition; Wikipedia, *Bhuvarahaswamy Temple*) [traditional]
  - *Variant (the eight-list):* the membership of the *aṣṭa svayam-vyakta* varies slightly by recension — some lists substitute **Puṣkara** or **Naimiṣāraṇya** with other shrines — but Śrīmuṣṇam is **constant** in nearly all of them. [disputed]

### The Śrīmuṣṇa sthala-purāṇa

- **The Brahmāṇḍa-Purāṇa māhātmya** — Śrīmuṣṇam has its own **Śrīmuṣṇa-māhātmya**, traditionally transmitted within the **Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa**, glorifying the site as the place where Varāha **stayed after lifting the Earth** and where the avatāra is permanently present. (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, Śrīmuṣṇa-māhātmya)
- **Etymology — "the snatched-away place"** — the name *Śrī-muṣṇam* is glossed from *muṣ-* ("to snatch / carry off"), commemorating that **Varāha snatched the Earth from the demon** here, or that the place itself "carries away" the sins of the pilgrim — a *sthala* etymology tied to the Earth-rescue. (Śrīmuṣṇa-māhātmya) [folk etymology]
- **The Nityapuṣkariṇī tank** — the temple's sacred tank, the **Nitya-puṣkariṇī ("ever-flowing pool")**, is held to **never dry up** and to be fed by all the holy *tīrthas*; bathing in it is the prescribed first act of the pilgrim, and its waters are central to the *māhātmya*'s merit-claims. (Śrīmuṣṇa-māhātmya; temple tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant (Bhū-devī's request):* some tellings have the Earth-goddess **Bhū-devī** ask Varāha to remain visible at this spot for the welfare of beings, grounding the shrine's perpetual-presence claim. [regional]
  - *Variant (Śveta-Varāha kalpa link):* the *māhātmya* situates the deed in the **Śveta-Varāha kalpa**, the present *kalpa* named for the white-boar avatāra — fixing the *kṣetra* on the cosmogonic clock. [traditional]

### Śrīmuṣṇam — the historical clock

- **The standing temple — Vijayanagara / Nāyaka monument** — the present complex is a **Vijayanagara–Nāyaka-period** foundation (largely 16th c. CE), built and endowed under **Vijayanagara** authority and the **Tanjore / Senji (Gingee) Nāyakas**; **Acyutarāya** and later **Kṛṣṇappa Nāyaka** figure in its patronage tradition. (Wikipedia, *Bhuvarahaswamy Temple, Srimushnam*; inscriptions)
- **The carved chariot-maṇḍapa & inscriptions** — the temple is noted for an ornate **stone *ratha*-maṇḍapa** and a substantial **inscriptional record** (Vijayanagara–Nāyaka grants) documenting its endowments and festival economy. (*South Indian Inscriptions*; ASI)
- **Two clocks** — *traditionally* the Boar-icon is **svayam-vyakta**, manifest in the Śveta-Varāha *kalpa*; *academically* the monumental temple and its continuous worship date from the **Vijayanagara–Nāyaka era (c. 16th c. CE)** into the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** historical period — the *māhātmya* being a **[late text]** layer over older Varāha-devotion. [scholarly]

## III. Network & cross-links

- **Completing the avatar-abode roster** — together Śrīkūrmam (Kūrma) and Śrīmuṣṇam (Bhū-Varāha) supply the **two thinnest avatāra-abodes**, complementing the Varāha-Narasiṃha fusion at **[Siṃhācalam](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md)**, the boar-priority legend at **[Tirumala](tirumala-tirupati-venkateshwara.md)**, and the broader **[sthala-purāṇa](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md)** tier of major living temples. [scholarly]
- **Neither is a Divya Deśam in the full sense** — Śrīmuṣṇam is an **Abhimāna-sthala** (a much-revered Vaiṣṇava shrine just outside the canonical 108) rather than one of the [108 Divya Deśams](108-divya-desams.md); Śrīkūrmam, in Andhra, lies outside the Tamil Āḻvār canon entirely — both belong to the **svayam-vyakta / sthala-purāṇa** tier rather than the saint-canonised [temple networks](temple-networks-and-circuits.md). [scholarly]
- **The svayam-vyakta family** — Śrīmuṣṇam's membership in the **Aṣṭa svayam-vyakta kṣetras** ties it to [Tirumala](tirumala-tirupati-venkateshwara.md), [Śrīraṅgam](srirangam-ranganatha.md) and the [Śālagrāma–Gaṇḍakī](muktinath-gandaki-shalagrama-kshetra.md) Vishnu-abode in a single class of "self-manifest" shrines. [traditional]
