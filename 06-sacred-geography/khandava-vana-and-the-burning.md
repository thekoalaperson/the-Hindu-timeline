# Khāṇḍava-vana & the Khāṇḍava-dāha

> Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up: [Master Index](../README.md) · Related: [Kurukṣetra — Dharmakṣetra](kurukshetra-dharmakshetra.md) · [Mathurā–Vṛndāvana & Braj](mathura-vrindavan-braj.md) · [Bhāratavarṣa & Purāṇic Geography](bharatavarsha-and-puranic-geography.md) · [Mahābhārata](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/README.md)
>
> The **Khāṇḍava forest (Khāṇḍava-vana)** on the **Yamunā** — the woodland that **Agni** consumes with the help of **Kṛṣṇa** and **Arjuna** to cure his ritual indigestion and to clear the ground on which **Indraprastha** is raised. The *Khāṇḍava-dāha* ("burning of Khāṇḍava") closes the *Ādi-parva*: Arjuna receives the **Gāṇḍīva** bow, two inexhaustible quivers and a divine chariot; the architect **Maya Dānava** is spared and pays his debt by building the **Mayasabhā**; the serpent **Aśvasena** and the four **Śārṅgaka** birds escape the flames; and **Indra**, the forest's protector, is fought to a standstill.

---

## I. The forest, its place & two clocks

- **Khāṇḍava-vana** — a great forest on the western bank of the **Yamunā**, part of the territory granted to the Pāṇḍavas at the partition of the kingdom; its clearing makes room for their new capital **Indraprastha** (later identified with the Delhi region, around Purāṇā Qila). (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 199–200, 214) — see [Bhāratavarṣa & Purāṇic Geography](bharatavarsha-and-puranic-geography.md)
- **Khāṇḍava-prastha → Indra-prastha** — the half-kingdom the Pāṇḍavas receive is the wild tract **Khāṇḍava-prastha**; once the forest is burnt and the land is cleared and built upon by Maya, it becomes the city **Indraprastha**. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva*)
- **Scriptural clock** — the episode falls in the **Dvāpara Yuga**, in the generation of the Pāṇḍavas before the great war, contemporary with **Kṛṣṇa's** career at [Mathurā / Dvārakā](mathura-vrindavan-braj.md); the war and the Dvāpara–Kali cusp lie a generation later at [Kurukṣetra](kurukshetra-dharmakshetra.md). (Purāṇic yuga-scheme) — see [Yuga System](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md)
- **Academic clock** — the *Khāṇḍava-dāha* is read by scholars as an epic memory of **forest-clearing by fire** to found a settlement in the Yamunā tract, mythologized as a contest with Indra (rain) and Agni (fire). [scholarly]
- **The Mandapāla frame** — the burning is bracketed by the tale of the ṛṣi **Mandapāla** and the **Śārṅgaka birds**, so the *parva* moralizes the holocaust through the survival of the innocent. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 220–225)

## II. Why the forest burns — Agni's indigestion

- **Śvetaki's twelve-year sacrifice** — the king **Śvetaki** performs an unbroken sacrifice so long that Agni, gorged on a ceaseless diet of clarified butter (*ghṛta*), grows pale, sick and **dyspeptic** — his fire-appetite ruined by surfeit. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 214–215)
- **Brahmā's prescription** — Agni begs **Brahmā** for a cure; Brahmā ordains that Agni must **consume the Khāṇḍava forest**, whose herbs, beasts and serpents are the medicine that will restore his digestion. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 215)
- **Indra's blockade** — each time Agni kindles the forest, **Indra** — its protector, and friend of the **Nāga Takṣaka** who dwells there — **quenches the blaze with rain**; Agni fails **seven times** to burn Khāṇḍava. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 215–216)
- **The need for human helpers** — Brahmā tells Agni that only when **Nara and Nārāyaṇa** (incarnate as **Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa**) are present at Khāṇḍava can he overcome Indra; Agni must seek their aid. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 216)

---

## III. Agni's petition — the brāhmaṇa at the Yamunā

- **The disguised mendicant** — Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna, idling by the **Yamunā** after their riverside excursion, are approached by Agni in the form of a **tall, tawny brāhmaṇa** who declares his great appetite and asks them to satisfy his hunger. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 216–217)
- **The boon of arms** — to enable them to withstand Indra and all the gods, Agni summons **Varuṇa**, who yields his celestial weapons: the bow **Gāṇḍīva**, the inexhaustible quivers, and the chariot. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 217)

---

## IV. Arjuna's divine arsenal & Kṛṣṇa's discus

- **Gāṇḍīva** — the supreme bow, originally **Soma's**, passed to Varuṇa and now given by Agni (via Varuṇa) to **Arjuna**; it becomes Arjuna's signature weapon for the rest of the epic, earning him the name **Gāṇḍīvadhanvan**. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 217)
- **The two inexhaustible quivers (*akṣaya tūṇīra*)** — twin quivers whose arrows **never run dry**, granted alongside the bow. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 217)
- **The chariot with the *kapi-dhvaja* banner** — a celestial car yoked with white horses, given by Agni/Varuṇa, bearing a flagstaff topped by **Hanumān (the ape, *kapi*)** — hence Arjuna's epithet **Kapidhvaja**. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 217)
- **Kṛṣṇa's Sudarśana cakra & Kaumodakī mace** — Agni grants **Kṛṣṇa** the fiery discus **Sudarśana-cakra** and the mace **Kaumodakī**, his enduring attributes thereafter. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 217)
  - *Note (Vaiṣṇava reading):* the *cakra* is elsewhere Viṣṇu's eternal weapon; the *Khāṇḍava* episode is treated as the occasion of its (re-)appearance in Kṛṣṇa's hand, not its origin. [late text]

## V. The burning — Agni and the two heroes versus the gods

- **Agni encircles the forest** — armed and warded, Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna take their stations on **opposite sides** of Khāṇḍava; **Agni blazes up in a thousand fronts** and the forest is set alight on every side. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 218)
- **The slaughter of the fleeing creatures** — as beasts, birds, *piśācas*, *rākṣasas* and *nāgas* break for the edges, **Arjuna's arrows and Kṛṣṇa's discus drive them back into the flames**; none of the burning forest's creatures is allowed to escape (save the few who are spared). (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 218–219)
- **Indra's storm and his army** — **Indra** pours down torrential rain to quench Agni and leads the **assembled gods** — Yama, Varuṇa, Kubera, the Maruts and others — against the pair; **Arjuna roofs the forest with a canopy of arrows** that holds back the rain, and the heroes drive off the divine host. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 218–219)
- **Indra fought to a standstill** — unable to save the forest, **Indra desists**; a heavenly voice (in some tellings) confirms that Nara and Nārāyaṇa cannot be overcome, and Indra, recognizing his son Arjuna's prowess, withdraws — later promising Arjuna celestial weapons. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 219)
- **Six survivors only** — the *parva* states that **only six beings** came out of Khāṇḍava alive: **Aśvasena**, **Maya**, and the **four Śārṅgaka birds**. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 219, 225)

---

## VI. The survivors

- **Aśvasena the serpent** — son of the Nāga **Takṣaka** (who was away at Kurukṣetra during the fire); his mother swallows him and bears him up out of the flames, and he escapes. **Arjuna shoots at the fleeing snake**, and Indra raises a wind to save him — sealing **Aśvasena's lifelong enmity toward Arjuna**, which resurfaces when Aśvasena rides Karṇa's *nāga* arrow at Kurukṣetra. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 219; cf. *Karṇa-parva*) — see [Kurukṣetra](kurukshetra-dharmakshetra.md)
- **Maya Dānava** — the *asura* architect, sheltering in Khāṇḍava, runs for his life; **Kṛṣṇa raises his discus to kill him**, but Maya rushes to **Arjuna** for protection, and Arjuna grants him *abhaya* (safety) — so Maya is spared from both Kṛṣṇa and Agni. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 219)
- **The four Śārṅgaka birds** — four nestling birds (sons of the ṛṣi **Mandapāla** by the hen-bird **Jaritā**), too young to fly; **Jaritā shields them** and Mandapāla had earlier won a promise from **Agni** to spare them, so the fire passes them by. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 220–225)
  - *The Mandapāla backstory:* the sage **Mandapāla**, childless and barred from the higher worlds for want of offspring, became a **Śārṅgaka bird** to breed quickly, fathered the four chicks on **Jaritā**, then left her for another mate **Lapitā** — but secured Agni's pledge of protection for his sons; the *parva* uses the family quarrel to frame the ethics of the burning. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 220–225) [a self-contained interpolated *upākhyāna*]
- **Takṣaka's escape by absence** — the Nāga king **Takṣaka**, lord of Khāṇḍava and Indra's friend, **survives only because he was at Kurukṣetra** when the forest burned; his clan is largely destroyed, fueling the long Nāga–Bhārata feud that culminates in the **Sarpa-satra** of Janamejaya. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva*; cf. *Āstīka-parva*)

---

## VII. Maya's debt — the Mayasabhā

- **Maya offers to repay Arjuna** — grateful to have been spared, **Maya Dānava** asks how he may serve; at **Kṛṣṇa's** suggestion he undertakes to build a peerless **assembly-hall (sabhā)** for **Yudhiṣṭhira**. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 219; *Sabhā-parva* 1–3)
- **The Mayasabhā (Maya-built hall)** — Maya raises the wondrous **Mayasabhā** at **Indraprastha** from materials he fetches from **Lake Bindusaras** near **Mount Maināka**, including the mace he gives Bhīma and the conch **Devadatta** he gives Arjuna; the hall's crystalline floors and illusory pools become the setting of **Yudhiṣṭhira's Rājasūya** and, fatefully, of **Duryodhana's humiliation** that helps spark the war. (*Mahābhārata, Sabhā-parva* 1–3) — see [Kurukṣetra](kurukshetra-dharmakshetra.md)
- **The clearing becomes the capital** — the burnt ground of Khāṇḍava is thus the literal foundation of **Indraprastha**, the Pāṇḍava capital, linking the *dāha* directly to the city-founding. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-/Sabhā-parva*)

## VIII. Readings & resonances

- **Nara–Nārāyaṇa typology** — the episode is the *Ādi-parva*'s great demonstration that **Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa are Nara and Nārāyaṇa**, invincible together — a theme the *Bhagavad Gītā* later raises to doctrine. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva*) — see [Mahābhārata](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/README.md)
- **The cost of the holocaust** — later tradition and modern readers note the **moral weight** of the wholesale destruction of forest life; the Mandapāla–Śārṅgaka frame and the karmic threads (Aśvasena's revenge, the Nāga feud) are read as the epic's own acknowledgment of that cost. [disputed — degree of intended critique]
- **Ecological / historical reading** — the burning is interpreted as a **mythic memory of deforestation by fire** in the Doāb to found a polity, with Indra (monsoon) and Agni (clearing-fire) as personified forces. [scholarly]
- **Weapon-origins anchor** — the *Khāṇḍava-dāha* is the canonical origin-point in the epic for **Gāṇḍīva**, the **inexhaustible quivers**, the **kapi-dhvaja chariot**, **Sudarśana** in Kṛṣṇa's hand, and the **Devadatta** conch — the kit Arjuna carries to [Kurukṣetra](kurukshetra-dharmakshetra.md). (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva*)
