# The Mahābhārata Tīrtha-yatra Parva — Catalogue of Fords

> *Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md)*
> *Related: [Tīrtha Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md) · [Sapta Purī & Sacred Cities](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md) · [Puṣkara](pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md) · [Prayāga / Triveṇī](prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md) · [Kurukṣetra](kurukshetra-dharmakshetra.md) · [Gayā](gaya-pitru-tirtha.md) · [Prabhāsa / Somnāth](prabhasa-somnath-tirtha.md) · [Naimiṣāraṇya](naimisharanya-chakra-tirtha.md) · [Muktinātha / Gaṇḍakī](muktinath-gandaki-shalagrama-kshetra.md)*

**Why a dedicated file.** The **Tīrtha-yatra Parva** (the "Pilgrimage-of-the-Fords" sub-parva of the *Mahābhārata*'s Vana Parva, adhyāyas **80–156**) is the **single oldest systematic gazetteer of Hindu holy fords** — a *tīrtha-māhātmya* embedded in the epic before the freestanding Purāṇic māhātmyas crystallised, and the master roster the later catalogues (Padma, Skanda, Matsya, Vāyu) elaborated. This node is the **structural index** for the section's tīrtha material: it enumerates the fords of the parva's two great speeches and one walked circuit, gives each its merit (*phala*), and links every major ford to its dedicated file while housing the lesser members. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 80–156)

**The three framing speeches.** The parva nests three distinct tīrtha-catalogues — keep them apart:
- **Pulastya to Bhīṣma** (adhy. 82–85) — the sage Pulastya recites the fords to a young **Bhīṣma**, who repeats them; the prototype *anukramaṇī* of tīrthas, ordered roughly by region. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 82–85)
- **Nārada / Dhaumya to the Pāṇḍavas** (adhy. 86–88) — **Nārada** (and the Pāṇḍava priest **Dhaumya**) lay out the four-quarter circuit (east, south, west, north) as a route-plan for the exiled **Yudhiṣṭhira**. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 86–88)
- **Lomaśa's walked circuit** (adhy. 89–156) — the sage **Lomaśa** actually *leads* the Pāṇḍavas around the ford-network, the catalogue dramatised as a journey with *upākhyānas* (inset legends) at the stations. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 89–156)

**Two clocks.** In *scriptural* time the fords are sanctified before yuga-reckoning and the Pāṇḍavas walk them in the late **Dvāpara**; in *terrestrial* time the parva is a roughly mid-first-millennium-BCE-to-early-CE textual stratum whose place-names anchor a real north-Indian pilgrim geography. [scholarly]

## I. The crown fords — the great five

- **Puṣkara** — named the **first and foremost** of all tīrthas, where Brahmā himself dwells; a bath here on the full moon of Kārttika yields the merit of countless sacrifices. The parva opens the catalogue with it. Dedicated file: **[Puṣkara — Brahmā-tīrtha](pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md)**. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 80, 82)
- **Prayāga** — the **Gaṅgā–Yamunā–(Sarasvatī) confluence**, the "king of tīrthas" (*tīrtha-rāja*); the parva extols *Prayāga-snāna* and death at the confluence as supremely liberating, naming the **Akṣayavaṭa** and the *veṇī*. Dedicated file: **[Prayāga / Triveṇī Saṅgama](prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md)**. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 83–85)
- **Kurukṣetra** — the *dharma-kṣetra*; the parva calls even its **dust** sanctifying — "the very dust of Kurukṣetra, borne on the wind, leads sinners to the highest path" — and clusters dozens of sub-tīrthas around it (Rāmahrada, Sthāṇu-tīrtha, etc., §IV). Dedicated file: **[Kurukṣetra](kurukshetra-dharmakshetra.md)**. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 83)
- **Gayā** — the supreme **pitṛ-tīrtha**; the parva names the **Akṣayavaṭa** and the offering of *piṇḍa* that liberates the ancestors, and the **Phalgu / Brahmasaras**. Dedicated file: **[Gayā — Pitṛ-tīrtha](gaya-pitru-tirtha.md)**. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 84, 87, 95)
- **Prabhāsa** — the foremost **western** ford, the Sarasvatī–ocean mouth where Soma regained his light; Lomaśa's circuit reaches it after the Sarasvatī line. Dedicated file: **[Prabhāsa / Somnāth](prabhasa-somnath-tirtha.md)**. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 80–82, 88, 118–120)

---

## II. Naimiṣa & the Gomatī fords

- **Naimiṣāraṇya (Naimiṣa)** — the recitation-forest on the **Gomatī**, ranked a supreme pan-sectarian ford; the parva says the gods themselves resort to it, and a *cāndrāyaṇa*-merit attends a bath. Dedicated file: **[Naimiṣāraṇya — Chakra-tīrtha](naimisharanya-chakra-tirtha.md)**. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 83, 87)
- **Gaṅgodbheda & the Gomatī-saṅgama** — lesser stations along the Gomatī named in Pulastya's roster; bathing where the Gomatī meets the Gaṅgā is praised. [scholarly]

---

## III. The Gaṇḍakī / Śālagrāma & the eastern-Himalayan fords

- **Gaṇḍakī (Śālagrāmī)** — the river yielding the **śālagrāma** ammonite-stones aniconic of Viṣṇu; the parva praises bathing in the **Gaṇḍakī** and at its source, a tīrtha "frequented by the gods." Dedicated file: **[Muktinātha / Gaṇḍakī Śālagrāma-kṣetra](muktinath-gandaki-shalagrama-kshetra.md)**. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 84, 87)
- **Kokāmukha** — a famed **Varāha (boar) tīrtha** of the eastern Himalaya, where one who dies fasting is said to gain unending worlds; named in both Pulastya's roster and Lomaśa's route as a Viṣṇu-station of great power. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 84, 87) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (location, Skanda):* later māhātmyas place Kokāmukha variously on the Kauśikī (Kosi) or the upper Gaṇḍakī fan; the epic fixes only its Varāha character. [disputed]
- **Kauśikī / Viśvāmitra-tīrtha** — the river **Kauśikī (Kosi)**, where **Viśvāmitra** won brahminhood; the bank is a tīrtha cancelling sin. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 87)

---

## IV. The Sarasvatī line & the Kurukṣetra sub-fords

- **Saptasaindhava (Sapta-Sārasvata)** — the **"seven Sarasvatīs,"** a heptad of the goddess-river's named streams (incl. *Suprabhā, Kāñcanākṣī, Viśālā, Manoramā, Oghavatī, Surveṇu, Vimalodakā*) gathered at one *sapta-sārasvata* ford where the sage **Maṅkaṇaka** sported; a crowning Sarasvatī-tīrtha. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 83, Śalya Parva 37–38) [scholarly]
- **Vinaśana** — where the Sarasvatī **sinks** into the desert sands (*antaḥsalilā*); a tīrtha of her disappearance. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 82)
- **Camasodbheda · Śivodbheda · Nāgodbheda** — the three **"resurfacings"** (*udbheda*) where the hidden Sarasvatī breaks ground again; named as a triad in Pulastya's western section. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 80–82)
- **Rāmahrada (the five lakes of Paraśurāma)** — at **Samanta-pañcaka / Kurukṣetra**, the five blood-pools Paraśurāma filled after slaying the kṣatriyas; bathing there satisfies the ancestors. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 83) — see **[Kurukṣetra](kurukshetra-dharmakshetra.md)**.
- **Sthāṇu-tīrtha · Pṛthūdaka (Pehowa) · Auśanasa** — principal Kurukṣetra-cluster fords (Śiva's *sthāṇu* post; the Sarasvatī ghāṭ at Pṛthūdaka praised above all others on that river; the tīrtha of **Uśanas-Śukra**). (*Mbh* Vana Parva 83)

## V. The southern & western arc (Lomaśa's circuit)

- **Agastya-tīrtha / Kanyā (Kanyākumārī)** — the southern sea-ford of the maiden-goddess at land's end. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 85, 88)
- **Godāvarī, Veṇṇā, Bhīmarathī** — Deccan river-fords of the southern quarter; the **Godāvarī** praised as a sin-destroying bath. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 85, 88)
- **Payoṣṇī, Daṇḍaka, Śūrpāraka (Sopārā)** — western-coast tīrthas; **Śūrpāraka** is the **Paraśurāma land-reclamation** ford. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 85, 88, 118)
- **Piṇḍāraka** — a Saurāṣṭra coastal tīrtha (near Dvārakā) where bathing is said to leave **lotus-marked piṇḍa-stones** in the bather's hand as a sign of merit. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 82, 88) [regional]
- **Dvāravatī (Dvārakā) & the Sindhu-saṅgama** — Kṛṣṇa's city and the river-sea confluences of Saurāṣṭra, closing the western arc toward Prabhāsa. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 88, 118–120)

---

## VI. The northern & Himalayan fords

- **Gaṅgādvāra (Haridwar) · Kanakhala** — the gateway where Gaṅgā enters the plain; a primary northern bathing-tīrtha. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 84, 90)
- **Badarī / Viśālā & Nara-Nārāyaṇa-āśrama** — the upper-Himalayan hermitage of **Nara-Nārāyaṇa** at Badarī; Lomaśa leads the Pāṇḍavas here and toward the **Kailāsa / Gandhamādana** heights and **Kubera's** Saugandhika lake (the Bhīma–Hanumān episode). (*Mbh* Vana Parva 145–153)
- **Bindusaras · Hemakūṭa · Ṛṣabha** — northern mountain-tīrthas of the Bhāgīrathī descent and the *gaṅgāvataraṇa* legend (Bhagīratha's tapas), recited along the route. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 108–109)

---

## VII. The stuti-tīrthas — fords praised "merely by name"

- **The stuti / nāma-kīrtana fords** — a large residual class the parva sweeps up in summary verses: scores of tīrthas (e.g. *Manojava, Vaṭeśvara, Jambūmārga, Plakṣāvataraṇa, Bhṛgutuṅga, Mahālaya, Vasiṣṭha, Bhāradvāja, Mainākam*) named only with a one-line merit-praise (*stuti*) rather than a legend. The parva closes its enumeration with the formula that *even hearing or reciting the list* of fords confers a share of their merit — making the catalogue itself a tīrtha. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 84–85, 156) [scholarly]
- **The phala-śruti coda** — the standard fruit-promise: one who hears Pulastya's tīrtha-recital "is freed from all sins and attains the world of the meritorious"; this *anukramaṇī*-of-fords becomes the template every later **[Purāṇic tīrtha-māhātmya](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)** expands. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 85)

---

## VIII. How the catalogue seeds the later tradition

- **Epic → Purāṇa lineage** — the Tīrtha-yatra Parva is the **seed-text** the Padma (*Sṛṣṭi / Uttara-khaṇḍa* tīrtha-sections), Skanda (the *khaṇḍa*-māhātmyas: *Prabhāsa, Kāśī, Reva, Kedāra*), Matsya and Vāyu Purāṇas expand into the standalone ford-gazetteers. The epic gives the **roster and the merit-formula**; the Purāṇas supply the **full legends**. (*Padma* & *Skanda* tīrtha-māhātmyas) [scholarly]
- **Index function** — for this section, treat the parva as the **master list**: every numbered circuit and named ford in **[Tīrtha Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)** and the **[Sapta Purī](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md)** descends from, or is cross-checked against, this catalogue. The major fords carry their own files; the minor *stuti*-fords (§VII) are housed here.
