# Mathurā–Vṛndāvana & the Braj Maṇḍala (84 Kos)

> **Braj / Vraja Maṇḍala** — the Kṛṣṇa-land: the circle of forest, river, hill and village around **Mathurā** where Viṣṇu descended as **Kṛṣṇa** in the [Dvāpara Yuga](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md) and played out his childhood and youthful *līlās*. Theologically it is **Goloka manifest on earth** — not an ordinary place but the eternal *dhāma* (abode) made visible. The dedicated node for the land of Kṛṣṇa: the birth-prison at Mathurā, the 84-kos parikrama, the 12 *vanas*, the *līlā-sthals* of Radha and Kṛṣṇa, and the 16th-c. rediscovery of Braj.
>

**Naming & extent** — *Braj* (Hindi) / *Vraja* (Sanskrit, "cow-pen, pastoral station," from √*vraj* "to roam," the migratory cowherd settlement) names the whole land; *Vraja-maṇḍala* its sacred circle. The traditional measure is **84 kos** (*chaurāsī kos*, ~252 km circumference) centred on Mathurā, embracing parts of modern **Uttar Pradesh** (Mathura district), **Rajasthan** (Bharatpur — Kāmavana/Kāman, Deeg) and **Haryana**. Held in Vaiṣṇava theology to be co-extensive with **Goloka Vṛndāvana**, Kṛṣṇa's supreme planet, descended to earth. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10; *Garga Saṃhitā*; *Varāha Purāṇa, Mathurā-māhātmya*)

## I. Mathurā — birth-city of Kṛṣṇa

- **Mathurā the city** - one of the **[Sapta Purī](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md)** (seven *mokṣa-dā* cities) and capital of the **Śūrasena** kingdom on the **Yamunā**; ancient *Madhupura/Madhuvana*, said to be founded where Śatrughna (Rāma's brother) slew the asura **Lavaṇa**, son of Madhu — so the land bridges the [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/) and Kṛṣṇa eras. By Kṛṣṇa's time the throne is usurped by **Kaṃsa**. (*Bhāgavata* 10; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*; *Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara-kāṇḍa*)
- **Kaṃsa the tyrant** - son of **Ugrasena** (king of Mathurā), Kaṃsa deposes and imprisons his father and rules as a despot allied to **Jarāsandha** of Magadha (his father-in-law). A heavenly voice (*ākāśavāṇī*) at the wedding of his sister **Devakī** to **Vasudeva** warns him her eighth child will kill him — the trigger for the whole birth-cycle. (*Bhāgavata* 10.1)
- **Kṛṣṇa-Janmasthān / Janmabhūmi** - the **prison cell (*kārāgāra*)** where Kaṃsa confined Devakī and Vasudeva and where **Kṛṣṇa was born at midnight on Bhādrapada Kṛṣṇa-aṣṭamī** (Janmāṣṭamī), his eighth incarnate child after Kaṃsa had dashed seven elder siblings. The chains fell, the guards slept, and Vasudeva carried the infant across the flooding **Yamunā** to **Gokula**. The present **Śrī Kṛṣṇa Janmasthān Temple Complex** (Keśavadeva temple, *Garbha-gṛha* birth-room, Bhāgavata-Bhavan) marks the site, adjacent to the Shahi Idgah — a long-disputed sacred ground. (*Bhāgavata* 10.3–4; mathura.nic.in) [disputed]
- **The seventh child — Balarāma's transfer** - the seventh embryo, **Śeṣa/Balarāma**, was mystically transferred (*saṃkarṣaṇa*) from Devakī's womb to **Rohiṇī** at Gokula by Yoga-māyā, so Kaṃsa believed Devakī had miscarried; hence Balarāma's epithet **Saṃkarṣaṇa**. (*Bhāgavata* 10.2)
- **Viśrāma Ghāṭ** - the chief of Mathurā's ~25 ghāṭs on the Yamunā and the spiritual centre of the city's riverfront; held to be where **Kṛṣṇa rested (*viśrāma*) after slaying Kaṃsa**. The traditional start- and end-point of the Mathurā and Braj parikramas; site of the evening **Yamunā āratī**. (Mathurā-māhātmya; local tradition) [folk]
- **Kaṃsa-vadha & liberation of Mathurā** - the grown Kṛṣṇa (with Balarāma), summoned to Kaṃsa's bow-sacrifice (*Dhanur-yajña*), kills the wrestlers Cāṇūra and Muṣṭika, then drags Kaṃsa from his throne and slays him — restoring **Ugrasena** to the throne. Sites: **Kaṃsa-ṭīlā** (where the body was dragged), the *raṅga-bhūmi* arena. (*Bhāgavata* 10.42–44)

## II. Gokula, Mahāvana & the Yamunā crossing

- **Gokula / Mahāvana** - the cowherd hamlet on the **east bank** of the Yamunā where Vasudeva delivered the infant Kṛṣṇa to **Nanda** and **Yaśodā**, exchanging him for their newborn daughter (**Yoga-māyā**, who slips from Kaṃsa's grasp and announces his doom). Kṛṣṇa's earliest infant *līlās* — **Pūtanā** (poison-breast demoness), **Śakaṭāsura** (cart-demon), **Tṛṇāvarta** (whirlwind), the **butter-thieving** and the **damodara-līlā** (bound to the mortar) — unfold here. **Mahāvana** ("the great forest") is counted among the 12 *vanas* and contains the *Caurāsī Khambā* (84-pillar) shrine. (*Bhāgavata* 10.3–11; *Garga Saṃhitā*)
- **The Yamunā** - the dark-watered daughter of Sūrya and sister of Yama, the sacred river threading all of Braj; scene of the **Kāliya-damana** (subduing the many-hooded serpent in the poisoned pool at Kālī-deha/Kāliya-ghāṭ), the **rāsa-līlā** on its banks, and Vasudeva's miraculous night-crossing. See [Yamunā among the sacred rivers](bharatavarsha-and-puranic-geography.md). (*Bhāgavata* 10.16)
- **Move to Vṛndāvana** - troubled by demons and ill omens at Gokula, Nanda relocates the whole cowherd community by bullock-cart to **Vṛndāvana**, where the boyhood and the great *gopī* *līlās* take place. (*Bhāgavata* 10.11)

## III. Vṛndāvana — the grove of Tulasī

- **Vṛndāvana the forest** - the **forest of Vṛndā** (Tulasī personified; *Vṛndā-vana*), 7th of the western *vanas* and the spiritual heart of Braj; held to be the eternal stage of Kṛṣṇa's youth and the **rāsa-līlā**, and identical with **Goloka** above. The *Garga Saṃhitā*'s *Vṛndāvana Khaṇḍa* glorifies it as a spiritual realm, not ordinary earth. (*Garga Saṃhitā*; *Bhāgavata* 10.29–33)
- **Rāsa-līlā & the circle dance** - the autumn full-moon (Śarad Pūrṇimā) dance of Kṛṣṇa with the *gopīs* on the Yamunā sands, where he multiplies himself to dance with each — the supreme emblem of *bhakti* as divine love (*mādhurya-rasa*). (*Bhāgavata* 10.29–33; *Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa*)
- **Vaṃśī-vaṭa & Sevā-kuñja** - the **flute-banyan** where Kṛṣṇa called the *gopīs* with his flute, and **Sevā-kuñja / Nidhivana** grove where the nightly *rāsa* is held to continue eternally (no one may stay after the evening āratī). (local *sthala* tradition) [folk]
- **Keśī-ghāṭa & Kāliya-ghāṭa** - riverside *līlā* sites: where Kṛṣṇa slew the horse-demon **Keśī** and where he danced on **Kāliya's** hoods. (*Bhāgavata* 10.16–17, 37)
- **Imlītalā, Cīra-ghāṭa, Gopeśvara Mahādeva** - the tamarind where Caitanya sat in *viraha*; the ghāṭ where Kṛṣṇa hid the *gopīs'* clothes (*vastra-haraṇa*); and **Gopeśvara**, the Śiva-liṅga held to guard Vṛndāvana (Śiva as a *gopī* to witness the *rāsa*). (*sthala* tradition; *Garga Saṃhitā*) [folk]

## IV. The Braj Chaurāsī Kos Parikramā (84 kos / ~252 km)

- **The vow & its fruit** - the **Chaurāsī Kos Yātrā** circumambulates the whole of Braj-maṇḍala, a circuit of **84 kos (~252 km)**; completing it once is held to **free the soul from the 84 lakh (8,400,000) yonis** — the full round of 8.4 million species-wombs of [transmigration](../05-concepts-and-dharma/) — and to grant Kṛṣṇa-*bhakti*. The numerical resonance (84 kos ↔ 84 lakh yonis) is deliberate. (radhavallabh.com; Braj-māhātmya tradition)
- **Route & contents** - the *parikramā* encloses **12 principal vanas (forests), 24 upavanas (groves), ~20+ kuṇḍas (sacred ponds)** and dozens of villages, taking in **Mathurā, Vṛndāvana, Gokula, Govardhana, Rādhā-kuṇḍa, Barsānā, Nandagāon, Kāman (Kāmavana), Kosī/Kotvan** and the Yamunā ghāṭs. (radhavallabh.com)
- **Duration** - **~30–40 days on foot** (the traditional barefoot vow) or **7–12 days by vehicle**; classically begun and ended at **Viśrāma Ghāṭ, Mathurā**. (radhavallabh.com)
- **Season** - the great organised parikramā traditionally runs in the **Caitra–Vaiśākha** window (Chaitra Pūrṇimā to Vaiśākha Pūrṇimā, Mar–Apr), with a major one in the monsoon for some lineages. (Braj tradition)
  - *Variant (count of forests):* the *Bhakti-ratnākara* / Gauḍīya geography counts **48 vanas** in all (12 *vanas* + 12 *upavanas* + further *prati-vanas* / *adhi-vanas*), of which 12 are the principal forests; lists vary by source. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (radius):* the "84 kos" is given variously as a circumference (~252 km) or a radius reaching ~84 kos from Mathurā; popular and pilgrim sources differ. [disputed]

## V. The Twelve Vanas (Dvādaśa-vana)

The *Padma Purāṇa* and *Garga Saṃhitā* enumerate the **twelve forests of Braj** — seven on the **west** bank of the Yamunā, five (with Mahāvana) on the **east**. (*Padma Purāṇa*; brajdiscovery / Vraja-māhātmya)

**West of the Yamunā (7):**
- **Madhuvana** - near Mahāvana/Maholi, named for the asura **Madhu**; site of **Dhruva-ṭīlā** (where the boy Dhruva did *tapas*) and Kṛṣṇa's noon-rest. (Vraja-māhātmya)
- **Tālavana** - the **palm-forest**, where **Balarāma slew Dhenukāsura** (ass-demon) and freed the *tāla* (palm) fruits; near Tarsi. (*Bhāgavata* 10.15)
- **Kumudavana** - the **lotus-forest** (Kudarvan/Kumudvan), a grove of white water-lilies with Kṛṣṇa-kuṇḍas. (Vraja-māhātmya)
- **Bahulāvana** - named for the cow **Bahulā**, who offered herself to a lion/tiger to fulfil her duty and was saved by Kṛṣṇa — emblem of *go-bhakti* and truth (*satya*). (Vraja-māhātmya) [folk]
- **Kāmyavana / Kāmavana (Kāman)** - the largest *vana*, in Bharatpur (Rajasthan); "forest of desire (*kāma*)" said to fulfil all wishes, dense with kuṇḍas, hills and *līlā*-sites (Vimala-kuṇḍa, Caraṇa-pahāḍī). (Vraja-māhātmya)
- **Khadiravana** - the **acacia (*khadira*) forest**, near where Kṛṣṇa's friends and the **Govardhana** region adjoin. (Vraja-māhātmya)
- **Vṛndāvana** - the supreme grove (see §III), 7th and chief of the western *vanas*. (*Garga Saṃhitā*)

**East of the Yamunā (5):**
- **Bhadravana** - the "auspicious forest" of cowherd play. (Vraja-māhātmya)
- **Bhāṇḍīravana** - site of the great **banyan (*bhāṇḍīra-vaṭa*)** where, in one tradition, **Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa were wedded by Brahmā**, and where the *gopas* played; Balarāma slew **Pralambāsura** here. (*Bhāgavata* 10.18; *Garga Saṃhitā* — Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa marriage) [late text]
- **Belvana / Bilvavana** - the **bel (Aegle) forest**, associated with **Lakṣmī's** *tapas* to enter the *rāsa*. (Vraja-māhātmya) [folk]
- **Lohavana** - named for the asura **Lohajaṅgha** slain here. (Vraja-māhātmya)
- **Mahāvana** - the "great forest" = old **Gokula**, scene of the infancy *līlās* (see §II). (*Bhāgavata* 10.3–11)
  - *Variant (spellings/order):* lists vary — *Vahulā/Bahulā*, *Kāmya/Kām*, *Khadira/Khidir*; the standard set of twelve is constant though the sequence differs across the *Padma Purāṇa*, *Garga Saṃhitā* and pilgrim guides. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (24 upavanas):* the *Varāha Purāṇa* / Gauḍīya tradition adds **upavanas** — e.g. **Brahma-vana, Apsarā-vana, Vihvala-vana, Kadamba-vana, Svarṇa-vana, Surabhi-vana, Prema-vana, Mayūra-vana, Mānas-iṅgita-vana, Śeṣaśāyī-vana, Nārada-vana, Paramānanda-vana** (and others, to make 24 with *prati-/adhi-vanas*); rosters are open-ended and not fixed. [folk] *(open-ended roster — counts and names vary by text)*

## VI. Govardhana — the lifted hill

- **Giriraj Govardhana** - the sacred hill west of Mathurā, worshipped as **Kṛṣṇa himself** (*Giriraja-jī*, "king of mountains"). When Kṛṣṇa persuaded the cowherds to worship the hill and the cows instead of **Indra**, the affronted Indra loosed catastrophic rains; Kṛṣṇa **lifted Govardhana on his little finger for seven days** as an umbrella, sheltering all Braj — the **Govardhana-dhāraṇa** *līlā*, source of the **Annakūṭa / Govardhana Pūjā** festival. (*Bhāgavata* 10.24–27)
- **Govardhana Parikramā (~21 km / 7 kos)** - the circuit of the hill via **Dāna-ghāṭī, Mānasī-Gaṅgā, Kusum-sarovara, Rādhā-kuṇḍa, Jatipura, Mukhāravinda (Hari-deva)**; held supremely meritorious — devotees forbid even setting foot *on* the hill out of reverence (it being Kṛṣṇa's body). (Braj tradition)
- **Mānasī-Gaṅgā & Kusuma-sarovara** - the lake "born of Kṛṣṇa's mind" (he summoned the Gaṅgā mentally to purify the cowherds) and the flower-garden lake; key Govardhana-circuit tīrthas. (Vraja-māhātmya) [folk]

## VII. The Rādhā līlā-sthals — Barsānā, Nandagāon, the twin kuṇḍas

- **Barsānā — Rādhā's birthplace** - ancient **Vṛṣabhānupura**, home of **King Vṛṣabhānu** and Queen **Kīrtidā**, where **Rādhā** (Rādhārāṇī, *Śrī-jī*) was born/found. The hilltop **Śrī Rādhā-Rāṇī (Lāḍilī-jī) Temple** crowns the **Brahmā-parvata** (the four peaks held to be Brahmā's four heads). Famous for **Lāṭhmār Holī**. (radhekrishn.com; Braj tradition)
- **Nandagāon — Nanda's village** - the hill-village across from Barsānā where **Nanda** and the cowherds settled (Nanda-bhavan); the **Nanda-Bhavan / Nandīśvara** temple crowns the hill. Barsānā (Rādhā) and Nandagāon (Kṛṣṇa) are the paired poles of the Holī play. (Braj tradition)
- **Rādhā-kuṇḍa & Śyāma-kuṇḍa** - the twin sacred ponds near Govardhana, the **holiest *tīrtha* of Braj** for Gauḍīyas — born when Kṛṣṇa (to atone for slaying the bull-demon **Ariṣṭāsura**) and Rādhā each dug a pond; supreme site of *prema-bhakti*, where Raghunātha dāsa Goswami did his *bhajana*. (*Garga Saṃhitā*; Gauḍīya tradition)
- **Sankarṣana-/līlā landmarks** - **Sāñkhī (Sāṅketa)** the meeting-village, **Prema-sarovara**, **Mor-kuṭī** (peacock-dance), **Gahvar-vana** — the Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa tryst-groves around Barsānā. (Vraja-māhātmya) [folk]

## VIII. The rediscovery of Braj (16th c.) & the great temples

By the medieval period the *tīrthas* of the *Bhāgavata* lay lost under forest and the ruin of repeated invasions. Three movements re-mapped and re-sanctified Braj. (Six Goswamis of Vrindavan — Wikipedia; radhavallabh.com)

- **Caitanya Mahāprabhu (1486–1534)** - the Bengali ecstatic and founder of the **[Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md)** path; visiting Braj c. 1515 he is said to have located lost sites (Rādhā-kuṇḍa, Imlītalā) by trance and deputed his disciples — the **Six Goswamis** — to settle in Vṛndāvana, recover the *tīrthas*, and write the theology of *bhakti-rasa*. (Gauḍīya tradition)
- **The Six Goswamis of Vṛndāvana** - **Rūpa** (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*), **Sanātana** (*Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*, *Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta*), **Jīva** (*Ṣaṭ-sandarbha*), **Gopāla Bhaṭṭa**, **Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa**, and **Raghunātha dāsa** — disciples of Caitanya who codified Gauḍīya theology and excavated/founded the temples. **Sanātana** wrote on Braj geography and pilgrimage; the *Vraja-bhakti-vilāsa* (of Nārāyaṇa Bhaṭṭa) and Nābhā/Narahari's works map the *dhāma*. (Six Goswamis — Wikipedia)
- **The Seven Goswami (Ṭhākura) temples of Vṛndāvana** - the seven primary deities established by the Goswamis and their circle:
  - **Rādhā-Madana-mohana** — first temple of Vṛndāvana, by **Sanātana Goswami** (deity originally Advaita Ācārya's). (theharekrishnamovement.org)
  - **Rādhā-Govinda-deva (Govind Dev)** — deity recovered by **Rūpa Goswami** at Gomā-ṭīlā (c. 1525); the great red-sandstone temple built (1590) under **Mān Singh** of Amber with Akbar's patronage. (Six Goswamis — Wikipedia)
  - **Rādhā-Gopīnātha** — recovered at **Vaṃśī-vaṭa**, established by **Madhu Paṇḍita** Goswami. (theharekrishnamovement.org)
  - **Rādhā-Dāmodara** — by **Jīva Goswami** (site of his and later Bhaktisiddhānta's *bhajana*). (theharekrishnamovement.org)
  - **Rādhā-Ramaṇa** — self-manifest from a **śālagrāma-śilā** for **Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Goswami** (1542). (theharekrishnamovement.org)
  - **Rādhā-Śyāmasundara** — by **Śyāmānanda** Prabhu.
  - **Rādhā-Gokulānanda** — by **Lokanātha Goswami**.
  - *(The original deities of Govind Dev, Gopīnātha and Madana-mohana were later removed to **Jaipur/Karauli** during Aurangzeb's reign for protection; *pratibhū* deities continue worship at Vṛndāvana.)* [disputed]
- **Banke Bihārī & the Haridāsī path** - **Svāmī Haridāsa** (16th c., reckoned an incarnation of the *sakhī* Lalitā), the singer-saint of **Nidhivana**, manifested the **Banke Bihārī** deity (the combined Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa form, "thrice-bent" *tribhaṅga*); the present **Banke Bihārī Temple** dates to ~1864. Founder of the **Haridāsī Sampradāya** of *dhrupad* devotional music. (Banke Bihari Temple — Wikipedia; radhekrishn.com)
- **Rādhā-Vallabha & Hita Harivaṃśa** - **Hita Harivaṃśa Mahāprabhu** founded the **Rādhā-Vallabha Sampradāya** (16th c.), centred on Rādhā as supreme; the **Śrī Rādhā-Vallabha Temple** of Vṛndāvana (no Rādhā mūrti — only her crown beside Kṛṣṇa) is its seat. (radhavallabh.com)
- **Vallabhācārya & the Puṣṭimārga** - **Vallabhācārya** (1479–1531), founder of the **[Puṣṭimārga / Śuddhādvaita](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md)**, rediscovered **Śrī Nāthajī** (the Govardhana-dhāraṇa form self-manifest on Govardhana hill) — later moved to **Nathdwara**, Rajasthan; his lineage anchors the **Mukhāravinda (Hari-deva)** and Gokula/Mahāvana *havelis* (Gokulnāthajī, the seat of the **Gokula** branch). See [Govardhana §VI]. (Puṣṭimārga tradition)
