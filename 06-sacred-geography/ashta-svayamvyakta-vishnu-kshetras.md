# Aṣṭa Svayam-vyakta Kṣetras — The Eight Self-Manifest Viṣṇu Shrines

> *The canonical Vaiṣṇava counterpart to the [12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md) (Śiva's self-manifest lights) and the [Aṣṭavināyaka](ashtavinayaka.md) (Gaṇeśa's eight self-born idols): the eight **svayam-vyakta** ("self-manifested") abodes where Viṣṇu's mūrti is held to have **arisen on its own** — not carved by sculptor, nor installed by ritual *prāṇa-pratiṣṭhā*, but disclosed by the Lord himself. Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md) · Related: [108 Divya Deśams](108-divya-desams.md) · [Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md) · [Tirumala–Tirupati](tirumala-tirupati-venkateshwara.md) · [Naimiṣāraṇya](naimisharanya-forest-of-the-puranas.md) · [Śrīraṅgam](srirangam-ranganatha.md) · [Śrīmuṣṇam](srimushnam-bhu-varaha.md)*

A **svayam-vyakta kṣetra** is a shrine whose icon is *self-manifest* — Sanskrit *svayam* ("of itself") + *vyakta* ("become manifest, disclosed"). The Pāñcarātra Āgama tradition classifies Viṣṇu's *arcā* (worship-image) into five descending grades of presence — **svayam-vyakta** (self-disclosed), **daiva** (set up by gods), **siddha** (by perfected sages), **ārṣa** (by ṛṣis), and **mānuṣa** (by humans). The **eight self-disclosed icons** stand at the top of this hierarchy, the most charged abodes on earth, where the deity is bodily present without human mediation. The **Garuḍa Purāṇa** and the **Padma Purāṇa (Pātāla Khaṇḍa)** transmit the closed list of eight; the *Divya Sūri Carita* and Śrī Vaiṣṇava commentarial tradition repeat it. This grouping binds members otherwise scattered across the [Divya Deśam](108-divya-desams.md) and pan-Indian *tīrtha* networks into one doctrinal set — the Vaiṣṇava "eight" that mirrors the Śaiva "twelve." [scholarly]

---

## I. The Grouping Concept — the *svayam-vyakta* class

- **The eight as a closed set** — Vaiṣṇava Āgama and the Purāṇas fix exactly **eight** self-manifest Viṣṇu kṣetras, the supreme grade of the *arcāvatāra* (icon-descent). The number is canon, like the Jyotirliṅgas' twelve and the Aṣṭavināyaka's eight. (Garuḍa Purāṇa; Padma Purāṇa, *Pātāla Khaṇḍa*; Pāñcarātra Āgama). [scholarly]
- **Pāñcarātra five-fold grading** — the doctrine that an image's holiness depends on *who manifested it*: self-disclosed > divine > sage-installed > ṛṣi-installed > human-installed. The eight occupy the apex tier. (Pāñcarātra Āgama; *Sātvata / Pauṣkara Saṃhitā* tradition).
- **The mnemonic verse** — Śrī Vaiṣṇava tradition preserves a *śloka* enumerating the eight (Śrīraṅgam, Śrīmuṣṇam, Veṅkaṭādri, Sālagrāma, Naimiśa, Toṭādri, Puṣkara, Badarī) as the abodes "*svayam-vyaktāni*" — manifest of themselves. (Vaiṣṇava sthala-māhātmya; *Divya Sūri Carita*). [regional]
- **Counterpart to the Śaiva twelve** — explicitly read in tradition as the Vaiṣṇava parallel to the **[12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md)**: where Śiva self-manifests as twelve columns of *jyotis* (light), Viṣṇu self-manifests as eight *arcā*-bodies. See **[Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)** for how such numbered sets interlock.

## II. The Eight Members

- **1. Śrīraṅgam — Raṅganātha** *(Tamil Nadu, Kāverī island)* — Viṣṇu reclining as **Raṅganātha** on Ādiśeṣa; the foremost of the **[108 Divya Deśams](108-divya-desams.md)** and the head temple of the Śrī Vaiṣṇava sampradāya. Called **Bhūloka Vaikuṇṭha** ("Vaikuṇṭha on earth"); the *Śrīraṅga-māhātmya* holds the reclining icon was given by Viṣṇu to Ikṣvāku and is uncarved by mortal hand. Detailed at **[Śrīraṅgam](srirangam-ranganatha.md)**. (Padma Purāṇa; *Śrīraṅga-māhātmya*).
- **2. Śrīmuṣṇam — Bhū-Varāha** *(Cuddalore dist., Tamil Nadu)* — Viṣṇu as the **Boar (Varāha)** lifting the Earth-goddess; the chief southern Varāha-kṣetra, the **Śveta-Varāha** (white-boar) self-manifest mūrti of the white-boar tank. Detailed at **[Śrīmuṣṇam](srimushnam-bhu-varaha.md)** and **[Avatāra Kṣetras](avatara-kshetras-srikurmam-srimushnam.md)**. (Varāha Purāṇa; *Śrīmuṣṇa-māhātmya*).
- **3. Veṅkaṭādri — Veṅkaṭeśvara** *(Tirumala, Andhra Pradesh)* — Viṣṇu as **Śrīnivāsa / Veṅkaṭeśvara (Bālājī)** standing on the **Seven Hills**; the *Veṅkaṭācala Māhātmya* frames the self-manifest hill-mūrti as the **Kali-yuga refuge** where Viṣṇu stays bodily present through the dark age. The busiest temple on earth. Detailed at **[Tirumala–Tirupati](tirumala-tirupati-venkateshwara.md)**. (Varāha / Bhaviṣyottara Purāṇa; *Veṅkaṭācala Māhātmya*).
- **4. Sālagrāma / Muktināth — Śrī Mūrti** *(Mustang, Nepal, on the Kālī-Gandakī at ~3,800 m)* — the Himalayan source of the **śālagrāma-śilā** (ammonite Viṣṇu-stones, self-formed and aniconically worshipped as Viṣṇu without consecration — the self-manifest principle in its purest form). The **only [Divya Deśam](108-divya-desams.md) outside India** (*Tiru Salagramam*, deity **Śrī Mūrti Perumāḷ**) and a node of the [Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) axis. (Garuḍa Purāṇa; *Devī Bhāgavata*; Gaṇḍakī-māhātmya).
- **5. Naimiṣāraṇya — Devarāja Perumāḷ** *(Sītāpur dist., Uttar Pradesh, on the Gomatī)* — the forest of the **Cakra-tīrtha** where Brahmā's / Viṣṇu's wheel struck the earth; the structural *frame-story* of the Purāṇas. A Divya Deśam (*Tiru Naimiśāraṇyam*), where the **Manomaya Cakra of Hari** is said to have fixed the self-manifest abode. Detailed at **[Naimiṣāraṇya](naimisharanya-forest-of-the-puranas.md)**. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.1; Skanda Purāṇa, *Naimiṣa-khaṇḍa*).
- **6. Toṭādri / Vānamāmalai — Deivanāyaga Perumāḷ** *(Nanguneri, Tirunelveli dist., Tamil Nadu)* — Viṣṇu reclining as **Deivanāyaga Perumāḷ** with **Varamaṅgai Nācciyār**; a [Divya Deśam](108-divya-desams.md) (*Tiruvāramaṅgai / Vānamāmalai*) and an Aṣṭāṅga-Vimāna abhāṣaṇa seat of the Vānamāmalai maṭha. The hill **Toṭādri** ("hill of the garden") gives the *svayam-vyakta* name. (Vaiṣṇava sthala-purāṇa; *Divya Sūri Carita*). [regional]
  - *Variant (member name):* lists give this member as **Toṭādri**, **Vānamāmalai**, or **Tiru-vāramaṅgai** interchangeably — all the same Nanguneri kṣetra; never a separate eighth site.
- **7. Puṣkara — — ** *(Ajmer dist., Rajasthan)* — the lake-tīrtha better known for Brahmā's near-unique temple, but counted here as a **Viṣṇu svayam-vyakta** seat (the icon variously **Varāha** or the local Veṇī-Mādhava / Puruṣottama Viṣṇu); the *Puṣkara-māhātmya* makes it the foremost tīrtha "where all gods reside." See **[Puṣkar – Brahmā Tīrtha](pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md)**. (Padma Purāṇa, *Sṛṣṭi / Pātāla Khaṇḍa*; *Puṣkara-māhātmya*).
- **8. Badarī / Naranārāyaṇāśrama — Badarīnārāyaṇa** *(Badrinath, Uttarakhand, on the Alaknandā)* — Viṣṇu as **Badrīnārāyaṇa**, the self-manifest **śālagrāma** mūrti recovered (in legend, by Śaṅkarācārya) from the **Nārada-kuṇḍa** in the Alaknandā; the *tapas*-seat of the twin sage-avatāra **Nara–Nārāyaṇa**. A [Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) and Divya Deśam node. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Skanda Purāṇa, *Badarī-māhātmya*).
  - *Variant (member name):* given as **Badarī**, **Badarikāśrama**, or **Naranārāyaṇāśrama** — all the Badrinath kṣetra.

## III. Catalogued Variants — the disputed members

The eight are stable in their *core*, but the boundary item shifts between recensions; the lists are catalogued, never merged.

- *Variant — the standard eight (Garuḍa / Padma Purāṇa):* **Śrīraṅgam, Śrīmuṣṇam, Veṅkaṭādri, Sālagrāma, Naimiśa, Toṭādri, Puṣkara, Badarī** — the most widely cited Vaiṣṇava list. [scholarly]
- *Variant — Naimiśa vs. Sālagrāma:* some recensions stress **Sālagrāma–Muktināth** and some **Naimiśāraṇya** as the northern self-manifest pole; a few short lists carry one and drop the other, but the canonical roster holds **both**. [disputed]
- *Variant — Puṣkara substituted:* a minority of *māhātmya* lists replace **Puṣkara** (a Brahmā-dominant tīrtha) with another Viṣṇu seat (occasionally **Śālagrāma** counted twice, or a local kṣetra), reflecting unease that Puṣkara is primarily a Brahmā site. The Garuḍa-Padma list keeps Puṣkara. [disputed]
- *Variant — "self-manifest" applied loosely:* in living regional devotion individual temples (e.g. Ahobilam Narasiṃha, Melukote) are *also* called *svayam-vyakta* in their own sthala-purāṇas; this is honorific and does not enlarge the canonical **eight**. [folk]

## IV. Two Clocks — Scriptural vs. Historical

- **Scriptural (mythic-eternal)** — in Purāṇic time the eight icons are **self-disclosed "from the beginning,"** outside human chronology; the *svayam-vyakta* status means precisely that no datable installation event exists. Where the *māhātmyas* place a disclosure, it falls in the deep past of the present **Vaivasvata [manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)** — Badarī to Nara–Nārāyaṇa's *tapas*, Veṅkaṭa to the dawn of **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)**, Naimiṣa to the great *sattra*. [scriptural]
- **Historical (academic)** — the *grouping* as a fixed list of eight is a **Pāñcarātra / Purāṇic doctrinal construct**, crystallised (like most numbered temple-sets) in the medieval Vaiṣṇava period and consolidated by Śrī Vaiṣṇava commentators reading the eight as one network; the member temples have independent (and varied) archaeological and inscriptional dates. The self-manifest claim is a **theological category, not a datable construction event** — the two clocks must not be collapsed. [scholarly]
