# Yādagirigutta — the Five Narasiṃhas Revealed to Yāda-ṛṣi

> The "hill of Yāda" (*Yādagiri*, Telugu *gutta* = "hillock") in the Yādādri-Bhuvanagiri district of Telangana, ~60 km north-east of Hyderabad: the **Pañcha-Narasiṃha kṣetra**, where the sage **Yāda-ṛṣi**, son of **[Ṛṣyaśṛṅga](../04-deep-dives/rishyashringa.md)**, won *darśana* of **[Narasiṃha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** in **five simultaneous forms** in a hill-cave — and where the Lord stayed on as **Vaidya Narasiṃha**, the "physician," who cures chronic and mental affliction, appearing to devotees in dreams.
>

**Yādagirigutta** (now officially **Yādādri**) is one of the foremost living **Narasiṃha kṣetras** of the Telugu country, ranked in devotion with **[Ahobilam](./ahobilam-nava-narasimha.md)**, **[Siṃhācalam](./simhachalam-varaha-narasimha.md)** and **[Maṅgalagiri](./mangalagiri-panakala-narasimha.md)**. Its distinction is that the presiding **Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha** is worshipped here not as a single icon but as **five forms of the Man-Lion** enshrined together in a natural cave — hence the temple's own titles *Pañcha-Narasiṃha Kṣetram* and *Ṛṣi-Ārādhana Kṣetram* ("the seat won by a sage's worship"). The founding legend belongs to the sage **Yāda-ṛṣi**, from whom the hill takes its name. Its *sthala-māhātmya* claims a warrant in the **Skanda-Purāṇa**. As throughout the corpus, the *traditional* (Yuga / Purāṇa-internal) frame is kept distinct from the *academic* (architectural / documented) frame — the **"two clocks."** [scholarly]

## The hill & its name

- **Yādagiri / Yādādri — "the hill of Yāda"** — the hillock (Telugu *gutta*) is named for the sage **Yāda-ṛṣi** whose penance drew Narasiṃha to manifest here; *Yāda-giri* → colloquial *Yādagirigutta*, re-Sanskritized in the modern rebuild as **Yādādri** ("Yāda-adri," Yāda's mountain). (Yādagiri-kṣetra-māhātmya; Devasthanam)
- **Pañcha-Narasiṃha Kṣetram** — the temple's self-title, marking it as the seat of **five** Narasiṃha forms in one cave — distinct from Ahobilam's **nine** shrines scattered across a whole hill, and from Siṃhācalam's single **composite** icon. (Devasthanam; Wikipedia, *Sri Lakshmi Narasimha Swamy Temple, Yadagirigutta*)
- **The cave-shrine (*guhā*)** — the original garbha-gṛha is a low natural **rock cave** on the hillside in which the five forms are held to be **self-manifest (*svayam-vyakta*)** in the living stone, not carved by human hand — the standard mark of a primordial Narasiṃha seat. [traditional] (Yādagiri sthala-māhātmya)
- **Anjaneya at the gate** — a **[Hanumān](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)/Añjanēya** shrine stands at the entrance to the main cave (and a large hill-foot Añjanēya), guarding the *kṣetra*; several recensions of the legend make Añjanēya the one who **directs Yāda-ṛṣi** to this hill and instructs him in the worship of Narasiṃha. [regional] (Devasthanam tradition)

## The sthala-purāṇa — Yāda-ṛṣi and the fivefold darśana

- **Yāda-ṛṣi, son of Ṛṣyaśṛṅga** — the local *māhātmya* makes the founder a son of the sage **[Ṛṣyaśṛṅga](../04-deep-dives/rishyashringa.md)** (the "antelope-horned" ṛṣi who ends the drought of Aṅga and performs Daśaratha's *putra-kāmeṣṭi* in the [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/prahlada-narasimha.md))** and his wife **Śāntā**. This filiation is a **kṣetra-legend attribution**, not a genealogy found in the classical Rāmāyaṇa, which names no such son. [regional] [folk] (Yādagiri-kṣetra-māhātmya)
- **The great penance** — seeking a *darśana* of the Man-Lion, Yāda-ṛṣi performs severe **tapas** on the hill, worshipping **Vishnu / Narasiṃha** in the cave; pleased at last, the Lord resolves to reveal himself. (Yādagiri sthala-māhātmya; claimed *Skanda-Purāṇa* warrant)
- **The five-form epiphany** — Narasiṃha manifests to the sage **not once but in five aspects at once** — **Jvālā, Yogānanda, Gaṇḍabheruṇḍa, Ugra** and **Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha** — filling the cave with the fierce and the gracious modes of the avatāra together. This simultaneous fivefold *darśana* is the founding miracle of the kṣetra. (Yādagiri-kṣetra-māhātmya; Wikipedia)
- **The sage's boon — "stay on the hill"** — his desire fulfilled, Yāda-ṛṣi begs the Lord **not to withdraw** but to remain fixed in the stone so that later devotees may share the vision and be healed; Narasiṃha consents to abide on the hill in his five forms — establishing the perpetual shrine. [regional] (sthala-māhātmya)
  - *Variant (Añjanēya's guidance):* one strand credits **Añjanēya (Hanumān)** with leading the sage to the site and revealing the mantra, so that the Man-Lion's coming is mediated by the great Vaiṣṇava servant — accounting for Hanumān's shrine at the cave door. [folk] [regional]

## The Pañcha Narasiṃha — the five forms in the cave

The five aspects run from the most terrible (*ugra*) to the most gracious (*saumya*), the same fierce-to-benign spectrum that Ahobilam distributes across its nine shrines, here compressed into a single cave. Membership and order are fixed; the arrangement is the temple's signature. (Devasthanam; Wikipedia, *Yadagirigutta*)

- **Jvālā Narasiṃha** — the **flaming (*jvālā*), most ferocious** aspect, held to appear in the cave as a **serpentine / coiled streak** in the rock; the wrath of the Man-Lion still burning from the slaying of Hiraṇyakaśipu. (Yādagiri sthala-māhātmya; Wikipedia)
- **Yogānanda Narasiṃha** — the Lord **seated in yogic meditation (*yoga-mudrā*)**, the pacified teaching-aspect who, his fury cooled, imparts *yoga* — the same "bliss-of-yoga" form venerated at [Ahobilam](./ahobilam-nava-narasimha.md) and [Śōḷiṅgūr](./sholingur-tirukkadigai-yoga-narasimha.md). (sthala-māhātmya)
- **Gaṇḍabheruṇḍa Narasiṃha** — the rare **two-headed-bird (*gaṇḍabheruṇḍa*)** super-form, the aspect Vishnu is said to have taken to master the fire of **Śarabha** in the Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava wrath-cycle — an ultra-fierce mode found at almost no other kṣetra as one of a fixed five. [regional] (sthala-māhātmya; cf. Śarabha–Gaṇḍabheruṇḍa lore)
- **Ugra Narasiṃha** — the raw **fierce (*ugra*)** Man-Lion frozen in the act of the kill, the terrible root-aspect that the sandal, sweetness or dream-grace of the other seats exists to cool. (sthala-māhātmya)
- **Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha** — the **benign, consort-bearing (*saumya*)** form, **Lakṣmī** on his lap, the aspect that presides over the temple's daily worship; the processional **silver icon (*utsava-mūrti*)** of the shrine is the Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha, and it is under this gracious name that the whole temple is popularly known. (Devasthanam; Wikipedia)

## Vaidya Narasiṃha — the healer who cures in dreams

- **The Lord as physician (*vaidya*)** — Yādagirigutta's living fame is as a **healing shrine**: the deity is invoked as **Vaidya Narasiṃha**, "Narasiṃha the doctor," who cures **chronic disease and mental affliction** — including disorders traditionally ascribed to **graha-doṣa** (planetary/spirit affliction) — that ordinary medicine has failed to touch. [regional] [folk] (Devasthanam tradition)
- **Cure by dream (*svapna-darśana*)** — the distinctive rite is that the afflicted **stay at the temple** and the Lord **appears in dream**, prescribing the remedy or effecting the cure directly in sleep — a *svapna*-healing tradition rather than a herb-or-image cure. Countless devotee testimonies of relief anchor the kṣetra's reputation. [folk] [oral tradition]
- **The mandala vigil** — devotees seeking a cure customarily reside and worship for a **maṇḍala** (a 40- to 48-day cycle), performing the sevas until the dream-grace comes — a living penitential-therapeutic practice still followed. [regional] (Devasthanam)
  - *Variant (the pacified Ugra):* as at [Siṃhācalam](./simhachalam-varaha-narasimha.md) (sandal) and [Maṅgalagiri](./mangalagiri-panakala-narasimha.md) (panakam), the healing grace is read as the **ugra fury turned to compassion** — the same wrath that tore Hiraṇyakaśipu, now bent to burn away the devotee's disease. [folk]

## The Prahlāda frame

- **Why Narasiṃha at all** — the five forms are aspects of the one **[Narasiṃha](../04-deep-dives/prahlada-narasimha.md)** avatāra who bursts from the pillar at twilight — neither day nor night, indoors nor out, man nor beast — to void **Hiraṇyakaśipu's** boon and rescue the child-devotee **Prahlāda** (*Bhāgavata* 7; *Vishnu Purāṇa* 1); Yādagiri localizes not the *slaying* (which Ahobilam claims) but the *abiding* of the Lord for his devotees' sake. (Bhāgavata-Purāṇa, skandha 7; Yādagiri sthala-māhātmya)
- **A seat of abiding, not of deed** — where Ahobilam is the hill of the **kill** and Siṃhācalam the site of Prahlāda's **rescue**, Yādagiri is the hill where the Man-Lion, his work done, **stays to heal** — the kṣetra's theological niche in the pan-Andhra Narasiṃha map. [regional]

## The two clocks — legend-time and documented history

- **Traditional frame** — Yāda-ṛṣi is a son of **Ṛṣyaśṛṅga**, a **[Tretā-Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)**-era ṛṣi of the Rāmāyaṇa generation; the sthala-māhātmya thus dates the five-form epiphany to that remote Yuga, and holds the cave a **timeless, self-manifest** seat active ever since. [traditional]
- **Historical frame** — documented continuous worship at the hill-cave belongs to the **Kali-Yuga historical period**; the shrine grew from a modest cave temple into a major pilgrimage centre over recent centuries, its fame as *Vaidya Narasiṃha* spreading widely in the 19th–20th c. [scholarly]
- **The Yādādri reconstruction (2016–2022)** — the Telangana state government (under Chief Minister **K. Chandraśekhar Rāo**) undertook a total **rebuild** of the temple in black **Kṛṣṇa-śilā** granite per **Āgama-śāstra** design — reportedly the largest temple construction of its era (budget cited at ~₹1,800 crore) — with the great **kumbhābhiṣeka / inauguration on 28 March 2022**, re-founding the site as **Yādādri**. The **original cave-garbhagṛha with the five forms was preserved** at the new complex's heart. [scholarly] (Wikipedia; Devasthanam; press coverage)

## Network & cross-links

- **The Andhra–Telangana Narasiṃha axis** — Yādagiri is a principal node of the informal pan-Telugu **Narasiṃha-kṣetra** pilgrimage circuit, strung with **[Ahobilam](./ahobilam-nava-narasimha.md)** (the *Nava Narasiṃha*, the pillar-bursting hill), **[Siṃhācalam](./simhachalam-varaha-narasimha.md)** (Varāha-Narasiṃha), **[Maṅgalagiri](./mangalagiri-panakala-narasimha.md)** (Pānakāla-Narasiṃha) and Vēdādri (Vedagiri) — the great living Man-Lion seats of the Telugu country. [regional]
- **A mahā-kṣetra, not a Divya Desam** — like Siṃhācalam and Maṅgalagiri, Yādagiri is **not** among the **[108 Divya Desams](./108-divya-desams.md)** (the **Ālvārs** did not sing it); it belongs to the broader **[sthala-māhātmya](./sthala-puranas-major-temples.md)** tier of major living Vishnu temples rather than the saint-canonized [temple networks](./temple-networks-and-circuits.md). [scholarly]
- **Five forms vs. nine vs. one** — the corpus's Narasiṃha seats catalog three counting-traditions of the Man-Lion: **five** in one cave here, **[nine](./ahobilam-nava-narasimha.md)** across the Ahobilam hill, and the single **[composite Varāha-Narasiṃha](./simhachalam-varaha-narasimha.md)** of Siṃhācalam — the same enumerative impulse as the [Aṣṭavināyaka](./ashtavinayaka.md) or [Pañcha Kedār](./pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md). [regional]
