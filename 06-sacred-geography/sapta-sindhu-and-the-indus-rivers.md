# The Sapta-Sindhu — the Indus and the Vedic Seven Rivers

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](README.md) → **Sapta-Sindhu & the Indus Rivers**. The **Sapta-Sindhu** ("Land of Seven Rivers") — the north-western river-country that is the geographic homeland of the **Ṛg Veda**, ruled by the mighty masculine flood **Sindhu** (the Indus), the river whose name became *Hindu* and *India*; with the five great Punjab streams under their Vedic hydronyms and the two battle-and-flood hymns set on their banks.

## I. The "two clocks" of the Sapta-Sindhu

- **Scriptural clock** — in the world of the **Ṛg Veda** the Sapta-Sindhu is the lived landscape of the **present Vaivasvata Manvantara**: the rivers are addressed as living deities by the *ṛṣis* of the family books, and the hymns record actual crossings, battles and floods on their banks — myth and place are one. (*Ṛg Veda*, family books 3 & 7; *RV* 10.75) [scholarly]
- **Historical clock** — read as geography, the Sapta-Sindhu is the **Indus–Punjab river-system** of the second millennium BCE; the Vedic hydronyms map (with scholarly confidence) onto the modern Indus and its eastern tributaries, and the **Sarasvatī** of the same hymns onto the now-dry Ghaggar-Hakra palaeo-channel. The identifications below are the standard *scholarly* equations, not later sectarian overlays. [scholarly]
- **Why this node** — unlike the Gaṅgā or the peninsular rivers, the Indus and most of the Punjab streams carry **no developed *avataraṇa* (descent) cult** of their own; their sanctity is **Vedic**, fixed in the praise-hymns rather than in later *māhātmya* goddess-stories — which is why they are consolidated here rather than each given a descent-file. (contrast the goddess-rivers of [Sacred Rivers & Descent Myths](sacred-rivers-and-descent-myths.md))

## II. Sindhu — the masculine flood and the name of a land

- **Sindhu, the eponym of the Seven Rivers** — *Sindhu* ("the river, the flood, the streaming one") is at once the proper name of the **Indus** and the common noun for any great river; the Indus is the eponymous river that gives the **Sapta-Sindhu** its name. (*Ṛg Veda*) [scholarly]
- **The masculine river-god** — unlike the **feminine** *nadī-devatās* of the later tradition, **Sindhu is grammatically and mythically masculine** — a roaring bull-flood, the *vṛṣabha* among rivers — one of the few major Indian rivers personified as a god rather than a goddess. (*RV* 10.75) [scholarly]
- **From Sindhu → Hindu → India** — the Old Persian shift of initial *s-* to *h-* turned **Sindhu** into **Hindu** (the river and the land beyond it); Greek *Indos / India* and English *India* descend from the same word, while *Hindu* later named the people and religion east of the river — so the entire civilizational label is the **Indus's name carried abroad**. [scholarly]

## III. The Nadīstuti Sūkta — the praise of the rivers (RV 10.75)

- **The Hymn to the Rivers** — *Ṛg Veda* **10.75**, the **Nadīstuti Sūkta** ("praise of the rivers," attributed to the *ṛṣi* **Sindhukṣit Praiyamedha**), is the single richest river-catalogue of the Veda — a litany invoking the streams from east to west and exalting the **Sindhu** above all. (*RV* 10.75) [scholarly]
- **Sindhu the mightiest of streams** — the hymn hails the Sindhu as **foremost in might and swiftness**, surpassing all other rivers, **roaring as it rushes** like a bellowing bull, its waters loud above the earth — "as a mother to her young, as a milch-cow to her calf" the Sindhu speeds her floods. (*RV* 10.75.1–4) [scholarly]
- **The river-roll-call** — *RV* 10.75.5–6 names the rivers in order so that the **Sapta-Sindhu** can be read off the Veda itself: **Gaṅgā, Yamunā, Sarasvatī, Śutudrī (Sutlej), Paruṣṇī (Ravi); Asiknī (Chenab), Marudvṛdhā, Vitastā (Jhelum), Ārjīkīyā, Suṣomā** — with the **Gomatī, Krumu (Kurram), Kubhā (Kabul)** and others of the western group following. (*RV* 10.75.5–6) — the eastern **Gomatī** of this list carries the only Ṛgvedic pedigree among the goddess-rivers of [Sacred Rivers & Descent Myths](sacred-rivers-and-descent-myths.md)
- **RV 10.64.9 — the triple-mother triad** — a second key praise-verse, *RV* **10.64.9**, invokes **three great goddess-rivers together — Sarasvatī, Sarayū and Sindhu** — as "great mothers" (*mahīr*) called to the sacrifice, the earliest pairing of the Sindhu with the [Sarayū](sarayu-ayodhya-river.md) and the Sarasvatī as a sanctifying triad. (*RV* 10.64.9) [scholarly]

## IV. The Sapta-Sindhu — which seven rivers?

- **"Land of the Seven Rivers"** — *Sapta-Sindhavaḥ* ("the seven rivers / seven streams") is the Veda's own name for its **homeland region**, the north-western quarter watered by the Indus system; the number **seven** is fixed and sacred, but the **membership varies** between hymns — the tradition never settles a single canonical seven. [scholarly]
  - *Variant — the eastern-anchored seven (RV 10.75):* counted from the Nadīstuti roll-call the seven are often read as **Sarasvatī + the five Punjab rivers (Śutudrī, Vipāśā, Paruṣṇī, Asiknī, Vitastā) + the Sindhu** — the classic "Indus and five eastern tributaries plus the Sarasvatī." (*RV* 10.75) [scholarly]
  - *Variant — Sindhu and her six sisters:* other readings take the **Sindhu (Indus) as the central river and six tributaries** as the seven, dropping or adding the Sarasvatī. [disputed]
  - *Variant — the cosmic seven:* in some hymns the **sapta sindhavaḥ are heavenly streams** (released by Indra when he slays Vṛtra, *RV* 1.32, 2.12), the seven rivers of the sky rather than of the Punjab — so "seven rivers" is at once a real region and a cosmic image. (*RV* 1.32; 2.12) [scholarly]
  - *Variant — the later Avestan echo:* the **Avesta**'s *Hapta-Həndu* ("Seven Rivers"), one of the lands of Ahura Mazda, preserves the same name in Iranian form — independent confirmation of the region's antiquity. [scholarly]

## V. The five Punjab rivers — Vedic hydronyms

- **Śutudrī — the Sutlej** — *Śutudrī* ("swift-flowing"), the easternmost of the five, the great river of the **Vipāśā–Śutudrī confluence** of *RV* 3.33 (below); later Sanskrit **Śatadru** ("running in a hundred channels"). (*RV* 3.33; 10.75) [scholarly]
- **Vipāśā — the Beas** — *Vipāśā* ("the unfettered / unbound," from *vi-pāśa*, "free of the noose"), the river whose very name the *ṛṣis* gloss in the flood-hymn; the modern **Beas** (Greek *Hyphasis*), the eastern limit of Alexander's march. (*RV* 3.33; 4.30) [scholarly]
- **Paruṣṇī — the Ravi** — *Paruṣṇī* ("the reedy / dappled one"), the river of the **Battle of the Ten Kings** (below); later **Irāvatī**, the modern **Ravi**. (*RV* 7.18) [scholarly]
- **Asiknī / Candrabhāgā — the Chenab** — *Asiknī* ("the dark one"), later **Candrabhāgā** ("moon-portion," a fusion of *Candra* + *Bhāga*); the modern **Chenab** (Greek *Akesines*). *Note* — this northern Candrabhāgā is **distinct** from the Deccan **Bhīmā-Candrabhāgā** of Paṇḍharpūr. (*RV* 8.20; 10.75) [scholarly]
- **Vitastā — the Jhelum** — *Vitastā* ("the wide-spread"), the westernmost and northernmost of the five, river of Kashmir; later legend (*Nīlamata Purāṇa*) makes her a form of **Pārvatī** released by **Śiva** to purify the Kashmir valley; the modern **Jhelum** (Greek *Hydaspes*). (*RV* 10.75; *Nīlamata Purāṇa*) [scholarly / regional]

## VI. Episodes on the banks

- **The Dāśarājña — the Battle of the Ten Kings (RV 7.18)** — the great war-hymn of the **Tṛtsu-Bhārata** king **Sudās**, son of Divodāsa, sung by the *purohita* **[Vasiṣṭha](../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md)**: a confederacy of **ten kings / ten tribes** (Pūru, Yadu, Turvaśa, Anu, Druhyu, and allies) is defeated on the banks of the **Paruṣṇī (Ravi)** when, by **Indra**'s grace, the river itself drowns the enemy host as they try to cross. It is the Veda's nearest thing to a historical battle-record, fixing the Bharata supremacy in the river-country. (*RV* 7.18, 7.33, 7.83) [scholarly]
- **Viśvāmitra and the swelling rivers (RV 3.33)** — the famous dialogue-hymn in which the *ṛṣi* **[Viśvāmitra](../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md)**, leading the **Bharatas** with their wagons and cattle, comes to the **swollen confluence of the Vipāśā (Beas) and Śutudrī (Sutlej)** in full flood. He **praises and entreats the two rivers** as sisters rushing like racing mares / like two bright cows licking their calf; the rivers, addressed as goddesses, **lower their waters** to let the Bharatas ford — the archetypal Vedic scene of sage, migration and river made passable by hymn. (*RV* 3.33) [scholarly]
- **Indra and the seven released floods** — in the cosmic register the **sapta sindhavaḥ** are the seven rivers **Indra sets free** when he slays the serpent **Vṛtra** and shatters the pent waters at *RV* 1.32 — the same "seven rivers" thus stand for both the Punjab streams and the liberated cosmic waters. (*RV* 1.32) [scholarly]

## VII. The Sindhu in Purāṇic geography

- **Sindhu among the Bhārata rivers** — in the **Bhārata-varṣa river-rosters** the Sindhu heads the list of great northern rivers rising from the **Himavat** and the boundary-mountains; the **Viṣṇu**, **Vāyu** and **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇas** each catalogue her among the holy streams of the *karma-bhūmi*, though by Purāṇic times the religious centre of gravity has shifted east to the **Gaṅgā**. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.3; *Vāyu* & *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇas*) — see [Bhārata-varṣa & Puranic Geography](bharatavarsha-and-puranic-geography.md) [scholarly]
- **The fading of the western rivers** — as the sacred map moved east, the **Triveṇī of [Prayāga](prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md)** (Gaṅgā–Yamunā–Sarasvatī) inherited the supreme-confluence theology that the **Sapta-Sindhu** once held; the Sindhu and her Punjab sisters survive mainly as **Vedic memory and *snāna*-mantra names** rather than as living pilgrimage rivers. [scholarly]
- **The seven-river *snāna* invocation** — the Sindhu lives on liturgically in the classic bathing-mantra *gaṅge ca yamune caiva godāvari sarasvati / narmade sindho kāveri jale 'smin sannidhiṃ kuru* — "O Gaṅgā, Yamunā, Godāvarī, Sarasvatī, Narmadā, **Sindhu**, Kāverī, be present in this water" — where the Indus stands as the lone north-western member of the all-India seven. (āhnika / *snāna* tradition) [regional]

## Sources

- **Ṛg Veda 10.75 (Nadīstuti Sūkta)** — the praise of the rivers; the Sindhu exalted as mightiest of streams (10.75.1–4) and the river roll-call naming the Sapta-Sindhu and the western rivers (10.75.5–6).
- **Ṛg Veda 10.64.9** — the triad of "great mothers" **Sarasvatī, Sarayū, Sindhu** called to the sacrifice.
- **Ṛg Veda 7.18 (with 7.33, 7.83)** — the **Dāśarājña**, the Battle of the Ten Kings of **Sudās** on the **Paruṣṇī (Ravi)**, sung by **Vasiṣṭha**.
- **Ṛg Veda 3.33** — **Viśvāmitra**'s dialogue-hymn at the swollen **Vipāśā–Śutudrī (Beas–Sutlej)** confluence, the Bharatas' ford.
- **Ṛg Veda 1.32; 2.12** — **Indra**'s slaying of **Vṛtra** and the release of the seven rivers (the cosmic *sapta sindhavaḥ*); **RV 4.30, 8.20** — Vipāśā and Asiknī references.
- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa (book 2, ch. 3), Vāyu Purāṇa & Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa** — the **Bhāratavarṣa river-rosters** listing the **Sindhu** among the holy northern rivers of the *karma-bhūmi*.
- **Nīlamata Purāṇa** — the Kashmiri **Vitastā (Jhelum)** as a form of Pārvatī released by Śiva [regional].
- *Linguistic / scholarly:* the **Sindhu → Hindu → Indos / India** etymology and the standard equations of the Vedic hydronyms with the modern Indus, Sutlej, Beas, Ravi, Chenab and Jhelum; the **Avestan *Hapta-Həndu*** parallel.
