# Lonar Sarovar — the Crater-Lake of Lavanāsura and the Daityasūdana Temple

> The saline crater-lake of **Lonār** (Buldhana district, Maharashtra) — a meteorite-impact basin whose hypersaline, alkaline water the *sthala-māhātmya* explains as the dissolved being of the asura **Lavaṇāsura / Lonāsura**, slain by **Viṣṇu** — and the adjacent **Daityasūdana** ("slayer of the daitya") Viṣṇu temple that commemorates the deed. Both a Purāṇic *tīrtha* with a charter in the *Skanda Purāṇa* tradition and a celebrated natural wonder (one of the few hypervelocity craters in basalt on Earth), ringed by a cluster of medieval shrines.

**Lonār Sarovar** (also *Lonar*, *Lonar Sarovar*) is a near-circular lake ~1.2 km across set in a deep crater ~150 m below the surrounding Deccan plateau, in **Lonār town, Buldhana district, Maharashtra**. Geologically it is a **hypervelocity meteorite-impact crater** gouged into Deccan-trap basalt — one of the very few such craters in basaltic rock on Earth — and its water is **saline and strongly alkaline** (both salt- and soda-bearing), so that the lake hosts an unusual microbial ecology. Tradition supplies its own charter: the name **Lonār / Lavaṇa** ("salt") and the brine itself are read as the residue of an **asura slain by Viṣṇu**. The "two clocks" run side by side here — a *Purāṇic* slaying outside ordinary yuga-reckoning, and a *terrestrial* impact event datable to deep prehistory (tens of thousands of years ago by most estimates) — and the *māhātmya* simply projects the first onto the second.

## The charter: Lavaṇāsura / Lonāsura and the saline water

- **The salt-asura of the place** — the local *sthala-māhātmya*, framed as a section of the **Skanda Purāṇa** tradition, makes the crater the lair of a mighty asura — variously **Lavaṇāsura** ("salt-demon") or **Lonāsura** (Prakrit/Marathi *lon* "salt") — who tyrannised the surrounding country and the sages and gods. (*Skanda Purāṇa* Lonar-māhātmya tradition; Lonar *sthala-purāṇa*) [regional]
- **Viṣṇu the daitya-slayer** — **Viṣṇu** descends (in the *māhātmya* he comes as **Daityasūdana**, "destroyer of the daitya," sometimes identified with his man-lion or with a distinct local form) and **slays the asura**, pressing or hurling him down into the earth so that his body is buried in the basin. (Lonar *sthala-purāṇa*; Daityasūdana temple tradition) [regional]
- **Why the water is salt** — the defining etiological point: the **blood / dissolved being of the slain Lavaṇāsura became the saline (lavaṇa) water** of the lake, so that the brine is the asura's substance and the very name *Lonār* preserves *lavaṇa* "salt." The natural hypersalinity is thus given a narrative cause. (Lonar *sthala-purāṇa*) [folk] [regional]
  - *Variant (the asura's identity):* tellings differ on **who** the demon is — some name him **Lavaṇāsura**, echoing the Rāmāyaṇa-era *Lavaṇa* (the Madhu-vana asura slain by **Śatrughna**), but the Lonar demon is a **distinct local figure** tied to this crater and should not be conflated with Śatrughna's Lavaṇa; others use the vernacular **Lonāsura**. The corpus keeps these apart as parallel names of the local *kṣetra*-asura, not a single canonical demon. [disputed]
  - *Variant (the manner of slaying):* in some local tellings Viṣṇu **traps the asura beneath a great mass / hill** rather than dissolving him, the crater being the impress of the blow; the saline-blood explanation and the buried-demon explanation circulate together. [folk]
- **The lake as buried-asura tīrtha** — the basin therefore reads as a **demon's grave sanctified by Viṣṇu's victory**, on the pattern of other "deity-slew-the-asura-here" charters catalogued under [Sthala-Purāṇas — Major Temple Origin Legends](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md); bathing and *darśana* here partake of the merit of the god's triumph over *adharma*. [regional]

## The Daityasūdana temple

- **Viṣṇu as "slayer of the daitya"** — beside the crater stands the **Daityasūdana Mandir**, dedicated to **Viṣṇu under the epithet *Daityasūdana*** ("destroyer of the daitya/asura"), commemorating exactly the slaying that the lake-charter records; the temple is the cult-centre that anchors the *sthala-purāṇa* to a living shrine. (Daityasūdana temple *sthala-tradition*) [regional]
- **Datable fabric** — the visible temple is medieval in fabric, conventionally credited to the **Yādava (Seuna) period** (c. 12th–13th c., with claims of earlier foundation and later repair); its star-shaped / irregular plan and carved exterior place it in the Deccan temple idiom — so the *historical* clock of the building is centuries-young against the *scriptural* clock of the slaying. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (the icon):* the principal image is described in local tradition as a metal/alloy Viṣṇu figure; details of date and metallurgy are reported variously and are not from a critical text. [disputed]

## The rim-cluster of shrines

- **A ring of temples around the crater** — the lake-rim and the descent into the basin carry a **cluster of shrines** of varied date and sect, so that the *tīrtha* is a small temple-landscape rather than a single building; pilgrims circuit the rim and descend to the water. (Lonar site tradition) [regional]
- **Kamalajā Devī** — a goddess shrine (a form of **Lakṣmī/Devī**, *Kamalajā* "lotus-born") near the crater is a focus of local Devī worship and seasonal fairs, pairing a Śākta pole with the Vaiṣṇava Daityasūdana centre. [regional]
- **Rāma-, Śiva- and Hanumān-shrines on the floor and rim** — temples to **Rāma (Rāma Gayā)**, to **Śiva**, and a celebrated **Hanumān** image (locally noted as unusually heavy/dense, and folklorically linked to the meteoritic iron of the impact) are scattered down the slope and at the water's edge; a perennial freshwater spring on the crater floor (**Dhāra**) waters the basin's inner temples despite the lake's brine. [folk] [regional]
  - *Note — the "magnetic" Hanumān:* the heavy Hanumān icon is popularly tied to impact-deposited metal; this is a **folk** natural-wonder gloss layered onto the cult, not a scriptural claim. [folk]

## Pilgrimage and natural-wonder status

- **A double pilgrimage** — Lonār functions simultaneously as a **Purāṇic *tīrtha*** (the Viṣṇu-slew-Lavaṇāsura *kṣetra*, with *darśana* at Daityasūdana and bathing/ritual at the rim shrines) and as a **scientific / natural-wonder pilgrimage** — drawing geologists, ornithologists and tourists to a rare basalt impact crater with a hypersaline, microbially distinctive lake and rich birdlife. The same basin carries both meanings without contradiction. [scholarly]
- **Saline lake among the sacred waters** — unlike the freshwater *pañca-sarovara* (see [Pañca-sarovara — The Five Sacred Lakes](panch-sarovar-sacred-lakes.md)), Lonār's sanctity is built **on** its anomalous brine rather than despite it — the salt *is* the charter — making it a distinctive entry in the corpus of sacred lakes and an outlier in any [temple/tīrtha circuit](temple-networks-and-circuits.md) of the Deccan. [regional]
- **Protected site** — the crater and its environs are a notified **wildlife / geo-heritage** zone and a Ramsar-listed wetland in modern administration, so the *terrestrial* clock now includes a conservation overlay atop the medieval shrines and the Purāṇic charter. [scholarly]

## The two clocks — scripture vs. terrain

- **Scriptural clock** — the slaying of Lavaṇāsura/Lonāsura by Viṣṇu-Daityasūdana is a **charter event outside ordinary yuga-reckoning**: the *māhātmya* fixes the place by the deed, not the deed by a dated yuga, so it sits in the same "demon-slain-here, hence holy" register as the other *sthala-purāṇa* charters — most naturally read in the **Kali-scriptural** layer where such site-legends crystallise, while pointing back to the timeless act of the god. [scholarly]
- **Historical clock** — the **impact crater** is a prehistoric geological event (deep-time, long pre-human in most datings); the **Daityasūdana temple and rim shrines** are **medieval (c. 12th–13th c. Yādava and later)**; the **conservation status** is modern. The *māhātmya* projects the single Purāṇic act onto a basin whose physical and built histories belong to entirely separate, far-younger clocks. [scholarly]

## Sources

- **Skanda Purāṇa** (Lonar / Lavaṇāsura–Lonāsura *māhātmya* tradition) — the charter tying the crater-lake to **Viṣṇu's slaying of the asura** and the saline water to the demon's dissolved being. Reliability: the specific Lonar verses are **regional *sthala-māhātmya*** material in the Skanda corpus rather than from a single critical edition; flagged [regional].
- **Daityasūdana temple *sthala-purāṇa*** (local tradition) — Viṣṇu as **Daityasūdana** ("slayer of the daitya"), the temple as cult-centre of the slaying, and the rim-cluster of shrines (Kamalajā Devī, Rāma, Śiva, the heavy Hanumān, the Dhāra spring).
- Geological / natural-history reporting — Lonār as a **hypervelocity meteorite-impact crater in Deccan basalt** with a **hypersaline, alkaline lake** and distinctive microbiology; the impact's deep-time dating and the lake's Ramsar/wildlife status. (General reference; e.g. Wikipedia, *Lonar Lake*) [scholarly]
- Architectural attribution — the **Daityasūdana Mandir** as **Yādava-era (c. 12th–13th c.)** Deccan-idiom fabric; details of icon and date reported variously [disputed].

*One basin, two readings: the **salt is the asura's blood** in the Purāṇic clock, and the **crater is a meteorite's scar** in the terrestrial clock — Lonār is the corpus's clearest case of a natural wonder annexed by a Viṣṇu-slays-the-daitya charter. The asura's name (Lavaṇāsura / Lonāsura) and the manner of slaying are the points of variance and are flagged [disputed]/[folk].*
