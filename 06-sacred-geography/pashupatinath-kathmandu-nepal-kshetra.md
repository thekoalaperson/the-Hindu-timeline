# Pashupatinath — the Shiva Kshetra of Nepal

> *The presiding **national deity of Nepal** (Paśupatinātha, "Lord of Beasts/Creatures") and a great pan-Himalayan Śaiva *kṣetra* on the **Bāgmatī** in Kathmandu: a **chaturmukha** (four-faced) silver-cased liṅga, the **head**-shrine counterpart of the bull-form Śiva whose hump is **[Kedarnath](kedarnath-kedara-kshetra.md)**, served by South-Indian **Bhaṭṭa** priests, ringed by the most sacred cremation ghats of the Himalayan world.*
>

This file is the **standalone shrine treatment** of Paśupatinātha as a *kṣetra* — the bounded Śiva-field of the Kathmandu valley on the banks of the **Bāgmatī**. The governing distinction is **two clocks**: the *scripture clock* of the *sthala-purāṇa* (the deer-form flight, the body-distribution, Śiva's vow to dwell here as Paśupati) seats the founding in undated *cross-yuga* myth-time; the *academic clock* dates the surviving pagoda-roofed temple to medieval/early-modern Nepal (rebuilt 1692 CE after earlier foundations). [scholarly]

---

## The Kṣetra and its Name

- **Paśupati — Lord of Beasts** — the deity is **Paśu-pati**, "lord of *paśus*" (beasts, and esoterically all bound *jīvas*/creatures); the *kṣetra* is **Paśupati-kṣetra** / **Śleṣmāntaka-vana**, the forest on the east bank of the **Bāgmatī** where Śiva is held to dwell as the patron of all living beings. (Nepāla-māhātmya; Paśupati Purāṇa)
- **National deity of Nepal** — Paśupatinātha is venerated as the **guardian/presiding deity of Nepal**, historically the *iṣṭa-devatā* of the Nepali monarchy, the country's foremost Śaiva shrine and a UNESCO World Heritage temple complex. [recorded history]
- **The source-texts** — the *kṣetra*'s glorification is concentrated in the **Nepāla-māhātmya** and the **Himavat-khaṇḍa**, Himalayan sections attached to the **Skanda Purāṇa**, supplemented by the dedicated **Paśupati Purāṇa** — the local *sthala-purāṇa* corpus mapping the valley's tīrthas around Paśupati. (Nepāla-māhātmya; Himavat-khaṇḍa, Skanda Purāṇa; Paśupati Purāṇa) [regional]

## The Deer/Antelope-Form Flight Legend

The dominant Nepali *origin-kathā* — a *cross-yuga* myth, not a dated event.

- **Śiva flees Kailāsa as a deer** — wearied of his duties (or seeking the pleasant **Śleṣmāntaka forest** on the Bāgmatī), **Śiva** abandons Kailāsa and roams the valley in the form of a **deer/antelope (*mṛga*)**, refusing to return. The **devas** (Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Indra) come to fetch him back. (Nepāla-māhātmya; Paśupati Purāṇa) [regional]
- **The seized antler breaks into fragments** — the gods seize the deer-Śiva; in the struggle his **antler/horn breaks**, and the **broken horn-fragment** falls and is enshrined — the **liṅga of Paśupatinātha** is read as this fragment, the place where the deer-Śiva was caught and consented to be worshipped as **Paśupati**. (Nepāla-māhātmya; Paśupati Purāṇa) [regional]
  - *Variant (the buried horn, four faces):* one telling has the horn buried and lost, later **rediscovered** when a cow (Kāmadhenu's descendant) spontaneously sprinkled milk over the spot; digging revealed the radiant liṅga, which manifested **four faces** — the *cow-and-milk* discovery-motif also attached to many Indian liṅgas. (local *māhātmya*) [folk]
  - *Variant (Pāṇḍava/cross-frame):* Nepali tradition sometimes fuses the deer-flight with the wider Himalayan bull-form cycle, treating the antelope-Śiva and the buffalo/bull-Śiva of Kedāra as the *same* fleeing god surfacing on both sides of the range. [folk — do not over-systematize]

## Head of the Bull/Buffalo-Form Śiva — the Body-Distribution Tradition

- **Head in Nepal, hump in Garhwal** — the dominant trans-Himalayan tradition makes **Paśupatinātha the HEAD/face** of the bull- (or buffalo-) form **Śiva** whose **hump** surfaced at **[Kedarnath](kedarnath-kedara-kshetra.md)** — the two shrines are read as the **two ends of a single dismembered bull** stretched across the modern India–Nepal border, Paśupatinātha the **direct northern counterpart** of Kedarnath. (Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa; Nepāla-māhātmya; folk tradition) [folk/regional]
- **The dive at Guptakāśī, re-emergence in two countries** — in the Kedāra *kathā* the **bull-form Śiva**, fleeing the **Pāṇḍavas'** pursuit for absolution of *gotra-hatyā*, dived into the earth at **Guptakāśī**; he re-emerged piecemeal, the **hump at Kedarnath** and (in this tradition) the **head/face at Paśupatinātha** — knitting the Pāṇḍava expiation legend across the whole range. (Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa; cross-link to [Kedarnath](kedarnath-kedara-kshetra.md)) [folk]
  - *Variant (do not conflate with Pañcha Kedār):* the **localized Garhwal scheme** keeps the *entire* bull *within* Uttarakhand — hump → Kedarnath, arms → Tuṅganātha, **face → Rudranātha**, navel → Madhyamaheśvara, hair → Kalpeśvara — and does **not** route the head to Nepal. The Paśupatinātha-head claim is a *separate* trans-Himalayan tradition and must not be merged with the five-shrine [Pañcha Kedār](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md) body-map. (Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa) [variant — do not conflate]
  - *Variant (buffalo vs bull):* some Nepali tellings specify the animal as a **buffalo (*mahiṣa*)** rather than the Garhwal **bull (*vṛṣabha*)**; the distinction shifts the iconographic register but preserves the head-here / hump-there geography. [regional]

## The Chaturmukha (Four-Faced) Liṅga

- **The four-faced liṅga** — the object of worship is a **chaturmukha-liṅga**, a stone liṅga with **four carved faces (*mukha*) facing the four directions**, plus an implicit fifth (*Sadyojāta–Vāmadeva–Aghora–Tatpuruṣa–Īśāna*) upward face — the classic Pāñcānana / Pañca-brahma Śiva, the totality of his five aspects. The liṅga is cased in a **silver/gold sheath** and tended within the gilt pagoda sanctum. (temple tradition; Paśupati Purāṇa)
- **Pañca-mukha symbolism** — the four visible faces are read as Śiva's directional powers — commonly **Tatpuruṣa (E)**, **Aghora (S)**, **Sadyojāta (W)**, **Vāmadeva (N)** — with **Īśāna** as the unseen apex, encoding the whole Śaiva *vidyā* in one image. [scholarly]

## The Bāgmatī Cremation Ghats

- **Āryā-ghāṭa and the Bāgmatī** — the **Bāgmatī**, the holy river of the valley (a Gaṅgā-of-Nepal), flows past the temple; its banks are the most sacred **cremation ghats** of the Himalayan Hindu world, where **Āryā-ghāṭ** (reserved historically for royalty/notables) and the public ghats burn the dead before the bones are committed to the river. To die and be cremated at Paśupati on the Bāgmatī is held to grant release, paralleling the **Maṇikarṇikā** logic of [Kāśī](kashi-varanasi.md). (temple tradition; Nepāla-māhātmya) [regional]
- **Bāgmatī → Gaṅgā** — the Bāgmatī is treated as carrying the merit of the Gaṅgā; the *kṣetra* thus fuses **liṅga-darśana, river-tīrtha, and antyeṣṭi (last rites)** at one node, as Muktinath fuses its own categories upstream (see [Muktinath](muktinath-gandaki-shalagrama-kshetra.md)). [regional]

## The Bhaṭṭa Priest Tradition

- **South-Indian Bhaṭṭas** — the chief priests (***mūl-bhaṭṭa*** / **Bhaṭṭa**) of Paśupatinātha are, by long-standing rule, **Brahmins from South India** — specifically **Karnataka / the Kannada-Telugu Smārta lineage** of the Daakshinatya tradition — said to have been instituted by **Ādi Śaṅkara**, who is held to have reformed the shrine and ordained that only Dravida (southern) Brahmins could perform the *mūla* worship. (temple tradition; regional history) [regional]
  - *Variant (Śaṅkara institution):* tradition credits **Ādi Śaṅkara** with restoring orthodox worship at Paśupati and decreeing the **Bhaṭṭa** appointment — paralleling his attributed reforms at Badrinath (the **Rāwal** Namboothiri priesthood) and Kedarnath; the historicity is *sthala-tradition* rather than firm record. [disputed]
- **Bhaṇḍārī and assistants** — alongside the Bhaṭṭas, local **Bhaṇḍārī** caretakers and Nepali assistant-priests administer the complex; only the Bhaṭṭas touch the liṅga for the principal abhiṣeka. [recorded history]

## Place on the Himalayan Śaiva Map

- **A pan-Himalayan pole** — Paśupatinātha anchors the **eastern/Nepali** corner of the high Śaiva triangle with **[Kedarnath](kedarnath-kedara-kshetra.md)** (Garhwal) and **[Amarnath](amarnath-ice-lingam-and-amar-katha.md)** (Kashmir) — three great northern Śiva-kṣetras, one of which (Kedarnath) is also a **[Jyotirliṅga](12-jyotirlingas.md)**. Paśupati is *not* counted among the canonical twelve Jyotirliṅgas but is often ranked beside them in popular devotion. [regional]
- **Two clocks** — the *scripture clock* seats all of the above — deer-flight, head-distribution, Śaṅkara's ordinance — in *cross-yuga* myth and *sthala*-memory; the *academic clock* records a temple complex with foundations possibly Licchavi-era, the standing structure rebuilt **1692 CE**, the Bhaṭṭa custom historically attested in the early-modern period. [scholarly]

## Sources

- **Nepāla-māhātmya** (associated with the Skanda Purāṇa) — the valley's *sthala-purāṇa*: Paśupati-kṣetra and the Śleṣmāntaka forest, the deer/antelope-form flight of Śiva, the seizing and the antler/horn-fragment liṅga, the Bāgmatī tīrtha. [regional]
- **Himavat-khaṇḍa** (Skanda Purāṇa, Himalayan section) — the broader Himalayan glorification framing Nepal's Śaiva geography around Paśupati. [regional]
- **Paśupati Purāṇa** — the dedicated local *purāṇa* of the shrine: the deer-form *kathā*, the four-faced liṅga, the *kṣetra*'s tīrthas. [regional/late text]
- **Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa** — the bull-form (*vṛṣabha-rūpa*) re-emergence and body-distribution that the trans-Himalayan tradition extends to make Paśupatinātha the **head** to Kedarnath's **hump**; see [Kedarnath](kedarnath-kedara-kshetra.md). [classical]
- **Temple tradition** — the chaturmukha silver-cased liṅga; the South-Indian **Bhaṭṭa** priesthood attributed to Ādi Śaṅkara; the Āryā-ghāṭ cremation ghats; the 1692 CE rebuild. [recorded history / disputed where noted]
