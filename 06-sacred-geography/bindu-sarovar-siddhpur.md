# Bindu-sarovar (Siddhpur) — The Lake of Mātṛ-Śrāddha

> **Bindu-sarovara** (*Bindu-saras*, "the lake of the drop") — the celebrated *tīrtha*-lake on the **Sarasvatī** at **Siddhpur** (Siddhapura, Pātan district, Gujarat / Saurāṣṭra), held in the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* to be the hermitage where the Prajāpati **Kardama** married **Devahūti** and where **Kapila**, the Sāṃkhya-teacher and *avatāra* of Viṣṇu, was born and expounded his mother's instruction (Kapilopadeśa). It is the foremost site of **mātṛ-śrāddha** — funerary rites for the *mother* and the maternal line — and is therefore styled **Mātṛ-gayā** ("the Gayā of the mother"), the explicit counterpart to [Gayā](gaya-pitru-tirtha.md), the *pitṛ*-tīrtha of the father. A second, Himālayan **Lake Bindusaras** of the *Mahābhārata* supplies the building-treasure for **Maya's** illusory hall — this node gathers both.

**Why Bindu-sarovara matters** — Hindu ancestral ritual is gendered into two complementary poles: the *pitṛ*-rite (father's line), discharged supremely at [Gayā](gaya-pitru-tirtha.md), and the *mātṛ*-rite (mother's line), discharged at **Siddhpur's Bindu-sarovara** — "*Pitṛ-gayā* at Gayā, *Mātṛ-gayā* at Siddhpur." The lake's prestige rests on its *Bhāgavata* charter as the cradle of **Kapila**, the very sage who teaches *self-knowledge to his mother*; a son's release of his mother thus mirrors the avatāra's primal mother-teaching at the same ground. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.21–33; Siddhpur *sthala-purāṇa*)

## I. The Bhāgavata charter — Kardama, Devahūti & the birth of Kapila

- **The location of Kardama's tapas** - the Prajāpati **Kardama**, son of Brahmā, performs ten thousand years of austerity on the **bank of the river Sarasvatī**, at the lake **Bindu-sarovara**, so named because **Viṣṇu's tears (*bindu*, "drops") of compassion fell there** when, pleased, he appeared to Kardama. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.21.1–38)
  - *Variant (name etiology — Bhāgavata):* the lake is called *Bindu-sarovara* because the **drops from Viṣṇu's eyes** fell into it; the same drops are the seed of its sanctifying power. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.21.38) [scholarly]
- **Viṣṇu's boon and the prophecy** - Viṣṇu grants that **Svāyambhuva Manu's** daughter **Devahūti** will come to wed Kardama, bear him nine daughters, and that **Viṣṇu himself will be born as Kardama's son** to teach Devahūti and dissolve the bonds of *karma*. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.21.27–32)
- **The marriage at Bindu-sarovara** - Manu and Śatarūpā bring Devahūti to the hermitage; Kardama weds her, and after years of ascetic married life he conjures by *yoga-māyā* a **flying palace (*vimāna*)** in which the couple traverse the worlds and Devahūti's youth is restored. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.22–23)
- **The nine daughters and the son** - Devahūti bears **nine daughters** — given as wives to the great *ṛṣis* (Kalā to Marīci, **Anasūyā to Atri**, etc.; see [Mārkaṇḍeya deep-dive](../04-deep-dives/markandeya.md)) — and finally a son, **Kapila**, who is **Viṣṇu incarnate**; Kardama then takes *sannyāsa* and departs, leaving the divine son to instruct the mother. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.24)
- **The Kapilopadeśa / Devahūti-putra Gītā** - at Bindu-sarovara, **Kapila expounds to Devahūti** the **Sāṃkhya** *tattva*-system fused with **bhakti-yoga** and the path of *mokṣa* — the "Gītā of Devahūti's son," a primary scriptural source for the Sāṃkhya school distinct from the atheistic *Sāṃkhya-kārikā* line. Devahūti attains liberation. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.25–33)
  - *Note — two Kapilas:* the *Bhāgavata's* theistic, Viṣṇu-avatāra Kapila of Bindu-sarovara is to be distinguished from the **Kapila who burns Sagara's sons** at the ocean-floor *āśrama* (a separate myth) and from the *darśana*-founder of the classical *Sāṃkhya-kārikā*; tradition often conflates all three. [disputed]

## II. Siddhpur — Mātṛ-gayā, the lake of the mother-rite

- **Siddhpur / Siddhapura** - the town on the Sarasvatī in north Gujarat that **hosts the Bindu-sarovara**; its name is glossed as the "place of the perfected (*siddha*)," linked in local tradition to **Siddharāja Jayasiṃha** of the Caulukya/Solanki dynasty (12th c.), who is credited with developing the *tīrtha* and the **Rudra-mahālaya** temple. (Siddhpur *sthala-purāṇa*; Wikipedia, *Siddhpur*) [regional]
- **Mātṛ-gayā — the mother's Gayā** - Siddhpur's Bindu-sarovara is the pre-eminent site for **mātṛ-śrāddha**: the *piṇḍa-dāna* and *tarpaṇa* owed specifically to the **deceased mother and the maternal ancestral line**, paired with [Gayā](gaya-pitru-tirtha.md) for the father — encapsulated in the dictum "**the father's rite at Gayā, the mother's rite at Siddhpur**." (Siddhpur *sthala-tradition*; Skanda Purāṇa) [regional]
- **Why the mother-rite localizes here** - the association is read back from the *Bhāgavata* episode: the ground where the **avatāra-son liberated his own mother (Devahūti)** is held the natural place for any son to liberate his mother's soul; the *mātṛ-tarpaṇa* re-enacts Kapila's gift to Devahūti. (Siddhpur *sthala-purāṇa*) [folk]
- **The ritual occasion** - mother-*śrāddha* at Bindu-sarovara is performed especially in **Kārtika** and during the **Pitṛ Pakṣa** fortnight, after bathing in the lake/Sarasvatī; pilgrims often pair a Gayā *pitṛ-śrāddha* with a Siddhpur *mātṛ-śrāddha* to discharge both lines. (Siddhpur *sthala-tradition*) [regional]
- **The "Sarasvatī" of the rite** - the lake draws on the prestige of the **Sarasvatī**, the vanished/holy river of the region whose bank-*tīrthas* (including Bindu-sarovara at Siddhpur) are counted in regional *māhātmya*. (see [Sacred rivers & descent-myths](sacred-rivers-and-descent-myths.md#sābarmatī)) [regional]
- **Note — the modern site** - the kuṇḍa at Siddhpur survives; the medieval **Rudra-mahālaya** complex was largely ruined (attributed to **Alauddin Khalji / Ahmad Shah I**, 13th–15th c.), so much of the visible monumentality is fragmentary — a *scriptural-clock* tīrtha whose *historical-clock* fabric is later and partly lost. [scholarly]

## III. The Saurāṣṭra setting & the tīrtha network

- **A Saurāṣṭra/Gujarat tīrtha** - Bindu-sarovara belongs to the western, Gujarat cluster of holy sites alongside **[Prabhāsa-Somnāth](prabhasa-somnath-tirtha.md)**, **[Dvārakā & the Saurāṣṭra Kṛṣṇa kṣetras](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md)**, and **Girnar/Raivataka** — though Siddhpur itself lies in north Gujarat on the Sarasvatī rather than the coast. [scholarly]
- **Functional place in the network** - within the *tīrtha* system Siddhpur fills the specific niche of the **maternal death-rite pole**, completing the pair with **Gayā** (paternal) — just as **Kāśī** (death-liberation) and **Prayāga** (confluence-bathing) hold their niches. [scholarly]
- **Not a Sapta-Purī** - Bindu-sarovara/Siddhpur is **not** counted among the seven **mokṣa-purīs** (see [Sapta Purī](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md)); its sanctity is a *śrāddha*-tīrtha sanctity (rite-of-the-dead), not a die-here-for-mokṣa city. [scholarly]

## IV. The Mahābhārata's Lake Bindusaras — Maya's treasure-source

> A **distinct, homonymous lake** of the same name in the **Himālaya**, important to the *Mahābhārata* rather than to the Siddhpur cult. Gathered here per the two-Bindusaras pairing; **do not conflate** with the Sarasvatī-bank Siddhpur lake.

- **The Himālayan Lake Bindusaras** - in the *Sabhā Parva*, after **Kṛṣṇa** and **Arjuna** spare the *asura* architect **Maya Dānava** from the Khāṇḍava fire, Maya undertakes to build the Pāṇḍavas a peerless **sabhā** (assembly hall) at **Indraprastha** in gratitude. (*Mahābhārata*, Sabhā Parva 1–3)
- **The materials fetched from Bindusaras** - Maya travels north to **Lake Bindusaras** in the Himālaya — a treasure-store once of the *dānava*-king **Vṛṣaparvan** — and brings back **gem-quality crystal/stone building material**, together with the great mace (later **Bhīma's**) and the conch **Devadatta** (later **Arjuna's**), to construct the hall of optical illusion. (*Mahābhārata*, Sabhā Parva 3; see [Sabhā Parva deep-dive](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md))
- **The illusory hall** - from these materials Maya raises the **Mayasabhā**, the dazzling, water-mimicking-floor hall whose illusions later humiliate **Duryodhana** and help precipitate the war. (*Mahābhārata*, Sabhā Parva 1–3)
  - *Variant (which Bindusaras):* the epic's Bindusaras is explicitly a **Himālayan** lake associated with the *dānavas* and Maya; medieval/regional traditions sometimes blur it toward the famous Siddhpur Bindu-sarovara, but the texts keep them separate — **two lakes, one name**. [disputed]

## V. Two clocks — scripture vs. history

- **Scriptural clock** - the *Bhāgavata* places Kardama–Devahūti–Kapila in the **Svāyambhuva Manvantara** (the first *manvantara*, near the dawn of the present *kalpa*) — i.e. cosmically primordial, long before the *Mahābhārata's* Dvāpara-Kali hinge. The Siddhpur *mātṛ-gayā* cult treats the lake as sanctified "from that beginning." [scholarly]
- **Historical clock** - the **textual** Bindu-sarovara material (Bhāgavata *Skandha* 3) belongs to the mature Purāṇic strata (roughly mid-1st-millennium CE and later), and the **Siddhpur tīrtha-town's** datable fabric (Rudra-mahālaya, the developed kuṇḍa) is **Caulukya/Solanki–era (11th–12th c. CE)** and after — the *māhātmya* projecting the cosmic charter onto the medieval site. [scholarly]
