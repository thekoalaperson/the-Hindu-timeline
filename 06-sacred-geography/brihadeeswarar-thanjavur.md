# Bṛhadīśvara Temple, Thanjavur — Rājarājeśvaram

> Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md) · Related: [275 Paadal Petra Sthalams](275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md) · [Sthala-Purāṇas of Major Temples](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md) · [Pañca Bhūta Sthalams](pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md) · [Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)
>
> The Chola imperial **"Big Temple"** (Tamil **Peruvuḍaiyār Kōvil**, "temple of the great Lord"; founder's name **Rājarājeśvaram**) at **Thanjavur**, Tamil Nadu — built by **Rājarāja Chola I** and consecrated **c. 1010 CE**: a **UNESCO "Great Living Chola Temples"** World Heritage Site and the apogee of **Drāviḍa (Dravidian)** temple architecture.

Bṛhadīśvara is the one temple in this section where the **"two clocks" collapse into one**: unlike the *māhātmya*-wrapped shrines whose origins reach back to creation, this monument is **dated to the year, the donor, and the architect** by its own inscriptions. Its sacred meaning is **imperial and devotional rather than mythological** — it is the Chola state's monumental statement of **Śaiva-Siddhānta** royal religion, set inside the **Tēvāram / Periya Purāṇam** Tamil Shaiva milieu. The presiding liṅga is **Bṛhadīśvara / Pẽruvuḍaiyār** (later sanskritised **Bṛhannāyakī** for the goddess); the consort is **Bṛhannāyakī (Periya Nāyaki)**. [scholarly]

## I. The Monument — Vimāna, Capstone, Nandi

- **The ~66 m vimāna (Dakṣiṇa Meru)** — the *śrī-vimāna* (the tower **over the sanctum**, not a gateway) rises ~**216 ft / 66 m** in **13–14 diminishing tiers**, the **tallest temple tower of its age** and one of the tallest in India; it is praised as the **"Dakṣiṇa Meru"** (southern Mount Meru). Note the **Drāviḍa signature**: the principal tower stands over the *garbhagṛha*, with the *gopuras* kept lower — the inverse of the later Madurai/Pāṇḍya gateway-dominated style. (Chola inscriptions; [scholarly])
- **The monolithic capstone (kumbha / śikhara)** — the cupola-like crowning stone is a single block, traditionally said to weigh **~80 tonnes** (estimates vary ~25–80 t); folk memory holds it was hauled up a **6 km earthen ramp** from the village **Sārapallam** ("scaffold-hollow") — a much-repeated [folk] etiology, plausible in principle but not inscriptionally attested. [folk][disputed]
- **The "non-existent shadow"** — a popular claim that the vimāna's shadow never falls on the ground at noon; **architecturally embellished folklore**, not a verified fact. [folk][disputed]
- **The giant monolithic Nandi** — Śiva's bull, carved from a **single block of granite** (~**16 ft long, ~13 ft high, ~25 t**), couchant before the sanctum in its own pavilion; one of the **largest monolithic Nandis** in India. The present pillared **Nandi maṇḍapa** is a **later (Nāyaka-period) addition**, the bull itself part of the great-temple programme. (Inscriptions; [scholarly])
- **The granite-built fabric** — almost the entire structure is **dressed granite** in a region where granite is not local, implying enormous quarrying-and-transport logistics — itself testimony to Chola state organisation. [scholarly]
- **The circumambulatory and frescoes** — the inner *prākāra* walls carry **Chola-era frescoes** (later overlaid by Nāyaka paintings) and a celebrated programme of **Bharatanāṭyam karaṇa** reliefs (the 81 of 108 *karaṇas* of the *Nāṭya Śāstra*) — encoding dance, the *āgamic* worship-arts, into the stone. [scholarly]

## II. Foundation — The Inscriptional Record (the "academic clock")

This is the temple's defining feature: a **dated, donor-named, audited** foundation.

- **Rājarāja I, c. 1003–1010 CE** — built by **Rājarāja Chola I** (r. c. 985–1014), the temple **consecrated in his 25th regnal year (~1010 CE)**; the king styles himself **Rājarājadeva**, and names the temple **Rājarājeśvaram** after himself, the deity **Rājarājeśvara-uḍaiyār** — a fusion of **god-king and royal cult**. (Rājarāja's Thanjavur inscriptions; [scholarly])
- **The architect Kunjara Mallan Rāja Rāja Perunthachan** — tradition and inscriptional reference credit the chief *sthapati* (master-builder) of this name; the temple is one of the few where the **architect is recorded**. (Inscriptions; [regional])
- **The great endowment inscriptions** — the *prākāra* base bears one of the **most detailed economic inscriptions in Indian history**: it records **lands, villages, gold, jewels, livestock**, and the **rosters of personnel** the king granted — singers, drummers, accountants, lamp-lighters, and **~400 devadāsīs (talicceri-pendugaḷ)** resettled from across the realm to staff the temple. A foundational document for **Chola social and economic history**. (Rājarāja I's inscriptions; [scholarly])
- **A self-auditing temple** — the inscriptions specify **measures, weights, and accountability** so precisely that the temple functioned as a **landed corporation and treasury**, not merely a shrine — the Chola "temple-economy" in miniature. [scholarly]
- **Completed under Rājendra I** — the empire's expansion under Rājarāja's son **Rājendra Chola I** is reflected in later additions and in the **sibling Great Living Chola Temples**: the **Bṛhadīśvara at Gangaikoṇḍa Cholapuram** (Rājendra's new capital, c. 1035) and the **Airāvateśvara at Darasuram** — the **three** together forming the UNESCO inscription. [scholarly]

## III. The Śaiva Milieu — Tēvāram, Naṭarāja, and the Bronze Tradition

The Big Temple is the **imperial capstone of a pre-existing devotional landscape**, not its origin — its sanctity borrows from the **Tamil Shaiva song-canon** that long predates it.

- **Within the Paadal Petra belt** — Bṛhadīśvara stands in the **Thanjavur–Kāverī heartland** thick with **[Paadal Petra Sthalams](275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md)** (the Chola-Nāḍu Thenkarai band of ~128 Tevaram-sung shrines). The Big Temple itself, being an **11th-c. royal foundation**, is **not one of the 275/276** Tevaram temples (the canon closed with the 7th–9th-c. **Moovar**) — but it is the **political crown** set upon that older sacred geography. (See [275 Paadal Petra Sthalams](275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md).) [scholarly]
- **The Tēvāram-recovery tradition** — Chola Śaivism is bound to the **recovery of the lost Tēvāram hymns**: tradition holds that **Rājarāja I** (with **Nambiyāṇḍār Nambi**) had the scattered palm-leaf hymns of **Appar, Sambandar, and Sundarar** retrieved from a sealed chamber at **Chidambaram** and **codified into the Tirumurai** — making Rājarāja the **canoniser of the Shaiva scripture**, a religious act paralleling his temple-building. (Tamil Śaiva tradition; Periya Purāṇam milieu) [regional][late text]
- **The Periya Purāṇam horizon** — the **63 Nāyanār** hagiography, **Sēkkiḻār's Periya Purāṇam** (12th c., the **12th Tirumurai**), is composed under Chola patronage in the **century after** the Big Temple — Bṛhadīśvara belongs to the political world that produced it. (Periya Purāṇam) [scholarly]
- **The Chola bronze / Naṭarāja tradition** — the Chola period is the **golden age of South Indian bronze (cire-perdue) casting**; the **Naṭarāja** ("Lord of the Dance") icon was perfected as the **dynastic emblem of Chola Śaivism**, its theological home the *cit-sabhā* of **Chidambaram**. Bṛhadīśvara's endowments funded a full **bronze processional pantheon** (*utsava-mūrtis*), anchoring the temple in this **metalwork apogee**. (Inscriptions; [scholarly])
- **Śaiva-Siddhānta state religion** — the temple embodies the **Śaiva-Siddhānta** theology that was the **official cult of the Chola court**: the liṅga as supreme reality, served by **āgamic ritual**, kingship sacralised as **Śiva-devotion in stone**. [scholarly]

## IV. Living Cult & Heritage Status

- **A living temple, not a ruin** — worship has been **continuous for ~1000 years**; daily *pūjā* to the great liṅga persists, which is why UNESCO styles the set the **"Great Living Chola Temples."** (UNESCO; [scholarly])
- **Millennium celebration (2010)** — the **1000th anniversary** of the consecration was marked in **2010** with state festivals, commemorative coinage, and Bharatanāṭyam performances — the temple's chronology is precise enough to celebrate a **millennium to the year**. [scholarly]
- **Periya Kōvil in the Thanjavur circuit** — locally the **"Periya Kōvil"** (Big Temple), it is the hub of the Thanjavur temple-tourism and pilgrimage **[circuit](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)**, paired with the Kāverī-delta Shaiva shrines and the elemental **[Pañca Bhūta Sthalams](pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md)** of the wider Tamil country. [regional]
