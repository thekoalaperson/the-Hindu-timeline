# Tārāpīṭha — Vasiṣṭha's Tārā Sādhana, the Fallen Third Eye, and Bāmākhepā

> Bengal's premier **living tantric kṣetra**: on the **Dwārkā (Dwaraka) river** at **Tārāpīṭha**, **Birbhum district, West Bengal**, where — in the *local* pīṭha-reckoning — the **eye / third eye (nayana-tārā) of [Satī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)** fell, and where the goddess **Tārā** (second of the **[Daśa Mahāvidyā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md)**) is worshipped in the *śmaśāna* (cremation-ground) mode. It is the seat where the ṛṣi **Vasiṣṭha** is said to have perfected Tārā-*sādhanā* by the transgressive **Cīnācāra** he learned from **Viṣṇu-as-Buddha in Mahācīna**, and the home ground of the cremation-saint **Bāmākhepā** (1837–1911).

**Names of the kṣetra** — **Tārāpīṭha** ("the seat of Tārā") turns on a deliberate double meaning: **Tārā** the goddess, and **tārā** (Sanskrit/Bengali) meaning the **pupil / eyeball** of the eye — so the "seat of Tārā" is at once the shrine of the goddess and the place where the **eye of Satī** fell. The village sits on the **Dwārkā** (a distributary in the Ajay–Mayurakshi system) in **Birbhum**; its adjoining **mahāśmaśāna** (great cremation ground) is inseparable from the shrine — Tārāpīṭha is worshipped precisely as a **death-ground pīṭha**, the cremation register of the Goddess, complementary to the erotic/yoni register of **[Kāmākhyā](kamakhya-nilachala-kamarupa.md)**. (Tārāpīṭha *sthala* tradition; Wikipedia, *Tarapith*) [regional]

---

## I. The Pīṭha-Reckoning — the eye/third eye of Satī

- **Satī's eye falls at Tārāpīṭha** — in the local Birbhum tradition, when **Viṣṇu** dismembered the corpse of **[Satī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)** with the **Sudarśana** cakra (as **Śiva** wandered the earth bearing her body after the Dakṣa-yajña), the **eye — the nayana / third eye (tṛtīya-netra)** — fell here, sanctifying the spot and giving it both its name and its goddess. (Tārāpīṭha *sthala-māhātmya*; Wikipedia, *Tarapith*) [regional] [folk]
  - *Caution — not in the canonical 51-list:* the standard **51 (var. 52) Śakti Pīṭha** enumeration (*Tantra Cūḍāmaṇi*) does **not** name Tārāpīṭha, and it fixes Satī's **eyes / nayana** at other sites; Tārāpīṭha's claim to a fallen body-part is a **regional Bengali reckoning** layered onto the pan-Indian scheme, not a place in the classical roster (see [51 Śakti Pīṭhas](51-shakti-peethas.md)). Its authority as a *pīṭha* rests instead on the **Tārā / Mahāvidyā** cult and the Vasiṣṭha-*sādhanā* charter below. [disputed] [scholarly]
- **The cremation-ground as the true pīṭha** — more than the fallen-limb myth, Tārāpīṭha's identity as a seat of power rests on its **mahāśmaśāna**: the burning-ground is held to be perpetually charged śakti-space where the Goddess is immediately present, and where *sādhakas* perform **śava-sādhanā** (corpse-ritual) and cremation-ground *tapas* — the site is a *pīṭha* because it is a **death-ground**, not merely because of a relic. [regional]

## II. Tārā of Tārāpīṭha — the fierce Saviouress who suckles Śiva

- **Tārā, the second Mahāvidyā** — the presiding goddess is **Tārā** ("the star / she who ferries across"), the second of the **[Ten Mahāvidyās](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md)**: blue-black, standing in **pratyālīḍha** on a corpse-Śiva, pot-bellied, tiger-skin skirt, a snake for a sacred thread, a garland of severed heads; she holds sword, scissors, a skull-cup/severed head, and a blue lotus. Her forms are **Ugra-Tārā, Ekajaṭā, and Nīla-Sarasvatī**. (*Tārā Tantra*; *Nīla Tantra*)
- **The suckling-Śiva icon (the halāhala episode)** — Tārāpīṭha's signature image shows Tārā **suckling the infant Śiva at her breast**, tied to the **Samudra Manthana**: when Śiva swallowed the **halāhala** poison to save the worlds and his throat burned (**Nīlakaṇṭha**), the Goddess took him as a child to her breast and **nursed away the fire of the venom** — so Tārā at Tārāpīṭha is at once the terrifying cremation-Mother and the tender **mother who saves**. (Tārāpīṭha *sthala* tradition; cf. [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)) [regional] [folk]
- **The stone image behind the mūrti** — the *garbhagṛha* enshrines a decorated metallic/silver **mātā Tārā** icon in the suckling posture, behind or over an older, roughly-carved **primordial stone (the svayambhū śilā)** that is the true object of worship — the ornamented image is the "face" shown to pilgrims, the stone the ancient seat. (Wikipedia, *Tarapith*) [regional]
- **Buddhist–Hindu cross-stream** — Tārā is **shared with Vajrayāna Buddhism**; her *Nīla-Sarasvatī* / *Ugratārā* forms and her whole *Tārā-Tantra* liturgy show the **absorption of a Buddhist-Tantric goddess** into Hindu Śāktism — the mythic memory of which is preserved precisely in the Mahācīna story below. (Wikipedia, *Mahavidya*; *Tārā Tantra*) [scholarly]

## III. Vasiṣṭha's Tārā Sādhana and the Cīnācāra Charter

- **Vasiṣṭha fails at conventional worship** — the ṛṣi **[Vasiṣṭha](../08-beings-and-bestiary/rishi-lineages-and-gotras.md)** (mind-born son of Brahmā) undertakes the worship of **Tārā** but, following ordinary **Vedic / Dakṣiṇācāra** rites, gains no vision of the goddess despite ages of austerity; in the local charter he performs this *tapas* at **Tārāpīṭha** itself, fixing the site as his seat of realization. (*Rudrayāmala Tantra*; *Brahmayāmala*; Tārāpīṭha *sthala* tradition) [regional]
- **Directed to Mahācīna** — despairing, Vasiṣṭha is told (by Brahmā, or by a voice / by Tārā herself) that Tārā can be reached only by a different **ācāra**, and is sent to **Mahācīna** ("Greater China" — the trans-Himalayan land, read by scholars as **Tibet / the Buddhist north**), the home of the goddess's secret mode of worship. (*Rudrayāmala*; *Brahmayāmala*) [regional]
- **Viṣṇu-as-Buddha and the transgressive rite** — in Mahācīna, Vasiṣṭha finds the **Buddha — identified as Viṣṇu in his Buddha-form** — surrounded by adepts practising **Kaulācāra / Vāmācāra**: wine, meat, fish, and the company of women (the **pañca-makāra**). Repelled, the brāhmaṇa ṛṣi recoils — until a disembodied voice (var. the Buddha himself) instructs him that **this** is **Cīnācāra**, the "Chinese/China conduct," the only path by which fierce Tārā is pleased; Vasiṣṭha is initiated into it and at last attains the **darśana and siddhi** of the goddess. (*Rudrayāmala Tantra*; *Brahmayāmala*; recounted in the *Tārā Tantra* / *Nīla Tantra*) [disputed]
  - *Scholarly reading:* the Mahācīna–Buddha episode is widely read as **tradition's own mythic acknowledgment** that the Tārā cult and its **left-hand (*vāmācāra*) technique entered Hindu Śāktism from Buddhist Vajrayāna** — Viṣṇu-as-Buddha personifies the Buddhist source being folded into the Hindu fold. (Kinsley, *Tantric Visions of the Divine Feminine*; N. N. Bhattacharyya, *History of the Tantric Religion*) [scholarly]
  - *Textual note:* the episode is variously placed in the **Rudrayāmala** and the **Brahmayāmala** and echoed across the Tārā-corpus (*Tārā Tantra*, *Nīla Tantra*); the traveller is named **Vasiṣṭha** — a distinct narrative frame from the Vaiṣṇava-vs-Śaiva quarrels of the Vedic Vasiṣṭha (the [Nimi–Vasiṣṭha mutual curse](../03-lineages-vamsha/nimi-vasishtha-mutual-curse.md), the [Viśvāmitra rivalry](../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md)) — do not conflate the *ācāra*-founder ṛṣi of this Tantric tale with those Purāṇic episodes. [scholarly]
- **The charter of vāmācāra at Tārāpīṭha** — this myth licenses Tārāpīṭha as a **Vāmācāra seat**: *pañca-makāra*, *śava-sādhanā*, blood-offering (*bali*) and cremation-ground *tapas* are practised here in continuity with the Cīnācāra Vasiṣṭha is said to have brought back, making the site a working home of left-hand Tantra alongside [Kāmākhyā](kamakhya-nilachala-kamarupa.md). (see [Śākta & Tantra Lineages](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/shakta-and-tantra-lineages.md)) [regional]

## IV. Bāmākhepā — the Mad Saint of the Cremation Ground

- **Bāmākhepā (1837–1911)** — Tārāpīṭha's most famous *sādhaka*, born **Bāmācaraṇ Caṭṭopādhyāy** in **Āṭlā village, Birbhum**, said to be born at midnight on **Śivarātri**. *Khepā* ("mad, divinely intoxicated") is a **title of praise**: "Bāmā-khepā" is "the God-mad devotee of Bāmā (Tārā)." He is the archetypal Bengali *pāgal* saint — a contemporary of **Rāmakṛṣṇa** of Dakṣiṇeśvar and belonging to the same 19th-c. Bengali Śākta efflorescence. (Wikipedia, *Bamakhepa*) [scholarly]
- **The cremation-ground sādhanā** — leaving home young, he became the disciple of **Kailāśpati Bābā** (and drew on the sādhaka Mokṣadānanda / the Kaula stream at the *śmaśāna*), and perfected *yoga* and Tantric practice **living in the mahāśmaśāna** among corpses and funeral pyres — treating the burning-ground as Tārā's own body and the goddess as his literal **Mother**. He became the spiritual head of Tārāpīṭha. (Wikipedia, *Bamakhepa*; academia.edu, *The Sweetening of Death*) [scholarly] [regional]
- **"Sweetening" the terrible Tārā** — Bāmākhepā is credited with **transforming the fierce, blood-drinking Ugra-Tārā into an intimate, accessible Mother**: in his visualization and songs the cremation-goddess is a nursing, indulgent mother and he her demanding child — the devotional "sweetening of death" that gives Tārāpīṭha its living emotional character. (academia.edu, *The Sweetening of Death: Bamakhepa's Visualization of Tara*) [scholarly]
- **The offering fed to the son first** — the most-told Tārāpīṭha legend: Bāmākhepā once **ate the goddess's *bhog* (food-offering) before its formal presentation** and was beaten and driven off by the temple priests; that night **Tārā appeared in a dream to the reigning queen** (the Rāṇī who patronized the shrine), declaring she would not eat because her **son had been struck and left hungry** — so it was ordained that **Bāmākhepā be fed before the Goddess**, an inversion still remembered as proof of his standing as Tārā's child. (Tārāpīṭha oral tradition; Wikipedia, *Bamakhepa*) [folk] [oral tradition]
- **Samādhi at Tārāpīṭha** — he **died in 1911** and his *samādhi* stands within the temple complex beside the cremation ground; his life is the reason Tārāpīṭha is today a **living saint-shrine** as much as a mythic pīṭha, and pilgrims worship Tārā and Bāmākhepā together. (Wikipedia, *Bamakhepa*) [scholarly]

## V. The Two Clocks — mythic charter vs. documented shrine

- **Scriptural / cyclic clock** — the pīṭha-fall of Satī's eye belongs to the **timeless Dakṣa-yajña cycle**; **Vasiṣṭha's Tārā-sādhanā** is set in **ṛṣi-time** (the mind-born son of Brahmā, deep-past Satya/Tretā register); the Tārā who suckles Śiva is fixed to the **Samudra Manthana**. In this frame Tārāpīṭha is a *standing* seat of the Goddess active through the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** (which began **3102 BCE** on the scriptural clock). [regional]
- **Academic / historical clock** — the **Mahāvidyā / Tārā cult** is post-Purāṇic, crystallizing **[scholarly] c. 6th–17th c. CE** (peaking ~1700); the **Cīnācāra Yāmala texts** (*Rudrayāmala*, *Brahmayāmala*) are **medieval Tantric** compositions preserving the Buddhist-Vajrayāna absorption; the **standing Tārā temple** at Tārāpīṭha is an **18th–19th-c. CE** structure on an older site; and **Bāmākhepā (1837–1911)** is firmly **documented history** ([recorded history](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md)). The corpus keeps both frames without collapsing one into the other. [scholarly]
