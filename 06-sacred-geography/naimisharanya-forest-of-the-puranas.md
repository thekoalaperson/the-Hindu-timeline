# Naimiṣāraṇya — The Forest Where the Purāṇas Were Recited

> The single most important **sattra-bhūmi** (perpetual-sacrifice ground) of the Purāṇic tradition: the forest on the **Gomatī** in Sītāpur district, Uttar Pradesh (modern **Nīmsār / Misrikh**) where the bard **Sūta** recites the eighteen Purāṇas and the Mahābhārata to **88,000 ṛṣis** under **Śaunaka** — the literal narrative *frame-story* of nearly the entire Purāṇa corpus. A Vaiṣṇava *svayaṃ-vyakta* kṣetra and **[Divya Desam](108-divya-desams.md)**, a **[Śakti Pīṭha](51-shakti-peethas.md)** of Liṅgadhāriṇī / Lalitā, and a major *tīrtha*-ford of the Mahābhārata's *Tīrtha-yātrā Parva*.
>

**Why Naimiṣāraṇya is the "navel" of the Purāṇas** — almost every major Purāṇa opens not with the deity but with a *scene*: the ṛṣis of Naimiṣa, gathered for a sacrifice that runs years on end, ask a visiting bard to tell them the old stories. That bard's answer *is* the Purāṇa. The forest is therefore the **structural frame** of the corpus — the place where the cosmic time-chart (manvantaras, yugas, royal genealogies) is transmitted to humanity. The name is glossed from *nimiṣa* ("a wink/instant") in several origin-legends below; the forest sits in the **Naimiṣa-kṣetra**, also called *Nimsar*. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.1; Vāyu Purāṇa 1–2; Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva 1)

---

## I. The Frame-Story — Sūta recites to the 88,000

- **The twelve-year sattra of Śaunaka** - the ṛṣis assemble in Naimiṣa for a *sattra* (a long Soma-sacrifice session) lasting a thousand years / twelve years, led by the **Kulapati Śaunaka** ("Śaunaka of the lineage of Śunaka"); during the intervals of ritual they hear the Purāṇas. The assembly is reckoned at **88,000 (aṣṭāśīti-sahasra) sages**. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.1.4; Vāyu Purāṇa 1.11–12).
- **Sūta Ugraśravas / Romaharṣaṇa** - the reciter is the **Sūta** (charioteer-bard caste), patronymically **Ugraśravas** (Ugraśravā), son of **Romaharṣaṇa** (also *Lomaharṣaṇa*, "he who makes the hair stand on end"), Vyāsa's bard-disciple who first heard the Purāṇas from Vyāsa himself. The chain is **Vyāsa → Romaharṣaṇa → Ugraśravas → the ṛṣis**. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.1–1.4; Vāyu Purāṇa 1.16; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.1).
  - *Variant (frame-reciter):* some Purāṇas name **Romaharṣaṇa/Lomaharṣaṇa** himself (the father) as the speaker at Naimiṣa; others name his son **Ugraśravas**. The two are routinely conflated in later tradition. [scholarly]
- **The Mahābhārata's outermost frame** - the epic's *Ādi Parva* opens with **Ugraśravas arriving at the Naimiṣa sattra** fresh from Janamejaya's snake-sacrifice (where Vaiśampāyana had recited the Mahābhārata), and re-telling the whole epic to Śaunaka's sages. Naimiṣa is thus the **outermost narrative layer** of the Mahābhārata. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva 1.1–1.4).
- **The Bhāgavata's opening question** - the *Śrīmad Bhāgavata* begins here: the sages, troubled by the dawning **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)**, ask Sūta for "the essence of all scriptures" — and he answers with the Bhāgavata he heard from **Śuka**. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.1.9–1.1.23).
- **Multi-Purāṇa frame** - the same Naimiṣa scene opens or anchors the **Viṣṇu, Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa, Kūrma, Padma, Vāmana, Varāha, Matsya, Liṅga** and other Purāṇas — making it the most-repeated *maṅgala*-scene in Sanskrit literature. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.1; Vāyu Purāṇa 1; Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa 1).

## II. Origin-Legends of the Name & the Kṣetra

The forest's foundation is told several ways; the texts do not agree and are catalogued, not merged.

- *Variant — the whirling wheel (Vāyu / Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa):* the ṛṣis, seeking a sin-free, demon-free site for a long sacrifice, ask **Brahmā**, who releases his **manomaya cakra** (mind-made wheel) and tells them to settle where its **felly/rim (nemi)** falls or shatters; the rim sinks at this spot, and the forest is named **Naimiṣa** from *nemi* ("rim of a wheel"). This gives the **Cakra-tīrtha / Cakra-kuṇḍa** (below).
- *Variant — the demons slain in a wink (Vāyu Purāṇa; Mahābhārata):* Gauramukha (or the ṛṣis) destroys a horde of **Nimiṣa demons "in an instant" (nimiṣa)**, so the place is *Naimiṣa* — "(where the demons were slain) in a wink." 
- *Variant — Devī Bhāgavata:* the wheel of Time / the *Manorama* wheel released by the Goddess or Brahmā stops here, fixing the *kṣetra* as supremely auspicious for *japa* and sacrifice. (Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa).
- **Status as the holiest forest** - the Mahābhārata and Purāṇas rank Naimiṣa as the foremost *tapovana*; a verse-formula holds that *tīrthas* of all the worlds assemble here during the **Parva** (lunar junctures), so a bath here equals bathing in every ford. [late text] (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva; Naimiṣāraṇya-māhātmya).

## III. Cakra-tīrtha / Cakra-kuṇḍa — Brahmā's Discus-Ford

- **The Cakra-tīrtha** - the central holy tank at Nīmsār, held to mark where **Brahmā's wheel** (or **Viṣṇu's Sudarśana Cakra**, in Vaiṣṇava retellings) struck the earth, opening a perennial spring. Its waters are said to never dry and to well up from **Pātāla**; pilgrims circumambulate and bathe here first. (Vāyu Purāṇa; Naimiṣāraṇya-māhātmya; Skanda Purāṇa, *Naimiṣa-khaṇḍa*). [regional]
  - *Variant (Vaiṣṇava sthala):* the wheel is **Sudarśana**, making the tank a Viṣṇu-discus ford and supporting the site's status as a *svayaṃ-vyakta* (self-manifest) Vaiṣṇava kṣetra.
- **Cakra-nārāyaṇa / the eighty-four-thousand** - the *māhātmya* claims **84,000 tīrthas** converge on the Cakra-tīrtha, ranking it with Puṣkar and Prayāga as a confluence of all fords. [late text]

## IV. The Vaiṣṇava Kṣetra — Tiru Naimiṣāraṇyam (Divya Desam)

- **Tiru Naimiśāraṇyam** - Naimiṣāraṇya is one of the **108 [Divya Desams](108-divya-desams.md)** — the only Divya Desam in this stretch of the Gangetic plain — sung by **Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār** in the **Nālāyira Divya Prabandham**. The presiding Vishnu is **Devarāja Perumāḷ** (Sri Hari/ "Śrī Devarāja"), with the Goddess as **Harilakṣmī / Puṇḍarīkavalli Thāyār**. (Divya Prabandham, Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār; *divyadesam* tradition). [scholarly]
- **Svayaṃ-vyakta kṣetra** - Vaiṣṇava tradition counts Naimiṣa among the eight **svayaṃ-vyakta** ("self-manifested") shrines of Viṣṇu, where the deity appeared without human installation (alongside Śrīraṅgam, Veṅkaṭa/Tirumala, Śālagrāma/Muktinātha, Naimiṣa, Puṣkara, Badarī, etc.). (Pāñcarātra tradition; *Vaiṣṇava sthala-purāṇa*). [regional] See [Sthala Purāṇas](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md).
- **Hari-kṣetra in the Bhāgavata** - the Bhāgavata explicitly calls Naimiṣa **the kṣetra of Viṣṇu**, where the **Manomaya Cakra of Hari** stopped — knitting the Vaiṣṇava theology to the wheel-origin legend. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.1.4, with commentaries).

## V. The Śakti Pīṭha — Lalitā / Liṅgadhāriṇī Devī

- **The Naimiṣa Śakti Pīṭha** - Naimiṣa is a **[Śakti Pīṭha](51-shakti-peethas.md)** where (in the Daksha-yajña dismemberment) a body-part of **Satī** fell — variously given as the **upper lip / a portion of the heart**. The resident Devī is **Liṅgadhāriṇī (Lingadharini)** — popularly identified with **Lalitā Devī** — with **Bhairava Caṇḍa / Mahārudra** as guardian. The temple of **Lalitā Devī** at Nīmsār is the living center of this association. (Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa VII; *Tantra Cūḍāmaṇi / Pīṭhanirṇaya*; Śiva Purāṇa). [tantra]
  - *Variant (body-part):* lists differ — *uttara-oṣṭha* (upper lip) in some Pīṭha-stotras, a heart-portion in others; the Devī-name **Liṅgadhāriṇī** is stable across them.
- **Devī Bhāgavata setting** - much of the **Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa** is itself framed as recited at Naimiṣa, binding the Śākta and Vaiṣṇava framings to one forest. (Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.1).

## VI. The Tīrtha-yātrā Ford & the Pilgrim Circuit

- **A Parva-ford of the Tīrtha-yātrā** - in the Mahābhārata's **Tīrtha-yātrā Parva (Vana Parva, c. ch. 87)**, the sage **Pulastya** (to Bhīṣma) / **Nārada** (to Yudhiṣṭhira) names **Naimiṣa on the Gomatī** among the supreme fords: a month's residence and bathing there is said to yield the merit of a horse-sacrifice and to purify the whole lineage. (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva, *Tīrtha-yātrā Parva*).
- **Skanda Purāṇa Naimiṣa-khaṇḍa** - a dedicated *khaṇḍa* of the Skanda Purāṇa catalogues the sub-tīrthas of the kṣetra — the Cakra-tīrtha, the Gomatī ghats, and the shrines of the resident ṛṣis. (Skanda Purāṇa, *Naimiṣa-khaṇḍa*). [late text]
- **Misrikh / Dadhīci-kuṇḍa** - the twin site **Misrikh** (≈10 km away) is associated with the sage **Dadhīci**, who at Naimiṣa gave up his **bones** to forge the **Vajra** that slew **Vṛtra** — a separate legend localized in the same kṣetra; its tank is the **Dadhīci-kuṇḍa**. (Mahābhārata; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 6.9–6.10; local māhātmya). [regional]
- **The Gomatī parikramā & 84-kos circuit** - living pilgrim practice threads an **84-kos parikramā** of the Naimiṣa-kṣetra around the Cakra-tīrtha, the Lalitā Devī shrine, the Vyāsa-gaddi (Vyāsa's seat), Hanumāngarhī, and the Gomatī ghats. [folk]

## VII. Two Clocks — Scriptural vs. Historical

- **Scriptural (mythic-eternal)** - in Purāṇic time the forest is sanctified "from the beginning"; the great sattra and the recitation belong to the deep past — most coherently to the **present (Vaivasvata) [manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)** at the **dawn of Kali Yuga** of the 28th Mahāyuga, the moment the Bhāgavata's frame sages explicitly fear. [scriptural]
- **Historical (academic)** - philologists read the **Naimiṣa frame as a literary device** layered onto the Purāṇas across the first millennium CE; the identification with **Nīmsār, Sītāpur dist.** is firm in the medieval pilgrim record, and the present Cakra-tīrtha tank and Lalitā/Devarāja temples are later (medieval-to-modern) constructions on an ancient sacred footprint. The "two clocks" must not be collapsed. [scholarly]
