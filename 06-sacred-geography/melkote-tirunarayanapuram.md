# Melkote / Tirunārāyaṇapuram — Yadugiri Kṣetra

> *The principal Śrī Vaiṣṇava center of Karnataka — the Yādava-hill (Yādavādri / Yadugiri) fortress-temple town that **[Rāmānuja](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/ramanuja-and-madhva.md)** rebuilt during his ~12-year Hoysaḷa exile into the institutional heart of his order. Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md) · Related: [Sthala-Purāṇas of Major Temples](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md) · [108 Divya Desams](108-divya-desams.md) · [Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)*

**Melkote** (Kannada *Mēlukōṭe*, "upper fort"; Sanskrit **Tirunārāyaṇapuram**, "city of holy Nārāyaṇa") sits on a rocky outcrop of the **Yādavagiri / Yadugiri** hills above the Kāverī plain in Mandya district, Karnataka. Unlike the [Divya Desams](108-divya-desams.md), it is **not** sung in the Nālāyira Divya Prabandham and so is classed an **Abhimāna Sthala** rather than a canonical Āḻvār abode (cf. [108 Divya Desams](108-divya-desams.md)); its glory is instead carried by a Sanskrit *kṣetra-māhātmya* and, above all, by its role as the **southern bastion Rāmānuja built in the Hoysaḷa country** during the great Cōḻa-persecution exile (see [Rāmānuja & Madhva](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/ramanuja-and-madhva.md)). The "two clocks" hold throughout: a **scriptural clock** (Kṛta/Tretā-Yuga foundation by Vishnu and the gods) running against a **historical clock** (Hoysaḷa–Vijayanagara temple-building, 12th–16th c. CE).

## The Kṣetra and its scriptural charter

- **Yadugiri / Yādavādri as a Kṛta-Yuga abode** — the *Yadugiri-/Nārāyaṇa-kṣetra-māhātmya* declares the hill one of the most ancient Vishnu sites, the self-chosen seat of **Nārāyaṇa** worshipped across all four Yugas; the very name *Yādavādri* ("hill of the Yādavas") links it to Vishnu's Kṛṣṇa-line. (*Nārāyaṇa-/Yadugiri-kṣetra-māhātmya*) [regional]
  - *Variant (the lineage of worshippers):* the māhātmya gives the *mūla-mūrti* (immovable image) **Tirunārāyaṇa / Celuvanārāyaṇa** a chain of cosmic worshippers before any human king — installed/worshipped in turn by **Brahmā**, by **Sanatkumāra**, by **Rāma** in the Tretā, and by **Kṛṣṇa** himself in the Dvāpara — before being lost and recovered in the historical age. [folk]
- **The self-manifest mūla-mūrti (Tirunārāyaṇa / Celuvanārāyaṇa)** — the principal hill-temple enshrines a standing **[Vishnu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** as *Tirunārāyaṇa* (Tamil), *Celuvanārāyaṇa Svāmī* (Kannada, "the comely/handsome Nārāyaṇa"), held *svayambhū* and far pre-dating Rāmānuja's restoration. (*kṣetra-māhātmya*; Hoysaḷa inscriptions) [regional]
- **Yoga-Narasiṃha on the summit** — crowning the higher peak (the *Yādavagiri* summit reached by ~hundreds of steps) is the older **Yoga-Narasiṃha** shrine — **[Narasiṃha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** in seated meditative (*yoga*) posture rather than the wrath of the Hiraṇyakaśipu-slaying; tradition makes this hill-top Narasiṃha the original guardian-deity of the kṣetra, consecrated (in legend) by **Prahlāda**. The pairing of a **valley Nārāyaṇa** with a **summit Yoga-Narasiṃha** mirrors other twin hill-shrines like Sholingur and Ahobilam (see [Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)). (*kṣetra-māhātmya*; local tradition) [regional]
- **The Kalyāṇī tīrtha** — the temple-tank below the Narasiṃha hill is the sacred **Kalyāṇī** (also *Vairamudi-puṣkariṇī*), where the māhātmya locates ritual bathing essential to the kṣetra's *darśana*. [regional]

## Rāmānuja's exile and the rebuilding of the kṣetra

- **Flight from Cōḻa persecution** — c. early 12th c., to escape the Śaiva-aligned Cōḻa king's persecution of Śrī Vaiṣṇavas at Śrīraṅgam, **Rāmānuja crossed into the Hoysaḷa Karnataka country**, where he is said to have spent roughly **twelve years** before returning south. Melkote/Tirunārāyaṇapuram became the chief institutional fruit of that exile. (Śrī Vaiṣṇava *guruparamparā* hagiography; cf. [Rāmānuja & Madhva](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/ramanuja-and-madhva.md)) [traditional]
  - *Dating — two clocks:* the exile sits in the **first ~1.2% of Kali Yuga** — traditional Śrī Vaiṣṇava reckoning places Rāmānuja **1017–1137 CE**, academic reckoning **c. 1077–1157 CE** (epigraphic), so the Melkote years fall in the **early–mid 12th c. CE** by either clock. [traditional] [scholarly]
- **Rediscovery of the buried mūla-mūrti** — finding the hill-shrine ruined and its *Celuvanārāyaṇa* image lost, Rāmānuja (guided in a dream by the deity, in the standard telling) **recovers the buried mūla-mūrti** from an anthill/thicket and re-consecrates it, restoring regular worship and re-founding the temple establishment. (Śrī Vaiṣṇava hagiography) [traditional] [folk]
- **Conversion of the Hoysaḷa king** — tradition credits Rāmānuja with the conversion of the Hoysaḷa ruler **Biṭṭideva** from Jainism to Śrī Vaiṣṇavism, the king thereafter styled **Viṣṇuvardhana** — the royal patron whose dynasty endowed Melkote, Belūr and Halebīdu. (*guruparamparā*; Hoysaḷa inscriptions) [traditional] [scholarly]
  - *Variant (the healed princess):* a popular strand attaches the conversion to Rāmānuja **curing the king's daughter** of possession; treated as hagiographic embroidery on the historically-attested Hoysaḷa–Śrī Vaiṣṇava patronage. [folk]

## The Selvapiḷḷai / Rāmapriya utsava-mūrti and the Delhi legend

- **Recovery of the utsava-mūrti (Rāmapriya / Selvapiḷḷai / Celuvarāya)** — distinct from the immovable *mūla-mūrti*, the temple's processional image **Rāmapriya** ("dear to Rāma") — Tamil **Selvapiḷḷai**, Kannada **Celuvarāya** — had (in legend) been **carried off to Delhi** by a Muslim Sultan's army among temple plunder. Rāmānuja journeys north to recover it. (Śrī Vaiṣṇava hagiography) [traditional] [folk]
- **The Sultan's daughter (Bībī Nācciyār / Tulukka Nācciyār)** — the icon is found in the keeping of the **Sultan's daughter**, who had grown devoted to it and played with it as a doll; Rāmānuja calls the deity, who comes to him as a child, and the image is borne back to Melkote. The princess follows out of love and dies of separation; she is **enshrined at Melkote as Bībī Nācciyār / Tuḷukka Nācciyār** ("the Turkic/Muslim consort"), worshipped at the deity's feet — a striking Hindu–Muslim devotional motif. (*guruparamparā*; local tradition) [folk]
  - *Variant (where she is honored):* the same legend is attached to Śrīraṅgam's *Tuḷukka Nācciyār* shrine; the Melkote and Śrīraṅgam traditions both claim the motif, and the historicity of the Delhi-recovery episode is **disputed** by scholars who read it as a memory of 14th-c. Sultanate raids retro-fitted onto the 12th-c. ācārya. [disputed] [scholarly]

## Festivals and institutions

- **Vairamudi (Vajra-mukuṭa) — the diamond-crown festival** — Melkote's signature annual *brahmotsava*: on one night the processional **Celuvarāya / Rāmapriya** is crowned with the **Vairamudi**, an ancient **diamond-studded crown** kept under state/treasury seal and brought out only for this rite. By custom the priest's eyes are **bound or averted** while fixing it, the crown never seen by daylight; legend derives it variously from **Vishnu's own crown won from the demon Virocana/Vairocana** or from a Garuḍa-borne celestial jewel. The Vairamudi draws the largest crowds of the Melkote calendar. (Local tradition; Vijayanagara-era endowment records) [regional] [folk]
  - *Variant (the crown's origin):* told as (a) the crown **Garuḍa** brought down for the deity; or (b) a jewel-crown **gifted by a Vijayanagara/Mysore ruler**; the priestly secrecy is constant across tellings. [folk]
- **The Yatirāja Maṭha (Yatirāja Sannidhi)** — Rāmānuja, titled **Yatirāja** ("king of ascetics"), established the **Yatirāja Maṭha** at Melkote as a seat of his order and of *paramparā* transmission; the town preserves a tradition that Rāmānuja resided here through the exile and that an image of him (the *Yatirāja* sannidhi) is venerated as having been consecrated in his own lifetime. The maṭha anchors Melkote as an enduring node in the Śrī Vaiṣṇava institutional network (cf. [Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)). (Śrī Vaiṣṇava *guruparamparā*) [traditional]
- **The Akademi / Veda-pāṭhaśālā tradition** — Melkote became a noted center of **Veda and Viśiṣṭādvaita learning**, a status reflected in the modern **Academy of Sanskrit Research** sited there; continuous with its medieval role as a teaching maṭha. [scholarly]
- **Epigraphic and dynastic layer** — the temples carry **Hoysaḷa (12th c.) through Vijayanagara and Mysore Woḍeyar (14th–19th c.)** inscriptions and endowments; the **gopuras, the Rāya-gopura** (left unfinished) and the tank steps belong largely to the **Vijayanagara** building phase, fixing the historical clock of the site even where its charter-legends reach to the Kṛta Yuga. (Hoysaḷa & Vijayanagara temple inscriptions) [scholarly]

## Why it counts as sacred geography (not just a biography stop)

- **A complete kṣetra geometry** — Melkote is read as a self-contained sacred landscape: **valley Nārāyaṇa + summit Yoga-Narasiṃha + Kalyāṇī tīrtha**, charter'd by its own *kṣetra-māhātmya* like the temples catalogued in [Sthala-Purāṇas of Major Temples](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md), and walked as a hill-pilgrimage rather than visited as a single shrine. [regional]
- **The Karnataka pole of the Śrī Vaiṣṇava map** — where Śrīraṅgam is the Tamil heart, Melkote/Tirunārāyaṇapuram is the **Kannada-country bastion**, the place the tradition itself uses to mark Rāmānuja's reach beyond the Tamil land into the Deccan. [traditional]
