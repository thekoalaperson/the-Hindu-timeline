# Naimiṣāraṇya — The Forest of the Sages & Chakra-tīrtha

> *Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md)*
> *Related: [108 Divya Desams](108-divya-desams.md) · [Aṣṭa Svayaṃ-vyakta Viṣṇu Kṣetras](ashta-svayamvyakta-vishnu-kshetras.md) · [Sthala Purāṇas of Major Temples](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md) · [Sapta Purī & Sacred Cities](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md) · [Tīrtha Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)*

**Why a dedicated file.** Naimiṣāraṇya (Naimiṣa-vana, modern **Nīmsār / Misrikh**, Sītāpur district, Uttar Pradesh, on the **Gomatī**) is the **recitation-forest that frames almost the entire epic-Purāṇic corpus** — the place where the bard **Ugraśravas Sūta** narrates the Mahābhārata and the eighteen Purāṇas to **Śaunaka and the assembled ṛṣis** during a perpetual twelve-year *sattra*. It is therefore not "a tīrtha" but the **structural origin-point of the literature that maps every other tīrtha**. This node treats four threads as one canonical site: (1) the **Sūta frame-narrative**; (2) the **discus origin-legend** (Brahmā's hurled *nemi*/cakra) and the **Chakra-tīrtha**; (3) its rank as a **supreme pan-sectarian ford** in the Mahābhārata's *Tīrtha-yātrā Parva* — not merely a Vaiṣṇava site; and (4) its standing as a **[Divya Desam](108-divya-desams.md)** (Tiru Naimiśāraṇyam) and one of the **[Aṣṭa Svayaṃ-vyakta Viṣṇu Kṣetras](ashta-svayamvyakta-vishnu-kshetras.md)**. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva 1; Vana Parva *Tīrtha-yātrā*; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.1)

---

## I. The frame-narrative — Ugraśravas Sūta recites to the sages

- **The twelve-year sattra of Śaunaka** — the ṛṣis gather in Naimiṣa for a *sattra* (a long perpetual sacrificial session) led by the **Kulapati Śaunaka** ("of the lineage of Śunaka"); during the ritual intervals they hear the old stories. The assembly is reckoned at **88,000 (aṣṭāśīti-sahasra) sages**. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva 1.1; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.1.4)
  - *Variant (duration):* the session is given as a **twelve-year sattra** in the epic frame and as a **thousand-year** sacrifice in some Purāṇic openings; both glosses coexist. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.1.4; Vāyu Purāṇa 1.11–12) [scholarly]
- **Ugraśravas Sūta / Romaharṣaṇa** — the reciter is the **Sūta** (charioteer-bard), patronymically **Ugraśravas** (Ugraśravā), son of **Romaharṣaṇa** (also *Lomaharṣaṇa*, "he who makes the hair bristle"), Vyāsa's bard-disciple. The transmission chain is **Vyāsa → Romaharṣaṇa → Ugraśravas → the ṛṣis**. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva 1.1–1.5; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.1; Vāyu Purāṇa 1.16)
  - *Variant (which Sūta speaks):* some Purāṇas name **Romaharṣaṇa/Lomaharṣaṇa** the father as the Naimiṣa reciter; others name his son **Ugraśravas**. The two are routinely conflated in later tradition. [scholarly]
- **The Mahābhārata's outermost frame** — the *Ādi Parva* opens with **Ugraśravas arriving at the Naimiṣa sattra** fresh from **Janamejaya's snake-sacrifice**, where Vaiśampāyana had recited the epic; he re-tells the whole Mahābhārata to Śaunaka's sages. Naimiṣa is thus the **outermost narrative layer** of the epic. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva 1.1–1.4)
- **The Bhāgavata's opening question** — the *Śrīmad Bhāgavata* begins here: the sages, troubled by the dawning **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)**, ask Sūta for "the essence of all scriptures," and he answers with the Bhāgavata he had heard from **Śuka**. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.1.9–23)
- **The pan-Purāṇic frame** — the same Naimiṣa scene opens or anchors the **Viṣṇu, Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa, Kūrma, Padma, Vāmana, Varāha, Matsya, Liṅga** and other Purāṇas — making it the **most-repeated *maṅgala*-scene in Sanskrit literature**. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.1; Vāyu Purāṇa 1; Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa 1)
- **The Devī-Bhāgavata frame** — the **Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa** is likewise framed as recited at Naimiṣa, binding the Śākta retelling to the same forest. (Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.1)

## II. The discus origin-legend — the *nemi* that named the forest

The name *Naimiṣa* is derived several ways; the texts disagree and are catalogued, not merged.

- *Variant — the hurled wheel (Vāyu / Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa):* the ṛṣis, seeking a sin-free, demon-free ground for a long sacrifice, ask **Brahmā**, who releases his **manomaya cakra** (mind-made wheel/discus) and bids them settle where its **felly/rim (*nemi*)** falls or shatters; the rim sinks at this spot, so the forest is named **Naimiṣa** from *nemi* ("the rim of a wheel"). This opens the spring of the **Chakra-tīrtha** (§III).
- *Variant — the demons slain "in a wink" (Vāyu Purāṇa; Mahābhārata):* a host of **Nimiṣa demons** is destroyed here "in an instant" (*nimiṣa*, "the blink of an eye"), so the place is *Naimiṣa* — "where (the demons fell) in a wink."
- *Variant — the wheel of Time (Devī-Bhāgavata):* the **wheel released by the Goddess/Brahmā** halts here, fixing the *kṣetra* as supremely auspicious for *japa* and sacrifice. (Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa)
- **The holiest forest** — the Purāṇas rank Naimiṣa as the foremost *tapovana* and *sattra-bhūmi*; a verse-formula holds that the *tīrthas* of all the worlds **assemble here during the Parva** (lunar junctures), so a bath here equals bathing in every ford. [late text] (Naimiṣāraṇya-māhātmya; Mahābhārata, Vana Parva)

## III. Chakra-tīrtha — Brahmā's discus-ford

- **The Chakra-tīrtha (Cakra-kuṇḍa)** — the central holy tank at Nīmsār, held to mark where the **rim of Brahmā's wheel** struck the earth, opening a perennial spring. Its waters are said to **never dry** and to well up from **Pātāla**; pilgrims circumambulate and bathe here first, before any other shrine of the *kṣetra*. (Vāyu Purāṇa; Skanda Purāṇa *Naimiṣa-khaṇḍa*; Naimiṣāraṇya-māhātmya) [regional]
  - *Variant (Vaiṣṇava reading):* in Vaiṣṇava retellings the wheel is **Viṣṇu's Sudarśana Cakra** (or the **Manomaya Cakra of Hari**), making the tank a Viṣṇu-discus ford and grounding the site's *svayaṃ-vyakta* status. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.1.4 with commentaries)
- **Confluence of all fords** — the *māhātmya* claims that **84,000 tīrthas** converge on the Chakra-tīrtha, ranking it with [Puṣkara](pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md) and [Prayāga](prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md) as a meeting-point of all waters. [late text]
- **The Naimiṣa-māhātmya & Naimiṣa-khaṇḍa** — a dedicated *khaṇḍa* of the **Skanda Purāṇa**, together with the **Vāmana and Varāha Purāṇa** *Naimiṣa-māhātmyas*, catalogues the sub-tīrthas of the *kṣetra*: the Chakra-tīrtha, the Gomatī ghats, the Vyāsa-gaddi (Vyāsa's seat), and the shrines of the resident ṛṣis. (Skanda Purāṇa *Naimiṣa-khaṇḍa*; Vāmana Purāṇa; Varāha Purāṇa) [late text]
- **Misrikh / Dadhīci-kuṇḍa** — the twin site **Misrikh** (≈10 km off) is tied to the sage **Dadhīci**, who at this *kṣetra* gave up his **bones** to forge the **Vajra** that slew **Vṛtra**; its tank is the **Dadhīci-kuṇḍa**, said to mix (*miśri*) all tīrthas. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 6.9–6.10; local māhātmya) [regional]

## IV. A supreme pan-sectarian ford — the Tīrtha-yātrā Parva

- **A first-rank ford of the Tīrtha-yātrā** — in the Mahābhārata's **Tīrtha-yātrā Parva (Vana Parva, ~ch. 87)** the sage **Pulastya** (to Bhīṣma) and **Nārada/Dhaumya** (to Yudhiṣṭhira) name **Naimiṣa on the Gomatī** among the supreme fords: a month's residence and bathing there is said to yield the merit of a horse-sacrifice and to purify the whole lineage. Crucially the epic frames Naimiṣa as a **pan-Hindu *tīrtha*-ford open to all sects** — not yet narrowed to any one deity's cult. (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva, *Tīrtha-yātrā Parva*)
- **Sattra-ground over sectarian shrine** — in the epic and the earliest Purāṇic strata, Naimiṣa's sanctity rests on its being the **forest of the sages' sacrifice and recitation**, prior to and larger than the later Vaiṣṇava and Śākta overlays. The sectarian claims of §V–VI are **secondary accretions on a pre-sectarian *sattra-bhūmi***. [scholarly]
- **The Gomatī parikramā & 84-kos circuit** — living pilgrim practice threads an **84-kos parikramā** of the Naimiṣa-kṣetra linking the Chakra-tīrtha, the Lalitā Devī shrine, the Vyāsa-gaddi, Hanumāngarhī, the Dadhīci-kuṇḍa at Misrikh, and the Gomatī ghats. [folk]

## V. The Vaiṣṇava kṣetra — Tiru Naimiśāraṇyam

- **A Divya Desam** — Naimiṣāraṇya is one of the **108 [Divya Desams](108-divya-desams.md)** — effectively the only one of this stretch of the Gangetic plain — sung by **Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār** in the **Nālāyira Divya Prabandham**. The presiding Viṣṇu is **Devarāja Perumāḷ** ("Śrī Devarāja"), with the Goddess as **Harilakṣmī / Puṇḍarīkavalli Thāyār**; the *tīrtha* is the Chakra-tīrtha and the *vimāna* the Śrīkara Vimāna. (Nālāyira Divya Prabandham, Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār; *divyadesam* tradition) [scholarly]
- **An Aṣṭa Svayaṃ-vyakta Viṣṇu kṣetra** — Vaiṣṇava (Pāñcarātra/Śrīvaiṣṇava) tradition counts Naimiṣa among the **eight self-manifested ([svayaṃ-vyakta](ashta-svayamvyakta-vishnu-kshetras.md))** shrines of Viṣṇu, where the deity appeared without human installation — alongside **Śrīraṅgam, Veṅkaṭa/Tirumala, Śālagrāma/Muktinātha, Puṣkara, Badarī, Naimiṣa, and Tothādri**. (Pāñcarātra tradition; *Vaiṣṇava sthala-purāṇa*; see [Sthala Purāṇas](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md)) [regional]
  - *Variant (list membership):* the precise roster of the eight self-manifest kṣetras varies by source; Naimiṣa's place in it is stable across the major lists. [disputed]
- **Hari-kṣetra in the Bhāgavata** — the Bhāgavata explicitly calls Naimiṣa **the kṣetra of Viṣṇu**, where the **Manomaya Cakra of Hari** stopped — knitting the Vaiṣṇava theology directly to the discus-origin legend of §II–III. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.1.4 with commentaries)

---

## VI. The Śākta overlay — Lalitā / Liṅgadhāriṇī Devī

- **The Lalitā Devī shrine** — the *kṣetra* also hosts a major **Devī** seat, **Liṅgadhāriṇī (Liṅgadhāriṇī Devī)**, popularly identified with **Lalitā Devī**, with **Bhairava Mahārudra** as guardian; the temple of Lalitā Devī at Nīmsār is its living center and is counted in several **Śakti Pīṭha** rosters. (Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa; *Tantra-cūḍāmaṇi / Pīṭhanirṇaya*) [regional] [disputed]
- **One forest, all three streams** — the convergence of the Vaiṣṇava (Devarāja), Śākta (Lalitā), and the pre-sectarian sage-sacrifice frame on a single forest makes Naimiṣa a rare **pan-sectarian *kṣetra*** rather than a single-deity temple-town. [scholarly]

---

## VII. Two clocks — scriptural vs. historical

- **Scriptural (mythic-eternal)** — in Purāṇic time the forest is sanctified "from the beginning"; the great *sattra* and the recitation belong to the deep past, most coherently to the present **(Vaivasvata) [manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)** at the **dawn of Kali Yuga** of the 28th Mahāyuga — the very juncture the Bhāgavata's frame-sages fear. [scriptural]
- **Historical (academic)** — philologists read the **Naimiṣa frame as a literary device** layered onto the Purāṇas across the first millennium CE; the identification with **Nīmsār, Sītāpur district** is firm in the medieval pilgrim record, and the present Chakra-tīrtha tank and the Devarāja/Lalitā temples are later (medieval-to-modern) constructions on an ancient sacred footprint. The two clocks must not be collapsed. [scholarly]
