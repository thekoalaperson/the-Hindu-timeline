# Muktinath, the Gaṇḍakī & Śālagrāma Kṣetra

> *The one *kṣetra* that yields Viṣṇu's body in stone: the **Kālī-Gandakī** gorge in Mustang, Nepal — sole geological source of the **śālagrāma-śilā** (ammonite Viṣṇu-stones) worshipped in every Vaiṣṇava home; **Muktinath**, a great mokṣa-kṣetra and *jwālā-mukhī* eternal-flame shrine shared with Buddhism; and the only **[Divya Deśam](108-divya-desams.md)** outside India. Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md) · Related: [108 Divya Deśams](108-divya-desams.md) · [Char Dhām & Chota Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) · [Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md) · [Sapta Purī & Sacred Cities](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md)*

The **Gaṇḍakī / Kālī-Gandakī** rises near the Tibetan border and cuts the world's deepest gorge between **Dhaulāgiri** and **Annapūrṇa** before joining the Gaṅgā in Bihar (where it is the **Nārāyaṇī** / Sapta-Gaṇḍakī). Its black Jurassic shale beds erode out **ammonite fossils** — spiral chambered shells — which Vaiṣṇavas revere as **śālagrāma-śilā**, the *svayam-vyakta* (self-manifest) aniconic form of Viṣṇu. Above the river, at ~3,800 m, stands **Muktinath / Mukti-kṣetra** ("place of liberation"), simultaneously the Hindu **Tiru Śālagrāmam** and the Buddhist **Chumig Gyatsa** ("hundred waters"). This single node fuses a river-*tīrtha*, a fossil-source, a mokṣa-kṣetra, an eternal-flame shrine, and a Divya Deśam.

---

## I. The Śālagrāma-Śilā — Viṣṇu Self-Manifest in Stone

- **The unique source** — the śālagrāma is found *only* in the bed of the **Kālī-Gandakī** (and its tributary the Mukti-nadī); the river is therefore called **Śālagrāmī**, and the *kṣetra* takes its name from the **śālagrāma** tree/village on its bank. No other river yields the authentic stone, making the Gaṇḍakī the irreplaceable supply-line of household Vaiṣṇava worship. (*Skanda Purāṇa*; *Padma Purāṇa, śālagrāma-māhātmya*)
- **Aniconic, needing no consecration** — unlike a carved mūrti, the śālagrāma requires no *prāṇa-pratiṣṭhā* (installation rite): being self-manifest, it is held to be Viṣṇu *already present*, worshippable in any home without a priest. It is the **only** form of Viṣṇu so worshipped pan-domestically. (*Padma Purāṇa*; *Skanda Purāṇa*)
- **Reading the cakra-marks** — the spiral suture-lines and chambers of the ammonite are read as Viṣṇu's **cakra**; their number, color and mouth (*vadana*) classify the stone into named forms — **Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa, Vāsudeva, Sudarśana, Dāmodara, Hayagrīva, Narasiṃha**, etc. A black, round, double-cakra stone is most prized. (*Skanda Purāṇa, śālagrāma-māhātmya*; *Garuḍa Purāṇa*)
- **Paired with Tulasī** — śālagrāma-pūjā is inseparable from the **Tulasī** (holy basil) plant; the annual **Tulasī-vivāha** weds Tulasī to the śālagrāma (Viṣṇu) on **Kārtika** Ekādaśī/Dvādaśī, ritually opening the marriage season. (*Padma Purāṇa*; *Skanda Purāṇa*)
- **Membership in the Aṣṭa Svayam-vyakta** — "**Sālagrāma**" is one of the **eight self-manifest Viṣṇu kṣetras** (with Śrīraṅgam, Veṅkaṭādri, Naimiṣa, Badarī, etc.); see **[Aṣṭa Svayam-vyakta Kṣetras](ashta-svayamvyakta-vishnu-kshetras.md)** and **[Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)**. (Vaiṣṇava *sthala-māhātmya*; *Divya Sūri Carita*) [regional]
  - *Variant (academic) [scholarly]:* the stones are **ammonite fossils** of the Jurassic Tethys sea, their spiral chambers the source of the "cakra" reading — distinguish this *geological* fact from the *scriptural* self-manifestation; the two clocks do not contradict, they name the same object on different registers.

## II. The Tulasī–Śālagrāma / Jālandhara Legend

The dominant Purāṇic *kathā* explaining *why* Viṣṇu dwells in stone and beside Tulasī — a cross-yuga moral narrative, not a dated event.

- **The chaste Vṛndā** — **Vṛndā** (later **Tulasī**), a devotee of Viṣṇu, is the faithful wife of the asura king **Jālandhara** (born of Śiva's wrath, cast into the sea). Her unbroken chastity (*pātivratya*) renders Jālandhara invincible in his war against the **devas**. (*Padma Purāṇa*; *Devī-Bhāgavata*)
- **Viṣṇu's deception** — to break the protection, **Viṣṇu** assumes Jālandhara's exact form and approaches Vṛndā; her chastity thus technically broken, Jālandhara is slain by **Śiva** in the same moment. (*Padma Purāṇa*; *Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa*)
- **The curse and the boon** — discovering the trick, the grief-stricken Vṛndā **curses Viṣṇu to become a black stone** (the śālagrāma) and immolates herself; from her ashes/body springs the **Tulasī** plant. Viṣṇu accepts the curse, decreeing that he will forever reside as the **śālagrāma** in the Gaṇḍakī and be worshipped only alongside **Tulasī**. (*Padma Purāṇa*; *Devī-Bhāgavata*; *Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa*) [late text]
  - *Variant (the worm/vajra-kīṭa):* in another telling the cakra-marks are gnawed into the stone by the **vajra-kīṭa** ("diamond-worm") that Viṣṇu becomes/inhabits, naturalizing the spiral grooves. (*Skanda Purāṇa*) [folk]
  - *Variant (Lakṣmī's curse):* some recensions assign the curse to **Lakṣmī** or merge Tulasī with Lakṣmī as a co-wife of Viṣṇu, three consorts (Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī/Gaṅgā, Tulasī) quarrelling. (*Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa, Prakṛti Khaṇḍa*) [late text]

## III. Muktinath — the Mokṣa-Kṣetra & Jwālā-Mukhī Shrine

- **The liberation-field** — **Muktinath / Mukti-kṣetra** is held to grant **mokṣa** (release from rebirth) to those who reach it; the central shrine houses **Viṣṇu as Śrī Mūrti / Mukti-Nārāyaṇa** (a self-manifest form), making the site a great Vaiṣṇava *mokṣa-dāyaka* like the **[Sapta Purī](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md)** cities. (*Viṣṇu / Varāha Purāṇa* tradition; Vaiṣṇava *sthala-māhātmya*)
- **The 108 dhārās** — pilgrims bathe under **108 carved bull-mouth water-spouts** (*mukti-dhārā*) channeling icy Gandaki source-water around the temple, plus two **kuṇḍas** — a circuit said to wash away all sin. The number 108 mirrors the Divya Deśam total and the *aṣṭottara-śata*. (Temple tradition)
- **The Jwālā-Mukhī eternal flame** — adjacent, a small **Jwālā-Māī** shrine burns a **natural gas flame issuing from rock beside flowing water** — fire and water together, read as the simultaneous presence of all five **[pañca-bhūtas](pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md)**. The continuously-burning flame makes Muktinath a rare *jwālā-mukhī* tīrtha (cf. the Śākta Jwālāmukhī of Himachal). (Temple tradition; local *māhātmya*) [regional]
- **Shared with Buddhism** — Tibetan Buddhists revere the same site as **Chumig Gyatsa** ("hundred springs"), a place of **ḍākinīs** and one of **Padmasambhava's** meditation sites; the flame is sacred to both faiths, and the temple is tended by **Buddhist nuns** though worshipped by Hindus — a living example of joint Hindu–Buddhist sanctity. [scholarly]
- **A Char-Dhām-tier pilgrimage** — though in Nepal, Muktinath functions in the trans-Himalayan pilgrim circuit alongside **[Kailāsa-Mānasarovar](manasarovar-kailash-yatra.md)** and the Char Dhām of Uttarakhand; see **[Char Dhām & Chota Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)**. [regional]

## IV. Tiru Śālagrāmam — the Only Divya Deśam Outside India

- **The 108th-grid outlier** — Muktinath is **Tiru Śālagrāmam**, the *sole* **[Divya Deśam](108-divya-desams.md)** located **outside India** (in the **Vada Nadu / northern** group of 11), the northernmost and highest of the 108. (*Nālāyira Divya Prabandham*; Śrī Vaiṣṇava tradition)
- **Deity and benediction** — the Lord is **Śrī Mūrti / Śrī Mūrti Perumāḷ** (standing posture, facing east) with **Śrī Devī / Śrī Devī Nācciyār**; the **[Ālvārs](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/alvars-and-nayanars.md)** sang its *maṅgalāśāsanam* in the Prabandham, fixing it on the canonical grid. (*Nālāyira Divya Prabandham*)
- **Why so far north** — its inclusion testifies that the Ālvār pilgrimage-imagination reached the Himalayan Viṣṇu-stone source itself, binding the Tamil devotional map to the geological womb of the śālagrāma. See **[Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)**. [scholarly]

## V. The Gaṇḍakī as Mahābhārata Tīrtha

- **A ford of the Tīrtha-yātrā Parva** — in the **Mahābhārata's Vana Parva** (*Tīrtha-yātrā Parva*), sage **Pulastya** / **Dhaumya** enumerates the **Gaṇḍakī** among the great sin-cleansing fords; bathing there and at its source (the **Gaṇḍakī-dvāra**) yields the merit of major sacrifices. (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva, Tīrtha-yātrā Parva*)
- **The śālagrāma already known** — the epic-Purāṇic stream treats the **śālagrāma-tīrtha** on the Gaṇḍakī as a Viṣṇu-charged crossing where the self-manifest stones are gathered, anchoring the cult to *itihāsa*-era geography. (*Mahābhārata*; *Varāha Purāṇa, śālagrāma section*) [late text]
- **The Nārāyaṇī downstream** — once it enters the plains the river is the **Nārāyaṇī / Sapta-Gaṇḍakī** of Bihar, and its confluence with the Gaṅgā (**Hājipur / Sonepur**) is itself a *tīrtha* — the river thus links the Himalayan stone-source to the Gangetic mokṣa-belt. (*Purāṇic māhātmya*; regional tradition)
  - *Variant (Gaṇḍakī as Tulasī):* some texts personify the **Gaṇḍakī river herself** as a form of **Tulasī / the goddess**, born of the same legend cycle — the river, the plant, and the stone all one Viṣṇu-presence. (*Devī-Bhāgavata*) [late text/regional]

## VI. Why This Kṣetra Is Unique on the Timeline

- The **only** place supplying the *physical body* of household Viṣṇu-worship across the Hindu world; it collapses normally-separate categories into one site — **river-tīrtha + fossil-source + svayam-vyakta mūrti + mokṣa-kṣetra + jwālā-mukhī + Divya Deśam + Hindu-Buddhist shared shrine**.
- Its legends are **cross-yuga** (undated moral *kathā*) while its fossils belong to deep geological time — a clean case of the **"two clocks"**: scriptural self-manifestation and Jurassic ammonite naming the same black stone.
