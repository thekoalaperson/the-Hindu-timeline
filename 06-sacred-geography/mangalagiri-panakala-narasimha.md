# Maṅgalagiri — the Pānakāla Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha Kṣetra

> The "Auspicious Hill" (*Maṅgala-giri*, "hill of good fortune/blessing") near Vijayawada–Guntur in coastal Andhra Pradesh: a foremost **living Narasiṃha kṣetra** ranked with **[Ahobilam](./ahobilam-nava-narasimha.md)** and **[Siṃhācalam](./simhachalam-varaha-narasimha.md)**, famed for the hilltop **Pānakāla Narasiṃha** who accepts *no food but jaggery-water (panakam)* poured into his open mouth — of which a mysterious half is never swallowed, however much is offered.
>

**Maṅgalagiri** (Telugu *Maṅgaḷagiri*, "auspicious hill") is one of the eight *mahā-kṣetras* (great abodes) of **Vishnu** in tradition and one of the principal **Narasiṃha kṣetras** of Andhra, on the Vijayawada–Guntur road south of the Kṛṣṇā river. The hill carries a **Pānakāla Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha** shrine near the summit, while a larger **Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha** temple stands at the hill-foot. Its *sthala-māhātmya* (drawn into Brahmāṇḍa- and Skanda-Purāṇa pilgrimage matter) names the hill itself a self-luminous form of the Lord. As elsewhere, the *traditional* (Yuga / Purāṇa-internal) frame is kept distinct from the *academic* (inscriptional / architectural) frame — the **"two clocks."** [scholarly]

---

## The hill & its names

- **Maṅgalagiri — "the auspicious hill"** — one of three hills in tradition whose very mass is held a divine body (with Tirumala and Vēṅkaṭācala class), Maṅgalagiri being the form on which the Lord stays *ever auspicious*; pilgrims circumambulate the hill itself as the deity. (Maṅgalagiri sthala-māhātmya) [regional]
- **Mukti-giri / Totādri associations** — the hill is reckoned in the broader **mahā-kṣetra** roll of South-Indian Vaiṣṇavism; some local lists fold it with **Totādri** (Vānamāmalai) and the *aṣṭa* (eight) great Vishnu seats, marking it a release-granting (*mukti-da*) site. [disputed] (regional māhātmya tradition)
- **The Kṛṣṇā-river setting** — sited just south of the **Kṛṣṇā** near Vijayawada (Bezawada), it anchors the dense coastal-Andhra temple belt; its hill-foot town shares the name Maṅgalagiri. (Wikipedia, *Mangalagiri*)

## Pānakāla Narasiṃha — the deity who drinks only panakam

- **Pānakāla Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha** — the hilltop deity: an icon shown essentially as a **wide-open mouth (face only)** set in the cave-shrine, who is offered and "drinks" nothing but **panakam** — water sweetened with **jaggery (bellaṃ / guḍa)**, sometimes spiced with pepper, cardamom and camphor. (Maṅgalagiri sthala-māhātmya; Wikipedia, *Panakala Narasimha Swamy*)
- **The half-consumed offering — the living wonder** — priests pour the panakam into the open mouth; a **gurgling sound** is heard as it is taken, and then **exactly about half is returned/never consumed**, *whatever the quantity poured* — a small vessel or many litres alike — the unconsumed remainder distributed as **tīrtha/prasāda**. The Lord is said to drink only **half** and leave half for his devotees. [folk] [regional] (sthala tradition)
- **No insects, no residue** — though the deity is bathed perpetually in sugar-water, the cave is held to draw **no ants or flies** and the jaggery leaves **no stickiness** on the stone — counted among the *kṣetra*'s standing marvels. [folk] (Devasthanam / local tradition)
- **Ugra cooled by sweetness** — as with other fierce (*ugra*) Narasiṃha seats, the sweet panakam is read as **pacifying the burning wrath** of the Man-Lion left over from the slaying of Hiraṇyakaśipu — the sweetness cools the *ugra-rūpa* much as sandal cools the deity at **[Siṃhācalam](./simhachalam-varaha-narasimha.md)**. [folk]

## The sthala-māhātmya (the legend layer)

- **Namuci / the asura and the boon of nectar** — the dominant local māhātmya ties the hill to the **asura Namuci** (var. a demon of the region): granted that he could be slain by **neither dry nor wet** weapon, he is destroyed by Vishnu/Indra through *foam* or a stratagem; the connection to a **liquid offering** (the "neither dry nor wet" theme) is read into the panakam rite. [folk] [late text] (regional sthala-purāṇa)
- **Prahlāda's hill** — as with the wider [Narasiṃha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) cycle, the māhātmya draws in **Prahlāda** and the descent of the Man-Lion to protect the child-devotee, locating an episode of the Lord's grace on this auspicious hill. (Maṅgalagiri sthala-māhātmya; cf. *Bhāgavata* 7) [regional]
- **A king's penance & the self-manifest form** — a frame story credits a royal devotee (variously named) whose **tapas** on the hill draws the Lord to manifest *svayam* in the open-mouthed Pānakāla form, after which the hilltop worship is established. [regional] [late text]
  - *Variant (Yuga-internal antiquity):* the māhātmya makes the hill a **Kṛta/Satya-Yuga** seat that has stood through the Yugas — the standard *svayam-vyakta* "timeless" claim, against which the historical clock below is set. [traditional]

## The hill-foot temple & the Gali Gopuram

- **Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha at the foot** — the larger temple at the base of the hill enshrines **Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha** (with **Rāja-gopuram** and tank), the main site for festival worship and the starting point of the climb to the Pānakāla shrine above. (Wikipedia, *Mangalagiri*)
- **The Gali Gopuram ("wind tower")** — a towering, multi-storey **gopuram** of the lower complex, an Andhra landmark, traditionally credited to **Vijayanagara**-era and later **Rāja of Challapalli** patronage; its open construction lets the wind blow through, giving the name *gāli* ("wind"). [regional] (local temple history)
- **The climb & steps** — pilgrims ascend a flight of stone steps from the lower temple up the hill to the open-mouthed Pānakāla cave-shrine, the two temples forming a single vertical *kṣetra* (foot-Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha → summit-Pānakāla). (Devasthanam)

## Place in the pan-Andhra Narasiṃha circuit

- **The Andhra Narasiṃha axis** — Maṅgalagiri is one node of an informal but strong **Narasiṃha-kṣetra** pilgrimage axis across the Telugu country, devotionally strung with **[Ahobilam](./ahobilam-nava-narasimha.md)** (the *Nava Narasiṃha*, the pillar-bursting hill), **[Siṃhācalam](./simhachalam-varaha-narasimha.md)** (Varāha-Narasiṃha), **Yādagiri/Yādādri** and **Vēdādri** — the great living Man-Lion seats of Andhra–Telangana. [regional]
- **Mahā-kṣetra, not a Divya Desam** — like Siṃhācalam, Maṅgalagiri is **not** among the **[108 Divya Desams](./108-divya-desams.md)** (the **Ālvārs** did not sing it); it belongs to the broader **sthala-māhātmya / mahā-kṣetra** tier of major living Vishnu temples. [scholarly]
- **Within the wider Narasiṃha network** — it sits in the same devotional family as the Tamil Man-Lion seats such as **[Śōḷiṅgūr's Yoga-Narasiṃha](./sholingur-tirukkadigai-yoga-narasimha.md)**, even though those belong to the Tamil Divya-Desam canon and Maṅgalagiri to the Telugu *kṣetra* tradition. [scholarly]

## The two clocks

- **Traditional frame** — the hill is a **timeless, self-manifest** abode of Narasiṃha, active since the Kṛta/Satya Yuga, the panakam-wonder a perpetual sign of the living Lord. [traditional]
- **Historical frame** — continuous attested worship and the standing structures (lower temple, Gali Gopuram) belong to the **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** historical period — **Vijayanagara-era and later** (15th–18th c. CE) building and endowment over an older core. [scholarly]
