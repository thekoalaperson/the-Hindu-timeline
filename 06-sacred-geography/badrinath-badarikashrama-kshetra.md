# Badrinath — Badarikāśrama / Naranārāyaṇāśrama

> *Viṣṇu's supreme Himalayan abode on the Alaknandā at ~3,133 m: the **only Vaiṣṇava member of the Greater [Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)** and the **single shrine shared by both the Bada and the Chota Char Dhām** circuits; an **[Aṣṭa Svayam-vyakta Viṣṇu kṣetra](ashta-svayamvyakta-vishnu-kshetras.md)** and a **[Divya Deśam](108-divya-desams.md)** (Badari-Nārāyaṇa); the eternal *tapas*-seat of the twin sage-avatāra **Nara–Nārāyaṇa**; head-shrine of the **[Pañca Badrī](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md)** circuit. Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md) · Related: [Char Dhām & Chota Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) · [108 Divya Deśams](108-divya-desams.md) · [Aṣṭa Svayam-vyakta Kṣetras](ashta-svayamvyakta-vishnu-kshetras.md) · [Pañca Kedār & Pañca Badrī](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md) · [Kedarnath](kedarnath-kedara-kshetra.md)*

**Badarikāśrama** ("hermitage of the *badarī* / jujube-berry") sits on the right bank of the **Alaknandā** in Chamoli district, Uttarakhand, between the **Nara** and **Nārāyaṇa** peaks, below **Nīlakaṇṭha**. It is the one *kṣetra* that simultaneously caps a pan-India quadrant and a Himalayan four, and the only point where the two **Char Dhām** networks touch. This file treats Badrinath as a dedicated *kṣetra*; the circuit-level treatments live in **[Char Dhām & Chota Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)** and **[Pañca Kedār & Pañca Badrī](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md)**.

## I. The Doubly-Shared Dhāma — Badrinath's Unique Position

- **The only Vaiṣṇava Greater-Char-Dhām pole** — in Śaṅkara's pan-India quadrant (**Badrinath N, Dwarka W, Puri E, Rameswaram S**), Badrinath is the **northern abode** and the **only one of the four sacred to Viṣṇu as Nārāyaṇa** without a Śaiva or avatāra-place overlay dominating; Dwarka and Puri are Kṛṣṇa/Jagannātha, Rameswaram is Śaiva. (Char Dhām tradition, Adi Śaṅkara; *Skanda Purāṇa, Badarī-māhātmya*)
- **The single node shared by both circuits** — Badrinath is the **only shrine common to the Bada (Greater) Char Dhām and the Chota (Himalayan) Char Dhām** (Yamunotri–Gangotri–[Kedarnath](kedarnath-kedara-kshetra.md)–Badrinath); the Chota circuit ends here, and the Kedār pilgrim is bound to *affirm* at Badrinath. (Char Dhām tradition) — see **[Char Dhām & Chota Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)**
- **Aṣṭa Svayam-vyakta member** — **Badarī / Naranārāyaṇāśrama** is one of the **eight self-manifest (*svayam-vyakta*) Viṣṇu kṣetras** (with Śrīraṅgam, Veṅkaṭādri, Sālagrāma, Naimiṣa, etc.) — the apex Pāñcarātra grade where the icon arises on its own. See **[Aṣṭa Svayam-vyakta Kṣetras](ashta-svayamvyakta-vishnu-kshetras.md)**. (Garuḍa Purāṇa; Padma Purāṇa, *Pātāla Khaṇḍa*) [scholarly]
- **Divya Deśam (Tiru Badari)** — Badrinath is the **Vada-nāḍu (northern) Divya Deśam Tiru-Vadari / Badari-Nārāyaṇa**, sung in the **Nālāyira Divya Prabandham**; the **[Ālvārs](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/alvars-and-nayanars.md)** Periyāḻvār and Tirumaṅgai fix it on the canonical 108-grid as one of the Himalayan Vaiṣṇava poles. See **[108 Divya Deśams](108-divya-desams.md)**. (*Nālāyira Divya Prabandham*; Śrī Vaiṣṇava tradition)
- **Head of the Pañca Badrī** — Badrinath (**Badrī Viśāl**) is the **supreme and first** of the five Badrī shrines (Badrinath, Yogadhyān, Bhaviṣya, Vṛddha, Ādi Badrī) strung up and down the Alaknandā. See **[Pañca Kedār & Pañca Badrī](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md)**. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*)

## II. Nara–Nārāyaṇa — the Twin-Sage Tapas-Seat (cross-yuga)

The defining legend of the *kṣetra*: Viṣṇu incarnate as twin ascetics performing eternal penance — an undated, cross-yuga presence rather than a single dated event.

- **The twin avatāra** — Viṣṇu descends as the **twin sages Nara and Nārāyaṇa**, sons of **Dharma** and **Mūrti/Ahiṃsā**, who take up perpetual *tapas* at **Badarikāśrama** on the Gandhamādana–Alaknandā; the flanking **Nara** and **Nārāyaṇa peaks** are named for them. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.3, 4.1; *Mahābhārata*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*)
- **Eternal penance for the world** — the two perform austerities "for the welfare of the worlds" (*loka-saṃgraha*); the **Mahābhārata** identifies **Kṛṣṇa as Nārāyaṇa and Arjuna as Nara** reborn, anchoring the epic's theology to this hermitage. (*Mahābhārata, Nārāyaṇīya*, Śānti Parva; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*)
- **The temptation by the apsarases** — when **Indra** sends nymphs to break the tapas, **Nārāyaṇa** creates the beautiful **Urvaśī** from his own thigh (*ūru* → Urvaśī), shaming them and proving his serenity. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa*; *Devī-Bhāgavata*) [late text]
- **The Badarī-tree / Lakṣmī shelter** — as Viṣṇu does open-air austerities exposed to Himalayan cold, **Lakṣmī** transforms into a **badarī (jujube-berry) tree** to shade and shelter him; the place is thereby named **Badari-kā-āśrama**. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*; *Skanda Purāṇa, Badarī-māhātmya*) [regional]
- **Nārada and the kṣetra's sanctity** — sage **Nārada** is said to have attained Viṣṇu's vision here; the **Nārada-kuṇḍa** in the Alaknandā below the temple is named for him and is the pool from which the self-manifest mūrti is recovered. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Badarī-māhātmya*) [regional]

## III. The Self-Manifest Mūrti & the Hot Spring

- **The śālagrāma Badrī-Nārāyaṇa** — the chief icon is a roughly **1-m black *śālagrāma*-stone image of Viṣṇu seated in *padmāsana* (meditation)**, *svayam-vyakta* (self-manifest, uncarved by mortal hand) — flanked by **Nara–Nārāyaṇa, Kubera, Nārada, Uddhava** and Garuḍa; worshipped as **Badrī Viśāl / Badrī-Nārāyaṇa**. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Badarī-māhātmya*; Vaiṣṇava sthala-māhātmya)
  - *Variant (recovery legend):* the image, lost or thrown into the **Nārada-kuṇḍa** during a Buddhist/heterodox interval, is **recovered from the Alaknandā by Adi Śaṅkara** and re-enshrined — the standard *sthala-purāṇa* of its rediscovery. (Char Dhām / Śaṅkara tradition) [disputed]
- **Tapt Kuṇḍ — the hot spring** — directly below the temple on the Alaknandā bank issue the **Tapt Kuṇḍ** (and Sūrya/Nārada *kuṇḍa*) **natural sulphurous hot springs (~40–55 °C)** in which pilgrims bathe before *darśana* — a thermal anomaly amid glacial snow, read as Agni's perpetual presence at the *kṣetra*. (Temple tradition; local māhātmya) [regional]
- **The two clocks** — *scripture clock:* the mūrti is self-manifest and timeless, the seat of an eternal Nara–Nārāyaṇa penance. *Academic clock:* the hot springs are geothermal; the present shrine fabric is medieval-to-modern, the cult-organization Purāṇic; the **two registers name the same site without contradiction**. [scholarly]

## IV. Adi Śaṅkara & the Re-establishment

- **8th-century re-foundation** — tradition credits **Adi Śaṅkarācārya (c. 8th c. CE)** with **recovering the Badrī image, founding/reviving the temple, and seating it as the northern pole of his pan-India [Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)** quadrant and monastic order — part of his Vaiṣṇava–Smārta consolidation of the Himalaya. (Char Dhām tradition, Adi Śaṅkara) [disputed]
- **The Rāwal priesthood** — Śaṅkara is held to have established that the head priest (**Rāwal**) be a **Nambūdiri Brahmin from Kerala** — a living link binding the southern *maṭha* tradition to the northernmost *dhāma*; the custom persists. (Temple tradition) [regional]
- **Academic caveat** — the surviving temple is repeatedly rebuilt (avalanche/earthquake damage); the "Śaṅkara founded it" claim is **traditional attribution**, the standing structure far younger, and the site's sanctity demonstrably **pre-Śaṅkara** (named in the epic-Purāṇic stream as Badarikāśrama). [scholarly]

## V. The Moving Seat — Winter Shift & the Eschatological Terminus

- **Snow-lock and the winter shift** — Badrinath is open only **~late April/early May to Oct/Nov** (astrologically fixed dates); when it closes for winter, the **utsava-mūrti (festival image) and worship descend the valley** so the deity is never dark. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*; temple tradition)
  - *Pāṇḍukeśwar (Yogadhyān Badrī):* the recognized **official winter abode** — the festival image is carried to **Yogadhyān/Yoga Badrī at Pāṇḍukeśwar**, where (in legend) **King Pāṇḍu** did penance and the **Pāṇḍavas were born**. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*) — see **[Pañca Kedār & Pañca Badrī](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md)**
  - *Joshimath (Narasiṃha temple):* the **Ādi-Śaṅkara maṭha seat at Joshimath**, with its **Narasiṃha (Narsingh) temple**, is the associated lower-valley winter centre and the *gaddi* from which the procession ascends; some practice locates the cold-season worship of Badrī-Nārāyaṇa here. (Char Dhām / Jyotirmaṭha tradition) [regional]
- **Bhaviṣya Badrī — the future terminus** — eschatology holds that when **adharma overwhelms the age**, the **Nara and Nārāyaṇa peaks will collapse and seal the route to Badrinath**, rendering it inaccessible; Badrinath will then **manifest permanently at Bhaviṣya Badrī** (Subhain, near Joshimath), Viṣṇu worshipped there as **Narasiṃha**. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*) — see [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) [folk eschatology]
  - *The thinning-arm sign:* the **Narsingh idol at Joshimath** is said to have a **withering arm**; when the arm fully breaks, the route shuts and Badrī shifts to **Bhaviṣya Badrī** — a folk **end-of-Kali marker**. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*) [folk]
- **Two clocks on the shift** — *scripture/future clock:* the final, permanent move to Bhaviṣya Badrī is an end-of-age event. *Calendar clock:* the annual Pāṇḍukeśwar shift is a present, repeating seasonal rite. [scholarly]

## VI. Relation to the Pañca Badrī Circuit

- **First of the five** — Badrinath heads the **Pañca Badrī** (Badrinath → Yogadhyān → Bhaviṣya → Vṛddha → Ādi Badrī), a *pañchaka* distributing Viṣṇu's presence across five trekking-distance Alaknandā shrines, itself a subset of a wider **Sapta Badrī**. The full roster, variants (Dhyān/Ardha/Narsingh Badrī) and per-shrine legends are catalogued in **[Pañca Kedār & Pañca Badrī](pancha-kedar-pancha-badri.md)**. (Wikipedia, *Sapta Badri*)
- **Shaiva–Vaiṣṇava sealing with Kedār** — the **[Kedarnath](kedarnath-kedara-kshetra.md)** / Pañca Kedār pilgrim closes by affirming at Badrinath, fusing the Śiva-five and the Viṣṇu-five into one Garhwal devotional arc; the **Pāṇḍava** sub-text (Kedār expiation; King Pāṇḍu at Pāṇḍukeśwar) threads both. (*Mahābhārata* tradition; Wikipedia, *Panch Kedar*)
