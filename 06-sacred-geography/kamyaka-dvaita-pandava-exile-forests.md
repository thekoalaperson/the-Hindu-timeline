# Kāmyaka & Dvaita — The Pāṇḍava Exile Forests

> *Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md)*
> *Related: [Kurukṣetra — Dharmakṣetra](kurukshetra-dharmakshetra.md) · [Bhāratavarṣa & Purāṇic Geography](bharatavarsha-and-puranic-geography.md) · [Sapta Purī & Sacred Cities](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md) · [Exile Forests — Daṇḍakāraṇya, Dvaitavana & Kāmyaka](exile-forests-dandakaranya-dvaitavana-kamyaka.md)*

**Why a paired file.** Kāmyaka-vana and Dvaitavana are the **twin forests of the Pāṇḍavas' twelve-year *vanavāsa***, both set on the edge of the **Sarasvatī** west of Kurukṣetra; the brothers oscillate between the two across the exile, so the *Vana / Āraṇyaka Parva* treats them almost as one wilderness with two camps. This node gathers the **Kāmyaka** thread (the first forest entered, Kirmīra-vadha, the Akṣaya-pātra, Kṛṣṇa and Mārkaṇḍeya, the launch of the *tīrtha-yātrā*) and the **Dvaitavana** thread (the Yakṣa-praśna at the lake, the Ghoṣa-yātrā capture of Duryodhana, Jayadratha's abduction of Draupadī) into a single paired entry. (Mahābhārata, *Vana / Āraṇyaka Parva* 1–11, 36ff; Ghoṣa-yātrā Parva; Āraṇeya Parva)

---

## I. Two clocks — the exile-forests in time and on the ground

- **Scriptural clock** — Kāmyaka and Dvaitavana belong to the **Dvāpara-yuga**, the close of the 28th Mahāyuga of the present **(Vaivasvata) [manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)**: the **twelve-year *vanavāsa* + one-year *ajñātavāsa*** imposed on the Pāṇḍavas after the dice-game, just before the Kurukṣetra war. [scriptural]
- **Academic clock** — both forests are **place-traditions localized along the vanishing Sarasvatī** in the Rajasthan–Haryana borderlands west of [Kurukṣetra](kurukshetra-dharmakshetra.md); **Dvaitavana** recurs as a **named Sarasvatī ford** in Balarāma's *Śalya-parva* pilgrimage, anchoring it to that river-corridor, but **no excavated site is fixed**. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Geographic frame** — in the Purāṇic map both lie within **Bhārata-varṣa's** northern river-plain, on the Sarasvatī between the desert (Marusthala) and Kurukṣetra; see [Bhāratavarṣa & Purāṇic Geography](bharatavarsha-and-puranic-geography.md). They sit *outside* the [Sapta Purī](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md) network of liberation-cities — these are wilderness-*tīrthas*, not temple-towns. [scholarly]
- **Note on overlap** — this file zooms in on the Pāṇḍava pair; the broader survey (which also covers Rāma's **Daṇḍakāraṇya**) is the companion file [Exile Forests — Daṇḍakāraṇya, Dvaitavana & Kāmyaka](exile-forests-dandakaranya-dvaitavana-kamyaka.md).

## II. Kāmyaka-vana — the first forest of exile

- **Kāmyaka (Kāmyaka-vana)** — the **first** forest the Pāṇḍavas settle in on leaving Hāstinapura after the dice-game, described as lying **on the eastern verge of the desert near the Sarasvatī**, a wide *tapovana* abounding in deer and birds; from here the brothers move back and forth to Dvaitavana through the twelve years, attended by the priest **Dhaumya** and a train of brahmins. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva* 5, 36)
- **Kirmīra-vadha — the threshold demon** — at the very entrance into Kāmyaka the cannibal *rākṣasa* **Kirmīra** (a friend or brother of **Baka**, kin to **Hiḍimba**) blocks the forest by night; **Bhīma** wrestles and **slays him bare-handed**, as he had earlier killed Baka and Hiḍimba, clearing the way into the wilderness. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva*, Kirmīra-vadha Parva ~11)
- **Kṛṣṇa's visit and Draupadī's grief** — at Kāmyaka **Kṛṣṇa**, with the Pāñcālas and Dhṛṣṭadyumna, comes to the exiles; **Draupadī** pours out her grievance over the *dyūta-sabhā* outrage and the dragging by her hair, and Kṛṣṇa vows that the wrong will be avenged — the resolve for war is renewed in the forest. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva* 12–13)
- **The Akṣaya-pātra — Sūrya's inexhaustible vessel** — to feed the thousands of brahmins following them, **Yudhiṣṭhira** propitiates **Sūrya**, who gives the **Akṣaya-pātra** ("the inexhaustible dish"): a copper cooking-vessel that yields **unlimited food each day until Draupadī herself has eaten**, after which it is empty till the next sunrise. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva* 3) [the Sūrya-stotra and the boon of the vessel]
  - *Variant (Durvāsas test, later in the exile):* the Akṣaya-pātra is famously the hinge of the **Durvāsas episode** — **Duryodhana** sends the irascible sage **Durvāsas** with thousands of disciples to the exiles **after Draupadī has eaten** (so the pātra is empty), hoping a curse will fall; **Kṛṣṇa** appears, eats a single clinging grain of rice/leaf of *śāka* from the vessel, and the whole host of disciples is mysteriously sated, sparing the Pāṇḍavas. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva*, Durvāsa episode) [a famed Vana-parva tale; some retellings place it at Kāmyaka, others at Dvaitavana]
- **Arjuna's departure for the divine weapons** — from Kāmyaka **Arjuna sets out north** to win the **Pāśupata-astra** from **Śiva** (the *Kirātārjunīya* combat with the *kirāta*-hunter) and the celestial *astras* of **Indra**, **Yama**, **Varuṇa** and **Kubera**; his long sojourn in Indra's heaven frames the others' wait in the forest. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva*, Kairāta / Indralokābhigamana Parva ~38–41)
- **The Mārkaṇḍeya discourses** — in the forest (Kāmyaka, with Kṛṣṇa present) the deathless sage **Mārkaṇḍeya** discourses at length to Yudhiṣṭhira: the **pralaya**-vision of the cosmic deluge and the child on the banyan-leaf, the tales of **Naḷa & Damayantī**, **Sāvitrī**, the **Matsya (fish) and Manu's flood**, the **Pativratā** and the **Dharma-vyādha** (the righteous butcher of Mithilā), and the prophecy of the **Kali-yuga**. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva*, Mārkaṇḍeya-samāsyā Parva ~182–232) — see [Exile Forests](exile-forests-dandakaranya-dvaitavana-kamyaka.md)
- **The Rāmopākhyāna and Sāvitrī as consolation** — when Draupadī's abduction (below) leaves the brothers brooding, Mārkaṇḍeya consoles Yudhiṣṭhira with the **Rāmopākhyāna** (the compressed Rāmāyaṇa, a king who lost and regained his wife) and the **Sāvitrī** legend (a wife who won her husband back from Yama) — the epic narrating its own elder exile back to itself. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva* ~262–283)
- **The Tīrtha-yātrā springboard** — it is from the exile-forest that the Pāṇḍavas launch the great **Tīrtha-yātrā** (pilgrimage-tour), the holy fords first **catalogued by Pulastya to Bhīṣma and by Nārada/Dhaumya to Yudhiṣṭhira**, then walked under the guidance of **Lomaśa**, threading the *tīrthas* of all Bhārata. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva*, Tīrtha-yātrā Parva ~80–156) — the catalogue's Kurukṣetra–Sarasvatī stretch overlaps this very geography; see [Kurukṣetra](kurukshetra-dharmakshetra.md)

## III. Dvaitavana & the Dvaita lake — the forest of trials

- **Dvaitavana (Dvaita-vana)** — the companion forest, set **by a great lake on the Sarasvatī**; the Pāṇḍavas shift here from Kāmyaka and back, and it becomes the stage for the exile's three sharpest tests — a royal humiliation, an abduction, and a riddle-contest with a god. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva* 24–27)
- **The Ghoṣa-yātrā — Duryodhana's capture and rescue** — **Duryodhana**, **Karṇa** and **Śakuni** come to Dvaitavana on a **cattle-inspection (*ghoṣa-yātrā*)**, ostensibly to count herds but really to flaunt their prosperity before the exiles; they clash with the **gandharva Citrasena** (Citraratha) and his host, who **rout the Kaurava army and take Duryodhana captive**. On Yudhiṣṭhira's express order **Arjuna and Bhīma rescue their own enemy** — the supreme display of the exiles' magnanimity. Shamed beyond bearing, Duryodhana resolves to fast to death (*prāya*) and is talked out of it by Karṇa and Śakuni. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva*, Ghoṣa-yātrā Parva ~239–256)
  - *Variant (the Vaiṣṇavī-yajña sequel):* stung by the rescue, Duryodhana performs the **Vaiṣṇava sacrifice** (a substitute for the Rājasūya he cannot equal) to restore his prestige. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva* ~256–257) [late / appended episode]
- **Jayadratha's abduction of Draupadī** — while the brothers are away hunting, **Jayadratha**, king of **Sindhu** (brother-in-law of Duryodhana, husband of **Duḥśalā**), comes upon **Draupadī** at the forest-hermitage and **carries her off** by force; the Pāṇḍavas pursue, **Bhīma and Arjuna defeat and seize him**, and — sparing his life because he is their sister's husband — **shave his head leaving five tufts** as a brand of disgrace, exacting an oath of vassalage. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva*, Draupadī-haraṇa / Jayadratha Parva ~248–256) [recensions place this in the Dvaita or the Kāmyaka phase]
  - *Variant (the seed of revenge):* humiliated, Jayadratha later wins a boon from **Śiva** that he can once hold back four of the Pāṇḍavas — the boon that lets him enable Abhimanyu's death and seals his own beheading by Arjuna at Kurukṣetra. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva* ~256; *Droṇa Parva*) [forward-link to the war]
- **The Yakṣa-praśna at the Dvaita lake** — the most celebrated Dvaitavana episode and the **last test of the exile**: chasing a deer that has snagged a brahmin's **araṇi (fire-sticks)** in its antlers, the brothers grow thirsty and reach a lake guarded by a **Yakṣa** — in truth **Dharma**, Yudhiṣṭhira's divine father, in disguise. **Nakula, Sahadeva, Arjuna and Bhīma** each drink despite the Yakṣa's warning and **fall dead**; **Yudhiṣṭhira** alone answers the Yakṣa's catechism of riddles on *dharma* (e.g. "What is the greatest wonder? — that men see others die daily yet think themselves immortal"). (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva*, Āraṇeya Parva ~311–313) — see [Exile Forests](exile-forests-dandakaranya-dvaitavana-kamyaka.md)
- **The choice of Nakula** — granted the revival of **one** brother, Yudhiṣṭhira asks not for Bhīma or Arjuna but for **Nakula**, so that **both his mothers — Kuntī and Mādrī — have a living son**; pleased by this impartial *dharma*, the Yakṣa **restores all four**. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva*, Āraṇeya Parva)
- **Dharma's boon of concealment** — revealing himself as **Dharma**, the god grants that the Pāṇḍavas will pass their coming **year of concealment (*ajñātavāsa*)** unrecognized by anyone; the Yakṣa-praśna thus **closes the twelve forest-years and opens the Virāṭa year** of disguise. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva*, Āraṇeya Parva ~313)

## IV. Localization & living memory

- **Along the Sarasvatī** — tradition places both forests on the **vanishing Sarasvatī** west of Kurukṣetra (Rajasthan–Haryana); **Dvaitavana** is named as a ford in **Balarāma's Sārasvata-tīrtha-yātrā** in the *Śalya Parva*, fixing it to that river-line, though no site is excavated. (Mahābhārata, *Śalya Parva*) [disputed / regional]
- **One wilderness, two camps** — that the brothers shuttle between **Kāmyaka and Dvaitavana** — sometimes named together as simply "the *vana*" — is why the *Vana Parva* reads them as a single exile-landscape rather than two distinct places; recensions disagree on which camp hosts which episode (notably the Jayadratha abduction and the Durvāsas test). [scholarly]
- **The forest as moral testing-ground** — unlike Rāma's **Daṇḍaka**, where the trial is *demonic* (abduction, rākṣasas), the Pāṇḍava forests stage a **dharmic and discursive** trial: the Yakṣa's riddles, Mārkaṇḍeya's sermons, the temptation to abandon forbearance, and the test of magnanimity in rescuing Duryodhana. [scholarly]
