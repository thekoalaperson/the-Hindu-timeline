# The Narmadā Parikramā & Revā-Khaṇḍa Tīrthas

> The great clockwise circumambulation of the river-goddess **Narmadā (Revā)** — the only major river whose entire course is itself a pilgrimage, walked never crossing the water and keeping her always on the right. The river is held to be **older and more sin-destroying than the Gaṅgā**: *"Liberation comes from bathing in the Gaṅgā, but the mere sight (darśana) of the Narmadā grants it."*

The **Narmadā** (also **Revā**; Sanskrit *narmadā* "the giver of delight/pleasure," *revā* from her "leaping" motion through a rocky bed) is the longest west-flowing river of the subcontinent (~1,312 km), rising at **Amarkantak** on the Maikal/Riksha hills (junction of Vindhya and Satpura ranges) and draining west into the **Gulf of Khambhat (Arabian Sea) at Bharuch**. Its self-circumambulation, the **Narmadā Parikramā**, is treated in the Purāṇas as a *vrata* (vow) of the highest merit, and its glory is the subject of the **Revā-Khaṇḍa** and the **Matsya Purāṇa** *Narmadā-māhātmya*.

## The Parikramā Vow — Structure & Distance

- **The circuit** - A pilgrim (*parikramāvāsī*) walks **downstream along one bank from source to mouth, then back upstream along the other bank**, encircling the entire river — conventionally **Amarkantak → south bank (Dakṣiṇa-taṭa) → Bharuch (mouth) → north bank (Uttara-taṭa) → Amarkantak** (Wikipedia, Narmada; mptourism.com).
- **Keep her on the right (pradakṣiṇā)** - The river is circumambulated like a deity/temple: kept always on the **right hand**, so the loop is clockwise around her body. This is the structural reason for the down-one-bank, up-the-other path.
- **Never cross the river** - The cardinal rule: the parikramāvāsī **must not cross the Narmadā** at any point of the loop, only completing the circuit by going around the two natural termini — the **source-spring at Amarkantak** and the **mouth at the sea**.
- **Distance** - Cited figures vary widely with the route taken: commonly **~2,600–3,600 km** (Wikipedia gives ~2,600 km; pilgrim guides ~3,200–3,600 km). The longer counts include detours that round the heads of tributaries rather than fording the main stream. [open-ended: distance varies by route]
- **Traditional duration** - Stated ideally as **3 years, 3 months, and 13 days** when every prescribed rite is observed (mptourism.com; Wikipedia); in modern practice typically **~200–250 days**, or 3–6 months on foot. The slower form lingers for ritual stops, *cāturmāsya* halts (stopping during the four monsoon months), and tirtha observances.
  - *Variant (Mārkaṇḍeya Parikramā):* the **most extensive form**, attributed to sage **Mārkaṇḍeya** (held to be the first parikramāvāsī), circumambulates **not only the Narmadā but each of her tributaries** — rounding the head of every feeder stream instead of merely the main river, vastly lengthening the route (mptourism.com). [folk/traditional]

## The Origin of Narmadā — Birth from Śiva

The Purāṇas and folk tradition catalog several mutually-overlapping origin myths; the variant policy is to record all attributed forms. (See [Shiva / Trimurti](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).)

- **Born of Śiva's sweat (perspiration)** - The dominant Puranic account: while **Śiva performed *tapas* on Mount Ṛkṣa / Mekala**, a drop of his perspiration fell to earth and became a maiden, **Narmadā**; he commanded her to flow as a river and to **destroy the sins of the people** — hence she is "Śiva's daughter" (Wikipedia, Narmada; Matsya/Skanda traditions).
- **Mekala-kanyā (daughter of Mekala)** - She is the **daughter of the ṛṣi/mountain Mekala**, whence her epithets **Mekalā / Mekala-kanyā / Mekalasutā**; this binds her birth to the **Maikal (Mekala) range at Amarkantak**, her geographic source (Wisdomlib, *Narmadā* definitions).
  - *Variant (lunar/Somapa):* she is named **"daughter of the moon,"** or the **mind-born daughter of the Somapas** (a class of pitṛs), and **sister of the Nāgas** — a cluster of alternate parentages recorded in the lexical/Puranic tradition (Wisdomlib).
- **Flowing from Śiva's matted hair (jaṭā)** - A folk strand parallels the Gaṅgā-descent: the Narmadā issues from **Śiva's *jaṭā***, whence Śiva at Amarkantak is invoked as **Jaṭāśaṅkar**. [folk]
- **The decreed start-day** - She is told to begin flowing on **Śukla Saptamī of Māgha**; this is commemorated as **Narmadā Jayantī** (her "birthday"), celebrated ~January around Makara Saṅkrānti at the Maa Narmada temple, Amarkantak (sattology.org; Incredible India).

## Theology: "Narmadā Darśana = Gaṅgā Snāna"

- **The river of sight, not just bathing** - The classic Puranic formula ranks the holy rivers by the *act* each requires: *"Mokṣa is gained by **bathing** in the Gaṅgā, **drinking** the Yamunā, **darśana** (mere sight) of the Narmadā, and **remembering** the Tāpī."* The Narmadā uniquely sanctifies by being **seen** (Wikipedia, Narmada; Matsya Purāṇa *māhātmya*).
- **Older than the Gaṅgā** - The Revā-Khaṇḍa repeatedly declares the Narmadā **more ancient and, for liberation, more powerful** than even the Gaṅgā, Yamunā, and Sarasvatī; where the Gaṅgā purifies after a bath, the Narmadā is said to purify by proximity (astroshastra.com; wisdomlib Revā-khaṇḍa).
- **Self-purifying** - Tradition holds the Gaṅgā herself periodically takes the form of a black cow and bathes in the Narmadā to cleanse the sins absorbed from her own pilgrims — a folk image of the Narmadā as the purifier-of-purifiers. [folk]

## The Self-Formed Stones — Bāṇaliṅga / Narmadeśvara

- **Bāṇaliṅga (Narmadeśvara) defined** - Smooth, ellipsoidal **self-formed (svayambhū) stones** found in the Narmadā's bed, naturally eroded into liṅga shape; worshipped as aniconic **Śiva without consecration**, especially by **Śaivas and Smārta Brāhmaṇas** (Wikipedia, *Banalinga*). They are **white quartz / cryptocrystalline silica**, hardness ~7 on the Mohs scale.
- **"Every pebble is Śaṅkara"** - The proverb **"Narmadā ke kaṅkar utte Śaṅkar"** — *every pebble of the Narmadā is Śiva* — encodes the belief that the river's stones spontaneously assume Śiva's emblem; a Narmadeśvara needs no *prāṇa-pratiṣṭhā* (installation rite) to be worshipped (narmadariverstone.in; Quora).
- **Pan-traditional icon** - Prized for home/temple worship across India precisely because they are *svayambhū*; commercial demand has depleted natural supply, so many marketed stones are now artificially shaped. [note]

## Key Tīrthas Along the Banks (source → mouth)

- **Amarkantak** - The **source (Narmadā-kuṇḍa)** on the Maikal plateau (~1,048 m), where the **Maa Narmadā temple** marks her spring; also origin of the **Son** and **Johila** rivers. Junction of Vindhya & Satpura; site of **Narmadā Jayantī** and the start/end point of the parikramā (Incredible India; sattology.org). [See the source-tirtha tradition; cf. [Sthala Puranas of Major Temples](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md)]
- **Bhedaghat (Marble Rocks) & Dhuandhar Falls** - Near Jabalpur, the Narmadā cuts a gorge of towering **white marble cliffs**; just upstream the river plunges as the **Dhuāndhār ("smoke-cascade") Falls**, named for its rising mist-spray. A celebrated darśana-tirtha of the upper river (Wikipedia, Narmada). [See [Meru, Kailash & Cosmic Mountains](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md) for cosmic-landscape framing]
- **Omkareshwar & Mamleshwar (Mandhātā island)** - The river splits to form the **Mandhātā island**, shaped like the **ॐ (Oṃ) symbol**; here stands the **Omkareshwar Jyotirliṅga** (on the island) paired with **Mamleshwar / Amaleshwar** (south bank). One of the **[12 Jyotirlingas](12-jyotirlingas.md)** — *Omkāramāmaleśvaram* in the Dvādaśa-Jyotirliṅga Stotra (Omkareshwar Temple, Wikipedia).
- **Maheshwar (Māhiṣmatī)** - Historic riverside fort-town, identified with ancient **Māhiṣmatī**; the **18th-c. capital of Rani Ahilyabai Holkar**, who rebuilt its ghats and temples and patronized the **Maheshwari** handloom. A great ghat-and-temple darśana site on the river (goheritagerun.com; Wikipedia).
- **Bharuch (Bhṛgukaccha / Broach)** - The **mouth**, where the Narmadā empties into the **Gulf of Khambhat / Arabian Sea**; the western terminus of the parikramā, anciently the port-city **Bhṛgukaccha** associated with sage **Bhṛgu** (Wikipedia, Narmada). [open-ended: many minor bank-tirthas — Mandla, Nemawar (the river's "navel"), Shulpaneshwar, etc. — are not exhaustively listed here]

## The Revā-Khaṇḍa & Matsya Purāṇa Sources

- **Revā-Khaṇḍa (Skanda Purāṇa)** - The Narmadā's chief *māhātmya*, narrating her origin, sanctity, and the tirthas along both banks (Siddhādhāra, Daśāśvamedha, Vālmīkeśvara, Gaṇeśa tīrthas, etc.). Transmitted in **232 chapters** (wisdomlib). [late text / textual dispute]
  - *Variant (textual attribution):* some scholars hold the 232-chapter Revā-Khaṇḍa is **originally the Revā-Khaṇḍa of the Vāyu Purāṇa**, later attached to the Skanda Purāṇa; the genuinely Skanda recension being a shorter **~116 chapters** (wisdomlib; Wikipedia, Skanda Purana). [disputed]
- **Matsya Purāṇa (Narmadā-māhātmya)** - An independent extended glorification of the Narmadā within the Matsya Purāṇa, source of the **darśana-grants-mokṣa** theology and many tirtha legends (Matsya Purāṇa tradition).
- **Reva-as-name** - Both texts use **Revā** alongside Narmadā; the Revā-Khaṇḍa ("the Revā section") takes its title from this older name.
