# The Navagraha Temples — the Nine-Planet Kumbakonam Circuit

> The **Navagraha Sthalams**: nine ancient Shiva temples clustered in the **Thanjavur–Mayiladuthurai–Kumbakonam belt** of the old Chola heartland (Tamil Nadu / Karaikal–Puducherry), each adopted as the parihāra (remedial) shrine of one of the **nine "grasping" planets** (*nava-graha*) of Hindu astrology — Sūrya, Chandra, Aṅgāraka, Budha, Guru, Śukra, Śani, Rāhu, Ketu. Pilgrims walk the circuit to neutralise the **doṣa** (affliction) cast by a malefic planet in their horoscope. Each is primarily a **Śiva temple** in which the graha sits as a **sub-shrine/parivāra-devata**, not as the presiding deity — the planet "worshipped Śiva here and was cured/blessed," and so the spot heals the same affliction in mortals.

The Navagraha circuit is a **medieval-to-modern devotional construct** layered onto far older Śaiva shrines: the temples themselves are **Tēvāram-era / Chola-period** (7th–12th c. CE) foundations, several counted among the [275 Pāḍal Petṟa Sthalams](./275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md), but their packaging as a single *one-planet-per-temple* pilgrimage tied to *jyotiṣa* parihāra is a comparatively **recent crystallisation** [late text] — there is no single ancient scripture that prescribes "these nine, in this order." Worship targets the **graha sub-shrine** for dosha relief, while the **mūla-vigraha (chief Śiva)** remains the theological centre. The grouping spans roughly a 60-km belt and is conventionally completed (ideally) in **one to two days**.

> **What is a "graha"?** *Graha* = "the one that seizes / grasps," a planetary power that grips the soul's destiny. The nine are seven luminaries-and-planets plus two **shadow nodes** (*chāyā-graha*), Rāhu and Ketu — lunar nodes, not physical bodies. See [Navagraha (deities)](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md) and [Jyotiṣa & the Zodiac](../00-time-and-cosmology/jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md).

## 1. Sūrya (Sun) — Sūryanār Kōvil, Suryanar Kovil

- **The node** - **Sūryanār Kōvil** near Aduthurai (≈ Kumbakonam–Mayiladuthurai belt), the *only* Navagraha temple where the **graha himself is the presiding deity** rather than a sub-shrine: Sūrya stands in his **chariot drawn by seven horses**, with consorts **Uṣā (Uṣādevī)** and **Pratyūṣā (Chāyā/Pratyūṣādevī)**. A 16-metre *rājagopuram* depicts the Sun's chariot. (Wikipedia, *Navagraha temples in Tamil Nadu*; navagrahatemple.com.)
- **Unique layout** - It is in effect a **mini-circuit in one compound**: it houses **separate shrines for all nine grahas** arranged around Sūrya, so that completing this temple symbolically completes the planetary court. Built / endowed under the **Cholas (Kulottuṅga I era)**. (Wikipedia.)
- **Sthala legend — sage Kālava** - The leprous, dosha-afflicted sage **Kālava** prayed to the Navagrahas, who cured him; Brahmā, angered that planets had altered a fixed fate, **cursed the nine** to suffer leprosy themselves — they in turn worshipped Śiva here and were restored, fixing this as the seat of planetary grace. [folk] (Sthala-purāṇa; navagrahatemple.com.)
- **Remedy** - General Sun affliction — diseases of bone/heart/eyes, loss of authority or paternal estrangement; worship strengthens *ātma-kāraka* (the soul-significator). (navagrahatemple.com.)

## 2. Chandra (Moon) — Kailāsanāthar, Thingalur

- **The node** - **Kailāsanāthar Temple, Thingalur** (near Tiruvaiyaru, N of Thanjavur). The Moon sub-shrine houses **Chandra in black granite clad in white**, the cool lunar contrast. *Tiṅgaḷ* is Tamil for "moon," naming the town. (Wikipedia, *Kailasanathar Temple, Thingalur*.)
- **Sthala legend — Dakṣa's curse** - **Chandra married the 27 (or 28) daughters of Dakṣa** (the nakṣatras) but doted only on **Rohiṇī**; the slighted wives complained, and Dakṣa cursed the Moon to **wane and lose his lustre**. Chandra worshipped Śiva at Thingalur and regained his light — hence Śiva wears the crescent and the spot heals lunar/mental affliction. (Sthala-purāṇa; cf. [Nakṣatras](../00-time-and-cosmology/nakshatras.md).)
  - *Variant (Appoothi Adigal):* the temple is also tied to the Nāyaṉmār **Appūthi Aḍigaḷ** and the resurrection of his son bitten by a snake — Appar restored the boy here. [folk]
- **Special feature** - In the Tamil months **Puraṭṭāci** and **Paṅkuṉi**, moonlight is said to fall directly on the deity. (Wikipedia.)
- **Remedy** - Mental unrest, emotional instability, afflictions of the *manas*; relief for a weak or afflicted Moon.

## 3. Aṅgāraka / Chevvāi (Mars) — Vaitheeswaran Koil

- **The node** - **Vaitheeswaran Koil**, ≈ 14 km from Mayiladuthurai. Śiva as **Vaidyanāthar / Vaitheeswaran — "the divine physician"** (consort **Bālāmbikā / Thaiyalnāyaki**); the temple is famed as a **healing shrine**, with the sacred tank *Siddhāmṛta Tīrtham* whose water and the *tīrtha-prasāda* (a jaggery-sandal paste, *piḷḷaikkiṇaṟu* mud) are taken to cure disease. (Wikipedia, *Vaitheeswaran Koil*; vaitheeswarankoil.org.)
- **Sthala legend — Aṅgāraka's leprosy** - **Aṅgāraka (Mars)** was afflicted with leprosy; he worshipped Vaidyanātha-Śiva here and was healed, fixing the temple as the Mars node — the "divine physician" who cured the planet now cures pilgrims. (Sthala-purāṇa.)
  - *Variant (Skanda/Surapadma):* Śiva-as-healer here tended the wounds of **Skanda's (Murugan's) army** after the war with the asura **Sūrapadma**; Murugan is venerated as **Muthukumāraswāmy/Selva-muthukumāra** in the temple. (Sthala lore.)
- **Remedy** - **Chevvāi / Maṅgala doṣa** (Mars affliction) — disease, blood disorders, debt, anger, and obstructed or delayed marriage (Maṅgalik / *Sevvāy doṣam*). Tuesday (Mars's day) is principal. (vaitheeswarankoil.org.)

## 4. Budha (Mercury) — Śvetāraṇyeśvarar, Thiruvenkadu

- **The node** - **Śvetāraṇyeśvarar Temple, Thiruvenkadu** ("the white forest," *śveta-araṇya*), ≈ 24 km from Mayiladuthurai, just E of Vaitheeswaran Koil. A major Pāḍal Petṟa Sthalam; Budha (Mercury) has his sub-shrine here. (Wikipedia.)
- **Sthala legend — Śveta / the white forest** - Named for a **white elephant (or sage Śveta)** who worshipped Śiva here; in one telling the boy-sage **Śveta**, fated to die at sixteen, was saved by Śiva from Yama at this shrine — Śiva subdued death itself (linking it to the Mārkaṇḍeya / Kālasaṃhāra motif). [folk] (Sthala-purāṇa.)
- **Remedy** - Budha affliction — disorders of intellect, speech, skin and the nervous system; strengthens learning, commerce and communication. Wednesday is principal. (navagrahatemple.com.)

## 5. Guru / Bṛhaspati (Jupiter) — Āpatsahāyeśvarar, Alangudi

- **The node** - **Āpatsahāyeśvarar Temple, Alangudi**, ≈ 17–18 km S of Kumbakonam; a Pāḍal Petṟa Sthalam. Śiva is **Āpatsahāyeśvarar — "the helper in adversity/distress"** (*āpat-sahāya*); Guru is venerated in the iconic form of **Dakṣiṇāmūrti**, Śiva-as-teacher, who *is* the cosmic Guru. (Wikipedia; grokipedia.)
- **Sthala legend — the hālāhala poison** - During the **Samudra Manthana** (churning of the ocean of milk), the world-destroying poison **hālāhala** rose first; **Śiva drank it** to save creation, and Alangudi is held to be the very spot where he swallowed it (his throat turned blue, *Nīlakaṇṭha*). Because he rescued the worlds from calamity here, he is **Āpatsahāya — savior in distress**. (Sthala-purāṇa.) → see [Samudra Manthana](../08-beings-and-bestiary/asura-wars-and-roster.md) churning motif, shared with Rāhu/Ketu (§8–9).
- **Remedy** - **Guru doṣa / Guru-peyarchi** afflictions — delayed marriage, lack of progeny, blocked wisdom and fortune; worship of Dakṣiṇāmūrti grants knowledge and *bṛhaspati-bala*. Thursday is principal; **Guru transit (Guru-peyarchi)** draws large crowds. (navagrahatemple.com.)

## 6. Śukra (Venus) — Agnīśvarar, Kanjanur

- **The node** - **Agnīśvarar Temple, Kanjanur**, ≈ 3 km from Sūryanār Kōvil. Distinctively, **there is no separate Śukra idol** — devotees worship **Śukra within the mūla-liṅga itself**: Śukra (Bhṛgu/Bhārgava) is believed to *reside inside the presiding Śiva-liṅga (Agnīśvarar)*, so the planet and the god are taken as one here. (Wikipedia, *Agniswarar Temple, Kanjanur*.)
- **Sthala legend — Agni & sage Parāśara** - Named **Agnīśvarar** because **Agni** (fire) worshipped Śiva here to be purified; the temple is also linked to **Bhṛgu / Śukra** as guru of the asuras, and tradition holds Śiva blessed sage **Parāśara** here. [folk] (Sthala-purāṇa.)
- **Remedy** - **Śukra doṣa** — afflictions of marriage, conjugal happiness, wealth, vehicles, comfort and the arts; strengthens *kalatra-kāraka* (significator of spouse). Friday is principal. (navagrahatemple.com.)

## 7. Śani (Saturn) — Darbhāraṇyeśvarar, Tirunallar

- **The node** - **Tirunallar Darbhāraṇyeśvarar Temple** (also *Saneeswaran Temple*), ≈ 5 km from Karaikal in the **Puducherry** UT, ≈ 33 km S of Mayiladuthurai. Śiva is **Darbhāraṇyeśvarar — "lord of the darbha-grass forest"** (*darbha-araṇya*), the site once a thicket of sacred *darbha/kuśa* grass; the **Śani** sub-shrine is the single most thronged graha shrine of the whole circuit. (Wikipedia, *Tirunallar Dharbaranyeswarar Temple*; thirunallartemple.org.)
- **Sthala legend — King Nala** - Under **Śani's malefic grip** the righteous king **Nala** lost his kingdom, was parted from his wife **Damayantī**, and was reduced to a deformed wretch (the *Nala–Damayantī* episode of the [Mahābhārata](../90-literature-corpus/README.md), *Nalopākhyāna*). Bathing in the temple's **Nala Tīrtham** and worshipping here, Nala was **released from Śani's affliction** and regained throne and queen — fixing this as the supreme Śani parihāra. The name **Tiru-nal-ār** is read as *Nala's place*. (Sthala-purāṇa; tamilnadutourism.com.)
- **Śani Peyarchi crowds** - The **Śani-peyarchi** (Saturn's transit between rāśis, ≈ every **2½ years**) is the temple's greatest festival: **hundreds of thousands** converge over the days around the transit for relief from **sāḍe-sātī** ("seven-and-a-half-year Saturn") and the *aṣṭama/ardhāṣṭama* (8th-house) Śani, making Tirunallar one of South India's largest single-day pilgrim gatherings. (Wikipedia; thirunallartemple.org.)
- **Remedy** - **Śani doṣa** — chronic illness, delay, poverty, loss, the sāḍe-sātī cycle; Saturday is principal, with *til (sesame) oil abhiṣeka* and offerings in black/blue. (navagrahatemple.com.)

## 8. Rāhu (N. lunar node) — Nāganāthar, Tirunageswaram

- **The node** - **Tirunageswaram Nāganāthar Temple** on the outskirts of Kumbakonam — the **Rāhu Sthalam**. Śiva is **Nāganāthar / Nāganātheśvarar** ("lord of serpents"), worshipped here by the serpent-kings; consort **Giri-gujāmbikā / Periya-nāyaki**. (Wikipedia, *Tirunageswaram Naganathar Temple*.)
- **The milk-turns-blue miracle** - The signature rite: during **Rāhu-kālam**, **abhiṣeka of milk** poured over the Rāhu idol is seen to **turn blue/violet** as it flows — taken as Rāhu absorbing and washing away the devotee's doṣa, drawing crowds specifically at Rāhu-kālam. (Wikipedia; cultureandheritage.org.)
- **Origin of Rāhu — Svarbhānu** - The **chāyā-grahas** Rāhu and Ketu are born of the **Samudra Manthana**: when the gods churned out the *amṛta*, the asura **Svarbhānu** slipped into the deva line in disguise to sip it; the Sun and Moon exposed him, and **Viṣṇu as Mohinī beheaded him with the Sudarśana Chakra** — but the nectar had already touched his throat, so head and body became **immortal**. The severed **head = Rāhu**, the **trunk/body (now serpent-tailed) = Ketu**. They take eternal revenge by periodically *swallowing* Sun and Moon — the cause of **eclipses**. (Samudra Manthana cycle; cf. [Nāgas & Serpents](../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md), [Asura Wars & Roster](../08-beings-and-bestiary/asura-wars-and-roster.md).)
- **Remedy** - **Rāhu doṣa**, **Sarpa doṣa** and the **Kāla-sarpa yoga** (all planets hemmed between Rāhu–Ketu); worship of the snake-lord neutralises serpentine affliction. (mynavagraha.com.)

## 9. Ketu (S. lunar node) — Nāganāthaswāmy, Keezhaperumpallam

- **The node** - **Nāganāthaswāmy Temple, Keezhaperumpallam (Keelaperumpallam)** near Poompuhar — the **Ketu Sthalam**, completing the circuit. Śiva as **Nāganāthaswāmy**; the temple celebrates Ketu as the **serpent-bodied** remnant of Svarbhānu (see §8). (grokipedia, *Nagannathaswamy Temple, Keelaperumpallam*.)
- **Sthala legend** - Ketu (the headless serpent-body) worshipped Śiva here and was blessed with planetary status, fixing the spot as the Ketu parihāra; the **Sūrya–Chandra–Rāhu–Ketu** eclipse-quarrel is the binding myth tying nodes 1, 2, 8, 9. (Sthala-purāṇa.)
- **Remedy** - **Ketu doṣa**, **Sarpa doṣa** and **Kāla-sarpa** — Ketu governs detachment, *mokṣa*, sudden loss and occult forces; worship grants release from karmic/serpentine bondage. (navagrahatemple.com.)

## The Circuit — Order, Logistics & Practice

- **No single fixed scriptural order** [disputed] - Different guides prescribe different sequences; the most common begins at **Sūryanār Kōvil (Sun)** and proceeds through the grahas, ending at **Keezhaperumpallam (Ketu)** — but some pilgrims simply visit the **temple of their own afflicting planet** as identified by an astrologer, rather than all nine. Flag: rosters and "the correct order" vary by source. (navagrahatemple.com; templepurohit.com.)
- **A common pilgrim sequence** - Sūrya (Suryanar Kovil) → Chandra (Thingalur) → Aṅgāraka (Vaitheeswaran Koil) → Budha (Thiruvenkadu) → Guru (Alangudi) → Śukra (Kanjanur) → Śani (Tirunallar) → Rāhu (Tirunageswaram) → Ketu (Keezhaperumpallam). The traditional *graha* ordering itself (Su–Mo–Ma–Me–Ju–Ve–Sa–Ra–Ke) mirrors the **weekday lords** plus the two nodes. (navagrahatemple.com.)
- **The "one-day" ideal** - The nine lie within a ≈ 60-km radius of Kumbakonam, so the circuit is marketed as completable in **1–2 days**; each planet has a **prescribed day, colour, grain, flower and oil** for its abhiṣeka (e.g. Sun–red/wheat–Sunday; Saturn–black/sesame–Saturday; Venus–white–Friday). (navagrahatemple.com.)
- **Timing to the graha's hour** - Devotees aim to worship each graha during its **hora / kālam** (planetary hour) — most famously the **Rāhu-kālam** milk rite at Tirunageswaram and **Śani-peyarchi** at Tirunallar. (Wikipedia; cultureandheritage.org.)
- **Theological note** - Throughout, the **chief deity is Śiva**; the graha is a **subordinate worshipper who was himself afflicted and then graced**. The pilgrimage logic is *imitatio*: as the planet was cured by Śiva here, so the dosha-bearing pilgrim is cured by visiting the planet's seat of grace. (Sthala-purāṇas, passim.)

> **Two clocks.** The *temples* are historically datable Chola-era Śaiva foundations (7th–12th c. CE inscriptions); their *Navagraha framing* — one planet per temple, a parihāra circuit keyed to *jyotiṣa* — is a **later devotional overlay** with no single ancient charter [late text]. The grahas' own mythic origins (Svarbhānu's beheading, Chandra's curse, Nala's affliction) belong to **Purāṇic / Epic time**, not to scripturally fixed dates. See [Jyotiṣa & the Zodiac](../00-time-and-cosmology/jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md).
