# Udupi Sri Krishna Matha

> The coastal **Kṛṣṇa-kṣetra** of **Uḍupi** (Tulu Nadu, Karnataka) — where **Madhvācārya** is said to have recovered a self-manifest **bāla-Kṛṣṇa** icon encased in **gopīcandana** from a wrecked ship, where the deity turned **west** so the outcaste saint **Kanakadāsa** could behold him through the **Kanakana Kindi** window, and the headquarters of the **Dvaita (Mādhva) sampradāya** with its eight-maṭha **paryāya** worship-rotation.
>
> Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up: [Master Index](../README.md) · Related: [Sthala-purāṇas of Major Temples](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md) · [Ācāryas & Sampradāyas](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md) · [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)

Uḍupi is unusual among Kṛṣṇa-kṣetras: it has no Purāṇic *sthala-māhātmya* reaching back to the Dvāpara Yuga and **no claim that Kṛṣṇa walked here**. Its origin is fixed firmly on the **historical clock** — the life of **Madhva (1238–1317 CE)** — while the icon it enshrines is tied by legend to the **scriptural clock** (Kṛṣṇa's own boyhood in Dvārakā). The two are kept distinct below.

---

## I. The deity — bāla-Kṛṣṇa, the churning child

- **The mūrti** — the sanctum holds a small standing icon of the **boy Kṛṣṇa** holding a **churning-rod (mantha / kaḍagōlu)** in one hand and a **rope (netra / churning-cord)** in the other — Kṛṣṇa as the **butter-churning Bālagopāla** of Dvārakā, not the flute-player of Vṛndāvana. (Mādhva tradition)
- **Made by Viśvakarmā for Rukmiṇī** — legend holds the icon was fashioned by the cosmic craftsman **Viśvakarmā** and worshipped by **Rukmiṇī**, Kṛṣṇa's queen, at **Dvārakā** during Kṛṣṇa's own lifetime — anchoring the image (not the site) to the Dvāpara Yuga. (Mādhva hagiographic tradition) [folk]
- **Why he faces west** — uniquely, the deity is **not** worshipped through the front (east) door at all; the main darśana is taken from the **rear, western** side through a small window — a reversal explained by the Kanakadāsa legend below. (Uḍupi tradition)
- **Theological status** — in **Dvaita (tattvavāda)**, the Kṛṣṇa of Uḍupi is the supreme independent reality (*svatantra Brahman*), wholly distinct from the dependent *jīvas*; he is served, never identified-with. (Madhva, *Brahma-sūtra-bhāṣya*) [scholarly]

---

## II. Madhva and the recovery of the icon (legend, 13th c.)

The foundation story belongs to **Mādhva hagiography**, principally the **Sumadhva Vijaya** of **Nārāyaṇa Paṇḍitācārya** (c. early 14th c.), the near-contemporary verse life of the ācārya.

- **The gopīcandana ship** — a merchant ship sailing from **Dvārakā** is caught in a storm off the **Malpe / Uḍupi** coast; **Madhva**, by his power, calms it and asks in payment only for two lumps of **gopīcandana** (sacred Dvārakā clay) used as ballast. (Sumadhva Vijaya) [folk]
- **Kṛṣṇa within the clay** — as the ballast-lump is washed, the **Kṛṣṇa icon** — long lost and hidden inside the gopīcandana — is revealed; Madhva recognises it as **Rukmiṇī's own Bālagopāla**, carries it (a heavy image he is said to have borne effortlessly) to Uḍupi, and installs it. (Sumadhva Vijaya) [folk]
  - *Variant (a second icon):* the same wreck is said to have yielded a **Balarāma / Baladeva** image, installed by Madhva at nearby **Vaḍabhāṇḍeśvara (Malpe)**; only the Kṛṣṇa came to Uḍupi. (Mādhva regional tradition) [regional]
- **Consecration of the Maṭha** — Madhva enshrines Kṛṣṇa, digs the adjoining **Madhva-sarovara** tank, and founds the institution that becomes the **Śrī Kṛṣṇa Maṭha**, headquarters of his **Tattvavāda / Dvaita** order. The traditional date is around **1285 CE**. (Mādhva tradition)
- **The eight disciples** — Madhva is held to have appointed **eight sannyāsī disciples** to worship Kṛṣṇa in turn; from these descend the **Aṣṭa Maṭhas** (see §IV). (Mādhva tradition)

---

## III. Kanakadāsa and the Kanakana Kindi (legend, 16th c.)

The most beloved Uḍupi legend belongs to the **Haridāsa / bhakti** layer and the saint-poet **Kanakadāsa (c. 1509–1609)**, of the **Kuruba (shepherd) caste**, then reckoned low-born and barred from the sanctum.

- **Barred at the gate** — **Kanakadāsa**, a devotee of Kṛṣṇa and disciple in the **Vyāsatīrtha / Haridāsa** stream, is refused entry to the temple on grounds of caste, and worships from outside the western wall, singing in anguish. (Kanakadāsa tradition) [folk]
- **The wall cracks, the deity turns** — moved by his devotion, the **Kṛṣṇa icon turns around** to face **west** (away from the front door), and the **back wall splits open** into a small window so the saint can have darśana — the deity choosing the bhakta over caste-rule. (Uḍupi / Haridāsa tradition) [folk]
- **The Kanakana Kindi** — that small grilled aperture in the west wall, named **"Kanakana Kindi"** ("Kanaka's window"), remains the **principal point of darśana** to this day; the main deity is still seen first through this **navagraha-window** (a nine-paned screen), so every pilgrim sees Kṛṣṇa as Kanakadāsa did. (Uḍupi tradition)
  - *Variant (mukha-darśana):* the customary first sight of the lord is the **Kanakana Kindi mukha-darśana** through the nine-square **navagraha kiṭikī**; the westward orientation of the whole shrine is read as the permanent imprint of the miracle. (regional) [regional]
  - *Variant (historicity) [disputed]:* the legend is **post-Madhva by ~250 years** and unattested in the *Sumadhva Vijaya*; many scholars treat the westward orientation and window as architecturally prior, later given the Kanakadāsa miracle-explanation. The **Kanakadāsa kṛtis** (kīrtanas) themselves are securely his.

## IV. The Aṣṭa Maṭha and the paryāya rotation

- **The Eight Maṭhas** — Madhva's eight disciple-lineages each maintain a maṭha at Uḍupi: **Palimāru, Adamāru, Kṛṣṇāpura, Puttige, Śīrūru, Sōde (Sondā), Kāṇiyūru,** and **Pejāvara**. Together they are the **Aṣṭa Maṭha**, custodians of the Kṛṣṇa Maṭha. (Mādhva tradition)
- **The paryāya system** — worship of Kṛṣṇa rotates: a single maṭha's **svāmī (pontiff)** holds exclusive charge of the deity's daily **pūjā, bhoga and administration** for a fixed term, then hands over to the next in cycle. (Aṣṭa Maṭha tradition)
  - *Variant (term length) [disputed]:* tradition holds Madhva first set a **two-month** rotation among the eight (a full round every 16 months); the **two-year (dvi-varṣa) paryāya** now in force is credited to the reformer **Vādirāja Tīrtha** of Sōde Maṭha (16th c.), so the eight-maṭha cycle completes once every **16 years**.
- **The Paryāya Mahotsava** — the handover, held on **Makara Saṅkrānti (mid-January)** of the changeover year, is Uḍupi's greatest festival; the incoming svāmī enters in the **Paryāya procession**, receiving the **akṣaya pātra** and ritual charge of the kṣetra. (Uḍupi calendar)
- **Vādirāja Tīrtha** — the Sōde pontiff who **reformed and regularised** the paryāya, instituted the daily ritual order, and is credited with the legend of feeding **Bhūta-rāja / Kṛṣṇa as a boy**; his **Vṛndāvana at Sōde** is itself a Mādhva pilgrimage seat. (Mādhva tradition) [scholarly]
- **Living institution** — unlike Purāṇic kṣetras, Uḍupi's "events" are **ongoing and dated**: each paryāya year (e.g. recent cycles 2014, 2016, 2018, 2020, 2022, 2024) is recorded by name of the presiding maṭha — a continuous historical-clock calendar. (Aṣṭa Maṭha records) [scholarly]

## V. Worship, kṣetra and circuit

- **Sumadhva Vijaya** — the verse-biography by **Nārāyaṇa Paṇḍitācārya**, recited annually, is the charter-text for nearly every Uḍupi legend; it presents Madhva as an *avatāra* of **Vāyu** (third after Hanumān and Bhīma), grounding the maṭha's authority. (Sumadhva Vijaya) [scholarly]
- **The kṣetra-cluster** — Uḍupi is the hub of a Dvaita circuit: the **Anantēśvara** and **Candramaulīśvara** Śiva temples flank the Kṛṣṇa Maṭha; nearby lie **Malpe / Vaḍabhāṇḍeśvara** (the Balarāma icon) and the broader Tulu-Nadu Mādhva seats. For the **Mādhva sampradāya** Uḍupi ranks with **Maṇṭrālaya** and **Sōde** as a primary node — the seat from which the **eight pontiffs** govern. See [Ācāryas & Sampradāyas](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md). [regional]
