# Kāśī / Vārāṇasī — the City of Light (Avimukta Kṣetra)

> The **mokṣa-purī** of Śiva: the "luminous" city (*Kāśī*, from √*kāś*, "to shine") on the **Gaṅgā**, where **dying itself confers liberation**. Held to be the oldest living city, never abandoned by Śiva (*Avimukta*, "the never-forsaken"), to rest on the prongs of his **triśūla**, and to stand outside ordinary cosmic dissolution (*pralaya*). The dedicated city-*kṣetra* node: its three *khaṇḍas*, jyotirliṅga, guardian deities, eight pilgrimage circuits, and cremation theology.

**Names of the city** — each name encodes a theology. **Kāśī** ("the luminous / shining one," the city of light). **Vārāṇasī** (the land between the two streams **Varaṇā** and **Asī** that flank it north and south, draining into the Gaṅgā). **Avimukta** ("never-forsaken," the zone Śiva vowed never to leave). **Ānandavana** ("the forest of bliss," its primordial garden-form). **Mahāśmaśāna** ("the great cremation-ground," the whole city as the universal burning place). **Rudravāsa** (Rudra's dwelling). (*Skanda Purāṇa, Kāśī-khaṇḍa*; Diana Eck, *Banaras: City of Light*)

---

## I. Avimukta — the never-forsaken & the city on the triśūla

- **Avimukta meaning** - the core theological claim of Kashi: it is the place Śiva *never abandons* (*a-vimukta*, "not-let-go"), and equally the place that, once entered with right death, *never lets go of the soul* into rebirth. The *kṣetra* is "ever-released" and "ever-releasing." (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa*; Eck, *Banaras*)
- **The city on Śiva's triśūla** - Kashi is said to be impaled on / borne aloft by the three prongs of Śiva's **trident**, lifted clear of the earth so that ordinary karma and ordinary time do not bind it. The three *khaṇḍas* (below) are mapped onto the three prongs. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa*; culturalheritageofvaranasi.com)
- **Standing outside pralaya** - at universal dissolution, when the rest of creation is dissolved, Śiva lifts Kashi up on his trident and holds it safe; when creation resumes he sets it back down. Kashi is therefore *nitya* (eternal), the one ground that survives the [cosmic night and re-creation](../00-time-and-cosmology/kalpa-and-pralaya.md). [late text]
- **Ānandavana / primal founding** - in its origin-myth Kashi is the first light-pillar (*jyoti*) of creation, the ground on which Brahmā first performed sacrifice and where the *liṅga of light* manifested. The whole **Ānandavana** ("grove of bliss") is the matrix from which the historical city descends. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa*)
  - *Variant (cosmic priority):* some recensions make Kashi the very first place Śiva set down for the dwelling of liberated souls, predating the rest of *Bhārata-varṣa*; the city is "older than creation." [late text]

## II. The three Khaṇḍas (the three prongs of the trident)

Kashi is divided into three sacred territories (*khaṇḍa*), each named for its patron Śiva-liṅga, together forming the trident-shape; each has its own *antargṛha* (inner-house) circuit. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa*; culturalheritageofvaranasi.com)

- **Omkāreśvara Khaṇḍa (north)** - the northern prong, centred on the **Omkāreśvara** liṅga (named for the *Oṃ*-syllable). Its inner circuit runs ~14 km and counts **108 sacred sites**, of which ~67 are Śaiva, with 12 sacred ponds (*kuṇḍa*), 6 Vaiṣṇava forms and 4 goddesses. The oldest and now most decayed of the three, in the northern Adi-Kashi quarter. (culturalheritageofvaranasi.com)
- **Viśveśvara Khaṇḍa (centre)** - the central prong and heart of the city, centred on **Viśveśvara / Viśvanātha** (the jyotirliṅga). Its *antargṛha* forms a seven-round spiral of **72 shrines** (~56 Śaiva, plus 6 goddesses, 6 Vināyakas, and Bhairava/Vishnu/Āditya/Vāpī shrines), bounded by the Gaṅgā (east), Gokarṇeśvara (west), Bharabhūteśvara (north) and Brahmeśvara (south). This circuit is the prescribed opening/closing rite of the Pañcakrośī. (culturalheritageofvaranasi.com)
- **Kedāreśvara Khaṇḍa (south)** - the southern prong, centred on **Kedāreśvara** (Kashi's "Himalayan" liṅga, a southern counterpart of [Kedārnāth](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)). Its inner circuit runs ~7.5 km with **126 shrines** total (72 Śaiva, 19 goddesses, 7 Vināyakas, others), in the southern Bengali-pilgrim quarter around Kedar Ghat. (culturalheritageofvaranasi.com)
  - *Variant (which liṅga heads the south):* the southern *khaṇḍa* is named **Kedāra** in the *Kāśī-khaṇḍa*, but some circuit-lists substitute or pair **Kedāreśvara** with **Hariścandreśvara**; the three-prong naming Omkāra–Viśveśvara–Kedāra is the standard set. [scholarly]

## III. The central deities of the kṣetra

- **Kāśī Viśvanātha / Viśveśvara (jyotirliṅga)** - "Lord of All," the supreme *Śiva-liṅga* of the city and the **7th of the [12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md)** (the self-manifest pillars of light). Śiva himself is said to have installed the *mukti-dāyaka* (liberation-giving) liṅga here and bound himself never to leave. The present temple (Gyanvapi/Vishwanath complex) was rebuilt by **Ahilyābāī Holkar** (1780) after repeated medieval demolitions. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa*; *Śiva Purāṇa*; greenmesg.org)
- **Annapūrṇā ("the full-of-food")** - the goddess-consort of Viśvanātha and queen of Kashi, who feeds the city; in the famous myth **Śiva himself begs alms** from her bowl, the deity of renunciation accepting food from the deity of abundance — a *darśana* of the interdependence of asceticism and nourishment. Her temple stands beside Vishwanath. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa*; folk myth-cycle) [folk]
- **Bindu Mādhava (Viṣṇu)** - the chief **Vaiṣṇava** deity of Kashi, presiding at **Pañcagaṅgā Ghāṭ**, where five streams (Kiraṇā, Dhūtapāpā, Sarasvatī, Yamunā, Gaṅgā) are held to converge. He is Vishnu's guardianship of the Śaiva city; even the city of Śiva keeps its Madhava. The original shrine was demolished by Aurangzeb (Ālamgīr mosque on the site). (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa*; varanasiguru.com)
- **Kāla Bhairava — Kotwāl of Kashi** - the fierce **Bhairava** form of Śiva who is the **police-chief (kotwāl)** of the city: no soul may die-and-be-liberated in Kashi without first settling its accounts before him, and the dying undergo *bhairavī-yātanā* (Bhairava's reckoning) that burns off residual karma. His emergence here ties to the **Brahmā-decapitation** myth (Bhairava severed Brahmā's fifth head; the skull clung to his hand until released at Kashi, the *Kapālamocana* tīrtha). Pilgrims seek his pass to enter the city's protection. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa*; Eck, *Banaras*)
- **Viśālākṣī ("wide/vast-eyed")** - the **[Śakti-pīṭha](51-shakti-peethas.md)** of Kashi (one site where a part of Satī fell — variously an earring/*maṇikarṇikā* or eyes); the resident Devī of the *kṣetra* alongside Annapūrṇā. (*Devī-Bhāgavata*; Śakti-pīṭha lists)
- **Ḍhuṇḍhirāja Gaṇeśa** - the great **Gaṇeśa** of the *antargṛha*, "the seeker," guardian-remover of obstacles at the door of the central circuit; pilgrims salute him before the inner yātrās. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa*) — see [Ganesha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md)

## IV. The five Vināyakas (guardian Gaṇeśas) & the ring of Vināyakas

- **The five (and the multiplied) Vināyakas** - Kashi is ringed by **Gaṇeśa/Vināyaka** guardians who protect the *kṣetra* at its cardinal and intermediate gates; the *Kāśī-khaṇḍa* names a celebrated set headed by **Ḍhuṇḍhirāja** with the inner five often given as **Ḍhuṇḍhi, Modakapriya, Rāja-vināyaka (Arka-vināyaka), Cintāmaṇi, and Kṣemaka/Durga-vināyaka** — the protective five at the heart of a larger fractal lattice. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa*) [late text]
  - *Variant (count):* the *Kāśī-khaṇḍa* most famously catalogs **56 (aṣṭa-pañcāśat) Vināyakas** arrayed in seven concentric rings of eight around the city, with the "five" being the innermost protective core; rosters of the 56 differ by manuscript and by guidebook — an **open-ended roster**. [disputed]

## V. Manikarnikā & the theology of cremation-moksha

- **Maṇikarṇikā Ghāṭ — the Mahāśmaśāna** - the supreme cremation-ground of Kashi (and, by extension, the cosmic burning-place), where funeral pyres burn **without interruption**, day and night. To be cremated here is held to grant **mokṣa**. The name ("jewelled earring") is glossed by twin myths: (a) Viṣṇu dug the **Cakra-Puṣkariṇī** *kuṇḍa* with his discus in primal devotion and Śiva, trembling with delight, dropped his jewelled **earring** (*maṇi-karṇikā*) into it; (b) the *kuṇḍa* is older than the Gaṅgā, the first tīrtha of creation. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa*; varanasiguru.com; prayagpandits.com)
- **The Tāraka-mantra at death = mokṣa** - the defining doctrine of Kashi: at the moment of death within the *kṣetra*, **Śiva himself whispers the *tāraka-mantra* (the "ferry-mantra," the mantra that crosses one over) into the ear of the dying**, cutting the knot of karma and granting immediate liberation regardless of the soul's prior deeds. Death in Kashi is *kāśī-maraṇa-mukti* — "liberation by dying in Kashi." (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa*; Eck, *Banaras*)
  - *Variant (content of the mantra):* the *tāraka* is variously identified as **"Rāma" / Tāraka-brahma-nāma**, as **"Oṃ Namaḥ Śivāya,"** or as an unspeakable secret syllable Śiva alone knows — texts and traditions disagree on its words while agreeing on its effect. [disputed]
  - *Variant (Bhairava's reckoning):* the soul is not liberated *gratis* — between death and release it must pass **Kāla Bhairava's** ordeal (*bhairavī-yātanā*) to burn off residual karma; mokṣa is granted, but the accounting is exacted first. [folk]
- **Why dying-anywhere-else differs** - elsewhere death sends the soul through Yama's [naraka/afterlife reckoning](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/narakas-and-afterlife.md) and back into rebirth; in Kashi alone the ordinary post-mortem machinery is short-circuited by Śiva's grace. This is what raises Kashi above a mere tīrtha to a **mokṣa-purī**. (*Garuḍa Purāṇa*; *Kāśī-khaṇḍa*)
- **Maṇikarṇikā as Śakti-pīṭha** - the spot is also counted among the [51 Śakti-pīṭhas](51-shakti-peethas.md) (Satī's earring/*maṇikarṇikā* fell here in one reckoning), fusing death-ground and goddess-ground. [disputed]

## VI. The eight classical Yātrās (pilgrimage circuits)

The *Kāśī-khaṇḍa* prescribes a graded set of circumambulatory pilgrimages; the eight classical *yātrās* together map ~774 divine images, ~81% Śaiva. (culturalheritageofvaranasi.com; Rana P.B. Singh, *Panchakroshi Yatra of Banaras*)

- **1. Pañcakrośī Yātrā** - the **great outer circuit**: ~**88.5 km** (a radius of ~5 *krośa*, hence the name), traditionally walked over **5 days**, visiting **108 shrines / *deva-sthala***, beginning and ending at **Manikarnikā** with the **Viśveśvara Antargṛha** as opening/closing rite, and resting at five overnight stations (Kardameśvara, Bhīmcaṇḍī, Rāmeśvara, Śivpur/Pañcakrośī, Kapildhārā). Defines the *liberating zone* of Kashi as the whole 5-krośa field, not merely the temple. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa*; Singh, *Panchakroshi Yatra*)
  - *Variant (Bṛhat / Cauryāsīkrośī):* an expanded **84-krośa** "greater Pañcakrośī" circuit (*Bṛhat Pañcakrośī*) maps the enlarged cosmic Kashi over a much wider arc. [scholarly]
- **2. Nagar-Pradakṣiṇā (city circumambulation)** - the circuit tracing the **outer urban boundary** of the city proper, the second-widest ring. (culturalheritageofvaranasi.com)
- **3. Avimukta Yātrā** - the "covering circuit" enclosing **all three khaṇḍas**, a four-fold spiral of **72 shrines** marking the inviolable *Avimukta* core that Śiva never leaves. (culturalheritageofvaranasi.com)
- **4. Viśveśvara Antargṛha Yātrā** - the **central inner-house** circuit, a seven-round spiral of **72 shrines** around Viśvanātha; the prescribed prelude/coda to the Pañcakrośī. (culturalheritageofvaranasi.com)
- **5. Kedāreśvara Antargṛha Yātrā** - the **southern inner-house** circuit, ~7.5 km, **126 shrines**, around Kedāreśvara. (culturalheritageofvaranasi.com)
- **6. Omkāreśvara Antargṛha Yātrā** - the **northern inner-house** circuit, ~14 km, **108 sites**, around Omkāreśvara. (culturalheritageofvaranasi.com)
- **7. Uttara-mānasa (Uttaramānasa) Yātrā** - the **northern "mind/lake" path**, the upper of the two *mānasa* circuits. (culturalheritageofvaranasi.com)
- **8. Dakṣiṇa-mānasa (Dakṣiṇamānasa) Yātrā** - the **southern "mind/lake" path**, lower counterpart of the *Uttara-mānasa*. (culturalheritageofvaranasi.com)
  - *Variant (rosters & dating):* the precise shrine-lists and even the membership of the "eight" vary across manuscripts of the *Kāśī-khaṇḍa* and across the modern revival-pamphlets (the Avimukta and Pañcakrośī were re-activated by religious bodies only in the late 1990s); treat the numeric counts as text-attributed ideals, not surveyed totals. [scholarly] — **open-ended roster**

## VII. Source layer & academic dating (two clocks)

- **Scripture clock** - Kashi presents itself as **eternal (*nitya*)**: older than creation, surviving every *pralaya* on Śiva's trident, founded before *Bhārata-varṣa* itself. Within the [yuga scheme](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md) it is the constant — the one place untouched by the turning of the four ages. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa*)
- **Academic clock** - Varanasi is one of the **oldest continuously-inhabited cities on earth**, with archaeological settlement at **Rajghat** from roughly the **8th–6th century BCE** (Iron Age / second urbanization), and textual mention from the **late Vedic** period onward (Kāśī as a *janapada* / *mahājanapada*). The **Kāśī-khaṇḍa** of the **Skanda Purāṇa** — the principal source-text for everything above — is itself a **late composition, c. 13th–14th century CE**, codifying older oral pilgrimage-lore into systematic circuit-maps. [late text] [scholarly]
- **Maya = Gaya debate (cross-reference)** - in the canonical Sapta-Purī verse (*Ayodhyā Mathurā Māyā Kāśī...*), Kashi sits beside *Māyā*; a minority gloss reads *Māyā* as **Gayā** (the *śrāddha*-city of Bihar) rather than **Māyāpurī/Haridwar** — a debate that bears on which seven cities frame Kashi. Cross-ref the full treatment at [Sapta Purī & Sacred Cities](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md#i-the-seven-mokṣa-purīs-sapta-purī). [disputed]
