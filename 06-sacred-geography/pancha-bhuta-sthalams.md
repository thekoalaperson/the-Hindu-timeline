# Pañcha Bhūta Sthalams — the Five-Element Shiva Temples

> The five temples where Shiva is worshipped as the *linga* of one of the five gross elements (*pañcha mahābhūta*): **earth (pṛthvī), water (āpas/jala/nīr), fire (agni/tējas), air (vāyu), and ether/space (ākāśa)**. Together they map the elemental cosmos onto sacred South-Indian geography — four in Tamil Nadu, one in Andhra Pradesh. All five are *svayambhu*-grade Saiva shrines glorified in the 7th-century **Tēvāram** and counted among the **[275 Pāḍal Petṟa Sthalams](./275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md)**.
>

The doctrine of the *pañcha bhūta sthalams* (Tamil *aimbūta talaṅkaḷ*) holds that the formless Shiva concretises in each shrine as the *liṅga* of one element, so that a pilgrim circuiting all five worships the whole material creation reabsorbed into Shiva. The grouping is associated with the Tamil Saiva-Siddhanta tradition and the Nayanmar saints; **Tirumūlar's *Tirumantiram*** and the **Tēvāram** of Appar, Campantar, and Cuntarar are the earliest hymnal witnesses, with later **sthala-purāṇas** (temple-mythologies, mostly medieval Sanskrit *māhātmyas* and their Tamil renderings) supplying the element-legends below.

## The Five Element-Lingas at a Glance

| Element | Tamil | Temple / Deity | Town (State) | Manifestation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Earth (pṛthvī) | *maṇ* | Ekambareswarar | Kanchipuram (TN) | sand *liṅga* (not bathed directly) |
| Water (āpas) | *nīr* | Jambukeswarar | Thiruvanaikaval / Trichy (TN) | perennial spring under the *liṅga* |
| Fire (agni) | *tī* | Arunachaleswarar | Tiruvannamalai (TN) | the fire-hill / pillar of flame |
| Air (vāyu) | *kāṟṟu* | Srikalahasti | Srikalahasti (AP) | a flame/lamp that flickers in still air |
| Ether (ākāśa) | *veḷi* | Thillai Nataraja | Chidambaram (TN) | empty space — the *Chidambara Rahasyam* |

## 1. Pṛthvī (Earth) — Ekambareswarar, Kanchipuram

- **Element-form** - Shiva as **Pṛthvī Liṅga**, the earth-linga, worshipped as **Ekambareswarar** ("Lord of the one mango tree," *eka-amra-nātha*); consort **Kamakshi/Elavarkuzhali**. The largest and oldest of Kanchi's Shiva temples. (Ekambareswarar sthala-purāṇa; Tēvāram.)
- **Physical manifestation** - The *liṅga* is fashioned of **sand/soil (*maṇ*)** and is therefore **never given direct *abhiṣeka*** — water is offered only to a surrounding channel or by a *kalaśa* held above, lest the earth-linga dissolve. This abstention is the living ritual proof of the earth element. (Temple practice; Wikipedia, *Pancha Bhuta Sthalam*.)
- **Sthala-purāṇa — Parvati's penance** - **Parvati**, in play, covered Shiva's eyes, plunging the cosmos into darkness; as penance she descended to Kanchi and worshipped a *liṅga* she moulded from sand under a mango tree by the **Vegavati (Kampā) river**. (Ekambareswarar māhātmya.)
  - **The flood-embrace** - To test her, Shiva loosed a flood (the river / the Ganga from his locks) to wash away the sand-linga; Parvati **embraced the linga with her whole body**, shielding it at the cost of her life — the soft clay still bears the imprint of her bangles and breast. Pleased, Shiva married her here. (Ekambareswarar māhātmya.)
- **Sthala-vṛkṣa — the mango tree** - The temple's sacred tree is a mango said to be **~3,500 years old**, whose four branches yield four kinds of mango (sweet/sour etc.), allegorised as the four **Vedas** or four **yugas**; Parvati did her *tapas* beneath it, and the deity's name "Lord of the one mango" derives from it. [folk dating] (Temple tradition; *South First*, *Sannidhi*.)
- **Cross-link** - As a *bhūmi/sthāvara* shrine it pairs naturally with the earth-bound symbolism of the *liṅga* itself; see [Sacred Flora & Fauna](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/sacred-flora-and-fauna.md) for the *sthala-vṛkṣa* concept.

## 2. Āpas (Water) — Jambukeswarar, Thiruvanaikaval

- **Element-form** - Shiva as **Appu Liṅga** (*āp/jala* = water), worshipped as **Jambukeswarar** (Lord of the *jambu* / java-plum tree); consort **Akhilāṇḍeśvarī** ("Mistress of all the worlds," *akhila-aṇḍa*). Near Tiruchirappalli, on the Kaveri. (Jambukeswarar sthala-purāṇa; Wikipedia.)
- **Physical manifestation** - Beneath the *liṅga* in the *Akhilāṇḍeśvara* sanctum a **perennial underground spring** wells up so that the *liṅga* sits in water year-round; priests must bail it out daily, and the spot is held to be fed by an unseen **subterranean Kaveri**. (Temple report; Wikipedia.)
- **Sthala-purāṇa — the spider and the elephant** - Two Shiva-gaṇas, **Mālyavān and Puṣpadanta**, quarrelled and cursed each other to be reborn on earth as a **spider** and an **elephant**. Both worshipped the *jambu*-tree linga: the **elephant** bathed it daily with Kaveri water carried in its trunk; the **spider** spun a canopy of web over it to keep leaves and sun off. (Jambukeswarar māhātmya.)
  - **The fatal rivalry** - The elephant, mistaking the web for filth, tore it away each day; the enraged spider crawled into the elephant's trunk and bit it — both died. Moved, Shiva (Jambukeswara) freed them from the curse; the spider was reborn as **Kōccengat Chōḻa**, a Chola king who built shrines with elephant-proof high doorways. (Jambukeswarar māhātmya; cf. Nayanmar lore.)
  - *Variant (place-name etymology):* because an **elephant** (*āṉai*) worshipped Shiva in a **forest** (*kā*), the site is *Tiru-āṉai-kā* → **Thiruvanaikaval / Thiruvanaikoil**. The *jambu* tree growing over the linga also names the deity.
- **The Akhilāṇḍeśvarī upadeśa** - Shiva taught the fierce goddess **Akhilāṇḍeśvarī** *Śiva-jñāna* here; she stands **facing east** as disciple, the linga **facing west** as guru — uniquely the two main sanctums face one another. Adi Shankara is said to have pacified her ferocity by installing **tāṭaṅka** (ear-ornaments) and a Sri-Chakra. [late text] (Temple tradition.)
- **Cross-link** - For the goddess-as-cosmos motif see [Trimurti & Tridevi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).

## 3. Agni (Fire) — Arunachaleswarar, Tiruvannamalai

- **Element-form** - Shiva as **Agni Liṅga** / *tēja-liṅga*, worshipped as **Arunachaleswarar / Aṇṇāmalaiyār**; consort **Apītakuchāmbāḷ / Uṇṇāmulai**. The shrine sits at the foot of the **Arunachala hill**, which *is* the fire-element manifestation. (Arunachaleswarar sthala-purāṇa; Wikipedia.)
- **Physical manifestation** - The element is embodied not in a dissolving linga but in the **hill itself as a frozen pillar of fire (jyoti)**; the hill *Aruṇa-acala* ("red mountain") is treated as Shiva's *agni* body, and a perpetual flame burns in worship. (Temple tradition.)
- **Sthala-purāṇa — Liṅgodbhava (the pillar of light)** - When **Brahma** and **Vishnu** disputed who was supreme, Shiva appeared as an **infinite column of fire** whose top and bottom neither god could find — Vishnu as the boar **Varaha** dug downward, Brahma as a swan flew up — and the contest revealed Shiva as the unbounded source. The fiery column cooled into the **Arunachala hill** and a *liṅga* at its base. (Arunachaleswarar / Skanda Purāṇa māhātmya; the same *Liṅgodbhava* myth that frames the [12 Jyotirlingas](./12-jyotirlingas.md).)
  - *Variant (Parvati's penance):* Parvati again covered Shiva's eyes (cosmic darkness); sent to Tiruvannamalai for penance, she merged as the left half of Shiva — the **Ardhanārīśvara** origin — after Shiva manifested as the hill of light.
- **Karthigai Deepam** - On the *Karttikai* full-moon (Nov–Dec) a giant **beacon (Bharani Deepam / Maha Deepam)** of ghee is lit atop Arunachala, re-enacting the original pillar of fire; visible for miles, it is one of South India's greatest fire-festivals. (Temple festival; Wikipedia.)
- **Girivalam** - Pilgrims **circumambulate the hill** on foot — *giri-valam*, ~14 km — especially on full-moon nights, the hill being worshipped as the linga itself; the practice is held to burn sins and grant liberation. (Temple tradition.) Associated with the modern sage **Ramana Maharshi**, who held Arunachala to be Shiva embodied. [folk/modern]
- **Cross-link** - The *Liṅgodbhava* fire-pillar is shared with the [Jyotirlingas](./12-jyotirlingas.md) origin myth; the fire element also frames Shiva's destroyer aspect.

## 4. Vāyu (Air) — Srikalahasti, Andhra Pradesh

- **Element-form** - Shiva as **Vāyu Liṅga**, worshipped as **Srikalahasteeswara**; consort **Jñānaprasūnāmbikā / Gnana Prasunamba**. On the Swarnamukhi river in Chittoor district, AP — the one *bhūta sthalam* outside Tamil Nadu. (Srikalahasti sthala-purāṇa; Wikipedia.)
- **Physical manifestation** - The white linga is **never touched by priests** (worshipped from a distance); the air-element is shown by a **flame/oil-lamp inside the sealed inner sanctum that visibly flickers and sways though no draught reaches it**. The linga is said to have been formed by Vāyu solidifying camphor. (Temple report; *indianmandirs*.)
- **Sthala-purāṇa — Śrī-Kāḷa-Hasti (spider-snake-elephant)** - The name encodes its three primordial devotees: **Śrī = spider, Kāḷa = serpent, Hasti = elephant**. (Srikalahasti māhātmya.)
  - **The spider (*śrī*)** spun a protective canopy over the linga; a flame burnt it, and on rebuilding it the spider attained liberation. **The serpent (*kāḷa*)** brought jewels from the Naga-world to adorn the linga. **The elephant (*hasti*)** brought water and bilva leaves to bathe it. Their daily worship clashed — the elephant washed away the serpent's jewels and the spider's web — and all attained *mokṣa*; their names fused into the town's. (Srikalahasti māhātmya.)
- **Kannappa Nayanar — the hunter-saint** - The temple's central devotee-legend: **Kaṇṇappa** (born **Tiṇṇaṉ**), a tribal **hunter** of the Bedar/forest folk, worshipped the linga in his own untaught way — offering **water carried in his mouth**, **flowers worn in his hair**, and **roasted game-meat**, and wearing his sandals into the sanctum — unorthodox acts that nonetheless flowed from pure *bhakti*. (Periya Purāṇa / Tēvāram tradition; one of the **63 Nayanmars**.)
  - **The eye-offering** - To test him, Shiva made the linga's **eye bleed**. Kaṇṇappa **gouged out his own eye** and pressed it onto the linga to stop the bleeding; when the second eye bled, he set his foot on the linga to mark the spot (being about to go blind) and began to pluck out his **remaining eye**. Shiva seized his hand, restored both eyes, and granted liberation — naming him **Kaṇṇappa** ("he who gave his eyes"). The most celebrated proof in Saiva lore that devotion outranks ritual correctness. (Periya Purāṇa, *Kaṇṇappa Nāyaṉār Purāṇam*.)
- **Cross-link** - Kannappa is a touchstone of the *bhakti* over *vidhi* (ritual rule) debate; see [Sacred Symbols & Emblems](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md) for the linga as form.

## 5. Ākāśa (Ether / Space) — Thillai Nataraja, Chidambaram

- **Element-form** - Shiva as **Ākāśa Liṅga** — the **space/ether element**, worshipped at **Thillai (Chidambaram)** as **Nataraja**, Lord of the Cosmic Dance; consort **Śivakāmasundarī**. *Chit-ambaram* = "atmosphere/sky of consciousness." Primary shrine of the [275 Pāḍal Petṟa Sthalams](./275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md). (Chidambaram māhātmya; Wikipedia, *Nataraja Temple*.)
- **Physical manifestation — the Chidambara Rahasyam** - Behind a black curtain (the *māyā* veil, hung with golden *bilva*-leaf strings) in the inner sanctum there is **no image at all — only empty space**, the **Chidambara Rahasyam** ("Secret of Chidambaram"). When the curtain is drawn the devotee "sees" the formless **Ākāśa Liṅga**: Shiva as pure space/consciousness, present yet imageless. This is the most literal of the five element-manifestations. (Temple ritual; *Grokipedia*, *Brhat*.)
  - **The three bodies of Shiva at Chidambaram:** (1) **with form (*sakala*)** — the Nataraja icon; (2) **form-and-formless (*sakala-niṣkala*)** — the **Chandramaulīśvara** crystal *sphaṭika* linga used in processions; (3) **formless (*niṣkala*)** — the empty space of the *Rahasyam*. (Temple theology.)
- **Sthala-purāṇa — Patañjali & Vyāghrapāda** - The sages **Patañjali** (born of/seated on the serpent Adisesha) and **Vyāghrapāda** ("tiger-foot," given tiger's claws to climb trees for unblemished flowers) did penance in the **Thillai forest** longing to witness Shiva's dance. (Chidambara Māhātmya.)
- **The Ānanda Tāṇḍava (Cosmic Dance)** - For them — and after a dance-contest in which Shiva defeated the goddess **Kālī** by raising his foot in the *ūrdhva-tāṇḍava* (a posture modesty forbade her to copy) — Shiva performed the **Ānanda Tāṇḍava**, the **Dance of Bliss**, in the golden hall **Cit-sabhā / Pon-ambalam**. The dance enacts the five cosmic acts (*pañca-kṛtya*): creation, preservation, destruction, concealment (*tirobhāva*), and grace (*anugraha*). The Nataraja icon here is the canonical form of the dancing Shiva. (Chidambara Māhātmya; Tirumūlar's *Tirumantiram*.)
- **Govindaraja — Vishnu in the Saiva sanctum** - Uniquely, a reclining **Vishnu, Govindarāja Perumāḷ**, shares the inner sanctum beside Nataraja, making Chidambaram simultaneously a **Saiva shrine and one of the 108 [Divya Desams](./108-divya-desams.md)** [disputed]. The image was reportedly removed/thrown into the sea under a Saiva ruler and later reinstated — a focal point of historic Saiva–Vaishnava friction. (Govindaraja Perumal sthala history; Wikipedia.)
- **The Dīkṣitars** - The temple is run by the hereditary **Podu Dīkṣitar** community (the "Thillai 3,000"), tuft worn in front, who alone perform the worship and preserved the **Tēvāram** hymns later recovered by **Raja Raja Chola I**. (Temple administration; Wikipedia.)
- **Cross-link** - The **Nataraja** dance-icon, the *Ānanda Tāṇḍava*, and the five *pañca-kṛtya* acts belong to the arts/iconography corpus: see [Arts, Symbols & Artifacts (Section)](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/README.md) and [Sacred Symbols & Emblems](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/sacred-symbols-and-emblems.md). ## Notes on Scope & Dating (two clocks)

- **Scriptural clock** - The element-legends are placed in mythic/Puranic time: the *Liṅgodbhava* (Tiruvannamalai) and the Parvati-penance myths (Kanchi, Tiruvannamalai) are framed as pre-/proto-creation events outside ordinary yuga reckoning; the Nayanmar saints (Kannappa) and the dance-witnessing sages belong to legendary Saiva history.
- **Academic clock** - The temples as built structures are largely **Pallava–Chola** and later (c. 7th–12th c. CE onward); the *pañcha bhūta* **grouping as a fivefold set** is a medieval Tamil Saiva-Siddhanta systematisation, post-dating the individual shrines. [scholarly] The earliest hymnal references are the 7th-c. **Tēvāram** and **Tirumantiram**; the formal "five elements" framing is later.
- **Variant policy note** - The element-assignment of the five is stable across sources; the *legends* attached to each (especially the spider-elephant story, which recurs at both Thiruvanaikaval and Srikalahasti) circulate in multiple sthala-purāṇa recensions and are catalogued above as attributed.
