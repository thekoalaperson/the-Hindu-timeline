# Lingarāja Temple, Bhubaneswar — Ekāmra Kṣetra

> The pre-eminent Śaiva temple of Odisha and the apogee of **Kaliṅga (Nāgara) temple architecture** (~11th c. CE) — its presiding deity **Tribhuvaneśvara** ("Lord of the Three Worlds"), a unique **Harihara** liṅga worshipped at once as **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** and **[Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** (bilva *and* tulasī), the third leg of the Odisha triad beside [Jagannātha Purī](jagannath-puri-purushottama-kshetra.md) and Konārk.
>

**Ekāmra Kṣetra** — the old sacred name of the temple-city **Bhubaneswar** ("Tribhuvaneśvara" → Bhubaneśwar) — is the "field of the one mango (*eka-āmra*)," a Śaiva *kṣetra* glorified in its own *sthala-purāṇa*, the **Ekāmra Purāṇa / Ekāmra-kṣetra-māhātmya**, and in the **Ekāmra Khaṇḍa** of the *Skanda Purāṇa*. As with [Purī](jagannath-puri-purushottama-kshetra.md), what follows separates the **legend-layer** (māhātmya / "scriptural clock") from the **historical layer** ("academic clock") wherever they diverge. See the general treatment of this genre in [Sthala-Purāṇas of Major Temples](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md).

---

## I. The Deity — Tribhuvaneśvara / Harihara

- **Tribhuvaneśvara ("Lord of the Three Worlds")** — the presiding liṅga is a *svayambhū* (self-manifest) form of Śiva, but **uniquely shared with Viṣṇu**: it is worshipped as **Harihara** (Śiva-Viṣṇu fused in one icon), receiving **bilva** leaves (Śaiva) *and* **tulasī** (Vaiṣṇava) together, with *bhoga* offerings prepared and shared in tandem with [Jagannātha](jagannath-puri-purushottama-kshetra.md) of Purī. (Ekāmra Purāṇa; Brahma Purāṇa) [regional]
  - *Variant (the half-and-half liṅga) [folk]:* local tradition describes the liṅga as divided — one half Hari, one half Hara — so that Vaiṣṇavas and Śaivas each claim it; the Harihara theology resolves a real sectarian overlay onto a Śaiva foundation.
  - *Variant (Kṛttivāsa naming) [regional]:* in some recensions the lord here is styled **Kṛttivāsa** ("clad in the [elephant/tiger] hide"), the Śaiva epithet, the māhātmya's name for the swayambhū liṅga before it became Tribhuvaneśvara.

## II. Ekāmra Kṣetra — the One-Mango Field (legend)

- **The single mango tree** — the *kṣetra* takes its name from a primordial **mango tree (eka-āmra)** under which Śiva is said to have dwelt with Pārvatī; the whole field around the **Bindusāgara** tank is sacralized as the lord's own forest-abode, denser with shrines than any other (tradition counts thousands of liṅgas, the "Hundred Hundred" of Ekāmra). (Ekāmra Purāṇa / Ekāmra-kṣetra-māhātmya) [late text]
- **Pārvatī as Gaurī and the demons Kirti & Vāsa** — in the māhātmya's foundation episode **Pārvatī**, wandering the field, is harassed by two *asuras*, **Kṛti and Vāsa** (var. **Kirti–Basa**); she slays them — and from the slaying / from the lord's "garment" (*vāsa*) the epithet **Kṛttivāsa** is etymologized for the Ekāmra liṅga. (Ekāmra Purāṇa) [folk]
- **The Bindusāgara ("ocean of drops")** — the great temple-tank beside the shrine is said to gather a **drop (bindu) of every sacred *tīrtha*** of India, so that a bath here equals all pilgrimages; its water is used for the deity's ritual baths. (Ekāmra-kṣetra-māhātmya) [regional]
- **Pārvatī's thirst and the cowherd wells** — a popular *sthala* episode: a thirsty Pārvatī (in disguise) is helped — or in another telling slays the demons after — and the field's sacred wells / the **Gaṅgā-Yamunā** spring within the compound are etiologized from this. (Ekāmra Purāṇa) [folk]

## III. The Temple-City Sthala Tradition

- **Bhubaneswar as a temple-city** — Ekāmra Kṣetra is not a single shrine but a **dense temple-cluster** around the Bindusāgara — **Parśurāmeśvara** (7th c.), **Muktēśvara** (10th c., the "gem of Kaliṅga architecture"), **Rājarāṇī**, **Brahmeśvara**, and others — with Lingarāja the culminating monument, so the *entire city* is the *kṣetra* (compare the city-as-kṣetra logic of [Kāśī](kashi-varanasi.md) and Madurai). (sthala tradition; academic) [scholarly]
- **The four-fold Lingarāja complex** — the temple proper is built in the mature Kaliṅga sequence of four attached halls along one axis: **Vimāna / Deula** (the towering sanctum, ~55 m *rekha-deula* spire), **Jagamohana** (assembly porch), **Naṭa-mandira** (dance hall), and **Bhoga-maṇḍapa** (offering hall) — the last two added later, the canonical Odishan four-chamber plan. (academic) [scholarly]
- **The consort Bhuvaneśvarī / Gopālinī** — the goddess of the field is **Bhuvaneśvarī** (Pārvatī), with a separate **Pārvatī temple** in the compound; the annual **Rukuṇā Rath Yātrā** (Aśokāṣṭamī, Caitra) carries Lingarāja as **Lord Liṅgarāja / Rāmeśvara** in chariot to the **Rāmeśvara temple**, an Odishan parallel to Purī's [Ratha Yātrā](jagannath-puri-purushottama-kshetra.md). [regional]
- **Pāśupata custodianship** — the Ekāmra cult sits within the early-medieval **Pāśupata / Lākulīśa Śaiva** sphere that dominated Odisha before the Vaiṣṇava ascendancy of Purī; the *māhātmya*-tradition and the Harihara compromise reflect this Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava negotiation across the Odisha triad (see [Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md)). [scholarly]

## IV. The Krittivāsa / Swayambhū Liṅga Legend

- **The self-manifest liṅga** — the Lingarāja liṅga is held to be **svayambhū** (uncarved, self-arisen), neither installed nor consecrated by a human hand — the highest class of liṅga, the same theological claim made for the **[Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md)** (though Ekāmra is *not* counted among the canonical twelve). (Ekāmra Purāṇa) [regional]
- **Why Hari shares Hara's seat** — the māhātmya explains the Harihara worship by a divine compact: when Viṣṇu (or the field's Vaiṣṇava claim) presses for a share, the swayambhū Śiva-liṅga of Kṛttivāsa is declared **Tribhuvaneśvara**, lord of all three worlds and both sects, so that **bilva and tulasī** are offered together and the *bhoga* circulates between Bhubaneswar and [Purī](jagannath-puri-purushottama-kshetra.md). [folk]

## V. Two Clocks — Legend vs History

- **Scriptural clock** — the **Ekāmra Purāṇa / Ekāmra-kṣetra-māhātmya** and **Skanda Purāṇa (Ekāmra Khaṇḍa)** set the one-mango field, the Kṛti–Vāsa slaying, the swayambhū Kṛttivāsa liṅga, and the Bindusāgara drop-ocean in **undatable māhātmya time** — Śiva's own eternal forest-abode. [late text]
- **Academic clock** — the standing **Lingarāja temple** is dated to **c. mid-11th c. CE**, begun under the **Somavaṃśī (Keśarī)** kings — tradition credits **Yayāti Keśarī** / the line associated with **Jajāti** — and elaborated under the **Eastern Gaṅgas**; it is the **mature masterpiece of the Kaliṅga (Nāgara) idiom**, the school that culminates next in the **Jagannātha temple of Purī (12th c.)** and the **Konārk Sun temple (13th c.)**, the three together defining classical Odishan architecture. The **Madala Pañji** (Purī temple chronicle) tradition supplies the regnal/legendary attributions; epigraphy and style supply the dating. (academic; Madala Pañji) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (founding dynasty) [disputed]:* the foundation is variously assigned to the **Somavaṃśīs** (most scholars, 11th c.) or pushed earlier by local chronicle to a **Keśarī** king of the *Madala Pañji* — a chronicle-vs-epigraphy discrepancy, the recurring "two clocks" problem of Odishan temple history.

## Sources

- **Ekāmra Purāṇa / Ekāmra-kṣetra-māhātmya** — the *sthala-purāṇa* of Ekāmra Kṣetra: the one-mango field, Pārvatī and the demons Kṛti–Vāsa, the Kṛttivāsa swayambhū liṅga, the Bindusāgara tank, Tribhuvaneśvara/Harihara worship.
- **Skanda Purāṇa**, *Ekāmra Khaṇḍa* — the Sanskrit māhātmya stratum of the Bhubaneswar *kṣetra* and its liṅga-cult.
- **Brahma Purāṇa** — Śaiva *kṣetra* and Harihara material associated with the Odisha coast (shared with the [Purī](jagannath-puri-purushottama-kshetra.md) māhātmya layer).
- **Madala Pañji** (Purī temple chronicle) — regnal/legendary attributions of the foundation (Keśarī / Somavaṃśī kings).
- **Academic / historical**: Somavaṃśī–Eastern Gaṅga patronage and the c. mid-11th-c. dating; Kaliṅga (Nāgara) architecture and the Bhubaneswar temple-cluster (Parśurāmeśvara, Muktēśvara, Rājarāṇī, Brahmeśvara); the Odisha triad with [Jagannātha Purī](jagannath-puri-purushottama-kshetra.md) and Konārk — see also [Sthala-Purāṇas of Major Temples](sthala-puranas-major-temples.md) and [Temple Networks & Circuits](temple-networks-and-circuits.md).
