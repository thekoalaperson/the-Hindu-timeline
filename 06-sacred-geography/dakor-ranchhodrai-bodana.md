# Dākor Raṇchhoḍrāī — Bodāṇā and the Idol that Left Dvārakā

> The best-loved *bhakti* legend of Gujarat: at **Ḍākor** (Kheḍā district), on the **Gomatī** tank of the Central Gujarat plain, the aged devotee **Bodāṇā** is rewarded when **Kṛṣṇa-Raṇchhoḍrāī climbs into his bullock-cart and leaves Dvārakā** (trad. **Vikrama Saṃvat 1212 / 1156 CE**); the pursuing Dvārakā priests are made to weigh the god against gold, and the black-stone image balances **nothing heavier than Gaṅgābāī's nose-ring**. The counterpart node to drowned [Dvārakā](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md) — the town that inherited its idol.

**Names of the kṣetra** — the town **Ḍākor** derives from the older **Ḍaṅkapura / Ḍaṅkanātha**, named for the ṛṣi **Ḍaṅka (Ḍaṅk muni)** who is said to have done penance to Śiva here; the Śaiva shrine **Ḍaṅkanātha Mahādeva** survives as the sthala's elder layer, so the site is a **Śaiva ground that became a Vaiṣṇava kṣetra**. The god is **Raṇchhoḍrāī** (*Raṇa-chhoḍa-rāya*, "the lord who quit the battlefield") — the same **Raṇacchoḍa** epithet Kṛṣṇa earned by abandoning [Mathurā](mathura-vrindavan-braj.md) for the western sea when hounded by **Jarāsandha** and **Kālayavana** (see [Dvārakā §I](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md)). Devotees call the image **Ḍākornāth** and **Ṭhākorjī**. The whole cycle is transmitted as the **Ḍākor Māhātmya** / **Raṇchhoḍrāī sthala-purāṇa**, a Gujarati regional tradition rather than a fixed Sanskrit recension. (*Ḍākor Māhātmya*; Wikipedia, "Dakor" / "Ranchhodrai Temple") [regional] [folk]

---

## I. The two clocks — Kṛṣṇa's Dvāpara vs. Bodāṇā's Kali

- **The god of the legend is Dvāpara-old** — *Raṇchhoḍrāī* is Kṛṣṇa of [Dvārakā](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md), whose life and the drowning of his city are fixed by scripture to the **Dvāpara–Kali hinge (3102 BCE)**; the image itself is claimed in tradition to be the very **Dvārakā mūrti** worshipped since Kṛṣṇa's own age. (*Bhāgavata* 10.50–52; Purāṇic chronology; see [Yuga cycle](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md))
- **The transfer belongs to documented Kali time** — the *event* that gives Ḍākor its idol — Bodāṇā's cart-journey — is dated by tradition to **V.S. 1212 = 1156 CE**, squarely in the historical [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md); the present temple is later still (1772 CE). The corpus keeps the two frames apart: a **Dvāpara god** relocated by a **12th-century CE** miracle, housed in an **18th-century** building. [regional] [scholarly]

---

## II. Bodāṇā — the aged devotee and his six-monthly yātrā

- **Who Bodāṇā was** — **Bodāṇā** (Bodāṇā Rāṇā), remembered as a **Rājpūt** cultivator of Ḍākor and a lifelong Kṛṣṇa-*bhakta*, the legend's human hero; his wife is **Gaṅgābāī**. His devotion is the whole engine of the tale — a poor, ordinary householder whose love outweighs priestly custody. (*Ḍākor Māhātmya*) [regional]
- **The tulsī pilgrimage** — from Ḍākor to [Dvārakā](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md) is the width of Gujarat; Bodāṇā vowed to make the journey **twice a year** (every **six months**, on the *Kārtika* and *Phālguna* full moons in the usual telling), each time carrying fresh **tulsī (holy basil)** to lay at Raṇchhoḍrāī's feet — a discipline he is said to have kept for decades, reputedly from youth into his **seventies**. (*Ḍākor Māhātmya*) [folk]
- **The god's mercy on old age** — as Bodāṇā grew too frail for the road, **Kṛṣṇa was moved by his constancy** and resolved to spare him the miles — the pivot from a devotee climbing to his god to a **god descending to his devotee**, the moral center of the sthala-purāṇa. (*Ḍākor Māhātmya*) [regional]

## III. The idol leaves Dvārakā — Kṛṣṇa in the bullock-cart (1156 CE)

- **"Bring a cart"** — on his last visit Bodāṇā is told by Raṇchhoḍrāī to **return with a bullock-cart (*gāḍā/bail-gāḍī*)**, and that the Lord will **come to live at Ḍākor** so the old man need travel no more; on the appointed night the god's image is found waiting, and Kṛṣṇa **himself mounts the cart** to be driven east. (*Ḍākor Māhātmya*) [regional] [folk]
- **The night flight** — Bodāṇā carries the mūrti out of Dvārakā under cover of dark, the cart rolling across Saurāṣṭra toward the Gomatī tank of Ḍākor — the miraculous **removal of the Dvārakā idol**, and the reason the god's name follows him there. (*Ḍākor Māhātmya*)
- **Hiding the god in the Gomatī** — when the theft is discovered and pursuit nears, Kṛṣṇa instructs Bodāṇā to **immerse / conceal the image in the Gomatī lake** at Ḍākor (a bath-tank Bodāṇā is said to have dug), so the god is literally submerged to escape recapture — the origin of the sacred **Gomatī Talāva** where pilgrims still bathe before *darśana*. (*Ḍākor Māhātmya*) [folk]
  - *Variant (buried, not sunk):* other tellings have the idol **buried in a field or hidden in Bodāṇā's house** rather than immersed, before the priests arrive. [regional]

---

## IV. The weighing — the god no heavier than a nose-ring

- **The Gugli priests give chase** — the hereditary **Gugli Brāhmaṇa** custodians of the Dvārakā temple, finding their deity gone, **pursue Bodāṇā to Ḍākor** and demand the image back — the legend's tension between **institutional ownership** and **personal love**. (*Ḍākor Māhātmya*) [regional]
- **Ransom by weight of gold** — Kṛṣṇa (in the standard telling, appearing in a **dream**) resolves it: the priests may reclaim the idol **only if Bodāṇā cannot match its weight in gold** — otherwise they must accept the gold and leave the god at Ḍākor. Bodāṇā, being poor, has almost nothing to offer. (*Ḍākor Māhātmya*) [folk]
- **Gaṅgābāī's nose-ring** — his wife **Gaṅgābāī** surrenders her one ornament, a small gold **nose-ring (*nath / nathnī*)**; placed on the scale against the black-stone image, **the god weighs no more than the nose-ring** — the mūrti makes itself light, the pans balance, and the priests are bound by their own bargain to depart with only the tiny ring's weight of gold. This weighing is the emotional climax of the Ḍākor cult: divinity that answers **bhakti, not gold**. (*Ḍākor Māhātmya*) [regional] [folk]
  - *Variant (the spear and Bodāṇā's death):* in a darker strand the frustrated Guglis **strike Bodāṇā with a spear/lance** and he dies of the wound (his blood marking the god's foot in some tellings) — a martyrdom motif absent from the gentler "gold-weighing" version; the two are told side by side. [folk] [disputed]

## V. The replacement idol & the Dvārakā–Ḍākor rivalry

- **A second Raṇchhoḍrāī promised** — to console the departing Guglis, Kṛṣṇa tells them they will **find another image in the Gomatī creek at Dvārakā** after some days; the idol worshipped on the Saurāṣṭra coast today is, in Ḍākor's telling, **this replacement**, while the **original** stands at Ḍākor — the sthala-purāṇa's bold claim of primacy over the far more famous coastal shrine. (*Ḍākor Māhātmya*) [regional] [disputed]
- **A contested inheritance** — the two centres advance rival identifications: Ḍākor holds its image to be the **Dvāpara-original** carried off by Bodāṇā, while Dvārakā's [Dvārakādhīśa / Jagat Mandir](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md) tradition keeps its own continuity through **Vajranābha's** re-founding after the drowning; the "which idol is the true one" dispute parallels the older [which-Dvārakā](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md) debate. [disputed]
- **One of the Pañca-Dvārakā** — Ḍākor is counted the **third station of the Pañca-Dvārakā** Kṛṣṇa circuit (Dvārakā · Beṭ Dvārakā · **Raṇchhoḍrāī-jī, Ḍākor** · Śrīnāthjī, Nāthadvārā · Dvārakādhīśa, Kāṅkrolī), the legend of the migrated idol being exactly what earns the inland town its place among the western Kṛṣṇa dhāms. (Vaiṣṇava tradition; see [Dvārakā §VI](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md)) [regional]
- **Bodāṇā's line honoured** — local tradition preserves **hereditary rights and honours** for Bodāṇā's descendants (the **Bhagat** family of Ḍākor) in the deity's service — the devotee written permanently into the ritual life of the shrine he founded. [folk] [regional]

## VI. The shrine, its cult, and the datable clock

- **The 1772 CE temple** — the present large white **Raṇchhoḍrāī temple**, on the Gomatī tank, was **built in 1772 CE** by the Maratha noble **Goplarāv Jagannāth Tāmbvekar** (Tāmbekar) of the Peshwa establishment, replacing an earlier modest shrine — the **historical** anchor beneath the timeless legend. The image is the four-armed black-stone **Raṇchhoḍrāī**, richly adorned and dressed in changing costumes through the day. (Wikipedia, "Ranchhodrai Temple, Dakor") [scholarly]
- **Gomatī snāna and the tank** — pilgrims first **bathe in the Gomatī Talāva** (the tank into which the idol was said to be immersed) before *darśana* — the myth of the hidden god written into the ritual sequence, as at coastal [Dvārakā's Gomatī](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md). [regional]
- **The Pūrṇimā padayātrā** — Ḍākor's signature living rite is the mass **full-moon (Pūrṇimā) pilgrimage on foot**, drawing huge crowds especially at **Śarad Pūrṇimā** and the **Phālgunī / Holī** and **Kārtikī** full moons — echoing Bodāṇā's own six-monthly Pūrṇimā journeys, now made *to* Ḍākor instead of from it. The temple's *magas* (offerings) and the giant **prasāda** (the famous Ḍākor *sākhaḍī*/*magas* sweets) feed the throngs. (temple tradition) [regional]
- **A Kṛṣṇa-*bhakti* parable, historicised** — the tale belongs to the same family of "god who prefers the poor devotee" legends as the beaten-rice of [Sudāmā at Beṭ Dvārakā](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md) and the [witness-Kṛṣṇa of Sākṣī-Gopāl](sakshi-gopal-witness-krishna.md) — a **mobile, choosing deity** who overrides priestly custody for love; but unlike those, Ḍākor pins its miracle to a **precise year (1156 CE)** and a named couple, giving Gujarat a dated *bhakti* origin-story. [scholarly] [folk]
