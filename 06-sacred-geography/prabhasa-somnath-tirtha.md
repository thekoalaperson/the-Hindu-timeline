# Prabhāsa Tīrtha — The Sarasvatī-Ocean Kṣetra at Somnāth

> **Prabhāsa** (*Prabhāsa-tīrtha*, "the radiant / shining ford") — the foremost **western tīrtha** of the *Mahābhārata* **[Tīrtha-yātrā Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md)** and the subject of a full *Skanda Purāṇa* **khaṇḍa** (the *Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa*), set at the **Sarasvatī–ocean confluence** on the **Saurāṣṭra** coast (modern **Prabhās Pātan / Verāval**, Gujarat). It is the place where the **Moon (Soma/Candra)** was freed of Dakṣa's curse and regained his light — naming the **[Somanātha jyotirliṅga](12-jyotirlingas.md)** — where the vanished **Sarasvatī** resurfaces to reach the sea, and where, at the **Dvāpara–Kali hinge**, **[Kṛṣṇa](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata)** departed the world and the **Yādava** race perished in the *mausala*.
>

**Naming & extent** — *Prabhāsa* ("luminous, splendour-giving") is glossed from the **regained radiance of the moon** worshipped here; the kṣetra is the strip of Saurāṣṭra coast where the **Sarasvatī** river meets the **Arabian Sea (Sindhu-sāgara)**, anchored by the **Somanātha** shrine at Prabhās Pātan and the nearby **Bhālka** and **Dehotsarga (Triveṇī)** sites. The two clocks are kept apart throughout: in *scriptural* time Prabhāsa is a primordial tīrtha sanctified before ordinary yuga-reckoning (Soma's curse) and the stage of the late-Dvāpara Yādava end; in *terrestrial / historical* time it is **Prabhās Pātan / Verāval**, home of the most-destroyed-and-rebuilt temple in India, whose worship runs through the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)**. (*Mbh* Vana Parva; *Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa*; *Bhāgavata* 11.30)

## I. The Moon's curse and the naming of Somanātha

- **Dakṣa's curse on Candra** — **Soma / Candra**, the Moon-god, married **27 daughters of Dakṣa** (the *nakṣatras*) but doted only on **Rohiṇī**; the neglected wives complained, and Dakṣa cursed Candra to **waste away with consumption (*kṣaya / rāja-yakṣmā*)**, dimming the moon and withering the world. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa*; *Śiva Purāṇa*; see [soma-tara-budha](../04-deep-dives/soma-tara-budha.md))
- **Penance at Prabhāsa** — Counselled by the devas (or by Dakṣa himself), Soma came to **Prabhāsa-tīrtha** and performed severe austerities to **Śiva**, installing and worshipping a **liṅga** at the Sarasvatī–sea confluence. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa*)
- **Śiva tempers the curse** — Śiva could not annul Dakṣa's word but **halved its force**: the moon would **wane for a fortnight and wax for a fortnight**, dying and reborn each month — the aetiology of the lunar phases. Restored to radiance, Soma made the place *prabhāsa*, "luminous." (*Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa*; *Śiva Purāṇa*)
- **Somanātha — "Lord of the Moon"** — The liṅga Soma worshipped became **Somanātha**, reckoned the **first (*ādi*)** of the twelve **[jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md)** (*Saurāṣṭre Somanātham*). (*Śiva Purāṇa*; *Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa*)
  - *Variant (temple tradition):* the shrine is said to have been first built of **gold by Soma**, then **silver by Rāvaṇa**, **wood/sandal by Kṛṣṇa**, and **stone by Bhīmadeva** — a four-age genealogy of the temple. (Somnath sthala tradition) [late text][folk]

## II. The Sarasvatī's hidden journey to the sea

- **The vanished river resurfaces** — The **Sarasvatī**, which "disappears" (becomes *antaḥsalilā / vinaśana*) in the Rajasthan desert, is held in the *māhātmya* to flow on unseen and **re-emerge at Prabhāsa to pour into the ocean** — making the confluence a meeting of the holiest lost river with the sea. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 80–82; *Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa*)
- **Sarasvatī's five streams / Triveṇī** — At Prabhāsa the Sarasvatī is said to join the **Hiraṇyā** and **Kapilā** in a *triveṇī* (triple confluence) before reaching the sea; the **Triveṇī-saṅgama** ghāṭ here is a primary bathing-tīrtha. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa*; local tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant (Mbh):* the Vana Parva pilgrim-route counts several **Sarasvatī-tīrthas** of the west — *Vinaśana* (where she sinks), *Camasodbheda*, *Śivodbheda*, *Nāgodbheda* (where she resurfaces) — with **Prabhāsa** the crowning sea-mouth tīrtha. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 80–82)
- **Balarāma's tīrtha-yātrā** — In the *Śalya Parva*, **Balarāma**, declining to fight at Kurukṣetra, instead makes a **pilgrimage up the Sarasvatī** from its mouth at Prabhāsa to its source, sanctifying the river-line. (*Mbh* Śalya Parva 34–54)

## III. Prabhāsa in the Tīrtha-yātrā Parva

- **First of the western tīrthas** — In the **[Vana Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md)** *Tīrtha-yātrā* sections, the sage **Pulastya** (to Bhīṣma) and **Lomaśa / Dhaumya** (to the Pāṇḍavas) name **Prabhāsa** as the foremost holy site of the **west**, where the gods and **Soma** themselves bathe; a bath here is said to yield the merit of great sacrifices. (*Mbh* Vana Parva, *Tīrtha-yātrā Parva*)
- **Where the devas dwell** — Prabhāsa is praised as **"ever-radiant," frequented by Indra, the Vasus, the Aśvins and Soma** — a tīrtha whose light never fails. (*Mbh* Vana Parva)
- **Linked to the Sarasvatī and the sea** — the parva fixes Prabhāsa's power precisely on the **Sarasvatī meeting the ocean**, twinning river-merit and sea-merit at one ford. (*Mbh* Vana Parva 80–82)

## IV. The Yādava end — the Mausala at Prabhāsa

> The same radiant ford is the scene of the darkest event of the [Mahābhārata](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata)'s close: the self-slaughter of Kṛṣṇa's own clan and his departure from the world, at the very **Dvāpara–Kali hinge** (3102 BCE in scripture-time).

- **The brahmin's curse on the Vṛṣṇis** — Sages (**Viśvāmitra, Kaṇva, Nārada**), mocked at Dvārakā when Yādava youths disguised **Sāmba** as a pregnant woman, cursed that Sāmba would bear an **iron pestle (*musala*)** that would destroy the race. (*Mbh* **Mausala Parva** 1; *Bhāgavata* 11.30)
- **The iron pestle ground to powder** — the pestle, filed down by order of King **Ugrasena** and cast into the sea, washed ashore at Prabhāsa as **reeds (*eraka* grass)** and a residual iron splinter swallowed by a fish, later a hunter's arrow-tip. (*Mbh* Mausala Parva 2–3; *Bhāgavata* 11.30)
- **Evil omens and the migration to Prabhāsa** — beset by portents, the **Yādavas journey to Prabhāsa-tīrtha** to perform expiatory rites, but fall instead to **drink** at Kṛṣṇa's (or fate's) prompting. (*Mbh* Mausala Parva 3; *Bhāgavata* 11.30)
- **The fratricidal slaughter** — drunk and quarrelling, the **Vṛṣṇi–Andhaka** chiefs tear up the **eraka reeds**, which turn to iron clubs in their hands; **Sātyaki, Kṛtavarman, Pradyumna, Aniruddha, Sāmba** and the rest **kill one another** until the clan is annihilated. (*Mbh* Mausala Parva 3–4; *Bhāgavata* 11.30)
- **Balarāma's departure (yogic exit)** — **Balarāma** sits in yoga on the Prabhāsa shore and gives up his body; the serpent **Śeṣa**, of whom he was the *aṃśa*, issues from his mouth and returns to the sea. (*Mbh* Mausala Parva 4; *Bhāgavata* 11.30)

## V. Bhālka & Dehotsarga — Kṛṣṇa's passing

- **The hunter Jarā's arrow (Bhālka Tīrtha)** — Kṛṣṇa, withdrawn to the Prabhāsa forest, lay in meditation with his foot raised; the hunter **Jarā** ("Old Age," held to be **Vālī reborn**), mistaking the reddish sole for a deer's ear/eye, loosed the **iron-tipped arrow** — the last fragment of the pestle — and pierced Kṛṣṇa's foot. The spot is venerated as **Bhālka Tīrtha**. (*Mbh* Mausala Parva 4; *Bhāgavata* 11.30; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 5.37) [regional]
- **Kṛṣṇa's return (Dehotsarga / Triveṇī)** — consoling the grieving Jarā, **Kṛṣṇa cast off his body** and returned to his transcendent abode, ending the **Dvāpara Yuga**. The river-sea Triveṇī site near Prabhāsa is revered as **Dehotsarga** ("the laying-down of the body") / **Bhagavān-Kṛṣṇa-niryāṇa-sthala**. (*Bhāgavata* 11.30–31; *Mbh* Mausala Parva 4) [regional]
- **Arjuna's last rites and the sea swallowing Dvārakā** — **Arjuna** comes, performs the **funeral rites** for the Yādavas and for Vasudeva, and evacuates the survivors; as he leaves, the **ocean reclaims [Dvārakā](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md)**, and his Gāṇḍīva-power fails — the formal close of the heroic age. (*Mbh* Mausala Parva 5–8; *Bhāgavata* 11.31)

## VI. Tīrtha geography & the historical temple

- **A composite kṣetra** — the modern circuit binds the **Somanātha** temple (jyotirliṅga), the **Triveṇī-saṅgama** of Sarasvatī–Hiraṇyā–Kapilā, **Bhālka Tīrtha** (Kṛṣṇa's wounding), and **Dehotsarga / Gītā Mandir** — a single pilgrimage knitting Śaiva and Vaiṣṇava strands. (Prabhās Pātan sthala tradition)
- **One of the Sapta-Mokṣa / tīrtha lists** — Prabhāsa is counted among the great river-sea *saṅgama-tīrthas* and is grouped with Saurāṣṭra's holy nodes alongside the **[Sapta Purī](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md)** and the **[Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)** point at Dvārakā. (Purāṇic tīrtha-lists)
- **The most-rebuilt temple (historical clock)** — the **Somnāth** shrine was **repeatedly destroyed and rebuilt**, famously sacked by **Mahmūd of Ghazni in 1025–26 CE**, and reconstructed many times — the present temple consecrated in **1951**. This is the *recorded-history* layer, distinct from the scriptural events above. (see [12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md); [recorded history](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md))
- **The "end-of-land" marker (Bāṇastambha)** — an arrow-pillar at the shore claims an **unobstructed sea-line to the South Pole**, marking Prabhāsa as the western edge of *Bhāratavarṣa*. (Somnath tradition) [folk]
