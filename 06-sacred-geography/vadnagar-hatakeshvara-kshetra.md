# Hāṭakeśvara-kṣetra (Vaḍnagar) — the Nāgara-khaṇḍa sthala and Nāgara-brahmin charter

> On the **Śarmiṣṭhā lake** in the **Ānarta** country of north Gujarat (modern **Vaḍnagar**, Mehsāṇā dist.) — the sacred ground **Hāṭakeśvara-kṣetra**, whose glory fills the **~279 chapters of the Nāgara-khaṇḍa** (Book 6 of the *Skanda Purāṇa*), the single largest *sthala-māhātmya* devoted to one town in the whole Purāṇa. Here a Śiva-liṅga rises out of the **golden nether-world (hāṭaka)** of Pātāla, King **Camatkāra** gifts a city to brahmins, and the **Nāgara Brāhmaṇas** of Gujarat receive their origin-charter.

## I. The kṣetra and its many names

- **Hāṭakeśvara-kṣetra** — "the field of **Hāṭakeśvara**," the presiding Śiva-liṅga whose name means *the Lord of hāṭaka* — **hāṭaka** being the special **gold of the nether-world (Pātāla / Bila-svarga)**. The kṣetra is the sacred perimeter of the town of Vaḍnagar, glorified chapter by chapter as a *tīrtha* whose bathing-, gifting-, and death-merit the khaṇḍa never tires of praising. (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Nāgara-khaṇḍa 1) — for the gold itself, see [Bila-svarga & the Pātālas](../00-time-and-cosmology/bila-svarga-and-the-patalas.md). [scholarly]
- **Camatkārapura** — the name most used *inside* the Nāgara-khaṇḍa, after the founder-king **Camatkāra** of Ānarta who gifts the city to brahmins (§IV below). *Camatkāra* = "wonder, marvel." (Nāgara-khaṇḍa) [scholarly]
- **The stack of toponyms** — the same town appears in inscriptions and texts as **Ānartapura** (capital of the Ānarta region), **Ānandapura** ("city of bliss"; the name the Chinese pilgrim **Xuanzang** used in 640 CE), **Skandapura** (after Skanda/Kārttikeya), and **Nāgaraka / Nagara** — from which the resident brahmins take their community name **Nāgara**. Modern **Vaḍnagar** ("great town," *vaḍ* + *nagara*) is the living form. *These are layers of one place; the corpus catalogues them, it does not collapse them.* (Nāgara-khaṇḍa; Harṣola plates 949 CE; Xuanzang) [scholarly] [regional]
- **The "Nāgara" pun** — the community's name is derived scripturally two ways at once, and both are kept: **(a)** *nāgara* = "of the **nagara** (town)," the town-brahmins of Vaḍnagar; **(b)** a thematic tie to the **Nāgas** (serpents) whose Pātāla is the liṅga's source and whom the brahmins learn to subdue (§V). The text exploits the resonance; scholars read the first as the real etymology. [scholarly] [disputed]

## II. The two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic frame** — in the Purāṇa's own reckoning the kṣetra is of **mythic antiquity**: the Hāṭakeśvara liṅga is beginningless (a **cross-yuga Śaiva** presence risen from Pātāla), King Camatkāra belongs to the **Ānarta** line of the early post-Manu age, and the embedded epic legends (Triśaṅku, Paraśurāma's birth, Viśvāmitra) belong to **Tretā Yuga** of the current 28th Mahāyuga, with **Kali** having begun **3102 BCE**. (Nāgara-khaṇḍa) 
- **Academic / historical frame** — as a *text*, the **Nāgara-khaṇḍa is late**: a discrete book **grafted onto the older *Skanda Purāṇa*** and composed in strata **from before the late 13th c. CE to c. the 17th c. CE**. Its allusion to the **Vāghela king Vīsaladeva** (r. c. 1244–1262 CE) organizing Nāgara sub-groups gives an internal *terminus*. It is best described as a **quasi-caste-purāṇa** for the Vaḍnagar Nāgaras — a temple-origin tale fused with a **community charter**. [scholarly] [late text]
- **The kṣetra itself is genuinely ancient** — independent of the text, Vaḍnagar shows **continuous human habitation since roughly the mid-1st millennium BCE** (radiocarbon and excavation give a settlement beginning c. **800 BCE**, Late-Vedic/pre-Buddhist, through seven cultural periods) — one of the oldest continuously inhabited towns in western India. The corpus keeps the **datable town** and the **timeless liṅga** apart. [scholarly]

## III. The Hāṭakeśvara liṅga — risen from Pātāla-gold

- **The liṅga from the golden nether-world** — the founding miracle (Nāgara-khaṇḍa ch. 1, "the birth of the liṅga") is that Hāṭakeśvara's original seat was in **Nāgaloka / Pātāla**, the serpent under-world, and the god **arose from the Pātāla region up to the surface at Vaḍnagar**, where the Nāgaras received him. Because he came from the realm whose gold is *hāṭaka*, he is **Hāṭakeśvara**, "Lord of the nether-gold." (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Nāgara-khaṇḍa 1) [scholarly]
- **Why gold, why serpents** — in Purāṇic cosmology the **Pātālas / Bila-svarga** are the jewelled subterranean worlds of the **Nāgas, Daityas and Dānavas**, paved with hāṭaka-gold; a liṅga surfacing *from there* marks Vaḍnagar as the point where the golden under-world breaks into the middle world. This is the same **hāṭaka** the corpus notes in its Pātāla cosmology, here made a Śaiva epithet. (Nāgara-khaṇḍa 1; cf. [Bila-svarga & the Pātālas](../00-time-and-cosmology/bila-svarga-and-the-patalas.md)) [scholarly] [regional]
- **A svayambhū Śiva, not a Vaiṣṇava kṣetra** — Hāṭakeśvara is a **self-manifest (svayambhū) Śaiva** ground; unlike the Kṛṣṇa kṣetras of the Gujarat seaboard ([Dvārakā](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md), [Ḍākor](dakor-ranchhodrai-bodana.md)), the Nāgara-khaṇḍa's whole devotional weight rests on **Śiva** and his brahmin household — fittingly, since the town's brahmins are said to be Śiva's own creation (§V). Today Hāṭakeśvara remains the tutelary deity of the Nāgaras of Gujarat. (Nāgara-khaṇḍa) [regional]

## IV. Camatkāra and the founding of Camatkārapura

- **The king who gifted a city to brahmins** — the Nāgara-khaṇḍa's civic origin-tale: **King Camatkāra of Ānarta built and endowed a town, giving it to Brāhmaṇas**, and it was named **Camatkārapura** after its royal donor — the *agrahāra* (brahmin-grant town) that becomes Vaḍnagar. The city is thus framed from the outset as a **brahmin foundation**, land held for the twice-born rather than a mere royal capital. (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Nāgara-khaṇḍa) [scholarly]
- **Ānarta the region** — *Ānarta* is the classical name of north Gujarat (the Ānarta *janapada*), remembered in the *vaṃśa* literature as descending from **Ānarta son of Śaryāti**, in Vaivasvata Manu's line; its old capital was **Ānartapura = Vaḍnagar**, before the Yādavas' later seat at [Dvārakā](dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md). Placing Camatkāra "of Ānarta" locates the founding in that deep dynastic past. (cf. [Solar & Lunar dynasties](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md)) [scholarly]
- **A charter of donation, not just a legend** — that the sthala's origin is a **land-grant to brahmins** is exactly what makes the Nāgara-khaṇḍa read as a **charter**: it scripturally underwrites the Nāgaras' hereditary claim to the town and its endowments, in the idiom of the *agrahāra* copper-plate but on a Purāṇic scale. [scholarly]

## V. The Nāgara-brahmin origin charter

- **Brahmins created for Śiva's wedding** — the community's proudest origin-claim: **Śiva created a class of Brāhmaṇas to officiate at his own marriage to Umā/Pārvatī**, and afterward settled them permanently at **Hāṭakeśvara-kṣetra** — making the Nāgaras *the god's own wedding-priests*, the highest possible pedigree. (Nāgara-khaṇḍa, origin cycle; cf. [Śatī, Dakṣa & Pārvatī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md), [Śiva myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)) [regional] [scholarly]
- **The Nāga persecution and Prabhāvadatta** — a second charter-thread turns on conflict with the serpents: the brahmins of Camatkārapura are **harassed by the Nāgas** (the Pātāla serpent-folk beneath the kṣetra). A brahmin, **Prabhāvadatta**, undertakes fierce **penance to Śiva** and is granted the **Nāgara-mantra**, by which the Nāgas can be **subdued** — securing the town for the brahmins. This "mastery over the serpents" episode is what braids the community's name to both *nagara* and *Nāga*. (Nāgara-khaṇḍa; Nāgara Brāhmaṇa tradition) [regional] [folk]
- **The origin proper (ch. ~163) and the eight families (ch. ~199)** — the khaṇḍa gives a dedicated account of the **Nāgara Brāhmaṇas' origin** (around ch. 163) and later enumerates their **families/gotra-groupings** (the "**eight Nāgara families**," around ch. 199) — the text descending from myth into the concrete social taxonomy of a caste it is chartering. (Nāgara-khaṇḍa 163, 199) [scholarly]
- **Historicized sub-divisions [Vīsaladeva]** — later Nāgara tradition (and the text's frame) attributes the **splitting of the Nāgaras into endogamous sub-groups** — the well-known **Vaḍnagarā, Viśalnagarā, Sāṭhodarā, Praśnorā, Citroḍā, Kṛṣṇorā** and others — to a great **sacrifice convened by the Vāghela king Vīsaladeva** in the 13th c. CE, at which brahmins were graded by where they sat or served. This is a **documented-history** overlay on the mythic charter, and dates the received shape of the community. [scholarly] [regional]
- **A "purity" motif** — allied tellings add that **Agni (fire-god) revealed that one among the assembled brahmins was impure**, precipitating tests and the retreat of Prabhāvadatta to the forest — a recurring anxiety-motif by which the charter polices the boundary of who counts as a true Nāgara. *Told beside the wedding-priest and Nāga-mastery threads, not merged with them.* (Nāgara Brāhmaṇa tradition) [folk] [disputed]

## VI. The gazetteer of tīrthas and grafted legends

Like every long *māhātmya*, the Nāgara-khaṇḍa is also a **gazetteer**, hanging famous stories onto local bathing-spots to lend the kṣetra epic prestige:

- **Sarasvatī-tīrtha & Tripuṣkara** — the khaṇḍa glorifies a **Sarasvatī-tīrtha** (ch. ~46) and the **Tripuṣkara** three-pool tīrtha (ch. ~45), tying Vaḍnagar's tanks to the sacred (now-vanished) **Sarasvatī** river-system of the region and to Puṣkara-type pilgrimage merit. (Nāgara-khaṇḍa 45–46) [late text]
- **Triśaṅku's bodily ascent (ch. ~7)** — the Ikṣvāku king **Triśaṅku**, kept out of heaven and hung mid-sky by **Viśvāmitra**, is localized to this kṣetra's *tīrtha*-cycle — a **Tretā-Yuga** Solar-dynasty tale re-homed at Vaḍnagar to advertise the ground's power. (Nāgara-khaṇḍa 7; full tale at [Triśaṅku](../04-deep-dives/trishanku.md)) [late text]
- **The birth of Paraśurāma (ch. ~166)** — the sixth avatāra's **nativity** is recounted among the kṣetra's glories — a **Tretā-Yuga** graft joining the Bhārgava axe-wielder to this Gujarat ground. (Nāgara-khaṇḍa 166; see [Paraśurāma](../04-deep-dives/parashurama.md), [Jamadagni & Paraśurāma](../04-deep-dives/jamadagni-parashurama.md)) [late text]
- **Viśvāmitra's renunciation (ch. ~167)** — the Kauśika king-turned-**brahmarṣi** and his abandonment of kingship for asceticism is likewise embedded here — apt for a *brahmin* charter, which foregrounds a *kṣatriya who becomes a brāhmaṇa*. (Nāgara-khaṇḍa 167; see [Viśvāmitra & Vasiṣṭha](../04-deep-dives/vishwamitra-vasishtha.md)) [late text]
- **Śrāddha-vidhi (ch. ~215–225)** — a long stretch on the **procedures of śrāddha** (ancestral rites) marks Vaḍnagar as a *pitṛ-tīrtha* as well, extending the kṣetra's use to the funerary economy that sustains a brahmin town. (Nāgara-khaṇḍa 215–225) [late text]
- **The accretive shape** — none of these tales originates at Vaḍnagar; each is a **canonical episode relocated to the kṣetra** to endow it with borrowed sanctity — the same *graft* technique the neighbouring [Kaumārikā-khaṇḍa](mahisagara-sangama-kumarika-kshetra.md) uses with Arjuna's crocodile-apsarās. *Catalogue the transposition; do not read Vaḍnagar as the tales' native home.* [scholarly]

## VII. The living kṣetra — the datable clock

- **Xuanzang at Ānandapura (640 CE)** — the Chinese Buddhist pilgrim **Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang)** visited the town as **Ānandapura ("O-nan-t'o-pu-lo")** c. **640 CE**, describing a walled, densely-peopled place with a flourishing Buddhist and brahminical presence — the earliest firm external date for the settlement the Purāṇa mythologizes. [scholarly]
- **The Harṣola copper-plates (949 CE)** — the **Paramāra (Siyaka II) Harṣola plates of 949 CE** — the earliest known Paramāra epigraph, issued by Siyaka II (Harṣa) while a *mahāsāmanta* of the Rāṣṭrakūṭa overlord Kṛṣṇa III — record grants to **Nāgara Brāhmaṇas said to have "come from Ānandapura,"** i.e. Vaḍnagar — hard epigraphic evidence that the Nāgaras were an established, migrating brahmin community by the mid-10th c., **centuries before** the Nāgara-khaṇḍa's fullest form. The **charter myth thus post-dates the community it charters.** [scholarly]
- **The Hāṭakeśvar (Hatkeshwar) Mahādev temple** — the present **Hāṭakeśvar Mahādev** temple, the Nāgaras' great family shrine, is a richly-carved **17th-century** Gujarat structure (its ornate *śikhara* and sculptural program of Purāṇic scenes) — the **datable fabric** beneath the timeless liṅga, roughly contemporary with the last strata of the Nāgara-khaṇḍa itself. [scholarly] [regional]
- **A protected heritage town** — Vaḍnagar's ring of ancient monuments — the twin **toraṇa** (12th-c. carved arches), the **Śarmiṣṭhā lake**, the excavated Buddhist monastery and the layered *tell* — has made it one of India's flagship archaeological towns; its continuous 2,700-year record is exactly the **documented Kali-Yuga** ground on which the Purāṇa's mythic antiquity is projected. [scholarly]
