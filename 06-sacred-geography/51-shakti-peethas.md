# The Śakti Pīṭhas (Satī's Fallen Body)

> Sacred geography of the Goddess: the *śakti pīṭhas* ("seats of power") are the places where the limbs, organs, and ornaments of **[Satī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)** fell to earth as the grief-maddened **Śiva** carried her corpse across the cosmos. Each fallen part sacralized a spot, fixing there a presiding **Devī (the Śakti)** and her guardian **Bhairava (a form of Śiva)**. The myth belongs to the Daksha-yajña cycle of the deep past; the pīṭhas are read here as *fixed, persisting* power-centers active through the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** of the 28th Mahāyuga.
>

A **śakti pīṭha** (Sanskrit *śakti* "power, the Goddess" + *pīṭha* "seat, pedestal, throne") is a shrine of the Mother Goddess sanctified by a fallen fragment of Satī's body. The pīṭhas are the master pilgrimage network of **Śāktism** (the Goddess-centric stream of Hinduism), the feminine counterpart to the Śaiva [jyotirlinga](12-jyotirlingas.md) circuit. Their *number is one of the most disputed lists in all of Hindu sacred geography* — texts variously enumerate **4, 18, 42, 51, 52, 64, or 108** pīṭhas — and this file catalogs the major enumerations with their differing textual sources.

## The Origin Myth: Satī's Self-Immolation and the Dismemberment

- **The slight at Daksha's yajña** - Satī's father **Daksha Prajāpati** (a [Saptarishi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md)-era patriarch, son of Brahmā) holds a great sacrifice and pointedly omits to invite Śiva and Satī, having despised his ascetic, cremation-haunting son-in-law (Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa VII; Kālikā Purāṇa; Mahābhāgavata Purāṇa). See the full [Daksha-yajña narrative](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md).
- **Satī's self-immolation** - Satī attends uninvited, is insulted, and — unable to bear the humiliation of her lord — gives up her body in the sacrificial fire (or, by yogic *agni*, by her own will). Hence *satī* as a word for self-offering wife. (Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Kālikā Purāṇa).
- **Śiva's cosmic grief-dance** - Mad with sorrow, Śiva takes up her charred corpse and roams the worlds, his *tāṇḍava* of grief threatening to unbalance creation (Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa VII.30; Kālikā Purāṇa 18).
- **Viṣṇu's Sudarśana Cakra** - To free Śiva from the corpse and restore cosmic order, **Viṣṇu** follows and severs the body piece by piece with his discus, the **Sudarśana Cakra**; each piece falls to earth and becomes a pīṭha (Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa; the *Pīṭhanirṇaya*).
  - *Variant (Mahābhāgavata / Bengali tradition):* Viṣṇu enters Satī's body and cuts it from within while Śiva carries it, so the dismemberment is invisible to Śiva.
  - *Variant (Kālikā Purāṇa):* the body falls apart over time as Śiva wanders, without explicit emphasis on the discus — the moralized "Viṣṇu intervenes to save the cosmos" framing is sharpest in the Devī Bhāgavata.
- **The body-part → place → Devī & Bhairava scheme** - At each landing site a triad is fixed: (1) the **aṅga** (body part or ornament) that fell, (2) the resident **Śakti / Devī** under a local name, and (3) her **Bhairava**, a fierce guardian form of Śiva who remains to watch over her. This three-column scheme (part / Śakti / Bhairava) is the organizing logic of the 51-pīṭha list and is most fully spelled out in the tantric *Tantra Cūḍāmaṇi* (= *Pīṭhanirṇaya*). [tantra]
- **Why the rebirth as Pārvatī follows** - Satī is reborn as **Pārvatī**, daughter of the mountain-king Himavān, and rewins Śiva — closing the loop between the destroyed first marriage and the [Śiva-Pārvatī union](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md).

## Why the Numbers Differ — A Map of the Enumerations

| List | Count | Primary textual source(s) | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ādi (primordial) pīṭhas** | **4** | Kālikā Purāṇa; *Aṣṭaśakti* tradition | The four where the energy is "most concentrated" |
| **Mahā (Aṣṭādaśa) pīṭhas** | **18** | *Aṣṭādaśa Śakti Pīṭha Stotram* (attrib. Ādi Śaṅkara) | The most-recited devotional subset |
| **The 51** | **51** | *Tantra Cūḍāmaṇi / Pīṭhanirṇaya*; Mahābhāgavata Purāṇa | Matches the 51 letters of the Sanskrit alphabet |
| **The 52 / 64** | 52, 64 | Bengal tradition (52); Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa (64) | Regional / expanded variants |
| **The 108** | **108** | Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa VII.38; Matsya Purāṇa | The maximal sacred-number list (108 names of Devī) |

- **The 51 = the alphabet** - The 51-count is widely read as corresponding to the **51 (or 50+1) akṣaras (letters/phonemes) of the Sanskrit / Devanāgarī alphabet** (the *mātṛkā* garland the Goddess wears), so each pīṭha is one sonic "limb" of the cosmic body of sound. [tantra] This is the symbolic backbone of the *Tantra Cūḍāmaṇi* count.
- *Note [scholarly]:* These are *scriptural / traditional* counts, not a settled historical geography. Sites were added over centuries; several "body parts" are claimed by more than one temple, and a few pīṭhas are unlocatable today. Distinguish the **textual lists** below from the **living temples** that assert membership.

## 1. The Four Ādi Śakti Pīṭhas

The four *primordial* seats, where Satī's energy is held most concentrated. The Sanskrit mnemonic (Aṣṭaśakti / Kālikā Purāṇa tradition) runs: *"Bimalā pāda-khaṇḍañca, sthāna-khaṇḍañca Tāriṇī, Kāmākṣyā yoni-khaṇḍañca, mukha-khaṇḍañca Kālikā..."*

- **Vimalā (Bimalā)** - **Puri, Odisha** (within the Jagannātha temple complex). Body part: *pāda-khaṇḍa* — **the feet**. (Kālikā Purāṇa; Aṣṭaśakti).
- **Tārā Tāriṇī** - hill near **Berhampur (Brahmapur), Ganjam, Odisha**. Body part: *stana-khaṇḍa* — **the breasts**. (Aṣṭaśakti).
- **Kāmākhyā** - **Nīlācala Hill, Guwahati, Assam**. Body part: *yoni-khaṇḍa* — **the womb / genitals**; the most powerful and tantric of all pīṭhas (see cross-border/notable below). (Kālikā Purāṇa).
- **Dakṣiṇa Kālikā / Kālīghāṭ** - **Kolkata, West Bengal**. Body part: in the Ādi-list given as *mukha-khaṇḍa* — **the face**.
  - *Variant:* in the 51-list, Kālīghāṭ's relic is the **right toes** (see #13 below) — the Ādi scheme and the 51 scheme assign Kālīghāṭ different parts. [disputed]

## 2. The Eighteen Mahā (Aṣṭādaśa) Śakti Pīṭhas

The most popular devotional set, fixed by the **Aṣṭādaśa Śakti Pīṭha Stotram** attributed to **Ādi Śaṅkarācārya**, opening *"Laṅkāyāṃ Śaṅkarī Devī, Kāmākṣī Kāñcikā pure..."* These pan-Indian "great" pīṭhas (Devī name — place):

1. **Śaṅkarī Devī** — Trincomalee, **Sri Lanka** [site destroyed; shrine commemorated]
2. **Kāmākṣī Devī** — Kāñcīpuram, Tamil Nadu
3. **Śṛṅkhalā Devī** — Pradyumna (Pandua), West Bengal / Gujarat tradition
4. **Cāmuṇḍeśvarī Devī** — Mysore (Chamundi Hills), Karnataka
5. **Jogulāmbā Devī** — Alampur, Telangana (Andhra)
6. **Bhramarāmbā Devī** — Śrīśailam, Andhra Pradesh (also a [jyotirlinga](12-jyotirlingas.md) site, Mallikārjuna)
7. **Mahālakṣmī Devī** — Kolhapur, Maharashtra
8. **Ekavīrā / Ekavīkā Devī** — Māhur (Nanded), Maharashtra
9. **Mahākālī Devī** — Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh (cf. Mahākāleśvara jyotirlinga)
10. **Puruhūtikā Devī** — Pīṭhāpuram, Andhra Pradesh
11. **Girijā / Birajā Devī** — Jajpur (Cuttack region), Odisha
12. **Māṇikyāmbā Devī** — Drākṣārāmam, Andhra Pradesh
13. **Kāmarūpā Devī** — Guwahati (Kāmākhyā), Assam
14. **Mādhaveśvarī (Alopī) Devī** — Prayāg (Prayagraj), Uttar Pradesh
15. **Vaiṣṇavī Devī** — Jwālāmukhī / Jwala, Himachal Pradesh

## 4. The Sixty-Four and the One Hundred Eight

- **64 pīṭhas** - The **Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa** (and some tantric *catuḥṣaṣṭi-yoginī* traditions) expand the network to **64**, harmonizing the pīṭhas with the 64 Yoginīs and 64 tantric arts. [tantra]
- **108 pīṭhas** - The **Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa (VII.38)** and the **Matsya Purāṇa** give the maximal list of **108** — the great sacred number, matching the 108 names/epithets of the Goddess. This is the source of the popular phrasing that "Viṣṇu cut Satī into 108 pieces." Many of the 108 are obscure or duplicate the 51; the 108-list is *encyclopedic* rather than a pilgrim circuit.
- *Variant counts:* a "**52**" is standard in much of **Bengal** (51 + the central pīṭha); local Śākta texts and *sthala-purāṇas* push individual regions' counts higher still.

## Cross-Border and Especially Notable Pīṭhas

- **Hinglaj Mātā (Hingula)** - in the Hingol/Hinglaj area of **Balochistan, Pakistan**, in a cave on the Hingol river. The **top of Satī's head (Brahmarandhra)** fell here; Śakti **Hiṅgulā/Koṭṭarī**, Bhairava **Bhīmalocana**. The **westernmost** pīṭha and a major living pilgrimage (the *Hinglaj Yātrā*) for Pakistani Hindus. [cross-border]
- **Kāmākhyā** - **Nīlācala Hill, Guwahati, Assam**, where the **yoni** fell; *the* pre-eminent tantric pīṭha. The shrine has no anthropomorphic image — a yoni-shaped cleft in the rock, kept moist by a spring. The **Ambubāchī Melā** celebrates the Goddess's annual menstruation (the river Brahmaputra reputedly runs red). Closely tied to the [Daśa Mahāvidyā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md) and the Kālikā Purāṇa.
- **Jwālāmukhī (Jwala Devi)** - **Kangra, Himachal Pradesh**, where the **tongue** fell. The shrine has **no idol**: the Goddess is worshipped as natural **perpetual flames** issuing from the rock (jets of natural gas) — *Jwālā* "flame-mouthed." One of the 18 Mahā pīṭhas (as Vaiṣṇavī).
- **Tārāpīṭh** - **Birbhum, West Bengal**, on the Dwarka river; a *third eye / nayana* tradition associates it with the falling of an eye, and it is the supreme seat of the Mahāvidyā **Tārā** and its cremation-ground (*śmaśāna*) tantra. Famed for the saint **Bāmākhepā**. [tantra] (Often listed alongside, though not always inside, the canonical 51.)
- **Sharada Peeth (Śāradā)** - **Kashmir** (Neelum valley, Pakistan-administered); pīṭha of **Sarasvatī/Śāradā** in the 18 Mahā list; once a great learning center, now ruined. [cross-border]
- **Śaṅkarī Devī (Trincomalee)** - **Sri Lanka**; the first of the 18 Mahā pīṭhas, the temple destroyed in the colonial era and now commemorated. [cross-border]
- **Guhyeśvarī & Dakṣiṇkālī** - **Kathmandu, Nepal**; the knees fell at Guhyeśvarī (beside Paśupatinātha). [cross-border]
- **Manasā / Manasarovar** - **Tibet**; the right hand fell at the sacred lake by Mount Kailāsa, linking the pīṭha network to Śiva's own axis-mundi. [cross-border]

## Theology: Why Pīṭhas Matter in Śāktism

- **Goddess as the land itself** - The pīṭha doctrine literally *embodies* the Goddess in the subcontinent: her dismembered body **is** the sacred map, making the whole of Bhārata a single divine corpus. This is the Śākta complement to the Śaiva [jyotirlinga](12-jyotirlingas.md) and Vaiṣṇava *divya-deśa* networks.
- **Śakti + Bhairava as inseparable polarity** - Every pīṭha pairs the **Devī (active power)** with a **Bhairava (Śiva as still ground)** — the tantric *Śiva-Śakti* non-duality fixed in geography. [tantra]
- **The mātṛkā / alphabet correspondence** - In tantra the 51 pīṭhas are mapped onto the 51 *akṣaras*, so reciting the alphabet or the pīṭha names traces the Goddess's whole body of sound (*śabda-brahman*). [tantra]
- **Link to the Mahāvidyās and Devī Māhātmya** - Several pīṭhas (Kāmākhyā, Tārāpīṭh, Vindhyāchal) are seats of the [Daśa Mahāvidyā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md); the underlying "supreme Goddess" theology is that of the [Devī Māhātmya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md).
