# Pancharama Kshetras

> The five great Śaiva *kṣetras* of coastal Andhra — **Amarārāma (Amaravati), Drākṣārāma, Kṣīrārāma (Palakollu), Kumārārāma (Samalkota), and Somārāma (Bhimavaram)** — each enshrining one fragment of a single *Śivaliṅga* that Kumāra/Skanda shattered when he slew the demon Tārakāsura. The first-tier named fivefold set of the Telugu country, parallel in stature to the [Pañcha Bhūta Sthalams](./pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md) of the Tamil south.

The **Pañcārāma** (Telugu *Pañcārāmālu*, "the five *ārāmas* / pleasure-groves") are five ancient Shiva temples clustered in the Godāvarī–Krishnā delta of coastal Andhra Pradesh, bound together by a single shared *sthala-purāṇa*: that the *liṅga* worshipped at all five is one original liṅga **broken into five pieces in the war against Tārakāsura**, each piece rooting itself where it fell. The grouping is a regional Śaiva systematisation — comparable to how the Tamil tradition bundles its [five element-shrines](./pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md) — and the constituent temples are individually old (Eastern Cālukya and Cōḻa-era fabric, c. 9th–11th c. CE) while the *fivefold* framing is a later devotional overlay. [regional] [scholarly]

## The Five at a Glance

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Amarārāma** | Amareśvara / Amaralingeśvara | Bālā Tripurasundarī | Amarāvatī (Guntur) | tall white *liṅga* on the Krishnā; said to "grow" |
| 2 | **Drākṣārāma** | Bhīmeśvara | Manikyāmbā (a Śakti Pīṭha) | Drākṣārāmam (East Godavari) | grandest; **Dakṣa-vāṭikā**, site of Dakṣa's yajña |
| 3 | **Kṣīrārāma** | Kṣīrarāmaliṅgeśvara / Rāmaliṅgeśvara | Pārvatī | Palakollu (West Godavari) | tall milk-white liṅga; *kṣīra* = milk |
| 4 | **Kumārārāma** | Kumāra Bhīmeśvara | Bālā Tripurasundarī | Samālkoṭa (East Godavari) | consecrated by Kumāra/Skanda himself |

## The shared origin myth — Tārakāsura's liṅga

- **The invincible demon** — The asura **Tārakāsura** (Tāraka), boon-protected so that only a son of Śiva could kill him, terrorised the *devas*; his death is the cosmic reason for Skanda's birth. This is the same Tāraka-war that frames the [Skanda birth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md) and the wider [Śiva myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md). (Skanda Purāṇa; Śiva Purāṇa, *Rudra-saṃhitā*, *Kumāra-khaṇḍa*.)
- **The liṅga at his throat** — Tārakāsura wore a great **Śivaliṅga** (his *iṣṭa-liṅga*, in some tellings swallowed or worn at his neck/heart) that made his body proof against death; he could not be slain while it was whole. (Local Andhra sthala-purāṇa tradition. [regional])
- **Skanda shatters it** — When **Kumāra/Skanda (Kārtikeya)**, the boy-general, struck Tāraka down, the liṅga refused to break — each time it was severed it **rejoined itself**. On the counsel of Viṣṇu (and/or Śiva), the gods held the pieces apart as Skanda cut, and the liṅga splintered into **five fragments** that flew off and **embedded themselves at the five sites**. (Pañcārāma sthala-purāṇa. [regional])
  - *Variant (number/agency):* some recensions credit **Indra, Sūrya, Candra/Soma, Viṣṇu and Kumāra** with pinning down the five pieces, each god then installing his fragment — hence names like **Kumārārāma** (Skanda's), **Somārāma** (Soma/Candra's), **Amarārāma** (Indra's, *amara* = the immortals). [folk]
  - *Variant (still-growing liṅgas):* because the fragments were "living" and tried to reunite, several of the liṅgas are said to be **still growing**; at Amarārāma a pin/peg or nail was driven into the top to hold it down, and the mark is shown to pilgrims. [folk]

## 1. Amarārāma — Amareśvara, Amarāvatī

- **Deity** — Śiva as **Amareśvara / Amaralingeśvara**, consort **Bālā Tripurasundarī**, on the south bank of the **Krishnā** at **Amarāvatī** (Guntur dist.), the ancient capital region (Dhānyakaṭaka / Andhra-mahā-caitya country). (Amareśvara sthala-purāṇa.)
- **The growing liṅga** — A tall white *liṅga* (~4–5 m, worshipped from two levels) held to be **Indra's** fragment — *amara* naming the immortals who installed it; its upward growth was checked by a nail at the crown. [folk] (Temple tradition.)
- **Note** — Amarāvatī is also a major **Buddhist** site (the Amarāvatī Stūpa); the Śaiva *kṣetra* and the Buddhist *mahā-caitya* coexist in the same locality. [scholarly]

## 2. Drākṣārāma — Bhīmeśvara, Drākṣārāmam

- **Deity** — Śiva as **Bhīmeśvara** (a tall liṅga of white-and-black *spaṭika*-like stone, worshipped from an upper gallery), consort **Manikyāmbā**, at **Drākṣārāmam** (East Godavari). The grandest of the five. (Bhīmeśvara / *Bhīmeśvara-māhātmya* — the Telugu *kāvya* by **Śrīnātha**, 15th c.)
- **Dakṣa-vāṭikā** — The name is read as *Dakṣa-vāṭika/Dakṣa-ārāma* — the **garden of Dakṣa** — and the site is identified with the place of **Dakṣa's yajña** that Sati's self-immolation and Vīrabhadra's destruction answered (see [Sati, Dakṣa & Pārvatī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)). [regional]
  - **Manikyāmbā as a Śakti Pīṭha** — The consort **Manikyāmbā** is counted among the **[Aṣṭādaśa Śakti Pīṭhas](./51-shakti-peethas.md)** (the eighteen *mahā* pīṭhas of the Ādi Śaṅkara stotra), held to be where Sati's **left cheek** fell. This makes Drākṣārāma simultaneously a Pañcārāma and a Śakti Pīṭha. [scholarly]
- **Sapta-godāvarī tīrtha** — The temple tank is fed by the **Sapta Godāvarī** (the seven streams of the Godāvarī said to converge here underground); see [Godāvarī — Dakṣiṇa Gaṅgā](./godavari-gautami-dakshina-ganga.md). (Local tradition.)

## 3. Kṣīrārāma — Kṣīrarāmaliṅgeśvara, Palakollu

- **Deity** — Śiva as **Kṣīrarāmaliṅgeśvara** (also **Rāmaliṅgeśvara**), consort **Pārvatī**, at **Palakollu** (West Godavari). *Kṣīra* = milk; Telugu *pāla-koḷḷu* / *pāla-kollu* likewise carries the "milk" sense. (Kṣīrārāma sthala-purāṇa.)
- **The milk-white liṅga** — A tall white *liṅga* said to be milk-coloured; one tradition holds **Rāma** worshipped here (hence *Rāmaliṅga*), petitioning Śiva — paralleling the Setu liṅga of [Rāmeśvaram](./rameswaram-setu-bandha.md). [regional]
- **The gopuram** — Famed for one of the tallest temple *gopurams* of the delta (~9 storeys). (Temple report.)

## 4. Kumārārāma — Kumāra Bhīmeśvara, Samālkoṭa

- **Deity** — Śiva as **Kumāra Bhīmeśvara**, consort **Bālā Tripurasundarī**, at **Samālkoṭa / Sāmarlakōṭa** (East Godavari). (Kumārārāma sthala-purāṇa; Eastern Cālukya inscriptions, Cālukya **Bhīma I**, 9th–10th c.)
- **Skanda's own shrine** — Named **Kumārārāma** because **Kumāra/Skanda himself installed and worshipped** his fragment here after the Tāraka war — the site that most directly carries the shattering-myth. (Local tradition. [regional])
- **The growing liṅga** — Like Amarārāma, a tall white liṅga (~4–5 m) worshipped from an upper floor and said once to have grown. [folk]

## 5. Somārāma — Someśvara, Bhimavaram

- **Deity** — Śiva as **Someśvara**, consort **Śrī Rājarājeśvarī / Annapūrṇā**, at **Gunupūdi–Bhimavaram** (West Godavari). Held to be **Candra/Soma's** fragment — hence the name. (Somārāma sthala-purāṇa.)
- **The moon-coloured liṅga** — The *liṅga* is said to **change colour with the lunar phases** — white at full moon, brown/dark at new moon — the element of Soma made visible. [folk] (Temple tradition.)
- **Annapūrṇā above Someśvara** — Uniquely, a shrine of the goddess **Annapūrṇā** stands **directly above** the Śiva sanctum (upper storey), an inversion of the usual layout. [regional]
- **Soma's curse-relief** — One legend has **Candra (Soma)**, afflicted by Dakṣa's wasting-curse (see [Candra-Soma](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/chandra-soma.md)), worshipping here to regain his light — tying the moon-coloured liṅga to the *kṣaya/vṛddhi* (waning/waxing) of the moon. [folk]

## Notes on Scope & Dating (two clocks)

- **Scriptural clock** — The shared myth is **cross-yuga / pre-historical** in framing: the Tāraka war and Skanda's birth belong to mythic Purāṇic time, the same cycle as the *Kumāra-saṃbhava*; the liṅga-fragments are installed by gods (Indra, Soma, Viṣṇu, Kumāra) outside ordinary yuga reckoning. The Dakṣa-yajña association at Drākṣārāma reaches back to the Sati story, earlier still. [regional]
- **Academic clock** — The five temples as **built structures** are largely **Eastern Cālukya, Cōḻa and Kākatīya-era** (c. 9th–13th c. CE), with later Vijayanagara and Nāyaka additions; Drākṣārāma's glorification in **Śrīnātha's *Bhīmeśvara-Purāṇamu*** (15th c. Telugu) is a key literary witness. The **Pañcārāma as a single fivefold set** is a regional medieval/early-modern devotional grouping that post-dates the individual shrines. [scholarly]
- **Variant policy** — The *assignment* of which god installed which fragment, the *number* shattered (always five, but the agents differ), and the "growing liṅga" motif circulate in multiple regional recensions and are catalogued above as attributed, never merged. The Tārakāsura-liṅga frame is local Andhra *sthala-purāṇa* and is **not** a fixed pan-Indian Purāṇic narrative; it draws on, but is distinct from, the canonical Skanda/Tāraka war of the Śiva and Skanda Purāṇas. [disputed]
