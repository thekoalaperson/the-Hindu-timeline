# Kurukṣetra — Dharmakṣetra & the Field of the Mahābhārata

> The **Dharmakṣetra** ("field of dharma") and **Kurukṣetra** ("field of Kuru") of the *Bhagavad Gītā*'s opening line — the great northern *yajña-bhūmi* of the Sarasvatī–Dṛṣadvatī doab where the **18-day Mahābhārata war** was fought and where **Kṛṣṇa delivered the Gītā** to Arjuna. King **Kuru's** tapas won the field its boon: to die here is liberation. The dedicated *kṣetra* node — etymology and the two-clock dating, the *Kurukṣetra-māhātmya* (Vāmana Purāṇa, Mahābhārata *Vana-parva*), **Samanta-pañcaka** and Paraśurāma's blood-lakes, **Jyotisar** (the Gītā-upadeśa banyan), **Brahma Sarovar** & **Sannihit Sarovar**, **Sthāneśvar Mahādev**, **Bhīṣma-kund** / **Bāṇgaṅgā**, **Karṇa-vadh**, and the **48-kos parikrama** with its 134+ tīrthas.

## I. Name & the "field of dharma" — *dharmakṣetre kurukṣetre*

- **Dharmakṣetra Kurukṣetra** - the *Bhagavad Gītā* opens (1.1) with Dhṛtarāṣṭra asking Sañjaya: *"dharma-kṣetre kuru-kṣetre samavetā yuyutsavaḥ..."* — "on the field of dharma, on the field of Kuru, gathered eager to fight, what did my men and the Pāṇḍavas do?" The double epithet frames the whole war as a *dharma*-contest, not a mere land-quarrel. (*Bhagavad Gītā* 1.1; *Mahābhārata, Bhīṣma-parva*) — see [Bhagavad Gītā](../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md)
- **Kuru-kṣetra = "Kuru's field"** - named for the **Pauravā / Bhārata king Kuru** (eponym of the **Kaurava** line and of both the Kauravas and Pāṇḍavas, who are alike *kauravas*); the field is his cultivated dharma-ground. (*Mahābhārata*; *Vāmana Purāṇa*) — see [lineages](../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md)
- **Dharmakṣetra = "realm of duty"** - the standing epithet of the region; Kurukṣetra is styled the **"Land of the Bhagavad Gītā"** and the *uttara-vedi* (northern altar) of Brahmā/Prajāpati. (Wikipedia, *Kurukshetra*; *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* tradition)
- **Why a "dharma"-field** - the *Mahābhārata* and Purāṇas hold the ground to have been **sanctified before the war** by Kuru's penance and Vedic sacrifice, so the place was already *dharmakṣetra*; the war merely happened *on* it. A devotional reading takes the phrase mystically — the *body itself* as the field where the higher (Pāṇḍava) and lower (Kaurava) impulses contend. [late text]

## II. Two clocks — scriptural epoch vs. academic site

- **Scriptural clock** - the war falls at the **junction of Dvāpara and Kali Yugas**; tradition fixes the start of **Kali Yuga to 3102 BCE**, with the war a few decades earlier (commonly 3138 BCE in traditional reckonings). The field is older still — sanctified by Kuru in an earlier age and by Paraśurāma "in the interval between Tretā and Dvāpara." (*Mahābhārata*; Purāṇic yuga-scheme) — see [Yuga System](../00-time-and-cosmology/yuga-cycle.md)
- **Academic clock** - scholars place the historical kernel of the Bhārata war (if any) in the **Iron Age Painted-Grey-Ware horizon, c. 1000–900 BCE**, and identify modern **Kurukṣetra / Thanesar district, Haryana** — the **Sarasvatī–Dṛṣadvatī** (mod. Ghaggar–Chautang) interfluve — as the geographic locus. [scholarly]
- **The "two altars" frame** - the *Taittirīya* and *Śatapatha* strata already revere **Kurukṣetra as the gods' sacrificial ground** (*deva-yajana*), independent of the epic war — i.e. it is a **Vedic yajña-bhūmi** first, an epic battlefield second. (Vedic Brāhmaṇa tradition) [scholarly]

## III. Kuru's tapas — the boon that death here = liberation

- **King Kuru's ploughing** - in the **Kurukṣetra-māhātmya** Kuru comes to this plain and **ploughs it with a golden plough** (drawn, in versions, by Yama's buffalo and Indra's bull / the Sun and Moon), intending to make it a **Dharma-kṣetra**. (*Vāmana Purāṇa*, *Kurukṣetra-māhātmya*, ch. on Saṃvarṇa–Tapatī & Kuru; *Mahābhārata, Śalya/Vana* strata)
- **The eight virtues sown (aṣṭāṅga-bhūmi)** - asked by **Indra** what seed he sows, Kuru answers he sows **tapas (austerity), satya (truth), kṣamā (forbearance/endurance), dayā (mercy), śauca (purity), dāna (charity), yoga, and brahmacarya (celibacy)** — eight dharmic qualities. (*Vāmana Purāṇa, Kurukṣetra-māhātmya*)
- **Viṣṇu's test & the self-offering** - **Viṣṇu** appears and asks where the seed will come from; Kuru offers his own body — Viṣṇu severs his **hands, thighs, and finally his head** to test resolve, and, satisfied, grants the boon. (*Vāmana Purāṇa* account)
  - *Variant (Indra grants the boon):* in a parallel telling **Indra**, alarmed that effortless heaven via this field would empty the sacrifices, consults the gods and finally concedes — promising that **those who fast, fall in battle, or die consciously on this plain reach heaven**. (*Vāmana Purāṇa / Mahābhārata* māhātmya strands)
- **The boon itself** - Kuru asks, and it is granted, that **this land be named after him (Kurukṣetra) for all time**, and that **bathing, dying, or even being touched by its wind-blown dust** here grants *mahā-puṇya* and **mokṣa / heaven** — every meritorious act done here yields multiplied fruit. (*Vāmana Purāṇa, Kurukṣetra-māhātmya*)
  - *Variant (dust of Kurukṣetra):* even the **dust carried off the field by the wind** leads sinners to the highest goal — a phrase echoed across the māhātmyas. [folk]

## IV. Samanta-pañcaka — the Vedic yajña-bhūmi & Paraśurāma's blood-lakes

- **Samanta-pañcaka** - the older / ritual name of the field, "the five [lakes] on all sides," demarcated as the **uttara-vedi (northern altar) of Brahmā** — the sacred precinct within which the war is fought. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 2; *Vāmana Purāṇa*)
- **Paraśurāma's five blood-lakes** - between **Tretā and Dvāpara**, **Paraśurāma** (6th [avatāra of Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)), in his twenty-one exterminations of the kṣatriyas, filled **five lakes (pañca-hradāḥ) with kṣatriya blood** here and offered tarpaṇa to his ancestors; the place was thus called **Samanta-pañcaka**. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 2.3–10) — see [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)
- **The two great encounters** - the *Mahābhārata* itself frames Samanta-pañcaka as the site of **two age-junction events**: Paraśurāma's slaughter (Tretā–Dvāpara cusp) and the **Kaurava–Pāṇḍava war** (Dvāpara–Kali cusp) — making the field the cosmic stage where each yuga-turn is sealed in blood. (*Mahābhārata, Ādi-parva* 2)
- **Seven forests & nine rivers** - the māhātmya describes Kurukṣetra as enclosing **seven vanas (forests)** and watered by **nine rivers** — Sarasvatī chief among them — a self-contained sacred landscape. (*Vāmana Purāṇa, Kurukṣetra-māhātmya*) [late text]

## V. The 18-day war — the field as battlefield

- **The two armies** - **18 akṣauhiṇīs** (11 Kaurava + 7 Pāṇḍava) assemble on the plain between the **Sarasvatī and Dṛṣadvatī**; the war runs **18 days**, narrated across the *Bhīṣma-, Droṇa-, Karṇa-, Śalya-* and *Sauptika-parvans*. (*Mahābhārata*) — see [war narrative](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md)
- **Bhīṣma's fall (day 10)** - **Bhīṣma** is felled by **Arjuna** (with **Śikhaṇḍin** before him) and lies on a **bed of arrows (śara-śayyā)**, withholding death until *uttarāyaṇa*; he expounds dharma there before passing. Site: **Bhīṣma-kund / Narkatari** (see §IX). (*Mahābhārata, Bhīṣma-parva*)
- **Droṇa's & Karṇa's falls** - **Droṇa** beheaded by **Dhṛṣṭadyumna** after the "Aśvatthāmā is dead" stratagem (day 15); **Karṇa** slain by **Arjuna** while lifting his mired chariot-wheel (day 17) — site marked at **Karṇa-vadh / Jyotisar–Aujas-ghāṭ vicinity** (see §IX). (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa-parva*, *Karṇa-parva*) — see [Karṇa](../04-deep-dives/karna.md)
- **Duryodhana & the Sauptika night** - **Duryodhana** felled by **Bhīma**'s mace at the thigh (day 18, *Śalya-parva*), then the **night-massacre of the sleeping Pāṇḍava camp** by Aśvatthāmā (*Sauptika-parva*). (*Mahābhārata*)

## VI. Jyotisar — the Gītā-upadeśa

- **Jyotisar (Jyotisthāna)** - the spot, ~5 km from Thanesar on the Kurukṣetra–Pehowa road, revered as where **Kṛṣṇa spoke the Bhagavad Gītā to Arjuna** as the armies faced off — *gītā-jñāna* arose here, hence *Jyoti(s)-sar*, "lake / abode of light." (Wikipedia, *Kurukshetra*; Haryana Tourism)
- **The akṣaya-vaṭa (immortal banyan)** - an ancient **banyan tree (vaṭa)** at Jyotisar is venerated as the very tree under which the *upadeśa* was given and the surviving witness of the *viśvarūpa-darśana* (Gītā ch. 11); a marble chariot-tableau of Kṛṣṇa–Arjuna stands beside it. (Haryana Tourism; local sthala tradition) [folk]
- **Festival focus** - Jyotisar is the ritual heart of the modern **International Gītā Mahotsav** (around *Gītā Jayantī*, Mārgaśīrṣa Śukla Ekādaśī); a son-et-lumière and the new Gītā experiential centre stand here. (Kurukshetra Development Board)

## VII. Brahma Sarovar & Sannihit Sarovar — the eclipse-snāna tanks

- **Brahma Sarovar** - the vast sacred tank at **Thanesar**, the focal water-body of the whole *kṣetra*; tradition holds **Brahmā performed the cosmic creative sacrifice here** (the *sṛṣṭi* yajña), or that the universe was created from this very ground. Lakhs bathe at **solar eclipse** and **Somavatī Amāvasyā** (a no-moon Monday). (Wikipedia; Kurukshetra Development Board)
- **Sannihit Sarovar** - the tank where, by belief, **all tīrthas of India assemble (sannihita) on Amāvasyā** and especially at a **solar eclipse** — a single bath here on that day equals bathing at every tīrtha; held also to be the **meeting-place of the seven Sarasvatīs**. Pitṛ-rites and the famous **Kurukṣetra genealogy registers (bahī / *paṇḍā* records)** are kept at Sannihit. (Wikipedia; Kurukshetra Development Board)
- **Eclipse-snāna doctrine** - the supreme merit of Kurukṣetra is the **sūrya-grahaṇa snāna**: a solar-eclipse bath at Brahma/Sannihit Sarovar is reckoned in the māhātmyas as equal to a thousand *aśvamedhas* or the merit of all tīrthas combined — the single most powerful ritual moment of the field. (*Vāmana / Mahābhārata* māhātmya tradition) — cf. [Prayāgrāj & eclipse/Kumbh cycles](kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md)

## VIII. Sthāneśvar Mahādev & the temple-core

- **Sthāneśvar Mahādev** - the great **Śiva** temple at **Thanesar** ("Sthāṇu-īśvara," "lord who is the [fixed] pillar/Śiva"); tradition holds the **Pāṇḍavas worshipped Śiva here for victory** before the war, and that **Bāṇāsura** or the gods established the liṅga. The town's very name *Thanesar* derives from *Sthāneśvara*. (Wikipedia; local sthala-purāṇa) [folk]
- **Capital of Vardhana / Harṣa** - Sthāneśvar/Thanesar was the **6th–7th c. CE capital of the Puṣyabhūti (Vardhana) dynasty** of **Harṣavardhana** — a historical anchor for the site's continuity. [scholarly]
- **Other core shrines** - **Bhadrakālī temple** (a **Śakti-pīṭha** where Sītā/Satī's *ankle* is said to have fallen; the Pāṇḍavas' tutelary; horses were offered here for victory), **Sthāṇu-tīrtha**, **Cakra-tīrtha**, and the **Nābhi-kamala / Kamal Nābhi** (Viṣṇu-navel) shrine. (Kurukshetra Development Board) — cf. [51 Śakti Pīṭhas](51-shakti-peethas.md)

## IX. War-memorial tīrthas of the field

- **Bhīṣma-kund (Narkatari)** - the kund/spring marking where **Bhīṣma lay on his bed of arrows**; here **Arjuna shot an arrow into the earth and a jet of Gaṅgā-water sprang up to slake Bhīṣma's thirst** — hence **Bāṇgaṅgā** ("Gaṅgā [drawn] by an arrow"). A temple to Bhīṣma stands on the spot at Narkatari, Thanesar. (Wikipedia; Haryana Tourism; Kurukshetra Development Board)
- **Bāṇgaṅgā** - the arrow-spring of the Bhīṣma episode, counted among the oldest holy spots of the *kṣetra*; the name literally "arrow-Gaṅgā." (local tradition) [folk]
- **Karṇa-vadh / Karṇa-kā-tīla** - the site associated with **Karṇa's death**, where his chariot-wheel sank and Arjuna slew him; commemorated near the Jyotisar–Aujas-ghāṭ stretch. (48-kos circuit tradition) — see [Karṇa](../04-deep-dives/karna.md) [folk — exact spot disputed]
- **Aujas-ghāṭ / Yudhiṣṭhira-tīrtha** - ghāṭ on the Sarasvatī where **Baladeva (Balarāma)** ended his Sarasvatī pilgrimage and where post-war *śrāddha* for the slain was performed. (*Mahābhārata, Śalya-parva*) [late text]
- **Other epic-marked tīrthas** - **Abhimanyu's killing-ground** (the *cakra-vyūha*), **Duryodhana's lake (Dvaipāyana-hrada)** where he hid, **Pārtha-kūp / Arjun-kund**, **Drona-tīrtha**, and the spots of **Drupada, Virāṭa, Śalya** etc. recur in the parikrama lists. (48-kos tradition) [folk — open-ended local roster]

## X. The 48-kos parikrama & its tīrthas

- **48-kos Kurukṣetra parikrama** - the great circumambulatory pilgrimage of the **whole Kurukṣetra *kṣetra*** — a roughly **90-km circle** (1 kos ≈ 3 km / ~2 miles → ~48 kos ≈ 96 miles boundary) traversed on foot, taking in the Mahābhārata-era and Vedic tīrthas scattered across the plain. (Wikipedia, *48 kos parikrama of Kurukshetra*; 48kosyatra.com)
- **Five (now five) districts** - the circuit spans **Kurukṣetra, Kaithal, Jind, Karnal, and Panipat** districts of Haryana (with edges touching Yamunanagar), bounded broadly by the **Sarasvatī, Dṛṣadvatī (Chautang), Mārkaṇḍā and Yamunā** drainages. (Wikipedia; Kurukshetra Development Board)
- **134+ / 182 tīrthas** - the parikrama threads **at least 134 identified tīrthas** (later surveys list **180+ / "182" Mahābhārata-era sites**); the Kurukshetra Development Board has been marking and restoring them. [open-ended roster — counts vary by survey]
- **Focal nodes on the circuit** - **Brahma Sarovar** (center), **Jyotisar**, **Sannihit Sarovar**, **Sthāneśvar / Sthāṇu-tīrtha**, **Bhīṣma-kund (Narkatari)**, **Bāṇgaṅgā**, **Karṇa-vadh**, plus outer seats such as **Pehowa (Pṛthūdaka)** on the Sarasvatī (Pṛthu's *śrāddha* tīrtha), **Pīpalī / Devī-kūp Bhadrakālī**, and **Kapil-muni / Phalgu-tīrtha**. (Kurukshetra Development Board; 48-kos tradition)
- **Pṛthūdaka (Pehowa)** - "Pṛthu's water," the major Sarasvatī tīrtha at the western edge, foremost for **pitṛ-śrāddha for those who died unnatural deaths** — among the holiest of the circuit's outer tīrthas. (*Mahābhārata, Vana-parva* tīrtha-yātrā; Pehowa tradition) — cf. [Gayā pitṛ-tīrtha](gaya-pitru-tirtha.md)

## XI. Kurukṣetra in the māhātmya & tīrtha-yātrā literature

- **Mahābhārata, *Vana-parva* (Tīrtha-yātrā)** - the great pilgrimage-catalogue spoken by **Pulastya/Nārada to Bhīṣma/Yudhiṣṭhira** opens its tour with **Kurukṣetra as the foremost tīrtha**, declaring that merely intending to go, or being dusted by its earth, removes sin. (*Mahābhārata, Vana-parva*, Tīrtha-yātrā-parva)
- **Vāmana Purāṇa, *Kurukṣetra-māhātmya*** - the most extended scriptural praise — Kuru's ploughing, the boon, the seven forests / nine rivers, and a tīrtha-by-tīrtha eulogy; also ties the field to **Aditi's penance for the birth of Vāmana**. (*Vāmana Purāṇa*) — see [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)
- **Vedic stratum** - the **Taittirīya Āraṇyaka / Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa** revere Kurukṣetra as **deva-yajana** (sacrificial ground of the gods) and the *uttara-vedi* of Prajāpati — the field's sanctity predates the epic and is independent of it. [scholarly]
- **Gītā-jayantī & living cult** - the field remains a living pilgrimage focus: **Gītā Jayantī / Gītā Mahotsav** at Jyotisar, **eclipse-snāna** at Brahma & Sannihit Sarovar, and the periodic **48-kos parikrama**. (Kurukshetra Development Board)
