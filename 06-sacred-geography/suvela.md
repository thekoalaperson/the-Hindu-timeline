# Suvela — The Hill the Army Watched Laṅkā From

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](README.md) → **Suvela**. The mineral-bright mountain facing **Laṅkā** across the shore, which **Rāma** occupies with the whole vānara host after the crossing of the [Setu](rameswaram-setu-bandha.md) and holds as his command height until the siege begins: the vantage from which he first sees Rāvaṇa's city and Rāvaṇa himself, and from which **Sugrīva** springs at the rākṣasa king and tears the crown off his head.

## I. Identity — a command height, not a shrine

- **Suvela = the mountain across from the city** — the wooded, many-summited peak on the Laṅkā side of the crossing, "**shining well with hundreds of minerals**" and marked by **conspicuous summits**, which Rāma chooses as the army's ground before the assault. Vālmīki gives it little more description than that; what the text is interested in is what can be *seen* from it. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 38.3, 38.8)
- **Distinct from Trikūṭa** — Laṅkā itself stands on the summit of the three-peaked **Trikūṭa**; **Suvela is the hill opposite**, and the whole point of the sarga is that from one peak the army looks across at the other. Collapsing the two loses the epic's staging of the siege as a thing watched from a height. (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 39.18–20; *Sundara-kāṇḍa* 2–3) — see [Aśoka-vāṭikā & the Laṅkā trail](lanka-ramayana-trail-ashoka-vatika.md)
- **Scriptural clock (Tretā-yuga)** — the Suvela occupation belongs to the **Tretā-yuga** of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara, in the last phase of Rāma's fourteen-year exile, between the building of the bridge and the opening of the siege. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*) — the mythic register; keep the **two-clocks** scriptural-vs-historical distinction.
- **A place with no cult** — unlike [Kiṣkindhā](kishkindha-hampi-anegundi.md), [Citrakūṭa](chitrakoot-kamadgiri.md) or the Aśoka-vāṭikā, Suvela has attracted **no living identification, no temple and no station on the modern Sri Lankan Rāmāyaṇa Trail**. It is a purely textual place: a height that exists to be stood on for three sargas and is never afterwards venerated. [scholarly]

## II. The occupation of the hill (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 31, 37–38)

- **Rāvaṇa learns of it first** — the epic's earliest mention of the mountain is in the enemy's intelligence: **Rāvaṇa's spies report that Rāma, with his imperturbable army, is encamped at Mount Suvela in Laṅkā**. The name enters the epic as a line in a scouting report. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 31)
- **The choice of ground** — after taking **Vibhīṣaṇa's** intelligence on Laṅkā's defences and assigning commanders to each of the four gates, Rāma **decides to stay on Mount Suvela, having observed its charming slopes**, and makes it his position for the campaign's preparation. (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 37)
- **"We shall ascend this Suvela"** — Rāma proposes the climb to **Lakṣmaṇa, Sugrīva and Vibhīṣaṇa**: they will pass the night on the mountain and from it he will **see Laṅkā, the abode of the demon who took his wife**. He goes up thinking of Rāvaṇa in anger, Lakṣmaṇa behind him with the bow ready. (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 38.3–9)
- **Who climbs** — with the four leaders go **Hanumān, Aṅgada, Nīla, Mainda, Dvivida** and the other vānara chiefs, and behind them "many hundreds of monkeys, fast-moving, with a speed like the wind"; the host halts on the mountain resort of Suvela. (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 38.10–19)

## III. The night on the summit and the sight of Laṅkā (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 39)

- **The army passes the night on the hill** — the vānara chiefs stay on Suvela for that night and look out over the **gardens and groves of Laṅkā**; some of the monkeys go down into the pleasure-gardens in delight and others toward the city itself. (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 39.1, 39.10–14)
- **What the summit shows them** — the peak of **Trikūṭa**, extraordinarily high and "touching the sky," with **Laṅkā set on its apex**: towering gates, **ramparts of gold and silver**, the mansions of the city, and **Rāvaṇa's thousand-pillared palace** rising as though to touch the heavens. The set-piece is one of Vālmīki's great descriptions of a city seen from outside by the men who are about to destroy it. (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 39.18–29)
- **Why the sarga is placed here** — the whole apparatus of Laṅkā — its wealth, its walls, its impossibility — is shown to the reader **through the eyes of the besiegers**, on the last night before the fighting starts. The hill is the epic's viewing platform. [scholarly]

## IV. What happens under the shadow of Suvela

- **The counterfeit head** — with the host encamped on the mountain and the news of it brought to him, **Rāvaṇa** has the sorcerer **Vidyujjihva** conjure a severed head and a bow, shows them to **Sītā** as her husband's, and adds a fabricated account of a night-massacre of the vānara army: an attempt to end the war by breaking the woman it is fought over. (This conjurer is a namesake of, and not the same as, the dānava Vidyujjihva whom Rāvaṇa killed at *Uttara* 23.) (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 31) — see [Rāmāyaṇa Objects & Tokens](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/ramayana-objects-and-tokens.md), [Saramā](../04-deep-dives/characters/sarama.md)
- **Mālyavān's counsel** — only now, with the enemy visibly encamped on Suvela, does the aged minister **Mālyavān** — Sumālī's elder brother, and so Rāvaṇa's maternal great-uncle — rise in council to argue from omens and statecraft that a king at war with dharma cannot win, and that Sītā must go back. Rāvaṇa abuses him and he withdraws. This counsel is **post-crossing**, and distinct from the earlier debate in which Vibhīṣaṇa was driven out. (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 35–36) — see [Yuddha Kāṇḍa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md)
- **Sugrīva's leap** — from the summit Rāma and Sugrīva see **Rāvaṇa standing above a gateway** in his royal insignia. Sugrīva rises all at once, bounds from the mountain-top to the gate, **pulls the bright crown from Rāvaṇa's head and throws it to the ground**, and grapples with him hand to hand through a long wrestling-bout of holds and throws; when Rāvaṇa reaches for his illusions Sugrīva springs into the sky and returns to Rāma. (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 40.1–30)
- **Rāma's rebuke** — the reproof is immediate and constitutional, not personal: "**Without consulting me, such a reckless act has been done by you; such rashness is not seemly in a king**" — Sugrīva has hazarded the whole campaign, and Rāma, the army and Vibhīṣaṇa on a private impulse. Rāma forbids any repetition. (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 41)
- **The descent and the investment of the city** — Rāma comes down from Suvela, reviews the host, and stations it at all **four gates** — himself with Lakṣmaṇa at the northern gate where Rāvaṇa is, Nīla, Aṅgada and Hanumān at the others, Sugrīva in the centre — then sends **Aṅgada** as envoy with the last ultimatum. The siege proper begins the moment the army leaves the hill. (*Yuddha-kāṇḍa* 41)

## V. Status & cautions

- **Vālmīki fixes the function, not the place** — the text gives Suvela a role (the command height), a look (minerals, summits, woods) and a position (facing Trikūṭa across the shore), and nothing that would locate it on a map. Any siting of it is inference from the prior, itself-contested identification of Laṅkā with Sri Lanka. [scholarly] [disputed] — cf. [Bhāratavarṣa & Purāṇic Geography](bharatavarsha-and-puranic-geography.md)
- *Variant (Suvela as a peak of Trikūṭa):* some later tellings and geographical harmonisations read Suvela as **one of Trikūṭa's own three peaks** rather than a separate mountain facing it. Vālmīki's staging — the army on one height looking across at Laṅkā on another — reads against that. [disputed]
- **Its afterlife in retelling** — Suvela keeps its name and its scene in the later Rāmāyaṇas, where the night on the mountain becomes a set-piece of its own, and it keeps its place in the mountain roster on the strength of that scene alone. — see [Meru, Kailāsa & the Cosmic Mountains](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md)
- **Why the hill matters** — Suvela is where the *Rāmāyaṇa* stops being a pursuit and becomes a war: the crossing is finished, the enemy is in sight, the diplomacy runs out and the army is put in position. It is the ground between the bridge and the battle. [scholarly]
