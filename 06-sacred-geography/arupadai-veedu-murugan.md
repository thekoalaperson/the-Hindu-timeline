# Aṟupaḍai Vīḍu — the Six War-Camps of Murugan

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](./README.md) → **Aṟupaḍai Vīḍu**. The six Tamil hill-and-shore abodes of **Murugan / Kārttikeya / Skanda** — Śiva's war-god son — read as the six "battle-camps" (*paḍai vīḍu*) of his campaign against the asura Sūrapadma. The deity's myth, birth and pan-Indian identity sit in [ganesha-and-kartikeya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md); the Tamil temple-network logic sits in [275 Paadal Petra Sthalams](./275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md) and [temple-networks-and-circuits](./temple-networks-and-circuits.md). All six are concrete temples in Tamil Nadu — a recorded **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** devotional geography whose *sthala-puranas* nonetheless reach back to the cosmic war.

**Aṟu paḍai vīḍu** = "six war-camps / military-encampment houses" (Tamil *aṟu* "six" + *paḍai* "army/weapon" + *vīḍu* "house/camp"). The canon is fixed not by a Purana but by a **Sangam-era Tamil poem**: **Nakkīrar's Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭai** ("Guide to Lord Murukan"), which names the six in a set order. The list maps the *life-arc* of the god: warrior (Tiruchendur) → bridegroom (Tirupparankundram, Tiruttani) → renunciate (Palani) → teacher of his own father (Swamimalai) → grace-giver in the wild (Pazhamudircholai). [scholarly]

## The textual layer — two clocks

- **Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭai** - The founding charter; an *āṟṟuppaṭai* ("guiding [a poor bard] along the road") poem by **Nakkīrar** (poet of the Madurai Sangam), the **first piece of the *Pattuppāṭṭu*** ("Ten Idylls") anthology. Academically dated **c. 2nd–4th c. CE** (some push later); it is the **single oldest text** to enumerate the six abodes and is itself recited as a sacred hymn (counted in the Śaiva **Tirumurai** as part of the **11th Tirumurai**). It catalogues each shrine's landscape, worship and the god's six faces. (Source: Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭai [Nakkīrar]; Wikipedia, *Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭai*; murugan.org [Layne Little tr.]). [scholarly]
- **The original list — note the fifth slot** - Nakkīrar's six read: **Tirupparankundram, Tiruchendur, Tiruvāvinankudi (Palani), Tiruvēragam (Swamimalai), Kunṟutōṟāḍal, Pazhamudircholai.** The fifth, **Kunṟutōṟāḍal** ("[the god who] dances on every hill"), is originally a **roving / generic abode** — *wherever there is a hill* — and was only **later fixed onto Tiruttani**. The mapping of the archaic poem's six names onto the six modern temples is thus partly a medieval harmonisation. (Source: murugan.org [Ramachandran]; Wikipedia). [disputed] [scholarly]
- **Kanda Purāṇam** - The Tamil **Kanda Purāṇam** of **Kacchiyappa Sivāchāriyar** (Kanchipuram, ~14th c.), a Tamil rendering/expansion of the Sanskrit *Skanda-purāṇa*'s *Śankara-saṃhitā* (Śiva-rahasya), supplies the full war-narrative — birth from Śiva's six sparks, the slaying of **Sūrapadma / Tāraka / Simhamukha**, the marriages — that the temple legends localise. (Source: *Kanda Purāṇam* [Kacchiyappar]; *Skanda Purāṇa*). [late text]
- **Thiruppugazh** - **Aruṇagirinātar** (15th c., Tiruvannamalai) sang the **Tiruppugazh** ("holy praise"), thousands of rhythmic (*chandam*) hymns to Murugan across his shrines — the great medieval *bhakti* layer over the Aṟupaḍai network. His **Kandar Anubhūti**, **Kandar Alankāram** and **Kandar Anthāthi** are companion classics. (Source: *Thiruppugazh* [Arunagirinathar]; kaumaram.com). [late text]
- **Other Sangam/early strata** - **Paripāḍal** (Sangam anthology) hymns to **Cevvēḷ / Murukaṉ** and to the river Vaiyai; the **Tolkāppiyam** already fixes Murukaṉ as the god of *kuṟiñci* (the hill landscape) — the indigenous Tamil hill-god later **syncretised** with the Sanskritic Skanda/Kārttikeya. (Source: Tolkāppiyam; Paripāḍal). [scholarly]

## The shared myth-cycle behind the camps

- **Sūrapadma and the boon** - The asura **Sūrapadma** (with brothers **Tārakāsura** and **Simhamukha**), unconquerable by all but a son of Śiva, terrorises the devas; Indra's realm is lost. Murugan is generated from Śiva's six fiery sparks (the *Kṛttikā* / Pleiades nurse the six infants, fused into one six-faced **Śaṇmukha**) to be the deva commander (*Devasenāpati*). (Source: *Kanda Purāṇam*; *Skanda Purāṇa*). See [ganesha-and-kartikeya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md).
- **The Vēl** - Murugan's lance, the **Vēl**, is given by his mother **Pārvatī/Śakti** as her own power crystallised — *Śakti-vēl*. The battle-cry **"Vēl! Vetri Vēl!"** ("the Vēl! the victorious Vēl!") is the network's mantra; at Tirupparankundram and elsewhere *abhiṣeka* is performed **to the Vēl itself**, not only the icon. (Source: Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭai; *Kanda Purāṇam*).
- **The seaside battle & the mango tree** - At **Tiruchendur** Murugan fasts six days, then fells Sūrapadma. The dying asura turns into a great **mango tree**; the Vēl splits it in two — one half becomes the **peacock** (Murugan's *vāhana*), the other the **cock/rooster** that mounts his banner (*sēval koḍi*). The defeated asura is thus *redeemed into service*, not annihilated. (Source: *Kanda Purāṇam*; Surasamharam legend).
- **Soorasamharam / Skanda Ṣaṣṭhī** - The six-day **Kanda Ṣaṣṭhī** fast (Tamil month Aippasi, Oct–Nov) culminates in **Sūrasamhāram** — a ritual folk re-enactment on the **Tiruchendur** seashore of the asura's defeat before hundreds of thousands; the next day is **Tiru-kalyāṇam** (Murugan's wedding to Deivānai). (Source: Wikipedia, *Surasamharam*; murugan.org). [folk]
- **The two consorts** - **Deivānai / Teyvayāṉai** (Skt. *Devasenā*, "army of the devas"), Indra's foster-daughter, won as the **dharmic deva-bride** after victory; and **Valli**, a hill/hunter (*kuṟavar*) girl wooed in the wild — the **love-marriage**, indigenous Tamil layer. The two consorts = the **Sanskritic + Dravidian** double face of the cult. (Source: Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭai; *Kanda Purāṇam*; Paripāḍal).

## The six abodes — in Nakkīrar's order

- **1. Tirupparankundram (Tiruppараṅkuṉṟam)** — *the marriage to Deivānai* - Rock-cut cave temple ~6 mi south of **Madurai**; the camp where, **after** the victory over Sūrapadma, Murugan **wed Deivānai** (Indra's foster-daughter). Sangam-period Tamil rock-cut architecture; the sanctum enshrines the **Vēl**, to which all *abhiṣekas* are made; summit shrine to Kāśi Viśvanāthar with a statue of poet **Nakkīrar**. Associated with the *east/sunrise*. (Source: murugan.org [Ramachandran]; Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭai 1). Also a [Paadal Petra Sthalam](./275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md) on the Śaiva side.
- **2. Tiruchendur (Tiruccentūr / Jayantipuram)** — *the battlefield by the sea* - The **only seashore** abode, facing the Bay of Bengal in the far south (Thoothukudi dist.); the **site of the slaying of Sūrapadma**. Main deity **Senthilnāthan / Subramaṇya**; the six-faced **Śaṇmukha** form; *theertham* uses sacred **sea-water**. The annual **Sūrasamhāram** is staged here. A Valli cave-shrine lies to the north. (Source: murugan.org; Wikipedia, *Surasamharam*; *Kanda Purāṇam*).
- **3. Palani (Paḻani / Tiruvāvinankudi)** — *Daṇḍāyudhapāṇi, the renunciate* - Western-Ghats hill temple. The **jñāna-paḻam (fruit-of-wisdom) legend**: Nārada brings a single fruit; Śiva sets a contest — it goes to whichever son **first circles the world thrice**. Murugan races on his peacock; **Gaṇeśa** simply circumambulates his **parents** (the world to him) and wins. The slighted Murugan storms off to the **Pazhani hill** as a renunciate; consoled with **"Paḻam nī"** ("*thou art* the fruit [of wisdom]") — folk-etymology of *Paḻani*. He stands as **Daṇḍāyudhapāṇi** — staff-bearing ascetic, stripped to a loincloth, having renounced the fruit. Icon traditionally credited to the siddha **Bhogar**, cast from **nine medicinal minerals (navapāṣāṇa)**. Disciple **Iḍumban** carries twin hills on a *kāvaḍi* — origin of **kāvaḍi**-bearing pilgrimage. (Source: murugan.org [Ramachandran]; *Kanda Purāṇam*). See the parallel race-story in [ganesha-and-kartikeya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md).
  - *Variant (race outcome):* in some tellings the fruit/race story is the *reason* Murugan leaves **Kailāsa** for the Tamil south altogether — a charter for the whole Aṟupaḍai geography. [folk]
- **4. Swamimalai (Swāmimalai / Tiruvēragam)** — *the son teaches the father* - On an artificial hillock near **Kumbakonam**. Murugan, having **imprisoned Brahmā** for failing to explain the meaning of **Praṇava (Oṃ)**, then expounds Oṃ **to Śiva himself**, becoming the *guru of his own father* — hence **Swāminātha(n)** ("lord of the Lord"). 60 steps = the 60-year Jovian cycle; the region is the great **Chola bronze-casting** centre. (Source: murugan.org; *Kanda Purāṇam*).
  - *Variant (vāhana):* local lore gives the Swamimalai Murugan an **elephant** vāhana (Airāvata, gifted by Indra) rather than the peacock — unique among the six. [folk]
- **5. Tiruttani (Tiruttaṇi / Kunṟutōṟāḍal)** — *blissful repose; the marriage to Valli* - Hill ~60 mi from Chennai; *taṇi* = "calm/repose" — Murugan's **post-war rest**. Here he **woos and weds Valli**, the hunter-girl of the *kuṟiñci* hills (the love-match counterpart to Deivānai). Identified with Nakkīrar's roving fifth abode **Kunṟutōṟāḍal**. 365 steps; **Aḍi Kṛttikai** festival. (Source: murugan.org [Ramachandran]; Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭai 5). [disputed — see "fifth slot" above]
- **6. Pazhamudircholai (Paḻamudircōlai)** — *grace in the wild; the Avvaiyār riddle* - Forest shrine on the **Alagar hills** ~12 mi from Madurai, amid waterfalls — Murugan **with both consorts** (Valli & Deivānai). Stage of the **Avvaiyār legend**: the aged poet-saint **Avvaiyār**, resting under a *nāval* (jamun) tree, is asked by a boy (Murugan in disguise) whether she wants **"sutta paḻam or sudāda paḻam"** — *roasted* fruit or *unroasted* fruit. The learned Avvaiyār is humbled by the unexpected riddle (paḻam = fruit; the boy means "do you want fruit that has been blown-cool or not"), realising the child is the god — a parable of the limits of bookish learning before grace. (Source: murugan.org; Tamil tradition; *Vinayaka/Avvaiyar* lore). [folk]

## The Vēl, the Vetri-Vēl and shared cult features

- **Śakti-vēl as the icon of the network** - The Vēl unifies all six; pilgrims chant **"Vēl Vetri Vēl"** ("Vēl, victorious Vēl") and **"Haro Hara"**. At Tirupparankundram the Vēl is the primary object of worship. (Source: Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭai; murugan.org).
- **Kāvaḍi** - The arched, decorated yoke-burden carried by penitents (originating with **Iḍumban** at Palani); **Tai Pūsam** and **Panguni Uttiram** are the great kāvaḍi festivals across the abodes. (Source: murugan.org [Palani]). [folk]
- **Peacock & cock** - *Vāhana* and banner, both born of the split Sūrapadma — the asura's energy *converted* to the god's service. (Source: *Kanda Purāṇam*).
- **The pilgrimage as a life-arc** - Completing all six (*Aṟupaḍai yāttirai*) is held to grant courage, wisdom and peace — each camp a "phase" from warrior to teacher to grace-giver. (Source: murugan.org; popular tradition).

## Folk extensions & contested seventh abode [folk] [disputed]

- **A "seventh paḍai vīḍu"** - No canon beyond Nakkīrar's six, but several temples are popularly nominated as an honorary **seventh**: chiefly **Marudhamalai** (Coimbatore — "equal to the Aṟupaḍai"), with **Kunrakudi**, **Sikkal**, **Vayalūr** and **Kundrathur** also advanced. None are scripturally part of the set. (Source: Wikipedia, *Maruthamalai*; murugan.org). [folk]
- **Kundrathur** - Murugan hill-temple near Chennai, locally styled a/the seventh camp. [folk]
- **Marudhamalai** - Western-Ghats Coimbatore shrine, the most common "seventh-abode" claimant. (Source: murugan.org [Marudhamalai]). [folk]
- **Kataragama (Kathirkāmam), Sri Lanka** - The great **trans-island** Murugan/Skanda shrine of the south of Sri Lanka — a major Kataragama-deviyo pilgrimage centre, often spoken of in the **diaspora** as belonging to the wider Murugan sacred geography though **outside the Tamil-Nadu Aṟupaḍai canon**. (Source: popular/diaspora tradition; murugan.org). [folk] [disputed]
- **Open roster note** - "Seventh-abode" claims are devotional and **non-canonical**; the fixed, source-cited set is and remains **six**, per Nakkīrar. Lists of honorary abodes vary by source and region. [flag: open-ended roster]
