# Aruṇācala / Tiruvaṇṇāmalai — the Fire-Hill that is Śiva

> Parent: [Sacred Geography](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md) · Related: [Pañca Bhūta Sthalams](pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md) · [12 Jyotirlingas](12-jyotirlingas.md) · [Meru, Kailāsa & Cosmic Mountains](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md) · [275 Pāḍal Petṟa Sthalams](275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md)

**Aruṇācala** ("red mountain," Tamil **Aṇṇāmalai**) at **Tiruvaṇṇāmalai** in northern Tamil Nadu is unique among Hindu sacred sites: the hill is not Śiva's *seat* but Śiva's *body* — the **agni-liṅga**, the fire-element manifestation among the [Pañca Bhūta Sthalams](pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md), worshipped as a *jyoti* (mass of light) congealed into earth. Where most *kṣetras* enshrine a stone *liṅga*, here the **whole mountain is the liṅga** and the temple at its foot is merely its forecourt. The **Aruṇācala-māhātmya** of the *Skanda Purāṇa* makes the claim explicit; the hill belongs in the first tier of **Tejas/fire sacred mountains** alongside the cosmic-mountain tradition of [Meru and Kailāsa](meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md). [scholarly]

---

## I. The Hill That Is Śiva — the Māhātmya Claim

- **Aruṇa-acala = "red, unmoving (mountain)"** — the name encodes the doctrine: *acala* ("immovable") is a standard epithet of Śiva as the unchanging Absolute, and *aruṇa* ("dawn-red") evokes fire. The hill is read as Śiva's static, fiery form. (Aruṇācala-māhātmya, *Skanda Purāṇa*.) [scholarly]
- **The hill IS Śiva, not his abode** — the *māhātmya* insists that whereas at Kāśī the Lord must be *sought* in a liṅga, **at Aruṇācala the form one sees with the eyes is the Lord himself**; merely *thinking of* (*smaraṇa*) Aruṇācala is said to grant liberation, a grace the text claims for no other *kṣetra*. (Aruṇācala-māhātmya.) [late text]
- **The "secret" mountain of the heart** — in the māhātmya's metaphysics Aruṇācala is identified with the **spiritual Heart (*hṛdaya*)**, the seat of the Self; the geographic hill is the outward sign of an inner reality. This reading is later foregrounded by Ramana Maharshi. [scholarly]
- **Frozen pillar of fire** — physically the element *agni/tejas* is embodied not in a dissolving liṅga (as earth at Kāñci or water at Tiruvāṉaikkāval) but in **the hill as a cooled column of flame**; a perpetual lamp is maintained in worship as its token. (Temple tradition; cf. [Pañca Bhūta Sthalams](pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md).)

## II. Liṅgodbhava — the Origin Myth (cross-yuga)

- **The dispute of Brahmā and Viṣṇu** — quarrelling over who was supreme creator and sustainer, the two gods are confronted by Śiva manifesting as an **infinite column of fire (*jyotir-liṅga*)** with no discernible top or bottom, challenging them to find its ends. (Liṅga Purāṇa, *Liṅgodbhava*; Aruṇācala-māhātmya.)
- **The boar and the swan fail** — **Viṣṇu as the boar Varāha** burrowed downward to find the base; **Brahmā as a swan/goose (haṃsa)** flew upward to find the summit. **Neither could reach an end** — the pillar was boundless, revealing Śiva as the unmanifest source transcending both creator and preserver. (Liṅga Purāṇa.)
  - *Variant (Brahmā's false witness — Liṅga Purāṇa):* Brahmā, failing, **falsely claimed** to have seen the top, suborning a *ketakī* (screw-pine) flower and a cow as witnesses; for this lie Śiva curses Brahmā to receive **no worship** and the *ketakī* to be **barred from his pūjā** — the standard pan-Indian etiology shared with the [12 Jyotirlingas](12-jyotirlingas.md).
- **The pillar cooled into the hill** — in the Aruṇācala recension the unbearable fiery column, at the gods' plea, **cooled and condensed into the Aruṇācala hill**, with a worshippable liṅga (Arunachaleswarar / **Aṇṇāmalaiyār**) installed at its base. Thus the mountain *is* the Liṅgodbhava pillar made tangible. (Aruṇācala-māhātmya, *Skanda Purāṇa*.)
  - *Variant (Pārvatī's penance — Aruṇācala-māhātmya):* Pārvatī, in play, **covered Śiva's eyes**, plunging the cosmos into darkness; sent to Tiruvaṇṇāmalai to do penance, she worshipped the hill, slew the demon **Mahiṣa** nearby, and was at last **re-merged into Śiva's left half** — an **Ardhanārīśvara** origin attached to the site. This is why the goddess here is **Apītakucāmbāḷ / Uṇṇāmulai** (see below).

## III. The Aruṇācaleśvara (Aṇṇāmalaiyār) Temple & Goddess

- **Arunachaleswarar / Aṇṇāmalaiyār** — the vast temple at the hill's eastern foot, one of the largest in India (~25 acres, nine-storey *gopurams*, the **Rājagopuram** ~66 m), enshrines the agni-liṅga; the deity is "Lord of the great hill." (Sthala-purāṇa; temple record.) [scholarly]
- **Apītakucāmbāḷ / Uṇṇāmulai** — the consort-goddess. The Sanskrit **Apītakucāmbāḷ** ("she whose breast has not been suckled") renders the Tamil **Uṇṇāmulai** ("unsuckled breast"); legend holds the milk in her breast was retained for grace and never drunk, or that she nursed a saint/child-devotee. (Sthala-purāṇa.) [regional]
- **Pātāḷa-liṅga** — within the temple, the underground cell where the sage-to-be **Ramana** sat in absorbed *tapas* while ants and vermin gnawed his body — now a pilgrimage spot in its own right. [folk]

## IV. Girivalam — Circumambulating the Hill

- **Giri-pradakṣiṇā / Girivalam** — because the **hill itself is the liṅga**, the supreme worship is to **circumambulate it on foot**, a ~**14 km** barefoot circuit (Tamil *giri-valam*), held to burn sins and grant liberation; performed especially on **full-moon (paurṇami)** nights, when hundreds of thousands walk the path. (Aruṇācala-māhātmya; living tradition.) [regional]
- **The eight directional liṅgams (Aṣṭa-liṅga)** — the Girivalam path is studded with **eight liṅgams** keyed to the eight directions and their guardian planets/*dikpālas*, worshipped in sequence around the hill:
  - *Indra-liṅga* (east) · *Agni-liṅga* (southeast) · *Yama-liṅga* (south) · *Niruti-liṅga* (southwest) · *Varuṇa-liṅga* (west) · *Vāyu-liṅga* (northwest) · *Kubera-liṅga* (north) · *Īśāna-liṅga* (northeast). (Temple/Girivalam tradition.) [regional]
- **Merit of the circuit** — the māhātmya promises that one *giri-pradakṣiṇā* equals incalculable other pilgrimages; the act, not a temple-entry, is the defining devotion of Aruṇācala. [late text]

## V. Karthigai Deepam — the Beacon of Fire

- **The Mahā-Dīpa atop the hill** — at the **Kārttikai** full moon (Tamil month Kārttikai, Nov–Dec) a colossal cauldron of **ghee with a great wick (the *Bharaṇi/Mahā Dīpam*)** is kindled **on the summit of Aruṇācala**, blazing for days and visible for many kilometres — a literal re-enactment of the **original pillar of fire (jyoti)**. (Temple festival; Aruṇācala-māhātmya.) [regional]
- **Theology of the beacon** — the flame is understood as Śiva once more showing his fiery, endless form to gods and men; lighting and seeing the Mahā-Dīpa is itself liberating *darśana*. It is among the greatest **fire-festivals** of South India. [regional]

## VI. Devotional & Modern Layers

- **Tēvāram (7th–8th c.)** — Aruṇācala is a **[Pāḍal Petṟa Sthalam](275-paadal-petra-sthalams.md)**, sung by the **Nāyanmār** (Sambandar, Appar, Sundarar) in the Tamil **Tēvāram** hymns — the earliest devotional witness anchoring the site in Tamil Śaiva-Siddhānta. (Tēvāram, Tirumuṟai 1–7.) [scholarly]
- **Tiruppugaḻ (15th c.)** — **Aruṇagirinātar**, born at Tiruvaṇṇāmalai, composed his **Tiruppugaḻ** hymns (primarily to Murugan) here; tradition holds the hill's grace turned him from dissolution to sainthood — Aruṇācala is woven into his hagiography. (Tiruppugaḻ; Nāyanmar-era lore.) [regional]
- **Ramaṇa Mahaṛṣi (1879–1950)** — the modern sage **Ramana**, drawn to Tiruvaṇṇāmalai in 1896, made the hill the seat of his lifelong *tapas*, holding **Aruṇācala to be Śiva himself** and the outward form of the Heart/Self. His **Five Hymns to Aruṇācala** (*Akṣaramaṇamālai*, etc.) re-articulate the māhātmya's "the hill IS the Lord" for the modern age; **Ramaṇāśrama** at its foot is now a global pilgrimage centre. (Ramana Maharshi corpus.) [scholarly][modern]
- **Two clocks** — the *scriptural* clock places Aruṇācala at the Liṅgodbhava, before structured time (cross-yuga / creation); the *historical* clock dates its attested cult to the **7th-c. Tēvāram**, its grand temple to the **Chola–Vijayanagara–Nāyaka** centuries, and Ramana to the **20th c.** The mountain bridges both. [scholarly]
