# Godavari — The Gautami and the Dakshina Ganga

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Sacred Geography](README.md) → **Godāvarī / Gautamī — Dakṣiṇa Gaṅgā**. The second-longest river of India and the "**Gaṅgā of the South**," brought down from Śiva's locks by the *tapas* of sage **Gautama** after he was framed for **go-hatyā** (cow-slaughter) by jealous sages — the descent that seats the **[Tryambakeśvar Jyotirliṅga](12-jyotirlingas.md)**, the **[Nāsik–Tryambak Siṃhastha Kumbh](kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md)**, and **Rāma's [Pañcavaṭī exile](panchavati-nashik-godavari.md)**.

## I. Identity, names & the "two clocks"

- **Godāvarī = the river "that bestows water/cattle"** — the great river of the Deccan, rising at **Tryambak (Trimbak)** below **Brahmagiri** hill in Nāsik district, Maharashtra, and flowing ~1,465 km east-southeast across the peninsula to the **Bay of Bengal** delta (Andhra Pradesh) — the **second-longest river of India** after the Gaṅgā. [scholarly]
- **Dakṣiṇa Gaṅgā ("Gaṅgā of the South")** — the river's foremost epithet, ranking it as the southern counterpart and equal of the Himalayan Gaṅgā; a bath in it is held equivalent to a bath in the Gaṅgā, and its descent-myth is consciously modelled on **Gaṅgāvataraṇa** (cf. [Sacred Rivers & Descent Myths](sacred-rivers-and-descent-myths.md)). (*Brahma Purāṇa, Gautamī-māhātmya*)
- **Gautamī (Gautamī Gaṅgā)** — the river's ritual/Puranic name, "the river of **Gautama**," after the sage whose penance drew it down; the *Brahma Purāṇa's* river-glorification is titled the **Gautamī-māhātmya**. (*Brahma Purāṇa*)
- **The Gautamī-māhātmya — the longest river-mahatmya in the Purāṇas** — the *Brahma Purāṇa* devotes an enormous block (traditionally ~105+ chapters) to the Godāvarī and its bank-tīrthas, the single most extensive river-glorification in Puranic literature. [scholarly]
- **Scriptural clock** — the descent is placed in the deep mythic past of the present **[Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)**, an age of *ṛṣi*-penance long before the *Rāmāyaṇa*; the river is thus already ancient and holy when **Rāma** settles on its banks in the **Tretā-yuga**. (*Brahma Purāṇa*; *Rāmāyaṇa*)
- **Historical clock** — as a living landscape, the Godāvarī's source-temples, ghāṭs and the Siṃhastha Kumbh are continuously worshipped medieval-to-modern overlays elaborated by the *māhātmya* literature and the Marāṭhā-era temple-building. [scholarly]

## II. The descent of the Gautamī — Gautama and the phantom cow

- **The drought and Gautama's grain** — in a great twelve-year **drought (anāvṛṣṭi)** in the Deccan, the sage **Gautama** (with wife **Ahalyā**) propitiates **Varuṇa** and obtains an unfailing supply of grain/water at his **Brahmagiri** hermitage, feeding the starving sages who gather around him. (*Brahma Purāṇa, Gautamī-māhātmya*)
- **The jealousy of the sages** — when the rains return and the other ṛṣis prepare to disperse, they grow **envious** of Gautama's accrued merit and the honour he enjoys, and conspire to drive him from his own hermitage by a stratagem. (*Brahma Purāṇa*)
- **The phantom-cow ruse (māyā-dhenu)** — the sages create (or send) an **illusory, dying cow** (a *māyā-gau*, in some tellings fashioned by the jealous ṛṣis or sent by Gaṇeśa at their behest) that wanders into Gautama's ripening field; when Gautama drives it off with a sprinkle of water or a wisp of *darbha* grass, the cow **collapses dead**, and he is at once charged with **go-hatyā** — the slaughter of a cow, a cardinal sin. (*Brahma Purāṇa, Gautamī-māhātmya*)
  - *Variant (Gaṇeśa-instrument):* in a strand close to the **Tryambakeśvar** legend, **Gaṇeśa** himself assumes the cow-form at the sages' request, making the framing a trial engineered through a god. (*Brahma Purāṇa* / sthala-tradition) [regional]
- **The false expiation demanded** — the same conspiring sages, feigning to help, tell Gautama his sin can be wiped only by **circling Brahmagiri thrice**, performing severe penance, and above all by **bringing the Gaṅgā down** to bathe the spot — an impossible task meant to expel him. (*Brahma Purāṇa*)
- **The propitiation of Śiva** — Gautama undertakes a fierce *tapas* to **Śiva** at Brahmagiri/Tryambak; Śiva, pleased, appears with **Pārvatī** and offers a boon. (*Brahma Purāṇa*; *Skanda Purāṇa*)
- **The release of Gaṅgā as Gautamī** — Gautama begs for the **Gaṅgā** to descend and purify him; Śiva **looses a strand of Gaṅgā from his matted locks (jaṭā)**, and she flows out at Brahmagiri as the **Gautamī Gaṅgā / Godāvarī**, washing away the cow-sin and remaining on earth as the Dakṣiṇa Gaṅgā for the welfare of the world. (*Brahma Purāṇa, Gautamī-māhātmya*)
- **Gautama vindicated** — the descent both clears Gautama of the engineered go-hatyā and turns the sages' malice into a permanent blessing for the south; the very plot meant to ruin him produces the holiest river of the Deccan. (*Brahma Purāṇa*)

## III. Variants of the descent

- *Variant (the bound river / Gaṅgā's reluctance):* the released Gaṅgā, unwilling to leave Śiva's locks, repeatedly tries to vanish underground or flee; Gautama (or Śiva) **binds her** with *darbha* grass and a vow to flow on, so she rises again at successive points — explaining the river's interrupted, spring-fed emergence below Brahmagiri. (*Brahma Purāṇa* / sthala-tradition) [regional]
- *Variant (Gaṅgādvāra / the river's many mouths):* the spot where the strand of Gaṅgā first issues is called **Gaṅgādvāra ("Gaṅgā's gate")** at Tryambak; the *māhātmya* further multiplies the river into branches and sub-tīrthas, each a "mouth" (the deltaic Godāvarī famously splits into many channels — popularly the **Sapta-Godāvarī**, "seven Godāvarīs"). (*Brahma Purāṇa*) [regional]
- *Variant (Kuśāvarta as the true source):* because the spring at Brahmagiri's very top is intermittent, tradition fixes the **Kuśāvarta kuṇḍa** at Tryambak — where Gautama is said to have caught and bound the river with *kuśa/darbha* grass — as the **ritual source** and bathing-point of the Godāvarī, even though the geographic spring is higher on the hill. (sthala-tradition) [regional]
- *Variant (drought-relief emphasis):* some retellings foreground the **drought-ending** boon over the cow-sin, casting Gautama's whole penance as an act to bring water to a parched south rather than a personal expiation. (*Brahma Purāṇa* recensional emphasis) [disputed]

## IV. Tryambakeśvar — the Jyotirliṅga at the source

- **Tryambakeśvar Jyotirliṅga** — at the Godāvarī's source below Brahmagiri stands **Tryambakeśvar**, one of the twelve **[Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md)**, named in the *Dvādaśa-Jyotirliṅga Stotra* as *"Tryambakaṃ Gautamī-taṭe"* — "Tryambaka **on the bank of the Gautamī**," welding the liṅga to the river-myth. (*Śiva Purāṇa*; *Brahma Purāṇa*)
- **The three-faced liṅga (tri-ambaka)** — uniquely, the liṅga bears **three faces** (*tri-ambaka*, "three-eyed") for **Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śiva**, set in a hollow; it is the *svayambhu* abode where Śiva remained after granting Gautama the river. (sthala-tradition) — see [12 Jyotirlingas](12-jyotirlingas.md)
- **Brahmagiri & Kuśāvarta** — the hill **Brahmagiri** is venerated as a form of Śiva (climbing it is itself a *pradakṣiṇā* of the liṅga), and the **Kuśāvarta** tank below is the principal bathing-tīrtha and the river's ceremonial head. (sthala-tradition) [regional]

## V. Sanctity & pilgrimage on the Godāvarī

- **The Nāsik–Tryambak Siṃhastha Kumbh** — the Godāvarī's source-region hosts one of the **four [Kumbh Mela](kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md)** fairs, the **Siṃhastha** ("Jupiter-in-Siṃha/Leo") Kumbh, held roughly every **twelve years** when **Bṛhaspati (Jupiter) enters Siṃha (Leo)**; bathing is split by sectarian custom between **Rāmkuṇḍ** at Nāsik (Vaiṣṇava akhāḍās) and **Kuśāvarta** at Tryambak (Śaiva akhāḍās). (pilgrimage tradition) — see [Kumbh Mela & Pilgrimage Cycles](kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md)
- **Rāma's Pañcavaṭī on the Godāvarī** — the river's middle course at **Nāsik** is the scriptural **[Pañcavaṭī](panchavati-nashik-godavari.md)**, where **Rāma, Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa** built their leaf-hut in the **Tretā-yuga** exile — the stage of the Śūrpaṇakhā mutilation, the golden-deer ruse, and **Sītā's abduction**; the **Rāmkuṇḍ** tank on the Godāvarī is Nāsik's chief *asthi-vilaya* ("bone-dissolving") tīrtha for immersing ashes. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa*; sthala-tradition) — see [Pañcavaṭī / Nāsik](panchavati-nashik-godavari.md)
- **Bank-tīrthas of the Gautamī** — the *Gautamī-māhātmya* threads the whole river with tīrthas; downstream the Godāvarī carries the great **island-shrine of Bhadrācalam** (Rāma) and the deltaic **Sapta-Godāvarī** at Rājahmundry / Kotipalli, where the river's seven mouths are themselves a pilgrimage circuit. (*Brahma Purāṇa*; regional sthala-tradition) [regional]
- **Standing among the rivers** — in the litany of India's holy rivers (*Gaṅgā, Yamunā, Godāvarī, Sarasvatī, Narmadā, Sindhu, Kāverī*), the Godāvarī is invariably the senior southern river, the **Dakṣiṇa Gaṅgā** that lends the Deccan its share in the Gaṅgā's purifying power. (invocatory tradition; cf. [Sacred Rivers & Descent Myths](sacred-rivers-and-descent-myths.md)) [scholarly]

## Sources

- **Brahma Purāṇa** — the **Gautamī-māhātmya** (the longest river-glorification in the Purāṇas): the twelve-year drought and Varuṇa's boon, the jealousy of the sages, the **phantom-cow (māyā-dhenu)** framing of Gautama for **go-hatyā**, his *tapas* to Śiva at Brahmagiri, and the descent of the **Gautamī Gaṅgā / Godāvarī** from Śiva's locks; the Gaṅgādvāra, Kuśāvarta and Sapta-Godāvarī bank-tīrthas.
- **Skanda Purāṇa** — Śaiva pilgrimage material on Tryambak/Brahmagiri and the Godāvarī tīrthas paralleling the Brahma Purāṇa account.
- **Śiva Purāṇa** — the **Tryambakeśvar Jyotirliṅga** legend (Gautama, the *māyā*-cow, the three-faced liṅga) and the *Dvādaśa-Jyotirliṅga Stotra* epithet *"Tryambakaṃ Gautamī-taṭe."*
- **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa** — Rāma's Pañcavaṭī residence on the Godāvarī (the leaf-hut, Śūrpaṇakhā, the golden deer, Sītā's abduction).
