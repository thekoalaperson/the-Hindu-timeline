# Dvārakā & the Saurāṣṭra Kṛṣṇa Kṣetras

> **Dvārakā** (*Dvāravatī*, "the many-gated," "the city of doors") — Kṛṣṇa's island-capital on the western tip of the **Saurāṣṭra** (Kāṭhiāwāḍ) peninsula of Gujarat, where the [Yādava](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) clan migrated from [Mathurā](mathura-vrindavan-braj.md) to escape **Jarāsandha**, and where, after the Yādava self-slaughter of the **Mausala Parva**, the sea reclaimed the city at the **Dvāpara–Kali hinge** (3102 BCE in scripture-time). One of the **[Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)** (western point), one of the **[Sapta Purī](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md)**, a **[Divya Deśam](108-divya-desams.md)** (Tirudvāraka) and a **Śaṅkara maṭha** seat — and the node of the famous "which-Dvārakā?" dispute.

**Naming & extent** — *Dvārakā / Dvāravatī*, "the gated city," is read either from its many fortified gateways or as the "gateway to mokṣa." The kṣetra spans the modern **Devbhūmi Dvārakā** district: the mainland temple-town of **Dvārakā** at the Gomatī mouth, the offshore island of **Beṭ (Beyṭ) Dvārakā / Śaṅkhoddhāra** in the Gulf of Kutch, and outlying claimants such as **Mūla-Dvārakā** at Koḍīnār — together feeding the "original Dvārakā" debate and the **Pañca-Dvārakā** pilgrim-circuit. (*Mahābhārata*; *Harivaṃśa*; *Bhāgavata*, *Viṣṇu* & *Skanda Purāṇa*; Wikipedia, "Dwarka" / "Dvārakā")

## I. The founding — Kṛṣṇa's flight west & the golden sea-fortress

- **Why Dvārakā was built** - harried 17 (or 18) times by **Jarāsandha** of Magadha (Kṛṣṇa's father-in-law's avenger) and threatened by **Kālayavana**, Kṛṣṇa abandoned [Mathurā](mathura-vrindavan-braj.md) and led the **Yādava/Vṛṣṇi–Andhaka** clans west to the sea-girt safety of Saurāṣṭra — earning the epithet **Raṇacchoḍa / Ranchhod** ("he who quit the battlefield"), later enshrined at Dākor. (*Bhāgavata* 10.50–52; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 5.23; *Harivaṃśa*)
- **Vishvakarman's one-day city** - Kṛṣṇa asked the ocean-god **Samudra** for land, and **Viśvakarman** (the deva-architect) raised the city — by the *Harivaṃśa* (2.58.40–44) built "in a single day" and even "mentally" — a fortified metropolis on reclaimed sea-land, ringed by ramparts of gold. (*Harivaṃśa* 2.58; *Bhāgavata* 10.50.50)
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata):* the sea yields **12 yojanas** of land at Kṛṣṇa's request; the city is laid out with gardens, lakes, jeweled palaces and the **Sudharmā** assembly-hall brought from Indra's heaven. (*Bhāgavata* 10.50.50–56)
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata, Sabhā/Mausala):* Dvārakā as a fortress-island defended by the sea on all sides, with mechanical gates and engines — hence *Dvāravatī*. (*Mbh* 2; 16)
- **The golden, jeweled splendour** - epic-Purāṇic descriptions make it a city of gold, with **900,000 royal palaces** of crystal and silver, gem-paved roads, and parks (*Raivataka* hill nearby) — the prototype "lost golden city." (*Harivaṃśa*; *Bhāgavata* 10.69, the *gṛhastha-līlā* of Kṛṣṇa's 16,108 queens)
- **Raivataka & the Yādava polity** - the hill **Raivataka** (Girnar's environs) hosted Yādava festivals; the clan was a republic of *saṅgha* chiefs (Ugrasena nominal king), with Kṛṣṇa, **Balarāma**, **Sātyaki**, **Kṛtavarman**, **Pradyumna**, **Aniruddha** and **Uddhava** among its leaders. (*Bhāgavata* 10; *Mbh* 16.1) [open-ended roster]

## II. The mainland temple-town — Dvārakādhīśa / Jagat Mandir

- **Dvārakādhīśa Jagat Mandir** - the five-storey **Jagat Mandir** ("temple of the world"), enshrining Kṛṣṇa as **Dvārakādhīśa** ("Lord of Dvārakā") in his four-armed **Trivikrama** form of black stone; the spire rises ~**78 m** (≈157 ft) on **72 pillars**. The sanctum (*Nija Mandir*) is held to stand on **Kṛṣṇa's own residential ground** (Hari-gṛha), said built by his great-grandson **Vajra(nābha)**; the visible structure was rebuilt/expanded c. 15th–16th c., the name *Jagat Mandir* linked to **Rājā Jagat Siṃha Rāṭhoḍ**. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Dvārakā-māhātmya*; Wikipedia, "Dwarkadhish Temple")
- **The two gates — Svarga & Mokṣa** - pilgrims **enter by the Svarga-dvāra** ("gate of heaven," from the Gomatī ghāṭ) and **exit by the Mokṣa-dvāra** ("gate of liberation") — the architectural sermon that darśana here grants release. (temple tradition; Wikipedia)
- **The flag (dhvajā) ritual** - the **52-yard pennant** atop the spire, marked with **sun and moon** (Kṛṣṇa's emblems, signifying he will shine while sun and moon endure), is changed **up to five times daily** by the hereditary **Abhoṭi/Brāhmaṇa** flag-bearers — an iconic living rite. (temple tradition; Wikipedia)
- **Vajranābha's restoration tradition** - post-submergence, Kṛṣṇa's lineage through **Aniruddha → Vajra** is credited with re-founding worship and raising the original Hari-gṛha shrine — the bridge from the drowned Dvāpara city to the historical temple. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Dvārakā-māhātmya*; Vaiṣṇava tradition) [late text]
- **Idol-shifting of 1551** - when **Mahmud Begada / "Turk Aziz" / Aurangzeb-era** raiders threatened Dvārakā, the deity is said to have been moved to **Beṭ Dvārakā** for safety — anchoring Beṭ's claim as Kṛṣṇa's home. (Wikipedia, "Dwarkadhish Temple")
- **Rukmiṇī Devī temple** - the 12th-c. temple of **Rukmiṇī**, Kṛṣṇa's chief queen, stands curiously **~2 km outside** the main town. Folk *sthala* tale: the sage **Durvāsā**, yoked to her chariot by Kṛṣṇa and Rukmiṇī, cursed that she would thirst and dwell apart — explaining the separation and the local custom of offering water. (local *sthala-purāṇa*) [folk]
- **Gomatī confluence & Gomtī-snāna** - the small **Gomatī** (distinct from the Gangetic Gomtī) meets the **Arabian Sea** at Dvārakā; the prescribed sequence is **bathe at the Gomatī ghāṭ first**, then take darśana of Dvārakādhīśa. The 56-step **Svarga-dvāra ghāṭ**, **Samudra-Nārāyaṇa** temple at the river-sea sangam, and shrines to **Sea-god / Sarasvatī / Lakṣmī** mark the confluence; the **Pañca-tīrtha / Gomtī-kuṇḍa** dip is a core ritual. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Dvārakā-māhātmya*; Wikipedia)

## III. Beṭ Dvārakā (Śaṅkhoddhāra) — Kṛṣṇa's residence

- **Beṭ / Beyṭ Dvārakā** - the island **~30 km north** of Dvārakā town in the **Gulf of Kutch** (reached by ferry from Okhā), traditionally held to be **Kṛṣṇa's actual dwelling-place (*niṣ-kuṭa* / residence)** where he lived with his queens, while the mainland was the *capital* — i.e., palace vs. throne-city. (Wikipedia, "Bet Dwarka"; Gujarat Tourism)
- **Śaṅkhoddhāra — the conch name** - the island is also **Śaṅkhoddhāra**, "raising of the conch," from (a) its abundant **śaṅkha (conch) beds**, and (b) the legend that Kṛṣṇa here slew the conch-demon **Śaṅkhāsura** and recovered the Vedas / the conch — the same myth tied to his **Pāñcajanya** conch. (*sthala-purāṇa*; Wikipedia) [folk]
- **Sudāmā / the rice legend** - Beṭ is the celebrated setting of the **Sudāmā (Kucela)** reunion, where the poor Brāhmaṇa friend's pinch of beaten rice (*pohā*) is repaid with untold wealth — a key *bhakti* parable of Kṛṣṇa's love for the lowly. (*Bhāgavata* 10.80–81)
- **Hanumān Ḍāṇḍī & other island shrines** - the **Hanumān Ḍāṇḍī** temple (Hanumān with his son **Makaradhvaja**, born of his sweat swallowed by a fish), Kṛṣṇa's palace-temple with the queens' shrines (**Rukmiṇī, Satyabhāmā, Jāmbavatī, Devakī, Rādhā, Lakṣmī**), and Keśava shrines dot the island. (Wikipedia; local tradition) [open-ended roster]
- **The "old port" claim** - tradition holds Beṭ was the **ancient port** of Kṛṣṇa's kingdom *before* the mainland **Okhā/Dvārakā** harbour developed — strengthening its "original residence" reputation. (Gujarat Tourism)

## IV. The "Which Dvārakā?" dispute & Mūla-Dvārakā

- **The core problem** - texts and tradition attach "Dvārakā" to several Saurāṣṭra sites, and Kṛṣṇa's *original* Dvārakā is **drowned**; competing places each claim primacy — the perennial **which-Dvārakā** question. (scholarly & traditional debate)
- **Mūla-Dvārakā (Koḍīnār)** - **"Mūla" = root/origin**: a coastal shrine near **Koḍīnār** (Gīr Somnāth dist.) held to be where Kṛṣṇa **first halted and established himself** on arriving from Mathurā, before founding the great sea-capital — i.e., the *first* Dvārakā. For many devotees the Pañca-Dvārakā *yātrā* **begins** here. (Gujarat tradition; gujaratexpert.com) [disputed]
- **Beṭ Dvārakā as "real" Dvārakā** - the rival view: Beṭ/Śaṅkhoddhāra was Kṛṣṇa's lived-in home, the mainland merely the administrative seat — so the "true" Dvārakā is offshore. (Wikipedia, "Bet Dwarka") [disputed]
- **Mainland Dvārakā as the capital** - the orthodox Char-Dhām / Divya-Deśam identification fixes the **Jagat Mandir town** as Dvārakā proper; the underwater ruins off **both** the mainland and Beṭ are read as the same submerged complex. [disputed]
- **The Bhalka / Prabhāsa knot** - confusingly close by, **Bhālkā Tīrtha** and **Prabhāsa (Somnāth)** belong to Kṛṣṇa's *death*, not his city — see §V; the geography of the Yādava end is itself contested. (*Mbh* 16; *Bhāgavata* 11)

## V. The Mausala Parva — the drowning at the Dvāpara–Kali hinge

- **The Mausala Parva** - the 16th and one of the shortest books of the [Mahābhārata](../90-literature-corpus/), narrating, **36 years after Kurukṣetra**, the self-destruction of the Yādavas, Kṛṣṇa's departure, and Dvārakā's submergence — the literal closing of the [Dvāpara Yuga](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md). (*Mbh*, Mausala Parva)
- **The iron-club curse (*mausala*)** - sages (**Viśvāmitra, Kaṇva, Nārada**), mocked when young Yādavas disguised **Sāmba** as a pregnant woman and asked the child's sex, curse that he will bear an **iron club (musala)** that destroys the clan. Sāmba births an iron bolt; the Yādavas grind it to **powder thrown into the sea** — which washes ashore as **eraka grass / reeds**, and the bolt's hard nub is swallowed by a **fish** later caught by the hunter **Jarā**. (*Mbh* 16.1–2; *Bhāgavata* 11.1)
- **The fratricidal massacre at Prabhāsa** - drunk on **maireya** liquor at the **Prabhāsa** seaside festival, the Yādavas quarrel; **Sātyaki** and **Kṛtavarman** spark it, and the warriors uproot the magically lethal **eraka reeds** as clubs and **slaughter one another** — Pradyumna, Sāmba, Aniruddha, Sātyaki, Kṛtavarman and nearly all the Vṛṣṇis die. (*Mbh* 16.3–4; *Bhāgavata* 11.30)
- **Balarāma's departure** - **Balarāma** withdraws into yoga on the Prabhāsa shore; the white serpent **Śeṣa** (whose portion he was) issues from his mouth into the sea — his return to source. (*Mbh* 16.4; *Bhāgavata* 11.30)
- **Kṛṣṇa & the hunter Jarā** - Kṛṣṇa, resting under a tree with his **left foot raised**, is shot in the **sole** by the hunter **Jarā** ("Old Age"), who mistook the foot for a deer — the wound from the very iron-bolt fish-nub. Kṛṣṇa consoles Jarā, casts off the mortal body and ascends — the **avatāra's exit** that ends the Yuga. (*Mbh* 16.4; *Bhāgavata* 11.30; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 5.37)
- **The sea takes Dvārakā** - on Kṛṣṇa's word, **Arjuna** evacuates the surviving women, children and elders toward Indraprastha; **the moment he leads them out, the ocean rushes in and engulfs Dvārakā**, "covering everything... leaving no trace," sparing only the temple/Kṛṣṇa's residence in some tellings. Arjuna, his powers waned, is then robbed of the refugees by **Ābhīra** brigands. (*Mbh* 16.5–8; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 5.38; *Bhāgavata* 11.31)
- **Chronological hinge** - this submergence is fixed by tradition at the **start of Kali Yuga, 3102 BCE**, Kṛṣṇa's ascension being its conventional trigger — the explicit Dvāpara→Kali boundary. (Purāṇic chronology; see [Yuga cycle](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md))
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata 11.31):* Dvārakā is reclaimed by the sea **immediately after** Kṛṣṇa leaves, **except** the place where the Lord himself dwelt, which endures as the eternally sacred kṣetra. [late text]

## VI. Saurāṣṭra neighbours — Śiva, the maṭha & the wider kṣetra

- **Nāgeśvara Jyotirliṅga** - one of the **[12 Jyotirliṅgas](12-jyotirlingas.md)** of Śiva, **~17 km from Dvārakā** on the Dvārakā–Beṭ road, enshrining **Nāgeśa/Nāgeśvara** ("lord of serpents") in a subterranean cell; tied to the legend of the demon **Dāruka** (and Śiva's rescue of his devotee Supriya) and famed for its colossal outdoor Śiva statue. The *Śiva Purāṇa* lists it among the *dvādaśa-jyotirliṅgas*. (*Śiva Purāṇa*; Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (location dispute):* the "Nāgeśvara" jyotirliṅga is also claimed at **Auṇḍhā Nāgnāth** (Maharashtra) and **Jāgeśwar** (Uttarakhand) — a separate jyotirliṅga-identity contest. [disputed]
- **Śāradā / Dvārakā Pīṭha (Śaṅkara maṭha)** - the **western āmnāya maṭha** founded by **Ādi Śaṅkarācārya** (trad. 8th c.), called the **Śāradā Maṭha / Dvārakā Pīṭha / Kālikā Maṭha**: presiding over the **Sāma Veda**, the *mahāvākya* **"Tattvamasi,"** the Tīrtha–Āśrama daśanāmī orders, and the deity **Siddheśvara** and goddess **Bhadrakālī** — the institutional western pole of [Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md). (Śaṅkara-vijaya traditions; Wikipedia)
- **Tirudvāraka — the Divya Deśam** - Dvārakā is the lone Gujarat **[Divya Deśam](108-divya-desams.md)** (**Tirudvāraka / Tiru-dvārakā**) among the 108 holy abodes of Viṣṇu sung in the **Nālāyira Divya Prabandham**; **Periyāḻvār** and **Āṇḍāḷ** glorify Dvārakādhīśa, threading Saurāṣṭra into the Tamil Śrīvaiṣṇava map (counted in some lists as Divya Deśam no. 98). (*Nālāyira Divya Prabandham*; divyadesam.org)
- **The Pañca-Dvārakā circuit** - a five-temple Kṛṣṇa pilgrimage of western India: **(1) Dvārakādhīśa, Dvārakā** · **(2) Beṭ Dvārakā** · **(3) Raṇacchoḍrāī-jī, Dākor** · **(4) Śrīnāthjī, Nāthadvārā** (Rajasthan) · **(5) Dvārakādhīśa, Kāṅkrolī** (Rajasthan). **Mūla-Dvārakā (Koḍīnār)** is often named the *starting* tīrtha though it sits outside the canonical five. (Vaiṣṇava tradition; hindupad.com; gujaratexpert.com) [folk]
- **Char Dhām western point** - Dvārakā anchors the **west** of the all-India **[Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)** (with Badrīnāth N, Purī E, Rāmeśvaram S), and is one of the **[Sapta Purī](sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md)** *mokṣa-dā* cities. (pan-Hindu tradition)

## VII. The academic clock — underwater archaeology [scholarly]

> Distinct from scripture-time: secular dating of the physical ruins. The two clocks must not be conflated.

- **Marine excavations (NIO, 1983–1990)** - the **Marine Archaeology Unit of the National Institute of Oceanography**, led by **Dr. S. R. Rao**, conducted onshore and offshore digs at **both Dvārakā and Beṭ Dvārakā**, recovering submerged stone structures, a fortification-like layout, a stone-built **jetty**, and distinctive **triangular three-holed stone anchors**. (S. R. Rao, *The Lost City of Dvaraka*, 1999)
- **Rao's dating & claim** - Rao argued the finds "confirm the existence of a **city-state with satellite towns** flourishing c. **1500 BCE**," which he linked to the legendary Dvārakā — proposing the legend rests on a real Late–Harappan/Bronze-Age port. (S. R. Rao; UNESCO Silk Roads note) [scholarly]
- **Beṭ Dvārakā's antiquity** - the island is also a genuine **Late Harappan** archaeological site, with a **thermoluminescence date of c. 1570 BCE** and Harappan-style seals/pottery — independent evidence of 2nd-millennium-BCE occupation. (NIO surveys; Wikipedia) [scholarly]
- **Scholarly caution** - the anchor typology and dating are **contested**; many archaeologists date the recovered anchors and much offshore material far later (some to the early–mid 1st millennium CE), and reject a direct equation of the ruins with the *epic* Dvārakā. The identification remains **disputed**. (mainstream archaeological critique) [disputed] [scholarly]
- **The two-clock reconciliation** - scripture places the drowning at **3102 BCE** (Dvāpara–Kali hinge); marine archaeology offers a **c. 1500 BCE** (or later) submerged port. The timeline catalogs both without collapsing one into the other. (see [Yuga cycle](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md))
