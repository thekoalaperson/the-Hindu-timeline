# Sapta Purī, Sacred Cities & Rivers

> The seven **mokṣa-purīs** ("liberation-giving cities") and the seven **sacred rivers** (*Sapta-Sindhu* / *Sapta-Nadī*) that together form the devotional spine of *Bhārata-varṣa*. Dying in any of the seven cities, or bathing in any of the seven rivers, is held in the *smṛti* corpus to grant **mokṣa** directly. This node catalogs the canonical lists, their text-attributed variants, and the cosmic-timeline events anchored at each.

A **purī** is a walled, divinely-charged city; a **tīrtha** is a "crossing-place" (a ford between worlds, usually a river-bank); a **kṣetra** is a sanctified field/zone. The Sapta Purī are the highest tier of *kṣetra* — places where the **act of dying confers liberation** (*mokṣa-dāyikā*), not merely merit. The Sapta-Sindhu are the river-network whose waters are invoked in *every* ritual bath across India regardless of where one stands.

## I. The Seven Mokṣa-Purīs (Sapta Purī)

The defining verse (attributed to the **Garuḍa Purāṇa**, and echoed in the **Skanda Purāṇa**):

> *अयोध्या मथुरा माया काशी काञ्ची अवन्तिका ।*
> *पुरी द्वारावती चैव सप्तैता मोक्षदायिकाः ॥*
>
> "**Ayodhyā, Mathurā, Māyā [Haridwar], Kāśī, Kāñcī, Avantikā, and Purī Dvārāvatī [Dwarka]** — these seven are the bestowers of liberation." (*Garuḍa Purāṇa*)

Three are **Vaiṣṇava** (Ayodhya/Rama, Mathura & Dwarka/Krishna), three are predominantly **Śaiva-Śākta** (Kashi, Kanchi, Avantika/Ujjain), and Haridwar is a **gateway** shared between Vishnu and Shiva — a deliberately ecumenical seven.

- **Ayodhyā** — on the **Sarayū** river, Uttar Pradesh; capital of the **[Sūryavaṃśa / Ikṣvāku dynasty](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md)** and birthplace of **[Rāma](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)**, the 7th avatar of Vishnu, in the **[Tretā Yuga](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md)**. Said to be one of the oldest cities, founded by **Manu Vaivasvata** himself; its name means "the unconquerable / not to be warred against." Rama's *jala-samādhi* (final departure) into the Sarayū at the close of his reign is the anchoring event. (*Garuḍa Purāṇa*; *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*; Wikipedia, *Sapta Puri*)
  - *Variant (presiding deity):* counted as **Rāma's** *janmabhūmi* in Vaishnava reckoning, but the *Ayodhyā-māhātmya* of the *Skanda Purāṇa* also seats **Śiva** as guardian of the *kṣetra*. [late text]
- **Mathurā** — on the **Yamunā**, Uttar Pradesh; **birthplace of [Kṛṣṇa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** in the **[Dvāpara Yuga](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md)**, born in the prison of his uncle **Kaṃsa**, whom he later slew. Centre of the **Braj / Vraja** pilgrimage region (with Vrindavan, Gokul, Govardhan, Barsana). Called *Madhupurī* / *Madhurā* after the demon **Madhu**. (*Garuḍa Purāṇa*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (name/founding):* the *Rāmāyaṇa* tradition holds Mathura was founded by **Śatrughna** (Rama's brother) after he killed the demon **Lavaṇa**, on the site of the older **Madhuvana**. [scriptural cross-reference]
- **Māyā / Haridwar (Haridvāra / Māyāpurī / Gaṅgādvāra)** — where the **[Gaṅgā](#ganga)** breaks from the Himalaya onto the plains, Uttarakhand. Name means "Gateway to Hari (Vishnu)" or, in Śaiva usage, **Haradvāra** ("Gateway to Hara/Shiva"). One of the **four sites where drops of *amṛta* fell** during the *Samudra Manthana* aftermath, hence a **Kumbha Mela** city; the bathing-step **Har-ki-Pauri** bears a "footprint of Vishnu." (*Garuḍa Purāṇa*; Wikipedia, *Sapta Puri*)
  - *Variant (which city is "Māyā"):* a minority gloss reads *Māyā* as **Gayā** (Bihar, the *śrāddha*/ancestor-rite city) rather than Haridwar; most authorities reject this and fix Māyā = Māyāpurī = Haridwar. [disputed]
- **Kāśī / Vārāṇasī (Avimukta, Ānandavana)** — on the **Gaṅgā**, Uttar Pradesh; the city of **[Śiva as Viśvanātha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)**, also one of the **[12 Jyotirlingas](12-jyotirlingas.md)**. Held to be the **oldest living city**, never abandoned (*Avimukta*, "never-forsaken" by Shiva); believed to rest on Shiva's *triśūla* and to stand outside ordinary cosmic dissolution. **Death in Kashi = guaranteed mokṣa**, for Shiva himself whispers the *tāraka-mantra* into the dying ear (*Kāśī-karaṇa* / *mukti*). (*Skanda Purāṇa, Kāśī-khaṇḍa*; *Garuḍa Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (extent):* the *Kāśī-khaṇḍa* defines the liberating zone as the **Pañcakrośī** circuit (~5 *krośa* radius), not merely the temple — a city-sized *kṣetra*. [late text]
- **Kāñcī / Kanchipuram** — Tamil Nadu, on the **Vegavatī / Palār**; the only **southern** mokṣa-purī, called the *"Banaras of the South."* Uniquely **bipartite**: **Śiva-Kāñcī** (north, around **Ekāmbareśvara**, an earth-element *Pañcabhūta-sthalam*) and **Viṣṇu-Kāñcī** (south, around **Varadarāja**), with the supreme goddess **Kāmākṣī** (a *Śakti-pīṭha*) at the centre. Seat of the **Kāñcī Kāmakoṭi Pīṭha** linked to **[Ādi Śaṅkara](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)**. (*Garuḍa Purāṇa*; Wikipedia, *Sapta Puri*)
  - *Variant (presiding deity):* listed under **Śiva**, **Viṣṇu**, *or* the goddess **Kāmākṣī** depending on the sect citing it — genuinely tri-sectarian. [scholarly]
- **Avantikā / Ujjain (Ujjayinī, Avantī)** — Madhya Pradesh, on the **Kṣiprā (Shipra)**; city of **[Śiva as Mahākāla](12-jyotirlingas.md)** (the south-facing *Jyotirlinga* whose *Bhasma-ārati* uses cremation ash) and the time-keeping zero-meridian of classical Hindu astronomy (the prime meridian of the *Sūrya-Siddhānta* passed through it). A **Siṃhastha Kumbha Mela** city (an *amṛta* site). (*Garuḍa Purāṇa*; *Skanda Purāṇa, Avantī-khaṇḍa*)
  - *Variant (name):* "Avantikā" also denotes the broader **Avantī** *janapada*; the purī proper is **Ujjayinī**. [scholarly]
- **Dvārāvatī / Dvārakā (Dwarka)** — Saurashtra coast, Gujarat, near the mouth of the **Gomatī**; the golden sea-fortress city built and ruled by **[Kṛṣṇa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** after he led the Yādavas out of Mathura. Its name means "the many-gated." Also a **[Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)** (West) and seat of a Śaṅkara *maṭha*. The **submergence of Dwaraka into the sea** after Krishna's departure marks the hinge into the present **[Kali Yuga (3102 BCE)](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)**. (*Garuḍa Purāṇa*; *Mahābhārata, Mausala Parva*; *Bhāgavata* & *Viṣṇu Purāṇas*)
  - *Variant (which Dwarka):* tradition distinguishes **Dwarka** (mainland temple-city) from **Bet Dwarka / Śaṅkhoddhāra** island (Krishna's residence) and from **Mūla-Dvārakā** — multiple sites claim the original. [disputed]

### Variant lists of the Seven Cities [disputed]

- *Variant (substitution of Gayā):* some regional recitations replace **Māyā (Haridwar)** with **Gayā**, the great *pitṛ-tīrtha* for ancestor-liberation (*Viṣṇupada*, Bihar). [folk]
- *Variant ("Sapta Mokṣapurī" vs "Sapta Purī"):* a few late sources distinguish a list of mokṣa-cities from a separate list of seven "great" cities; the standard seven are stable. [late text]
- *Variant (eighth city):* devotional lists occasionally append an eighth — **Puṣkara** (Rajasthan, the rare **Brahmā** city) or **Pūrī/Jagannātha** (Odisha) — but these break the canonical seven. [folk]
- *Note (Purī ambiguity):* the verse's word *purī* ("city") is sometimes misread as the place-name **Puri (Jagannath)**; in the Garuḍa Purāṇa verse it is the common noun qualifying **Dvārāvatī**, i.e. "the city Dwarka." [scholarly]

## II. The Seven Sacred Rivers (Sapta-Sindhu / Sapta-Nadī)

The river-invocation (*nadī-stuti*) recited at every ritual bath:

> *गङ्गे च यमुने चैव गोदावरि सरस्वति ।*
> *नर्मदे सिन्धु कावेरि जलेऽस्मिन् सन्निधिं कुरु ॥*
>
> "**O Gaṅgā, Yamunā, Godāvarī, Sarasvatī, Narmadā, Sindhu, and Kāverī** — be present in this water." (traditional *snāna-mantra*; rivers named per *Purāṇa* tradition)

The term **Sapta-Sindhu** ("seven rivers") is **older than the canonical list** — it appears in the **Ṛgveda** (≈ the rivers of the Punjab–Sindhu basin), so the *identity* of the seven shifted from the Vedic northwest to the pan-Indian set below. The Puranic list deliberately spans **North (Ganga, Yamuna, Saraswati, Sindhu)** and **South (Godavari, Kaveri, Narmada-central)** to bind the subcontinent.

<a id="ganga"></a>
- **Gaṅgā (Ganges)** — the supreme purifier, personified as a goddess and one of the **seven mothers**. Its **descent (*Gaṅgāvataraṇa*)** is among the great timeline events: King **Sagara's** 60,000 sons were burnt to ash by the wrath of sage **Kapila**; their descendant **Bhagīratha** (Sūryavaṃśa) performed millennia of *tapas* to bring Ganga from heaven to liberate their ashes; **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** caught her crashing flow in his matted locks (the **Bhagīrathī** strand) to spare the earth. Hence Ganga = *Tripathagā* ("flowing in the three worlds"). Sourced at **Gangotri/Gaumukh**. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa*; *Mahābhārata*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (heavenly origin):* she springs from **Vishnu's foot** (*Viṣṇupadī*) in the *Bhāgavata*, vs. from **Brahma's kamaṇḍalu** in other Puranas. [disputed/late text]
- **Yamunā (Jamunā)** — twin-sister of **Yama** (death) and daughter of **Sūrya**; bathing in her is said to remove the fear of death. The river of **[Kṛṣṇa's](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) Braj-līlā** — his birth-night crossing, the subduing of the serpent **Kāliya**, the *rāsa-līlā*. Confluences with Ganga (and the invisible Saraswati) at the **Triveṇī Saṅgam, Prayāga**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*)
- **Godāvarī (Gautamī / Dakṣiṇa-Gaṅgā)** — the longest peninsular river, the **"Ganga of the South."** Brought down by sage **Gautama**, who used a tuft of Ganga's hair (or Shiva's gift) to atone for the accidental death of a cow on his hermitage — sourced at **Tryambakeśvar** (a **[Jyotirlinga](12-jyotirlingas.md)**), Nashik. Host of the **Siṃhastha Kumbha Mela** at Nashik; associated with **[Rāma's](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md)** forest exile at **Pañcavaṭī**. (*Brahma Purāṇa, Gautamī-māhātmya*; *Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-kāṇḍa*)
- **Sarasvatī** — the **invisible / lost** river, goddess of speech, learning and music. Vedic hymns praise her as the mightiest river (*ambitame, nadītame, devitame*); she is held to have **gone underground (*antaḥsalilā*)** and to surface only mystically as the third, hidden strand at the **Triveṇī Saṅgam**. (*Ṛgveda* 7.95; *Mahābhārata* — her drying noted at *Vinaśana*)
  - *Variant (curse of disappearance):* the *Mahābhārata* narrates Saraswati cursed to sink into the desert; she reappears at named *tīrthas* (Prabhāsa, Kurukṣetra). [late text]
  - *Variant (academic) [scholarly]:* often identified with the dried palaeo-channel of the **Ghaggar–Hakra** system; distinguish this *academic* hypothesis from the *scriptural* goddess-river.
- **Narmadā (Revā)** — the great **central** river, flowing **westward** (uniquely, in a rift valley) into the Arabian Sea. Born of **Śiva's** body (from his sweat/penance on Mt. Riksha), so holy that *its mere sight* purifies where other rivers require bathing (*"Gaṅgā snāne, Narmadā darśane"*). Its bed yields the self-formed **Bāṇa-liṅga** (Narmadeśvara) Shiva-stones. The river-circumambulation (**Narmadā Parikramā**) is a major vow. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Revā-khaṇḍa*; *Matsya Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (birth):* alternately the daughter of the **Mekala** mountain (*Mekala-kanyā*), hence *Mekala-sutā*. [late text]
- **Sindhu (Indus)** — the **rare masculine** sacred river (*nada*, not *nadī*), the river that gave **"Hind / India"** its name; the central artery of the Ṛgvedic *Sapta-Sindhu* homeland. Associated devotionally with **[Rāma](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md)** in late lists. (*Ṛgveda, Nadī-stuti* 10.75; Purāṇic tradition)
  - *Variant (inclusion):* because the Sindhu basin lies largely outside the later devotional heartland, some southern lists quietly substitute a local river — yet the canonical *snāna-mantra* retains **Sindhu**. [disputed]
- **Kāverī (Cauvery / Dakṣiṇa-Gaṅgā of the far south)** — the lifeline of Tamil country, personified as the goddess **Kāverī**, a form of **Lopāmudrā / Vishnumāyā**. Brought south by sage **Agastya** (released from his *kamaṇḍalu*, often via the trick of **Gaṇeśa-as-crow**); sourced at **Talakāveri** (Coorg/Kodagu). Associated with **Dattātreya** and the great **Raṅganātha** (Vishnu) island-shrines of **Śrīraṅgam / Śrīraṅgapaṭṭaṇa**. (*Skanda Purāṇa*; *Agastya* legend cycle)

- *Variant (the seven listed) [disputed]:* the *snāna-mantra* set (above) is canonical, but regional lists swap members — e.g. **Tāmraparṇī, Kṛṣṇā, Tuṅgabhadrā, or Mahānadī** appear in southern recensions in place of **Sindhu** or **Saraswati**. [folk]
- *Variant (Sapta-Sindhu, Vedic) [scholarly]:* the *Ṛgvedic* "seven rivers" denote the northwestern (Punjab–Sindh) river-system, not the later pan-Indian seven — a historically distinct usage of the same phrase.

## III. Why "Seven," and How the Two Sets Interlock

- The number **seven** (*sapta*) recurs structurally across the timeline — **[seven sages (Saptarṣi)](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md)**, seven *lokas*, seven *dvīpas*, seven *mātṛkās*, seven horses of Sūrya — and the purīs/rivers replicate this cosmic heptad on the **terrestrial plane**.
- Each mokṣa-purī **stands on or near a sacred river**, fusing the two heptads: Ayodhya–Sarayū, Mathura–Yamunā, Haridwar & Kashi–Gaṅgā, Kanchi–Vegavatī, Ujjain–Kṣiprā, Dwarka–Gomatī. The *city* offers death-liberation; the *river* offers life-purification.
- Several purīs double as nodes in other sacred-geography sets: **Dwarka** (a [Char Dhām](char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md)), **Kashi & Ujjain** ([Jyotirlingas](12-jyotirlingas.md)), **Kanchi** (a [Śakti-pīṭha](../06-sacred-geography/README.md)) — the networks deliberately overlap to weave one continuous pilgrimage geography.
