# Varuṇa

> Cosmic address: the once-supreme sovereign of the Vedic pantheon — the unblinking guardian of **ṛta** (cosmic-moral order), the binder of sinners with his **noose (pāśa)**, lord of the **waters** and the **western direction**, who across the strata is gradually demoted from universal asura-king to a regional sea-god. Varuṇa is the fifth/chief of the twelve [Adityas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md), one of the eight [Lokapālas/Dikpālas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md), and the Vedic twin of [Mitra](surya-and-his-family.md). Parent: [02 — Deities and Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md). Master index: [the Hindu Timeline](../README.md).

Varuṇa is the clearest case in the whole tradition of the **two-clock problem of theological demotion**: in the oldest layer (Ṛg Veda) he is the most ethically exalted of all gods — the all-seeing emperor whose spies are the stars and whose law no being escapes. By the epics and Purāṇas he has shrunk to a courteous sea-king and corner-guardian of the west. Reading him across time is to watch a high god slowly fall.

---

## Names, etymology & essential identity

- **Varuṇa (वरुण)** — name usually derived from root *vṛ-* "to cover, encompass, bind, restrain" — hence "the Encompasser / the Binder," fitting both his all-covering sky-sovereignty and his snaring noose. [scholarly]
- **The Asura par excellence** — in the Ṛg Veda Varuṇa is the foremost **asura** (in the *old, positive* sense: "lord, possessor of occult power, *māyā*"), titled *Asura Mahat* ("the Great Asura") and *Asura Medha* ("the Wise Asura"). Only later does "asura" curdle into "demon" — Varuṇa is the bridge-figure showing the word's reversal (Ṛg Veda; [scholarly]).
- **An Aditya** — son of **Aditi** ("Boundlessness") and the sage **Kaśyapa**; counted among the [twelve Adityas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md). Rosters place him 5th (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.15) or otherwise; in the dual Vedic system he is one of the great pair with Mitra.
- **Possessor of māyā** — repeatedly called master of *māyā* (creative/measuring magical power) by which he upholds the worlds and measures out the cosmos — a term that later shifts toward "illusion." [scholarly]

## Lord of ṛta — the cosmic-moral order

- **Guardian of ṛta** — Varuṇa is *the* upholder of **ṛta**, the truth-order underlying nature, ritual, and morality; the sun moves, rivers flow, and oaths bind because Varuṇa sustains ṛta. Falsehood (*anṛta*) and sin are offenses against him directly (Ṛg Veda).
- **The all-seeing ethical judge** — uniquely among Vedic gods, Varuṇa has a developed **conscience-and-sin morality**: he knows the flight of birds, the path of ships, the secret thought; **the stars are his eyes / spies (*spaśaḥ*)** keeping watch over men's deeds. Nothing — not even a blink — escapes him (Ṛg Veda 1.25; Atharva Veda 4.16, the great "espionage" hymn).
- **Ṛg Veda 7.86 — the penitential lament** — the most famous Varuṇa hymn: a sufferer, afflicted (traditionally read as **dropsy/edema**, the disease Varuṇa sends — the body swelling with retained *water*, his element), pleads to know what sin angered the god and begs release. This hymn is celebrated as the nearest the Veda comes to a personal sense of sin and grace. (Ṛg Veda 7.86; cf. 7.88–89.)
- **Punisher by binding** — sin makes Varuṇa "bind" the offender; release is "loosening the noose." His justice is consequence and accountability, not cruelty (Ṛg Veda; [scholarly]).

## The noose (pāśa), the makara & iconography

- **The pāśa (noose/snare)** — Varuṇa's defining weapon and emblem: a noose (often described as made from a **serpent/nāga**) with which he **binds the guilty** and snares the falsehearted; "the snares of Varuṇa" (*varuṇasya pāśāḥ*) are proverbial in the Veda. The noose later becomes the standard attribute passed to Yama and to Gaṇeśa as well. (Ṛg Veda; Purāṇic iconography.)
- **The makara mount** — in classical iconography Varuṇa rides the **makara**, a composite sea-monster (crocodile/fish/elephant hybrid), underscoring his lordship of the waters; the makara is also his banner. [late text]
- **Form** — depicted as a fair/radiant figure, often four-armed, holding the **noose** and sometimes a **vessel/pot of jewels or a conch**, seated on the makara or in a chariot, dwelling in a jeweled underwater palace. (Purāṇic; iconographic śilpa texts.)
- **The waters as his domain** — by the epic-Purāṇic period he is **Apām pati / Jaleśvara**, lord of all waters — oceans, rivers, lakes, springs — and patron of aquatic life and of the **nāgas**.

## Mitra–Varuṇa — the sovereign dual deity

- **The dvandva Mitrā-Varuṇā** — in the Ṛg Veda Varuṇa appears most often paired with **Mitra** as a fused dual-god, jointly the chief guardians of ṛta and the *sovereignty (kṣatra)* of the cosmos. See [Sūrya & family / the Adityas](surya-and-his-family.md).
- **The functional split** — a celebrated [scholarly] (Dumézilian) reading: **Mitra = the contract, daylight, benevolent, the *near* sovereign** (covenant between men), while **Varuṇa = the oath/bond, night, awesome, the *far/magical* sovereign** (the law between gods and men). Together they form the two faces of legitimate authority.
- **Day/night drift** — in late-Vedic Brāhmaṇas Mitra slides toward the morning/sun and Varuṇa toward the **evening and night**, prefiguring Varuṇa's later association with the dark, the deep, and the west. [scholarly]
- **The etymology of Mitra** — Indo-Iranian *mitra* "that which binds" → "covenant, contract"; the pairing thus literally couples Binding (Varuṇa) with Covenant (Mitra). [scholarly]

## The decline — from universal monarch to corner sea-god

- **Vedic apex** — in the oldest stratum Varuṇa is arguably the **highest god**: the universal emperor (*samrāj*), upholder of heaven and earth, before whom even gods stand. (Ṛg Veda.)
- **Eclipse by Indra** — across the Ṛg Veda's later layers, the warrior storm-god **[Indra](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md)** rises and displaces Varuṇa as effective king of the gods; the slaying of **Vṛtra** and Indra's martial cult overtake Varuṇa's contemplative law-sovereignty. Hymns like the Indra-Varuṇa pieces (RV 7.82–85) negotiate the handover. [scholarly]
- **Restructuring after Vṛtra** — one reading: with the cosmos re-ordered by Indra's victory, Varuṇa is shifted from asura-king toward the **deva** ranks proper, his "asura" title decaying with the word itself. [scholarly]
- **Epic-Purāṇic demotion** — by the **Rāmāyaṇa** and **Mahābhārata** Varuṇa is the **lord of waters and Dikpāla of the WEST**, a dignified but secondary god of the court of [Indra](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) / under the new high gods. The cosmic emperor has become a regional regent.
- **Lokapāla of the west** — one of the eight (or four) **directional guardians**: Varuṇa = **WEST**, with his **makara** mount and noose; see the dikpāla scheme in [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
- **Varuṇa-loka** — his world/abode (**Sukhā / a jeweled oceanic palace**), counted among the loka-palaces of the guardians. (Purāṇic.)

## Family & associated figures

- **Wife — Vāruṇī / Vāruṇānī** — his consort, sometimes identified as **Gaurī** or **Vāruṇī**, the goddess of wine/liquor (*surā*) said to have arisen at the [churning of the ocean](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md) and given to Varuṇa. [folk]/[late text]
  - *Variant (consort names):* texts variously name **Vāruṇānī, Gaurī, Jyeṣṭhā, or Cārṣaṇī** as Varuṇa's wife (Purāṇas) — [flag: roster open-ended].
- **Sons & "born-of-Varuṇa" sages** — a notably *fluid* roster. Commonly attached: **Puṣkara** (a son); the great sages **Vasiṣṭha** and **Agastya** are in some traditions born from the seed of Mitra-Varuṇa (the *Mitrāvaruṇi* lineage); **Bhṛgu** linked through Varuṇa's wife in the Bhāgavata. (Ṛg Veda 7.33 on Vasiṣṭha's birth; Purāṇas.) [flag: roster open-ended]
  - *Variant (Mitrāvaruṇa birth of Vasiṣṭha & Agastya):* RV 7.33 narrates Vasiṣṭha's miraculous birth from Mitra-Varuṇa at the sight of the apsaras **Urvaśī**; Agastya ("pitcher-born," *kumbhayoni / kumbhasambhava*) is likewise traced to the same seed in a jar. [scholarly] connecting both sages to Varuṇa. See [Saptarishi](saptarishi.md).
  - *Variant (other listed children):* Suṣeṇa, Vandī, Bala, Sura, Adharmaka, **Dakṣasāvarṇi** (the 9th Manu), and even **Vālmīki** appear in various Purāṇic son-lists — [flag: list open-ended].

## Varuṇa in epic & Purāṇic episodes

- **Giver of divine weapons** — in the **Mahābhārata**, Varuṇa bestows on **Arjuna** the great bow **Gāṇḍīva**, two inexhaustible quivers, and a divine chariot at the burning of the Khāṇḍava forest (with Agni). He is keeper of mighty water-weapons (*vāruṇāstra*). See [Artifacts, Symbols & Arts](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/README.md).
- **The vāruṇāstra** — the "water missile," a counter to the fire-weapon (*āgneyāstra*), wielded by epic heroes via Varuṇa's grace (Mahābhārata; Rāmāyaṇa).
- **Rāma threatens the ocean** — in the **Rāmāyaṇa**, when the sea-lord (Varuṇa / Samudra) does not yield passage to Laṅkā, Rāma readies the **brahmāstra**; Varuṇa/Samudra appears in person, submits, and advises building **Rāma Setu** with the help of Nala. (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa.)
- **The water-ordeal & oaths** — Varuṇa presides over **water ordeals** and the binding force of oaths sworn by water; his element is the test of truth. (Dharmaśāstra; [scholarly].)
- **Lord of nāgas & sea-treasure** — guardian of the ocean's wealth and the serpent-races; invoked in rituals for rain and for relief from his own diseases (dropsy, ear/throat ailments). (Atharva Veda; Purāṇic.)

## Comparative & survivals

- **Indo-European kin** — Varuṇa is widely (if debatedly) linked to the Greek sky-god **Ouranos** (both possibly from a root for "covering/binding"), and his ethical-sovereign function compared to the Iranian **Ahura Mazda** ("the Wise Lord" — note *asura* = *ahura*, *medha* ≈ *mazdā*). [disputed]/[scholarly]
- **Living cult** — though eclipsed, Varuṇa survives in ritual practice as the god invoked for **rain** and at water-rites (*tarpaṇa*, *jala-pūjā*, and the **Cheṭṭi/Chaiti Chand–Jhulelal** Sindhi water-deity tradition, where Jhulelal is held an avatāra of Varuṇa). [folk]/[late text]
- **Two clocks summary** — *Scripture clock:* an eternal cosmic sovereign of ṛta and the waters. *Academic clock:* a likely Proto-Indo-Iranian high god whose textual prominence visibly **declines from Ṛg Veda → Brāhmaṇas → epics → Purāṇas** as Indra, then Viṣṇu/Śiva, rise. [scholarly]
