# The Navagraha — Nine Planetary Deities

> Cosmic address: within the deity hierarchy of the present cosmos. The Navagraha ("nine seizers") are the planetary deities and cosmic forces who administer the ripening of karma upon embodied beings. They sit below the great gods (see [Trimurti and Tridevi](trimurti-and-tridevi.md)) and overlap with the deva/asura order (see [Devas, Asuras and the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md)). Parent: [02 — Deities and Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md).

The word **graha** means "seizer / grasper," not merely "planet": the grahas *seize* the embodied soul and deliver to it the fruits of its past deeds at the appointed time. They are simultaneously (a) astronomical bodies, (b) deities with myth-cycles and vahanas, and (c) the operative instruments of the law of karma (see [Karma, Samsara, Moksha](../05-concepts-and-dharma/karma-samsara-moksha.md) when written). Their science is **Jyotisha**, the "eye of the Veda," one of the six Vedangas (see [Upavedas, Vedangas, Darshanas](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md)).

---

## The Nine, at a glance

- **Surya** — Sun; soul (*atman*), the king, father, authority. Vahana: chariot of **seven horses** (or one seven-headed horse) driven by **Aruna**. Day: Sunday. Direction: East. Metal: copper/gold. Gem: ruby. Grain: wheat.
- **Chandra (Soma)** — Moon; mind (*manas*), mother, emotion, fluids. Vahana: chariot drawn by **ten white horses** (or an antelope/deer). Day: Monday. Direction: NW (SE in some schemes). Gem: pearl. Grain: rice.
- **Mangala (Angaraka / Kuja)** — Mars; energy, war, brothers, land, the warrior. Vahana: **ram** (or peacock). Day: Tuesday. Direction: South. Gem: red coral. Grain: toor dal.
- **Budha** — Mercury; intellect, speech, commerce; son of Chandra and Tara. Vahana: a **lion** or the chimeric **yali**. Day: Wednesday. Direction: North. Gem: emerald. Grain: green gram.
- **Brihaspati (Guru)** — Jupiter; wisdom, dharma, the *guru of the devas*; greatest natural benefic. Vahana: **elephant** (or chariot of eight horses). Day: Thursday. Direction: NE. Gem: yellow sapphire. Grain: chana (chickpea).
- **Shukra** — Venus; love, art, fertility, the *guru of the asuras*, holder of the **Mritasanjivani vidya**. Vahana: **horse** (or chariot/crocodile). Day: Friday. Direction: SE. Gem: diamond. Grain: white beans.
- **Shani** — Saturn; the slow one, the dispenser of karma, justice, longevity, sorrow, discipline. Vahana: **crow / vulture** (also buffalo or iron chariot). Day: Saturday. Direction: West. Gem: blue sapphire. Grain: black sesame/urad.
- **Rahu** — north lunar node (ascending); shadow-graha, the severed head of the asura **Svarbhanu**; obsession, foreigners, the unconventional. Vahana: **black lion** (or chariot drawn by eight black horses). Direction: SW. Gem: hessonite (gomedha).
- **Ketu** — south lunar node (descending); shadow-graha, the **headless trunk** of Svarbhanu; detachment, moksha, the occult. Vahana: **vulture** (or fish/serpent). Direction: NW. Gem: cat's-eye (vaidurya).

> *Variant (on the count):* though "nava" = nine, classical Jyotisha treats **seven physical grahas + two nodes**. Some schools dispute whether the **luminaries** (Surya, Chandra) are truly "grahas" at all — see "The dispute" below.

## Surya — the Sun, soul of the cosmos

- **King of the grahas** — Surya is *atmakaraka* in significance (the soul); the visible Brahman, "the eye of the world," addressed by the **Gayatri** mantra (Rig Veda 3.62.10) directed to Savitr, the solar impeller.
- **Twelve Adityas** — Surya is reckoned as twelve solar forms (the Adityas), one per month; the Adityas are sons of Aditi (Vishnu Purana; Bhagavata 6). See [Devas, Asuras and the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
- **Marriage to Saranyu/Sanjna** — Surya weds Saranyu (Sanjna), daughter of Vishvakarma. Unable to bear his blaze, she leaves behind her shadow-double **Chhaya** and flees as a mare (Rig Veda 10.17; Markandeya & Matsya Puranas).
- **Vishvakarma trims the Sun** — to win his wife back, Surya's excess radiance is shaved off on Vishvakarma's lathe; the trimmings are forged into Vishnu's discus, Shiva's trident, and other divine weapons (Markandeya Purana; Matsya Purana).
- **Children of Surya** — by Saranyu: **Yama** (god of death/dharma) and **Yami/Yamuna**, the **Ashvins** (twin physician-gods), and **Vaivasvata Manu** (progenitor of the present [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)); by Chhaya: **Shani** and **Tapati**; **Sugriva**, **Karna** [epic], and **Revanta** are also called solar sons.
- **Aruna the charioteer** — Surya's chariot is driven by the legless dawn-deity Aruna (elder brother of Garuda), hatched prematurely from Vinata's egg (Mahabharata; Garuda Purana).
- **Krishna heals via Surya** — the leper sage/king **Samba** (Krishna's son) is cured by worshipping Surya, founding solar worship at Mitravana/Multan (Samba Purana; Bhavishya Purana).

---

## Chandra (Soma) — the Moon, lord of mind

- **Birth** — variously the son of sage Atri and Anasuya (churned from Atri's eye, Vishnu Purana), or risen at the **Samudra Manthana** among the fourteen treasures (Bhagavata 8). See [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md) when written.
- **27 wives / Daksha's curse** — Chandra marries the 27 **Nakshatras** (daughters of Daksha) but favors **Rohini**, so Daksha curses him to waste away; relenting, the curse becomes cyclic — hence the **waxing and waning** of the Moon (Mahabharata; Puranas).
  - *Variant (Bhagavata):* the waning is tied to the gods and pitris drinking the **Soma** (lunar nectar) which the Moon then refills.
- **Abduction of Tara** — Chandra abducts **Tara**, wife of Brihaspati; the resulting **Tarakamaya war** between devas and asuras is settled by Brahma. Tara bears **Budha**, claimed by both Chandra and Brihaspati (Vishnu Purana; Bhagavata 9) — origin of the **Chandravamsha** (see [Suryavamsha and Chandravamsha](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md)).
- **Ganesha's curse** — Chandra mocks Ganesha's broken-tusk form and is cursed; whoever sees the Moon on **Ganesha Chaturthi** is falsely accused (Puranic/folk; cited re: Krishna and the Syamantaka gem).

---

## Mangala (Angaraka, Kuja) — Mars

- **Born of Earth/Shiva's sweat** — most accounts make Mangala the son of **Bhumi (Earth)**, hence *Bhauma/Kuja* ("born of earth"); in one telling a drop of Shiva's sweat falls to earth and becomes the red planet (Skanda Purana; Brahmavaivarta).
- **Lord of warriors and land** — natural malefic governing energy, conflict, courage, siblings, and real estate; **Mangala dosha** (manglik) afflicting marriage is a major folk-astrological concern. [folk tradition]
- **Reddened seer** — sometimes identified with the war-god aspect; Tuesday fasts and red offerings propitiate him; presides at Vaitheeswaran Koil among the Navagraha temples.

---

## Budha — Mercury

- **Child of an irregular union** — son of **Chandra and Tara** (Brihaspati's wife), born of the Tara abduction; thus Budha is the seed of the lunar (Chandravamsha) line (Vishnu Purana; Bhagavata 9).
- **Father of Pururavas** — Budha marries **Ila** (the gender-changing offspring of Vaivasvata Manu) and fathers **Pururavas**, first king of the Chandravamsha (see [Suryavamsha and Chandravamsha](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md)).
- **Governance** — intellect, speech, trade, learning; a natural benefic when alone but takes the color of whatever graha it joins ("neutral / chameleon").

---

## Brihaspati (Guru) — Jupiter, preceptor of the devas

- **Guru of the gods** — son of the sage **Angiras**; the priest (*purohita*) and counsellor of Indra and the devas; embodiment of dharma, knowledge, and the *brahmana* function (Rig Veda hymns to Brihaspati/Brahmanaspati).
- **Greatest natural benefic** — in Jyotisha, Jupiter is the supreme *shubha graha*, *karaka* for wisdom, children, wealth, and the guru.
- **Loss of Tara to Chandra** — his wife Tara is abducted by Chandra; their son **Budha** is contested (see above) — the original "deva-guru vs the Moon" episode.
- **Rivalry with Shukra** — the perennial counterpoint: Brihaspati guides the devas, Shukra the asuras; their cosmic contest underlies the deva-asura wars (Mahabharata; Puranas).
- **Kacha sent to steal the secret** — Brihaspati sends his son **Kacha** to become Shukra's pupil to obtain the **Mritasanjivani vidya** (see Shukra below) (Mahabharata, Adi Parva).

---

## Shukra — Venus, preceptor of the asuras, holder of life-from-death

- **Son of Bhrigu** — Shukra (Ushanas Kavya) is son of the sage **Bhrigu**; becomes guru of the **asuras/daityas**, balancing Brihaspati (Mahabharata; Puranas).
- **The Mritasanjivani vidya** — Shukra obtains from **Shiva** (through severe tapas) the **Sanjivani / Mritasanjivani**, the science of reviving the dead, with which he keeps resurrecting slain asuras — a decisive war-asymmetry against the devas (Mahabharata; Matsya Purana).
- **Kacha and Devayani** — Brihaspati's son **Kacha** infiltrates as Shukra's disciple to learn the vidya. The asuras kill Kacha repeatedly; Shukra revives him at his daughter **Devayani's** plea. Finally the asuras burn Kacha, mix his ashes in wine, and feed it to Shukra. Shukra teaches the dying Kacha the mantra *from inside his own belly*; Kacha bursts forth (killing Shukra), then revives Shukra with the newly learned vidya. Shukra thereafter forbids brahmins to drink wine. Devayani loves Kacha, but he, having emerged from Shukra's body, calls her "sister" and refuses; she curses the vidya to fail for him, he curses her to marry a non-brahmin — fulfilled in her marriage to King **Yayati** (Mahabharata, Adi Parva 71–76).
- **Swallowed by Vishnu/Vamana** — at the **Vamana** episode, Shukra (foreseeing doom) tries to stop King **Bali** from granting the three steps; he hides in the spout of Bali's water-pot to block the pour, and Vamana pierces it with a blade of grass, putting out Shukra's eye — hence Shukra is said to be **one-eyed** (Bhagavata 8; cross-ref [Dashavatara](dashavatara.md)).
- **Governance** — love, beauty, art, music, marriage, vehicles, luxury, semen/fertility (*shukra* = "semen, the bright"); a great natural benefic.

---

## Shani — Saturn, the dispenser of karma

- **Son of Surya and Chhaya** — born to **Chhaya** (Surya's shadow-wife). His dark complexion is attributed to Chhaya's fierce tapas under the sun while pregnant (Brahmavaivarta; Skanda Purana). Brother of **Yama** (both judge deeds) and of **Yamuna**.
- **Surya-vs-Shani father-son tension** — Surya doubts Shani's parentage and rejects him because of his dark color and lameness; the rift makes the Sun (soul, ego, father) and Saturn (karma, restriction) natural enemies in Jyotisha (Puranic; folk tradition). Shani is also called lame, having injured a leg.
- **The destroying gaze (drishti)** — Shani's look brings ruin. Variant origins:
  - *Variant (folk):* he turns his gaze from his wife absorbed in devotion; she curses him so that whatever he gazes upon is destroyed — hence his lowered eyes.
  - *Variant (folk):* invited to baby Ganesha's first-rite, Shani averts his eyes knowing the danger; pressed to look, his glance severs Ganesha's head — leading to the elephant-head substitution (see [Ganesha cycle](ganesha.md) when written).
- **Sade Sati and dhaiya** — the **seven-and-a-half-year** transit of Saturn over the Moon (*Sade Sati*) and the 2.5-year *dhaiya* are the most feared karmic periods; intense but purifying. [astrological tradition]
- **Shani humbles even the gods** — Vikramaditya, Dasharatha (the *Shani-stotra* of Dasharatha), and even Shiva/Vishnu are said to undergo Saturn's tests; the *Shani Mahatmya* recounts his transit through King Vikramaditya's life. [late text]
- **Hanuman shields from Shani** — folk tradition: Hanuman frees/defeats Shani, who then grants that devotees of Hanuman are spared his worst affliction — basis of Saturday Hanuman worship. [folk tradition]
- **Governance** — karma, justice, longevity, servants, sorrow, discipline, old age, iron, the lowly; "Shani does not punish, he delivers exactly what was earned." The slowest graha — the cosmic auditor.

## Rahu and Ketu — the shadow-grahas (chhaya-graha)

The two nodes are the lunar nodes — the points where the Moon's path crosses the ecliptic — astronomically the cause of **eclipses**. Mythologically they are the **two halves of one severed asura, Svarbhanu**.

- **Origin at the Samudra Manthana** — during the churning of the ocean, the asura **Svarbhanu** disguises himself among the devas to steal a sip of **amrita**. Surya and Chandra expose him; **Vishnu as Mohini** beheads him — but the amrita has already touched his throat, so head and trunk both become immortal. The head is **Rahu**, the trunk **Ketu** (Bhagavata 8.9; Vishnu Purana 1.9; Mahabharata, Adi Parva). Cross-link: the existing **Rahu note** in [Devas, Asuras and the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) and (when written) [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md).
- **The eclipse vendetta** — Rahu (and Ketu) periodically swallow Surya and Chandra in revenge for exposing him; the luminary passes through the headless/bodiless throat and reappears — the eclipse (*grahana*) (Puranas).
  - *Variant (count):* in older Vedic/epic strata Svarbhanu is a **single** eclipse-demon; the split into Rahu (head) + Ketu (tail) as two distinct grahas is the developed Puranic/Jyotisha form.
  - *Variant (Ketu):* some texts treat **Ketu** less as the asura's trunk and more as a class of "comets/banners" (*ketu* = banner, comet); later standardized as the south node.
- **Karmic meaning** — Rahu = insatiable desire, illusion, obsession, foreignness, the head without a body (appetite without satiety); Ketu = renunciation, the body without a head (action without ego), moksha, the occult, past-life residue. They are always **180° apart** and move **retrograde**.
- **No physical body** — being shadows, they own no zodiac sign in classical schemes (though later astrologers assign Rahu co-lordship of Aquarius and Ketu of Scorpio). They are reckoned **always malefic / functional** depending on association.

## Governance of karma-ripening — the grahas as karmic agents

- **Grahas deliver, not decree** — the grahas are the *timing-instruments* of karma already sown; they ripen the seed at its season. They neither create the deed nor its fruit; they administer the schedule (classical Jyotisha doctrine, rooted in [Karma, Samsara, Moksha](../05-concepts-and-dharma/karma-samsara-moksha.md) when written).
- **Karakas (significators)** — each graha "signifies" domains: Surya–soul/father, Chandra–mind/mother, Mangala–energy/siblings, Budha–intellect, Guru–wisdom/children, Shukra–love/spouse, Shani–karma/longevity, Rahu–desire, Ketu–liberation.
- **Natural benefics vs malefics (BPHS)** — *Benefics:* Brihaspati, Shukra, waxing Chandra, well-associated Budha. *Malefics:* Surya, Mangala, Shani, Rahu, Ketu, waning Chandra (Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra).
- **Functional benefic/malefic** — no graha is universally good: its quality depends on the **sign-lordship relative to the ascendant (lagna)**, so the same graha helps one chart and harms another (BPHS).
- **Sign lordships (BPHS)** — Surya: Leo. Chandra: Cancer. Mangala: Aries & Scorpio. Budha: Gemini & Virgo. Brihaspati: Sagittarius & Pisces. Shukra: Taurus & Libra. Shani: Capricorn & Aquarius. (Rahu/Ketu: no classical sign; modern co-lordships disputed.)
  - *Variant (luminary dispute):* Surya and Chandra each rule **one** sign, while the five tara-grahas rule **two** each — a structural asymmetry some traditions read as evidence the luminaries are a different category from the planets.
- **Exaltations / debilitations, dignities, aspects (drishti)** — each graha has signs of exaltation/fall and special aspects (Mangala 4th/8th, Brihaspati 5th/9th, Shani 3rd/10th; all aspect the 7th) (BPHS).

## Jyotisha — the planetary science (the Vedanga "eye of the Veda")

- **One of six Vedangas** — Jyotisha is the limb of the Veda governing time and the heavens, indispensable for fixing the times of Vedic rites; see [Upavedas, Vedangas, Darshanas](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md).
- **Three skandhas** — Jyotisha divides into *Siddhanta* (mathematical astronomy), *Samhita* (mundane/omens), and *Hora* (natal astrology).
- **Surya Siddhanta** — foundational astronomical treatise (received, per legend, from a *Maya* devotee of Surya); fixes planetary periods, eclipse computation, and the order of the grahas; frames the chronology used in [Units of Time](../00-time-and-cosmology/units-of-time.md) and the [Yugas](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md).
- **Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra (BPHS)** — the master classical text of natal Jyotisha attributed to **Parashara** (father of Vyasa); defines grahas, rashis, nakshatras, dignities, the *dasha* (planetary-period) systems (esp. Vimshottari, 120-yr cycle apportioned among the nine grahas), and remedial measures.
- **The seven-day week** — the seven non-nodal grahas name the days of the week (Ravivara…Shanivara), in the order of the "planetary hours"; the nodes have no weekday — evidence they were added to make the count nine.

## Temple worship — the Navagraha shrine

- **The standard arrangement** — a square dais (*peetha*), usually in the NE of the sanctum, with **Surya in the center** and the eight others around him; crucially **no two grahas face each other**, each looking a different direction (each in its assigned direction). Two installation modes: **Vaidika pradishta** and **Agama pradishta** (temple-agama practice).
- **Day-specific propitiation** — each graha has its weekday, color, grain (the *nava-dhanya*), metal, gem, flower, and number; *graha-shanti* rites and *homa* are performed to soften a malefic transit.
- **The nine Navagraha temples of Tamil Nadu (Kumbakonam region)** — the most famous circuit, one planet per shrine:
  - **Surya** — Suryanar Koil. **Chandra** — Thingalur. **Mangala** — Vaitheeswaran Koil. **Budha** — Thiruvenkadu. **Brihaspati** — Alangudi. **Shukra** — Kanjanur. **Shani** — Thirunallar (chief Shani kshetra). **Rahu** — Thirunageswaram. **Ketu** — Keezhperumpallam.
- **Major standalone Shani shrines** — **Shani Shingnapur** (Maharashtra, doorless houses), Thirunallar (Tamil Nadu).
- **Surya temples** — **Konark** (Odisha) — the great stone chariot of the Sun; **Modhera** (Gujarat); Martand (Kashmir, ruined).
- **Ritual remedies** — gemstones (each graha's stone), mantras (the **Navagraha Stotra**, *graha* bija-mantras), charity of the graha's grain/color, fasts on the graha's weekday, and the **Navagraha homa**. [astrological/temple tradition]

## The dispute — what counts as a graha?

- **Luminaries vs planets** — Surya and Chandra are stars/satellites, not planets; some astronomers/traditions argue they are *not* grahas in the strict ("wandering star") sense, included only because Jyotisha is geocentric and concerned with visible motion.
- **Nodes vs bodies** — Rahu and Ketu are mathematical points, not bodies; their inclusion makes the count "nine," but several systems list only **seven** true grahas.
- **Eleven or more** — some later schools add **Upagrahas** (sub-grahas like Gulika/Mandi, Dhuma) and **Mandi**, and a few Vedic enumerations differ; the canonical Puranic/Jyotisha number remains **nine (nava)**.
- **Order disputes** — the *order* of the grahas differs by purpose: weekday order (planetary hours), dasha order (Vimshottari: Ketu–Shukra–Surya–Chandra–Mangala–Rahu–Guru–Shani–Budha), and "speed" order (Surya Siddhanta) are all distinct.
