# Dakṣiṇāmūrti — Śiva as the Silent South-Facing Ādi-Guru

> Where this sits in the hierarchy: `02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/` — node in [Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md). The benign (*saumya*) Maheśvara-mūrti of Śiva as the **supreme silent teacher** (*Ādi-Guru*); promoted from the single bullet in [shiva-avatars-and-forms.md §E](shiva-avatars-and-forms.md). [scholarly]

**Dakṣiṇāmūrti** (Skt. *Dakṣiṇā-mūrti*, "the south-facing form") is Śiva's form as the first and supreme **Guru** — the *Ādi-Guru* who, seated in eternal youth under a banyan (*vaṭa*) tree facing **south** (*dakṣiṇa*), transmits the highest knowledge (*jñāna*) to the four aged primordial sages not by speech but by **silence** (*mauna*) and the gesture of teaching (*cin-mudrā*). He is the patron-presiding deity of *jñāna*, *yoga*, music (*saṃgīta*), and the *śāstras*. Theologically he grounds the entire guru-cult by collapsing the distinction **Guru = Śiva**: the silent teacher within and the cosmic teacher above are one. The form is central to Śaiva Āgamic iconography, to Advaita (through Śaṅkara's *Dakṣiṇāmūrti Stotra*), and to South-Indian temple worship, where his niche is fixed on the **south wall** of the central shrine of every Śiva temple. This file catalogs his myth, iconography, sub-forms, theology, and scriptural roots, keeping the traditions separate rather than merging them.

---

## The teaching-scene (the core narrative)

- **The four Kumāras seek liberation** — the four mind-born sons of Brahmā — **Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanātana, Sanatkumāra** (the eternal *brahma-jñāna* youths; see [saptarishi.md](saptarishi.md) for the Prajāpati / mind-son context) — wander the cosmos as aged ascetics still unsatisfied, seeking the truth of the Self (*ātman*). (*Sūta Saṃhitā*, *Skanda Purāṇa*; *Śiva Purāṇa*)
- **Śiva manifests as the youthful Guru** — Śiva appears beneath a banyan tree as a radiant **sixteen-year-old** (*ṣoḍaśa-varṣa*) yogin, seated facing south, surrounded by the four hoary sages who sit before him as disciples — the eternal paradox of the **young teacher and the old pupils**, signifying that wisdom is timeless and not a function of age. (*Sūta Saṃhitā*; iconographic tradition)
- **Teaching by silence (*mauna-vyākhyāna*)** — Dakṣiṇāmūrti raises the **cin-mudrā / jñāna-mudrā** (thumb-tip joined to forefinger, the three remaining fingers extended) and the doubts of the disciples dissolve instantly; the supreme reality (*para-brahman*), being beyond words, is communicated through stillness, not exposition. This is the archetype of the **wordless transmission** (*nirvacana / mauna*) later prized in Advaita and in tantric *śaktipāta*. (*Dakṣiṇāmūrti Stotra*; *Sūta Saṃhitā*)
  - *Variant (expounder reading):* in the *Vyākhyāna* / *śāstra* aspect the teaching is understood as **commentarial exposition** of the *Āgamas* and *Vedānta* — silence as the limit-case of perfect speech, not its negation. [scholarly]

## Why "south-facing" (the symbolism of *dakṣiṇa*)

- **South = the direction of Death and of Time** — south (*dakṣiṇa*) is the quarter of **Yama** and of mortality; the Guru who faces south is the one who has **conquered death** and so can teach the disciple (facing north, toward life) the knowledge that ends rebirth. (*Dakṣiṇāmūrti Upaniṣad*; commentarial tradition) [scholarly]
- **Temple placement** — accordingly the Dakṣiṇāmūrti icon is canonically installed in the **south-facing niche (*devakoṣṭha*)** of the *garbhagṛha* outer wall in South-Indian Śiva temples, the *kṣetrapāla* of *jñāna* on that wall. (*Śilparatna*; Āgamic iconographic manuals) [regional]
  - *Variant (folk etymologies):* popular glosses derive *dakṣiṇā* from *dakṣiṇā* ("gift/skill") or read it as the form "of the right side"; the death-direction reading is the standard scholastic one. [folk] [disputed]

## Iconography (the seated yogin)

The classical *dhyāna* (per the **Aṃśumadbhedāgama**, **Kāmikāgama**, and the *Śilparatna*) fixes a four-armed (sometimes two-armed) seated figure of serene white complexion:

- **Posture** — seated on a lotus or rock pedestal beneath the banyan, in *vīrāsana* / *sukhāsana*, often with one foot pendant and one folded; eyes half-closed in *samādhi*. (*Aṃśumadbhedāgama*; *Kāmikāgama*)
- **Apasmāra-puruṣa underfoot** — the pendant right foot rests on the dwarf-demon **Apasmāra (Muyalaka)**, the personification of **ignorance / forgetfulness / spiritual epilepsy** — knowledge treading down nescience. (Shared with the [Naṭarāja](shiva-avatars-and-forms.md) icon.) (Iconographic tradition)
- **Cin-mudrā / jñāna-mudrā** — the front right hand makes the gesture of instruction (thumb + forefinger circle); the front left commonly holds a **palm-leaf manuscript** (*pustaka*, the *śāstras*) or rests in the lap. (*Śilparatna*)
- **Rear hands** — the upper right holds the **akṣamālā** (rosary, *japa*) or **ḍamaru**; the upper left holds **agni** (fire, *jñānāgni* / destruction of ignorance) or a **serpent**. (*Aṃśumadbhedāgama*)
- **The deer (*mṛga*)** — a leaping antelope beside or in the hand signifies the restless mind / the wandering of the senses that the Guru masters; with it the **paraśu** (axe) or a kamaṇḍalu may appear. (Iconographic tradition)
- **Surroundings** — the banyan above, the four Kumāra-ṛṣis seated below, often *ṛṣis* and animals at peace; matted locks (*jaṭā*) with crescent moon, third eye, serpent ornaments. (*Kāmikāgama*; *Śilparatna*)

## The four classical sub-forms

The Āgamic and *Śilpa* manuals distinguish several aspect-mūrti of Dakṣiṇāmūrti by the dominant teaching-domain; the four standard ones:

- **Jñāna-Dakṣiṇāmūrti (Vyākhyāna-D.)** — the primary form: the silent expounder of *brahma-vidyā* with the cin-mudrā and *pustaka*, teaching the Kumāras. Patron of **knowledge** (*jñāna*) and metaphysics. (*Aṃśumadbhedāgama*; *Dakṣiṇāmūrti Stotra*)
- **Yoga-Dakṣiṇāmūrti** — the form deep in *yogic* trance, often in *yoga-paṭṭa* (meditation band) round the knees, hands in *dhyāna-mudrā*; patron of **yoga** and *tapas*, the supreme *Yogeśvara*. (*Kāmikāgama*; *Śilparatna*)
- **Vīṇādhara-Dakṣiṇāmūrti** — Śiva holding/playing the **vīṇā**, the patron of **music and the arts** (*saṃgīta-śāstra*); the divine source of *nāda* (sacred sound) and *rāga*, from whom the musical tradition descends. (*Aṃśumadbhedāgama*; iconographic tradition) [regional]
- **Vyākhyāna / Śāstra-Dakṣiṇāmūrti** — the expounder of the **śāstras** (Veda, Āgama, Vyākaraṇa) — the patron of grammar, exegesis, and the textual sciences; the silent guarantor of all learning. (*Śilparatna*; commentarial tradition)
  - *Variant (extended lists):* some manuals add a **Vyādha / hunter** Dakṣiṇāmūrti and a separate count, while others fold Yoga- and Jñāna- together, yielding lists of three or up to seven aspects. [Āgamic — roster varies]

## Theology: Guru = Śiva

- **The Ādi-Guru** — Dakṣiṇāmūrti is the **first teacher** (*Ādi-Guru*) of the universe, the fountainhead of the *guru-paramparā* (teacher-lineage); every human guru is held to be a partial manifestation of this single silent teacher. This is the doctrinal root of the **guru-cult** across Hindu sampradāyas. (*Dakṣiṇāmūrti Stotra*; *Sūta Saṃhitā*) [scholarly]
- **Identity of teacher and taught** — the form embodies Advaita's central claim: the *guru* who teaches, the *jñāna* taught, and the *ātman* of the disciple are **one undivided consciousness**; the teaching "happens" only as the recognition (*pratyabhijñā*) of what was never absent. (*Dakṣiṇāmūrti Stotra*)
- **Within the Trimūrti scheme** — as the *anugraha* (grace-bestowing) and *jñāna*-giving face, Dakṣiṇāmūrti expresses Śiva's role as **revealer** rather than creator/destroyer; see the function-split in [trimurti-and-tridevi.md](trimurti-and-tridevi.md), and his place among the aspect-mūrti in the broader [shiva-myth-cycle.md](shiva-myth-cycle.md). (Śaiva theology)
- **Mantra & worship** — invoked by the **Dakṣiṇāmūrti / Medhā-dakṣiṇāmūrti mantra** as the giver of *medhā* (intellect) and *jñāna*; worshipped especially by students, scholars, and on **Guru-pūjā** occasions; presides over **Thursday (Guru-vāra)** and is identified with **Bṛhaspati / Guru** as the planetary teacher (see [the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md](the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md)). [regional]

## Śaṅkara's *Dakṣiṇāmūrti Stotra* (the Advaita charter)

- **The hymn** — Ādi **Śaṅkara**'s *Dakṣiṇāmūrti Stotra* (10 verses + the *Dhyāna-ślokas*) is the most famous text of the cult, making Dakṣiṇāmūrti the **iṣṭa-deva of Advaita Vedānta** — Śiva-as-silent-Self equated with *nirguṇa Brahman*. (*Dakṣiṇāmūrti Stotra*) [scholarly]
- **The mirror / world-as-reflection verse** — the celebrated opening (*viśvaṃ darpaṇa-dṛśyamāna-nagarī-tulyaṃ…*) likens the **whole universe to a city seen reflected in a mirror**: the world appears within consciousness as a reflection appears within glass, seemingly external yet non-different from the seer — Dakṣiṇāmūrti is saluted as the Guru who reveals this *vivarta* (apparent transformation). (*Dakṣiṇāmūrti Stotra*, v. 1)
- **Mānasollāsa vārttika** — Śaṅkara's disciple **Sureśvara** (Maṇḍana Miśra, by tradition) composed the ***Mānasollāsa***, the authoritative *vārttika* (explanatory commentary) on the *Stotra*, expanding its verses into a full Advaita treatise and a *navaratna* (nine-jewel) scheme of meditation. (*Mānasollāsa* of Sureśvara) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (authorship):* the identification of Sureśvara with **Maṇḍana Miśra** is the traditional view but is contested by scholars; the *Stotra*'s ascription to Śaṅkara himself, though near-universally accepted, is occasionally questioned. [disputed]

## Scriptural roots (the "two clocks")

- **Scriptural time** — the Dakṣiṇāmūrti teaching-scene belongs to **primordial / cross-yuga** mythic time: the four Kumāras are dateless *manvantara*-spanning sages and the instruction predates ordinary cosmic history; it is not pinned to any one yuga. (*Sūta Saṃhitā*; *Śiva Purāṇa*) [cross-yuga]
- **Historical/textual time** — as a *named, codified* form, Dakṣiṇāmūrti crystallizes in the **Śaiva Āgamas** (Aṃśumadbhedāgama, Kāmikāgama; iconographic chapters), is theologized in the *Sūta Saṃhitā* of the *Skanda Purāṇa* and the *Śiva Purāṇa*, and reaches its Advaita apex in **Śaṅkara's** *Stotra* (8th c. CE traditionally) — a literary-historical sequence distinct from the mythic scene it depicts. (Composite tradition) [scholarly]

## Sources

- **Śaiva Āgamas** — *Aṃśumadbhedāgama* and *Kāmikāgama* (iconographic chapters fixing the seated form, the four sub-forms, the apasmāra, deer, akṣamālā, agni, vīṇā, and cin-mudrā).
- **Śaṅkara**, *Dakṣiṇāmūrti Stotra* (with the *Dhyāna-ślokas*) — the mirror/world-as-reflection verse and the Guru = Brahman equation.
- **Sureśvara** (trad. = Maṇḍana Miśra), *Mānasollāsa* — the *vārttika* on the *Stotra*.
- *Sūta Saṃhitā* (of the *Skanda Purāṇa*) — the teaching of the four Kumāras and the *Ādi-Guru* theology.
- *Śiva Purāṇa* — Dakṣiṇāmūrti among the benign aspect-mūrti.
- *Dakṣiṇāmūrti Upaniṣad* — the *mantra*, the south-direction symbolism, and the *medhā/jñāna* invocation.
- *Śilparatna* and South-Indian iconographic manuals — temple placement (south niche) and the *dhyāna* prescriptions.
- T. A. Gopinatha Rao, *Elements of Hindu Iconography* (vol. II) — the standard modern survey of the Dakṣiṇāmūrti types.
