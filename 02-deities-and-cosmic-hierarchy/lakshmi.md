# Lakṣmī

> Where this sits: a deep-dive within **[Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md)** (master index: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md)). Lakṣmī is the goddess of wealth, fortune, prosperity, beauty and auspiciousness (*śrī*), and the eternal consort and *śakti* of **[Viṣṇu](vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md)**. She is one of the **Tridevī** (see **[Trimūrti & Tridevī](trimurti-and-tridevi.md)**), alongside **[Sarasvatī](saraswati.md)** and **[Pārvatī/Durgā](sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)**. Her canonical emergence is the **[Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)**; her best-known later form is **Kamalā**, the tenth **[Mahāvidyā](dasha-mahavidya.md)**.

Lakṣmī (also **Śrī**, **Mahālakṣmī**, **Kamalā**, **Padmā**, **Ramā**) is among the oldest continuously worshipped goddesses in the tradition, traceable from the Vedic **Śrī Sūkta** to the Purāṇic consort of Viṣṇu to the household deity of **[Diwali](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md)**. She personifies *śrī* — the abstract quality of radiance, abundance, sovereignty and good fortune — and is the active *prakṛti*/*śakti* through which Viṣṇu's will becomes manifest creation. Two clocks: scripturally she is beginningless (*nitya*, ever-present with Nārāyaṇa); academically her recognizable cult coalesces from c. 1000 BCE (Vedic hymn-stratum) and is firmly fused with Viṣṇu by c. 400 CE [scholarly].

---

## Names, etymology and the concept of śrī

- **Lakṣmī** - from the root *lakṣ* ("to perceive, observe, aim at, recognize a mark/sign"); the *lakṣmī* is the "sign/token" — here the auspicious sign, hence good fortune. Originally *lakṣmī* could be neutral (a "sign" that could be lucky or unlucky); the unqualified form came to mean only the *good* sign, with **a-lakṣmī** ("ill-omen") as its opposite (lexical/Vedic usage).
- **Śrī** - the older Vedic term: radiance, splendour, glory, sovereignty, prosperity. By the epic period **Śrī** and **Lakṣmī** are fully merged into one goddess **Śrī-Lakṣmī**; in Śrī Vaiṣṇavism **Śrī** remains the preferred theological name.
- **Kamalā / Padmā** - "the lotus one" (she sits/stands on a lotus and holds lotuses); **Ramā** - "the delightful / pleasing one," consort of Rāma the epithet of Viṣṇu; **Indirā**, **Hari-priyā** ("beloved of Hari/Viṣṇu"), **Cañcalā / Lolā** ("the restless, fickle one"), **Loka-mātā** ("mother of the world").
- **Mahālakṣmī** - "Great Lakṣmī," the supreme cosmic form; in Śākta usage (esp. the **[Devī Māhātmya](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)**) Mahālakṣmī is elevated to the supreme Goddess presiding over the three guṇas, distinct from the wealth-goddess sense — a name-overlap to watch [scholarly].

## Vedic and early origins (the two clocks)

- **Śrī Sūkta (Śrī Sūktam)** - a *khila* (appendix) hymn attached to the **Ṛgveda** (5th maṇḍala addendum), the foundational liturgical text for the goddess. It invokes **Śrī-Lakṣmī** as golden-hued (*hiraṇya-varṇām*), lotus-garlanded (*padma-mālinīm*), moist/lustrous (*ārdrām*), seated on a lotus and of lotus-complexion (*padma-sthitām, padma-varṇām*), drawn by elephants, bringing gold, cattle, horses, fame and food; it explicitly asks her to **drive away her sister Alakṣmī** (poverty, hunger, misfortune) (**Ṛgveda Khila / Śrī Sūkta**).
  - *Dating:* the Śrī Sūkta is a late Vedic appendix, commonly placed c. 1000–500 BCE [scholarly] — the point at which the recognizable wealth-and-lotus goddess emerges.
- **Earlier Vedic threads** - *śrī* as an abstract quality of glory pervades the Ṛgveda; the **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa** narrates Śrī emerging radiant from Prajāpati and the gods seizing her many powers (food, sovereignty, beauty, etc.), an early "distribution of fortune" myth.
- **Personifications absorbed into Lakṣmī** - **Lajjā/Hrī** (modesty), **Puṣṭi** (nourishment), **Tuṣṭi** (contentment); the **Pāñcarātra/Lakṣmī Tantra** later systematizes her as the cosmic *śakti* itself.

## Birth — the Samudra Manthana (and rival accounts)

The signature birth-myth is the **churning of the milk-ocean** (see the full episode at **[Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)** and **[Kūrma avatāra](dashavatara.md)**). It is an *appearance/re-appearance*, not an absolute origin, since theologically she is eternal.

- **Emergence from the ocean** - when devas and asuras churned the Kṣīra-sāgara (ocean of milk) for *amṛta*, Lakṣmī (Śrī) rose seated on a full-blown lotus, radiant and fully grown, among the *ratnas* (treasures) the churning yielded. The gods and sages hymned her; the celestial elephants (Diggajas) bathed her with water from golden pots, and she garlanded **Viṣṇu**, choosing him as her eternal consort (**Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.9**; **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.8**; **Mahābhārata, Ādi & Āraṇyaka parvans**).
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.9, the elaborate account):* her appearance is preceded by **Durvāsa's curse** — the sage gives Indra a divine garland, Indra slights it (his elephant Airāvata tramples it), and Lakṣmī (Śrī) abandons the three worlds, which lose their lustre; the churning is undertaken specifically to recover her. Here she is daughter of the sage **Bhṛgu and Khyāti** in a prior cosmic age, re-manifesting from the ocean.
  - *Variant (motherless, ocean-born):* most accounts treat her as **Kṣīrābdhi-tanayā** ("daughter of the milk-ocean") / **Sāgara-kanyā**, with no parents — directly arisen from the churned sea.
  - *Variant (daughter of Bhṛgu):* in genealogical passages she is **Bhārgavī**, daughter of Bhṛgu and Khyāti, sister of the *Dhātṛ/Vidhātṛ* — harmonized with the ocean-birth as a re-birth.
  - *Variant (born of the primordial lotus from Viṣṇu's navel):* some Vaiṣṇava texts place her on the cosmic lotus at the very dawn of creation, eternally with Nārāyaṇa, the churning being only her public re-appearance.
- **Order of the ratnas** - the *fourteen jewels* lists vary, but Lakṣmī/Śrī is consistently among them, usually counted near the goddess **Vāruṇī** (wine) and the **Apsaras**, with the **Kaustubha gem** and **Pārijāta tree** that become Viṣṇu's; see the variant rosters at **[Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)** [variants flagged there].

## Consort of Viṣṇu — and incarnation alongside the avatāras

- **Eternal pairing** - Lakṣmī is the inseparable *śakti* of Viṣṇu; he is *Lakṣmī-pati / Śrīnivāsa* ("abode of Śrī"). She rests on his chest as the **Śrīvatsa** mark/curl, and in iconography sits on his left thigh or at his feet pressing them (**Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa** form).
- **She incarnates whenever he does** - "when Hari takes a body, she too takes a body" (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.9). Standard correspondences across the **[Daśāvatāra](dashavatara.md)**:
  - With **Vāmana/Viṣṇu in heaven** → **Padmā / Kamalā**.
  - With **Paraśurāma** → **Dharaṇī** (his wife).
  - With **Rāma** → **Sītā** (born of the earth-furrow; see Bhū-devī overlap) — see **[Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana)**.
  - With **Kṛṣṇa** → **Rukmiṇī** (his chief queen), and in devotional tradition **Rādhā** (**[Kṛṣṇa-līlā](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md)**).
  - With **Veṅkaṭeśvara** (Tirupati) → **Padmāvatī / Alamēlu-maṅga**, born of a lotus.
  - *Variant:* the Sītā = Lakṣmī identification competes with the **Sītā = Bhū-devī (Earth)** reading (she is *Bhūmijā*, born of the earth, and returns to it) — in Śrī Vaiṣṇavism resolved by treating Bhū-devī as a form of Lakṣmī (see below).
  - *Variant (folk):* local traditions multiply consort-incarnations (e.g. various queens of Kṛṣṇa, the Buddha's consort for the Buddha avatāra); these are open-ended [folk].

## Śrī-devī and Bhū-devī — the two (or three) consorts

A major South Indian / Śrī Vaiṣṇava theology splits Lakṣmī into complementary consorts of Viṣṇu (esp. as Veṅkaṭeśvara/Nārāyaṇa, with Śrī to his right, Bhū to his left):

- **Śrī-devī** - the spiritual energy (*para-prakṛti*), grace, sovereignty and prosperity; the "lotus" goddess.
- **Bhū-devī (Bhūmi-devī)** - the Earth-goddess, material/manifest energy (*apara-prakṛti*), patience and forbearance; she is the earth Viṣṇu rescues as **Varāha** (see **[Daśāvatāra](dashavatara.md)**) and the mother of **Sītā** and (in some texts) of **Naraka/Narakāsura**. Treated as a form/aspect of the one Lakṣmī.
- **Nīlā-devī (Nappinnai)** - a third consort named in the **Lakṣmī Tantra** and Pāñcarātra; the *āhlāda-śakti* (energy of bliss/delight), seldom depicted, "standing behind the Lord." In Tamil tradition identified with **Nappiṉṉai**, the cowherd-bride of Kṛṣṇa [regional].
- **Theological frame (Śrī Vaiṣṇava)** - Rāmānuja's school makes **Śrī** central as the eternal mediatrix (*puruṣakāra*) who intercedes between the soul and Viṣṇu; the **Tāyār** ("Mother") shrine is integral to every temple. The **Pāñcarātra Lakṣmī Tantra** makes Lakṣmī the active creatrix — Viṣṇu's will, she does the actual creating, sustaining and dissolving.

## Aṣṭa-Lakṣmī — the eight forms

The **Aṣṭa-Lakṣmī** ("Eight Lakṣmīs") are eight manifestations presiding over eight domains of wealth/wellbeing; the set is liturgical (popularized by the **Aṣṭalakṣmī Stotra**, c. 20th c. in its common form, on older Purāṇic-tantric roots) [late text]. The standard eight:

- **Ādi-Lakṣmī (Mahā-Lakṣmī)** - the primeval, original form; leads the seeker to the source (*ātman*); attendant of Nārāyaṇa.
- **Dhana-Lakṣmī** - goddess of money/material wealth and gold.
- **Dhānya-Lakṣmī** - goddess of grain and agricultural abundance, sustenance.
- **Gaja-Lakṣmī** - goddess of animal wealth, cattle, and royal splendour (the elephant-flanked form; see below).
- **Santāna-Lakṣmī** - bestower of progeny, offspring and family continuity.
- **Vīra-Lakṣmī (Dhairya-Lakṣmī)** - bestower of valour, courage and fortitude; inspired by Durgā.
- **Vijaya-Lakṣmī (Jaya-Lakṣmī)** - bestower of victory and success over obstacles; Durgā-inflected.
- **Vidyā-Lakṣmī** - bestower of knowledge, learning, arts and sciences (overlaps Sarasvatī's domain).
  - *Variant (membership):* lists differ — some substitute **Aiśvarya-Lakṣmī**, **Saubhāgya-Lakṣmī**, **Rāja-Lakṣmī**, **Saumya-Lakṣmī**, or **Vara-Lakṣmī** for one of the above; the count stays eight but the roster is not perfectly fixed [open-ended roster].
  - *Variant (sixteen):* a **Ṣoḍaśa-Lakṣmī** (sixteen forms) enumeration also circulates in some Stotra traditions [folk/late].

## Gaja-Lakṣmī — the elephant motif

- **Gaja-Lakṣmī** - the goddess flanked by two (sometimes four) elephants who, with raised trunks, pour water/lustral pots over her in *abhiṣeka*. One of the oldest and most widespread Lakṣmī images, appearing on early Buddhist railings (Bharhut, Sanchi, c. 2nd–1st c. BCE), Hindu temples, and ubiquitously over doorways as an auspicious threshold-guardian [scholarly: pre-Purāṇic iconographic type].
- **Meanings** - the bathing-elephants recall her ocean-birth *abhiṣeka*; elephants signify royal power, rain/fertility (water = prosperity), and the *rāja-lakṣmī* (sovereign fortune) that legitimizes kingship. Counted among the Aṣṭa-Lakṣmī as the giver of animal/royal wealth.

---

## Lakṣmī's cañcalā ("restless/fickle") nature, and Alakṣmī

- **Cañcalā / Lolā** - "she who does not stay in one place"; Lakṣmī is famously *fickle*, moving from house to house, person to person — fortune is impermanent. This motif (esp. in the **Mahābhārata** and Purāṇas) explains the rise and fall of kings and the precariousness of wealth.
  - *Theological softening:* once her bond with Viṣṇu becomes exclusive (by c. 400 CE), her character shifts from restlessness toward **steadfast loyalty** to Viṣṇu and his devotees [scholarly].
  - *Mahābhārata teaching:* Śrī declares (to Indra/in dialogues) that she dwells only where there is truth, generosity, dharma, exertion and cleanliness, and flees pride, sloth, falsehood and quarrelling — fortune as the reward of virtue.
- **Alakṣmī (Jyeṣṭhā / Dhūmāvatī-affinity)** - the inauspicious "anti-Lakṣmī": poverty, hunger, strife, sloth, filth, inauspiciousness — already named as Lakṣmī's antithesis in the **Śrī Sūkta** ("*alakṣmīr me naśyatām*," let ill-fortune perish).
  - **As Jyeṣṭhā ("the Elder")** - in South Indian and Purāṇic tradition Alakṣmī = **Jyeṣṭhā Devī**, the *elder sister* of Lakṣmī who also rose from the churning (before the nectar/before Lakṣmī, hence "elder"). Dark-complexioned, riding a donkey/crow, with a broom or crow-banner; she resides in quarrels, dirt and idleness. She had her own minor cult (esp. early medieval Tamil country) before fading.
    - *Variant:* Jyeṣṭhā is married off to the sage **Duḥsaha** (or to "Kali"), and dwells wherever inauspiciousness is found (**Liṅga Purāṇa**, **Padma Purāṇa**).
  - **Diwali connection** - lamps, lights, cleaning the home, and the clatter of pots are said to *welcome Lakṣmī and drive out Alakṣmī* — the auspicious displacing the inauspicious.
  - *Affinity:* Alakṣmī's profile (inauspicious widow, crow-banner) closely parallels **Dhūmāvatī** among the **[Mahāvidyās](dasha-mahavidya.md)** [scholarly].

## Kamalā — the Mahāvidyā form

- **Kamalā / Kamalātmikā** - Lakṣmī as the **tenth and final Mahāvidyā** (the gentlest, most "beneficent" of the ten Tantric wisdom-goddesses), the Tantric Lakṣmī. Golden, four-armed, lotus-seated, bathed by four elephants — essentially Gaja-Lakṣmī absorbed into the Śākta-Tantra scheme. Her avatāra-correspondence in the **Todala Tantra** is the **Buddha** (Kamalā/Mahālakṣmī → Buddha; it is Kālī, not Kamalā, who maps to Kṛṣṇa). See **[Daśa Mahāvidyā](dasha-mahavidya.md)** for the full set and origin myth [late text].

## Iconography

- **Form** - a serene, radiant, golden- or rose-complexioned woman of mature beauty, seated or standing on a **lotus** (*padmāsana*); usually **four-armed** (two-armed in many household/Gaja-Lakṣmī images).
- **Hands/attributes** - two hands typically hold **lotuses** (red/pink); the lower hands show **varada-mudrā** (boon-giving, often with gold coins streaming from the palm) and **abhaya-mudrā** (fearlessness), or hold a **kalaśa/pot of treasure** and an **akṣamālā** (rosary). Coins (*dhana*) flowing from her hand are her signature.
- **Vāhana / associated animals** - the **owl (ulūka)** is her best-known vāhana, especially in eastern India and at Diwali; it signifies wakeful watchfulness over wealth and the ability to "see" what is hidden (penetrating sight). **Elephants** flank her (Gaja-Lakṣmī). Some images place her on a lotus alone, or beside Garuḍa/Viṣṇu.
  - *Variant (owl ambivalence):* folk readings make the owl a caution — wealth pursued blindly can lead to "owl-like" blindness in daylight; others read the owl positively as wisdom. The owl is also, by counter-tradition, an inauspicious bird, so its role as Lakṣmī's mount is itself debated [folk].
- **Colour symbolism** - **red** = activity/prosperity; **golden** = wealth; **white** = purity (in her Sarasvatī-adjacent forms); seated on red lotus over white = purity within abundance.
- **Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa** - the iconic couple: Lakṣmī on Viṣṇu's left thigh/lap, or Viṣṇu reclining on **Śeṣa** with Lakṣmī pressing his feet (**Śeṣa-śāyī / Anantaśayana**).
- **Śrīvatsa & Kaustubha** - she is identified with the **Śrīvatsa** mark on Viṣṇu's chest; the **Kaustubha** gem (also ocean-born) is sometimes read as the *jīva*/soul beside her *prakṛti*.

## Worship, festivals and household cult

- **Diwali / Deepāvalī (Lakṣmī Pūjā)** - her paramount festival: on the new-moon night of Kārtika, homes are cleaned, lit with lamps and rangoli, and Lakṣmī is invited in for the year's prosperity; in many regions paired with **Gaṇeśa** (see **[Gaṇeśa](ganesha-and-kartikeya.md)**) for wealth + auspicious-beginnings. See **[Festivals & Vratas](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md)**.
  - *Variant (regional):* in eastern India (Bengal/Odisha/Assam) the main **Lakṣmī/Kojāgarī Pūjā** is on the **Kārtika full moon** (Śarad Pūrṇimā / Kojāgarī), distinct from the all-India new-moon Diwali Lakṣmī Pūjā.
- **Vara-Lakṣmī Vrata** - a major South Indian vow (Śrāvaṇa month, Friday before the full moon) by married women for family wellbeing, worshipping Lakṣmī as **Vara-Lakṣmī** ("boon-granting"). See **[Regional Temple Festivals](../05-concepts-and-dharma/regional-temple-festivals.md)**.
- **Friday (Śukravāra)** and the autumn season are sacred to her; **Akṣaya Tṛtīyā** and **Dhanteras** (Dhana-trayodaśī, two days before Diwali) are wealth-auspicious days.
- **Tāyār shrines** - in every Śrī Vaiṣṇava temple the goddess (e.g. **Raṅganāyakī** at Srirangam, **Padmāvatī** at Tiruchanur near Tirupati, **Mahālakṣmī** at **Kolhāpur**) has a dedicated sanctum; Kolhāpur Mahālakṣmī (Ambābāī) is one of the **Śakti Pīṭhas**.

## Key relations (quick map)

- **Consort:** Viṣṇu (and his every avatāra — Sītā with Rāma, Rukmiṇī/Rādhā with Kṛṣṇa, Dharaṇī with Paraśurāma, Padmāvatī with Veṅkaṭeśvara).
- **Co-Tridevī:** Sarasvatī (consort of **[Brahmā](trimurti-and-tridevi.md)**), Pārvatī (consort of **[Śiva](shiva-myth-cycle.md)**). In folk rivalry, Lakṣmī (wealth) and Sarasvatī (learning) "do not stay together."
- **Shadow/sister:** Alakṣmī / Jyeṣṭhā (elder sister, misfortune).
- **Forms within her:** Śrī-devī, Bhū-devī, Nīlā-devī; the Aṣṭa-Lakṣmī; Kamalā the Mahāvidyā; Mahālakṣmī (Devī Māhātmya).
- **Origin-companions:** ocean-born among the *ratnas* of the **[Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md)**; in the **[Gajendra-Mokṣa](../04-deep-dives/gajendra-moksha.md)** episode the elephant-king's rescue echoes the Gaja-Lakṣmī water-symbolism.
