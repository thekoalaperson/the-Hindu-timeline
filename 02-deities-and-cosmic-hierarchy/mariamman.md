# Māriyammaṉ — the Tamil Rain-and-Pox Mother

> Where this sits: a dedicated node within **[Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md)**, expanding the **grāmadevatā (village-goddess)** line in the **[Forms of the Devī & the Navadurgā](devi-forms-and-navadurga.md)** roster. Her North Indian smallpox-counterpart is **[Śītalā](shitala.md)**; her Sanskritizing back-stories pull on the **[Reṇukā–Paraśurāma](dashavatara.md)** cycle.

**Māriyammaṉ** (Tamil *māri-ammaṉ*, "Mother Māri") is the single most widely worshipped **grāmadevatā** of South India — the tutelary mother of village, field and body across Tamil Nadu and the Tamil diaspora. She is at once **fierce and maternal**: the goddess who *sends* smallpox, pox and epidemic and the goddess who *cures* them, and equally the bringer of the monsoon **rain** (*māri* itself means "rain" in Tamil). She belongs to the **living, folk** stratum of Devī worship, not the Purāṇic core; her cult is overwhelmingly a "two-clocks" **kali-historical** phenomenon — temple foundations, festival forms and Sanskritizing myths attested in the last several centuries, with no fixed scriptural date.

---

## Name, function, dual nature

- **Māri / Māriyammaṉ** — the name reads two ways: **māri = "rain"** (the monsoon mother) and, by a fierce folk gloss, **māri = "death / change / the smiting one"**; both senses are deliberately held together — she gives life-rain and the killing fever from one hand (Source: Tamil folk tradition; *grāmadevatā* studies).
- **The pox-mother** — chief guardian against **smallpox (periya ammai, "the big mother"), chickenpox/measles (siṟu ammai), cholera and epidemic**; the eruptive pustules of smallpox are read as the goddess's own "pearls" or her presence *in* the body, so the sick person is treated as one possessed/graced by Māri, cooled with **neem (vēppilai) leaves**, turmeric and buttermilk rather than simply medicated (Source: Tamil folk medical-religious tradition).
- **The rain-mother** — invoked in drought; her festivals cluster in the hot pre-monsoon months (Tamil **Āḍi**/**Cittirai**, roughly Jul–Aug and Apr–May) precisely when both heat-disease and rain-need peak — the agrarian and the epidemic calendars are the same calendar (Source: festival ethnography [scholarly]).
- **Ambivalent heat** — the governing folk physics is **heat vs. cooling**: the goddess "heats" (anger, fever, drought) and must be **cooled** (neem, water-pots, buttermilk, firewalking that "spends" the heat) — the same logic that governs **[Śītalā](shitala.md)** in the North (Source: comparative grāmadevatā studies [scholarly]).

## Iconography — aniconic, fierce head, pot and neem

- **Often aniconic** — in countless village shrines Māri is **not** an anthropomorphic image but a **plain stone, a daubed termite-mound, a trident, or simply a pot (karagam) under a neem tree**; the neem tree itself is her body (Source: village-shrine ethnography [regional]).
- **The fierce severed head** — a characteristic form is a **large disembodied face/head** (sometimes with bulging eyes, fangs, lolling tongue) set at ground level — linked to the Reṇukā beheading myth below (Source: Tamil temple iconography [folk]).
- **The seated benign form** — in the great Sanskritized temples she is a full four-armed seated goddess in red, holding **triśūla and ḍamaru** (or pāśa/aṅkuśa), crowned and bedecked like Pārvatī/Durgā — the "cooled," temple-domesticated Māri (Source: Samayapuram and Punnainallur sthala tradition).
- **Emblems** — **neem leaves** (worn, waved, strung as garlands and as the sick-bed bedding), the **karagam pot** (a decorated water-pot, often topped with a neem-and-flower cone, embodying the goddess's cooling presence), **turmeric and margosa**, the **vēppilai (neem) skirt** worn by vow-takers (Source: festival iconography).

## Major temple cults

- **Samayapuram Māriyammaṉ (Ariyamala/Tiruchirappalli, Tamil Nadu)** — the **largest and most famous** Māriyammaṉ temple, near the Kāveri; the central deity is a single soft-clay-and-sand mūrti (Mūla-vigraha) never anointed with abrasive substances, with a separate metal processional image; draws enormous crowds at its **Cittirai** flower-and-fire festivals (Source: Samayapuram sthala tradition [regional]).
- **Punnainallur Māriyammaṉ (near Thanjavur)** — a celebrated shrine said to have arisen when the goddess directed a devotee (in tradition, linked to a Maratha-era Thanjavur ruler) to an anthill (puttu) image in a punnai-grove; a major Sanskritized Māri seat (Source: Punnainallur sthala-purāṇa [late text]).
- **Mariamman Teppakulam (Madurai)** — the great temple **tank (teppakulam)** of Vandiyur, associated with the float-festival (**teppam**); a Māriyammaṉ tank-cult adjoining the Mīnākṣī complex (Source: Madurai sthala tradition).
- **Diaspora flagships** — the **Sri Mariamman Temple, Singapore (1827)** and the **Sri Mariamman Temple, Bangkok**, plus major shrines across **Malaysia, Sri Lanka, Mauritius, South Africa, Fiji, Guyana, Trinidad and Réunion** — Māri travelled with indentured and migrant Tamil labour and is, in many of these lands, the **principal Hindu goddess** (Source: diaspora temple records [scholarly]).

## Festival forms

- **Firewalking (tīmiti)** — barefoot crossing of a bed of glowing coals, undertaken as a vow; the unburnt feet prove the goddess's cooling grace and "spend" her heat; a signature Māri (and Draupadī-amman) rite across Tamil Nadu and the whole diaspora (Source: festival ethnography [scholarly]).
- **Karagam (karagāṭṭam) pot-dance** — a dancer balances the decorated **karagam pot** (the goddess herself) on the head through an athletic dance; carrying Māri's cooling water in procession (Source: Tamil folk-performance tradition [regional]).
- **Neem and the vēppilai vow** — devotees, especially the formerly pox-stricken, wear **skirts/garlands of neem leaves**, sometimes little else, in fulfilment of vows; neem-water is sprinkled to cool the goddess and the village (Source: vow ethnography [folk]).
- **Body-piercing, cavaṭi and the milk-pot** — hook-swinging, cheek/tongue piercing (alaku), and **pāl-kuḍam** (milk-pot) processions appear at Māri festivals as austerity-vows, overlapping with the Murukaṉ Taipūcam repertoire (Source: festival ethnography [regional]).
- **The goddess's "fever" cycle** — many temples enact an annual sequence in which the goddess **heats** (her anger/illness rises), is **propitiated and cooled**, and is **paraded** to bless the village — a dramatized epidemic-and-recovery (Source: temple festival studies [scholarly]).

## Pan-regional equation — Reṇukā / Yellammā and the Sanskritizing back-stories

The independent Tamil pox-mother is, across the Deccan, repeatedly **identified with Reṇukā/Yellammā** and given a Purāṇic pedigree to legitimize the village cult.

- **The Reṇukā beheading etiology** — **Reṇukā**, chaste wife of sage **[Jamadagni](dashavatara.md)**, is beheaded on his command by their son **Paraśurāma**; granted a boon, he restores her — but (in the folk version) her **head is rejoined to the body of a low-caste/outcaste woman**, or vice versa, producing a **split/hybrid goddess**; the severed-and-rejoined head becomes the etiology of the fierce, ground-level **Māri / Yellammā** head-icon (Source: *Mahābhārata*/Purāṇa Reṇukā tale + South Indian folk variants [folk]).
  - *Variant (one goddess, many names):* **Reṇukā = Yellammā (Saundatti) = Ekvīrā = Māriyammaṉ = Padmākṣī = Aai** — a single pan-Deccan mother under local names, the Tamil node being Māri (Source: Deccan folk tradition [folk]).
  - *Variant (Śītalā/Mariyammaṉ overlap):* in some tellings the head-and-body split yields **two** sisters/goddesses — a "pure" Brahmin head and an "impure" body — read as the high-temple Māri vs. the blood-offering village Māri ([disputed]).
- **Sanskritizing the cult** — temple back-stories progressively reframe Māri as **Pārvatī/Durgā/Kālī** or as **Ādi Parāśakti** descended to a village, replacing buffalo/goat **blood-sacrifice (bali)** with vegetarian, Agamic worship; this is a documented, ongoing **Sanskritization** of a folk goddess, contested between reform temples and village shrines (Source: grāmadevatā studies [scholarly/disputed]).
- **The pox-demon back-story** — a parallel etiology makes Māri a goddess who slew (or who *is*) the disease-demon, so that the pustules are her victory-marks or her seed sown in the body (Source: Tamil folk tradition [folk]).

> **Two clocks.** None of the above carries a scriptural yuga-date. The Reṇukā tale sits in **Treta-yuga** *as myth* (Paraśurāma's age — see **[Daśāvatāra](dashavatara.md)**), but Māriyammaṉ as a *worshipped temple goddess* is a **kali-historical** cult: her shrines, festivals and Sanskritizing myths are attested in recent centuries, not in the Vedas or core Purāṇas.

## Diaspora following

- **A goddess of migration** — carried by 19th–20th-c. indentured and merchant Tamils, Māri became a **principal Hindu goddess of the South Indian diaspora**: temples in **Singapore, Malaysia, Sri Lanka, South Africa, Mauritius, Réunion, Fiji, Guyana and Trinidad** (Source: diaspora studies [scholarly]).
- **Firewalking abroad** — the **tīmiti** firewalk is one of the most visible diaspora Hindu festivals (e.g. Singapore's annual firewalking festival at the Sri Mariamman Temple), exporting the cooling-the-goddess logic intact (Source: diaspora ethnography).
- **Creole syntheses** — in **Réunion and the Caribbean** Māri-worship fused with local and other-faith elements, sometimes as the umbrella name for the whole village-goddess complex abroad (Source: diaspora studies [regional]).
