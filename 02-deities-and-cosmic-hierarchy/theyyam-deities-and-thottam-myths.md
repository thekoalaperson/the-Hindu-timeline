# Theyyam Deities & Thoṭṭam Myths of North Malabar

> Where this sits in the hierarchy: `02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/` — node in [Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md). A catalog of the **theyyam pantheon** of North Malabar (Kannur–Kasaragod, Kerala) and the **thoṭṭam (tōṟṟam)** origin-songs that script it — the divine cast behind the possession-ritual described in [Regional Temple Festivals](../05-concepts-and-dharma/regional-temple-festivals.md). The most famous theyyam, **[Muthappan](muthappan.md)**, keeps his own canonical file; the goddess-pole of the tradition roots in **[Bhadrakālī & the Kerala Bhagavati tradition](bhadrakali-kerala-bhagavati.md)**. [folk] [regional]

**Theyyam** (Malayalam *teyyam*, from Sanskrit *daivam*, "god") is the North-Malabar ritual in which, after the sung **thoṭṭam**, face-painting (*mukhatezhuthu*) and costume, the placing of the **mudi** (headdress) marks the **god entering the dancer**, who is then venerated **as the living deity across caste lines**. The pantheon is **open-ended — 400+ named theyyams** — mixing Purāṇic gods, fierce goddesses, deified heroes, ancestors and spirits. This file catalogs the institution (thoṭṭam song, *kāvu* grove, performing communities) and the charter-myths of seven prominent theyyams — **Pottan, Kathivanūr Vīran, Muchilōṭṭu Bhagavathi, Vishṇumūrti, Kuṭṭichāttan, Gulikan and Thee Chāmuṇḍi** — cataloging variants rather than merging them. [folk] [regional] [oral tradition]

---

## The institution: thoṭṭam song, kāvu grove, performing communities

- **The thoṭṭam (tōṟṟam) song-form** — every theyyam is scripted by its **thoṭṭam**, an oral Malayalam **origin-and-praise song** sung by the performer and his troupe (with *chenda* drums) before the full costume is donned; it **narrates the deity's birth, deeds and enshrinement**, and its recitation is itself the invocation drawing the god into the dancer. The thoṭṭam corpus is a major body of **unwritten Malayalam folk literature**, transmitted within the performing families. (Theyyam / Kaliyāṭṭam oral tradition) [folk] [oral tradition] [regional]
  - *Variant (stages):* performance unfolds in stages — the plain **thoṭṭam/vellāṭṭam** (preliminary, partial-costume appearance with the song) and the full **theyyam** with *mudi*; terminology varies by deity and locality. [folk] [open-ended]
- **The kāvu (sacred grove-shrine)** — theyyams are enacted not in Brahmanical temples but in **kāvus** (sacred groves), family shrines (*tharavāḍu*, *kōṭṭam*, *palliyara*, *madappura*) and community shrines; the kāvu preserves a patch of sacred forest, and its annual **kaliyāṭṭam** is its festival. (North Malabar kāvu tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **The season** — roughly **Thulam 10 (Oct)** to **Edavam (May–Jun)**, kāvu by kāvu; a **perumkaliyāṭṭam** is the rare "great" festival a shrine holds at long intervals. (Theyyam calendar tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **The performers — oppressed-caste priesthood** — theyyam is the hereditary right of specific **Dalit and backward-caste communities** — **Vaṇṇān, Malayan, Vēlan, Pulayan, Māvilan, Koppāḷan** and others — each holding particular deities; during the ritual the **landlord and Brahmin bow before the possessed Dalit performer**, a structured inversion of the caste order. (Theyyam tradition) [folk] [regional]
  - *Note:* scholars read theyyam as a **pre-Brahmanical / Dravidian substrate** of village-deity, hero and ancestor worship, later syncretized with Purāṇic figures, its caste-inversion a ritualized social protest. [scholarly]
- **The open-ended roster** — the 400+ theyyams include **amma (mother-goddess) theyyams** (Chāmuṇḍi, Bhagavathi and kin of the [Kerala Bhadrakālī](bhadrakali-kerala-bhagavati.md) sphere), **vīra (hero) theyyams** (deified dead like Kathivanūr Vīran), Purāṇic gods in local dress (Vishṇumūrti), spirit-theyyams (Gulikan), and the daily-performed **[Muthappan](muthappan.md)** — no two localities share an identical pantheon. (Theyyam tradition) [folk] [regional] [open-ended]

## Pottan Theyyam — the casteless Śiva who rebuked Śaṅkara

- **Śiva as the Pulaya** — in the Pottan thoṭṭam, **Śiva takes the form of a Pulaya** (an "untouchable" agrestic-labourer caste man) — the **Pulappottan** — carrying toddy and accompanied by his household, and **blocks the narrow path of the philosopher [Śaṅkarācārya](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/adi-shankara.md)**, who (in the tellings) is on his way to ascend the *sarvajña-pīṭha* (throne of omniscience). (Pottan Theyyam thoṭṭam) [folk] [oral tradition] [regional]
- **The demand and the dialogue** — Śaṅkara orders the polluting Pulaya off the path; the Pottan refuses and answers with the thoṭṭam's celebrated **anti-caste argument**: the string of questions of which the most quoted is — **"if you cut us, is it not blood that flows? if you cut you, is it not the same blood?"** (*"nāṅgaḷe kottiyālum chorayille, niṅgaḷe kottiyālum chorayille?"*) — pressing that the same blood, the same rice grown by Pulaya labour, the same bathing water and the one indwelling self make purity-by-birth meaningless. (Pottan thoṭṭam song) [folk] [oral tradition]
- **The revelation** — Śaṅkara, defeated by the "untouchable's" Vedānta, realizes his interlocutor is **Śiva himself** (with Pārvatī and Nandikēśan in the Pulaya household's guise); he **prostrates**, and the god ordains that he be worshipped in this very casteless form — the charter of the Pottan Theyyam. (Pottan thoṭṭam) [folk] [oral tradition]
  - *Variant (Śaṅkara hagiography parallel):* the Sanskrit Śaṅkara-vijaya hagiographies carry the same structure at Kāśī — Śiva confronting Śaṅkara as a **caṇḍāla** whose challenge occasions the *Manīṣā-pañcakam* ("who is the 'move aside' addressed to — body or self?"); scholars read Pottan as the North-Malabar folk transposition of this encounter, with the caste-critique sharpened and localized. (Śaṅkara-vijaya traditions; cf. [Ādi Śaṅkara](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/adi-shankara.md)) [scholarly] [disputed]
- **The fire-bed** — Pottan is a **fire theyyam**: the performer (of the Pulayan and other communities, by shrine-right) **lies upon and rolls in the *mēleri*** — the great bed of glowing embers — repeatedly through the night, unburnt by the god's grace; the fire-ordeal is read as the god's testimony against the "heat" of caste. (Pottan Theyyam performance tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **The Pottan trio** — Pottan is commonly enacted with companion theyyams — **Pulachāmuṇḍi** (the goddess of the Pulaya guise) and **Pulamāruthan** — the divine household that walked with Śiva in the tale; groupings vary by kāvu. (Pottan Theyyam tradition) [folk] [regional] [open-ended]

## Kathivanūr Vīran — the deified archer-hero

- **Mandappan of Mangāṭṭ** — the thoṭṭam sings of **Mandappan Chekavar**, born at Mangāṭṭ (near Taliparamba, Kannur) after his parents' long childlessness; grown into a superb **archer**, he neglects work for sport and companions, and his enraged father **disowns him**, upon which Mandappan leaves home for the east. (Kathivanūr Vīran thoṭṭam) [folk] [oral tradition] [regional]
- **Kathivanūr and Chemmarathi** — Mandappan settles with his maternal kin at **Kathivanūr** in **Kudaku (Coorg/Kodagu country)**, trades and prospers, and marries **Chemmarathi**; the thoṭṭam dwells on their passionate and quarrelsome marriage. (Kathivanūr Vīran thoṭṭam) [folk] [oral tradition]
- **The quarrel and the battle** — when war breaks out with the **Kodagu (Coorg) raiders**, Mandappan arms for battle; delayed (in the common telling by a domestic quarrel, Chemmarathi's hasty words becoming a curse), he plunges into the fight and turns the battle, but returning for something forgotten (his betel-pouch/ring in the tellings) he is **ambushed and killed**, his **finger with its ring severed**. (Kathivanūr Vīran thoṭṭam) [folk] [oral tradition]
  - *Variant (the severed finger as token):* the severed **ring-finger** is carried back / found by Chemmarathi and is the token by which his death is known; details of the delay and the forgotten object differ between kāvu recensions. [folk] [open-ended]
- **Chemmarathi in the pyre** — at his funeral pyre **Chemmarathi throws herself into the fire** and dies with him; hero and wife are **deified together**, and the Kathivanūr Vīran theyyam enshrines them both, Chemmarathi represented beside/within his rite. (Kathivanūr Vīran thoṭṭam) [folk] [oral tradition]
- **The performance** — Kathivanūr Vīran is among the most **athletic** theyyams — the performer (Vaṇṇān community by prevailing right) enacts the hero's archery and combat around a marked arena (his "Kathivanūr"), with martial (*kaḷari*-register) movement; devotees petition the hero-god for courage, matrimony and the healing of domestic strife. (Kathivanūr Vīran performance tradition) [folk] [regional]

## Muchilōṭṭu Bhagavathi — the wronged scholar-virgin

- **The debater of Perinchellūr** — a **Brahmin virgin girl of Perinchellūr (Taliparamba)**, extraordinarily learned, **defeats the assembled scholars in disputation**; humiliated, they trap her with a malicious double question — *what is the greatest pain, and what the greatest pleasure?* — and when she answers **"the pain of childbirth; the pleasure of union"**, they declare that a virgin could know these only by unchastity and **excommunicate her**. (Muchilōṭṭu Bhagavathi thoṭṭam) [folk] [oral tradition] [regional]
- **The fire at Karivellūr** — cast out, the girl wanders south, prays to **Śiva at Karivellūr**, and — proclaiming her innocence — **immolates herself in a pyre**, her *tapas* transmuting the injustice into divinity. (Muchilōṭṭu Bhagavathi thoṭṭam) [folk] [oral tradition]
- **The Vāṇiya charter** — a **Vāṇiyan (oil-presser)** — the **Muchilōṭṭu Paḍanāyar** — passing with his oil, witnesses the marvel (in the tellings his oil-measure miraculously overflows / a divine flame follows him); divination reveals the girl-goddess's will, and he **enshrines her at Muchilōṭṭu**, whence she is **Muchilōṭṭu Bhagavathi**, supreme **kuladevatā of the Vāṇiya community**, with a network of Muchilōṭṭu kāvus across North Malabar. (Muchilōṭṭu Bhagavathi thoṭṭam; Vāṇiya community tradition) [folk] [oral tradition] [regional]
- **The benign bride-goddess** — unusually among the fierce amma theyyams, Muchilōṭṭu Bhagavathi is enacted as a **radiant, benign bride** — resplendent costume, measured dance, motherly blessing — her **perumkaliyāṭṭam** festivals being among the grandest theyyam events; she is honoured as a chaste-virgin apotheosis akin in type (not in story) to the Kaṇṇaki layer of the [Kerala Bhagavati tradition](bhadrakali-kerala-bhagavati.md). (Muchilōṭṭu performance tradition) [folk] [regional]

## Vishṇumūrti — Narasiṁha in the kāvu

- **The Purāṇic charter** — the Vishṇumūrti thoṭṭam sings the **Narasiṁha avatāra**: Hiraṇyakaśipu's boon and tyranny, **Prahlāda's** steadfast devotion, and Viṣṇu bursting **from the pillar as the Man-Lion** to disembowel the demon-king — the full cycle catalogued at [Prahlāda & Narasiṁha](../04-deep-dives/prahlada-narasimha.md) (cf. [Daśāvatāra](dashavatara.md)). The theyyam's climactic movements enact the **pillar-breaking and the slaying**. (Vishṇumūrti thoṭṭam; Bhāgavata/Viṣṇu Purāṇa narrative) [folk] [regional]
- **The local frame — Palanthāyi Kaṇṇan** — the thoṭṭam wraps Narasiṁha in a North-Malabar story: **Palanthāyi Kaṇṇan**, a low-caste youth of **Nīleshwaram (Kasaragod country)**, plucks a mango from the tree (or transgresses the pond/property) of the **Kuruvāṭṭ landlord (Kuruvāṭṭ Kurup)** and is **beaten and driven from the land**; he flees to **Mangalore**, where he becomes a fervent **devotee of Viṣṇu**. (Vishṇumūrti thoṭṭam) [folk] [oral tradition] [regional]
- **The killing and the wrath** — returning home after years, Kaṇṇan is **killed by the landlord's men** (in the common telling cut down while bathing in the pond); thereupon **calamities strike the Kuruvāṭṭ house**, and the astrologer's divination reveals the cause — **Viṣṇu's wrath for the murdered devotee**. The Kurup repents and **institutes the Vishṇumūrti theyyam**, in which the god and his martyred devotee are honoured together. (Vishṇumūrti thoṭṭam) [folk] [oral tradition]
- **Fire and pre-eminence** — Vishṇumūrti is the **pre-eminent Vaiṣṇava theyyam** of the tradition and a great **fire theyyam** — the performer repeatedly approaches/falls upon the *mēleri* embers (*agni-praveśam* register); the deity is widely enshrined in Kasaragod–Kannur kāvus, often with Rakta-Chāmuṇḍi and other companions. (Vishṇumūrti performance tradition) [folk] [regional]

## Kuṭṭichāttan — the fiery imp

- **The divine imp** — **Kuṭṭichāttan** ("little Chāttan/Śāstā") is the **trickster boy-theyyam**, a hot, mischievous spirit-deity propitiated both to **avert his pranks** (stones on the roof, fouled wells, fires) and to **turn his ferocity to the household's protection**; he is also the deity most entangled with **Malabar sorcery (mantravādam)**. (Kuṭṭichāttan tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **Born of Śiva, fostered by the illam** — in the prevailing thoṭṭam, the child is **born of Śiva and Pārvatī** (Pārvatī in a fierce/forest guise in several tellings) and given in fosterage to the Brahmin **Kāḷakāṭṭ Illam** (Kāḷakāṭṭ Nampūtiri); the boy grows wild and transgressive — profaning the household's purity, and in the famous episode **slaughtering the illam's cow and drinking its blood**. (Kuṭṭichāttan thoṭṭam) [folk] [oral tradition]
- **Killed and multiplied** — the enraged Nampūtiri **cuts the boy down**, but the imp is undying: from the spilt blood/severed body **Chāttan multiplies** — the tellings count **39, 390 or 400 Chāttans** — who torment the illam with fire and stone until they are **propitiated and enshrined**; the theyyam and the Chāttan-service shrines descend from that settlement. (Kuṭṭichāttan thoṭṭam) [folk] [oral tradition] [open-ended]
  - *Variant (identity):* Kuṭṭichāttan is variously read as a form of the **Śāstā** family (the name Chāttan < Śāstā, cf. [Ayyappa](ayyappa.md)), as an independent fierce spirit, and in central-Kerala parallel cults as **Vishṇumāya**, a Kṛṣṇa-guised sorcery deity — distinct regional theologies never merged. [folk] [disputed]
- **The performance** — a small-statured, red-and-black, quicksilver theyyam (Malayan and allied communities), whose rite may include sitting on/beating at fire; devotees engage him for protection, for finding lost things, and — ambivalently — through sorcerer-priests. (Kuṭṭichāttan performance tradition) [folk] [regional]

## Gulikan — the death-lord's shadow

- **Born from Śiva's left toe** — the Gulikan thoṭṭam and lore make **Gulikan a death-being sprung from the left great toe of Śiva**: when **Yama** himself was struck down by Śiva (the Kālāntaka rescue of Mārkaṇḍeya — cf. [forms of Śiva](shiva-avatars-and-forms.md) and [Yama](yama.md)), the god brought forth Gulikan from his toe and **deputized him over death**, making him both death-agent and — once propitiated — a mighty **guardian against untimely death and evil**. (Gulikan thoṭṭam; North Malabar tradition) [folk] [oral tradition] [regional]
  - *Variant (astrological Gulika):* Gulikan is identified with **Gulika/Māndi, the shadow-son of Śani** of Kerala astrology, whose inauspicious **Gulika-kālam** governs each day — the theyyam and the jyotiṣa figure read as one being (cf. [Navagraha](navagraha.md)). [regional] [disputed]
- **The Yama-register** — Gulikan is thus North Malabar's **Yama/Kāla aspect** (so glossed in the theyyam rosters), worshipped at kāvus and household shrines to ward off disease, spirit-affliction and death-omens; offerings and his rite carry the mortuary register (cf. [narakas & the afterlife](narakas-and-afterlife.md)). (Theyyam tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **The iconography** — the Gulikan theyyam is stark and uncanny — towering headgear, body-blackening/palm-leaf elements, and a dance of the death-lord's stalking gait; he frequently closes a kāvu's festival as its guardian. Performance details vary by community and shrine. (Gulikan performance tradition) [folk] [regional] [open-ended]

## Thee Chāmuṇḍi — the goddess who sits in fire

- **The fire-Chāmuṇḍi** — **Thee Chāmuṇḍi** (*tī*, "fire") is the most spectacular of the **fire theyyams**: after the thoṭṭam, the performer — as the goddess — **plunges again and again into the great pyre/ember-heap (mēleri)**, dozens of times through the night, caught and raked out by attendants, the goddess proving herself unburnt. (Thee Chāmuṇḍi performance tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **The Chāmuṇḍā charter** — her Purāṇic anchor is **Chāmuṇḍā**, the skeletal fury who springs from the Goddess to behead **Chaṇḍa and Muṇḍa** and drink **Raktabīja's** blood in the [Devī-Māhātmya](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md) (cf. the [Mātṛkās](../08-beings-and-bestiary/matrikas-and-64-yoginis.md)); the Kerala thoṭṭams fold this fury into the regional goddess-cycle. (Devī-Māhātmya; theyyam tradition) [folk] [regional]
  - *Variant (Dārika-cycle reading):* North-Malabar tellings attach Chāmuṇḍi to the **Dārika-vadham** sphere of [Bhadrakālī](bhadrakali-kerala-bhagavati.md) — the fire-entry read as the goddess pursuing the demon through fire / cooling her post-battle rage — and the Chāmuṇḍi family multiplies locally (**Rakta-Chāmuṇḍi, Madayil Chāmuṇḍi, Puliyūr Kāli**, the **Pulachāmuṇḍi** of the Pottan trio), rosters open-ended. [folk] [regional] [open-ended]
- **The ordeal as theology** — as with Pottan's ember-bed and Vishṇumūrti's fire-falls, the unburnt performer is the tradition's living proof that **the god, not the man, is present** — fire-ordeal as theophany. (Theyyam tradition) [folk] [scholarly]

## Theological placement (the "two clocks")

- **Scriptural/cyclic clock** — where a theyyam carries a Purāṇic charter it inherits that charter's cyclic address: Vishṇumūrti's Narasiṁha episode belongs to the **Satya (Kṛta) Yuga** slaying of Hiraṇyakaśipu ([Prahlāda & Narasiṁha](../04-deep-dives/prahlada-narasimha.md)); Pottan, Kuṭṭichāttan and Gulikan attach to the **timeless Śaiva manifestation register** (no fixed yuga); Thee Chāmuṇḍi to the recurring Devī-battles. The hero and heroine theyyams (Kathivanūr Vīran, Muchilōṭṭu Bhagavathi) are **Kali-age apotheoses** by the tradition's own reckoning — mortals of remembered generations made gods. [folk] [regional]
- **Academic/historical clock** — the theyyam institution itself — kāvu, thoṭṭam, hereditary Dalit performing rights, the caste-inverting possession rite — is a **kali-historical North-Malabar phenomenon**: a pre-Brahmanical Dravidian substrate of ancestor/hero/mother-goddess worship, syncretized with Purāṇic deities and crystallized under the medieval Kōlattunāḍu (Chirakkal) social order; the hero-legends (Kathivanūr Vīran's Kodagu-border war; Vishṇumūrti's landlord-murder frame) reflect **late-medieval/early-modern** agrarian Malabar, and the Pottan–Śaṅkara dialogue presupposes the **c. 8th-century CE** Śaṅkara of history while its thoṭṭam is far later. Precise dates for individual thoṭṭams are unrecorded. [scholarly] [regional]
