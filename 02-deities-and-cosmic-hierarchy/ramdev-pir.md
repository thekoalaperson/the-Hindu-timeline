# Ramdev Pir — Rāmdevjī / Rāmāpīr, the Rider-Saint of Runicha

> Deities-and-hierarchy node. **Ramdev Pir** (also **Rāmdevjī**, **Rāmāpīr**, **Bābā Rāmdev**, Muslim **Rāmsā Pīr / Rām Shāh Pīr**) is the great folk deity of western Rajasthan, Gujarat, Sindh and Kutch — a **Tanwar (Tomar) Rajput of Pokaran** venerated as an **avatāra of Kṛṣṇa/Dvārakānātha**, champion of the untouchable castes, and one of Marwar's **pañc pīr**, whose living-samādhi shrine at **Ramdevra (Runicha)** draws Hindus and Muslims alike. ## 1. Two Clocks — Who Ramdev Is

Like [Gugga](gugga-gogaji.md) and Pābūjī, Ramdev is a deified Rajput hero with a thin historical kernel and a thick devotional overlay; unlike them, his cult's central theological claim is a full **Vaiṣṇava avatāra identity**.

- **Traditional clock** — hagiography places his birth on **Bhādrapada śukla dvitīyā (the "Bābā rī Bīj")** of **V.S. 1409 (c. 1352 CE)** and his living samādhi on **Bhādrapada śukla ekādaśī of V.S. 1442 (c. 1385 CE)**, at Runicha; other reckonings shift the dates into the **15th century CE**. The dates are devotional-bardic, not documentary. [folk] [disputed]
- **Historical clock** — modern scholarship treats Ramdev as a **14th–15th-century CE folk saint/chieftain of the Pokaran–Runicha tract** (Jaisalmer district, Thar desert) about whom no contemporary documents survive; everything known comes from later hagiography, bhajan corpora and shrine tradition. [scholarly]
- **Scriptural clock** — Ramdev has **no Vedic or Purāṇic footprint**; the cult itself supplies the cosmic frame by declaring him **Kṛṣṇa of Dvārakā returned in the Kali Yuga**, thus splicing a Dvāpara-yuga deity onto a Kali-yuga hero (compare the same folk mechanism in [Barbarīka/Khāṭū Śyām](../04-deep-dives/characters/barbarika-khatu-shyam.md) and in Pābūjī-as-Lakṣmaṇa — see [regional-and-oral-epics.md](../90-literature-corpus/regional-and-oral-epics.md)). [folk]
- **What he governs** — healing (especially of the sick, blind, lepers and the possessed), rescue of the poor and low-caste, fulfillment of vows (*mannat*), and the social gospel of **caste-equality and Hindu–Muslim amity**; his devotees span all castes but he is above all the beloved deity of the **Meghwal and other Dalit communities** of the desert belt. [folk] [regional]

## 2. Names and Epithets (catalog)

- **Rāmdevjī / Bābā Rāmdev** — the everyday Rajasthani devotional name. [regional]
- **Rāmāpīr / Rāmāpir** — the dominant form in **Gujarat, Kutch and Sindh**, where his cult is among the largest of all folk cults. [regional]
- **Rāmsā Pīr / Rām Shāh Pīr** — "Rāma the lord-saint," the title by which **Muslim devotees** venerate him; in the hagiography it is bestowed by the five pīrs of Mecca themselves (§4). [folk] [regional]
- **Runicha rā dhaṇī / Runiche rā Rājā** — "Lord of Runicha," after his seat. [regional]
- **Tanwar/Tomar Rajput** — his clan identity; bardic genealogy carries the Tanwars back to the Tomars of Delhi and ultimately to the Lunar line of the Pāṇḍavas. [folk] [disputed]

## 3. The Birth Cycle — Ajmal's Pilgrimage and the Avatāra Claim

- **The childless king of Pokaran** — **Ajmāljī Tanwar (Ajmal / Ajaisī)**, ruler of Pokaran, and his queen **Maiṇāde (Minal De)** are childless and taunted for it; Ajmal resolves to petition **Kṛṣṇa-Dvārakānātha** himself and journeys to **[Dwarka](../06-sacred-geography/dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md)**. (Ramdev hagiographic tradition / bhajan corpus) [folk]
- **The plunge into the sea** — receiving no answer from the temple image, Ajmal **leaps into the ocean at Dwarka** (in other tellings, angrily strikes or berates the image); Kṛṣṇa appears to him beneath the waters (or in vision), is pleased by his desperate faith, and **promises to be born in his house** — himself as a younger son, preceded by an elder. (Ramdev hagiographic tradition) [folk]
  - *Variant (Balarāma too):* many tellings make the elder son **Vīramdev an incarnation of Balarāma**, so that both brothers of Dvārakā return together to the desert. [folk]
- **The cradle-birth at Undu-Kashmir** — Ramdev **appears miraculously in the cradle** in the palace at **Undu-Kashmir (Uṇḍū-Kāsmīr, Barmer district)** — in the most common telling the household finds the newborn already lying in the rocking cradle, the lamp self-lit and sea-water in the house-vessel as Dwarka's sign — rather than by ordinary birth. Dated Bhādrapada śukla dvitīyā, V.S. 1409. (Ramdev hagiographic tradition) [folk]
- **Kṛṣṇa-avatāra doctrine** — the cult therefore worships Ramdev not as a deified man but as **Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa's own descent for the Kali age**, sent to succor the poor and the outcaste; devotional verse calls him the Kali-yuga avatāra outright. This is a **folk avatāra-claim outside the classical [Daśāvatāra](dashavatara.md) lists**, exactly parallel to the grafting documented for Pābūjī and Devnārāyaṇ. (Ramdev bhajan corpus) [folk]; classification [scholarly]

## 4. The Twenty-Four Parachas (miracle-proofs)

Ramdev's miracles are counted in tradition as the **caubīs paracā** ("twenty-four proofs/miracles"; *parcā/parachā* = the demonstrated proof of divine power), sung serially in his bhajans and *jammā* night-vigils. The list's contents and order vary by singer and region; the fixed, universally sung episodes are below. [folk] [oral tradition]

- **The cradle-rocking infant** — the newborn Ramdev rocks his own cradle, revealing his divinity to Maiṇāde — commonly counted the first parcha. (Ramdev hagiographic tradition) [folk]
- **The flying cloth horse** — a tailor (in some tellings on the queen's commission) stitches the child a **toy horse of cloth and rags**; Ramdev mounts it and it **flies into the sky**. When the frightened tailor is accused of witchcraft (or of having stolen the fine cloth), Ramdev returns and vindicates him. **The cloth/wooden toy horse remains the signature votive offering** at every Ramdev shrine to this day. (Ramdev hagiographic tradition) [folk]
- **The slaying of Bhairava** — Ramdev destroys (in gentler tellings, subdues and banishes) the demon **Bhairav (Bhairava rākṣasa)** who had been devouring the people around Pokaran, freeing the tract — his chief warrior-exploit. (Ramdev hagiographic tradition) [folk]
- **The five pīrs of Mecca and the bowls** — hearing of the desert saint's fame, **five pīrs come from Mecca to test him**. Ramdev serves them food; they demur, saying they eat only from their own bowls, left behind in Mecca. Ramdev smiles and **the bowls come flying from Mecca to their hands**. Convinced, the pīrs bow and hail him **"Rāmsā Pīr" / "Rām Shāh Pīr"** — the in-narrative charter of his Muslim veneration and the most famous parcha of all. (Ramdev hagiographic tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **Healing miracles** — the cycle credits him with **restoring sight to the blind, curing lepers and the sick, and reviving the dead**; individual bhajan lineages attach names and villages to these episodes, which vary too widely to fix a single canonical list. (Ramdev bhajan corpus) [folk] [oral tradition]

## 5. Dali Bai — the Dalit Devotee-Sister

- **The foundling of the Meghwal** — **Ḍālī Bāī (Dali Bai / Dalibai)**, a girl of the **Meghwal (Dalit/"untouchable") community**, is found by Ramdev — in the common telling as an abandoned infant beneath a tree (variants: in a box/trunk, or brought to him as a devotee-child) — and is **adopted by him as his sister** and raised in the household. (Ramdev hagiographic tradition) [folk]
- **Foremost disciple** — Dali Bai becomes his **first and foremost devotee**, the living demonstration of his teaching that caste is no bar to God; her centrality makes the cult a standing **anti-untouchability charter**, and she is worshipped in her own right. [folk] [regional]
- **Her samādhi precedes his** — Dali Bai takes **samādhi shortly before Ramdev himself**, and her tomb-shrine stands beside his at Ramdevra; **pilgrims by custom bow at Dali Bai's samādhi before Ramdev's**. (Ramdevra sthala tradition) [regional]
- **Dali Bai's bangle (kaṅgan)** — near her samādhi is the stone ring called **Ḍālī Bāī kā kaṅgan**, through which pilgrims pass their bodies as an act of purification/vow — a signature rite of the Ramdevra pilgrimage. (Ramdevra sthala tradition) [folk] [regional]

## 6. Samādhi at Ramdevra and the Bhādwā Melā

- **The living samādhi at Runicha** — Ramdev **takes jīvit samādhi (entombment while alive, in yogic trance)** at **Runicha — thereafter Rāmdevrā**, ~12 km from Pokaran (Jaisalmer district) — traditionally on **Bhādrapada śukla ekādaśī, V.S. 1442 (c. 1385 CE)**, aged thirty-three, after announcing his departure to his followers. His samādhi, not a cremation-memorial, is the cult's living center. (Ramdevra sthala tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **The temple** — successive structures rose over the samādhi; the **present temple was built in 1931 CE under Maharaja Ganga Singh of Bikaner**. The shrine's tomb-form reads simultaneously as Hindu samādhi-mandir and Muslim dargāh. (Ramdevra sthala tradition) [regional]; 1931 date [scholarly]
- **The Bhādwā melā** — the great annual **fair at Ramdevra runs from Bhādrapada śukla dvitīyā (Bābā rī Bīj, his birthday) to the ekādaśī (his samādhi day)**, in the monsoon month of Bhādwā (c. August–September); **lakhs of pilgrims — Hindu, Muslim and Dalit-caste congregations foremost — walk to Runicha on foot** from Rajasthan, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh and beyond, many carrying the deity's flag. (Ramdevra melā tradition) [regional]
- **Cult emblems** — the **pañcaraṅgī nejā** (five-colored pennant) hoisted over the samādhi and carried by foot-pilgrims; the **pagliyā** (carved footprint-pair), his chief aniconic emblem in wayside shrines and household worship; the **cloth toy horse** votive (§4); and the equestrian icon of Ramdev as a **rider on his white-blue horse with lance and banner**. (Ramdevra tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **The jammā and the Kāmaḍ bards** — his liturgy is the **jammā / jāgraṇ** (all-night vigil) of bhajans sung by the **Kāmaḍ (Kamad/Kamadiya) community** to the **tandūrā** (five-stringed drone-lute); the **Terah Tāli** dance, performed with thirteen manjīrā cymbals tied to the body, is offered to him by Kāmaḍ women. This bardic corpus is the cult's "scripture" — see the Rajasthani oral-epic complex in [regional-and-oral-epics.md](../90-literature-corpus/regional-and-oral-epics.md). [folk] [oral tradition]

## 7. Rāmsā Pīr — Shared Hindu–Muslim Veneration

- **One tomb, two idioms** — Hindus worship Ramdev as Kṛṣṇa's avatāra at a samādhi; Muslims of Rajasthan, Sindh and Kutch revere him as the **pīr Rām Shāh**, his shrine functioning as a dargāh; the Mecca-bowls parcha (§4) writes this dual identity **into the myth itself**, exactly as Gugga's earth-descent episode does for [his](gugga-gogaji.md) cult. [folk] [regional]; comparison [scholarly]
- **Sindh and the diaspora** — as **Rāmāpīr** he is a principal deity of **Sindhi Hindus**, with the great **Rāmāpīr melā at Tando Allahyar (Sindh, Pakistan)** counted among the largest Hindu festivals in Pakistan; his cult travelled with Sindhi and Kutchi migrants worldwide (compare the parallel Sindhi river-saint [Jhulelal](jhulelal.md)). [regional]
- **The Ismaili hypothesis** — the scholar **Dominique-Sila Khan** (*Conversions and Shifting Identities: Ramdev Pir and the Ismailis in Rajasthan*, 1997) argued that the Ramdev cult preserves traces of **Nizārī Ismāʿīlī (Satpanth) missionary religion** in medieval Rajasthan — reading "Rāmsā Pīr," the Kāmaḍiyā panth's esoteric rites and the *jammā* liturgy as a later-Hinduized Satpanth stratum. The thesis is influential but contested, and is rejected by the mainstream devotional tradition. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **A nirguṇa-adjacent social gospel** — verse attributed to Ramdev and his tradition preaches against untouchability and empty ritualism, aligning him with the north-Indian sant milieu of [Kabīr](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/kabir.md) and the devotional Rajasthan of [Mīrābāī](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/mirabai.md); tradition also gives him a yogī initiation from the guru **Bālīnāth**, tying the cult to the [Nāth](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/nath-sampradaya-and-the-siddhas.md) stratum that also patronizes Gugga. [folk]; alignment [scholarly]

## 8. Among the Pañc Pīr of Marwar

- **The five pīrs** — Marwari tradition groups its five great protector hero-saints as the **pañc pīr**, fixed in the famous couplet: **"Pābū, Harbhū, Rāmde, Māṅgaḷiyā Mehā — pāñcū pīr padhārjyo, Gogājī jehā"** — *Pābūjī, Harbhūjī, Rāmdevjī, Mehājī Māṅgaḷiyā, and Gogājī*: come, all five pīrs. Ramdev is the youngest and today by far the most widely worshipped of the five. (Marwari bardic tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **The peer-cults** — [Gugga/Gogājī](gugga-gogaji.md) has his own node here; Pābūjī's epic and the deified-hero mechanism common to the whole set are treated in [regional-and-oral-epics.md](../90-literature-corpus/regional-and-oral-epics.md); Harbhūjī (of Bengti) and Mehājī Māṅgaḷiyā remain roster-level figures. [regional]
- **Family and succession** — tradition gives Ramdev a wife, **Netalde (Ṇetal De), daughter of the Soḍhā ruler of Amarkot (Umerkot, Sindh)**, and sons from whom the **Tanwar hereditary priests of the Ramdevra temple** claim descent. (Ramdevra sthala tradition) [folk] [regional]
