# Reṇukā / Yellammā / Ekvīrā — the Pan-Deccan Mother-Goddess of Saundatti

> Deities-and-hierarchy node. **Reṇukā–Yellammā–Ekvīrā** is the first-tier mother-goddess and **kuladevī** of millions across **Karnataka and Maharashtra** — the chaste wife of **[Jamadagni](saptarishi.md)** and mother of **[Paraśurāma](dashavatara.md)** in the Purāṇic substrate, and, in the living cult, the fierce-and-maternal **grāmadevatā** of the great **Saundatti** hill. ## Two clocks — who Reṇukā-Yellammā is

- **Two figures, one node** — the **scriptural Reṇukā** (Treta-yuga *as myth*, the age of Paraśurāma — see **[Daśāvatāra](dashavatara.md)**) and the **worshipped Yellammā** (a **kali-historical** living temple cult attested in recent centuries) are deliberately kept on separate clocks; the Purāṇic tale is the *charter*, the Saundatti shrine is the *institution* (Source: house "two-clocks" convention; *grāmadevatā* studies [scholarly]).
- **The names** — **Reṇukā** (Sanskrit, the brāhmaṇ wife), **Yellammā / Ellamma** (Kannada–Marathi, "mother of all," *ellā-ammā*), **Ekvīrā / Ekavīrā** ("the sole heroine," the Maharashtrian kuladevī form), and the folk equation **= Māriyammaṉ = Padmākṣī = Aai** — one pan-Deccan mother under local names (Source: Deccan folk tradition [folk]).
- **Function** — **kuladevī** (lineage-goddess) of vast Maharashtrian and Karnatak communities; goddess of **fertility, chastity-power, disease-aversion (especially pox), and the marginalized**; both the giver and the curer of affliction, like her southern twin **[Māriyammaṉ](mariamman.md)** (Source: *grāmadevatā* studies [scholarly]).

## The Purāṇic substrate — Reṇukā, Jamadagni, Paraśurāma

- **Reṇukā the chaste wife** — daughter of king Reṇu/Prasenajit, wife of the irascible sage **[Jamadagni](saptarishi.md)**, mother of five sons, the youngest **[Paraśurāma](dashavatara.md)**; her chastity was so perfect that each day she **moulded a fresh unbaked pot of wet sand/clay at the river** and carried water in it, the vessel holding together by the force of her faith alone, often balanced atop a live coiled cobra as a head-ring (Source: *Mahābhārata* 3.116 (Vana-parvan); *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*; South Indian folk Māhātmyas).
- **The lapse — loss of chastity by thought** — fetching water, she glimpsed the **gandharva Citraratha** (in variants a king, the gandharva Citrasena, sporting with celestial women) and for a single instant felt admiration/desire; that momentary wavering of *mind alone* broke her power — the unbaked pot **dissolved in her hands** and she returned empty and afraid (Source: *Mahābhārata* 3.116; folk Reṇukā Māhātmya [folk]).
- **The order to behead her** — perceiving her lapse by tapas-sight, Jamadagni blazed with rage and commanded each son in turn to **slay their mother**; the elder four refused and were cursed (turned to stone / made witless) until only Paraśurāma remained (Source: *Mahābhārata* 3.116; *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*).
- **The beheading** — in absolute filial obedience **Paraśurāma took up the axe and struck off Reṇukā's head** at one stroke (Source: *Mahābhārata* 3.116).
- **The reviving boon** — pleased by his obedience, Jamadagni offered boons; the son asked that his **mother be restored to life with no memory of her death** and his **cursed brothers revived** — a refusal-proof reward that undid the killing (Source: *Mahābhārata* 3.116; *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*).

> **Two clocks.** The episode is *narrated* in Treta-yuga (Paraśurāma's age); but the **goddess Yellammā** built upon it is a **kali-historical** cult — her shrines, festivals and dedications are documented in recent centuries, not in the Vedas or core Purāṇas.

## The head-and-body etiology — the split goddess

The Mahābhārata's clean revival is, in the Deccan folk layer, complicated into the foundational **split-goddess** myth that explains why one goddess is served across the entire caste spectrum.

- **The swapped head** — Paraśurāma rushes the severed **head** to rejoin the body, but in the confusion **Reṇukā's brāhmaṇ head is joined to the body of a low-caste/outcaste (Mātaṅgī / Cāṇḍāla) woman** beheaded alongside her — and the outcaste head to Reṇukā's body (Source: South Indian folk variants; oral *Yellamma* traditions [folk]).
  - *Variant (high head / low body):* the **brāhmaṇ-headed, outcaste-bodied** figure is worshipped by upper castes as **Reṇukā**, the goddess of the high-caste head on the low-caste body (Source: Deccan folk tradition [folk]).
  - *Variant (low head / high body):* the **outcaste-headed, brāhmaṇ-bodied** figure becomes the fierce ground-level **Yellammā / Māriyammaṉ** head-icon — the goddess served with blood-offering by the marginalized (Source: South Indian folk variants [folk/disputed]).
  - *Variant (two goddesses):* the swap yields **two** sisters/goddesses — a "pure" temple Reṇukā and an "impure" village Yellammā/Māri — rather than one two-natured deity ([disputed]).
- **The Mātaṅgī link** — because the joined body is that of an outcaste woman, Reṇukā-Yellammā is folk-linked to **[Mātaṅgī](dasha-mahavidya.md)**, the Daśa-Mahāvidyā "outcaste goddess" / Tantric Sarasvatī worshipped through the impure and marginal — the same purity-inversion logic (Source: folk equation built on *Mātaṅgī Tantra* [folk/disputed]).
- **The Māriyammaṉ overlap** — the severed-and-rejoined fierce **head-icon** is precisely the ground-level face-form of the Tamil pox-mother; across the Deccan the Tamil **[Māriyammaṉ](mariamman.md)** and Yellammā are repeatedly **identified as one goddess** (Source: South Indian folk tradition [folk]).

## The living cult — Saundatti-Yellamma

- **The hill shrine** — the cult's great center is the hill temple of **Yellammā / Ellamma at Saundatti (Saundatti-Yellamma, Belagavi/Belgaum district, Karnataka)**, atop **Yellamma Gudda**; one of the most-visited goddess shrines of the Deccan (Source: Saundatti sthala tradition [regional]).
- **The Yellamma jātre / jatara** — the annual festival, peaking at the **Bhārata Hunnime / Māgha full-moon** (and other full-moon *hunnime*), draws enormous pilgrim crowds bathing in the **Jogul-bhāvi** tank; vow-takers carry **water-pots, neem, and turmeric (bhandāra)**, the goddess's emblems (Source: Saundatti festival ethnography [regional]).
- **The jogti / jogappa** — devotees dedicated to Yellammā who renounce ordinary householder life: **jogti** (women) and **jogappa / jogta** (men, often living and dressing as women); they beg in the goddess's name, wear her **bhandāra**, and carry the **jaga** (sacred basket) — a contested, marginalized order today (Source: jogti/jogappa ethnography [regional/disputed]).
- **The devadasi dedication** — historically girls were **dedicated to Yellammā as devadasis (basavi / muraḷi)** at Saundatti, a practice **legally banned** (Karnataka Devadasis Act 1982; later Maharashtra legislation) yet ethnographically persistent and bound up with caste, poverty and the goddess's cult — a deeply **contested** dimension of the living tradition (Source: devadasi ethnography; reform/legal record [disputed]).

## Ekvīrā — the Maharashtrian kuladevī

- **Ekvīrā Āī of Karla** — the great Maharashtrian seat is the **Ekvīrā temple beside the Karla (Karli) Buddhist caves**, near Lonavala; here Reṇukā is worshipped as **Ekvīrā / Ekavīrā ("the sole heroine")**, distinct in name yet identified with the Saundatti mother (Source: Karla Ekvīrā sthala tradition [regional]).
- **Kuladevī of the CKP and Agri-Koli** — Ekvīrā is the **kuladevī of the Candraseniya Kāyastha Prabhu (CKP)** community and the patron goddess of the coastal **Agri and Koli** (fisherfolk) of the Konkan, who throng Karla at her annual festival (Source: community kuladevī tradition [regional]).
- **The Mahar tradition** — Yellammā/Reṇukā also figures as a lineage-goddess in **Mahar** and other Maharashtrian communities, part of the broad lower-caste devotional base that the head-and-body etiology accounts for (Source: Deccan folk tradition [folk]).
- **Māhur (Māhūr) Reṇukā** — among the **Sāḍe-tīn Śakti-pīṭhas (three-and-a-half power-seats) of Maharashtra**, the **Reṇukā temple at Māhūr (Nanded district)** is a principal seat where she is venerated directly as Reṇukā-Mātā, kuladevī of countless Marathi families (Source: Maharashtrian Śakti-pīṭha tradition [regional]).

## Regional folk variants and the pox overlap

- *Variant (one mother, many names):* **Reṇukā = Yellammā = Ekvīrā = Māriyammaṉ = Padmākṣī = Aai** — the single pan-Deccan mother under local tongues (Source: Deccan folk tradition [folk]).
- *Variant (smallpox-goddess):* in South India Yellammā overlaps the **smallpox / epidemic mother**, served like **[Māriyammaṉ](mariamman.md)** in the South and **[Śītalā](shitala.md)** in the North under the same **heat-vs-cooling** physics — neem, turmeric, water-pots cooling the "heated" goddess (Source: comparative *grāmadevatā* studies [scholarly]).
- *Variant (Sanskritization):* reform temples reframe Yellammā as **Pārvatī / Durgā / Ādi-Parāśakti** and replace **blood-sacrifice (bali)** with Agamic vegetarian worship — a documented, contested Sanskritization of a folk goddess (Source: *grāmadevatā* studies [scholarly/disputed]).
