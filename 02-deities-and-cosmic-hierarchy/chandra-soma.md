# Chandra (Soma)

> Cosmic address: the Moon-god — luminary of the night, lord of plants, waters, the mind (*manas*) and the tides; bearer of the immortal nectar; husband of the 27 lunar mansions; and the divine founder of the lunar royal line. Chandra is one of the [Navagraha](navagraha.md), counted among the [Devas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md), and the apical ancestor of the [Chandravaṃśa lunar dynasty](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md). Parent: [02 — Deities and Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md). Master index: [the Hindu Timeline](../README.md).

The Moon is worshipped on **two registers at once** (the project's "two clocks"): as the Vedic **Soma** — a pressed plant-juice, an entheogenic ritual drink, and a personified god of the sacrifice — and as the later Puranic **Chandra**, a personal Moon-deity with a domestic mythology of jealous wives, a consumptive curse, an adulterous abduction, and a royal dynasty. The two are the same name historically: "Soma" began as the drink/plant and was *transferred* to the moon. This file gathers both the Vedic Soma layer and the full Puranic Chandra cycle. Cross-clock note: the nakshatra-wife myth literally encodes the **synodic/sidereal lunar month** in narrative form. See [Nakshatras](../00-time-and-cosmology/nakshatras.md) and [Hindu Calendar & Panchanga](../00-time-and-cosmology/hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md).

## Names, domains, and identity

- **Soma** — the oldest name; the deified ritual drink/plant and the god who *is* that drink. Etymon *su-* "to press/extract." In the Ṛg Veda primarily the juice; in post-Vedic texts the proper name of the Moon-god (Ṛg Veda Maṇḍala 9; Wilkins). [scholarly]
- **Chandra / Candra** — "the shining one, bright"; the dominant epic-Puranic name for the personal Moon-god (Rāmāyaṇa; Mahābhārata; Purāṇas).
- **Chandramas / Candramas** — "moon-measurer," the disk itself as time-measurer; the form that links the deity to the month (*māsa*). [scholarly] the Vedic transition-term where Soma starts meaning the moon.
- **Common epithets** — **Indu** ("bright drop," of the pressed juice), **Śaśin / Śaśāṅka** ("hare-marked," the lunar markings seen as a hare), **Niśākara / Niśāpati** ("night-maker / lord of night"), **Kalānidhi** ("treasury of the digits"), **Oṣadhīśa / Oṣadhipati** ("lord of herbs/plants"), **Nakṣatrapati / Nakṣatranātha** ("lord of the nakṣatras"), **Atrisuta / Ātreya** ("son of Atri"), **Kumuda-bāndhava / Kairava** ("friend of the night-lotus"), **Mṛgāṅka** ("deer/hare-marked"), **Sudhākara / Sudhāṃśu** ("nectar-rayed"), **Dvijarāja** ("king of the twice-born / of the stars/brahmins").
- **Cosmological functions** — lord of **plants and herbs** (their sap is "soma"), of the **waters and tides**, of **fertility and procreation**, of the **mind (*manas*)** in jyotiṣa, and the reservoir into which the **Pitṛs (ancestors) and gods drink the nectar** that causes the waning (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa; Purāṇas).
- **Iconography** — fair/white-complexioned, two- or four-armed, holding a **club (gadā)** and **lotus**, riding a **chariot drawn by ten white horses** (or an **antelope/deer**), crowned by the crescent; cool, watery, benefic. As a [graha](navagraha.md): white, *sattvic*, significator of mind, mother, emotions; exalted in Taurus (Rohiṇī's sign), debilitated in Scorpio. See [Jyotiṣa & the Zodiac](../00-time-and-cosmology/jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md).

## Soma the drink and the plant (the Vedic layer)

- **Soma Pavamāna — Maṇḍala 9** — the entire **ninth maṇḍala of the Ṛg Veda (114 hymns)** is dedicated to **Soma Pavamāna**, the "self-purifying / flowing" Soma, hymns sung as the juice is pressed and strained through wool. Soma is the most-addressed deity after Indra and Agni (Ṛg Veda 9). [scholarly]
- **The drink of immortality** — pressed soma was an exhilarating, possibly entheogenic ritual juice that "makes the gods immortal"; "we have drunk soma, we have become immortal" (Ṛg Veda 8.48.3). Indra drinks vast quantities of soma before slaying **Vṛtra**. Soma here is the prototype of **amṛta**.
- **Identity of the plant [disputed]** — the botanical original is unknown and fiercely debated: candidates include **Ephedra**, the **fly-agaric mushroom (*Amanita muscaria*)** (Wasson), **Syrian rue (*Peganum harmala*)**, **Sarcostemma/Cynanchum** creepers, and others. Indo-Iranian cognate: the Avestan **Haoma**. [scholarly] [flag: open question]
- **Soma born of the churning** — *Variant (Puranic):* in the **Samudra Manthana** (churning of the milk-ocean), the Moon (Soma/Chandra) emerges among the fourteen *ratnas* and is taken by **Śiva** onto his head; the same churning yields **amṛta**, fusing the soma-as-nectar and soma-as-moon ideas (Bhāgavata, Viṣṇu, Matsya Purāṇas). See [Cosmogony / Creation Accounts](../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md).
- **The transfer from drink to moon [scholarly]** — by the later Vedic hymns and the Brāhmaṇas the *name* Soma had migrated to the moon; one rationale: the moon is the celestial **cup of soma/amṛta** that the gods and ancestors drain monthly (hence waning) and that refills (waxing). "How and why this change took place is not known" (Wilkins). By the Rāmāyaṇa, Mahābhārata and Purāṇas, Soma = Chandra unambiguously.

## Birth of Chandra — competing accounts

- **Son of Atri and Anasūyā (mainstream Puranic)** — Chandra is born to the Saptarṣi **Atri** and his chaste wife **Anasūyā**, alongside (in the fullest version) **Dattātreya** and **Durvāsā**. Chandra is born from Atri's penetrating, watery **gaze/tears of austerity** (*soma* welling from his eye) and placed in the sky by Brahmā (Viṣṇu, Bhāgavata, Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇas). See [Saptarṣi](saptarishi.md).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata 4 — the boon):* Atri and Anasūyā do tapas on Ṛkṣa mountain; the **Trimūrti** appear and are born as their sons — **Chandra as an *aṃśa* (portion) of Brahmā**, **Dattātreya of Viṣṇu**, **Durvāsā of Śiva**. Devī Bhāgavata names Chandra the avatāra of Brahmā.
  - *Variant (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa — the *kāma* energy):* Brahmā, smitten with Sarasvatī, blames **Kāma** and curses him to be burned by Śiva; the residual desire passes from Brahmā → Atri → Anasūyā (who cannot bear it) → back to Atri, and finally **issues from Atri's eye as Chandra**. [late text]
  - *Variant (other fathers):* some texts make Chandra the son of **Dharma**, or of one **Prabhākara**; the **churning of the ocean** gives an entirely impersonal birth. [flag: birth roster variant]

## The 27 nakṣatra-wives and the Rohiṇī favouritism

- **Marriage to Dakṣa's 27 daughters** — Chandra weds the **twenty-seven daughters of Dakṣa Prajāpati**, who personify the **27 nakṣatras** (lunar mansions): Aśvinī, Bharaṇī, Kṛttikā, **Rohiṇī**, Mṛgaśīrā, Ārdrā, Punarvasu, Puṣya, Āśleṣā, Maghā, Pūrvaphalgunī, Uttaraphalgunī, Hasta, Citrā, Svātī, Viśākhā, Anurādhā, Jyeṣṭhā, Mūla, Pūrvāṣāḍhā, Uttarāṣāḍhā, Śravaṇa, Dhaniṣṭhā, Śatabhiṣā, Pūrvabhādrapadā, Uttarabhādrapadā, Revatī. As **Nakṣatrapati**, the Moon "visits" one wife per night — the calendrical core of the myth. See [Nakshatras](../00-time-and-cosmology/nakshatras.md).
- **The favouring of Rohiṇī** — Chandra dotes on **Rohiṇī** (the brightest, the fourth wife) and neglects the other 26. They complain repeatedly to their father **Dakṣa**, who warns Chandra to treat all wives equally; Chandra ignores him (Viṣṇu, Bhāgavata, Skanda Purāṇas). Astronomical encoding: the moon is "exalted" in Rohiṇī (Taurus / Aldebaran), where it shines brightest — hence the favouritism. [scholarly]

## Dakṣa's curse and the waxing & waning of the Moon

- **The curse of consumption** — enraged by Chandra's neglect of his daughters, **Dakṣa curses him with *kṣaya-roga*** ("wasting disease," consumption/tuberculosis): Chandra begins to dwindle, his light and vitality decaying day by day (Viṣṇu, Bhāgavata, Skanda Purāṇas). The cosmos suffers as the plants and waters, dependent on the Moon, wither.
- **A curse that cannot be revoked, only modulated** — Dakṣa's curse is irreversible; the resolution is to make it **periodic** rather than total. This is the etiological myth of the **lunar phases**: the Moon wanes (the curse acting) then waxes (recovery), forever.
  - *Variant (Śiva's grace — Somanātha):* the dying Chandra worships **Śiva** at **Prabhāsa** on the Sarasvatī coast; Śiva places the crescent on his own head and grants that Chandra will **wane for the dark fortnight (kṛṣṇa pakṣa) and wax for the bright fortnight (śukla pakṣa)**, never fully dying. This founds the **Somanātha jyotirliṅga** in Gujarat ("Lord of Soma"); Chandra's restoration is its origin legend. See [Sacred Geography](../06-sacred-geography/README.md) and [Shiva Myth Cycle](shiva-myth-cycle.md).
  - *Variant (the Pitṛs and gods drink him — Brāhmaṇa rationale):* the waning is explained physiologically as the **gods and ancestors drinking the soma/amṛta stored in the moon**, draining it to one digit; it then refills (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa). The fifteenth waning digit feeds the Pitṛs, linking the dark moon (**Amāvasyā**) to ancestral rites. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (compromise on the wives):* in some tellings Dakṣa relents on condition that Chandra spend **equal time with all 27 wives** — the moon's monthly transit through every nakṣatra — making the dynastic harmony and the calendar one and the same. [folk]
- **The hare/deer in the moon (*śaśāṅka*)** — the lunar markings are read as a hare (*śaśa*) or deer; folk etiologies tie the spots to the disease-scarred body of the cursed Moon, or to a self-sacrificing hare (a Jātaka/folk motif). [folk]
- **Monday — Somavāra** — the Moon's weekday is **Monday (Somavāra / Chandravāra)**, observed with fasts to propitiate Chandra/Śiva; the Moon governs the *tithis* (lunar days) and the entire **lunar month**. See [Hindu Calendar & Panchanga](../00-time-and-cosmology/hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md).

## The abduction of Tārā, the Tārakāmaya War, and the birth of Budha

- **Soma seizes Tārā** — after the arrogance bred by performing the **Rājasūya sacrifice**, Chandra abducts **Tārā ("Star"), wife of Bṛhaspati** (Jupiter, *guru* of the gods), and refuses every plea to return her (Viṣṇu Purāṇa; Bhāgavata 9.14). This is the great scandal of the Moon-god.
- **The Tārakāmaya War ("the war over Tārā / Tāraka")** — the dispute escalates into a cosmic war:
  - **Bṛhaspati's side** — the gods, **Indra**, and notably **Rudra/Śiva** (Bṛhaspati's friend, who takes up his bow **Ajagava**).
  - **Chandra's side** — **Śukra (Venus)**, *guru* of the asuras, and the **Asuras/Daityas** (Śukra's old enmity with Bṛhaspati pulls him to Soma's faction).
  - The war threatens universal destruction. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.6; Bhāgavata.)
- **Brahmā's intervention** — **Brahmā** mediates and compels Chandra to **release Tārā** to Bṛhaspati, ending the war.
- **The disputed pregnancy and birth of Budha** — Tārā returns **pregnant**; both Bṛhaspati and Chandra claim the child. Pressed (and in the Bhāgavata, shamed before the assembly), **Tārā declares Chandra the father**. The radiant son is **Budha (Mercury)** — "the wise one." Bṛhaspati raises/accepts him in some versions; the paternity stain follows the child. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa; Bhāgavata 9.14.) See [Navagraha § Budha](navagraha.md).
  - *Variant (revisionist/comparative):* some modern writers read the Bṛhaspati–Tārā–Chandra triangle as a parallel to the Greek Helen/Trojan-War motif. [disputed] [scholarly]

## The Lunar Dynasty (Chandravaṃśa / Somavaṃśa)

- **The founding genealogy** — the lunar royal line descends: **Brahmā → Atri → Chandra (Soma) → Budha → Purūravas** (first earthly king). Chandra is thus the divine ancestor of one of the two great kṣatriya houses (the other being the [Sūryavaṃśa](surya-and-his-family.md)). (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4; Mahābhārata Ādi Parva.) Full treatment: [Sūryavaṃśa & Chandravaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
- **Budha weds Ilā** — Budha marries **Ilā/Iḍā**, who originated as **Sudyumna**, the son of [Vaivasvata Manu](../01-manvantaras/README.md) transformed into a woman by a curse (Śiva's grove / sage's curse) and alternating between sexes. Their son **Purūravas** begins the **Aila** (descendants of Ilā) branch — the human lunar kings. [scholarly] this is the literal genealogical junction between the solar Manu-line and the lunar line.
- **The great houses of the line** — the Chandravaṃśa flowers into the **Yadus, Pūrus, Kurus and Pāñcālas**: it includes **Yayāti** and his sons (Yadu → the **Yādavas/Vṛṣṇis** and so **Kṛṣṇa**; Pūru → the **Kauravas and Pāṇḍavas** of the Mahābhārata). The Moon-god is therefore the ultimate ancestor of both sides at Kurukṣetra. See [Lineages (Vaṃśa)](../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md).

## Relations, cult, and connections (quick map)

- **Parents:** Atri & Anasūyā (mainstream); Brahmā (*aṃśa*); ocean-churning (impersonal). **Brothers:** Dattātreya, Durvāsā.
- **Wives:** the **27 nakṣatra daughters of Dakṣa** (chief: **Rohiṇī**); plus **Tārā** (abducted, mother of Budha).
- **Children:** **Budha (Mercury)** by Tārā — founder of the human lunar line; **Varcas** and others in some lists.
- **As a Graha:** the 2nd of the [Navagraha](navagraha.md); lord of nakṣatras Rohiṇī, Hasta, Śravaṇa; significator of *manas* (mind) and the mother; gemstone **pearl**; metal silver; direction NW. See [Jyotiṣa & the Zodiac](../00-time-and-cosmology/jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md).
- **Cult sites:** **Somanātha** jyotirliṅga (Prabhāsa, Gujarat) — origin in Chandra's cure; the **Soma sacrifices (Agniṣṭoma, etc.)** of Vedic ritual. See [Sacred Geography](../06-sacred-geography/README.md).
- **Related figures:** [Sūrya](surya-and-his-family.md) (the day-luminary, complementary), [Sati, Dakṣa & Pārvatī](sati-daksha-and-parvati.md) (Dakṣa the curser), [Shiva Myth Cycle](shiva-myth-cycle.md) (crescent-bearer, restorer).
