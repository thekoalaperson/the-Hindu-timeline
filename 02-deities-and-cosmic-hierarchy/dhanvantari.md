# Dhanvantari

> Cosmic address: the **divine physician** (*deva-vaidya*) and god of **Āyurveda**, who rises from the churned Ocean of Milk bearing the pot of *amṛta*; an avatāra of Viṣṇu and the fountainhead of Indian medicine. Parent: [02 — Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md) · Master index: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md).
> A dedicated deep file expanding the [minor-deities subsection](kamadeva-and-minor-deities.md#dhanvantari--physician-of-the-gods); see also [Daśāvatāra](dashavatara.md) and [Vishnu's Forms & Vyūhas](vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md) for the churning and avatar schemata.

Dhanvantari (Sanskrit *Dhanvantari*, धन्वन्तरि) is the physician of the **devas**, the patron deity of **Āyurveda**, and the bearer of the *amṛta-kalaśa* at the climax of the [Samudra Manthana](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md). The tradition holds at least **two Dhanvantaris** — the cosmic deity who emerges from the ocean, and a later mortal **king of Kāśī (Divodāsa)** who systematizes surgery and teaches Suśruta — a duplication the Purāṇas themselves try to reconcile via rebirth. Every attested variant of his parentage, avatāra status, and the "one-or-two-Dhanvantaris" problem is cataloged below.

---

## Emergence from the Samudra Manthana

- **The churning** — when devas and asuras churn the **Kṣīra-sāgara** (Ocean of Milk) with **Mount Mandara** as the rod and the serpent **Vāsuki** as the rope, Dhanvantari is among the *ratnas* (jewels/treasures) thrown up by the ocean. He rises last or near-last, carrying the **pot (*kalaśa*) of *amṛta*** — the elixir of immortality — thereby precipitating the deva–asura war over the nectar. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.8; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 1.9; *Rāmāyaṇa* Bāla-kāṇḍa 45)
  - *Variant (what he carries):* (a) the *amṛta-kalaśa* alone; (b) the *amṛta-kalaśa* **and the texts of Āyurveda** (the *Āyurveda* itself emerging in his other hand) — the popular Dhanteras account; (c) the pot plus medicinal herbs. (festival/Purāṇic tradition)
  - *Variant (order of emergence):* in some lists Dhanvantari appears immediately **before** the *amṛta* itself as a distinct treasure; in others he **is** the bearer who personifies the nectar's appearance. (*Bhāgavata*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*)
- **The Mohinī sequel** — the asuras seize the pot; Viṣṇu takes the **Mohinī** enchantress-form to recover and redistribute the *amṛta* to the devas. Dhanvantari's role ends with the delivery of the pot; the contest over it is the next episode (see [Vishnu's Forms & Vyūhas](vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md) for Mohinī). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.9)
- **Significance** — the emergence is read as a charter myth: health, vitality, and the science of medicine are *amṛta*-adjacent gifts won only by sustained, cooperative cosmic effort (the churning). (devotional gloss)
- **Number of churnings** — the timeline places the Samudra Manthana in the current 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara; the event is the canonical "birth" of Dhanvantari-the-deity, prior to and distinct from his later mortal incarnation. (Purāṇic frame)

## Parentage and the "two Dhanvantaris"

- **Cosmic Dhanvantari** — ocean-born (*ayonija*, "not womb-born"), with no human parents; he arises directly from the churned sea and is at once appointed **physician of the gods** and entitled to a **share in sacrifices** (a yajña-portion granted by the devas). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa*; *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*)
- **Royal Dhanvantari (Kāśī line)** — a later Dhanvantari is **reborn into the royal house of Kāśī (Vārāṇasī)**, in the genealogy of **Kāśirāja**; identified with / titled **Divodāsa**. The *Bhāgavata* and *Brahmāṇḍa* genealogies trace him through the lunar-dynasty Kāśī kings (Dīrghatapas → Dhanvantari → ... → Divodāsa in some recensions, telescoped into one figure in others). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.17; *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*) [late text]
  - *Variant (one figure or two):* (a) the ocean-Dhanvantari **is reborn** as the Kāśī king — a single being across two appearances; (b) **two distinct** Dhanvantaris (a deity and a mortal teacher) whom tradition conflates; (c) "Dhanvantari" is a **dynastic title/lineage** (a *sampradāya*) carried by successive Kāśī physician-kings, not one person. (Purāṇic genealogy; scholarly reading) [disputed]
  - *Variant (Divodāsa identity):* Divodāsa is sometimes the **same** as the royal Dhanvantari, sometimes his **descendant/successor** who inherits the medical office. (*Suśruta Saṃhitā* framing vs Purāṇic genealogy) [disputed]
- **Teacher of the deity** — accounts of where Dhanvantari himself learned medicine vary: (a) self-existent knowledge as the ocean-born god; (b) instructed by the sage **Bharadvāja** (who received Āyurveda from **Indra**, who had it from the **Aśvins**, from **Prajāpati/Brahmā**); the latter is the classic *paramparā* chain. (Wikipedia; Caraka/Suśruta tradition)

## Avatāra status

- **Avatāra of Viṣṇu** — Dhanvantari is widely counted an **avatāra of Viṣṇu**, his four-armed iconography deliberately mirroring Viṣṇu's. (Wikipedia; *Bhāgavata*) — see [Daśāvatāra](dashavatara.md).
  - *Variant (which list / number):* he appears in the **24-avatāra** roster of the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (counted among the *līlā-avatāras*, in the ordinal range commonly given as the 12th), and is included in extended avatāra lists; he is **not** in the standard **Daśāvatāra (10)**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.3; Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (independent deity):* an alternative strand treats Dhanvantari as an **independent deity** — the god who *promulgated* medicine — rather than as a Viṣṇu-avatāra proper. (Wikipedia)
- **Cross-link** — for the full 10 / 22 / 24-avatāra schemata and where Dhanvantari sits among them, see [Daśāvatāra](dashavatara.md).

## Iconography

- **Four-armed form** — depicted **four-armed** like Viṣṇu, of pleasing/handsome appearance, often dark- or amber-complexioned. The *Viṣṇudharmottara Purāṇa* gives the standard iconographic prescription. (Wikipedia; *Viṣṇudharmottara*)
- **Attributes (caturbhuja)** — typically the **śaṅkha** (conch) and **cakra** (discus) of Viṣṇu, the **amṛta-kalaśa** (pot of nectar), and a fourth attribute that varies:
  - *Variant (4th hand):* (a) a **jalūka / leech** — emblem of *raktamokṣa* (therapeutic bloodletting) in classical surgery; (b) medicinal **herbs / oṣadhi**; (c) the **Āyurveda text / palm-leaf manuscript**; (d) the *cakra* replaced by the pot in two hands. (Wikipedia; iconographic tradition)
- **Modern temple image** — popular Āyurvedic-college and clinic icons show him standing or seated on a lotus, the *amṛta-kalaśa* prominent, sometimes with a halo of healing light. (devotional/[folk])

## The Kāśī incarnation, Divodāsa, and Suśruta

- **King of Kāśī** — in his mortal incarnation Dhanvantari reigns as **king of Kāśī (Vārāṇasī)**, where he establishes and openly teaches the complete Āyurvedic system — the first systematic public instruction of medicine, per the tradition. (Wikipedia; *Suśruta Saṃhitā*)
- **Suśruta as pupil** — a group of sages led by **Suśruta** approaches the Kāśī king-physician to learn medicine; he is taught **Śalya-tantra (surgery)** in particular. The resulting **[Suśruta Saṃhitā](../90-literature-corpus/README.md)** — a foundational Āyurvedic text — frames itself as Divodāsa-Dhanvantari's teaching transmitted through Suśruta. (*Suśruta Saṃhitā* Sūtrasthāna 1; Wikipedia) [late text]
- **Surgical legacy** — the Dhanvantari-Suśruta transmission is credited with rhinoplasty (nose reconstruction), cataract couching, lithotomy, suturing, surgical instruments (*yantra*/*śastra*), and post-operative care — the **Dhanvantari Sampradāya**, the surgical school of Āyurveda (vs the medicine-focused **Ātreya/Caraka Sampradāya**). (Suśruta tradition; Wikipedia)
- **Eight branches (*Aṣṭāṅga Āyurveda*)** — Dhanvantari is said to have classified medical knowledge into **eight fields**: *Kāya-cikitsā* (internal medicine), *Śalya* (surgery), *Śālākya* (ENT/eye), *Kaumāra-bhṛtya* (pediatrics/obstetrics), *Agada-tantra* (toxicology), *Bhūta-vidyā* (psychiatry/spirit-medicine), *Rasāyana* (rejuvenation), *Vājīkaraṇa* (virility/reproduction). (Āyurvedic tradition)
- *Scholarly note [scholarly]:* academically (the "two clocks"), the historical Suśruta Saṃhitā is dated to roughly the **mid-1st millennium BCE – early 1st millennium CE** in its layered form, with the Kāśī/Divodāsa attribution treated as a traditional authorship frame rather than a datable biography. Scripture places Dhanvantari in deep yuga-time; academic dating addresses only the *text*.

## Dhanteras, Dhanvantari Jayantī & Āyurveda Day

- **Dhanteras / Dhanatrayodaśī** — celebrated on the **13th lunar day (trayodaśī) of Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa** of the month **Āśvina (amānta)** / **Kārtika (pūrṇimānta / Bikram Sambat)**, the first day of the five-day **Dīpāvali** cycle. Marks the day Dhanvantari rose from the ocean with the *amṛta*-pot. (Wikipedia: Dhanteras)
- **Dhanvantari Jayantī** — the same day is observed as **Dhanvantari Jayantī** (his "birth"/emergence day); Āyurvedic practitioners and institutions worship him in the **evening**. (Hindu calendar tradition)
- **Triad worshipped on Dhanteras** — **Lakṣmī** (wealth), **Kubera** (treasurer), and **Dhanvantari** (health) — wealth, prosperity, and well-being together. The buying of metal/gold on Dhanteras is the popular wealth-aspect; the Dhanvantari aspect sanctifies **health as the truest wealth**. ([Lakṣmī](trimurti-and-tridevi.md); Wikipedia)
- **National Āyurveda Day** — the Government of India (Ministry of AYUSH) designated Dhanteras as **National Āyurveda Day**, first observed **28 October 2016**, anchoring modern Āyurveda's identity to Dhanvantari. (Ministry of AYUSH; Wikipedia)
- *Variant (folk etymology):* "Dhan-teras" is popularly glossed as *dhana* (wealth) + *trayodaśī* (13th); the Dhanvantari association links the same root to the "wealth" of health. ([folk])

## Worship, mantras & lineage

- **Dhanvantari mantra** — the common invocation is **"Oṃ Dhanvantaraye namaḥ"**; a longer dhyāna-mantra ("*Oṃ namo bhagavate vāsudevāya dhanvantaraye amṛta-kalaśa-hastāya...*") invokes him holding the nectar-pot, conch, discus, and dispelling disease/fear. (devotional tradition)
- **Tutelary deity of physicians** — *vaidyas* (Āyurvedic doctors) traditionally invoke Dhanvantari before practice and study; many Āyurvedic colleges and pharmacies bear his name and image. ([folk]/institutional)
- **Lineage of transmission** — the Āyurvedic *paramparā* runs **Brahmā → Prajāpati (Dakṣa) → the Aśvins → Indra → Bharadvāja / Dhanvantari → human disciples (Suśruta, Caraka tradition)**; Dhanvantari anchors the **surgical (Śalya)** line. (Caraka/Suśruta prologues)
- *Open roster note:* the precise Kāśī-dynasty genealogy linking ocean-Dhanvantari to Divodāsa varies across Purāṇic recensions (Bhāgavata 9.17, Brahmāṇḍa, Viṣṇu Purāṇa differ in intervening names); treat the king-list as an **open/variant roster**.
