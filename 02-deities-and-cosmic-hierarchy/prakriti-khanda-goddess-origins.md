# Ṣaṣṭhī, Maṅgalā Caṇḍī, Svadhā and Dakṣiṇā — the Prakṛti-khaṇḍa goddess aetiologies

> Deities-and-hierarchy node. The origin-tales of four "minor" goddesses issued their Sanskritic birth-certificates as named portions (*aṁśa* / *kalā*) of **Mūla-Prakṛti** in the **Prakṛti-khaṇḍa** of the **Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa** and its parallel in **Devī-Bhāgavata Book IX**: **Ṣaṣṭhī** the sixth-day child-mother, **Maṅgalā Caṇḍī** the beneficent fortune-goddess, **Svadhā** given to the ancestral Fathers, and **Dakṣiṇā** wed to the personified Sacrifice. This is the canonical narrative home flagged by the [Ṣaṣṭhī cross-link rider](gramadevata-shashthi-cross-links.md). Parent: [Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md) · Master index: [../README.md](../README.md).

## 1. The Prakṛti-khaṇḍa emanation frame — one catalogue, many goddesses

- **The scheme** — the *Prakṛti-khaṇḍa* (the second khaṇḍa of the Brahmavaivarta, mirrored in Devī-Bhāgavata Book IX) opens with **Mūla-Prakṛti** manifesting as **five full goddesses** — **Durgā, Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī, Sāvitrī, Rādhā** (the *Pañca-Prakṛti*) — and then a long train of **part-portions (aṁśa-rūpā)** and **digit-portions (kalā-rūpā)**; it is in this secondary catalogue that the folk goddesses receive their Prakṛti-pedigrees. (Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa; Devī-Bhāgavata IX) [late text]
- **Sanskritization in the act** — this khaṇḍa is the clearest single textual case of a late Bengal-region Purāṇic corpus **issuing scriptural genealogies to living village goddesses**; the same upward mapping is described system-wide in [gramadevatas-village-deities.md](gramadevatas-village-deities.md) and rostered in [devi-forms-and-navadurga.md](devi-forms-and-navadurga.md). [scholarly]
- **The two clocks** — the aetiologies are set on the **cyclic clock** (creation-time emanation of Prakṛti; the Ṣaṣṭhī charter in the **first (Svāyambhuva) Manvantara**; Kali began 3102 BCE), but the **texts carrying them are late on the academic clock**: the extant Brahmavaivarta is a Bengal recomposition of **[scholarly] c. 15th–16th century CE**, and the Devī-Bhāgavata is dated **[scholarly] c. 9th–14th century CE** (Hazra: 11th–12th c.). Never collapse the one frame into the other. [late text]
- **Chapter-numbering caveat** — printed editions of both texts divide these chapters differently; the block reference **Devī-Bhāgavata IX.44–47** and **Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa chs. 41–44** is used as a range, not a single verse-pin, because the counts do not agree edition to edition. [late text]

## 2. Ṣaṣṭhī — the sixth-day child-mother and the Priyavrata charter

- **Who she is** — **Ṣaṣṭhī** ("the Sixth"; Bengali *Ṣaṣṭhī / Shôshthi*, Hindi *Chhathī / Chhathī Māī*) is the presiding goddess of **childbirth, infants and the sixth day after birth**, called Ṣaṣṭhī because she is the **sixth portion (ṣaṣṭhāṁśa) of Mūla-Prakṛti** and because her worship falls on **sixth tithis** and the sixth day of the birth-chamber. In this block she is identified with **Devasenā**, consort of **[Skanda-Kārttikeya](ganesha-and-kartikeya.md)**. (Devī-Bhāgavata IX.44–47; Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa 41–44) [late text]
- **The Priyavrata charter-myth** — King **Priyavrata**, son of **[Svāyambhuva Manu](../08-beings-and-bestiary/the-prajapatis.md)** (which places the tale in the **first Manvantara** on the cyclic clock), long childless, at last obtains by his queen **Mālinī** a son who is **born dead**. As the grieving king carries the corpse to the burning-ground, a radiant goddess descends on her celestial car, reveals herself as **Devasenā-Ṣaṣṭhī, sixth portion of Prakṛti**, takes the infant on her lap and **restores it to life**; the boy is named **Suvrata**. In gratitude Priyavrata **institutes her worship** — fixing the rites of the birth-chamber's sixth day and the monthly bright-sixth — the aetiology of every **Ṣaṣṭhī-vrata**. (Devī-Bhāgavata IX.44–47; Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa 41–44) [late text]
  - *Variant (folk Chhath charter):* the same Priyavrata story is told in Bihar as the **origin of Chhath Pūjā**, with **Chhathī Maiyā** as the reviving goddess — catalogued with its Karṇa and Draupadī alternates in [../05-concepts-and-dharma/regional-temple-festivals.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/regional-temple-festivals.md). [folk]
  - *Variant (parentage):* the Devī-Bhāgavata block styles Devasenā a **mind-born (mānasī) daughter of Brahmā**; epic-Purāṇic tradition elsewhere makes her a **daughter of Indra** (or of the Prajāpati Dakṣa's line) — the genealogies are **not harmonized**. [disputed]
- **The sixth-night destiny-writing** — in pan-North-Indian folk belief Ṣaṣṭhī (or **Vidhātā** acting with her) comes to the birth-room on the **sixth night (*chhathī*)** and **writes the infant's fate on its forehead**; a lamp, pen and paper are kept ready — the rationale of the *sūtikā-ṣaṣṭhī* rite. [folk] [oral tradition]
- **Iconography** — a golden, motherly goddess **with an infant on her lap**, whose mount is the **cat (mārjāra-vāhanā)**; in village practice overwhelmingly **aniconic** (a stone beneath a banyan, a pot, a daubed niche in the birth-room). Bengali *bratakathā* lore makes the cat her sacred animal, whom offended households must appease. [folk] [regional]
- **Disambiguation** — this Ṣaṣṭhī (a goddess, sixth *aṁśa* of Prakṛti) is not to be confused with the *ṣaṣṭhī tithi* (the sixth lunar day) on which she is worshipped, though the two are folk-etymologically fused. Her full cross-link mesh sits in [gramadevata-shashthi-cross-links.md](gramadevata-shashthi-cross-links.md).

## 3. Maṅgalā Caṇḍī — the beneficent goddess Śiva worshipped before Tripura

- **Who she is** — **Maṅgalā Caṇḍī** ("auspicious Caṇḍī") is the **beneficent (maṅgala) form of Caṇḍī/Durgā**, giver of household weal, welfare and fortune — a *kalā* of Prakṛti distinguished from the terrible battle-Caṇḍī of the [Devī-Māhātmya](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md). (Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa 41–44; Devī-Bhāgavata IX.44–47) [late text]
- **The Tripura charter-myth** — the block's aetiology has **[Śiva](shiva-myth-cycle.md)** himself, before setting out to burn the three flying cities of the asuras (**Tripura**), **worship Maṅgalā Caṇḍī** for victory; by her grace he conquers as **Tripurāntaka**, and thereafter her worship spreads through the worlds — first the gods, then men and women adopting it. Because the god of destruction bows to her for success, her rite is a **charter of protective auspiciousness**. (Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa 41–44; Devī-Bhāgavata IX.44–47) [late text]
- **The Tuesday cult** — her worship is fixed above all to **Tuesday (maṅgala-vāra)** and to women's household weal-vratas; in Bengal and eastern India she is a distinct household goddess, worshipped for the safety of husband, children and prosperity. [regional] [folk]
- **The Bengali maṅgal-kāvya face** — medieval Bengal's **Caṇḍīmaṅgal** (Mukundarāma Cakravartī, c. 1590s) makes this same Maṅgalcaṇḍī the goddess who **forces her worship on the hunter Kālketu and the merchant Dhanapati/Śrīmanta** — the theology of "the goddess who compels recognition," treated in full at [../04-deep-dives/chandimangal-kalketu-and-srimanta.md](../04-deep-dives/chandimangal-kalketu-and-srimanta.md). The Purāṇic charter (cyclic clock) and the *maṅgal* poem (**[scholarly]** datable to named poets, 15th–18th c. CE) are two clocks on one cult. [regional] [scholarly]
- **Disambiguation** — "Maṅgalā Caṇḍī" (this beneficent household goddess and her Śaiva-Tripura charter) must not be merged with **Caṇḍī / Caṇḍikā** the demon-slayer of the Devī-Māhātmya, nor with the *Caṇḍī* who is Pārvatī in the [Manasāmaṅgal](manasa.md); the name is shared, the aetiologies are separate. [scholarly]

## 4. Svadhā — the oblation-call given to the Pitṛs

- **Who she is** — **Svadhā** is the **personified ritual exclamation *"svadhā!"*** pronounced when pouring oblations to the **ancestral Fathers (Pitṛs)** — the counterpart, for the ancestors, of **Svāhā**, the call for offerings to the gods (see [agni.md](agni.md) §on Agni's consorts). She is a named portion of Prakṛti in the same catalogue. (Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa; Devī-Bhāgavata IX) [late text]
- **The charter-myth** — the **Pitṛs (the orders of divine ancestors)** had no wife and their rites no efficacy; **Brahmā** creates **Svadhā** from Prakṛti and **gives her to the Pitṛs as wife**, so that thereafter an ancestral offering "reaches" only when sealed with her name — the aetiology that welds the *śrāddha* liturgy to a goddess. (Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa) [late text]
- **Her daughters and the mountain-river genealogy** — the Pitṛs' **mind-born daughters (mānasī kanyā)** — foremost **Menā (Mainā)**, wed to Mount **Himavat** and thereby maternal grandmother-line of **Pārvatī and Gaṅgā** — are counted through Svadhā in this frame; the standard Purāṇic Pitṛ-genealogy is detailed in [../08-beings-and-bestiary/pitrs-and-ancestors.md](../08-beings-and-bestiary/pitrs-and-ancestors.md). (Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa, Viṣṇu Purāṇa for the Menā line; Brahmavaivarta welds it to Svadhā) [late text] [disputed harmonization]
- **Disambiguation** — **Svadhā** (ancestral call, wife of the Pitṛs) is a **distinct goddess from Svāhā** (the *svāhā!* call, wife of **[Agni](agni.md)** and a strand in the birth of Kārttikeya); the two oblation-goddesses are routinely paired in the catalogue and just as routinely confused. She is likewise unrelated to the *dakṣiṇāyana* (southern solar course) that shares a root with §5's Dakṣiṇā. [scholarly]

## 5. Dakṣiṇā — the sacrificial fee wed to Yajña-puruṣa

- **Who she is** — **Dakṣiṇā** is the **personified sacrificial fee / priestly honorarium** (*dakṣiṇā*, the gift given to the officiant that completes a rite), a portion of **[Lakṣmī](lakshmi.md)/Prakṛti** in the catalogue. (Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa; Devī-Bhāgavata IX) [late text]
- **The charter-myth** — the **Yajña-puruṣa** (the Sacrifice personified, a form of **Viṣṇu**) performed rites that yielded **no fruit**, because a sacrifice without its fee is barren; **Dakṣiṇā** is manifested and **given in marriage to Yajña**, and from their union is born a son, **Phala** ("Fruit / Result"). Thereafter **no sacrifice bears fruit unless the *dakṣiṇā* is paid** — the theological charter for the indispensability of the priestly fee. (Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa) [late text]
  - *Variant (Suśīlā origin):* one recension makes Dakṣiṇā a former Goloka companion (**Suśīlā**, a portion of Kamalā/Lakṣmī) who, through a curse, is born on earth and then becomes the sacrificial-fee goddess wed to Yajña — the emanation-then-descent pattern typical of the khaṇḍa. [late text] [disputed]
- **Disambiguation** — **Dakṣiṇā** (the fee-goddess, wife of Yajña) is **not** the Prajāpati **Dakṣa** (Sati's father → [sati-daksha-and-parvati.md](sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)), nor the *dakṣiṇā*-direction (south); the shared root *dakṣ-* ("able, competent; that which is given") links but does not identify them. [scholarly]

## 6. The four as a set — theology and worship

- **A grammar of ritual auspiciousness** — read together, the four aetiologies personify **the missing element that makes a rite work**: Ṣaṣṭhī makes childbirth safe and the sixth-day rite meaningful, Maṅgalā Caṇḍī makes an undertaking (even Śiva's war) auspicious, Svadhā makes the ancestral offering reach the Fathers, and Dakṣiṇā makes the sacrifice bear fruit. The khaṇḍa is, in effect, a **catalogue of ritual efficacies deified**. [scholarly]
- **Where each cult actually lives** — Ṣaṣṭhī and Maṅgalā Caṇḍī became **thick living folk cults** (household vratas, *bratakathā* and *maṅgal-kāvya* literature across Bengal, Bihar and the east); Svadhā and Dakṣiṇā remained largely **liturgical abstractions** invoked in the *śrāddha* and *yajña* formulae rather than goddesses with festivals of their own. The Prakṛti-khaṇḍa is the one place the four stand side by side. [regional] [scholarly]
- **The wider catalogue** — the same run also issues pedigrees to **Manasā** (snake-goddess → [manasa.md](manasa.md), in the immediately adjacent chapters), **Tulasī, Gaṅgā, Surabhi, Svāhā** and others; **Śītalā**, though a cluster-member by cult, is anchored **outside** this block (in the Śītalāṣṭaka ascribed to the Skanda Purāṇa → [shitala.md](shitala.md)), and no DBP IX.44–47 aetiology should be claimed for her. [late text]
