## 1. Editorial scope — what this rider is

- **Purpose** — this file is a **linking rider** to the planned *Prakṛti-khaṇḍa goddess-origins* item (the aetiologies of the "minor" goddesses as portions of Mūla-Prakṛti). The canonical narrative home for those origin-stories is that forthcoming file; until it lands in the corpus, **this rider holds the Ṣaṣṭhī thread and the cluster's mutual cross-links** so no claim is orphaned. *(No link is given to the goddess-origins file because it does not yet exist in the corpus; when it lands, this rider's checklist in §6 applies to it as well.)*
- **The cluster** — four goddesses form a single Bengali-eastern devotional complex, each with (a) a thin, late Purāṇic aetiology as a portion (*aṁśa* / *kalā*) of Prakṛti, and (b) a thick living folk cult with its own vrata-calendar and (for three of them) a *maṅgal-kāvya* literature: **Manasā** (snakes → [manasa.md](manasa.md)), **Śītalā** (pox and fevers → [shitala.md](shitala.md)), **Ṣaṣṭhī** (childbirth and infants → this file, pending the goddess-origins item), and **Maṅgala Caṇḍī** (household fortune → narrative home [../04-deep-dives/chandimangal-kalketu-and-srimanta.md](../04-deep-dives/chandimangal-kalketu-and-srimanta.md)). [regional] [scholarly]
- **Source block** — the shared scriptural anchor, cited throughout as in the merged item: **Devī-Bhāgavata IX.44–47** and the parallel **Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa 41–44** — the run of chapters giving the Ṣaṣṭhī and Maṅgala Caṇḍī aetiologies within the Prakṛti-catalogue (the Manasā story sits in the immediately adjacent chapters of the same run). Chapter numbering varies across printed editions of both texts, so the block reference, not a single verse-pin, is used here. [late text]
- **Two clocks** — the Purāṇic aetiologies are set on the **cyclic clock** (creation-time emanation of Prakṛti; the first Manvantara for the Priyavrata charter); the texts carrying them are **late medieval on the academic clock** — the extant Brahmavaivarta a Bengal recomposition of c. the 15th–16th century CE, the Devī-Bhāgavata c. 9th–14th century CE (Hazra 11th–12th c.) — and the living cults and their *maṅgal* literature are documentable only from the medieval period onward. See [../90-literature-corpus/puranas/brahmavaivarta-purana.md](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/brahmavaivarta-purana.md) and [../90-literature-corpus/puranas/devi-bhagavata-upapurana.md](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/devi-bhagavata-upapurana.md). [scholarly]

## 2. Ṣaṣṭhī — the goddess of the sixth day (the thread this rider holds)

- **Who she is** — **Ṣaṣṭhī** ("the Sixth," Bengali *Ṣaṣṭhī / Shôshthi*, Hindi *Chhathī / Chhathī Māī*) is the presiding goddess of **childbirth, infants and the sixth day after birth** — the great domestic protectress of children across Bengal, Bihar, eastern UP, Odisha and Nepal. In the Purāṇic frame she is identified with **Devasenā**, the wife of **[Skanda-Kārttikeya](ganesha-and-kartikeya.md)**. (Devī-Bhāgavata IX.44–47; Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa 41–44) [late text]
- **The name** — she is called Ṣaṣṭhī because she is declared the **sixth portion (ṣaṣṭhāṁśa) of Mūla-Prakṛti**, and because her worship falls on **sixth (ṣaṣṭhī) tithis** and the **sixth day after a birth**. (Devī-Bhāgavata IX.44–47) [late text]
- **The Priyavrata charter-myth** — King **Priyavrata**, son of **Svāyambhuva Manu** (placing the tale in the **first Manvantara** on the cyclic clock), long childless, obtains by his queen **Mālinī** a son who is **born dead**; as the grieving king carries the corpse to the burning-ground, the goddess appears, reveals herself as **Devasenā-Ṣaṣṭhī**, and **revives the child**, who is named **Suvrata**; in gratitude Priyavrata **institutes her worship**, fixing the rites of the birth-chamber sixth day and the monthly bright-sixth — the aetiology of every Ṣaṣṭhī-vrata. (Devī-Bhāgavata IX.44–47; Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa 41–44) [late text]
  - *Variant (folk Chhath charter):* the same Priyavrata story is told as the **origin of Chhath Puja** in the Bihar tradition, with **Chhathī Maiyā** as the reviving goddess — already catalogued with its Karṇa and Draupadī alternates in [../05-concepts-and-dharma/regional-temple-festivals.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/regional-temple-festivals.md). [folk]
- **Devasenā the wife of Skanda** — in the Purāṇic-epic frame Devasenā is given to **Kārttikeya** after the demon-war (in the Tamil telling as **Deivānai**, co-wife with Vaḷḷi) — see [ganesha-and-kartikeya.md](ganesha-and-kartikeya.md); the Prakṛti-khaṇḍa/Devī-Bhāgavata block is what welds this epic consort to the folk sixth-day mother. (Devī-Bhāgavata IX.44–47) [late text]
  - *Variant (parentage):* the Devī-Bhāgavata block styles her a **mind-born (mānasī) daughter of Brahmā**; epic-Purāṇic tradition elsewhere makes Devasenā a **daughter of Indra** (or of Prajāpati Dakṣa's line) — the genealogies are not harmonized. [disputed]
- **The sixth-night destiny-writing** — in pan-North-Indian folk belief, on the **sixth night after birth** (*chhathī*), Ṣaṣṭhī (or **Vidhātā** acting with her) comes to the birth-chamber and **writes the infant's destiny on its forehead**; the household keeps a lamp, pen and paper ready — the rationale of the sixth-day rite. [folk] [oral tradition]
- **Iconography and shrine-form** — a golden, motherly goddess **with an infant (or many infants) on her lap**, whose mount is the **cat (mārjāra-vāhanā)**; in village practice she is overwhelmingly **aniconic** — a stone beneath a **banyan** or other sacred tree, a pot, a daubed niche in the birth-room — placing her squarely in the grāmadevatā shrine-typology catalogued in [gramadevatas-village-deities.md](gramadevatas-village-deities.md). [folk] [regional]
  - *Variant (cat lore):* Bengali Ṣaṣṭhī tales (the *bratakathā* cycle) make the cat her sacred animal whom offended households must appease — injuring a cat invites her wrath on one's children. [folk] [oral tradition]
- **Ṣaṣṭhī-vratas of Bengal** — a household woman's vrata-cycle runs through the year's sixth tithis, most prominently **Araṇya-Ṣaṣṭhī / Jāmāi-Ṣaṣṭhī** (Jyaiṣṭha bright sixth, when mothers fast for children and feast the son-in-law) and **Nīl-Ṣaṣṭhī** (the child-welfare fast of the Chaitra Gājan eve, already catalogued in [../05-concepts-and-dharma/regional-temple-festivals.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/regional-temple-festivals.md)); these are recited with **Ṣaṣṭhīr bratakathā** tales. [folk] [regional]

## 3. The Prakṛti-khaṇḍa frame — one catalogue, four goddesses

- **The emanation scheme** — the Prakṛti-khaṇḍa (mirrored in Devī-Bhāgavata Book IX) opens with **Mūla-Prakṛti** manifesting as **five full goddesses** (Durgā, Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī, Sāvitrī, Rādhā) and then a train of **part-portions (aṁśa-rūpā) and digit-portions (kalā-rūpā)** — and it is in this secondary catalogue that the Bengal folk goddesses receive their Sanskritic birth-certificates. (Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa; Devī-Bhāgavata IX) [late text]
- **Ṣaṣṭhī in the catalogue** — the sixth aṁśa of Prakṛti, protectress of children (§2). (Devī-Bhāgavata IX.44–47; Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa 41–44) [late text]
- **Maṅgala Caṇḍī in the catalogue** — the **beneficent (maṅgala) form of Caṇḍī/Durgā**, giver of household weal; the block's charter-myth has **[Śiva](shiva-myth-cycle.md)** himself worship her before the **Tripura war** and conquer by her grace, after which her worship — associated with **Tuesdays (maṅgalavāra)** and with women's household vratas — spreads through the worlds. (Devī-Bhāgavata IX.44–47; Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa 41–44) [late text]
  - *Variant (Bengali maṅgal-kāvya):* medieval Bengal's **Caṇḍīmaṅgal** poems make this same Maṅgalcaṇḍī the goddess who forces her worship on the hunter Kālketu and the merchant Dhanapati — full treatment in [../04-deep-dives/chandimangal-kalketu-and-srimanta.md](../04-deep-dives/chandimangal-kalketu-and-srimanta.md). [regional] [scholarly]
- **Manasā in the adjacent chapters** — the snake-goddess's Purāṇic aetiology (Kaśyapa's mind-born daughter, wife of Jaratkāru, mother of Āstīka) sits in the same Prakṛti-catalogue run immediately adjacent to the Ṣaṣṭhī–Maṅgalacaṇḍī block; her canonical home in this corpus is [manasa.md](manasa.md), which already flags the Brahmavaivarta/Devī-Bhāgavata stratum as [late text]. (Brahmavaivarta, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa; Devī-Bhāgavata IX) [late text]
- **Śītalā — the cluster member *outside* the block** — Śītalā belongs to the Bengal folk cluster by cult and by maṅgal-literature, but her scriptural anchor is **not** the Prakṛti-khaṇḍa block: it is the **Śītalāṣṭaka** ascribed to the Skanda Purāṇa (see [shitala.md](shitala.md)). Any linking language must not imply she has a DBP IX.44–47 aetiology. [late text] [scholarly]
- **Why the block matters** — this is the clearest textual case of **Sanskritization in the act**: a late Bengal-region Purāṇic corpus issuing Prakṛti-pedigrees to living village goddesses. The same upward mapping is described system-wide in [gramadevatas-village-deities.md](gramadevatas-village-deities.md) and in the roster [devi-forms-and-navadurga.md](devi-forms-and-navadurga.md). [scholarly]

## 4. Calendar lines — the Ṣaṣṭhī/Chhath tithi-cluster

All dated on the luni-solar ritual calendar ([../00-time-and-cosmology/hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md](../00-time-and-cosmology/hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md)); the observances themselves are medieval-to-modern in attestation [scholarly].

- **Chhath Puja** — Kārtika bright sixth (Oct–Nov), Bihar–Jharkhand–eastern UP–Nepal Terai: the four-day riverside sun-vrata to **[Sūrya](surya-and-his-family.md)** and **Chhathī Maiyā**; the folk tradition identifies Chhathī Maiyā with **Ṣaṣṭhī-Devasenā** (also, variantly, with Uṣā or Sūrya's sister). Catalogued with its charter-variants (Priyavrata; Karṇa; Draupadī and the Pāṇḍavas) in [../05-concepts-and-dharma/regional-temple-festivals.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/regional-temple-festivals.md). [folk] [regional]
  - *Variant (Chaitī Chhath):* a smaller spring observance of the same vrata in Chaitra. [regional]
- **Skanda Ṣaṣṭhī / Sūrasaṁhāram** — the Tamil six-day fast ending on the Aippasi sixth with Murugan's slaying of Sūrapadma and, next day, his wedding to **Deivānai (Devasenā)** — the southern face of the same sixth-tithi consort-goddess; see [../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md) and [../05-concepts-and-dharma/regional-temple-festivals.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/regional-temple-festivals.md). [regional]
- **Sūtikā-ṣaṣṭhī / chhathī** — the domestic **sixth-day-after-birth rite** instituted, per the charter, by Priyavrata (§2) and still the commonest occasion of Ṣaṣṭhī worship. (Devī-Bhāgavata IX.44–47) [late text] [folk]
- **Araṇya-/Jāmāi-Ṣaṣṭhī, Nīl-Ṣaṣṭhī and the Bengali sixth-tithi cycle** — as in §2. [folk] [regional]

## 5. The maṅgal-kāvya face of the cluster

- **Shared genre** — Manasā, Maṅgala Caṇḍī and Śītalā each carry a Bengali *maṅgal-kāvya* (Manasāmaṅgal, 15th c. onward; Caṇḍīmaṅgal, Mukundarāma c. 1590s; Śītalāmaṅgal, later), and Ṣaṣṭhī a humbler **bratakathā/Ṣaṣṭhīmaṅgal** vrata-tale literature — all dramatizing the same theology of the **goddess who compels recognition**. Genre home: [../90-literature-corpus/regional-and-oral-epics.md](../90-literature-corpus/regional-and-oral-epics.md); sister cult in the same milieu: [dharma-thakur.md](dharma-thakur.md). [regional] [scholarly]
- **Two-clocks note** — the *maṅgal* poems are firmly datable (named poets, 15th–18th c. CE [scholarly]) even though the goddesses' Purāṇic charters float on the cyclic clock — the same double-clock pattern documented in [manasa.md](manasa.md).
