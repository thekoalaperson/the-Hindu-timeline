# Forms of the Devī & the Navadurgā

> Where this sits: a roster node within **[Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md)**, enumerating the manifest **forms of the one Goddess (Ādi Parāśakti / Mahādevī)**. It complements the narrative **[Devī Māhātmya & the Great Goddess](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)**, the Tantric **[Ten Mahāvidyās](dasha-mahavidya.md)**, the **[Sati, Daksha & Pārvatī](sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)** cycle, and the **[Trimūrti & Tridevī](trimurti-and-tridevi.md)** overview. The Mātṛkās and Yoginīs live in **[08-beings-and-bestiary/matrikas-and-64-yoginis](../08-beings-and-bestiary/matrikas-and-64-yoginis.md)**.

The Shākta axiom is that **all goddesses are one Devī** — Mahālakṣmī, Mahākālī and Mahāsarasvatī as the three guṇa-aspects of a single Śakti (**Devī Māhātmya**), each fanning out into named, worshipped forms. This roster catalogs the major **classes** of those forms: the Navadurgā cycle, the supreme Śrīvidyā goddess Lalitā Tripurasundarī, the Durgā/Kālī/Caṇḍī complex, the Pārvatī/Gaurī/Annapūrṇā saumya forms, the Mātṛkās (cross-linked), the forms of Lakṣmī and Sarasvatī, the village Grāmadevatās, and the modern Santoṣī Mā. The total set is **open-ended** — every Indian village and lineage adds its own — so this catalogs the canonical and pan-Indian forms with variants flagged to source.

---

## The Threefold Root — Mahādevī as Three Guṇas

The master template under which the named forms are organized.

- **Mahākālī** - the *tāmasika* (dissolving / dark) aspect; slays Madhu-Kaiṭabha in the first carita of the **Devī Māhātmya**; ten faces/arms; root of the Kālī forms (Source: *Devī Māhātmya* 1; *Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa*).
- **Mahālakṣmī** - the *rājasika* (active / sustaining) aspect and, in the Śākta reading, the supreme integrating Devī herself; slays **Mahiṣāsura** in the middle carita; root of the Durgā/Caṇḍī forms (Source: *Devī Māhātmya* 2–4).
- **Mahāsarasvatī** - the *sāttvika* (luminous / knowing) aspect; slays **Śumbha-Niśumbha** and emanates Kālī/Caṇḍikā in the third carita; root of the Sarasvatī forms (Source: *Devī Māhātmya* 5–13).
  - *Variant (guṇa-assignment):* sources differ on which of the three is "supreme" — Śrīkula texts elevate Lalitā/Lakṣmī; Kālīkula texts elevate Kālī. The three-guṇa scheme itself is the *Devī Māhātmya*'s frame ([scholarly]).

## The Navadurgā — Nine Forms of the Nights of Navarātri

The **Navadurgā** ("Nine Durgās") are nine forms worshipped on the nine nights of **Śāradīya Navarātri**, one per day (tithi), read as nine stages of the Goddess's spiritual ascent from mountain-daughter to power-giver. The canonical sequence and dhyāna-verses derive from the **Devī Kavaca** appended to the *Devī Māhātmya* (the "Navadurgā" stotra) and Purāṇic dhyānas (Source: *Devī Kavaca*; *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa*).

1. **Śailaputrī** ("Daughter of the Mountain", Himavat) - Day 1; absolute Pārvatī/Sati reborn; rides the bull **Nandī**, holds triśūla and lotus; the embodiment of Mūlādhāra/nature (Source: *Devī Kavaca*; *Skanda Purāṇa*).
2. **Brahmacāriṇī** ("the ascetic maiden") - Day 2; Pārvatī performing tapas to win Śiva; walks barefoot, holds rosary (akṣamālā) and water-pot (kamaṇḍalu); penance and resolve (Source: *Devī Kavaca*).
3. **Candraghaṇṭā** ("she with the bell-shaped crescent moon") - Day 3; ten-armed, golden, rides a tiger/lion, crescent moon on brow; the warrior-bride, courage in battle (Source: *Devī Kavaca*).
4. **Kūṣmāṇḍā** ("the cosmic-egg / little-warmth one") - Day 4; eight-armed, rides a lion, said to have created the cosmos (the Aṇḍa) with a smile and to dwell in the sun's core; bestows vitality (Source: *Devī Kavaca*; folk etymology *ku + ūṣma + aṇḍa*).
5. **Skandamātā** ("mother of Skanda/[Kārttikeya](ganesha-and-kartikeya.md)") - Day 5; four-armed, holds the infant Skanda on her lap, rides a lion; maternal grace (Source: *Devī Kavaca*).
6. **Kātyāyanī** - Day 6; born to/in the hermitage of sage **Kātyāyana** specifically to slay **Mahiṣāsura**; the fierce warrior-form, identified with Durgā-of-the-Māhātmya; lion-mounted (Source: *Devī Māhātmya*; *Devī Kavaca*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10 — the gopīs' Kātyāyanī-vrata).
7. **Kālarātri** ("the dark night [of dissolution]") - Day 7; the fiercest Navadurgā; jet-black, disheveled hair, three eyes, rides a donkey/ass, flames from her breath; destroyer of ignorance, demons and black magic; also called **Śubhaṅkarī** (the auspicious) (Source: *Devī Kavaca*).
8. **Mahāgaurī** ("the great white one") - Day 8; brilliant white, the purified Gaurī after tapas; rides a bull, holds triśūla and ḍamaru; forgiveness and serenity (Source: *Devī Kavaca*).
9. **Siddhidātrī** ("giver of siddhis") - Day 9; seated on a lotus, four-armed, grants the eight occult perfections; said to have given Śiva his powers, making him **Ardhanārīśvara**; worshipped by devas, ṛṣis and siddhas (Source: *Devī Kavaca*; *Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa*).
   - *Variant (Navarātri Tridevī overlay):* a widespread devotional scheme groups the nine nights as **three days each** to Durgā/Kālī (destroy faults), then Lakṣmī (gain wealth), then Sarasvatī (gain knowledge) — orthogonal to the Navadurgā list above [folk].
   - *Variant (Nepāl/Newar Navadurgā):* the Bhaktapur Navadurgā tradition is a masked-dance pantheon centered on the eight Mātṛkās + Mahālakṣmī/Tripurasundarī, a *different* nine than the all-India Śailaputrī…Siddhidātrī set ([regional]).
   - *Variant (count/spelling):* some Purāṇic lists swap Kūṣmāṇḍā's and Kātyāyanī's day-order or substitute regional names; the nine names above are the pan-Indian standard fixed by the *Devī Kavaca*.

## Durgā, Kālī, Caṇḍī — the Fierce Complex

The martial, demon-slaying forms; the "active" face of the Goddess.

- **Durgā** ("the impassable / fort-goddess") - the supreme warrior-form, lion/tiger-mounted, eight/ten/eighteen-armed, bearing every god's weapon; assembled from the combined *tejas* of all the devas to slay **Mahiṣāsura** (hence **Mahiṣāsuramardinī**) (Source: *Devī Māhātmya*). Etymon also linked to slaying the asura **Durgama** (Source: *Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa* — the **Śatākṣī/Śākambharī** episode).
- **Mahiṣāsuramardinī** - Durgā in the precise act of impaling the buffalo-demon; the most-sculpted form of the Goddess across India (Source: iconographic tradition; *Devī Māhātmya*).
- **Caṇḍī / Caṇḍikā** ("the fierce one") - the form invoked throughout the *Devī Māhātmya* (the text is itself called the **Caṇḍī** / **Durgā Saptaśatī**, 700 verses); the Goddess as wrath that creates Kālī from her brow (Source: *Devī Māhātmya*).
- **Kālī** ("the black one / Time") - springs from Caṇḍī's furrowed brow to drink the blood of **Raktabīja**; garland of skulls, lolling tongue, stands on the supine **[Śiva](shiva-myth-cycle.md)**; first of the **[Ten Mahāvidyās](dasha-mahavidya.md)** (Source: *Devī Māhātmya* 7–8; *Mahānirvāṇa Tantra*).
  - *Variant forms of Kālī:* **Dakṣiṇā-Kālī** (benign, worship-form), **Bhadrakālī** (auspicious/protective, born from Śiva's wrath at the Dakṣa-yajña), **Śmaśāna-Kālī** (cremation-ground), **Guhya-Kālī**, **Mahākālī** (cosmic), **Kāmākhyā** (the Assam yoni-pīṭha goddess, often read as Kālī/Tripurā). See [Mahāvidyā](dasha-mahavidya.md) for the Kālī facet-list.
- **Cāmuṇḍā** - Kālī under the name earned by slaying the generals **Caṇḍa** and **Muṇḍa**; emaciated, fanged, the seventh **[Mātṛkā](../08-beings-and-bestiary/matrikas-and-64-yoginis.md)** (Source: *Devī Māhātmya* 7).
- **Bhavānī** - Durgā/Pārvatī as the dynamic life-power and tutelary goddess (e.g. **Tuljā Bhavānī** of Maharashtra, kuladevī of Śivājī); a *both*-fierce-and-maternal form (Source: regional Māhātmyas).
- **Vaiṣṇavī Devī** - the Trikūṭa-hill goddess of Jammu, worshipped as a triad of **Mahākālī, Mahālakṣmī, Mahāsarasvatī** (the piṇḍīs); a major pilgrimage form [late text].
  - *Variant (the "Nine Durgās" as a different nine):* some Śākta sources list nine *Durgā* names (Nanda, Rakta-dantikā, Śākambharī, Durgā, Bhīmā, Bhrāmarī, etc., from the *Devī Māhātmya*'s "future manifestations") distinct from the Navarātri Navadurgā — flagged because the two "nine" lists are frequently conflated ([scholarly]).

## Lalitā Tripurasundarī — the Supreme Śrīvidyā Goddess

The crown form of the **Śrīkula / Śrīvidyā** tradition, third of the **[Mahāvidyās](dasha-mahavidya.md)** but worshipped there as the totality.

- **Lalitā Tripurasundarī** - "the Playful Beauty of the Three Worlds/Cities"; radiant red, sixteen-year-old, four arms holding noose (pāśa), goad (aṅkuśa), sugarcane bow and flower-arrows; enthroned on a couch whose legs are Brahmā-Viṣṇu-Rudra-Īśvara with Sadāśiva as the seat; her subtle body is the **Śrī Yantra / Śrī Cakra** (Source: *Lalitā Sahasranāma*; *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa* — Lalitopākhyāna).
- **Her chief myth** - emerges from the fire of a yajña to slay the demon **Bhaṇḍāsura** (born of Kāma's ashes), commanding the Śrī Cakra battle-array (Source: *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*, Lalitopākhyāna).
- **Names / forms of the same goddess:**
  - **Ṣoḍaśī** - "the Sixteen", the sixteen-syllable Śrīvidyā mantra personified (Source: *Vāmakeśvara/Nityāṣoḍaśikārṇava Tantra*).
  - **Rājarājeśvarī** - "Queen of kings", the sovereign/imperial aspect.
  - **Kāmeśvarī** - consort of Kāmeśvara (Śiva), the desire-fulfilling aspect.
  - **Bālā Tripurasundarī** - the child-form (nine years old), her own daughter/junior mantra-form.
  - **Kāmākṣī** (Kāñcīpuram), **Mīnākṣī** (Madurai), **Viśālākṣī** (Vārāṇasī) - the great South/pan-Indian temple goddesses identified with Lalitā/Tripurā (and equally with [Pārvatī](sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)) (Source: temple sthala-purāṇas).
    - *Variant (Tripurā vs Pārvatī):* devotionally Mīnākṣī/Kāmākṣī are Pārvatī (Śiva's bride); doctrinally Śrīvidyā reads them as Lalitā Tripurasundarī — both attributions are canonical [disputed].

## Pārvatī, Gaurī, Annapūrṇā — the Saumya (Gentle) Forms

The benign household and consort forms; see the **[Sati–Dakṣa–Pārvatī cycle](sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)** for the narrative.

- **Pārvatī** ("daughter of the mountain") - reborn **Sati**, daughter of Himavat and Menā; wins **[Śiva](shiva-myth-cycle.md)** through tapas; mother of **[Gaṇeśa and Kārttikeya](ganesha-and-kartikeya.md)**; the substrate of which Durgā/Kālī are the fierce projections (Source: *Śiva Purāṇa*; *Kumārasambhava*).
- **Gaurī** ("the fair/golden one") - Pārvatī after she sheds her dark sheath (becoming **[Kālī](dasha-mahavidya.md)**) through tapas; goddess of marital felicity and harvest (Gaṇgaur, Hartālikā Tīj) (Source: *Skanda Purāṇa*).
- **Umā** - the Vedic-into-Purāṇic name; appears as **Umā-Haimavatī** revealing Brahman to the gods in the **Kena Upaniṣad** — the earliest named appearance of the Goddess as teacher (Source: *Kena Upaniṣad* 3–4 [scholarly]).
- **Annapūrṇā** ("full of food") - Pārvatī as the goddess of nourishment who feeds even Śiva (who, as ascetic, declared the world illusory; she proved food is real); enshrined at **Kāśī/Vārāṇasī**, ladle and bowl in hand (Source: *Annapūrṇā Upaniṣad* tradition; Kāśī sthala-purāṇa).
- **Aparṇā** ("she who took no leaf [as food]") - Pārvatī's extreme-austerity epithet during her tapas for Śiva (Source: *Kumārasambhava*).
- **Haimavatī, Girijā, Śivā, Mṛḍānī, Bhavānī** - further consort-epithets of the same goddess (Source: *Lalitā Sahasranāma*; *Devī Māhātmya* — the Aparājitā/Nārāyaṇī stuti).
  - *Variant:* in the **Tridevī**, Pārvatī (not Lakṣmī or Sarasvatī) is the consort assigned to Śiva; she is the **Śakti** without whom Śiva is *śava* (a corpse) — the Śākta thesis (Source: *Saundaryalaharī* v.1).

## The Mātṛkās & Yoginīs — cross-link

The **Sapta/Aṣṭa Mātṛkā** (Seven/Eight Mothers) — **Brāhmī, Māheśvarī, Kaumārī, Vaiṣṇavī, Vārāhī, Indrāṇī/Aindrī, Cāmuṇḍā** (+**Nārasiṃhī/Mahālakṣmī** for eight) — are the śaktis of the male gods, emanated to fight **Raktabīja** (Source: *Devī Māhātmya* 8). The **64 (or 81) Yoginīs** expand the Mātṛkā retinue. **Full roster, counts and variant lists:** see **[08-beings-and-bestiary/matrikas-and-64-yoginis](../08-beings-and-bestiary/matrikas-and-64-yoginis.md)**.

## Forms of Lakṣmī

- **Aṣṭalakṣmī** ("the Eight Lakṣmīs") - eight wealth-aspects, popularized by the 20th-c. *Aṣṭalakṣmī Stotra* (U. Ve. Śrīnivāsa Varadāchārya, c. 1970s) (Source: *Aṣṭalakṣmī Stotra*):
  - **Ādi Lakṣmī** (primordial), **Dhana Lakṣmī** (monetary wealth), **Dhānya Lakṣmī** (grain/food), **Gaja Lakṣmī** (elephant-flanked royal abundance — the oldest, Vedic-rooted form), **Santāna Lakṣmī** (progeny), **Vīra/Dhairya Lakṣmī** (courage), **Vijaya/Jaya Lakṣmī** (victory), **Vidyā Lakṣmī** (knowledge).
    - *Variant (Source: Aṣṭalakṣmī Aṣṭottara-śatanāmāvalī):* **Aiśvarya Lakṣmī** replaces **Dhana Lakṣmī**; some lists give **Saubhāgya/Saubhāgya Lakṣmī**. The eightfold scheme is post-medieval/modern, not Purāṇic [late text].
- **Śrī / Mahālakṣmī** - the supreme Lakṣmī; consort of **[Viṣṇu](dashavatara.md)**, born at the **Samudra Manthana** (Source: *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*; *Śrī Sūkta*, Ṛgveda khila).
- **Bhū Devī & Nīḷā/Śrī Devī** - in Śrī Vaiṣṇavism Viṣṇu's two/three consorts: **Śrī** (prosperity), **Bhū** (earth, = Sītā/Āṇḍāḷ), **Nīḷā** (cowherd-grace) (Source: Pāñcarātra / Śrī Vaiṣṇava tradition).
- **Gaja Lakṣmī** - the lustration-by-elephants icon; the single oldest attested Lakṣmī form (Sāñcī, Bharhut reliefs) [scholarly].
- **Ālakṣmī / Jyeṣṭhā** - Lakṣmī's inauspicious elder sister/shadow (misfortune), worshipped to be averted (Source: *Liṅga Purāṇa*; *Padma Purāṇa*) [disputed counterpart].
- **Kamalā** - Lakṣmī as the tenth **[Mahāvidyā](dasha-mahavidya.md)** (the Tantric Lakṣmī).

## Forms of Sarasvatī

- **Sarasvatī** - goddess of speech, learning, music; consort of **[Brahmā](trimurti-and-tridevi.md)**; deified from the Vedic river **Sarasvatī** and the speech-goddess **Vāc** (Source: *Ṛgveda*; *Sarasvatī Rahasya Upaniṣad*).
- **Mahāsarasvatī** - her cosmic *sāttvika* form in the *Devī Māhātmya* (slayer of Śumbha-Niśumbha) (Source: *Devī Māhātmya* 5–13).
- **Śāradā** - Sarasvatī as "she of the autumn / of the vīṇā"; the goddess of **Śāradā Pīṭha (Kashmir)** and of **Śṛṅgerī**; namesake of Ādi Śaṅkara's **[Śāradā Pīṭha](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md)** (Source: regional tradition).
- **Vāgdevī / Brāhmī / Bhāratī** - speech-power epithets; **Brāhmī** is also her Mātṛkā form (Source: *Lalitā Sahasranāma*; *Devī Māhātmya*).
- **Nīla-Sarasvatī** - the Tantric blue form merging with **[Tārā](dasha-mahavidya.md)** (Mahāvidyā) (Source: *Tārā Tantra*).
- **Gāyatrī / Sāvitrī** - the Vedic mother-of-the-Vedas, sometimes a fourth consort of Brahmā and a Sarasvatī-aligned form (Source: *Padma Purāṇa*) [disputed assignment].

## Grāmadevatās — Village Goddesses

The **grāmadevatā** stratum is the living, folk substrate of Devī worship across South and rural India — guardian-mothers of fields and villages who bring rain and avert epidemic. The roster is **effectively unbounded** (one or more per village); the pan-regional figures:

- **Māriyammaṉ (Mariamman)** - "Mother of rain/death"; the chief Tamil village goddess, guardian against **smallpox, chickenpox, cholera** and drought; neem leaves and pots (karagam) are her emblems; often aniconic or a fierce head (Source: Tamil folk tradition; *grāmadevatā* studies).
- **Reṇukā / Yellammā** - the Karnataka–Maharashtra–Telugu mother-goddess; mythically **Reṇukā**, chaste wife of sage **[Jamadagni](saptarishi.md)**, beheaded on his order and restored by her son **[Paraśurāma](dashavatara.md)** — her severed-and-rejoined head (sometimes fused with an outcaste woman's body) is the etiology of **Yellammā / Mariyammaṉ** (Source: *Mahābhārata*/*Purāṇa* Reṇukā tale; South Indian folk variants).
  - *Variant (one goddess, many names):* **Reṇukā = Yellammā = Ekvīrā = Māriyammaṉ = Padmākṣī = Aai** across regions — a single pan-Deccan mother under local names [folk].
  - Associated with the **jogtī/devadāsī** dedication tradition at Saundatti [disputed/contested practice].
- **Śītalā** - North Indian smallpox/pox goddess; rides a donkey, carries broom, winnowing-fan and water-pot; cooling the "heat" of disease (Source: *Skanda Purāṇa*; Bengali *Śītalā Maṅgal*).
- **Mā Mansā / Manasā** - Bengali serpent-goddess, averter of snakebite; daughter of Śiva in folk Maṅgal-kāvya (Source: *Manasāmaṅgal*) [folk].
- **Other major village mothers:** **Gaṅgammā** (Tirupati), **Pochamma / Maisamma** (Telangana), **Aṅkāḷamman / Draupadī-amman** (Tamil cults), **Khaṇḍobā's Mhālsā/Banāī**, **Karṇī Mātā** (Rajasthan, rat-temple) — each with local Māhātmya [folk/regional].
  - *Open-ended:* this class cannot be exhaustively enumerated; the seven-sisters / grāma-Mātṛkā motif recurs region to region.

## Santoṣī Mā — the Modern Goddess

- **Santoṣī Mā** ("Mother of Contentment/Satisfaction") - a goddess of recent popular origin, said to be a daughter of **[Gaṇeśa](ganesha-and-kartikeya.md)**; worshipped via the **Śukravār (Friday) vrata** with jaggery and roasted chickpeas, and an avoidance of sour foods (Source: vrata-kathā pamphlet tradition).
- **Origin** - not found in the Vedas or Purāṇas; emerged through North Indian temple-cult and vrat literature in the 1960s, then propelled to all-India fame by the **1975 film *Jai Santoshi Maa*** — a documented case of modern goddess-formation (Source: *Jai Santoshi Maa* (1975); scholarship on the cult) [late text / modern].
  - *Variant (theological back-fit):* later devotional texts assimilate her to Mahālakṣmī or to the Mahādevī to give Purāṇic legitimacy [disputed].
