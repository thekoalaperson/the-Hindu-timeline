# The Narakas & the Afterlife

> The post-mortem map of the cosmos. This is a roster node of [`02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](./README.md): the hells (Naraka) under Yama's jurisdiction, the soul's journey after death, the ancestral rites that build its subtle body, and the upward path to Svarga and the Pitris. The Narakas sit *below* the seven Patalas of the [14-loka stack](./devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) and are distinct from them — Patala is a splendid Naga realm; Naraka is the realm of punishment. The fullest catalogs come from the [Bhagavata Purana, Skandha 5](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md), the [Garuda Purana](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/garuda-purana.md), the [Manu Smriti](../90-literature-corpus/agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md), and the [Vishnu Purana](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/vishnu-purana.md).

---

## 1. Geography & Jurisdiction of Naraka

- **Location** — Below the seven Patalas, above the Garbhodaka ocean at the bottom of the universe; in the **southern** quarter, the direction of Yama (Dakshina = south = death). (Bhagavata 5.26.5)
- **Nature** — Naraka is a **temporary purgatory**, not eternal damnation. The soul exhausts the karmic fruit of its sins, then is reborn; severe sinners may pass through several hells in sequence before rebirth. (Bhagavata 5.26; Garuda Purana)
- **Lord** — **Yama** (Yamaraja, Dharmaraja, Pitripati), son of Vivasvat (Surya) and twin of Yami/Yamuna; first mortal to die, hence "king of the dead" (RV 10.14). His city is **Yamapura/Samyamani**; his messengers are the **Yamadutas**. (Rig Veda 10.14; Garuda Purana)
- **Scribe** — **Chitragupta**, Yama's recorder, who maintains the **Agrasandhani** ledger of every being's deeds (great and small) and reads the audit at judgment; the law of karma is supreme — "beyond bribes, beyond favoritism." (Garuda Purana)
- **Count is a roster variant, not a fixed number** — texts give **28**, **21**, **7**, or "innumerable thousands." The Bhagavata says the 28 "principal" hells head an open-ended list. [open-ended] (Bhagavata 5.26.7)

## 2. The 28 Narakas of the Bhagavata Purana (Skandha 5.26)

The canonical 28, in the order Shuka recites them to Parikshit, each with its assigned sin:

- **Tamisra** ("darkness") - one who seizes another's wealth, wife, or children; bound, starved, beaten by Yamadutas until he faints. (Bhagavata 5.26.7)
- **Andhatamisra** ("blind darkness") - one who deceives a man and enjoys his wife or children; loses sight and intelligence as if uprooted by the root. (Bhagavata 5.26.9)
- **Raurava** ("terrible") - one who lives for body and family alone at others' expense; the creatures he injured become **rurus** (cruel serpent-beasts) that devour him. (Bhagavata 5.26.10–11)
- **Maharaurava** ("great Raurava") - one who maintains his body by harming others; flesh-eating **kravyada** rurus consume him. (Bhagavata 5.26.12)
- **Kumbhipaka** ("cooked in a pot") - one who cooks animals and birds alive; boiled in oil for as many years as there were hairs on the creatures' bodies. (Bhagavata 5.26.13)
- **Kalasutra** ("thread of time/death") - one who kills a brahmana (or, per text, disrespects parents/elders); roasted on a copper plate hot above and below, hungry and thirsty. (Bhagavata 5.26.14)
- **Asipatravana** ("forest of sword-leaves") - one who deviates from the Vedic/own-dharma path under heretical influence; flogged and slashed by sword-bladed leaves. (Bhagavata 5.26.15)
- **Sukaramukha** ("hog-mouth") - a king or official who punishes the innocent or a brahmana; crushed like sugarcane, screaming. (Bhagavata 5.26.16)
- **Andhakupa** ("blind well") - one who torments lower beings (insects, etc.) without cause; attacked by all the creatures he harmed. (Bhagavata 5.26.17)
- **Krimibhojana** ("worm-food") - one who eats without sharing or performing the five daily sacrifices (pancha-mahayajna); becomes a worm in a 100,000-yojana lake of worms, eating and eaten. (Bhagavata 5.26.18)
- **Sandamsa** ("pincers") - one who robs a brahmana or steals gold/jewels (not in emergency); body torn with red-hot iron balls and tongs. (Bhagavata 5.26.19)
- **Taptasurmi** ("red-hot iron image") - a man or woman in illicit intercourse; forced to embrace red-hot iron figures of the opposite sex. (Bhagavata 5.26.20)
- **Vajrakantaka-salmali** ("thunderbolt-thorn silk-cotton tree") - one who indulges with animals or in excess; impaled on the thunderbolt-like thorns of the Salmali tree. (Bhagavata 5.26.21)
- **Vaitarani** ("river to be crossed") - a kshatriya/official who transgresses bounds; cast into the foul river of pus, blood, urine, hair, bones, gnawed by river-creatures. (Bhagavata 5.26.22)
- **Puyoda** ("pus-water") - shameless men living like animals (shudras per text) and their wives; plunged into an ocean of pus, feces, urine, mucus. (Bhagavata 5.26.23)
- **Pranarodha** ("breath-obstruction") - brahmanas keeping hunting dogs/asses who needlessly kill animals; pierced with arrows as targets. (Bhagavata 5.26.24)
- **Visasana** ("slaughter") - one who sacrifices animals out of pride/for show; cut and whipped by Yama's agents. (Bhagavata 5.26.25)
- **Lalabhaksa** ("saliva-eating") - a twice-born who forces his wife to drink his semen out of lust; thrown into a river of semen and made to drink it. (Bhagavata 5.26.26)
- **Sarameyadana** ("food of Sarama's dogs") - arsonists, poisoners, plunderers, and rapacious officials; devoured by **720** thunderbolt-fanged dogs. (Bhagavata 5.26.27)
- **Avici** ("waveless / no rest") - the false witness, perjurer, and cheat in trade or charity; hurled headfirst from a 100-yojana stone cliff resembling stone waves. (Bhagavata 5.26.28)
- **Ayahpana** ("iron-drink") - a brahmana, his wife, or anyone under vow who drinks liquor; molten iron poured down the throat. (Bhagavata 5.26.29)
- **Ksarakardama** ("acid-mud") - one of low birth who, proud of wealth/birth, fails to honor superiors; tortured head-down in caustic mire. (Bhagavata 5.26.30)
- **Rakshogana-bhojana** ("food of the rakshasa horde") - those who practice human/animal sacrifice for cannibal rites; their victims, now rakshasas, cut and eat them as they did. (Bhagavata 5.26.31)
- **Shulaprota** ("impaled on a spike") - one who betrays/kills the trusting (birds, beasts, or men who took refuge); impaled on lances, pierced by herons and cranes. (Bhagavata 5.26.32)
- **Dandasuka** ("serpent / punishing-fanged") - the envious, snake-natured man who harms others; devoured by five- and seven-hooded serpents. (Bhagavata 5.26.33)
- **Avatanirodhana** ("confinement in a hole") - one who imprisons living beings in dark wells/caves; sealed in dark, poisonous-gas pits. (Bhagavata 5.26.34)
- **Paryavartana** ("turning back / returning the evil eye") - the householder who eyes guests cruelly/with miserly anger; eyes torn out by sharp-beaked vultures, herons, crows. (Bhagavata 5.26.35)
- **Sucimukha** ("needle-mouth") - the proud miser who hoards and suspects everyone of theft; body sewn all over with needles by Yama's agents. (Bhagavata 5.26.36)

> The Bhagavata adds that these are **only the principal** hells; "there are hundreds and thousands" matched to specific sins, through all of which the sinful pass before returning to earth. [open-ended] (Bhagavata 5.26.37)

- *Variant (Devi Bhagavata Purana 8.21–22):* gives the same **28** with minor name-forms — **Taptamurti** for Taptasurmi, **Apahpana** for Ayahpana, **Rakshogana-sambhoja** for Rakshogana-bhojana, **Avatarodha** for Avatanirodhana, **Paryavartanaka** for Paryavartana. (Devi Bhagavata 8; Naraka, Wikipedia)

## 3. The 28 Narakas of the Vishnu Purana (II.6)

A parallel "28" with substantially **different names** (only some overlap), each tied to a sin-class:

- Named list: **Raurava, Sukara (Shukara), Rodha, Tala, Visasana, Mahajwala, Taptakumbha, Lavana, Vimohana, Rudhirandha, Vaitarani, Krimisha, Krimibhojana, Asipatravana, Krishna, Lalabhaksa, Daruna, Puyavaha, Papa, Vahnijwala, Adhahsiras, Sandamsa, Kalasutra, Tamas, Avichi, Svabhojana, Apratishtha,** and a second **Avichi (Aprasitha)**. (Vishnu Purana II.6)
- Representative assignments: **false witness / partiality / lying** → Raurava; **killing a cow / brahmanicide / embryo-killing** → Rodha; **drinking wine, slaying a brahmana, stealing gold, adultery with a guru's wife** → the great hells in sequence; **destroyer of a beehive / sacker of a town** → Taptakumbha; **maker of false weights** → Krimibhojana; **one who reviles the Vedas** → Krimisha. (Vishnu Purana II.6)
- *Note:* the Vishnu Purana frames these as cycled through **before** rebirth and stresses that **repetition of Vishnu's name** annuls the karma sending one to Naraka — a bhakti override of the karmic ledger. (Vishnu Purana II.6)

## 4. The 21 Narakas of the Dharmashastra (Manu & Yajnavalkya)

The Smriti tradition fixes **21** hells; the lists differ between authorities.

- *Variant (Manu Smriti 4.88–90):* **Tamisra, Andhatamisra, Maharaurava, Raurava, Kalasutra, Mahanaraka, Samjivana, Mahavichi (Mahavici), Tapana, Sampratapana, Samhata, Sakakola (Kakola), Kudmala, Putimrittika, Lohasanku, Rijisha, Pathana (Panthana), Vaitarani, Salmali, Asipatravana, Lohadaraka.** Manu warns the wicked endure these before being reborn in degraded wombs. (Manu Smriti 4.88–90)
- *Variant (Yajnavalkya Smriti):* a different **21** — **Tamisra, Lohasanku, Mahaniraya, Salmali, Raurava, Kudmala, Putimrittika, Kalasutraka, Sanghata, Lohitoda, Savisha, Sampratapana, Mahanaraka, Kakola, Sanjivana, Mahapatha, Avichi, Andhatamisra, Kumbhipaka, Asipatravana, Tapana.** (Yajnavalkya Smriti 3)
- *Variant (Mahabharata, "innumerable"):* the epic names many of the same hells but treats the number as indefinite; the **Svargarohana Parva** depicts Yudhishthira's brief tour of Naraka. (Mahabharata, Svargarohana Parva)

> Comparison note: **Tamisra, Andhatamisra, Raurava, Maharaurava, Kalasutra, Asipatravana, Vaitarani** are the stable core shared across Bhagavata / Manu / Yajnavalkya; **Kumbhipaka** is the most famous popularly (the cauldron) and appears in Yajnavalkya and the Garuda but not Manu's 21.

## 5. The Garuda Purana — Sins, Hells & the Soul's Journey

The Garuda Purana (Preta-kanda / Pretakalpa) is the locus classicus for the **after-death itinerary** and is recited during the 13-day mourning. See [`../90-literature-corpus/puranas/garuda-purana.md`](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/garuda-purana.md).

### 5a. The Hells

- The Garuda enumerates a set of hells (popularly cited as **28**, sometimes **21** or "**84**"), each a calibrated punishment: **Kumbhipaka** (boiling oil for killing creatures), **Asipatra/Asipatravana** (sword-leaf forest for Veda-abandoners), **Raurava, Maharaurava, Tamisra, Andhatamisra, Kalasutra, Sukaramukha, Andhakupa, Krimibhoja, Vaitarani, Taptasurmi,** etc., largely paralleling the Bhagavata roster. [folk count "84"] (Garuda Purana, Pretakhanda)

### 5b. The Preta Stage & Pinda Rites

- **Preta** — at death the soul, stripped of its gross body but still carrying hunger, thirst, and desire, becomes a **preta** (disembodied/wandering spirit), suffering until a new subtle body is built. (Garuda Purana)
- **Preta-karma / Pinda-dana** — the family offers **pindas** (balls of rice and sesame) daily from **day 1 to day 10**; each day's pinda constructs one part of the preta's **Pindaja Sharira** ("body made of pindas") — head, neck, shoulders, etc. (Garuda Purana; Antyeshti)
- **Ekoddishta & Sapindikarana** — on **day 11–12** the **sapindikarana** rite merges the individual preta with the three paternal ancestors, dividing the preta's pinda into the ancestral pindas; this graduates the **preta → pitri** (ancestor). (Garuda Purana; Antyeshti)
- **The 13-day to 1-year passage** — after the rites, **Yamadutas** escort the Pindaja-Sharira **southward** to Yamaloka. The journey spans roughly **12 months / ~348 days**, crossing **86,000 yojanas** of barren terrain and the river **Vaitarani**; the soul is sustained only by ongoing **pinda and tarpana** (water libations) from the family. (Garuda Purana; The Vaishnava / Dipika)
- **Vaitarani crossing** — the foul river of blood and filth bars Naraka's threshold; a virtuous soul (or one whose family gifted a cow, *Vaitarani-dana*) crosses by holding the cow's tail; the sinful are dragged through. (Garuda Purana)
- **Judgment** — at Yamapuri, **Chitragupta** reads the Agrasandhani; Yama assigns **Svarga**, a specific **Naraka**, or direct **rebirth** by the net karmic balance. (Garuda Purana)

## 6. Svarga — the Heavens of Merit

- **Svarga / Svarloka** — Indra's heaven on/above Meru, the third of the [upper lokas](./devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md); reward for meritorious (punya) karma, abode of devas, gandharvas, apsaras. (Vishnu Purana; Bhagavata 5)
- **Temporary, like Naraka** — residence in Svarga lasts only "until the merit is exhausted," after which the soul **falls back** to rebirth (Bhagavad Gita 9.21 — *kshine punye martya-lokam vishanti*). Neither heaven nor hell is final; both are stations of samsara. (Bhagavad Gita 9.20–21)
- **Higher heavens** — beyond Svarga lie **Maharloka, Janaloka, Tapoloka, Satyaloka** (Brahmaloka) for sages and yogis; the very highest, **Vaikuntha** (Vishnu) and **Kailasa/Shivaloka** (Shiva) and **Goloka** (Krishna, in Vaishnava texts), are liberation-realms outside the recycled cosmos. (Bhagavata 5; Brahmavaivarta)

## 7. The Pitris & the Two Paths of the Dead

### 7a. Pitriloka & the Classes of Pitris

- **Pitriloka / Pitris** — the realm of the **manes** (deified ancestors), in/near Bhuvarloka and the lunar sphere; ruled with Yama, nourished by shraddha and tarpana. (Vishnu Purana III; Pitri, Wikipedia)
- **Seven classes of Pitris** — three **incorporeal (arupa)** and four **corporeal (sarupa)**:
  - *Incorporeal:* **Vairajas** (highest, dwelling in Santanika loka), **Agnishvattas**, **Barhishads**. (Vishnu Purana III; Pitri)
  - *Corporeal:* **Somapas, Havishmanas (Havirbhujas), Ajyapas, Sukalins (Manasas).** (Vishnu Purana III)
- **Domains (Markandeya/Vishnu):** **Agnishvattas** (sons of Marichi) — manes of the **gods**, in Somapata/Somasada loka; **Barhishads** (sons of Atri) — manes of daityas, danavas, yakshas, gandharvas, nagas, rakshasas, suparnas, kinnaras, in Vibhraja loka; **Somapas** (sons of Bhrigu) — manes of the **brahmanas**; the corporeal classes serve as manes of the other varnas. (Markandeya Purana; Pitri, Wikipedia)
- *Variant (counts):* texts give the Pitris variously as **seven classes** (3+4) or, in some lists, additional named groups; the membership of the corporeal four (esp. Sukalin vs. Manasa) is unstable across Puranas. [disputed] (Pitri, Wikipedia)

### 7b. Devayana vs. Pitriyana — the Two After-Paths

The Upanishads (Chandogya 5.10; Brihadaranyaka 6.2; Gita 8.24–26) distinguish two cosmic routes the dead may take:

- **Devayana** ("path of the gods" / *archiradi-marga*, the bright/northern/solar path) - the way of yogis and knowers of Brahman: through fire, day, the bright fortnight, the six months of the sun's northern course, the sun, lightning → **Brahmaloka**; from there **no return** (krama-mukti). (Chandogya 5.10; Gita 8.24)
- **Pitriyana** ("path of the fathers" / *dhumadi-marga*, the smoky/southern/lunar path) - the way of ritualists and householders of merit: through smoke, night, the dark fortnight, the sun's southern course → the **Pitriloka / Chandraloka** (moon); there they enjoy the fruit of works, then, merit spent, **return to earth** (re-descend via rain → grain → seed → new birth). (Chandogya 5.10; Brihadaranyaka 6.2; Gita 8.25)
- *Variant (Gita 8.26):* names them **shukla** (bright) and **krishna** (dark) gatis — "by the one there is no return, by the other one returns again." (Bhagavad Gita 8.26)
- *Third destiny:* those of neither path (the grossly sinful) take a **third place** — repeated birth and death as lower creatures / direct passage through Naraka without the lunar sojourn. (Chandogya 5.10.7–8)
