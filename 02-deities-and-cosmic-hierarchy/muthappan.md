# Muthappan of Parassinikkadavu — the Hunter-God of Theyyam

> Where this sits in the hierarchy: `02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/` — node in [Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md). A North-Malabar **folk Śaiva-Vaiṣṇava hunter-god** worshipped as a living *theyyam*, read beside the celibate Keralan [Ayyappa / Dharma Śāstā](ayyappa.md), the Tamil village guardian [Ayyaṉār / Śāstā](ayyanar-sastha.md), and the Deccan hunter-warrior [Khaṇḍobā / Mārtāṇḍa Bhairava](khandoba-martanda-bhairava.md), with his Śiva-pole rooted in the fierce manifestations of [Śiva's avatars & forms](shiva-avatars-and-forms.md). [folk] [regional]

**Muthappan** (Malayalam *Muttappan*, "the grand-father / the elder lord") is the great folk deity of **North Malabar** (Kannur–Kasaragod, northern Kerala), worshipped above all at the **Parassinikkadavu Madappura** on the bank of the Valapattanam river. He is unique among the **400+ Kerala theyyams** in being performed as a *theyyam* (the deity-possession ritual, *Kaliyāṭṭam*) **daily** rather than only in the seasonal *kavu* calendar, and in being venerated **across all castes and religions** at a shrine that historically refused Brahmanical mediation. His worship is defiantly non-Sanskritic: he is offered **toddy (*kaḷḷ*), fish, and meat**, and his signature *prasāda* is **fish-curry and boiled black gram (*payaṅkari* / *kaḍala*)**. This file catalogs his dual nature, origin legend, and cult on their own terms, cataloging variants rather than merging them. [folk] [regional]

## Dual nature: Tiruvappana & Vellatam

- **One god, two figures** — Muthappan is theologically a **fusion of Viṣṇu and Śiva** manifested as a paired deity, performed in the Muthappan Theyyam as **two distinct *theyyam* figures appearing together**: **Tiruvappana** (the **Viṣṇu / fisher** aspect) and **Vellatam** (the **Śiva / hunter** aspect). The two are venerated as a single Muthappan. (Parassinikkadavu temple lore; theyyam oral tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **Tiruvappana** — the senior, **Vaiṣṇava fisher-form**, with a large beard-like *mukhatezhuthu* (face-painting) and an imposing tiered headdress; associated with the river and with calm, settled aspect. (Theyyam / Kaliyāṭṭam tradition) [folk]
- **Vellatam** — the **Śaiva hunter-form**, the wilder figure carrying **bow and arrows**, embodying Muthappan's forest, hunting, toddy-and-meat character; the *Vellāṭṭam* is the more energetic dance. (Theyyam / Kaliyāṭṭam tradition) [folk]
- **Why both Hari and Hara** — the pairing makes Muthappan a **Harihara folk deity** of North Malabar, holding together the Vaiṣṇava (fisher/river) and Śaiva (hunter/forest) poles in one cult — structurally akin to, but independent of, the Hariharaputra synthesis behind [Ayyappa](ayyappa.md). (North Malabar folk Śaiva-Vaiṣṇava tradition) [scholarly] [regional]
  - *Variant (single-Śiva reading):* some devotees treat Muthappan simply as **Śiva himself** in hunter guise, with Tiruvappana/Vellatam as two aspects of the one Śiva rather than a Viṣṇu–Śiva pair. [folk] [disputed]
  - *Variant (which form is which):* tellings differ on the exact Viṣṇu/Śiva assignment of Tiruvappana versus Vellatam; the **two-figures-one-god** structure is constant, the precise deity-mapping is not. [folk] [open-ended]

## The origin legend (the foundling hunter-god)

- **The childless Brahmin couple** — at Ayyankara (Ēramam, North Malabar) the wealthy but childless illam-couple **Ayyankara Vazhunnavar** and his wife **Padikutty Amma** pray and perform austerities for a child. (Parassinikkadavu Muthappan lore) [folk]
- **The divine child found** — bathing at the river (or finding the babe while gathering flowers / by the water), **Padikutty Amma discovers a baby boy** and, taking him as the answer to her prayers, the couple raise him as their own. The child is a divine descent, not a mortal son. (Muthappan origin legend) [folk]
- **The wild boy** — as he grows the boy takes to **untamed, non-Brahmanical ways**: roaming the forest, **hunting with bow and arrow**, **drinking toddy**, and **eating meat and fish** — conduct scandalous to his Brahmin foster-household. (Muthappan origin legend) [folk] [regional]
- **The rebuke and the revelation** — when the foster-father (or mother) rebukes him for his wild, caste-breaking habits, the boy **reveals his true divine form** — a self-manifestation (often with a blaze of cosmic light / a *viśvarūpa*-like vision) showing he is **god, not child**. The couple worship him and accept his nature. (Muthappan origin legend) [folk]
- **Departure to Parassini** — Muthappan leaves the household and, by the tale of his **arrow** (shot to find his resting-place) or his wandering, comes to settle at **Parassinikkadavu** on the Valapattanam river, where his principal shrine (*madappura*) stands. (Parassinikkadavu temple lore) [folk] [regional]
  - *Variant (the dog):* Muthappan is closely associated with **dogs**, which roam the Parassinikkadavu shrine freely and are treated as sacred to him — a hunter's-companion motif paralleling the dog of [Khaṇḍobā](khandoba-martanda-bhairava.md); some legends make a dog central to his finding or his journey. [folk] [regional]
  - *Variant (the toddy-tapper):* a strand of the legend has Muthappan befriend or test a **toddy-tapper (Chandan)**, drinking his toddy and rewarding his devotion — anchoring the toddy-offering in the founding story. [folk]

## Non-Brahmanical, all-caste worship

- **A god of all castes and faiths** — Parassinikkadavu Muthappan is famously **open to every caste and to non-Hindus** (Muslims and Christians included); there is **no Brahmin priesthood and no Sanskritic temple protocol** mediating his worship — a deliberate inversion of caste-temple norms. (Parassinikkadavu temple lore) [folk] [regional]
- **The Madappura, not a Brahmin temple** — his shrine is a *madappura*, served by **non-Brahman ritual performers** of the theyyam communities; the deity is approached directly, not through Vedic intermediaries. (Theyyam tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **Hunter-god, low-caste register** — Muthappan's whole iconographic and behavioural register (bow, forest, toddy, meat) marks him as a **deity of the marginalized and the working communities** of North Malabar, in pointed contrast to the vegetarian, Brahman-served Puranic temple gods. (North Malabar folk tradition) [scholarly] [regional]

## Offerings & prasāda

- **Toddy (*kaḷḷ*)** — fermented palm toddy is offered to Muthappan and figures in his cult — an offering unthinkable at an orthodox temple, integral here. (Parassinikkadavu lore) [folk] [regional]
- **Fish & meat** — **fish and meat** are offered to the hunter-god, consistent with his legend; the *theyyam* itself may consume toddy and fish in performance. (Theyyam tradition) [folk]
- **The trademark prasāda** — the distinctive *prasāda* distributed to devotees is **fish-curry and boiled black gram / pulses (*kaḍala*, with grated coconut)** — sometimes called *payaṅkari* — a non-vegetarian temple offering virtually unique in Kerala. (Parassinikkadavu temple lore) [folk] [regional]
- **Free meal (*annadāna*)** — the shrine is also known for feeding all comers; the egalitarian table mirrors the egalitarian deity. [folk]

## Daily theyyam & cult standing

- **Performed daily** — the **Muthappan Theyyam** (here called **Muthappan Thira / Vellattam**) is enacted at Parassinikkadavu **every day** (morning and evening rituals), where most theyyams are **seasonal** (the *Thulam*–*Edavam* / Oct–Jun *kavu* season). This daily performance is exceptional among the 400+ theyyams. (Theyyam tradition; Kerala folklore) [folk] [regional]
- **The performer as living god** — after the song (*tōṟṟam*), face-painting (*mukhatezhuthu*) and costume, the placing of the headdress marks **Muthappan entering the dancer**, who is then venerated as the **living deity** — blessing devotees, dispensing toddy and counsel in the god's person. (Theyyam tradition) [folk]
- **Most prominent of the theyyams** — Muthappan is widely regarded as **the most prominent and most popular of the 400+ Kerala theyyams**, and Parassinikkadavu the most famous Muthappan centre; subsidiary Muthappan shrines spread across North Malabar and the diaspora. (Kerala folklore; Parassinikkadavu temple lore) [folk] [regional]
- **Anjootambalam / Kunnathur Padi** — the **Kunnathur Padi** shrine (Muthappan's forest abode in the legend) and other *madappuras* form a network around the Parassinikkadavu centre; the **Puthari** festival is a major annual occasion. [regional]

## Theological placement (the "two clocks")

- **Scriptural/cyclic clock** — insofar as Muthappan is read as **Viṣṇu-and-Śiva (Harihara) in hunter form**, he attaches to the timeless Śaiva-Vaiṣṇava manifestation register (cf. the fierce forms in [shiva-avatars-and-forms.md](shiva-avatars-and-forms.md)) with **no fixed yuga or Purāṇic date** — a **cross-yuga** folk descent, not a dated event. [scholarly]
- **Academic/historical clock** — the **living Muthappan cult** — the Parassinikkadavu madappura, the daily theyyam, the toddy/fish/meat offerings, the non-Brahman all-caste worship — is a **kali-historical** North-Malabar phenomenon, part of the **pre-Brahmanical Dravidian theyyam substrate** later syncretized with Puranic Viṣṇu/Śiva; its crystallization is **late/medieval and undated by scripture**. [scholarly] [regional]
