# Trimūrti & Tridevī

> Where this sits in the hierarchy: `02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/` — the personified machinery that runs the cosmic clock described in [`../00-time-and-cosmology/`](../00-time-and-cosmology/). The Trimūrti are the three functional faces of the Absolute (Brahman) that drive a Kalpa's create→preserve→destroy cycle; the Tridevī are their consort-energies (Śakti) without which the functions are inert. This node maps the deities, their myths, and the sectarian "who is supreme?" debates that color every Purāṇa.

---

## 1. The Core Concept: Trimūrti and the Three Functions

- **Trimūrti ("three forms")** — the triad in which the cosmic functions of **creation, preservation, destruction** are personified as **Brahmā** (sṛṣṭi/creator), **Viṣṇu** (sthiti/preserver), and **Śiva** (saṃhāra/destroyer–dissolver). The trio is also called the "Hindu triad" or Brahmā-Viṣṇu-Maheśa. (*Trimurti concept, Purāṇic synthesis; classic statement in the Maitrāyaṇīya / late Upaniṣadic and Purāṇic strata*)
  - *Note:* The functions are not three separate Gods in mainstream Vedānta but **three aspects of one Brahman**; the *Maitrī Upaniṣad* and Purāṇic prefaces repeatedly say the one Self assumes the three guṇas — **rajas → Brahmā, sattva → Viṣṇu, tamas → Rudra/Śiva**. (*Maitrī/Maitrāyaṇīya Upaniṣad; Purāṇic guṇa-correlation*)
  - *Variant (Vaiṣṇava):* The single Supreme is **Viṣṇu**, who emanates Brahmā (from his navel-lotus) and Rudra; the Trimūrti is Viṣṇu's threefold self-management. (*Bhāgavata, Viṣṇu Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (Śaiva):* The single Supreme is **Śiva/Maheśvara/Sadāśiva**, who generates Brahmā, Viṣṇu, **and** Rudra as the destroyer-function — i.e. the Trimūrti's "Śiva" is only the destroyer aspect, while the transcendent Sadāśiva is above all three. (*Śiva Purāṇa, Liṅga Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (Śākta):* All three Gods are powerless without **Śakti/Devī**; She is the Supreme and the Trimūrti are her instruments. (*Devī Māhātmya, Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (Smārta/Pañcāyatana):* No one face is supreme; Brahman is worshipped impartially through five (or six) chosen deities. (*Śaṅkara's Smārta synthesis; see §6*)

- **Guṇa / function correspondences** (standard Purāṇic grid):
  | God | Function | Guṇa | Consort (Śakti) | Element/seat | Vāhana |
  |-----|----------|------|-----------------|--------------|--------|
  | Brahmā | Creation | rajas | Sarasvatī | Satya-loka | Haṃsa (swan/goose) |
  | Viṣṇu | Preservation | sattva | Lakṣmī | Vaikuṇṭha / Kṣīra-sāgara | Garuḍa |
  | Śiva | Destruction/dissolution | tamas | Pārvatī | Kailāsa | Nandī (bull) |

## 2. Brahmā — The Creator (Sraṣṭā)

- **Birth from the lotus (Padmanābha / Hiraṇyagarbha)** — Brahmā emerges seated on the lotus that grows from Viṣṇu's navel as Viṣṇu reclines on Śeṣa in the cosmic ocean at the start of each Kalpa. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Bhāgavata Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (Hiraṇyagarbha hymn):* In the older Vedic frame, creation springs from the **Golden Embryo (Hiraṇyagarbha)** floating on the primal waters — later identified with Brahmā. (*Ṛgveda 10.121; Manusmṛti 1*)
  - *Variant (self-born / Svayambhū):* Brahmā is Svayambhū, self-existent, with no progenitor — emphasized where Brahmā is treated as the first cause. (*Manusmṛti 1.5–1.9*)
- **The four heads** — Brahmā originally had five heads; the four surviving faces recite the four Vedas and face the four directions, symbolizing total knowledge. (*Purāṇic iconography*)
  - **Loss of the fifth head** — Śiva, as **Bhairava**, severs Brahmā's fifth head for arrogance (the head spoke insolently / lusted after Brahmā's own daughter); the skull sticks to Bhairava's hand → the Kapālika penance. (*Śiva Purāṇa; Skanda Purāṇa*) [Śaiva-primacy myth]
- **Creation of the Prajāpatis & Manus** — Brahmā emanates the mind-born sons (Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanātana, Sanatkumāra; the Saptarṣi; Marīci, Atri, etc.) and the Manus who govern each Manvantara. (*Manusmṛti; Bhāgavata*) — see [`../01-manvantaras/`](../01-manvantaras/).
- **Incest / Śatarūpā myth** — Brahmā creates the goddess **Śatarūpā (Sarasvatī/Sāvitrī)** and, smitten by her beauty, grows extra heads to keep gazing at her as she circles to avoid him; Śiva curses Brahmā that he will not be worshipped for this. (*Matsya Purāṇa, Skanda Purāṇa, Brahmā Purāṇa*) — a stock explanation for Brahmā's lack of temples.
- **Curse(s) of non-worship** — multiple etiologies for why Brahmā has almost no temple cult:
  - *Variant 1:* Cursed by Śiva over the fifth-head/incest affair. (*Śiva Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant 2:* Cursed by the sage **Bhṛgu** for failing to attend/host his yajña properly. (*Padma Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant 3:* Cursed in the **Lingodbhava** episode for lying about reaching the pillar's top (see §4). (*Śiva Purāṇa, Liṅga Purāṇa*)
- **Pushkar temple** — the principal surviving Brahmā temple is at **Pushkar, Rajasthan**, tied to a yajña Brahmā performed there. (*Padma Purāṇa; living tradition*)
- **Lifespan & "current address"** — Brahmā lives 100 Brahmā-years (~311.04 trillion human yrs); we are in the **1st day of his 51st year**, Śveta-Varāha Kalpa. (Framework constants; *Bhāgavata, Viṣṇu Purāṇa*)

## 3. Viṣṇu — The Preserver (Pālanahartā)

- **Cosmic role** — maintains ṛta/dharma; sleeps in **Yoga-nidrā** on the serpent **Śeṣa/Ananta** atop the milk-ocean (Kṣīra-sāgara) between cycles, with Lakṣmī at his feet. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Bhāgavata*)
- **The Daśāvatāra** — Viṣṇu descends (avatāra) age after age to restore dharma (*"yadā yadā hi dharmasya…"*, Bhagavad Gītā 4.7–8). The standard ten: Matsya, Kūrma, Varāha, Narasiṃha, Vāmana, Paraśurāma, Rāma, Kṛṣṇa, Buddha, Kalki. (*Bhāgavata, Garuḍa Purāṇa, Gītā Govinda*) — each is dated to a yuga; see [`../03-lineages-vamsha/`](../03-lineages-vamsha/) and the yuga files.
  - *Variant:* Some lists swap **Balarāma** for Buddha, or include Kṛṣṇa as source-of-all rather than an avatāra (Gauḍīya: *"kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam"*). (*Bhāgavata 1.3.28; Gauḍīya theology*)
  - *Variant (count):* Lists of **22 or 24 avatāras** appear in the Bhāgavata (1.3), making "ten" a popular abridgement.
- **Samudra-manthana (churning of the ocean)** — devas and asuras churn the milk-ocean (Mt. Mandara as churn, Vāsuki as rope); Viṣṇu as **Kūrma** supports the mountain and as **Mohinī** redistributes the amṛta to the devas. Lakṣmī, Dhanvantari, Halāhala poison, etc. emerge. (*Bhāgavata, Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Mahābhārata Ādi Parva*)
- **Vāmana / three steps** — dwarf avatāra reclaims the three worlds from Bali in three strides. (*Bhāgavata*) — see the Treta/Satya yuga files.
- **Supremacy claims** — in Vaiṣṇava texts Viṣṇu/Nārāyaṇa is the unmanifest source (Para-Brahman) from whom Brahmā and Śiva arise; his "preserver" label understates his actual rank as the Absolute. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Bhāgavata, Nārāyaṇīya of Mahābhārata*)

## 4. Śiva — The Destroyer / Transformer (Saṃhārakartā)

- **Cosmic role** — dissolves the universe at the end of a Kalpa (pralaya) so creation can renew; as **Mahākāla** he is Time-the-devourer, as **Naṭarāja** he dances the Tāṇḍava that ends and re-begins the cosmos. (*Śiva Purāṇa, Liṅga Purāṇa; Naṭarāja iconography, Cidambaram*)
- **Lingodbhava — the pillar of fire** — when **Brahmā and Viṣṇu quarrel over who is supreme**, an endless **Jyotirliṅga** (pillar of fire) appears; whoever finds its end wins. Brahmā (as **haṃsa/swan**) flies up, Viṣṇu (as **Varāha/boar**) digs down; neither finds the limit. Viṣṇu honestly admits defeat; **Brahmā falsely claims** he reached the top, producing the **ketakī/screwpine flower** as a false witness — Śiva curses Brahmā (no worship) and the ketakī flower (barred from his worship). Śiva is thereby established as supreme. (*Śiva Purāṇa, Liṅga Purāṇa, Kūrma Purāṇa, Vāyu Purāṇa*) [Śaiva-primacy myth; the foundational liṅga origin story]
  - *Variant:* In some tellings Viṣṇu also lies/both lie; or the witness is the cow (which lies with its mouth, telling truth with its tail — hence cows worshipped from behind). (*regional/folk variants*)
- **Sati's self-immolation & Dakṣa-yajña** — Śiva's first wife **Satī** burns herself at her father **Dakṣa's** sacrifice after Śiva is insulted; Śiva's grief unleashes **Vīrabhadra**, who destroys the yajña and beheads Dakṣa (later restored with a goat's head). Satī's scattered body parts → the **Śakti Pīṭhas**. (*Śiva Purāṇa, Devī-Bhāgavata, Kālikā Purāṇa*) — bridge into §7.
- **Hālāhala / Nīlakaṇṭha** — Śiva swallows the world-poison from the ocean-churning, holding it in his throat (blue-throated). (*Bhāgavata, Mahābhārata*)
- **Gaṅgā in the hair** — Śiva breaks the fall of the descending Gaṅgā in his matted locks. (*Rāmāyaṇa, Bhāgavata*)
- **Tripurāntaka, Gajāsura, Andhaka, Jalandhara** — Śiva's demon-slaying feats asserting his power. (*Śiva Purāṇa, Liṅga Purāṇa, Matsya Purāṇa*)
- **Ardhanārīśvara** — Śiva fused half-male/half-female with Pārvatī, expressing the inseparability of Śiva (consciousness) and Śakti (energy). (*Śiva Purāṇa, Liṅga Purāṇa*) — a Śaiva ↔ Śākta hinge.
- **Twelve Jyotirliṅgas** — Śiva's chief shrines (Somnath, Mahakaleshwar, Kashi Vishwanath, Rameshwaram, etc.). (*Śiva Purāṇa Koṭirudra Saṃhitā; living tradition*)

## 5. The Tridevī — The Three Consort-Energies (Śakti)

The **Tridevī** are the female counterparts whose Śakti animates the Trimūrti's functions; without consort-energy the male principle is **śava** (a corpse) — a core Śākta doctrine. (*Saundaryalaharī 1; Devī-Bhāgavata*)

- **Sarasvatī** — goddess of knowledge, speech (Vāc), music, the arts; consort of Brahmā; depicted with vīṇā, book, mālā, swan. **Without her, Brahmā cannot order creation** (knowledge precedes form). (*Ṛgveda — Sarasvatī as river/Vāc; Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa; Sarasvatī as Vāgdevī*)
  - *Variant:* Sometimes called consort of **Viṣṇu** in eastern/Vaiṣṇava lists (a "three wives of Viṣṇu — Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī, Gaṅgā" tradition, with quarrels leading to curses redistributing the wives). (*Devī-Bhāgavata, Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa*)
- **Lakṣmī (Śrī)** — goddess of wealth, fortune, prosperity, sovereignty; consort of Viṣṇu; born/re-emerges from the **ocean-churning**; reincarnates as **Sītā** (with Rāma) and **Rukmiṇī/Rādhā** (with Kṛṣṇa). Eight forms = **Aṣṭalakṣmī**. (*Śrī Sūkta of Ṛgveda khila; Viṣṇu Purāṇa; Bhāgavata*)
  - *Note:* In Śrī Vaiṣṇavism Lakṣmī is the inseparable mediatrix (puruṣakāra) between soul and Viṣṇu — near-co-equal, not subordinate. (*Śrī Vaiṣṇava theology, Rāmānuja school*)
- **Pārvatī (Umā/Gaurī)** — goddess of love, devotion, fertility, power; consort of Śiva; reincarnation of **Satī**; daughter of the mountain **Himavān**; wins Śiva through tapas; mother of **Gaṇeśa** and **Kārttikeya/Skanda**. Her fierce expansions are **Durgā** and **Kālī** (see §7). (*Śiva Purāṇa, Skanda Purāṇa, Kālidāsa's Kumārasambhava*)

## 6. Sectarian Primacy — "Who Is Supreme?"

Hinduism is, functionally, a federation of denominations that each crown a different head of the pantheon as the Absolute (Para-Brahman), reading the others as derivative.

- **Vaiṣṇavism — Viṣṇu/Nārāyaṇa (or Kṛṣṇa) supreme.** Brahmā and Śiva are created/empowered by Viṣṇu; mokṣa via bhakti to Viṣṇu and his avatāras. Sub-schools: Śrī Vaiṣṇava (Rāmānuja, Viśiṣṭādvaita), Gauḍīya (Caitanya, Kṛṣṇa svayaṃ-bhagavān), Madhva (Dvaita), Vallabha, Nimbārka. (*Bhāgavata, Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Bhagavad Gītā*)
- **Śaivism — Śiva/Maheśvara supreme.** The transcendent Sadāśiva is above the Trimūrti; the Lingodbhava and fifth-head myths assert Śiva's rank over Brahmā and Viṣṇu. Sub-schools: Kashmir Śaivism (Pratyabhijñā, monist), Śaiva Siddhānta (dualist, Tamil), Vīraśaivism/Liṅgāyat, Pāśupata, Kāpālika, Nātha. (*Śiva Purāṇa, Liṅga Purāṇa, Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad — earliest Rudra-as-Supreme text*)
- **Śāktism — Devī/Śakti supreme.** Adi Parāśakti is the sole ultimate reality; the Trimūrti act only by her power; all goddesses are her faces. The **Devī Māhātmya** ("Caṇḍī/Durgā Saptaśatī", 700 verses, part of *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa*) is the foundational scripture; the **Devī-Bhāgavata** is its full Purāṇa. Sub-streams: Śrīkula (Śrīvidyā/Lalitā Tripurasundarī, Saundaryalaharī) and Kālīkula (Kālī, Tantra). (*Devī Māhātmya; Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Saundaryalaharī*)
- **Smārtism (Pañcāyatana) — impartial / qualified non-dual.** Attributed to **Ādi Śaṅkara**, worship of five deities — **Viṣṇu, Śiva, Devī, Sūrya, Gaṇeśa** (Tamil "**Ṣaṇmata**" adds **Skanda/Kārttikeya** = six) — as equal personal faces of the one impersonal **Nirguṇa Brahman** (Advaita). The worshipper may foreground any one (iṣṭa-devatā) without sectarian exclusivity. (*Śaṅkara tradition; Smārta practice*)
- **The "Harihara" / synthesis position** — Viṣṇu and Śiva are one (Harihara, the fused deity); many Purāṇas explicitly say the quarrel over supremacy is a delusion (māyā) and the Gods are non-different. (*Harihara iconography; conciliatory Purāṇic passages; Ṛgvedic henotheism precedent*)

- **How the texts "play favorites" (variant lens):**
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata/Viṣṇu Purāṇa):* Śiva is a great devotee/servant of Viṣṇu; Vaiṣṇava ranking.
  - *Variant (Śiva/Liṅga Purāṇa):* Viṣṇu worships Śiva, obtains the Sudarśana cakra from him; Śaiva ranking.
  - *Variant (Devī-Bhāgavata):* Both Viṣṇu and Śiva bow to the Goddess; Śākta ranking.
  - These reciprocal subordinations are why **variant policy = catalog all**: no Purāṇa is neutral.

## 7. The Great Goddess — Durgā, Kālī, and the Śakti Spectrum

The "Pārvatī" slot of the Tridevī expands, in Śākta theology, into the **single supreme Devī** of whom every goddess (benign Lakṣmī/Sarasvatī, fierce Durgā/Kālī) is a face.

- **Durgā & Mahiṣāsura-mardinī** — the buffalo-demon **Mahiṣāsura**, unkillable by any male god, is destroyed by **Durgā**, formed from the combined tejas (radiant energies) of all the gods, armed by each with a weapon, riding a lion. (*Devī Māhātmya, ch. 2–3; Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa*) — the central Śākta supremacy myth and the basis of Durgā Pūjā / Navarātri.
- **Kālī from Durgā's brow** — in the battle with **Caṇḍa, Muṇḍa, and Raktabīja**, Kālī springs from Durgā's forehead; she drinks Raktabīja's blood (each drop of which would spawn a clone) to win. (*Devī Māhātmya, ch. 7–8*)
- **Kālī on Śiva** — the standard image of Kālī standing on a supine Śiva: her rampage is halted only when Śiva lies in her path; she steps on him and bites her tongue in abashment. Theologically: **Śakti is dynamic, Śiva is the still ground.** (*Devī-Bhāgavata; Tantric tradition*)
- **Satī → Śakti Pīṭhas** — Satī's dismembered body parts fall across the subcontinent, each becoming a Śakti Pīṭha (51 or 108 depending on the list). (*Kālikā Purāṇa, Devī-Bhāgavata, Tantra-cūḍāmaṇi*)
- **Daśa Mahāvidyā** — ten Tantric wisdom-goddesses (Kālī, Tārā, Tripurasundarī, Bhuvaneśvarī, Bhairavī, Chinnamastā, Dhūmāvatī, Bagalāmukhī, Mātaṅgī, Kamalā) as ten faces of the one Mahādevī. (*Tantric texts; Devī-Bhāgavata*)
- **Identity question — Durgā = Pārvatī?**
  - *Variant (mainstream):* Durgā and Kālī are forms/roles of Pārvatī (same goddess as warrior vs. wife). (*Purāṇic consensus; both addressed as Ambikā*)
  - *Variant (Śākta-strict):* Durgā/Mahādevī is the **primordial** Adi Parāśakti, and Pārvatī is *her* manifestation — i.e. the priority is reversed. (*Devī Māhātmya, Devī-Bhāgavata*)
- **Lalitā Tripurasundarī** — in Śrīvidyā, the supreme Goddess as transcendent beauty, worshipped via the **Śrī Yantra**; her hymn is the **Lalitā Sahasranāma** (*Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*).
