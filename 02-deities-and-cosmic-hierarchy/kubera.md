# Kubera

> Cosmic address: the **lord of wealth** (*Dhanapati*) and **king of the yakṣas**, a second-tier functional deity beneath the [Trimūrti & Tridevī](trimurti-and-tridevi.md) who serves as the **Lokapāla of the North** among the eight directional guardians (see [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md)). Half-brother of Rāvaṇa, friend of Śiva, keeper of the nine *nidhis*. Parent: [02 — Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md) · Master index: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md).

Kubera is the divine **treasurer of the gods** and overlord of all hidden wealth — buried gold, gems, minerals, and the earth's riches. He rose from an ambiguous early-Vedic status (a chief of darkness-spirits) to a respectable Purāṇic deity through severe austerity, won lordship of wealth and a flying chariot from Brahmā, ruled and then lost golden Laṅkā to his demon half-brother Rāvaṇa, and rebuilt his court at **Alakā** in the Himālayas beside Mount Kailāsa. Every attested variant of his parentage, names, family, and treasures is cataloged below as nested bullets.

---

## Names, epithets & etymology

- **Kubera (Kuvera)** — the personal name; folk etymology derives it from *ku* ("bad/deformed") + *vera* ("body"), i.e. "the ill-formed one," referencing his dwarfish, deformed body. An alternative reading takes it as "the one who hides/holds (wealth)." (Wikipedia; Britannica)
- **Vaiśravaṇa (Vaisravana)** — his most famous patronymic, "son of Viśravas/Vaiśravaṇa"; this is the name under which he passes into Buddhism (as a Heavenly King) and Jainism. In the earliest layer it is itself a parent's name. (Wikipedia; Atharvaveda tradition)
- **Dhanapati / Dhanādhipati / Dhaneśa** — "lord of wealth"; **Yakṣarāja(n)** — "king of the yakṣas"; **Guhyādhipa / Guhyakeśvara** — "lord of the hidden (ones)," ruling the *guhyakas* (secret treasure-spirits). (Wikipedia)
- **Rājarāja** ("king of kings"), **Naravāhana** ("borne by a man," after his mount), **Ekapiṅgala / Ekākṣipiṅgala** ("the single tawny-eyed one"), **Aiḍaviḍa / Aiḷaviḷa** (matronymic, "son of Iḍaviḍā/Iḷaviḷā"), **Mayurāja**, **Kinnareśa** (lord of the kinnaras). (Wikipedia; Mahābhārata)

## Vedic & academic two-clock note

- **Scriptural placement** — Kubera is an eternal cosmic officer; his lordship of wealth and the northern quarter is granted by **Brahmā** and is in force across the present **7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara**, with his Laṅkā/Alakā story falling in the **Tretā Yuga** of the Rāmāyaṇa era. (Purāṇic frame)
- **Academic dating** — the figure evolves visibly across texts: the **Atharvaveda** and **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa** present "Vaiśravaṇa Kubera" as a chief of **evil/darkness spirits**; by the epics and Purāṇas he is rehabilitated into a benevolent wealth-god and respectable Lokapāla. The shift from a sinister to an auspicious deity is a classic case of textual stratification. (Wikipedia) [scholarly]

## Parentage & genealogy (variants)

- **Epic version (Mahābhārata):** Kubera is the son of the *prajāpati*-sage **Pulastya** (one of the [Saptarṣi](saptarishi.md)) and **Iḍaviḍā** (Iḷaviḷā), and is the **brother** of the sage **Viśravas**. (Mahābhārata; Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (Purāṇic revision):* later Purāṇas demote him a generation — Kubera becomes the **grandson** of Pulastya, born to **Viśravas** and **Iḷaviḷā/Iḍaviḍā** (daughter of the sage **Bharadvāja**, or of Tṛṇabindu). This is the standard genealogy that makes him Rāvaṇa's half-brother. (Wikipedia; Rāmāyaṇa)
  - *Variant (mother's name):* the mother is given as **Iḍaviḍā / Iḷaviḷā / Iḍaviḍa / Devavarṇinī** across recensions. (Wikipedia)
- **The rākṣasa half-siblings** — Viśravas also married **Kaikasī (Kaikeyī/Nikaṣā)**, daughter of the dānava **Sumālī**; their children are the rākṣasas **Rāvaṇa**, **Kumbhakarṇa**, **Vibhīṣaṇa**, and the sister **Śūrpaṇakhā**. Kubera (the cultured, dharma-keeping son of the brahmin line) is thus the **half-brother** of Laṅkā's demon dynasty. (Rāmāyaṇa; Wikipedia) — see [Lineages](../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md).
- **Wife** — **Bhadrā** ("the auspicious"), also called **Ṛddhi** ("prosperity"), **Kauberī** ("Kubera's lady"), **Cārvī** ("splendour"), and **Yakṣī**; in one account a daughter of the demon **Mura**. (Wikipedia)
- **Children (variants):**
  - *Sons:* **Nalakūbara** ("reed-axle") and **Maṇigrīva** ("jewel-necked"; = **Maṇibhadra**?) are the common pair; other lists add **Mayurāja** ("king of man-like beasts") and **Varṇakavi** ("colourful poet"). (Wikipedia; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.10)
  - *Daughter:* **Mīnākṣī** ("fish-eyed") is named in some sources. (Wikipedia)
  - *Note:* **Maṇibhadra** is elsewhere described as Kubera's **son and chief attendant / general of his army** of yakṣas, distinct from Maṇigrīva. Rosters of Kubera's offspring are not standardized across texts. [flag: open-ended roster]
- **Nalakūbara's curse** — Kubera's son Nalakūbara (with his wife/consort Rambhā) is wronged by **Rāvaṇa**, who violates Rambhā; Nalakūbara curses Rāvaṇa that he can never force any unwilling woman on pain of his head bursting — a curse later credited with sparing **Sītā** in captivity. (Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa)
  - *Variant (Kṛṣṇa cycle):* Nalakūbara and Maṇigrīva, sons of Kubera, are cursed by the sage **Nārada** to become two **arjuna trees**, and are liberated when the infant **Kṛṣṇa**, tied to a mortar, drags it between them and uproots them (the *Yamalārjuna* episode). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.9–10.10)

## How Kubera became the god of wealth — penance & boon

- **The austerity** — Kubera performs **severe tapas** (in some accounts for thousands of years, standing on one leg) to win Brahmā's favour. (Purāṇic tradition)
- **Brahmā's boons** — pleased, **Brahmā** grants Kubera: (a) **immortality** and elevation to the rank of a god; (b) **lordship over all wealth** and the **nine nidhis** (treasures) of the earth; (c) the office of **Lokapāla** — guardian of the **North** quarter; and (d) the **Puṣpaka vimāna**, the self-moving aerial chariot. (Wikipedia; Rāmāyaṇa)
  - *Variant (Śaiva past-life account):* the Śiva Purāṇa instead derives Kubera's rank from a former birth as **Guṇanidhi**, a brahmin's son of Kāmpilya ruined by **gambling** and disowned, who — starving on a **Śivarātri** night — crept into a Śiva shrine to steal the food-offerings and, to see them, fed his torn garment as a wick to the dying **lamp before the liṅga**, unknowingly completing the vrata (fast, vigil, lamp); killed as a thief, he was claimed from Yama's messengers by **Śiva's gaṇas**, reborn as **Dama, king of Kaliṅga** (a devotee of temple lamps), and finally reborn to receive from **Śiva** the name Kubera, lordship of wealth, and the god's friendship — full story at [Guṇanidhi → Kubera](../04-deep-dives/gunanidhi-kubera.md). (Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra-saṁhitā, Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa 17–19)
    - *Note:* popular retellings compress this into "Kubera was a **thief** who robbed a Śiva temple and kept a lamp burning" — a garbling of the gambler-and-naivedya plot. [folk]
- **Friendship with Śiva** — Kubera's court at **Alakā** lies beside **Mount Kailāsa**, and he is counted a close companion and devotee of **Śiva**; this is the basis of much of his later auspicious, Śaiva-aligned worship. (Britannica; Śiva Purāṇa) — see [Śiva Myth-Cycle](shiva-myth-cycle.md).

## Iconography & the deformed body

- **General form** — a **dwarfish, pot-bellied** figure, fair or covered in gold ornaments and gems, holding a **mace (gadā)**, a **money-pot / treasure-pot**, a **citron (or pomegranate)**, and often a **mongoose (nakula)** that vomits jewels. (Wikipedia; Britannica)
- **The deformities** — Purāṇic texts describe him as physically marred: **three legs (tripāda)**, only **eight teeth**, and (in many sources) **one eye** — hence *Ekākṣipiṅgala*, "the one tawny eye." (Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (the lost eye):* the single/tawny eye is explained by an encounter with **Pārvatī** — Kubera gazed at her enviously (or was thought to leer); her glance scorched/withered one of his eyes yellow. As recompense, she renamed the surviving eye and accepted him. (Folk Śaiva tradition) [folk]
- **The man-mount (Naravāhana)** — uniquely among the gods, Kubera's *vāhana* is a **man** (*nara*) — sometimes interpreted as the **State / political power personified** that bears wealth, or simply a man symbolizing dominion. Hence his epithet **Naravāhana**. (Wikipedia)
- **Color & direction** — as the northern guardian he is associated with the colour **gold/yellow** and the substance gold; some traditions instead place him in the East. (Wikipedia)

## Alakā, the Puṣpaka, and the loss of Laṅkā

- **Original lordship of Laṅkā** — Kubera first ruled the golden island-city of **Laṅkā** (built by [Viśvakarmā](vishvakarma-and-tvashtar.md)), bestowed on him as the wealth-god's seat. (Rāmāyaṇa; Wikipedia)
- **Rāvaṇa's usurpation** — Kubera's half-brother **Rāvaṇa**, after his own austerities and boons, drives Kubera out and **seizes Laṅkā**, making it the rākṣasa capital. (Rāmāyaṇa)
- **Alakā / Alakāpurī** — dispossessed, Kubera builds a magnificent new capital, **Alakā (Alakāpurī)**, in the **Himālayas near Kailāsa**, watered by the Mandākinī and lush with gardens (the **Caitraratha** grove); it is the archetypal city of inexhaustible riches, immortalized in Kālidāsa's **Meghadūta** as the cloud-messenger's destination. (Wikipedia; Meghadūta) — see [Sacred Geography](../06-sacred-geography/README.md).
- **The Puṣpaka vimāna** — Kubera's **self-willed flying palace-chariot**, Brahmā's gift, that moves at its owner's thought and expands to any size. (Rāmāyaṇa)
  - **Rāvaṇa steals the Puṣpaka** — Rāvaṇa later assaults Kubera again and carries off the Puṣpaka itself. (Mahābhārata, *Rāmopākhyāna*; Rāmāyaṇa)
  - **Kubera's curse & the return to Rāma** — Kubera curses that the one who **slays Rāvaṇa** shall ride the Puṣpaka away; thus after Rāvaṇa's death **Rāma** flies home to Ayodhyā in the Puṣpaka with Sītā and his retinue — the homecoming celebrated at **Dīpāvalī**. Rāma later restores the vimāna to Kubera. (Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa; *Rāmopākhyāna*) — see [Daśāvatāra](dashavatara.md).
- **The court of Alakā** — Kubera presides over a retinue of **yakṣas** and **guhyakas**, with **gandharvas** (celestial musicians), **kinnaras**, and **apsarās** (nymphs) attending. His **sabhā** (assembly hall) is described in the Mahābhārata (Sabhā Parva) as one of the great divine courts. (Mahābhārata; Wikipedia)

## The nine nidhis (treasures)

- **Definition** — the **Nava-nidhi** ("nine treasures") are the inexhaustible hoards Kubera guards; the canonical list comes from the **Amarakoṣa** of Amarasiṃha. They are sometimes personified as semi-divine attendants. (Amarakoṣa; Wikipedia)
- **The nine (Amarakoṣa list):**
  - **Padma** — the lotus-treasure; often glossed as agricultural / land-and-grain wealth.
  - **Mahāpadma** — "great lotus"; gold and the most precious metals.
  - **Śaṅkha** — the conch-treasure; silver.
  - **Makara** — gems / the wealth of weaponry.
  - **Kaccha­pa (Kacchapa / Kacchhapa)** — the tortoise-treasure; land/real-property wealth.
  - **Mukunda** — pearls / quicksilver.
  - **Kunda** — jewelry / arsenic; honour.
  - **Nīla** — sapphires / blue-gem wealth.
  - **Kharva (Varāha in some lists)** — mixed / mineral wealth; vessels of mingled metals.
  - *Variant (lists & glosses):* the exact members, order, and what each "stands for" differ markedly across sources (Amarakoṣa, Tantric, and South-Indian temple traditions); common alternates include **Varāha** for the ninth. The associations (which nidhi = which commodity) are not fixed. (StotraVeda; Amar Chitra Katha; Wikipedia) [disputed]
- **Śaṅkhanidhi & Padmanidhi as guardians** — the two foremost nidhis, **Śaṅkha** (conch) and **Padma** (lotus), are personified and appear flanking doorways/gateways in **South Indian temple** and **Jain** art as auspicious **dvārapāla**-like figures, their human heads emerging from a conch and a lotus respectively — emblems of abundance and well-being. (DharmaRenaissance; Wikipedia) — see [Artifacts & Symbols](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/README.md).

## Lokapāla — guardian of the North

- **Dikpāla of the North (Uttara)** — Kubera is the **Lokapāla / dikpāla** assigned to the **northern** quarter in the standard set of eight (Indra-E, Agni-SE, Yama-S, Nirṛti-SW, Varuṇa-W, Vāyu-NW, **Kubera-N**, Īśāna-NE). (Wikipedia; Purāṇic frame) — see [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
  - *Variant (eastern association):* a minority tradition instead links Kubera to the **East**. (Wikipedia)
- **Weapons forged from the Sun** — in the Viśvakarmā myth, the shavings trimmed from **Sūrya's** excess radiance are forged into the great divine weapons, including **Kubera's** — tying the wealth-god into the armoury of the gods. ([Viśvakarmā & Tvaṣṭṛ](vishvakarma-and-tvashtar.md); [Sūrya & His Family](surya-and-his-family.md))

## Worship, festivals & later traditions

- **Dhanteras / Dīpāvalī** — Kubera is invoked together with **Lakṣmī** at **Dhanteras** (the wealth-festival opening Dīpāvalī) and in **Kubera-Lakṣmī pūjā**, where he is treated as the *guardian/treasurer* of wealth while Lakṣmī is its *bestower*. (Britannica; popular practice)
- **Kubera yantra & mantra** — in Tantric and modern devotional practice a **Kubera yantra** and the mantra *Oṃ Yakṣāya Kuberāya...* are used to attract prosperity. (folk/Tantric) [folk]
- **Buddhism** — as **Vaiśravaṇa** (and the wealth-form **Jambhala**), Kubera becomes one of the **Four Heavenly Kings (Cāturmahārāja)**, guardian of the **North**, protector of monasteries; he carries a banner and a jewel-spitting mongoose. (Wikipedia) [scholarly]
- **Jainism** — venerated as **Sarvānubhūti / Sarvāhṇa**, the attendant *yakṣa* of the 19th Tīrthaṅkara **Mallinātha**; Śaṅkha- and Padma-nidhi motifs appear in Jain gateway art. (Wikipedia) [scholarly]
