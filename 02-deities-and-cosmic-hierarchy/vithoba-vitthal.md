# Vithoba / Viṭṭhal / Pāṇḍuraṅga of Pandharpur

> Where this sits: a deep-dive within **[Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md)** (master index: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md)). Viṭṭhal is the brick-standing, hands-on-hips form of **Kṛṣṇa / [Viṣṇu](vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md)** (the 8th [Daśāvatāra](dashavatara.md)) enshrined at **Pandharpur** on the Bhīmā (Candrabhāgā) river — the *kula-daivat* (clan deity) of Maharashtra and the heart-deity of the **Vārkarī** *bhakti* movement.

Viṭṭhal (also **Viṭhobā**, **Viṭhṭhal**, **Pāṇḍuraṅga**, **Pāṇḍurang**, **Viṭṭhalanātha**, **Pāṇḍharināth**) is a regional Marathi-Kannada form of Viṣṇu-Kṛṣṇa whose worship is inseparable from the *sant*-poets who sang him. **Two clocks:** scripturally he is Kṛṣṇa himself, *nitya* and yuga-spanning (Dvāpara-rooted); academically the Pandharpur cult is a **Kali-historical** South-Deccan phenomenon, with the standing image and the Vārkarī sant-lineage attested from roughly the 11th–13th c. CE onward [scholarly].

---

## Names and etymology

- **Viṭṭhal / Viṭhobā** — etymology genuinely disputed. Candidates: (a) from **Viṣṇu** via Kannada/Marathi phonetic softening (*Viṣṇu → Viṭṭhu → Viṭṭhal*); (b) from **viṭ** ("brick") + **ṭhā** ("to stand"), "he who stands on a brick" — the *folk* etymology tied directly to the Puṇḍalīka legend; (c) from **viṭ** ("ignorant/the lowly") + **ṭhā**, "he who accepts/stands by the lowly." The affectionate suffix **-obā / -bā** ("father, dear one") gives **Viṭhobā**. [disputed]
- **Pāṇḍuraṅga (Pāṇḍurang)** — Sanskrit "the white/pale-limbed one" (*pāṇḍu*). Paradoxical, since the deity's image is **black stone**; harmonized either as a Śaiva-substratum trace (Pāṇḍuraṅga as an epithet of a pale, ash-smeared Śiva later Vaiṣṇavized) or as referring to the *form*, not the colour. [scholarly / disputed]
- **Vārī / Vārkarī** — *vārī* = "the pilgrimage/turn"; a **Vārkarī** is "one who makes the *vārī*," i.e. the recurring Pandharpur pilgrimage; the term names the whole *sampradāya*. See lineage context in [`../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md`](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md).
- **Rakhumāī / Rukmiṇī** — his consort, enshrined in a separate adjoining shrine; locally **Rakhumāī**, theologically **Rukmiṇī**, Kṛṣṇa's queen and a form of **[Lakṣmī](lakshmi.md)**.

## Iconography — the brick-stander

- **The standing pose (samacaraṇa / kara-kaṭāvalambita).** Viṭṭhal stands erect and frontal on a **brick (vīṭ)**, both **hands resting on the hips** (*kaṭi* — "hands-on-hips" / *kara-kaṭāvalambita*), an unusually static, almost waiting posture unique among Viṣṇu images. He holds a **conch (śaṅkha)** in the left and a **lotus / cakra** in the right (attribute-set varies by description), wears the **kaustubha** and the *makara-kuṇḍala* earrings, and a tall conical crown sometimes read as a **Śiva-liṅga**. (Pandharpur temple tradition; *Pāṇḍuraṅga-māhātmya*)
  - *Variant [scholarly], the syncretic reading:* the liṅga-like crown, the cowherd associations, and the Pāṇḍuraṅga ("pale, ash-smeared") name lead several scholars to argue an underlying **pastoral hero / Śaiva or Vīraśaiva substratum** later absorbed into Vaiṣṇavism — Viṭṭhal as a deity who **unites Śiva and Viṣṇu** in Marathi devotion. [disputed]
  - *Variant [folk]:* the deity stands "**akimbo**" because he is *waiting* — patiently posed for his devotee Puṇḍalīka to finish serving his parents (see below). The pose is read devotionally, not just iconographically.

## The Puṇḍalīka legend — origin of the brick

- **Puṇḍalīka the devoted son.** **Puṇḍalīka (Puṇḍarīka)**, a once-wayward man transformed into the model of *mātṛ-pitṛ-bhakti* (service to parents), is so absorbed in tending his aged mother and father that when **Viṭṭhal (Kṛṣṇa / Viṣṇu)** himself arrives to grace him, Puṇḍalīka — unwilling to interrupt his service — **tosses a brick** out the door for the Lord to stand on and wait. Pleased by this supreme filial devotion, the Lord **remains standing on that brick to this day** at Pandharpur. (*Pāṇḍuraṅga-māhātmya*, in the *Skanda Purāṇa* / *Padma Purāṇa* recensions; Vārkarī hagiography)
  - *Variant (catalog):* in some tellings the Lord comes specifically because Puṇḍalīka's service has made him *greater than the gods*; in others Viṭṭhal arrives from **Dvārakā** seeking Rukmiṇī (see next section), and the Puṇḍalīka episode is woven into that quest. Traditions are kept distinct, not merged. [regional]
- **Theology of the brick.** The brick (*vīṭ*) is the visible token that **bhakti binds God in place** — the devotee's love literally *halts* the otherwise free-moving Lord. It anchors the "hands-on-hips, waiting" iconography to a narrative cause.

## Viṭṭhal–Rukmiṇī theology

- **Kṛṣṇa-and-Rukmiṇī, not Rādhā.** Pandharpur's divine couple is **Viṭṭhal and Rukhumāī (Rukmiṇī)** — the *queen*, not Rādhā — distinguishing the Vārkarī Kṛṣṇa from the Braj / Gauḍīya Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa devotion. Rukmiṇī, as Kṛṣṇa's chief queen, is a manifestation of **[Lakṣmī](lakshmi.md)**. (Pandharpur temple tradition; abhaṅga corpus)
  - *Variant (catalog):* a popular cycle holds that Rukmiṇī, angered, withdrew to nearby **Diṇḍīrvan**, and Viṭṭhal followed her to Pandharpur — explaining the **two separate shrines** (Viṭṭhal's and Rakhumāī's) within the temple complex. [regional / folk]
- **Mode of descent.** Vārkarī theology treats Viṭṭhal as the full **Kṛṣṇa-avatāra** ([Daśāvatāra](dashavatara.md), 8th) standing in arcā (image) form — a *para/arcā* presence in the [Vyūha/Arcā schema](vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md) made locally accessible. Caste and learning are declared irrelevant to his *darśan* — the egalitarian core of the *sampradāya*.

## The Pāṇḍuraṅga-māhātmya

- **The sthala-purāṇa text.** The **Pāṇḍuraṅga-māhātmya** ("Greatness of Pāṇḍuraṅga") is the Sanskrit glorification of the Pandharpur *kṣetra*, transmitted as a section of the **Skanda Purāṇa** and (in a separate recension) the **Padma Purāṇa** [late text]. It narrates the Puṇḍalīka legend, the sanctity of the **Candrabhāgā** (Bhīmā) river, and the merit of *vārī* and *darśan*.
  - *Variant (catalog):* the Skanda and Padma recensions differ in detail, and several later Marathi *māhātmya* retellings exist; it is sthala-glorificatory rather than a unified critical edition. [scholarly / late text]
- **Function.** Like other *māhātmyas*, it sacralizes a living pilgrimage site by retro-fitting it into Purāṇic frame — the textual half of the "two clocks."

## The Vārkarī sant lineage and the abhaṅga corpus

The Vārkarī *sampradāya* is defined less by a founding ācārya than by a **garland of poet-saints** (*sants*) across c. 13th–17th c., all singing Viṭṭhal in **Marathi *abhaṅgas*** (devotional couplet-songs). Roster (kept in conventional sequence; biographies in [`../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md`](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md)):

- **Jñāneśvar / Jñāndev (Dnyāneshwar, c. 1275–1296 CE).** The fountainhead. Author of the **Jñāneśvarī (Bhāvārtha-dīpikā)** — a Marathi *ovī*-verse commentary on the *Bhagavad Gītā* — and of *abhaṅgas* and the **Haripāṭh**. Took *sañjīvana samādhi* at **Āḷandī**. [scholarly]
- **Nāmdev / Nāmadeva (c. 1270–1350 CE).** The tailor-saint of Pandharpur whose *abhaṅgas* spread the cult; uniquely, **61 of his hymns enter the Sikh *Guru Granth Sāhib***, carrying Viṭṭhal-devotion into the Punjab. [scholarly]
- **Eknāth (c. 1533–1599 CE).** Of **Paiṭhaṇ**; restored a critical text of the **Jñāneśvarī**, composed the **Eknāthī Bhāgavata** (Marathi rendering of *Bhāgavata* Bk XI) and the **Rukmiṇī-svayamvara**, and championed social inclusion. [scholarly]
- **Tukārām (c. 1598/1608–1649/1650 CE).** Of **Dehu**; the emotional and poetic summit, author of the **Tukārām Gāthā** (several thousand raw, plain-Marathi *abhaṅgas*). See the dedicated node [`../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/tukaram.md`](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/tukaram.md). [scholarly]
- **Broader garland:** also counted — **Cokhāmeḷā** (the Mahar "untouchable" saint, whose inclusion dramatizes the movement's anti-caste ethic), **Janābāī** (Nāmdev's maidservant-poet), **Sāvtā Māḷī**, **Gorā Kumbhār**, **Nivṛttināth**, **Sopān** and **Muktābāī** (Jñāneśvar's siblings), and **Sena Nhāvī**. The tradition prizes that its saints span every caste and both sexes. [scholarly]
- **The abhaṅga.** *Abhaṅga* ("unbroken") = a Marathi devotional metre/song sung in *kīrtan* and on the march; the collective **abhaṅga corpus** (Jñāneśvarī, Nāmdev, Eknāth, Tukārām Gāthā, Janābāī, etc.) is the living scripture of the Vārkarīs, supplementing the Sanskrit māhātmya.

## The Pandharpur Wārī (pilgrimage)

- **The Wārī / Vārī.** Twice yearly — chiefly on **Āṣāḍhī Ekādaśī** (Jun–Jul) and **Kārttikī Ekādaśī** (Oct–Nov) — hundreds of thousands to **over a million** pilgrims (*vārkarīs*) walk to Pandharpur for Viṭṭhal's *darśan*, singing *abhaṅgas* the whole way. [scholarly / living tradition]
- **The pālkhī processions.** Foot-processions carry the **pādukās** (sandals) of the sants — most famously **Jñāneśvar's pālkhī from Āḷandī** and **Tukārām's pālkhī from Dehu** — converging on Pandharpur in great walking columns (*diṇḍīs*), an organizational form crystallized in the **18th c.** under **Haibatrāo (Haibatbābā)**. [scholarly]
- **At the goal.** Pilgrims bathe in the **Candrabhāgā (Bhīmā)**, take the *pad-sparśa darśan* (touching the feet), and chant **"Viṭṭhal Viṭṭhal!"** and **"Jñānobā-Tukārām!"** — the egalitarian, congregational climax that has made Pandharpur the *bhū-Vaikuṇṭha* ("Vaikuṇṭha on earth") of Maharashtra.
