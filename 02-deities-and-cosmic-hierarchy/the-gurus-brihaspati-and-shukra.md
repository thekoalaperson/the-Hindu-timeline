# Bṛhaspati & Śukra — the Two Gurus

> The two preceptors who balance the cosmos: **Bṛhaspati** (Jupiter), guru of the *devas*, and **Śukra / Śukrācārya** (Venus), guru of the *asuras* and keeper of the death-defeating *mṛtasañjīvanī* lore. Parent: [02 — Deities and Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md). See also the [Navagraha](navagraha.md), the [Saptarishi](saptarishi.md), [Devas, Asuras and the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md), and the master index ([../README.md](../README.md)).

The cosmic order runs on a deliberate *symmetry*: the gods and the anti-gods each have a brahmin preceptor of equal rank, so that neither side can be exterminated and the churning tension that drives the world (creation, the Samudra Manthana, the unending deva–asura wars) is sustained. Bṛhaspati's wisdom serves *dharma* and the sacrifice; Śukra's wisdom serves the asuras yet is, paradoxically, the greater single power — for he alone holds the *mṛtasañjīvanī vidyā* that raises the slain. The two are also the two great **benefic grahas** of Jyotiṣa: Guru (Jupiter) and Śukra (Venus). Note the **two clocks** below: a scriptural genealogy (Vedic → Puranic) and an academic/astronomical layer.

---

## Bṛhaspati — guru of the devas, Jupiter

- **Vedic Bṛhaspati / Brahmaṇaspati** — in the Ṛgveda the "Lord of the brahman (sacred utterance/prayer)," the divine purohita who chants the gods to victory; born of the first great light, he drives away darkness and carries a bow whose string is **Ṛta** (cosmic order, the root of dharma) (Ṛgveda, esp. RV 4.50; he and Brahmaṇaspati merge over the Vedic period).
- **Identified with Agni and with the priesthood** — early texts equate or pair him with **Agni** (fire) and cast him as the archetype of the sacrificing brahmin; he is the *purohita* of the gods (Vedic literature). [scholarly: Vedic Bṛhaspati is a *deity of prayer*, only later astralized as the planet]
- **Son of Aṅgiras** — patronymic **Āṅgirasa**; one of the three sons of the ṛṣi **Aṅgiras** (a mind-born son of Brahmā) and **Śraddhā** (Bhagavata; Puranic genealogies). This makes Bṛhaspati a **Saptarṣi-line sage** as well as a god — see [Saptarishi](saptarishi.md).
  - *Variant (Puranic birth):* Śraddhā was unfaithful during pregnancy, causing a stillbirth; Aṅgiras forgave her and infused his own life into the child, who became the wise Bṛhaspati ([folk]/Puranic vrata-katha tradition).
- **Greatest natural benefic / Guru graha** — as the planet **Jupiter** he is the chief benefic of Jyotiṣa, kāraka of wisdom, dharma, children, wealth, gurus and priests; presides over **Thursday (Guruvāra)**, direction NE, gem yellow sapphire, grain chana. See [Navagraha](navagraha.md).
- **Vāhana & iconography** — golden-yellow, riding an **elephant** (Airāvata-type) or a chariot drawn by eight pale/yellow horses (called *Aśva* or *Gītā* in some lists); holds a daṇḍa, akṣamālā and kamaṇḍalu (Puranic dhyāna verses).
- **Wives and children** — consort **Tārā** ("star"); a wife **Śubhā/Śubhāṅgī** (seven daughters incl. Bhānumatī, Haviṣmatī, Sinīvālī, Rākā in some lists); and **Mamatā**, by whom (and by complication) come **Bharadvāja** and the disputed **Dīrghatamas/Kacha** material (Puranic genealogies; lists vary). [open-ended roster — wife and child lists differ widely across Puranas]
- **Author-figure of niti and the "Bārhaspatya"** — credited with a lost **Bārhaspatya** arthaśāstra/nīti tradition; ironically his name is also attached (probably pseudonymously) to the materialist **Cārvāka/Lokāyata** school ("Bṛhaspati sūtra") — a deliberate inversion casting the gods' guru as patron of skeptics (later doxographies). [late text][disputed]
- **Counsellor of Indra** — throughout the epics and Puranas he is Indra's adviser and the devas' war-priest; his withdrawal or curse repeatedly imperils the gods (see the Tārā affair and the Nahuṣa/Indra cycles).

## Śukra (Śukrācārya / Uśanas Kāvya) — guru of the asuras, Venus

- **Son of Bhṛgu** — patronymic **Bhārgava**; son of the ṛṣi **Bhṛgu** (a Saptarṣi / Prajāpati) and **Kāvyamātā/Puloma/Uśanā** in varying accounts — hence his Vedic name **Uśanas Kāvya** (Ṛgveda; Mahābhārata). A Bhṛgu-line brahmin, structurally the asuras' answer to the Aṅgirasa Bṛhaspati. See [Saptarishi](saptarishi.md).
- **"Śukra" — the bright/clear one, and "semen"** — the Sanskrit *śukra* means "bright, white, clear" (the brilliant evening/morning star) and also "semen," a pun the Śiva Purana exploits in his rebirth myth (see Mritasanjivani episode below).
- **Guru and purohita of the asuras** — preceptor, priest and statecraft-counsellor of the daityas/dānavas; teacher of warfare, polity and dharma to the anti-gods, repeatedly reviving them so the deva–asura wars never end (Mahābhārata; Bhāgavata; Matsya).
- **Keeper of the Mṛtasañjīvanī vidyā** — the unique mantra/science that restores the dead to life, his decisive cosmic advantage; the devas have no equivalent, which is why Bṛhaspati schemes to steal it (see Kacha episode).
- **Greatest benefic after Jupiter / Śukra graha** — the planet **Venus**, kāraka of love, beauty, art, music, fertility, luxury, vehicles and marriage; presides over **Friday (Śukravāra)**, direction SE, gem diamond, grain white beans. See [Navagraha](navagraha.md).
- **Vāhana & iconography** — often one-eyed (see Vāmana episode); white-complexioned, riding a **horse** (or a chariot, or in some lists a crocodile/makara); holds daṇḍa and kamaṇḍalu (Puranic dhyāna verses).
- **Śukra-nīti** — a treatise on statecraft, kingship and ethics is ascribed to him (the *Śukranīti / Uśanas-nīti*), making him the asuras' political philosopher; much of the surviving text is later. [late text]
- **Daughter Devayānī; son Caṇḍa(?)** — daughter **Devayānī** (central to the Kacha and Yayāti cycles), who marries King **Yayāti**, founding part of the [Lunar (Candravaṃśa) lineage](../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md). His wife is generally **Jayantī** (Indra's daughter) and/or **Ūrjasvatī** (lists vary). [open-ended roster]

## The Tārā affair & the birth of Budha

The most famous Bṛhaspati myth turns on the abduction of his wife **Tārā** by **Candra (Soma)**, the Moon — a scandal that splits the cosmos and produces the planet **Budha** (Mercury).

- **The abduction** — **Candra (Soma)**, infatuated, carries off (or seduces) **Tārā**, the wife of his own teacher Bṛhaspati. Refusing all demands to return her, Soma triggers a cosmic war (Padma Purana — the fullest account; Vishnu, Bhāgavata, Devī Bhāgavata).
- **The Tārakāmaya War (Tārā-battle)** — the heavens divide: **Bṛhaspati, Indra and the devas** demand Tārā's return on one side; **Soma, Śukra and the asuras** (with **Rudra/Śiva** as Soma's old preceptor backing him) on the other — a rare line-up that pits Śukra and even Śiva *against* the devas. Brahmā intervenes to halt the devastation and forces Tārā's return (Padma Purana; Devī Bhāgavata, "Birth of Budha").
  - *Variant (Śiva's role):* Śiva sides with Soma because Soma had studied under him, drawing a near-universal war until Brahmā's mediation; in other tellings Śiva is neutral and only Śukra leads the asuras [disputed].
- **The contested child** — Tārā returns pregnant. Bṛhaspati and Soma both claim the radiant child; pressed by Brahmā (or by the child himself), Tārā confesses the father is **Soma**. The boy is named **Budha** ("the wise," planet **Mercury**) (Vishnu Purana; Bhāgavata 9; Devī Bhāgavata).
- **Founding of the Lunar Dynasty** — Budha weds **Ilā/Ilā-Sudyumna** and fathers **Pururavas**, first king of the **Candravaṃśa** (Lunar Dynasty) — so this adulterous union seeds an entire royal lineage. See [Lineages (Vaṃśa)](../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md) and [Navagraha](navagraha.md) on Budha.
  - *Variant (consent):* some Puranas frame the affair as seduction/mutual; others (Devī Bhāgavata) as outright abduction and even rape of Tārā, with Budha's parentage the same in either case [folk/variant].
  - *Variant (Tārā the goddess):* this Tārā (Bṛhaspati's wife) is sometimes conflated with the Mahāvidyā Tārā — they are distinct; see [Daśa Mahāvidyā](dasha-mahavidya.md) [disputed conflation].

## The Kacha episode — stealing the Sañjīvanī

A counter-intrigue: with Śukra reviving the asuras after every battle, the devas send **Kacha** to steal the *mṛtasañjīvanī vidyā* from him. Told fully in the **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva** (and in the Matsya & Agni Puranas).

- **The mission** — **Kacha**, handsome son of **Bṛhaspati**, becomes Śukra's disciple, vowing a thousand years' service, secretly to learn the life-restoring science the asuras alone possess (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva).
- **Devayānī's love** — Śukra's daughter **Devayānī** falls in love with Kacha; their closeness makes the asuras suspect his true aim.
- **Three killings** — the asuras murder Kacha to stop him; twice Devayānī begs her father and Śukra revives him with the Sañjīvanī. The third time they **burn his body, mix the ash into wine, and feed it to Śukra himself**, so reviving Kacha would kill the guru.
- **The clever reversal** — to satisfy Devayānī, Śukra teaches the mantra to Kacha *from inside his own belly*, then revives Kacha — who bursts out (killing Śukra) and at once uses the freshly-learned Sañjīvanī to restore his teacher. Thus Kacha obtains the secret through the asuras' own folly (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva).
- **Śukra bans wine for brahmins** — disgusted at having been tricked through liquor, Śukra decrees that no brahmin shall henceforth drink intoxicants — an etiological "first prohibition" (Mahābhārata).
- **The mutual curses** — Kacha refuses to marry Devayānī, declaring that since he was reborn *from her father's body* she is now his sister. Furious, **Devayānī curses** that the vidyā he stole will never work *in his own hands*; **Kacha counter-curses** that no sage's son will marry her — driving her, later, to wed the kṣatriya king **Yayāti** (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva). This launches the Yayāti–Devayānī–Śarmiṣṭhā cycle of the Lunar line.

## Śukra and Bali — the lost eye (Vāmana)

- **Warning Bali** — when **Viṣṇu as Vāmana** comes begging "three paces of land" from the asura king **Mahābali**, Śukra (Bali's family guru) sees through the dwarf and warns Bali not to grant the boon, knowing it will cost him the three worlds (Bhāgavata 8; Vāmana Purana).
- **Blocking the spout** — when Bali insists on giving the gift anyway, Śukra shrinks himself and lodges in the spout of the water-pot (*kamaṇḍalu*) to stop the consecrating water from flowing, which would void the vow.
- **Pierced by Vāmana** — Vāmana (or his grass blade) thrusts a stalk of **kuśa/darbha grass** into the spout to clear it, **piercing and blinding Śukra's eye** — hence Śukra's iconographic association with one eye. The water flows, the gift is sealed, and Vāmana grows to span the worlds (Bhāgavata 8; folk retellings).
  - *Variant:* Śukra enters as an insect to block the spout and is poked out; details of which eye and the implement vary [folk].
- **Reviving the fallen asuras** — across the deva–asura wars and the Samudra Manthana cycle, Śukra repeatedly uses the Sañjīvanī to raise slain asuras, the structural reason the gods seek the churning's amṛta and Kacha's theft. See [Devas, Asuras and the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).

## Śukra, Śiva, and the origin of the Sañjīvanī

- **The penance in Kāśī** — to gain a power the devas lack, Śukra performs extreme tapas to **Śiva** at Vārāṇasī for thousands of years (the Śiva Purana gives 5,000 years of worship of the liṅga, then a thousand years inhaling husk-smoke head-down) (Śiva Purana, Rudra-saṃhitā 2.5.50).
- **Swallowed and reborn as "semen"** — pleased, Śiva tells Śukra to enter his belly; Śukra dwells inside, then is expelled through Śiva's organ as semen — *śukra* — fixing his name and granting him the **Mṛtasañjīvanī mantra** (Śiva Purana 2.5.50). [late text]
  - *Variant:* Pārvatī, seeing the youth emerge, treats him as her son; in some tellings she swallows him or keeps him until released, layering Śukra's "son of Śiva" status onto his Bhārgava parentage [variant].
  - *Variant (acquisition):* other accounts credit the Sañjīvanī to his Bhārgava heritage or to penance, not the swallowing myth; the texts disagree on the source [disputed].

## The two gurus as a cosmic pair

- **Structural balance** — Bṛhaspati (Āṅgirasa) and Śukra (Bhārgava) are deliberately matched brahmin preceptors so devas and asuras remain evenly armed; their rivalry is the engine of the recurring wars, not an accident.
- **Asymmetry of power** — yet Śukra holds the Sañjīvanī and Bṛhaspati does not, tilting the balance toward the asuras and motivating the gods' counter-schemes (Kacha; the quest for amṛta).
- **Two great benefics** — in Jyotiṣa, Jupiter and Venus are the two natural benefics, jointly governing the auspicious; their teacher-roles map onto their astrological significations (dharma/wisdom vs. desire/art). See [Navagraha](navagraha.md).
- **Bṛhaspati impersonated** — in several myths a deva or sage disguises as Bṛhaspati (or the real Bṛhaspati defects/curses) to mislead the asuras while Śukra is away — a recurrent trope of the guru as the side's irreplaceable strategic asset (Bhāgavata; Matsya). [folk/variant]
