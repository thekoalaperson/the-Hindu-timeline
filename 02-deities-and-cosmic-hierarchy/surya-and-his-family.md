# Sūrya & His Family

> Cosmic address: the visible solar deity of the present cosmos and the divine head of a vast family — death-god, river-goddess, physician-twins, the progenitor of the human race, and the karmic judge. Sūrya is the chief luminary of the [Navagraha](navagraha.md), one of the twelve [Adityas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md), and the apical ancestor of the [Sūryavaṃśa solar dynasty](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md). Parent: [02 — Deities and Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md). Master index: [the Hindu Timeline](../README.md).

The Sun is worshipped in Hinduism on **two registers at once**: as a physical, life-giving star (the Vedic Sūrya/Savitṛ of the [Gāyatrī mantra](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md)) and as a personal deity with a full domestic mythology — a wife who cannot bear his heat, a shadow-double, a divine father-in-law who literally machines down his brilliance, and a brood of children who become some of the most important figures in the entire mythos. This file gathers that household.

---

## The Sun-god: names, forms, and Vedic identity

- **Sūrya / Sūrya Nārāyaṇa** — the Sun as personal god: visible Brahman, "the eye of the world" (*jagataḥ cakṣuḥ*), the *ātmakāraka* (significator of the soul) among the grahas. Riding a chariot of **seven horses** (or one seven-headed horse) driven by **Aruṇa** (Ṛg Veda; Purāṇas). See [Navagraha § Surya](navagraha.md).
- **Vivasvat / Vivasvān** — the oldest name (Ṛg Veda), "the shining-forth one"; the *primordial sacrificer* and father of Manu and Yama. In the Vedic layer Vivasvat is the proper name; "Sūrya" becomes the dominant Puranic name later (Ṛg Veda 10.17; Bṛhaddevatā).
- **Savitṛ** — the "impeller," the solar power that sets all in motion, addressed by the **Gāyatrī** (Ṛg Veda 3.62.10: *tat savitur vareṇyam...*); sometimes distinguished from Sūrya as the dawn/dusk aspect vs. the risen disk. [scholarly] Vedic hymns treat Sūrya, Savitṛ, Mitra, Pūṣan, Bhaga and the Adityas as overlapping solar functions, not one fixed person.
- **The Twelve Adityas (Dvādaśa Aditya)** — Sūrya counted as twelve solar forms, one per month, sons of **Aditi** and Kaśyapa: rosters vary (Viṣṇu Purāṇa; Bhāgavata 6). See [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
  - *Variant (roster):* a common list is Dhātṛ, Mitra, Aryaman, Rudra/Śakra, Varuṇa, Aṃśa, Bhaga, Vivasvat, Pūṣan, Savitṛ, Tvaṣṭṛ, Viṣṇu (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.15); other Purāṇas substitute names — [flag: roster open-ended].
- **The Twelve Names (used in worship)** — Mitra, Ravi, Sūrya, Bhānu, Khaga, Pūṣan, Hiraṇyagarbha, Marīci, Āditya, Savitṛ, Arka, Bhāskara — chanted in the twelve **Sūrya-namaskāra** mantras (see below). [late text] formalized in yogic/devotional practice.
- **Iconography** — uniquely depicted in **boots** and a north-Iranian (Śaka) tunic, holding two lotuses, attended by **Daṇḍa** and **Piṅgala** and the archer-goddess **Uṣā/Pratyūṣā** who shoot away darkness; charioteer **Aruṇa** legless before him. The "booted Sūrya" reflects Iranian (Mithra/Mitra) solar-cult influence on the Maga Brahmin priesthood. [scholarly]

## Vivasvat / Sūrya's wives — Saraṇyū, Sañjñā & Chhāyā

- **Saraṇyū (Vedic) = Sañjñā (Puranic)** — Sūrya's chief wife, daughter of the divine craftsman **Tvaṣṭṛ** (Vedic) / **Viśvakarmā** (Puranic). The two names are the same figure across the two textual strata. [scholarly]
- **The flight and the substitute (Ṛg Veda 10.17.1-2)** — the oldest version: Tvaṣṭṛ makes a wedding for his daughter Saraṇyū; she bears Vivasvat twin children (Yama and Yamī), then **vanishes**. The gods "conceal the immortal" from mortals, fashion **a likeness (savarṇā)** in her place, and give it to Vivasvat — who does not at first notice. The Aśvins are then born (Ṛg Veda 10.17; Nirukta 12.10; Bṛhaddevatā 6.162ff).
  - *Variant (Bṛhaddevatā / Sāyaṇa's commentary):* Saraṇyū, unable to endure Vivasvat's ardour/heat, leaves behind her **shadow (Chhāyā)** and flees to **Uttarakuru as a mare**; Vivasvat begets **Manu** on the substitute, then discovers the deception and follows her as a **stallion**, begetting the **Aśvins** in horse-form. This is the seed of the full Puranic tale.
- **Sañjñā / Chhāyā (Puranic — Mārkaṇḍeya, Matsya, Viṣṇu, Bhāgavata Purāṇas)** — Sañjñā weds Sūrya and bears **Vaivasvata Manu, Yama, and Yamunā (Yamī)**. Scorched by his blaze, she creates an identical shadow-double, **Chhāyā** ("shadow"), instructs her to act as the wife, and flees to her father — then to the northern forests as a **mare (Vaḍavā/Aśvinī)**.
- **Chhāyā the shadow-wife** — Chhāyā keeps the secret and bears Sūrya more children: **Śani (Saturn)**, **Sāvarṇi Manu** (the 8th Manu, of the coming [Sāvarṇi Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-08-savarni.md)), and the daughter **Tapatī** (some lists). The deception is exposed when Chhāyā shows favoritism to her own children.
  - *The cursing incident:* Chhāyā curses **Yama** (in anger), revealing she is not the true mother — for a real mother could never curse her son. Yama complains to Sūrya, who interrogates Chhāyā and uncovers the truth (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa).
  - *Variant (who curses whom):* in some tellings Yama raises his foot at Chhāyā in anger and she curses the foot to fall off (mitigated to worms eating flesh) — but no birth-mother would curse a child, exposing her. [folk]

## Viśvakarmā trims the Sun — the lathe and the divine weapons

- **The trimming of Sūrya** — to win Sañjñā back, Sūrya submits to his father-in-law **Viśvakarmā / Tvaṣṭṛ**, who places him on a **cosmic lathe (bhrāmaṇa)** and grinds away the excess **one-eighth** (some say a larger portion) of his unbearable radiance, leaving him gentler so his wife can rejoin him (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa; Matsya Purāṇa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.2).
- **The off-cuts become divine weapons** — the shorn solar brilliance, fallen to earth, is forged by Viśvakarmā into the great āyudhas of the gods: **Viṣṇu's Sudarśana Cakra**, **Śiva's Triśūla**, **Kārttikeya's Śakti/spear**, **Kubera's palanquin**, and other weapons (Mārkaṇḍeya & Matsya Purāṇas). See [Artifacts, Symbols & Arts](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/README.md).
  - *Variant (extent):* texts disagree whether one-eighth, one-sixteenth, or a "portion" was removed; the residue's weapon-list also varies by Purāṇa — [flag: list open-ended].
- **Reunion** — gentled, Sūrya finds Sañjñā in mare-form; he takes the form of a **stallion** and unites with her, producing the horse-faced **Aśvins** (and in some texts **Revanta**). Sañjñā then returns as full wife (Mārkaṇḍeya; Matsya). Cross-link: the same horse-coupling motif sits in the Vedic stratum at [Ṛg Veda 10.17 above].

## The children of Sūrya

By **Saraṇyū/Sañjñā** (the true wife):

- **Yama (Yamarāja)** — first mortal to die and thus first king of the dead; the **Dharmarāja**, lord of the southern realm and judge of karma. Twin of Yamī. See [The Narakas & the Afterlife](narakas-and-afterlife.md) and [Navagraha](navagraha.md) (as Śani's brother). (Ṛg Veda 10.14, 10.17; Purāṇas.)
- **Yamī / Yamunā** — Yama's twin sister, who becomes the sacred **river Yamunā**. The Ṛg Vedic dialogue-hymn **10.10** preserves her unsuccessful proposal of incestuous union to Yama, who refuses on grounds of dharma. (Ṛg Veda 10.10; Purāṇas where she is the river.)
- **Vaivasvata Manu (Śrāddhadeva Manu)** — son of Vivasvat; the **progenitor of present humanity** and ruler of the current 7th [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md); survivor of the **Matsya flood**; founder of the [Sūryavaṃśa / Ikṣvāku line](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md). Hence the dynasty of Rāma is "solar."
  - *Variant:* in the Vedic version Manu is born of the **substitute/Chhāyā**, not the true wife (Ṛg Veda 10.17 commentary) — sources split on which mother bore Manu. [disputed]
- **The Aśvins (Nāsatya & Dasra)** — twin **physician-gods** of the devas, horse-headed/horse-born, gods of dawn, healing, and rescue; conceived when Sūrya and Saraṇyū met as stallion and mare. They later father **Nakula and Sahadeva** (the Pāṇḍava twins) by Mādrī. (Ṛg Veda; Mahābhārata.)
  - *Variant (parentage):* a strand makes the Aśvins children of the substitute-figure rather than Saraṇyū herself; another makes them sons of "the sky" — [disputed].

By **Chhāyā** (the shadow-wife):

- **Śani (Saturn)** — the slow, just dispenser of karma; estrangement and rivalry with his father Sūrya is a major theme (the father of light vs. the son of shadow). See [Navagraha § Shani](navagraha.md). (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa; Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa.)
- **Sāvarṇi Manu** — the **8th Manu** of the next manvantara; "sā-varṇi" = "of the same caste/colour [as Sūrya]," tied to Chhāyā/savarṇā the look-alike. See [Sāvarṇi Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-08-savarni.md).
- **Tapatī** — solar daughter (of Chhāyā in most lists); marries the Kuru king **Saṃvaraṇa** and becomes mother of **Kuru**, eponym of the Kuru dynasty — making the Mahābhārata clan partly solar-descended. The river **Tāpī/Tapti** is named for her. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva.)
  - *Variant:* some lists make Tapatī a daughter of Sañjñā, not Chhāyā. [disputed]

Other solar children (varying texts):

- **Revanta** — youngest son, **god of hunters, horsemen, and travelers**, leader of the Guhyakas; depicted on horseback with a hunting retinue, invoked for protection on journeys and against forest dangers. Born of Sañjñā in mare-form (so closely allied with horses). (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa; Devī Bhāgavata.) [a distinct guardian-cult, esp. eastern India]
- **Sugrīva [epic]** — vānara king of Kiṣkindhā in the Rāmāyaṇa, called a son of Sūrya; ally of Rāma. See [Hanuman](hanuman.md). (Rāmāyaṇa.)
- **Karṇa [epic]** — born to **Kuntī** by Sūrya's boon-mantra before her marriage, the great tragic warrior of the Mahābhārata, born with divine **kavaca-kuṇḍala** (armor and earrings). (Mahābhārata.)
- **Aśvinī / extended roster** — daughters and the dawn-goddesses **Uṣā** and **Pratyūṣā** as attendants; Bhadrā and others appear in scattered lists — [flag: roster open-ended across Purāṇas].

## Sūrya in worship & practice

- **Aditya Hṛdayam ("Heart of the Sun")** — a 31-verse hymn in the **Yuddha Kāṇḍa (ch. 107) of Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa**, taught by sage **Agastya** to a fatigued **Rāma** on the battlefield moments before the final duel with **Rāvaṇa**; chanting it (three times) restores Rāma and he prevails. A foundational solar stotra, naming Sūrya as the supreme deity (Āditya, "best of the gods who is honored by all the worlds"). (Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa 107.)
  - *Note:* it identifies Sūrya with Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Śiva, the Vedas, Kāla (Time), and Yama — a strongly **henotheistic/Saura** theology.
- **Gāyatrī mantra** — the most sacred Vedic verse (Ṛg Veda 3.62.10), addressed to **Savitṛ** (solar impeller); recited at the three sandhyās. See [Upavedas, Vedangas, Darshanas](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md).
- **Sūrya-namaskāra (Sun Salutation)** — a flowing sequence of **twelve linked āsanas**, each paired with one of the **twelve names** of the Sun (Oṃ Mitrāya namaḥ ... Bhāskarāya namaḥ) and a bīja, often timed to sunrise. [late text] Vedic/devotional sun-greeting is ancient; the **specific twelve-posture flow** was systematized and popularized in the early 20th c. by **Bhavanrao Pant Pratinidhi, Rajah of Aundh** (*The Ten-Point Way to Health*, 1928). [scholarly]
  - *Variant:* yogic tradition claims far older origins for the practice (wrestlers' exercises, temple sun-rites); the modern *codified āsana sequence* is the part historians date late. [disputed]
- **The Saura sampradāya** — one of the historic **five (pañcāyatana) cults** (Saura/Sun, Gāṇapatya, Śaiva, Śākta, Vaiṣṇava). The **Maga / Śākadvīpīya Brahmins** (of Iranian Magi descent) served as solar priests; **Sāmba**, son of Krishna, is said to have founded sun-worship at **Mitravana/Mūlasthāna (Multan)** after Sūrya cured his leprosy (Sāmba Purāṇa; Bhaviṣya Purāṇa). See [Navagraha § Surya](navagraha.md).

## Konārk — the Sun in stone

- **Konārk Sun Temple (Odisha, c. 1250 CE)** — built by King **Narasiṃhadeva I** of the Eastern Gaṅga dynasty; a UNESCO World Heritage Site conceived as Sūrya's colossal **stone chariot**, ~100 ft, in Kaliṅga (Nāgara) style. The most monumental solar temple in India.
- **The seven horses** — the chariot is drawn by **seven carved horses**, named for the **seven Vedic metres** of prosody: **Gāyatrī, Bṛhatī, Uṣṇih, Jagatī, Triṣṭubh, Anuṣṭubh, Paṅkti** — also read as the seven days of the week and the seven colors of sunlight.
- **The twenty-four wheels** — **24 great stone wheels** (~12 ft diameter), read as the 24 fortnights of the year (or hours of the day); several function as accurate **sundials**, their spokes casting time-telling shadows.
- **Decline** — the main *deul* (tower) collapsed (and/or was partly dismantled); the surviving *jagamohana* was filled with sand and sealed by the ASI in the 20th c. for stability. Other major sun-temples: **Modhera (Gujarat)**, **Martand (Kashmir)**, **Dakshinaarka (Gaya)**. See [Sacred Geography](../06-sacred-geography/README.md).
