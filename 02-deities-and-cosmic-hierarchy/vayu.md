# Vāyu

> Cosmic address: [Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md) → the elemental and directional gods. Vāyu is the wind-god, one of the **Pañca Mahābhūta** (air element) and one of the eight **Dikpālas** (guardian of the northwest), the cosmic *prāṇa* (life-breath), father of [Hanumān](hanuman.md) and Bhīma, and — in the [Mādhva](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/) tradition — the supreme jīva who incarnates thrice. Parent index: [Master README](../README.md). See also [Kāmadeva & the Minor Deities](kamadeva-and-minor-deities.md) (the Vasus, Sūrya), [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).

Vāyu (वायु, "wind/air") is among the oldest active deities of Hinduism — a major Ṛgvedic god who never faded, because he carries two inseparable meanings: the **physical wind** that moves the worlds, and the **vital breath (*prāṇa*)** that animates every living body. He is the *deva* of motion, swiftness, smell-bearing, and purification; the divine **messenger**; the father of the two greatest mortals-of-might in the epics; and, uniquely, the god whom one full living philosophical school (Dvaita) places at the very top of the hierarchy of created souls. This file catalogs his names, Vedic nature, the *prāṇa* doctrine, his fatherhood of Bhīma and Hanumān, the threefold Mādhva avatāra claim, his role as messenger, and the myth of his breaking the peak of Meru/Trikūṭa to form Laṅkā — with variants.

---

## Names & Epithets

- **Vāyu** - the standard name, "wind, air"; the moving, blowing element. (Ṛgveda; pan-textual)
- **Vāta** - the older, more impersonal Vedic name for "wind"; sometimes treated as a distinct but overlapping deity hymned alongside Vāyu (RV 10.168, the *Vāta* hymn). (Ṛgveda)
- **Pavana / Pāvana** - "the purifier, the one who makes clean"; hence Hanumān is *Pavanaputra* / *Pavana-suta* ("son of Pavana"). (Epic/Purāṇic usage)
- **Anila** - "the un-resting, the breath/wind"; also the name of one of the eight [Vasus](kamadeva-and-minor-deities.md) representing wind, sometimes conflated with Vāyu. (Mahābhārata; Purāṇas)
- **Marut / Maruta** - associated with the **Maruts**, the storm-host; the Maruts are said to be born "from Vāyu's belly," and *Maruta* is used of Vāyu himself. (Ṛgveda)
- **Mukhya-prāṇa** - "the chief life-breath, the foremost prāṇa"; the philosophical/Vedāntic name, central to Mādhva theology. (Upaniṣads; Dvaita Vedānta)
- **Prāṇa** - "breath, life-force"; the Upaniṣads identify Vāyu *as* the cosmic prāṇa. (Bṛhadāraṇyaka, Chāndogya Upaniṣads)
- **Sadāgati** - "ever in motion, the never-still one." (Purāṇic)
- **Gandhavaha / Gandhavāha** - "carrier of scent" — wind as the bearer of smell to the nose. (lexical/Purāṇic)
- **Jagatprāṇa** - "breath of the world." (Upaniṣadic praise)
- **Other epithets** - Mātariśvan (a Vedic wind/fire-bringer figure identified with Vāyu), Samīra, Samīraṇa, Prabhañjana ("the great shatterer/breaker"), Vāhana, Śvasana ("the breather"). (Vedic/Purāṇic)

## Vedic Nature (the Ṛgvedic Vāyu)

- **A first-rank Ṛgvedic deity** - hymned in his own right and very frequently paired with **Indra** as the dual deity *Indra-Vāyu*; the two together are invoked to come to the soma sacrifice. (Ṛgveda, Maṇḍala 1 and elsewhere)
- **First to drink soma** - in the ritual order Vāyu is the **first god offered soma**, drinking before (or jointly with) Indra; this priority marks his honor and swiftness. (Ṛgveda; Brāhmaṇa ritual texts)
- **Lord of the Maruts** - the storm-gods (Maruts/Rudras' host) are his troop, said to be **born from his belly**; Vāyu leads the wind-host. (Ṛgveda)
  - *Variant:* the Maruts are elsewhere reckoned sons of **Rudra** and Pṛśni, not of Vāyu — the two roster-claims coexist. [disputed]
- **Swiftness incarnate** - Vāyu is the *thousand-eyed*, *swift-as-mind* god whose chariot is drawn by many horses (99, 100, or 1000 in different verses); speed is his defining attribute. (Ṛgveda)
- **The Vāta hymn** - RV 10.168 personifies wind as the cosmic life-soul: "the breath (*ātman*) of the gods, the germ of the world," roaring, thundering, dust-raising — wind as both terror and life. (Ṛgveda 10.168)
- **Mātariśvan link** - the Vedic Mātariśvan, who brought (hidden) fire to mankind like a Promethean wind-spirit, is identified with or as Vāyu. (Ṛgveda; later tradition)

## Cosmic Functions

- **Pañca Mahābhūta — the air element** - Vāyu is the divine governor and personification of *vāyu* the element, third of the five gross elements (after ākāśa/ether and before tejas/fire) in Sāṅkhya/Vedāntic cosmology; air carries the quality of *sparśa* (touch). (Sāṅkhya; Purāṇas) — see [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) for the elemental tier.
- **Dikpāla of the northwest (vāyavya)** - one of the eight **Lokapālas/Dikpālas** (guardians of the directions); Vāyu rules the **northwest (vāyavya koṇa)**, the corner named for him; vāstu-śāstra still treats the NW as Vāyu's quarter. (Purāṇas; Vāstu-śāstra)
- **Carrier of scent (gandhavaha)** - because smell travels on air, Vāyu is the medium by which odor reaches the senses; a recurrent poetic and ritual role. (Purāṇic/lexical)
- **The divine messenger** - the wind goes everywhere unseen, so Vāyu serves as the gods' **courier and go-between**, bearing words, scents, and signs across the worlds; this messenger-function underlies later "wind carries the message" motifs. (Vedic/Purāṇic; see iconography below)
- **Purifier** - as *Pavana* he cleanses; wind disperses corruption and carries sacrificial fragrance upward. (Purāṇic)

## Vāyu as Prāṇa — the Life-Breath Doctrine

This is Vāyu's deepest theological identity: the macrocosmic wind = the microcosmic breath that keeps the body alive.

- **Prāṇa = cosmic Vāyu** - the Upaniṣads equate the breath in the body with the wind in the cosmos; the same power blows the worlds and animates the lungs. (Bṛhadāraṇyaka, Chāndogya Upaniṣads)
- **The contest of the senses (prāṇa is supreme)** - the famous Upaniṣadic parable: the faculties (speech, eye, ear, mind, breath) dispute which is most essential. Each departs the body in turn; the body survives blind, dumb, deaf, mindless — but the moment **prāṇa (breath/Mukhya-prāṇa)** prepares to leave, all the other faculties are wrenched from their seats and cannot stay. Hence prāṇa is declared the foremost. (Bṛhadāraṇyaka Up. 6.1; Chāndogya Up. 5.1; Kauṣītaki Up.)
- **Mukhya-prāṇa — the "chief breath"** - the principal vital air, distinguished from the five sub-prāṇas (*prāṇa, apāna, vyāna, udāna, samāna*); identified with Vāyu as the indwelling life-governor of every being. (Upaniṣads; Vedānta)
- **Why this matters downstream** - the prāṇa-doctrine is the root of (a) the [yogic prāṇāyāma](../05-concepts-and-dharma/) breath disciplines and (b) the Mādhva elevation of Vāyu to supreme jīva (below). The wind-god is thus the bridge between cosmology and the body.

## Father of Bhīma & Hanumān

Vāyu's most celebrated narrative role is paternity of the two strongest beings of the epics — both conceived by his wind-agency rather than ordinary union.

- **Hanumān (Treta Yuga, Rāmāyaṇa)** - Vāyu fathers **Hanumān** upon the apsarā-turned-vānarī **Añjanā** (wife of the vānara chief Kesari); hence Hanumān is *Vāyuputra / Pavanaputra / Māruti*. Vāyu's agency is non-physical (*mānasa*) in Vālmīki. (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā 66; Uttara 35–36) — full birth-cycle and variants in [Hanumān](hanuman.md).
  - *Variant (Śiva Purāṇa):* Vāyu carries Śiva's essence/seed (Hanumān as the 11th Rudra) into Añjanā's womb. [see [Hanumān §Rudrāvatāra](hanuman.md)]
  - *Variant (the pāyasa/oblation telling):* a portion of the divine *pāyasa* (sacred pudding) given to Daśaratha's queens is borne by Vāyu (a kite snatches it / wind carries it) to Añjanā, who consumes it and conceives Hanumān. [folk/Purāṇic]
  - **Vāyu's wrath at Hanumān's "death"** - when the infant Hanumān is struck down by Indra's *vajra*, Vāyu in grief **withdraws all air from the worlds**, suffocating creation, until the gods revive Hanumān and grant boons. This act dramatizes that without Vāyu there is no life. (Vālmīki, Uttara Kāṇḍa 36; Śiva Purāṇa)
- **Bhīma (Dvāpara Yuga, Mahābhārata)** - Queen **Kuntī**, using Durvāsā's mantra, invokes Vāyu and conceives **Bhīma** (Bhīmasena), second of the [Pāṇḍavas](../03-lineages-vamsha/); Bhīma is the strongest of the five, a mace-wielding force of nature — Vāyu's might made mortal. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva)
  - **The Hanumān–Bhīma meeting** - the two sons of Vāyu meet when Bhīma, seeking the *saugandhika* flower, finds his path blocked by an old monkey's tail he cannot lift; the monkey reveals himself as **Hanumān**, his elder half-brother, and blesses him. (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva) — see [Hanumān §Mahābhārata role](hanuman.md).
- **Theological reading** - both sons embody **prāṇa-śakti** (the power of vital force): Hanumān as devotion-plus-strength, Bhīma as raw battle-might; together they make Vāyu the divine ancestor of *bala* (strength) itself.

## The Mādhva Claim — Vāyu as the Threefold Avatāra

The most distinctive doctrine attached to Vāyu belongs to **Dvaita Vedānta**, founded by **Madhvācārya (Ānandatīrtha, 1238–1317 CE [scholarly dating])**.

- **Vāyu as the supreme jīva** - in Dvaita's strict hierarchy, **Viṣṇu alone is the independent Supreme (*svatantra*)**; everything else is dependent (*paratantra*). At the very top of all created/dependent souls stands **Vāyu (Mukhya-prāṇa)** — the highest *jīva*, the chief mediator between Viṣṇu and the world, and the eventual successor to the post of Brahmā (*Brahma-pada*). (Madhva's works; Dvaita doctrine)
- **The three incarnations of Vāyu** - Dvaita holds that Vāyu descends in three successive ages to uphold dharma and right teaching:
  1. **Hanumān** (Tretā Yuga) — perfect servant of [Rāma](dashavatara.md).
  2. **Bhīma** (Dvāpara Yuga) — destroyer of the adharmic Kauravas.
  3. **Madhvācārya** (Kali Yuga) — restorer of true (Vaiṣṇava, dualist) Vedānta. (Madhva tradition; *Madhva-vijaya* of Nārāyaṇa Paṇḍitācārya)
- **Scriptural self-identification** - Madhva is held to have quoted a *Pāśupata*/Vedic verse foretelling the three avatāras, and the tradition reads the **Balittha-sūkta (RV 1.141)** as prophesying the three forms of Vāyu. (Mādhva exegesis)
  - *Variant / critique (Appayya Dīkṣita, 16th c. [scholarly]):* in his *Madhvatantra-mukha-mardana*, the Advaitin polemicist **Appayya Dīkṣita** charged that Madhva's threefold-avatāra claim and several supporting "scriptural" citations were **adduced to lend authority to otherwise untraceable quotations** — a major inter-school controversy. [disputed]
- **Living cult** - Mādhva maṭhas install **Mukhya-prāṇa / Hanumān** prominently; Vāyu-Hanumān worship is structurally central to Dvaita devotion, not peripheral. (Dvaita practice) — see [Ācāryas & Sampradāyas](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/).

## Breaking the Peak of Meru — the Origin of Laṅkā

A widely-told Purāṇic myth makes Vāyu the literal sculptor of [Laṅkā](../06-sacred-geography/) by snapping off a peak of the cosmic mountain.

- **Core myth (Nārada incites Vāyu vs. Garuḍa)** - the sage **Nārada**, ever the mischief-maker, goads Vāyu to break off the summit of **Mount Meru**. Vāyu blows against the peak for a full year, but **Garuḍa** spreads his wings and shields the mountain, taking the blast. When Garuḍa at last leaves his post, Nārada urges Vāyu to redouble his effort; Vāyu shears off the peak and **hurls it into the southern ocean**, where it becomes the island of **Laṅkā** (Sri Laṅkā). (Purāṇic; widely retold)
- **The Trikūṭa naming** - the broken three-peaked summit becomes **Trikūṭa** ("three-peaked"), the mountain on which the city of Laṅkā is built — the Rāvaṇa-citadel of the [Rāmāyaṇa](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md). (Purāṇic; Rāmāyaṇa geography)
  - *Variant (Vāyu vs. Vāsuki contest):* Vāyu and the cosmic serpent **Vāsuki** dispute who is stronger; Vāsuki coils around Meru while Vāyu blows. **Viṣṇu** orders them to stop; when Vāsuki releases his grip, Vāyu's pent breath blows the peak (Trikūṭa) off into the sea — becoming Laṅkā. [variant]
  - *Variant (epithet Prabhañjana):* this shattering deed is read as the origin/illustration of Vāyu's title **Prabhañjana**, "the great breaker." [folk]
- **Significance** - the myth ties Vāyu directly to the Rāmāyaṇa stage: the very ground on which his son Hanumān will later leap and burn (Sundara Kāṇḍa) was wind-made. See [Hanumān §Sundara Kāṇḍa](hanuman.md).

## Iconography & Vāhana

- **Form** - a strong, often white or smoke-hued figure, two- or four-armed, of swift and restless aspect; lord of motion. (Purāṇic dhyāna; Śilpa texts)
- **Banner / attribute** - bears a **white banner/flag** and (as messenger and herdsman of winds) a **goad (aṅkuśa)** as weapon; sometimes a discus or fan. (Iconographic tradition)
- **Vāhana (mount)** - a **chariot drawn by horses** (the many swift steeds of the Ṛgveda), and/or a **deer/gazelle/antelope** (the springing, fleet animal) as his mount. (Purāṇic; iconography)
  - *Variant:* the antelope/deer vāhana is the common temple form; the multi-horse chariot is the Vedic form. [variant]
- **Direction marker** - in maṇḍalas and Dikpāla sets he stands in the **northwest**, holding banner and goad. (Vāstu/Tantric maṇḍalas)

## Family & Associations

- **Consort** - named in some lists as **Bhāradvājī / Añjana** (varies); Vāyu's "wives" are loosely cataloged and the roster is **not fixed across texts**. [open-ended roster]
- **Sons** - **Hanumān** (by Añjanā), **Bhīma** (by Kuntī); both *spiritual/wind-born* sons rather than offspring of a divine consort. (Rāmāyaṇa; Mahābhārata)
- **Indra** - constant Vedic partner (Indra-Vāyu); the two share the first soma. (Ṛgveda)
- **The Maruts** - his storm-host / "belly-born" troop (with the variant Rudra-parentage noted above). (Ṛgveda)
- **Vāyu Purāṇa** - one of the eighteen **Mahāpurāṇas**, traditionally **narrated by / attributed to Vāyu** (sometimes counted in place of, or alongside, the Śiva Purāṇa in Mahāpurāṇa lists); covers cosmology, geography, royal genealogies, and yuga-reckoning. [disputed placement] — see [Literature Corpus](../90-literature-corpus/).

## Two Clocks — Scripture vs. Scholarship

- **Scriptural clock** - Vāyu is a beginningless cosmic principle (the wind/prāṇa present from creation); his sons act in the [Tretā](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) (Hanumān, Rāmāyaṇa) and [Dvāpara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) (Bhīma, Mahābhārata) Yugas of the current 28th Mahāyuga; his third "descent," Madhva, falls in the present **Kali Yuga** (begun 3102 BCE by traditional reckoning).
- **Academic clock** - Vāyu as a *literary deity* is attested from the **Ṛgveda (composed c. 1500–1200 BCE [scholarly])**; the prāṇa-as-Vāyu doctrine crystallizes in the **principal Upaniṣads (c. 800–500 BCE [scholarly])**; the Mādhva threefold-avatāra theology is **13th–14th c. CE [scholarly]**, with the Appayya Dīkṣita critique in the **16th c. CE**.
