# Khodiyar Mata

> Deities-and-hierarchy node. **Khodiyār Mātā** (also **Khodal**, **Janbāī's sister**, **Āvad-sister**) is a major **Gujarati folk goddess (lok-devī)** and **kuladevī (clan-mother)** to many western-Indian communities — a Cāraṇ-born śakti who rides a **crocodile (magar)** and presides over great pilgrimage temples in Saurāṣṭra. She is treated as a manifestation of **[Ādi-Śakti / the great Devī](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)** in her Cāraṇ *āvataraṇa*. ## 1. Two Clocks — Who Khodiyar Mata Is

Khodiyār is a **Gujarati lok-devī / kuladevī** with a thin legendary-historical kernel and a thick living devotional cult; her "scriptural clock" runs only through her equation with **[Ādi-Śakti / Durgā](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)**, not through any text that names "Khodiyār."

- **Scriptural clock** — Khodiyār has **no Vedic or Purāṇic footprint under that name**; her theological legitimacy rests on the Cāraṇ doctrine that the great **[Devī](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)** takes birth (*āvataraṇa*) in Cāraṇ households as the *āī* ("mother"), so that she is read as a localized manifestation of **Ādi-Śakti / Durgā**. [folk] [late text]
- **Historical / legendary clock** — her birth-legend is set in **early-medieval Saurāṣṭra (Gujarāt)**, conventionally placed around the **7th–9th century CE** in some tellings (the dating is devotional-traditional, not documentary); she is born **Janbāī** to the Cāraṇ **Mamaḍiyā (Mamaiyā)** and his wife **Devaḷ (Mīṇbāī)** of the **Gadhvi / Cāraṇ** caste. [folk] [disputed]
- **What she governs** — protection of the **clan and lineage** (as kuladevī), childbirth and the welfare of children, the fortunes of warriors and rulers, and the **boons of barren or threatened families**; she is a guardian-mother rather than a fierce blood-goddess, though she is fully *śakti*. [regional]
- **The theological engine** — a **Cāraṇ-born śakti**: the great Mother incarnates in a bardic-pastoral household in answer to an **issueless couple's prayer**, lives among kin, performs miracles, and finally **merges / disappears** into the divine, leaving a *sthāna* (seat) that becomes a pilgrimage temple — the same lok-devī pattern that produces **Āī Śrī Āvaḍ, Bahuchar, and Mōmāī**. [folk]

## 2. Names and Epithets (catalog)

- **Khodiyār / Khodiyār Mātā** — her cult-name, popularly derived from *khoḍa* (lameness/limp) — she is said to have **walked with a limp** (or, in some tellings, to bear the by-name after an episode of lameness), whence "Khoḍiyār," the lame-footed mother. [folk]
- **Janbāī (Jānbāī)** — her **birth-name** in the legend, one of the seven (or several) Cāraṇ sisters born to Mamaḍiyā and Devaḷ. [regional]
- **Khoḍal / Khoḍal Mā** — the affectionate everyday form; the great modern temple-complex **Khoḍaldhām** (at Kāgvad, Rajkot district) bears this form. [regional]
- **Āī / Āī Śrī** — "the Mother," the Cāraṇ honorific applied to the lineage of Cāraṇ goddesses (cf. **Āvaḍ**, **Mōgal**) to which she belongs. [folk]
- **Magarvāhinī / the crocodile-rider** — descriptive epithet from her **crocodile (magar) mount** (§4). [regional]

## 3. The Birth-Legend (the core narrative, in order)

The story is a Cāraṇ folk *sākhī* of an issueless couple, divine daughters, a brother's death, and a miraculous rescue; recensions differ in detail, the through-line is constant.

- **The childless Cāraṇ couple** — the Cāraṇ **Mamaḍiyā (Mamaiyā Gaḍhvī)** and his wife **Devaḷ** of Saurāṣṭra are **childless and socially scorned** for their lack of issue; in some tellings Mamaḍiyā is taunted at a royal court (under the **Bhāvnagar / Saurāṣṭra ruler** of legend). (Cāraṇ folk tradition) [folk]
- **The penance and the boon** — the grieving Mamaḍiyā does **severe penance to Śiva / the Devī** (tellings vary), and is granted that **goddesses will be born as his daughters**; the great **[Devī](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)** consents to take birth in his house. (Cāraṇ folk tradition) [folk]
- **The seven sisters and a brother** — the couple are blessed with **several daughters — among them Janbāī (the future Khodiyār) — and one son, Merakhiyā (Mērakh)**; the daughters are recognized as *āīs*, divine Cāraṇ mothers. (Cāraṇ folk tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **The serpent-venom and the rescue (the central miracle)** — the brother **Merakhiyā is bitten by a snake / falls mortally ill**, and only the **amṛta / nāga-maṇi (life-restoring jewel) from the Pātāla nāga-realm** can save him; **Janbāī plunges into the netherworld waters** to fetch it. (Cāraṇ folk tradition) [folk]
- **The limp and the name** — racing back with the jewel before her brother's pyre, Janbāī is delayed; in haste she **injures / strains her foot and walks with a limp (*khoḍa*)** — whence the cult-name **Khoḍiyār** — yet returns in time and **restores Merakhiyā to life**, revealing her divine power. (Cāraṇ folk tradition) [folk]
  - *Variant (the agreed signal, Cāraṇ folk tradition):* in many tellings the sisters had arranged a **signal** (a cry or a horn) to halt the funeral until the jewel arrived; the limp arises from her hurry to beat the pyre, and the rescue marks the sisters' public recognition as goddesses. [folk] [regional]
- **The seat at Galdharā / the disappearance** — Khodiyār establishes her **sthāna**; in tradition she finally **withdraws into the divine / merges at her seat**, leaving the springs and shrines (notably **Galdharā** on the Tāntaṇiyā river) that become her tīrthas. (sthala-tradition) [regional]

## 4. Iconography — the Crocodile-Rider

- **The crocodile (magar) vāhana** — Khodiyār's defining emblem is her **crocodile mount**, unusual among Hindu goddesses; she is shown **seated on or accompanied by a magar (mugger crocodile)**, often near water — the crocodile is tied to the **netherworld-water rescue** of her brother and to her river-seats. [regional] [folk]
- **The Devī form** — she is iconographically a **Durgā-type mother** — multi-armed in temple images, bearing **triśūla (trident) and weapons**, red-robed, sometimes on a lion in Sanskritizing images, but in her own tradition marked by the **crocodile** and a **limp**. [regional]
- **Aniconic and footprint forms** — older village seats keep her as a **threshold-stone, footprints (*pagliyā*), or a simple *māṇḍvī*** by water; the magar may appear as a relief or guardian-figure at the shrine. [folk]
- **Not Brahmanical in origin** — like **[Jhulelal](jhulelal.md)** in Sindh and **[Gugga](gugga-gogaji.md)** in the north-west, Khodiyār sits **outside Sanskrit temple-orthodoxy**: her authority is the **Cāraṇ *sākhī*, the kuladevī bond, and the river-seat**, retrofitted to **[Ādi-Śakti / Durgā](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)** theology. [scholarly]

## 5. Temples and Pilgrimage (the living cult)

- **Rājpara (Galdharā), Bhāvnagar** — the **principal historic temple** at **Rājpara near Galdharā** (Bhāvnagar district), on the bank of the **Tāntaṇiyā / Gala-dharā** stream, is her oldest great seat and a major Saurāṣṭra pilgrimage centre. [regional]
- **Matel, Rajkot** — the temple at **Matel (Wankaner taluka, Rajkot district)**, by a sacred *talāv* (tank), is among her most-visited shrines, drawing huge crowds at festival. [regional]
- **Galdharā / Bhāvnagar springs** — the **Galdharā** spring-shrine is tied directly to her birth-legend and the water-rescue. [regional] [folk]
- **Khoḍaldhām, Kāgvad** — the vast modern temple-complex **Khoḍaldhām** at **Kāgvad (Rajkot district)**, inaugurated 2017, was raised by the **Leuvā Pāṭīdār (Patel) community** who hold Khodiyār as kuladevī — making her one of the **most prominently institutionalized lok-devīs of contemporary Gujarat**. [regional]
- **Festival calendar** — her chief day is **Khoḍiyār Jayantī / Mahā Sud Āṭham (the eighth *tithi* of bright Māgha**, c. Jan–Feb), her appearance-day; **Navarātri** and **Āṣāḍha/āno** fairs (*melā*) at her seats draw mass pilgrimage, with all-night *garbā*, *bhajan* and *jot* (lamp) worship. [regional] [folk]

## 6. Kuladevī and Significance

- **Kuladevī of many communities** — Khodiyār is the **clan-mother (kuladevī)** of numerous Gujarati and Saurāṣṭran groups — notably **Leuvā Pāṭīdār (Patel)**, many **Rajput and Kṣatriya lineages**, **Gaḍhvī / Cāraṇ**, **Koḷī, Khānt, Aḥir** and trading castes — invoked at births, weddings and lineage rites. [regional]
- **A Cāraṇ goddess** — she belongs to the distinctive **Cāraṇ (bardic) tradition of god-bearing women (*āīs*)**, alongside **Āvaḍ, Bahuchar(ā), Mōgal and Hingḷāj-linked mothers**, in which the great Mother is held to take human Cāraṇ birth — a regional theology of incarnate śakti. [folk] [scholarly]
- **Patron of rulers and warriors** — Saurāṣṭra royal houses (e.g. **Bhāvnagar / Gohil Rajputs** in legend) revered her as **iṣṭadevī and battle-protectress**, a function shared with other Rajput kuldevīs of western India. [regional] [disputed]
- **Among the first-tier regional goddesses of western India** — by temple-scale, pilgrim-numbers and kuladevī reach, Khodiyār ranks with **Ambājī, Bahuchara and Hingḷāj** as a leading goddess of the Gujarat–Saurāṣṭra zone, her cult **living and recent**, richly attested in folk song and modern temple-building, not in scripture. [regional]

> **Two clocks.** Khodiyār carries **no scriptural yuga-date**. The Janbāī birth-legend is Cāraṇ oral *sākhī*, and Khodiyār as a *worshipped goddess* is a **kali-historical** lok-devī cult — seats, fairs and kuladevī bonds attested in recent centuries and consolidated in a modern temple-renaissance, not in Veda or Purāṇa. Her equation with the Purāṇic **[Devī / Ādi-Śakti](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)** is a **Sanskritizing overlay**, kept on a separate clock from the Cāraṇ mother herself.
