# Viśvakarma & Tvaṣṭṛ

> The divine architect(s) of Hindu tradition: the cosmic engineer who builds the gods' cities, palaces, chariots, *vimānas*, and weapons, and the older Vedic fashioner-god who forges Indra's *vajra* and shapes forms in the womb. A deep dive expanding the capsule in [Kāmadeva & the Minor Deities](kamadeva-and-minor-deities.md). Parent: [02 — Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md) · Master index: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md).

This file catalogs **two overlapping artisan-deities** — the Vedic **Tvaṣṭṛ** ("the Fashioner") and the Epic-Purāṇic **Viśvakarmā** ("All-Maker") — whose identities partly merge over time. The first runs on the **Vedic clock** (Ṛg Veda, Brāhmaṇas); the second crystallizes in the **Epics and Purāṇas**. Every attested variant of parentage, roster, and myth is given as a nested bullet. Two-clock note: scriptural attributions are dated by *text-layer* (Vedic → Epic → Purāṇic), not by the academic chronology of when those texts were composed.

---

## The two architects, and how they relate

- **Tvaṣṭṛ (त्वष्टृ, "the Fashioner / Shaper")** — the **earliest** Vedic craftsman-god; the divine artisan who shapes all forms, fashions creatures in the womb, and forges the gods' first implements. Mentioned ~65 times in the Ṛg Veda. Father of Viśvarūpa and (in the revenge-myth) of Vṛtra; an **Āditya** in some lists. (Ṛg Veda; Wikipedia: Tvashtr)
- **Viśvakarmā (विश्वकर्मा, "All-Maker / All-Doer")** — originally an **abstract epithet** of the supreme creative principle (Ṛg Veda 10.81–82, the *Viśvakarman* hymns address an "all-making" demiurge), later personified as the **discrete architect-god** of the Epics and Purāṇas: engineer of the devas, builder of cities and weapons, patron of all craft. (Ṛg Veda 10.81–82; Britannica: Vishvakarman)
- **The identity question** — three positions coexist in the tradition:
  - *Variant (a) — distinct (Vedic stratum):* Tvaṣṭṛ and Viśvakarman are **separate** powers; Tvaṣṭṛ is the hands-on smith, Viśvakarman the cosmic all-maker / abstract demiurge. (Ṛg Veda)
  - *Variant (b) — merged (Purāṇic stratum):* Viśvakarmā **absorbs** Tvaṣṭṛ; the two names denote one deity, and Tvaṣṭṛ-myths (the *vajra*, Viśvarūpa, the womb-shaper role) are reassigned to Viśvakarmā. (Purāṇas; Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (c) — Tvaṣṭṛ = an epithet/role of Viśvakarmā:* "Tvaṣṭṛ" read as a title ("the chiseling craftsman") for the same architect. (folk/late commentarial)

## Tvaṣṭṛ — the Vedic Fashioner

- **The shaper of forms** — Tvaṣṭṛ is *viśvarūpa* ("of all forms"); he **fashions living beings and the womb** (*garbha*), grants offspring, and shapes the embryo. Invoked for progeny and longevity in Vedic ritual. (Ṛg Veda; Wikipedia: Tvashtr)
- **The first smith of the gods** — credited in the Ṛg Veda with forging:
  - **Indra's vajra** (the thunderbolt) — Tvaṣṭṛ's signature creation, the weapon with which Indra slays Vṛtra. (Ṛg Veda 1.32, 1.61, etc.)
  - **the axe (*paraśu*) of Bṛhaspati** and other divine tools. (Ṛg Veda)
  - **the *camasa*** — the single cup/bowl for the gods' food and *soma*, which the **Ṛbhus** (artisan-elves) later split into four, a feat that earns them divine status and provokes Tvaṣṭṛ's jealousy. (Ṛg Veda 1.20, 4.33)
- **Parentage / family (Vedic)** — father of **Bṛhaspati** (in some hymns) and, by several readings, **of Indra himself**; father of **Saraṇyū** (the cloud/dawn maiden who weds Vivasvat-Sūrya — see the Sūrya-trimming myth below). (Ṛg Veda 10.17; Wikipedia)
- **Tvaṣṭṛ as an Āditya** — counted among the sons of **Aditi** in some Āditya-lists, placing him in the solar deity-class (see [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) and [Navagraha](navagraha.md)).

### The Viśvarūpa / Triśiras episode

- **Viśvarūpa "Triśiras" (the three-headed)** — Tvaṣṭṛ's son, also called **Triśiras** ("three-headed"); a priest of the gods who secretly favors the asuras (his maternal kin). His three mouths drink *soma*, wine, and food / chant the Vedas. (Taittirīya Saṃhitā; Mahābhārata, *Udyoga Parva*)
- **Slain by Indra** — fearing Viśvarūpa's growing power and divided loyalty, **Indra beheads him** (cutting off all three heads), an act of *brahmahatyā* (Brahmin-slaying) that stains Indra with grave sin. From the severed heads spring birds (partridge, sparrow, etc.). (Taittirīya Saṃhitā 2.5.1; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 6.9)
- **Tvaṣṭṛ's revenge — the creation of Vṛtra** — enraged at his son's murder, Tvaṣṭṛ:
  - bars Indra from his **soma sacrifice** (Indra forcibly drinks the leftover soma); and
  - performs a fire-rite to generate an avenger, **Vṛtra**, the great drought-serpent who imprisons the cosmic waters. (Taittirīya Saṃhitā; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 6.9)
  - *Variant (the fatal mispronunciation):* Tvaṣṭṛ intends to create "**indra-śatru**" — a slayer *of* Indra (*indrasya śatru*) — but mis-accents the compound so it means a foe *whose slayer is Indra*; the verbal slip dooms Vṛtra to die by Indra's hand. A classic Vedic etymological myth about the power of correct accent. (Taittirīya Saṃhitā 2.4.12; Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa)
- **Vṛtra slain with the vajra** — Indra kills Vṛtra (whom Tvaṣṭṛ himself had armed indirectly, since Tvaṣṭṛ forged the very *vajra*), releasing the waters — the central Vedic combat-myth. (Ṛg Veda 1.32; see [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md))

## Viśvakarmā — the Epic-Purāṇic divine architect

### Parentage & identity (variant roster)

- **Viśvakarmā's origin** — multiple, irreconcilable genealogies across text-layers:
  - *Variant (Nirukta / Brāhmaṇa):* son of **Bhuvana**. (Nirukta)
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata & Harivaṃśa):* son of the **Vasu Prabhāsa** and **Yoga-siddhā** — i.e., grandson-class of the eight **Vasus**. (Mahābhārata; Harivaṃśa)
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa):* son of the Vasu **Prabhāsa** and **Varastrī**, the sister of **Bṛhaspati**. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (Purāṇic):* son of **Vāstu** (the genius of building-sites — whence *Vāstu-śāstra*), or directly of **Brahmā**. (Purāṇas; Britannica)
  - *Variant (Tvaṣṭṛ-merge):* identified outright **with Tvaṣṭṛ**, inheriting Viśvarūpa as son. (Wikipedia)
- **Consort & children** — wife **Ghṛtācī** (an apsarā, per *Vāmana Purāṇa*); daughters include **Saṃjñā / Saraṇyū** (Sūrya's wife), **Barhiṣmatī**, **Citrāṅgadā**; the *vānara* architect **Nala** (builder of Rāma's bridge) is called his son in the **Rāmāyaṇa**; and the **five artisan-sons** (below). (*Vāmana Purāṇa*; Rāmāyaṇa; Wikipedia)

### Builder of the cities

- **Laṅkā** — the golden city of **Rāvaṇa** on the southern island; in the standard account Viśvakarmā **built Laṅkā** (originally for Kubera, before Rāvaṇa seized it), the most famous of his constructions in the **Rāmāyaṇa**. (Rāmāyaṇa; Britannica)
  - *Variant:* in some retellings Laṅkā is built for **Kubera** (Viśvakarmā's patron among the Lokapālas) and only later usurped by his half-brother Rāvaṇa.
- **Dvārakā** — the sea-girt capital of **Kṛṣṇa**, raised in a single night on land reclaimed from the ocean, built by Viśvakarmā at Kṛṣṇa's request. (Mahābhārata; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*; *Harivaṃśa*) — see [03 — Lineages / Vaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md) for the Yādava line.
- **Indraprastha** — the magical capital of the **Pāṇḍavas**; in the **Mahābhārata** the asura-architect **Maya Dānava** designs the wondrous illusion-hall (the *Māyā-sabhā*) for Yudhiṣṭhira, while Viśvakarmā is the celestial counterpart who builds the gods' assembly-halls. (Mahābhārata, *Sabhā Parva*)
  - *Variant (architect of Indraprastha):* attributed to **Viśvakarmā** in the Purāṇic / popular tradition, but the epic itself credits the illusion-palace specifically to **Maya** — the asuras' Viśvakarmā (see [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md)).
- **Hastināpura, Sudharmā-sabhā & the divine courts** — also credited with **Hastināpura**, the gods' assembly-hall **Sudharmā** (later relocated to Dvārakā), the **celestial courts of the Dikpālas/Lokapālas** in Svarga, **Tripura** (the three flying cities of the asuras, in some accounts), and the underwater realm of Varuṇa. (Mahābhārata; Purāṇas)

### Maker of the weapons, chariots & vimānas

- **The gods' arsenal** — Viśvakarmā forges the **weapons (*astra*/*śastra*) of all the gods**, most famously:
  - **Indra's vajra** (the thunderbolt) — here re-attributed to Viśvakarmā (originally Tvaṣṭṛ's). (Purāṇas; Wikipedia)
  - **Viṣṇu's Sudarśana cakra** and **Śiva's triśūla** — chiefly from the Sūrya-shavings (below). (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa*; *Matsya Purāṇa*) — see [09 — Artifacts, Symbols & Arts](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/README.md).
  - **Śiva's chariot** for the burning of **Tripura** (the *Tripurāntaka* war-car). (Purāṇas)
- **Chariots (*ratha*)** — maker of the war-chariots of the devas. (Wikipedia)
- **Vimānas (flying craft)** — fashioner of the **Puṣpaka vimāna**, the self-moving aerial chariot (made for Kubera, taken by Rāvaṇa, returned to Rāma). (Rāmāyaṇa) — see [09 — Artifacts](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/README.md).
- **The image of Jagannātha** — by the **Skanda Purāṇa**, Viśvakarmā (disguised, demanding to work unobserved) carves the **wooden image of Jagannātha** at Puri, leaving it unfinished (armless) when interrupted. (*Skanda Purāṇa*; Wikipedia) — see [06 — Sacred Geography](../06-sacred-geography/README.md).
- **Tilottamā** — fashions the supremely beautiful apsarā **Tilottamā** from "tila"-measures of every excellence, on Brahmā's order, to undo the asura brothers **Sunda and Upasunda**. (Mahābhārata, *Ādi Parva*)

### The trimming of Sūrya (the Sūrya-Saṃjñā cycle)

- **The problem** — **Saṃjñā / Saraṇyū**, daughter of Viśvakarmā (or of Tvaṣṭṛ), cannot endure the unbearable blaze of her husband **Sūrya**; she flees, leaving her shadow-double **Chāyā** in her place, and retreats as a mare. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa*; *Matsya Purāṇa*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*) — see [Navagraha](navagraha.md).
- **The lathe-remedy** — to relieve his daughter, Viśvakarmā (or Tvaṣṭṛ) **mounts Sūrya on his lathe (*bhrami*) and grinds off one-eighth of the Sun's radiance**, making him bearable; from the **shavings of solar fire** are forged the great weapons. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*; Wikipedia)
- **What the shavings became (variant rosters):**
  - *Standard set:* **Viṣṇu's Sudarśana cakra** and **Śiva's triśūla**. (Wikipedia: Vishvakarma)
  - *Expanded set:* additionally **Kubera's puṣpaka/weapons**, **Kārttikeya's śakti (lance)**, and **the daṇḍa/weapons of Yama** — the shavings distributed among the gods. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa*; folk tradition)
- **Cross-myth note** — this episode is the bridge that joins the artisan-god to the **solar dynasty**: Saṃjñā's children by Sūrya are **Vaivasvata Manu** (manvantara-eponym; see [01 — Manvantaras](../01-manvantaras/README.md)), **Yama**, **Yamī/Yamunā**, and the **Aśvins** (born of the mare-form), and by Chāyā, **Śani** and **Sāvarṇi Manu**. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa*)

### The five artisan-sons (the *pañca-śilpī*)

- **Five sons of Viśvakarmā** — per the **Skanda Purāṇa**, the ancestral teachers of the five hereditary craft-lineages (the *Viśvakarma / Viśvabrāhmaṇa* community's five *gotras*):
  - **Manu** — blacksmiths / ironwork (*lohakāra*). (Skanda Purāṇa)
  - **Maya** — carpenters / woodcraft (*takṣaka / sūtradhāra*). [Distinct from Maya Dānava, though the names invite conflation.] (Skanda Purāṇa)
  - **Tvaṣṭṛ (Tvaṣṭā)** — metal-casters / bell-metal, brass-smiths (*kāṃsyakāra*); the Vedic Tvaṣṭṛ folded in as a "son." (Skanda Purāṇa)
  - **Śilpī (Śilpin)** — stonemasons / sculptors, idol-makers (*śilpakāra*). (Skanda Purāṇa)
  - **Daivajña (Viśvajña)** — goldsmiths / workers in gold and silver (*svarṇakāra*). (Skanda Purāṇa)
  - *Note [open-ended roster]:* spellings and craft-assignments vary by community source (e.g., **Viśvajña** vs **Daivajña**; some lists swap which son holds which craft). The five-son scheme is primarily a **caste-origin / *gotra* charter** rather than a uniform Purāṇic narrative. [folk]

## Viśvakarmā Pūjā — the living festival

- **The festival** — **Viśvakarmā Pūjā** (also *Viśvakarmā Jayantī*), the worship-day of the divine architect, observed chiefly by **artisans, engineers, mechanics, smiths, weavers, factory and industrial workers**, who venerate their **tools, machines, vehicles, and workplaces** for skill, safety, and prosperity. (Wikipedia: Vishvakarma Puja)
- **Date** — falls on **Kanyā Saṅkrānti** (the Sun's entry into Virgo), fixed by the *solar* almanac at **~16–18 September** each Gregorian year (commonly **17 September**) — note this is a **saṅkrānti-fixed** (solar) date, unlike most lunar-tithi Hindu festivals. (Wikipedia; Vedantu)
  - *Variant:* some communities additionally keep a **Viśvakarmā Jayantī** on a separate lunar date (e.g., in the month of Māgha / on a Bhādrapada tithi), and Nepali/regional calendars differ. [regional]
- **Where strongest** — most prominent in **West Bengal, Odisha, Assam, Jharkhand, Tripura, Bihar, and Karnataka**, and in industrial/workshop settings nationwide and in Nepal. (Wikipedia)
- **Practice** — tools and machinery are cleaned and decorated, a *mūrti* or image of Viśvakarmā is installed and worshipped, *yantra*/implements receive *tilaka* and offerings, and (in Bengal/Odisha) **kite-flying** marks the day; immersion of the image follows, paralleling Durgā/Gaṇeśa immersions. (Vedantu; Wikipedia)
