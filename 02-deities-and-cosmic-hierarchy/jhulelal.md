# Jhulelal / Uderolal — the Sindhi Water-God

> Deities-and-hierarchy node. **Jhulelal** (also **Uderolāl**, **Amarlāl**, **Lāl Sāī'n**, **Dariyā Lāl**, and the syncretic **Zinda Pīr** / **Khwāja Khizr**) is the supreme patron deity (*iṣṭadeva* / *kuladevatā*) of the **Sindhi Hindu** community — held to be an **avatāra of [Varuṇa](varuna.md)**, the Vedic lord of waters, born on the banks of the **Indus (Sindhu)** to deliver the Sindhis from the tyrant **Mirkhshah**. ## 1. Two Clocks — Who Jhulelal Is

Jhulelal is a **folk / regional water-deity** of the Indus valley with a thin (and disputed) historical kernel and a thick devotional overlay; his "scriptural clock" runs entirely through his identification with [Varuṇa](varuna.md), not through any text that names "Jhulelal."

- **Scriptural clock** — Jhulelal has **no Vedic or Purāṇic footprint under that name**; his theological legitimacy rests on his being an **incarnation of [Varuṇa](varuna.md) / the water-god (*Jaleśvara*, *Apām pati*)** answering a community's prayer — the avatāra-of-Varuṇa lore is the only "scripture clock" he has. [folk] [late text]
- **Historical clock** — the deliverance-legend is set in **medieval Sindh** under a Muslim ruler, **Mirkhshah** (Mirakh Shah) of **Thatta**, conventionally placed around the **10th century CE** (some tellings ~951 CE / the Sindhi era reckoning); the dating is devotional-traditional, not documentary. [folk] [disputed]
- **What he governs** — the **Indus (Sindhu) river and its waters**, deliverance of the Sindhi people, and, by his diaspora function, the very **religious identity of the Sindhi Hindu community**, who lost a territorial homeland at Partition (1947) and carry Jhulelal as a portable patron. [regional]
- **The theological engine** — a water-god **incarnates in response to collective prayer** to defend a threatened community and its freedom of worship; the same logic that lets [Varuṇa](varuna.md) be petitioned for relief and rain produces, in the Indus context, a deliverer-avatāra. [folk]

## 2. Names and Epithets (catalog)

- **Jhulelāl (झूलेलाल)** — the everyday devotional name; popularly glossed from *jhūlaṇo* "to swing/sway" (the cradle-swing of the infant, or the swaying of his river-boat). [folk]
- **Uderolāl / Udero Lāl** — his birth-name in the legend (born at **Nasarpur**); many shrines and the town **Udero Lal** (Sindh) bear this form. [regional]
- **Amarlāl** — "the immortal Lāl," stressing his deathless, ever-living nature. [folk]
- **Lāl Sāī'n / Lāl Sāhib** — "the Red Lord / Master" (*lāl* "red, beloved, ruby"), his common honorific. [regional]
- **Dariyā Lāl / Dariyāh Shāh / Zindā Pīr** — "Lord of the River" / "the Living Saint," the forms under which **Sindhi Muslims also venerate** him at the river-shrine. [regional]
- **Khwāja Khizr / Khwāja Khidr** — the Islamic immortal water-saint with whom Jhulelal is syncretically **identified** at the floating Indus shrines (see §6). [regional] [disputed]
- **Jyotī / the flame** — the *amar jyot* (eternal flame) and **water-pot** are his aniconic emblems. [folk]

## 3. The Deliverance Legend (the core narrative, in order)

The story is a Sindhi folk *sākhī* of tyranny, collective prayer, and a water-god's incarnation; recensions differ in detail, the through-line is constant.

- **The tyrant Mirkhshah** — the Muslim ruler of **Thatta (Sindh)**, **Mirkhshah**, presses the Hindu population to convert to Islam under threat, allowing them a stay of forty days to decide. (Sindhi folk tradition) [folk]
- **The forty-day penance on the Indus** — the despairing Sindhi Hindus gather on the **bank of the Sindhu (Indus)**, fast and pray for forty days to **[Varuṇa](varuna.md) / the water-god (Daryā Shāh)** to save them and their faith — the prototype of the later **Chāliho** observance (§5). (Sindhi folk tradition) [folk]
- **The voice from the river** — a voice answers from the waters promising that the deliverer will be **born in the womb of Mātā Devakī, wife of Ratanchand Lohano, at Nasarpur**, to humble the tyrant. (Sindhi folk tradition) [folk]
- **The birth of Uderolal at Nasarpur** — the child **Uderolāl** is born at **Nasarpur** on the **Indus**; marvels attend him — an old greybeard warrior on a blue horse seen in his cradle, the infant himself appearing as both babe and bearded sage. He is hailed as the **avatāra of [Varuṇa](varuna.md)**. (Sindhi folk tradition) [folk]
- **The confrontation with Mirkhshah** — grown (or miraculously manifesting), Uderolal goes to **Mirkhshah**; when the ruler's men move against him, he reveals his divine power — **water and fire** burst forth at his command, flooding/scorching the court — until Mirkhshah submits and grants the Hindus freedom of worship. (Sindhi folk tradition) [folk]
  - *Variant (the reconciling counsel, Sindhi folk tradition):* in many tellings Jhulelal does **not destroy** Mirkhshah but **converts his heart**, counselling that Hindus and Muslims alike drink the same Indus water and worship one Lord under different names — the charter for his **shared Hindu–Muslim cult** (§6). [folk] [regional]
- **The shrine of Udero Lal / the merger** — Jhulelal directs that a **shrine** be built; at the spot he **vanishes / merges into the river or a tomb-shrine**, and the place becomes the town and tīrtha **Udero Lal**, where a single sanctuary serves both a Hindu *mandir* and a Muslim *dargāh* over one eternal flame. (Udero Lal sthala-tradition) [regional]

## 4. Cheṭī Chand — the Sindhi New Year

- **The day** — **Cheṭī Chand (Chaiti Chand)** falls on the **second *tithi* (Dvitīyā) of the bright fortnight of Chaitra** (c. March–April), the day after the lunar new moon — celebrated as **Jhulelal's birthday (*janmotsava*)** and as the **Sindhi New Year**. [regional]
- **Why the date** — the festival commemorates the **appearance / birth of Uderolal** in answer to the forty-day prayer; it is the single most important communal festival of the Sindhi Hindu calendar. [regional]
- **The bahirāṇo procession** — devotees prepare a **bahirāṇo sāhib** (also *baharāno*): a portable shrine-tray bearing a **water-jar (*jot* and *kalaśa*), an oil-lamp / eternal flame, sugar-candy (*misri*), cardamom, fruit and a coconut**, carried in procession and then **immersed in flowing water / the river**, re-enacting the river-merger. [regional] [folk]
- **Palav / Chhej and the ārtī** — the procession features the **Chhej** stick-dance and the singing of the great Sindhi **ārtī "Pa'inja'ra Lāl Pataṇje" / "Jhule Lalan…"** ("O Lāl, ferry us across"), the community's signature hymn. [regional]
- **Civic recognition** — Cheṭī Chand is the Sindhi community's principal public festival in India and the diaspora; it is observed as a holiday in some Indian states and as **Sindhi New Year** worldwide. [regional]

## 5. Chāliho Sāhib — the Forty-Day Vow

- **The observance** — **Chāliho** (*chālīho* "a set of forty") is a **forty-day period of penance, fasting and prayer** kept by Sindhi devotees in gratitude to Jhulelal, directly re-enacting the **forty-day Indus prayer** of the deliverance-legend (§3). [regional] [folk]
- **The timing** — traditionally observed across **roughly mid-July to mid-August** (the dates fixed by community reckoning), culminating in **Thanksgiving (Thandyaun / the concluding celebration)** to Lāl Sāī'n. [regional]
- **The discipline** — participants abstain from certain foods and comforts, keep an austere routine, and conclude with communal worship of the *bahirāṇo* and the eternal flame — a living memorial of the founding vow. [folk]

## 6. Iconography and the Syncretic Zinda-Pīr Cult

- **Riding the palla-fish on the Indus** — Jhulelal's standard icon is a **bearded elder seated on a fish — the *palla* (hilsa, *Tenualosa ilisha*)**, the prized migratory fish of the lower Indus — or upon a **lotus/throne on the water**, holding a **book/scripture** and with an **eternal flame** before him; the *palla* swimming upstream against the current is read as his emblem. [regional] [folk]
- **The blue horse and white beard** — an older folk image shows him as a **warrior-saint with a white beard on a blue horse (*nīlo gho'ro*)**, a form shared with the *pīr* iconography of the region. [folk]
- **Zinda Pīr / Khwāja Khizr identity** — at the floating Indus river-shrine traditions Jhulelal is identified with **Zinda Pīr ("the Living Saint")** and with the pan-Islamic immortal water-saint **Khwāja Khizr (al-Khiḍr)**; Sindhi Muslims and Hindus both venerated the river-deity, and the **Udero Lal** shrine is jointly served. [regional] [disputed]
- **The Zindapir / floating-shrine cult** — colonial-era accounts describe a **river-island shrine (*Zinda Pir* near Sukkur / Bukkur)** and *bahirāṇo* lamp-floats set adrift on the Indus, where boatmen of both faiths honored **Khwāja Khizr / Daryā Shāh** — the substrate from which the formalized Jhulelal cult crystallized. [scholarly] [regional]
- **Not Brahmanical in origin** — like [Khaṇḍobā](khandoba-martanda-bhairava.md) in the Deccan and [Gugga](gugga-gogaji.md) in the northwest, Jhulelal sits **outside Sanskrit temple-orthodoxy**: his authority is the **Sindhi folk *sākhī*, the *bahirāṇo*, and the river-shrine**, retrofitted to [Varuṇa](varuna.md)-avatāra theology. [scholarly]

## 7. Patron of the Sindhi Diaspora (significance)

- **Iṣṭadeva of the Sindhis** — Jhulelal is the **community deity of Sindhi Hindus**, invoked at thresholds of life and at every Cheṭī Chand; the cult is **pan-sectarian within Sindhi society**, embracing Nānakpanthī, Daryāpanthī and temple-Hindu strands alike. [regional]
- **The portable homeland after 1947** — with **Partition (1947)** the Sindhi Hindus were displaced from Sindh (which went to Pakistan) and dispersed across India and the world **without a territorial homeland**; Jhulelal became the **central, portable identity-marker** binding the scattered community — "the religion of the river" carried far from the river. [scholarly] [regional]
- **The Daryāpanthī tradition** — the **Daryāpanth ("path of the river")** venerates Jhulelal / Daryā Lāl as supreme; its temples (*tīkāno*) across India and the diaspora keep the *amar jyot* and the *bahirāṇo* rite. [regional]
- **Roster cross-reference** — Jhulelal expands the single **avatāra-of-Varuṇa** clause noted in [varuna.md](varuna.md) (§"Comparative & survivals," the *Cheṭī/Chaiti Chand–Jhulelal* survival of the Vedic water-cult); this file is the first-tier node for that line and consolidates the duplicate gap-list items into one entry. [scholarly]
