# Devī Māhātmya / Durgā Saptaśatī — The Goddess's Three Carita

> Where this sits in the hierarchy: `02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/` — a deep dive on the foundational scripture of [Śāktism](trimurti-and-tridevi.md#6-sectarian-primacy--who-is-supreme), expanding §7 of [`trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](trimurti-and-tridevi.md#7-the-great-goddess--durgā-kālī-and-the-śakti-spectrum). This is the 700-verse text in which the supreme Devī, by her own power and with the Trimūrti reduced to spectators or petitioners, slays the demons no male god could kill — the canonical statement that **Śakti is the Absolute and the Gods her instruments**.

---

## 1. What the Text Is

- **Devī Māhātmya ("Glory of the Goddess")** — a self-contained block of **~700 ślokas** forming chapters **81–93** of the **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa**; also called **Durgā Saptaśatī** ("Seven Hundred [verses] of Durgā"), **Caṇḍī / Caṇḍī-pāṭha**, or **Saptaśatī Caṇḍī**. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Mahatmya)*)
  - *Note (verse count):* The "700" is traditional; actual editions count slightly differently. The text is also chaptered as **13 adhyāyas** (ch. 1–13), which is how recitation tracks it. (*standard recensions*)
- **Earliest crystallized statement of Goddess-as-Supreme** — it welds together prior local goddess cults (Mahiṣa-slayer, Kālī, the Mātṛkās) into a single **Mahādevī / Ādi Parāśakti** theology; the first text to treat the feminine as the ultimate ground of being. (*[scholarly]; Coburn, "Encountering the Goddess"*)
- **Scholarly dating** — composed **c. 400–600 CE** (often narrowed to **~550 CE**); a *terminus ante quem* is fixed by the **Dadhimatī Mātā inscription (608 CE)**, which quotes it. (*[scholarly]; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Mahatmya)*)
- **Recited as mantra, not merely read** — treated as a sound-body of the Goddess; central to **Navarātri / Durgā Pūjā** liturgy (see §8). (*living tradition*)

---

## 2. The Frame Story (Madhu's hermitage)

- **King Suratha & the merchant Samādhi** — a king (**Suratha**) who has lost his kingdom and a merchant (**Samādhi**) betrayed and driven out by his own family meet, both still inexplicably attached to those who wronged them, at the forest hermitage of the sage **Medhas (Sumedha)**. (*Devī Māhātmya ch. 1; [Indica Today](https://www.indica.today/quick-reads/devi-mahatmya-part-i-madhu-kaitabha-mahamaya/)*)
- **Medhas's teaching = Mahāmāyā** — the sage explains their delusory attachment as the work of **Mahāmāyā / Yoganidrā**, the Great Goddess who binds even the wise; he then narrates her three deeds to teach them devotion. The frame returns at the end: both worship Devī, who grants the king his kingdom back and the merchant **mokṣa**. (*Devī Māhātmya ch. 1 & 13*)
  - *Function:* the frame makes the entire battle-narrative a **discourse on Māyā and liberation**, not just war-myth — the Goddess is simultaneously the cosmic delusion *and* the release from it.

---

## 3. Architecture — Three Carita, Three Forms, Three Guṇas

The text is consciously tripartite: each **carita** ("deed/exploit") is presided over by one of three macro-forms of the Goddess, mapped to the three **guṇas**. (*[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Mahatmya); traditional Saṃkhya correlation*)

| Carita | Chapters | Episode | Presiding form | Guṇa | Ṛṣi (reciter-seer) |
|--------|----------|---------|----------------|------|---------------------|
| **Prathama** (first) | 1 | Madhu-Kaiṭabha | **Mahākālī** | tamas | Brahmā |
| **Madhyama** (middle) | 2–4 | Mahiṣāsura-mardinī | **Mahālakṣmī** | rajas | (Madhyama, sage) |
| **Uttama** (final) | 5–13 | Śumbha-Niśumbha | **Mahāsarasvatī** | sattva | (Uttama, sage) |

- *Note (counter-intuitive pairing):* the fierce Madhu-Kaiṭabha deed is governed by **Mahākālī** (tamas), the Mahiṣa war by **Mahālakṣmī** (rajas), and the long Śumbha cycle — which produces the bright Ambikā/Kauśikī and the terrible Kālī alike — by **Mahāsarasvatī** (sattva). The names do **not** match the familiar consort-goddesses' usual valence; here they are cosmic principles, not Viṣṇu's/Brahmā's wives. (*Mūrti Rahasya; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Mahatmya)*)
- *Variant (the unified Devī):* throughout, the text insists all three are **one Devī** under many names — Caṇḍikā, Ambikā, Bhadrakālī, Durgā — taking forms "as occasion demands." (*Devī Māhātmya, refrain*)

## 4. Prathama Carita — Madhu & Kaiṭabha (ch. 1)

- **The demons from Viṣṇu's ear** — at the end of a cosmic cycle, while **Viṣṇu lies in Yoganidrā (yogic sleep)** on Śeṣa in the cosmic ocean, two asuras, **Madhu and Kaiṭabha**, arise (from the wax/dirt of his ears) and move to kill **Brahmā**, seated on the navel-lotus. (*Devī Māhātmya 1; [Indica Today](https://www.indica.today/quick-reads/devi-mahatmya-part-i-madhu-kaitabha-mahamaya/)*) — see Viṣṇu's cosmic sleep in [`trimurti-and-tridevi.md §3`](trimurti-and-tridevi.md#3-viṣṇu--the-preserver-pālanahartā).
- **Brahmā hymns Yoganidrā** — unable to wake Viṣṇu, Brahmā praises the goddess **Yoganidrā / Mahāmāyā**, the very sleep personified that holds Viṣṇu, begging her to withdraw so Viṣṇu can rouse. This hymn is the **Brahma-stuti / Tantrika Rātri Sūktam**. (*Devī Māhātmya 1.73–87*)
- **The Goddess withdraws → Viṣṇu wakes** — Mahāmāyā exits Viṣṇu's body (eyes, mouth, limbs); Viṣṇu awakens and battles the two demons for **five thousand years**. (*Devī Māhātmya 1; [Indica Today](https://www.indica.today/quick-reads/devi-mahatmya-part-i-madhu-kaitabha-mahamaya/)*)
- **The trick of the boon** — deluded by the Goddess into arrogance, Madhu-Kaiṭabha grant Viṣṇu a boon ("ask what you wish"); Viṣṇu asks to slay them. To honor the boon's letter, they demand to be killed **where the earth is not covered by water**; Viṣṇu sets them on his thighs and beheads them with his discus. (*Devī Māhātmya 1*)
  - *Variant (their legacy):* their fat/marrow (medas) covering the waters is folk-etymologized as the origin of "**medinī**" (the earth). (*Purāṇic etymology*)
- **Theological point** — the supreme preserver Viṣṇu is **powerless until the Goddess permits**; victory is hers, not his. (*Devī Māhātmya 1*)

## 5. Madhyama Carita — Mahiṣāsura-mardinī (ch. 2–4)

### The crisis and the birth of Durgā

- **Mahiṣāsura conquers heaven** — the buffalo-demon **Mahiṣāsura**, after a hundred-year deva-asura war, defeats **Indra**, usurps the sovereignty of the three worlds, and expels the gods from svarga. (*Devī Māhātmya 2*)
- **The boon of male-invincibility** — by prior tapas Mahiṣa is unkillable by **any male** (deva, asura, man); the loophole is a woman — the gods' undoing becomes their weapon. (*Devī Māhātmya; Mahiṣa's boon-tradition*)
- **Birth from combined tejas** — the enraged gods emit **tejas (radiant energy)**: a great flame streams from the faces of Viṣṇu, Śiva, Brahmā, Indra and the rest, coalescing into a blazing mountain of light that becomes the **eighteen-armed Goddess (Durgā / Caṇḍikā)** — each part of her body formed from a specific god's light (Śiva's tejas → face, Yama's → hair, Viṣṇu's → arms, etc.). (*Devī Māhātmya 2; [Durga — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga)*)
- **Weapon-by-weapon arming** — each god gives her a replica of his own weapon/emblem:
  - **Śiva** → triśūla (trident); **Viṣṇu** → cakra (Sudarśana discus); **Varuṇa** → śaṅkha (conch) & noose; **Agni** → flaming dart/śakti; **Vāyu** → bow & quiver of arrows; **Indra** → vajra (thunderbolt) & a bell from Airāvata; **Yama** → kāladaṇḍa (staff of death) / noose; **Brahmā** → kamaṇḍalu & rosary; **Kāla** → sword & shield; **Viśvakarman** → axe & impenetrable armor; **Himavān** → a **lion (siṃha)** as mount; **Kubera** → a cup of wine; **Śeṣa** → a serpent-necklace. (*Devī Māhātmya 2; [Mythlok](https://mythlok.com/durga/); [Durga — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga)*)
  - *Variant (arm-count):* she is described variously as **eight-, ten-, or eighteen-armed**; the popular Mahiṣāsura-mardinī icon shows **ten arms (Daśabhujā)**. (*iconographic variants*)

### The buffalo war (ch. 2–3)

- **Devī's war-cry & laugh** — her roar fills the worlds; she single-handedly annihilates Mahiṣa's vast army and his generals across ch. 2. (*Devī Māhātmya 2*)
- **The generals fall** — successive asura commanders are slain: **Cikṣura, Cāmara, Udagra, Mahāhanu, Asiloman, Bāṣkala, Bidāla**, and others, with their armies. (*Devī Māhātmya 3*)
- **The shape-shifting death of Mahiṣa** — Mahiṣa fights as a **buffalo**, then morphs into a **lion**, then a **man with sword**, then a **tusked elephant**, then back to buffalo, hurling mountains. The Goddess, having drunk the divine wine (her eyes red), pins the buffalo with her foot, pierces its neck with her trident; as the **true (puruṣa) demon half-emerges from the buffalo's mouth**, she **beheads him with her great sword**. (*Devī Māhātmya 3; [Mahishasura — Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Mahishasura)*)
- **"Mahiṣāsura-mardinī"** — "Crusher of the Buffalo-Demon," her most worshipped epithet and icon. The gods sing the **Śakrādi-stuti / Devī-stuti** ("Yā Devī sarvabhūteṣu…") in ch. 4. (*Devī Māhātmya 4*)
  - *Note (9 days):* popular tradition stretches the duel to **nine days/nights**, mapping onto **Navarātri**; the text itself does not number it nine. (*[folk/liturgical]; [Durga — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga)*)

## 6. Uttama Carita — The Śumbha-Niśumbha Cycle (ch. 5–13)

The longest and most populous episode; it introduces Kālī, the Mātṛkās, and the doctrine that all goddesses reabsorb into the One.

### Setup — Ambikā / Kauśikī (ch. 5)

- **The brothers usurp heaven** — asura brothers **Śumbha & Niśumbha** seize the cosmos, stripping the gods of their shares of sacrifice. (*Devī Māhātmya 5*)
- **Birth of Kauśikī from Pārvatī's sheath** — the gods hymn the Goddess at the Himalayas (the famous **"Yā Devī"** litany expands here); **Pārvatī**, coming to bathe, asks whom they praise, and from her **bodily sheath (kośa)** emerges the radiant **Ambikā / Kauśikī**, while Pārvatī's remainder darkens into **Kālikā (Kālarātri)**. (*Devī Māhātmya 5; [Indica Today](https://www.indica.today/quick-reads/devi-mahatmya-part-iii-dhumralochana-chanda-munda-raktabija-nishumbha/)*)
  - *Variant (identity):* this makes the war-Goddess a **projection of Pārvatī** — reconciling the Śaiva consort with the independent Devī (see [`trimurti-and-tridevi.md §7`](trimurti-and-tridevi.md#7-the-great-goddess--durgā-kālī-and-the-śakti-spectrum)).
- **The demon proposal & Devī's vow** — servants **Caṇḍa & Muṇḍa** report her unmatched beauty; Śumbha sends an envoy demanding she marry him or one of them. She refuses, having **vowed to marry only one who defeats her in battle** — inviting the war. (*Devī Māhātmya 5*)

### Dhūmralocana (ch. 6)

- **The general burnt to ash** — Śumbha sends **Dhūmralocana ("Smoke-Eyed")** with 60,000 troops to drag her by the hair; the Goddess reduces him **to ashes with a single huṃkāra (humming syllable)**, and her **lion** mauls his army. (*Devī Māhātmya 6; [DNA of Hinduism](https://www.dnaofhinduism.com/ancient-wisdom-via-stories/the-story-of-slaying-dhumralochana-chanda-munda-and-raktabija-sri-durga-saptashati-chapter-6-7-8)*)

### Caṇḍa, Muṇḍa → Cāmuṇḍā (ch. 7)

- **Kālī springs from the brow** — facing **Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa**, the Goddess's wrath blackens her brow, and out leaps **Kālī**: gaunt, gaping-mouthed, lolling tongue, sunken red eyes, **skull-garland**, tiger-skin clad, wielding sword and noose. She devours the asura army — crushing elephants, riders and chariots in her mouth. (*Devī Māhātmya 7*)
- **The naming of Cāmuṇḍā** — Kālī beheads **Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa** and presents the two heads to Caṇḍikā, who decrees she shall be famed as **"Cāmuṇḍā"** (Caṇḍa + Muṇḍa). (*Devī Māhātmya 7; [Indica Today](https://www.indica.today/quick-reads/devi-mahatmya-part-iii-dhumralochana-chanda-munda-raktabija-nishumbha/)*)

### The Mātṛkās & Raktabīja (ch. 8)

- **The Seven (or Eight) Mothers emerge** — the **śaktis** of the major gods burst forth as goddesses, each a feminine mirror of her god, with his vāhana and weapons: **Brāhmaṇī** (from Brahmā, swan), **Māheśvarī** (Śiva, bull, trident), **Kaumārī** (Skanda, peacock), **Vaiṣṇavī** (Viṣṇu, Garuḍa, discus), **Vārāhī** (Varāha, boar-faced), **Nārasiṃhī** (Narasiṃha, lion-faced), **Aindrī / Indrāṇī** (Indra, elephant, vajra). With **Cāmuṇḍā** they form the **Saptamātṛkā / Aṣṭamātṛkā** circle. (*Devī Māhātmya 8; [Indica Today](https://www.indica.today/quick-reads/devi-mahatmya-part-iii-dhumralochana-chanda-munda-raktabija-nishumbha/)*)
  - *Variant (count):* sometimes eight (adding **Mahālakṣmī / Nārāyaṇī** or **Yogeśvarī**); lists differ across Purāṇas and Tantras. (*[disputed]; later Mātṛkā traditions*)
- **The unkillable Raktabīja** — the asura **Raktabīja ("Blood-Seed")** has the boon that **every drop of his blood reaching the ground spawns an identical clone**; conventional blows only multiply the army into thousands of Raktabījas. (*Devī Māhātmya 8*)
- **Kālī drinks the blood** — Caṇḍikā orders **Kālī/Cāmuṇḍā to swallow every drop**: as Caṇḍikā strikes him, Kālī spreads her tongue/mouth and **drinks the falling blood and devours the spawned clones** before any touch ground; bloodless at last, the single Raktabīja collapses dead. (*Devī Māhātmya 8; [DNA of Hinduism](https://www.dnaofhinduism.com/ancient-wisdom-via-stories/the-story-of-slaying-dhumralochana-chanda-munda-and-raktabija-sri-durga-saptashati-chapter-6-7-8)*)

### Niśumbha & Śumbha (ch. 9–10)

- **Death of Niśumbha (ch. 9)** — after the lion and the Devī rout the host, **Niśumbha** duels Caṇḍikā; she fells him. As he falls a being tries to emerge from his body, but the Goddess decapitates it. (*Devī Māhātmya 9; [Indica Today](https://www.indica.today/quick-reads/devi-mahatmya-part-iii-dhumralochana-chanda-munda-raktabija-nishumbha/)*)
- **"You depend on others!" — the absorption (ch. 10)** — enraged Śumbha taunts that she fights only with the help of all these goddesses. The Devī replies **"I alone exist here in the world; what second, other than I, is there?"** and **reabsorbs all the Mātṛkās and Kālī back into her single self** — then fights Śumbha one-on-one. (*Devī Māhātmya 10*) — the text's clearest **non-dual Śākta statement**: the many goddesses are one.
- **Death of Śumbha** — they battle in the sky and on earth; Śumbha hurls every weapon from his **eight arms**, but the Goddess **pierces him with her trident (or spear) through the heart**, and he falls dead. With the demon-kings gone, the gods regain heaven and sing the **Nārāyaṇī Stuti**. (*Devī Māhātmya 10–11; [Indica Today](https://www.indica.today/quick-reads/devi-mahatmya-part-iii-dhumralochana-chanda-munda-raktabija-nishumbha/)*)

### The Goddess's promise & epilogue (ch. 11–13)

- **Future incarnations foretold (ch. 11)** — in the **Nārāyaṇī Stuti** the Devī promises to descend again whenever demons trouble the world, listing future forms: **Raktadantikā, Śatākṣī / Śākambharī** (the "hundred-eyed," who feeds the world with vegetables in a great drought), **Durgā** (slayer of Durgama), **Bhīmā, Bhrāmarī** (the bee-goddess who slays Aruṇa). (*Devī Māhātmya 11; cf. Devī's "Vibhūti" list*)
- **The Phalaśruti (ch. 12)** — the Goddess proclaims the **merits of reciting the Māhātmya itself**: protection, prosperity, removal of calamity; the text becomes its own self-glorifying mantra. (*Devī Māhātmya 12*)
- **The frame closes (ch. 13)** — Suratha and Samādhi worship Devī for three years; she grants **Suratha** rebirth as the future **Manu Sāvarṇi** (eighth Manvantara, see [`../01-manvantaras/`](../01-manvantaras/)) and **Samādhi** spiritual liberation. (*Devī Māhātmya 13*)
  - *Note:* tying the merchant-king to the **future Sāvarṇi Manu** anchors this myth into the Manvantara chronology of the wider timeline.

## 7. The Three Great Forms (Tri-Mahādevī of the Text)

Distinct from the Tridevī (Lakṣmī/Sarasvatī/Pārvatī) of [`trimurti-and-tridevi.md §5`](trimurti-and-tridevi.md#5-the-tridevī--the-three-consort-energies-śakti), the **Mūrti Rahasya** appendix systematizes the text's own triad: (*Mūrti Rahasya; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Mahatmya)*)

- **Mahākālī** — tamas; ten faces, ten arms, dark; the power that put Viṣṇu to sleep (Prathama Carita). Iconography: weapons in every hand, treads the cosmos.
- **Mahālakṣmī** — rajas; the **Mahiṣāsura-mardinī** herself (Madhyama Carita); often counted the *central* form, born of all the gods' tejas. (*[disputed naming]:* this "Mahālakṣmī" is the warrior-Devī, **not** the wealth-Lakṣmī consort of Viṣṇu, though traditions conflate them.)
- **Mahāsarasvatī** — sattva; the **Kauśikī/Caṇḍikā** of the Śumbha cycle (Uttama Carita), eight-armed.

## 8. The Aṅgas (Limbs) and Liturgical Use

Recitation is bracketed by auxiliary texts ("aṅgas"), without which a ritual pāṭha is held incomplete: (*[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Mahatmya); living tradition*)

- **Preceding (pūrvāṅga):**
  - **Durgā Saptaślokī** — a "seven-verse" mini-Caṇḍī for when time is short. (*tradition*)
  - **Devī Kavaca (Kavacam)** — ~61 verses; an **armor** invoking the Goddess to guard each part of the body and direction. (*aṅga text*)
  - **Argalā Stotra** — ~27 couplets removing obstacles ("argalā" = bolt/bar). (*aṅga text*)
  - **Kīlaka Stotra** — ~16 verses; the **"pin/nail"** that unlocks the otherwise "sealed" potency of the Saptaśatī (the *kīlaka* tradition holds Śiva pinned the text's power, releasable only via this hymn). (*aṅga text*)
  - **Rātri Sūktam** — night-hymn (a **Vedic** version from Ṛgveda 10.127 and a **Tāntrika** version = the Brahma-stuti of ch. 1). (*Ṛgveda 10.127; Devī Māhātmya 1*)
  - **Kuñjikā Stotra** — a compact "key" hymn substituting for the others. (*later tradition*)
- **Succeeding (uttarāṅga) — the three Rahasyas:**
  - **Prādhānika Rahasya** — the Goddess as **Mahālakṣmī Prakṛti**, source of the gods.
  - **Vaikṛtika Rahasya** — her **transformations** into the three great forms.
  - **Mūrti Rahasya** — her descending **incarnations** (Śākambharī etc.).
  - plus **Devī Sūktam** (Vedic = Ṛgveda 10.125 **Vāk/Ambhṛṇī Sūkta**; Tāntrika = the ch. 5 "Yā Devī" litany). (*Ṛgveda 10.125; Devī Māhātmya 5*)

- **Festival use:**
  - **Navarātri** (autumn **Śāradīya** and spring **Vāsantī**) — the full Saptaśatī is recited over the nine nights, one carita per phase; **Durgā Pūjā** (Bengal) enacts the Mahiṣa-slaying climactically. (*living tradition*)
  - **Partial-recitation rule** — if the whole cannot be read, **the Madhyama (Mahiṣa) carita alone** may be recited as a complete unit, but an episode must never be left half-finished. (*[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Mahatmya); ritual manuals*)
  - **Caṇḍī Homa / Śata-Caṇḍī / Sahasra-Caṇḍī** — fire-rituals reciting the text 100 or 1000 times for major undertakings. (*Tantric ritual tradition*)

## 9. Mārkaṇḍeya vs. Devī-Bhāgavata — Two Framings

- **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa (Devī Māhātmya)** — the **compact, foundational** Śākta core (700 verses, 13 chapters), narrated by the sage **Medhas** to Suratha & Samādhi; treats the three caritas as above. This is the *liturgical* text. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa 81–93*)
- **Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa** — a **full Mahāpurāṇa (18,000 verses)**, the Śākta counter-scripture to the Vaiṣṇava **Bhāgavata**; it **retells and vastly expands** the same myths (Madhu-Kaiṭabha, Mahiṣa, Śumbha-Niśumbha) across its books, embeds the **Devī Gītā** (book 7) as the Goddess's own Vedānta discourse, and frames Devī as **Para-Brahman** above Viṣṇu/Śiva. (*Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Bhagavata_Purana)*)
  - *Variant (which is "the" Bhāgavata?):* Śāktas claim the **Devī-Bhāgavata** is the authentic Bhāgavata Mahāpurāṇa; Vaiṣṇavas reserve that title for the **Kṛṣṇa-centered Bhāgavata**. A live sectarian dispute. (*[disputed]; sectarian polemic*)
  - *Variant (frame detail):* both keep Suratha, Samādhi and the sage, but the Devī-Bhāgavata calls the sage **Sumedha** and elaborates the Devī Gītā teaching far beyond the Mārkaṇḍeya's terse frame. (*[Medha rishi — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medha_rishi)*)
- **Lalitā / Śrīvidyā strand (contrast)** — a *separate* Śākta supremacy text-cycle (Lalitā Tripurasundarī, Lalitā Sahasranāma, Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa) emphasizes the **benign-transcendent** Goddess rather than the **martial** Caṇḍī; the two streams = **Kālīkula** (fierce, Caṇḍī/Kālī) vs **Śrīkula** (graceful, Lalitā). (*see [`trimurti-and-tridevi.md §6–7`](trimurti-and-tridevi.md#6-sectarian-primacy--who-is-supreme)*)

## 10. Why It Matters in the Timeline

- It is the **scriptural charter of Śāktism** — the variant lens under which the Trimūrti are subordinate instruments of the Devī (catalogued as the "*Variant (Śākta)*" in [`trimurti-and-tridevi.md §1`](trimurti-and-tridevi.md#1-the-core-concept-trimūrti-and-the-three-functions)).
- Its frame seeds a **future Manu** (Sāvarṇi), tying a stand-alone myth into the [Manvantara chronology](../01-manvantaras/).
- Its goddesses (Durgā, Kālī, Cāmuṇḍā, the Mātṛkās, Śākambharī) become the trunk from which the **Daśa Mahāvidyā** and the Śakti-spectrum branch (see [`trimurti-and-tridevi.md §7`](trimurti-and-tridevi.md#7-the-great-goddess--durgā-kālī-and-the-śakti-spectrum)).
