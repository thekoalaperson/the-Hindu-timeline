# Devas, Asuras & the 14 Lokas

> Cosmic geography and its principal beings. This is a core-spine node of `02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`: the vertical map of worlds (lokas) stacked along Mount Meru, the gods (devas) who hold the upper realms, and the asuras (daityas/danavas) who contest them. Events here recur across every yuga of the [current Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/) — the deva–asura conflict is the engine of cosmic history.

---

## 1. The Vertical Cosmos — 14 Lokas

The universe (Brahmanda, "Brahma's egg") is structured as **14 worlds**: 7 upper (`urdhva`) and 7 lower (`adho`/Patala), stacked along the cosmic axis with **Mount Meru** at the center of the middle world (Bhuloka). The 7 upper are also the three **vyahritis** of the Gayatri (Bhu, Bhuvar, Svar) extended upward by four more.

### 1a. The 7 Upper Lokas (Urdhva-loka) — Bhu up to Satya

- **Bhuloka (Bhu)** — the earth-plane; realm of humans, animals, embodied beings. Center is Mount Meru. (Vishnu Purana II; Bhagavata Purana 5)
- **Bhuvarloka (Bhuvar)** — the antariksha/atmosphere between earth and sun; realm of siddhas, munis, and the departed manes; the space the sun, moon, planets traverse. (Vishnu Purana; Bhagavata 5)
- **Svarloka (Svar / Svarga)** — Indra's heaven, "the heaven of the gods," located around/above the polar star Dhruva; abode of the 33 devas, gandharvas, apsaras; reached by meritorious karma. (Vishnu Purana; Bhagavata 5)
- **Maharloka (Mahar)** — abode of great sages (e.g. Bhrigu) and beings who survive the cosmic dissolution at the end of a Kalpa; said to persist one Day of Brahma. (Vishnu Purana; Bhagavata 5)
- **Janaloka (Jana)** — realm of Brahma's mind-born sons (the Kumaras, sages) and beings of pure mind. (Vishnu Purana; Bhagavata 5)
- **Tapoloka (Tapas)** — realm of austerity; abode of the Vairaja deities / Vaibhraja ascetics; intense tapas. (Vishnu Purana; Bhagavata 5)
- **Satyaloka (Satya / Brahmaloka)** — highest world, the abode of **Brahma** the creator; liberation-adjacent; those who reach it are not reborn (krama-mukti). (Vishnu Purana; Bhagavata 5)

  - *Variant (naming):* the seventh is called **Satyaloka** in most Puranas but interchangeably **Brahmaloka**; some lists separate "Brahmaloka" as a distinct higher abode of Vishnu/Vaikuntha beyond the 14. (Loka, Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (alternative loka list):* one tradition substitutes a 14-fold list of *Bhur, Bhuvar, Suvar, Mahar, Janar, Tapar, Satya, Brahma, Pitri, Soma, Indra, Gandharva, Rakshasa, Yaksha lokas* — a being-based rather than strictly stacked scheme. [late/variant text] (Loka, Wikipedia)

### 1b. The 7 Lower Lokas (Patala / Adho-loka)

The seven subterranean worlds are described **not as hells but as splendid realms** of wealth, palaces, and Naga (serpent) civilizations — Narada is said to have praised Patala as more beautiful than Svarga. Located ~70,000 yojanas below the earth, each region 10,000 yojanas thick. (Vishnu Purana II.5)

**Bhagavata Purana & Padma Purana scheme** (most commonly cited, top→bottom):
- **Atala** — realm of Bala, son of Maya; said to generate maddening women. (Bhagavata 5.24)
- **Vitala** — abode of Shiva as Hatakeshvara with Bhavani; river Hataki flows here. (Bhagavata 5.24)
- **Sutala** — kingdom of the virtuous Asura king **Bali**, guarded by Vishnu as Vamana's gatekeeper. (Bhagavata 5.24)
- **Talatala** — realm of the Danava architect **Maya**, master of sorcery, protected by Shiva. (Bhagavata 5.24)
- **Mahatala** — abode of many-hooded Nagas (sons of Kadru: Kuhaka, Taksaka, etc.). (Bhagavata 5.24)
- **Rasatala** — dwelling of the Daityas and Danavas (Panis, Nivata-kavachas), enemies of the devas. (Bhagavata 5.24)
- **Patala** — lowest; kingdom of the Naga king **Vasuki** and serpents; below it rests **Shesha/Ananta** bearing the worlds. (Bhagavata 5.24)

  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana II.5):* lists the seven as **Atala, Vitala, Nitala, Gabhastimat (Garbhastimat), Mahatala, Sutala, Patala** — note **Nitala** and **Gabhastimat** replace Sutala/Talatala/Rasatala of the Bhagavata order, and the sequence differs. (Patala, Wikipedia; Vishnu Purana II.5)
  - *Variant (other lists):* some texts give the lower seven as Tala, Atala, Vitala, Nitala, Talatala, Mahatala, Sutala — reflecting unsettled nomenclature across Puranas. (Loka, Wikipedia)
  - *Note:* the 7 Patalas are distinct from the **28 Narakas** (hells of punishment, e.g. Tamisra, Raurava, Kumbhipaka), which the Bhagavata Purana places below Patala under Yama's jurisdiction — see hells, not part of the 14-loka stack proper. (Bhagavata 5.26)

### 1c. Mount Meru — the Cosmic Axis

- **Mount Meru (Sumeru / Mahameru)** — golden, five-peaked mountain at the center of Bhuloka and Jambudvipa; the axis (axis mundi) around which sun, moon, and stars revolve; abodes of the gods crown its summit (Brahma's city Brahmapuri at the peak, the Lokapalas' cities around it). Made of gold, jeweled. Height given as **84,000 yojanas**. (Matsya Purana; Bhagavata Purana 5; Surya Siddhanta — "bhuva-madhya")
  - The earth-disc Bhu-mandala is centered on Meru, surrounded by concentric continents (dvipas) and oceans; **Jambudvipa** (with Bharata-varsha, our region) lies at the center/south. (Bhagavata 5; Vishnu Purana II)
  - *Variant:* Meru is shared cosmology — Buddhist and Jain texts place it centrally too, with differing dimensions; "yojana" length itself varies (~9–11.5 km). (Mount Meru, Wikipedia)

## 2. The Devas — the 33 and the Lokapalas

The canonical Vedic pantheon is the **Trayastrimsha — "the 33"** (often inflated in popular speech to "33 crore," a misreading of *koti* = "type/category" as "ten million"). Grouped as **8 Vasus + 11 Rudras + 12 Adityas + 2** (the +2 vary by source). (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 3.9; Thirty-three gods, Wikipedia)

- **The 8 Vasus** (elemental/material deities): Dhara (earth), Anala/Agni (fire), Anila/Vayu (wind), Apas (water), Dyaus/Prabhasa (sky), Soma/Chandra (moon), Surya/Pratyusha (sun), Dhruva (pole-star). (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad; Mahabharata)
  - *Variant:* lists of the 8 Vasus differ across texts (e.g. Mahabharata vs. Puranas swap members like Ahar/Pratyusha/Prabhasa). Bhishma was the incarnated Vasu Dyaus, cursed to be born human. (Mahabharata, Adi Parva)
- **The 11 Rudras** (fierce/storm forms of Shiva): names vary widely — common Puranic set includes Manyu, Manu, Mahinasa, Mahan, Shiva, Ritadhvaja, Ugraretas, Bhava, Kala, Vamadeva, Dhritavrata; another counts them as the 10 pranas/senses + mind. (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad; Vishnu Purana)
- **The 12 Adityas** (solar/sovereign deities, one per month): Vishnu, Aryaman, Indra, Tvashtr, Varuna, Bhaga, Savitr, Vivasvat, Amsha, Mitra, Pushan, Daksha (lists vary). Born of Aditi and Kashyapa. (Bhagavata Purana; Vishnu Purana)
  - *Variant:* the +2 completing 33 are given as **Indra & Prajapati** (Brihadaranyaka), or as the **2 Ashvins** (Brahmanas), or Indra + the Ashvins. (Thirty-three gods, Wikipedia)

### 2a. Principal Devas (individual)

- **Indra (Shakra)** — king of the devas, lord of Svarga, wielder of the vajra (thunderbolt); rain/storm god, foremost warrior of the gods. Slays Vritra; his throne is perpetually threatened by ascetics' tapas and by asuras. (Rigveda; Puranas)
- **Agni** — fire god; mouth of the gods who carries offerings; mediator between humans and devas; one of the Vasus. (Rigveda)
- **Varuna** — god of cosmic order (rita), waters, and the oceans; once a sovereign Asura/Aditya guardian of moral law; later lord of the western quarter. (Rigveda; Puranas)
- **Yama** — god of death and dharma, judge of the dead, lord of the southern quarter and of the Narakas; son of Vivasvat (the Sun), brother of Vaivasvata Manu and the Yamuna. (Rigveda; Katha Upanishad; Puranas)
- **Vayu** — wind/breath god (prana); father of Hanuman and Bhima; one of the Vasus. (Rigveda; Ramayana; Mahabharata)
- **Surya (Vivasvat)** — the sun, an Aditya; father of Vaivasvata Manu, Yama, and the Ashvins; central to the [Surya/Solar lineage](../03-lineages-vamsha/). (Rigveda; Surya Siddhanta)
- **The Ashvins** — twin physician-horsemen of dawn; restore youth and heal. (Rigveda)

### 2b. The Lokapalas — Guardians of the Directions

The eight **Dikpalas** guard the cardinal/intercardinal directions: **Indra** (East), **Agni** (SE), **Yama** (South), **Nirriti** (SW), **Varuna** (West), **Vayu** (NW), **Kubera** (North, lord of wealth & the Yakshas), **Ishana/Shiva** (NE). (Puranas; Vastu tradition)

## 3. The Asuras — Daityas, Danavas & the Cosmic Antagonists

**Asura** is the broad term for the devas' adversaries. In the earliest Rigveda, *asura* meant "mighty lord" and applied to Varuna and other Adityas (the "good asuras"); only later did it polarize into "anti-god." (Asura, Wikipedia)

- **Daityas** — descendants of **Diti** and the sage Kashyapa (e.g. Hiranyakashipu, Hiranyaksha, Prahlada, Bali). (Vishnu Purana; Bhagavata)
- **Danavas** — descendants of **Danu** and Kashyapa (e.g. Vritra, Maya, the Nivata-kavachas); often the older/primordial asuras led against Indra. (Rigveda; Puranas)
- **Rakshasas** — flesh-eating night-demons (e.g. Ravana's line); descended via Pulastya/Kashyapa; distinct from Daityas/Danavas though loosely grouped as asuras. (Ramayana; Puranas)
- *Variant (origin myth):* Daityas and Danavas were exiled from heaven by the devas in **Krita/Satya Yuga** and took refuge in the Vindhya mountains and the sea (Patala). (Asura sources; Puranas)
- *Note:* devas and asuras are **half-brothers** (both sons of Kashyapa by different mothers, Aditi vs. Diti/Danu) — the conflict is a family civil war over cosmic sovereignty, not pure good vs. evil. Many asuras are devout (Prahlada, Bali). (Bhagavata Purana)

### 3a. Key Asura Kings & Their Stories

- **Hiranyakashipu** — Daitya king who won a boon (from Brahma) against death by man or beast, day or night, indoors or outdoors, on earth/sky, by any weapon; tyrannized the cosmos, persecuted his Vishnu-devotee son **Prahlada**. Slain by **Vishnu as Narasimha** (man-lion) at twilight, on a threshold, on Vishnu's lap, with claws — evading every clause of the boon. Brother of **Hiranyaksha** (killed by Vishnu as **Varaha**, the boar, who lifted the earth from the cosmic ocean). (Bhagavata Purana 7; Vishnu Purana) — celebrated at **Holika/Holi**.
  - Lineage: Hiranyakashipu → Prahlada → **Virochana** → **Bali**.
- **Bali (Mahabali)** — virtuous, generous Daitya emperor (grandson of Prahlada) who conquered the three worlds. Vishnu incarnates as the dwarf **Vamana**, begs three paces of land, then expands to cosmic size — covering earth and heaven in two strides; for the third Bali offers his own head. Rewarded for his integrity with kingship of **Sutala** and a promise to be a future Indra; returns yearly (festival **Onam** in Kerala). (Bhagavata Purana 8; Vishnu Purana)
- **Vritra** — the great Danava/serpent-drought demon who held back the cosmic waters; archetypal enemy of **Indra**, slain by Indra's vajra (fashioned from sage Dadhichi's bones) — the foundational deva–asura combat. (Rigveda 1.32; Bhagavata 6 reframes Vritra as the pious devotee Vritrasura). 
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana 6):* Vritra is a noble Vishnu-devotee (former gandharva/brahmin Chitraketu cursed), who fights Indra righteously and attains liberation — inverting the Vedic "demon" reading. (Bhagavata 6.9–13)
- **Ravana** — ten-headed **Rakshasa** king of Lanka, son of sage Vishrava, devotee of Shiva and master of the Vedas; abducts Sita; slain by **Vishnu as Rama**. The central antagonist of the [Treta Yuga / Ramayana](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/treta/). (Ramayana)
  - *Variant:* Ravana (with brother Kumbhakarna) is identified as a reincarnation of **Jaya/Vijaya**, Vishnu's cursed gatekeepers — also born as Hiranyakashipu/Hiranyaksha and Shishupala/Dantavakra across three lives. (Bhagavata Purana 7)
- **Mahishasura** — buffalo-demon, boon of invincibility against all males; slain by the goddess **Durga** (Mahishasuramardini) — central to Shakta tradition and Durga Puja/Navaratri. (Devi Mahatmya / Markandeya Purana) [Shakta tradition]
- **Maya (Mayasura)** — Danava architect-sorcerer, builder of celestial palaces (incl. the Pandavas' Maya Sabha); dwells in Talatala. (Mahabharata; Bhagavata)
- **Tarakasura** — asura slayable only by Shiva's son; killed by **Kartikeya/Skanda**. (Skanda Purana; Shiva Purana) [Shaiva tradition]
- **The Samudra Manthana asuras** — at the churning of the ocean of milk, devas and asuras cooperate then clash; **Rahu** (asura who steals amrita) is beheaded by Vishnu as Mohini, becoming the eclipse-node. (Bhagavata 8; Vishnu Purana)

## 4. The Deva–Asura Dynamic (cross-cutting pattern)

- Sovereignty of the three worlds oscillates: asuras win Svarga through tapas/boons; a Vishnu avatara or a goddess restores deva rule — the **recurring structural event of every mahayuga**.
- The **office of Indra** is impermanent: Bali is promised a future Indra-ship; 14 Indras reign across the 14 Manvantaras.
- Variant valuations: Vaishnava texts (Bhagavata) ennoble asura devotees (Prahlada, Bali, Vritra); Shakta texts (Devi Mahatmya) center the Goddess as asura-slayer; Shaiva texts foreground Shiva/Skanda's asura-conflicts (Tarakasura, Tripura).
  - *Variant (Tripurantaka):* Shiva destroys the three flying cities (Tripura) of the asuras Tarakaksha, Vidyunmali, Kamalaksha with a single arrow. (Shiva Purana; Mahabharata) [Shaiva tradition]
