# Agni

> Cosmic address: the **fire-god** — the divine mouth that eats every offering, the priest-messenger who carries oblations up to the gods, and the one deity present on earth, in the atmosphere, and in heaven at once. Agni is the most-invoked god of the [Ṛg Veda](../90-literature-corpus/shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) after Indra (≈200 hymns), the *Lokapāla* (world-guardian) of the **southeast (Āgneya)** quarter, a member of the Vedic triad with Sūrya and Vāyu/Indra, and the indispensable hinge of every [yajña](../90-literature-corpus/yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md). Parent: [02 — Deities and Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md). Master index: [the Hindu Timeline](../README.md).

Agni is the god the sacrifice cannot do without: the [yajña](../90-literature-corpus/yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md) is *literally* him. Every offering is "eaten" by his flames and conveyed as smoke to the [devas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md); he is therefore both **priest** (*hotṛ*, *purohita*) and **messenger** (*dūta*) — the two-way switchboard between humans and gods. He is also uniquely *immanent*: the same fire is in the sun, in lightning, in the hearth, in the digesting belly, in the funeral pyre, and in the inner heat (*tapas*) of the ascetic. The Ṛg Veda's very first word is his name (*agním īḷe* — "I praise Agni").

## Names, epithets & nature

- **Agni** — "fire" (cognate with Latin *ignis*, Russian *ogon'*, English *ignite*); the personified sacrificial fire and one of the oldest Indo-European deities (Ṛg Veda 1.1).
- **Vaiśvānara** — "belonging to all men / relating to all people"; Agni as the universal, cosmic, all-pervading fire — in heaven the sun, in the body the digestive fire (Ṛg Veda; [Chāndogya](../90-literature-corpus/principal-upanishads-detail.md) 5.11–18; [Gītā](../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md) 15.14). See § Vaiśvānara below.
- **Jātavedas** — "knower of all creatures / all that is born," or "in whom wealth is found"; Agni as omniscient witness who knows every being's deeds — invoked especially in the funeral and *kravyāda* contexts (Ṛg Veda 1.44; 10.16).
- **Hutāśana / Hutabhuj / Havyavāhana** — "eater of the oblation" / "conveyor of the offering"; the function-name for Agni-as-fire-of-sacrifice.
- **Pāvaka** — "purifier"; fire that cleanses. **Śuci** — "the bright/pure." **Anala** — "fire" (a name also of one of the eight [Vasus](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md), Agni being chief of the Vasus). **Vahni** — "the bearer."
- **Saptajihva / Saptārci** — "seven-tongued" / "seven-rayed": his seven flame-tongues by which he licks up offerings (named in the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 1.2.4: Kālī, Karālī, Manojavā, Sulohitā, Sudhūmravarṇā, Sphuliṅginī, Viśvarucī).
- **Kravyāda** — "flesh-eater"; the funeral-pyre aspect of Agni who consumes corpses, distinguished in the Veda from the auspicious sacrificial Agni (Ṛg Veda 10.16: "do not scorch him utterly, O Jātavedas... consign the kravyāda fire elsewhere").
- **Dūta** — "messenger"; **Hotṛ / Purohita** — "invoker / chief priest"; Agni's two ritual offices.
- **Āgneya / Diś-pāla of the SE** — guardian of the southeast direction in the [Lokapāla](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) scheme.

## The mouth of the gods & the messenger (Vedic core function)

- **The mouth of the gods (*devānām mukham*)** — the gods have no other way to eat; the oblation poured into Agni *is* the meal of the devas. Whatever is offered into fire reaches its intended deity. This makes Agni the **first** invited and **first** worshipped deity of any rite (Ṛg Veda 1.1; Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa).
- **The conveyor (*havyavāhana*)** — the smoke carries the offering upward; Agni is the chariot/vehicle that transports *havya* (offering to gods) and *kavya* (offering to ancestors) to their destinations.
- **Priest of the gods & of men** — Agni is simultaneously the *purohita* (domestic/household priest) of the human sacrificer and the divine *hotṛ* who invokes the other gods to the rite; he stands at the threshold "facing both ways" (Ṛg Veda 1.1.1, *purohitam... ratnadhātamam*).
- **The double messenger** — he carries mortals' prayers up *and* brings the gods down to the sacrificial grass (*barhis*); the rite is conceived as Agni summoning the gods to be seated and fed.
- **Witness & oath-keeper** — as Jātavedas who "knows all that is born," Agni is the witness to vows; the **seven steps (saptapadī)** and **circumambulation of the fire** seal Hindu marriage to this day (a custom traced to the Vedic householder's fire). See [Varṇa-Āśrama & Saṃskāras](../05-concepts-and-dharma/varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md).

## Agni's births & hiding

Agni is the most "born" of the gods — the Veda dwells on his triple/multiple origin because fire visibly *appears* in many places.

- **The three births / three abodes (*tridhātu*)** — Agni is born in **three** stations: in **heaven** (as the sun / lightning), in the **atmosphere/waters** (as lightning in the storm-cloud, the "embryo of the waters," *apām garbha*), and on **earth** (as the kindled, hearth and altar fire). This triplicity makes him the link uniting the three worlds (Ṛg Veda 1.95; 10.45; Bṛhaddevatā).
  - *Variant (count):* texts also speak of Agni's **three** or even **many** births — "thrice-born," "born in three places," and, with the daily re-kindling, "born again and again," ever-young yet ancient (Ṛg Veda 2.1; 1.95.2 "ten maidens" = the ten fingers that produce him by friction).
- **Born from the fire-drills (*araṇi*)** — kindled daily by churning the two fire-sticks (*adhararaṇi* / *uttararaṇi*); the "ten maidens" / "ten sisters" are the kindler's ten fingers, and Agni is the new-born infant they bring forth each dawn (Ṛg Veda 1.95.2; 3.29).
- **Embryo of the waters (*Apām Napāt*)** — "child/grandson of the waters"; Agni latent within water (lightning in cloud, fire in the sea). *Apām Napāt* is sometimes a near-independent deity, sometimes identified with Agni (Ṛg Veda 2.35).
- **Son of Dyaus/Pṛthivī; chief of the Vasus** — variously called son of Heaven-and-Earth, son of Aditi, or the foremost of the eight [Vasus](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) (where his name is **Anala**) (Ṛg Veda; Mahābhārata).
- **Carried to men by Mātariśvan** — when fire was hidden/lost to humans, **Mātariśvan** (a divine being, often identified with Vāyu the wind, or a Prometheus-like figure) **stole/fetched Agni from heaven** and gave him to the **Bhṛgus**, who "established him among the clans of men" (Ṛg Veda 1.60; 3.9; 6.8). The **Bhṛgu** clan is repeatedly credited with first kindling/spreading sacrificial fire.
  - *Variant (Atharvan):* in another strand the priest-sage **Atharvan** is the first to churn forth and install Agni; the **Aṅgirases** and **Bhṛgus** are his ancient fire-finding priestly families. [scholarly]
- **Agni in hiding (the recurring myth)** — Agni, weary of his role (afraid the gods would, like his "elder brothers," be consumed; or fleeing an oppressive duty), **hides in the waters and in the plants**. The gods search; he is betrayed and found.
  - *Variant (the fish/frogs/elephant betray him):* aquatic creatures — in tellings the **fish** (or **frogs**) — disclose Agni's hiding-place in the waters; Agni curses them (e.g., that fish be eaten by all). When he hides in trees/plants the **elephant** or **parrot** reveals him; hence Agni is "in" wood and is released by friction (Mahābhārata, *Vana*/*Anuśāsana* strands; Brāhmaṇa myths). [folk]
  - *Variant (curse to consume all):* sage **Bhṛgu** (in some texts) curses Agni to become *sarva-bhakṣa* / *sarva-bhuj* — "eater of everything, pure and impure"; distressed, Agni withdraws and hides, and the world loses fire until the gods coax him back with the boon that fire purifies whatever it touches, so nothing it eats defiles it (Mahābhārata). [late text]
- **The Aṅgirases & "the gods kindled him"** — myth that the gods (or the Aṅgirases) first generated Agni and made him the immortal among mortals, the "guest" (*atithi*) in every home (Ṛg Veda 1.31; 5.1).

## The burning of the Khāṇḍava forest

The single greatest narrative episode of Agni, from the **Ādi Parva** of the [Mahābhārata](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata-minor-episodes.md) (Khāṇḍava-daha Parva).

- **Agni's sickness from Śvetaki's sacrifice** — King **Śvetaki** performed a colossal twelve-year sacrifice in which Agni was fed an unbroken stream of ghee/offerings; gorged and over-sated, Agni fell ill, lost his lustre and appetite, and grew pale (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva).
  - *Variant (Brahmā's prescription):* **Brahmā** tells Agni that the cure is to consume the **Khāṇḍava forest** — full of medicinal, fatty, varied creatures and herbs whose rich fat will restore his vigour (Mahābhārata).
- **Indra's protection of Khāṇḍava** — the forest is home to Indra's friend, the **Nāga king Takṣaka**, so **Indra** repeatedly extinguishes Agni's fire with rain whenever he tries to burn it; Agni fails seven times (Mahābhārata).
- **Agni approaches Kṛṣṇa & Arjuna** — disguised as a **Brāhmaṇa**, Agni asks the two heroes (resting by the Yamunā) to help him "eat" the forest by holding off Indra's rain (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva).
- **The divine weapons given in payment** — to enable them, Agni (via **Varuṇa**) procures: for **Arjuna** the bow **Gāṇḍīva**, two inexhaustible quivers, and the divine chariot with the **Hanumān-bannered** flag and white celestial horses; for **Kṛṣṇa** the **Sudarśana Cakra** and the mace **Kaumodakī** (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva). See [Artifacts, Symbols & Arts](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/README.md).
- **The conflagration** — Agni surrounds Khāṇḍava with his seven flames; Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna ring the forest and shoot down every creature that flees and beat back Indra and the gods who come to its defense, for **five (or fifteen) days/nights** until Agni is fully sated (Mahābhārata).
  - *Variant (duration):* some recensions say the burning lasted **fifteen** days; the figure varies.
- **The six survivors** — Khāṇḍava's near-total destruction spares only six: **Aśvasena** (Takṣaka's son, who escaped via his mother), the **Maya Dānava** (the architect-demon, saved by Arjuna), and **four śārṅgaka birds** (the chicks of the sage **Mandapāla**, protected by a mother bird) (Mahābhārata).
- **Maya's gift** — spared by Arjuna, the asura-architect **Maya** later builds the wondrous illusion-palace **Mayasabhā** at Indraprastha — the hall whose optical tricks provoke Duryodhana's humiliation and the chain leading to the war (Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva). Cross-link: the [Mahābhārata cycle](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata-minor-episodes.md).
- **Ecological reading** — modern readings treat Khāṇḍava as a parable of conquest/clearing of forest and displacement of forest-dwelling (Nāga) peoples by the Indo-Aryan/agrarian order. [scholarly]

## Vaiśvānara — the digestive / cosmic fire

- **Vaiśvānara = the all-pervading fire** — Agni as the single fire manifest at every level: as the **sun** in the sky, as **lightning** in the atmosphere, as the **altar fire** on earth, and as the **digestive fire** in the belly of every living being (Ṛg Veda; [Chāndogya Upaniṣad](../90-literature-corpus/principal-upanishads-detail.md) 5.11–18, the *Vaiśvānara-vidyā*).
- **The Gītā verse** — Kṛṣṇa: *"Becoming Vaiśvānara, I abide in the bodies of living beings; joined with the in-breath and out-breath (prāṇa-apāna), I digest the four kinds of food"* ([Bhagavad Gītā](../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md) 15.14). The god of the hearth and altar is here interiorized as the metabolic fire — eating becomes an offering into the inner Agni.
- **Prāṇāgnihotra** — the doctrine (Prāṇāgnihotra Upaniṣad; Chāndogya) that **eating is an internal fire-sacrifice**: the food is the oblation, the digestive Agni the receiving fire, the five *prāṇas* the five fires; one need not light an external altar to perform the *agnihotra*. [scholarly]
- **The inner fires in the body** — texts map the gārhapatya to the navel/belly (digestion), the dakṣiṇa fire (half-moon shaped) to the heart, the āhavanīya to the prāṇa/face — the three ritual fires re-read as fires within the person (Prāṇāgnihotra & related Upaniṣads). [late text]
- **Vaiśvānara as the waking self** — in [Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad](../90-literature-corpus/principal-upanishads-detail.md), **Vaiśvānara** names the **waking state (jāgrat)** of the self, enjoying the gross world — extending the "universal fire" into a name for ordinary consciousness. [scholarly]

## The three sacred fires (Tretāgni) & ritual structure

- **The three śrauta fires (*tretāgni*)** — the established Vedic householder maintains three fires arranged on the sacrificial ground (see [Yajñas and Vedic Rituals](../90-literature-corpus/yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md)):
  - **Gārhapatya** — the round, domestic/household fire in the west, kindled first and kept perpetually burning; the "household-master's" fire from which the others are taken; symbolically earth.
  - **Āhavanīya** — the square "offering" fire in the east, into which oblations to the gods are actually poured; the fire of invocation/welcome; symbolically heaven.
  - **Dakṣiṇāgni** (a.k.a. **Anvāhāryapacana**) — the crescent/half-moon-shaped southern fire, used for offerings to ancestors (*pitṛs*) and to ward off hostile/evil forces; symbolically the atmosphere/the south (realm of Yama).
- **The single-fire (*aupāsana / smārta*) household** — the ordinary householder maintains the one domestic **aupāsana** fire (the "nuptial fire" lit at marriage) for the daily/seasonal *gṛhya* rites; the threefold *śrauta* set belongs to the more elaborate solemn ritual. See [Varṇa-Āśrama & Saṃskāras](../05-concepts-and-dharma/varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md).
- **Agnyādhāna / Agnihotra** — the **establishment of the fires** (*agnyādheya*) is the foundational consecration of a Vedic householder; the **Agnihotra** is the twice-daily (dawn/dusk) milk-oblation into fire, the simplest perpetual offering. See [Yajñas and Vedic Rituals](../90-literature-corpus/yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md).
- **Agnicayana** — the grandest fire-altar rite: the year-long construction of a **falcon-shaped (śyena) brick altar** of 10,800 bricks to "rebuild" the dismembered Prajāpati as the cosmic Agni (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa); one of the oldest continuously-performed rituals on earth. [scholarly]
- **Symbolic triads** — the three fires are mapped onto earth-atmosphere-heaven, body-life-mind, and (interiorized) the three inner fires of the Vaiśvānara doctrine above.

## Iconography, family & associations

- **Form** — typically depicted **red** or flame-coloured, with **two heads/faces** (read as immortality & life, or his benevolent & devouring aspects), **two (or up to seven) arms**, a **pot-belly**, flaming hair/garland, **seven tongues of flame** (*saptajihva*), **seven arms** or seven rays in some forms, smoke for a banner, and carrying a flaming spear/javelin, an axe, a torch, a fan, and a ladle/spoon (*sruk/sruva*) for offerings.
- **Vāhana: the ram (meṣa)** — Agni rides a **ram**, emblem of sacrificial fire; in the **Rohitāśva** form he has no ram but a **chariot drawn by seven red (rohita) horses** (or one seven-tongued flame-horse), paralleling the sun's chariot.
- **Svāhā — his consort** — **Svāhā** ("so be it / well-said"), the personified ritual exclamation uttered when pouring oblations (*idam agnaye svāhā*), is Agni's wife and his Śakti; the offering only "reaches" when sealed with her cry. (A separate consort **Svadhā** is the call for offerings to the ancestors.)
  - *Variant (Svāhā's pursuit):* in Purāṇic/[Skanda](../04-deep-dives/README.md) lore Svāhā, in love with Agni, takes the forms of six of the seven [Saptarṣi](saptarishi.md) wives to unite with him — a strand woven into the **birth of [Kārttikeya](ganesha-and-kartikeya.md)** (the seed of Agni/Śiva and the six "Kṛttikā" mothers producing the six-headed war-god). See [Gaṇeśa & Kārttikeya](ganesha-and-kartikeya.md). [variant]
- **Three sons (Vāyu Purāṇa)** — Agni's three forms as sons: **Pāvaka** (the electric/lightning fire), **Pavamāna** (fire from friction, *araṇi*-born), and **Śuci** (the solar fire) — together the "three fires" personified.
- **Son Skanda/Kārttikeya** — by the most common Purāṇic account Agni receives Śiva's seed and is the proximate father (with the Kṛttikās/Gaṅgā) of the war-god [Kārttikeya](ganesha-and-kartikeya.md). [variant]
- **Chief of the Vasus** — among the eight [Vasus](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) Agni (as **Anala/Pāvaka**) is foremost; his elemental brothers are the other natural powers.
- **Lokapāla of the southeast** — guardian of the **Āgneya** (SE) quarter; the SE corner of a house/temple (the **agni-koṇa**) is therefore the canonical place for the kitchen/hearth in [Vāstu](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/README.md).
- **Decline & persistence** — though pre-eminent in the Veda, Agni recedes as an independent *worshipped* god in the Purāṇic/devotional age, yet remains **structurally indispensable**: no Hindu marriage, cremation, homa, or major saṃskāra occurs without him. His worship "did not die — it dissolved into ritual." [scholarly]
