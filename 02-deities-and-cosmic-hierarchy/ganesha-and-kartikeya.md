# Ganesha & Kartikeya — The Sons of Shiva-Parvati

> Where this sits in the hierarchy: `02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/` — a deep expansion of the
> household of Śiva-Pārvatī sketched in [`trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](trimurti-and-tridevi.md) and
> [`shiva-myth-cycle.md`](shiva-myth-cycle.md). Those nodes fix the parents on the cosmic clock; **this
> file** catalogues the two sons — **Gaṇeśa** (the elder-or-younger remover of obstacles) and
> **Kārtikeya / Skanda / Murugan** (the war-god born to slay Tārakāsura) — each myth as a dense,
> source-attributed event with full Purāṇic and regional (Tamil) variants. These deeds recur across
> yugas but cluster in the [Devāsura wars](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) and the dictation of the
> [Mahābhārata](../04-deep-dives/) in the [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md).

## Part I — Gaṇeśa (Gaṇapati, Vighneśvara)

The lord of Śiva's *gaṇas* (hosts), invoked first before any rite — patron of beginnings, remover of
obstacles (*vighna-harta*) and, paradoxically, their placer (*vighna-karta*). The texts do **not** agree
on how he came to be or how he got his head; all major versions are catalogued below.

### Birth & the elephant head

- **The turmeric/body-dirt origin (most popular)** — Pārvatī, wanting a guardian while she bathed, forms a
  boy from the turmeric paste (*haridrā*) / scurf rubbed from her own body and breathes life into him; he
  is wholly *her* creation, with no part of Śiva. (Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra-saṃhitā)
- **The beheading by Śiva** — the boy, posted at the door, bars Śiva himself from entering Pārvatī's
  chamber; not recognising the stranger as his master, he fights Śiva's gaṇas and is finally decapitated
  by Śiva's triśūla (or by Viṣṇu's discus in some tellings). (Śiva Purāṇa)
- **The restoration with an elephant's head** — to console the enraged, grieving Pārvatī (who threatens to
  destroy creation), Śiva orders the head of the first living creature found facing north — an elephant —
  fixed onto the body, reviving him as **Gajānana**, and grants him precedence over all gods and first
  worship. (Śiva Purāṇa)
  - *Variant (Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa):* Gaṇeśa is born a perfect, radiant infant from Pārvatī's womb,
    blessed by Śiva and Viṣṇu. At the naming feast **Śani (Saturn)**, warned his gaze is destructive,
    is pressed by Pārvatī to look; his glance burns off the child's head. **Viṣṇu** flies on Garuḍa,
    finds a young elephant sleeping head-to-north on the bank of the Puṣpabhadra, and brings its head.
  - *Variant (Śiva laughter / given by Śiva):* in some Purāṇic and folk strands Gaṇeśa is produced from
    Śiva's own laughter or directly gifted by Śiva to Pārvatī as a boon-child — making him Śiva's, not
    purely Pārvatī's. [variant]
  - *Variant (Mātsya / Varāha / Liṅga Purāṇas):* parallel accounts differ on who beheads him and who
    supplies the elephant head; some make Pārvatī create him from her bath-substance mixed with Gaṅgā's
    waters (hence the epithet **Gāṅgeya** shared confusingly with Skanda). [late text]
  - *Variant (the "always elephant-headed" view):* a strand holds he was elephant-headed from creation
    with no beheading at all, the head being an emblem of cosmic *praṇava* (Oṃ), not a repair. [scholarly]

### The broken tusk (*Ekadanta*)

- **Scribe of the Mahābhārata** — when Vyāsa needs a writer fast enough to keep up with his composition,
  Gaṇeśa agrees on condition that Vyāsa never pause; Vyāsa counters that Gaṇeśa must understand each verse
  before writing it (so Vyāsa inserts knotty verses to buy thinking time). Mid-dictation Gaṇeśa's reed
  pen breaks, so he snaps off his own right tusk and writes on, completing the epic — hence **Ekadanta**,
  the one-tusked. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva — frame narrative; widely retold)
- *Variant (fight with Paraśurāma):* Paraśurāma comes to Kailāsa to see Śiva (asleep); Gaṇeśa blocks him;
  in the duel Paraśurāma hurls Śiva's own axe (*paraśu*, a gift to him), which Gaṇeśa — out of reverence
  for the weapon — takes on his tusk rather than dodge, breaking it. (Brahmavaivarta / Purāṇic folk)
- *Variant (the moon-throwing):* the very tusk he ripped out to fling at the moon (see below) is the lost
  one. [folk tradition]

### The race around the parents (vs Kārtikeya)

- **The contest for the fruit / for marriage** — Śiva and Pārvatī set their two sons a race: first to
  circle the world (three times) wins a divine fruit (of wisdom/immortality) — in southern versions, the
  prize is the *right to marry first*. **Kārtikeya** races off on his peacock around the earth; **Gaṇeśa**
  circumambulates his parents, declaring that mother and father *are* his whole world, and wins. (Śiva
  Purāṇa / Skanda Purāṇa; widely retold)
  - *Variant (the mango of Nārada):* the prize is a single mango fruit of knowledge brought by Nārada,
    deliberately to spark sibling rivalry. [folk tradition]
  - *Consequence:* the slighted Kārtikeya leaves Kailāsa in a sulk and goes **south**, settling in the
    Tamil country (see Part II) — a myth that explains Murugan's southern home. (Skanda Purāṇa; Tamil
    tradition)

### The moon's curse & Saṃkaṣṭī

- **The moon (Candra) mocks him** — gorged on *modakas* at a feast, the pot-bellied Gaṇeśa rides home on
  his mouse, which stumbles (startled by a snake); his belly bursts and the sweets spill. He ties a snake
  round his middle as a belt; **Candra**, watching, laughs. The furious Gaṇeśa breaks off his tusk (or
  curses) and decrees that whoever looks at the moon on his festival day (Bhādrapada Caturthī / Gaṇeśa
  Caturthī) will be falsely accused — softening it so the moon waxes and wanes and is fully reviled only
  once a month. (Gaṇeśa Purāṇa; Purāṇic folk)
  - *Variant (the Syamantaka link):* Kṛṣṇa himself is falsely accused of stealing the Syamantaka jewel
    precisely because he glimpsed the moon on Gaṇeśa Caturthī; the curse is cited to explain his ordeal.
    (Bhāgavata / Purāṇic tradition) — see [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](../04-deep-dives/).

### Forms, sect & sacred sites

- **The Aṣṭavināyaka avatāras** — eight emanations of Gaṇeśa, each slaying a demon-vice, listed in the
  Gaṇeśa Purāṇa & Mudgala Purāṇa: **Vakratuṇḍa** (vs Matsarāsura, envy), **Ekadanta** (Madāsura, pride),
  **Mahodara** (Mohāsura, delusion), **Gajānana** (Lobhāsura, greed), **Lambodara** (Krodhāsura, anger),
  **Vikaṭa** (Kāmāsura, lust), **Vighnarāja** (Mamatāsura, attachment), **Dhūmravarṇa** (Abhimānāsura,
  ego). (Mudgala Purāṇa) — *not to be confused with* the Aṣṭavināyaka **temple pilgrimage** of eight
  self-manifest (*svayambhū*) Gaṇeśa shrines around Pune, Maharashtra (Moreshwar/Morgaon, Siddhivinayak/
  Siddhatek, Ballaleshwar/Pali, Varadavinayak/Mahad, Chintamani/Theur, Girijatmaj/Lenyadri, Vighnahar/
  Ozar, Mahaganapati/Ranjangaon). [regional tradition]
- **Epithets** — Vighneśvara/Vighnaharta (lord/remover of obstacles), Gaṇapati (lord of gaṇas), Lambodara
  (pot-bellied), Heramba, Vināyaka, Siddhidātā; consorts **Riddhi & Siddhi** (and **Buddhi**) in northern
  lore — *Variant:* in much of the south Gaṇeśa is a celibate bachelor (*brahmacārī*). [regional tradition]
- **Mūṣika** the mouse/rat as his vāhana — symbol of desire/ego subdued, or of a transformed gandharva
  (Krauñca) cursed to mouse-form. (Gaṇeśa Purāṇa)
- **The Gāṇapatya sect** — one of the historic *pañcāyatana* worship-streams (alongside Śaiva, Vaiṣṇava,
  Śākta, Saura), holding Gaṇeśa as the supreme Absolute (*para-brahman*); its core texts are the **Gaṇeśa
  Purāṇa**, **Mudgala Purāṇa**, and the **Gaṇapati Atharvaśīrṣa** Upaniṣad. [scholarly]

## Part II — Kārtikeya / Skanda / Murugan

The six-faced war-god, **Devasenāpati** (commander of the army of the gods), conceived by the devas for a
single purpose: to kill the otherwise-invincible **Tārakāsura**. Known as Skanda, Kumāra, Subrahmaṇya,
Guha, Mahāsena in the north; **Murugan / Cēyōṉ** — the Tamil *Tamiḻ-kaḍavuḷ* ("god of the Tamils") — in
the south. See the [Devāsura wars](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) for the cosmic-war framing.

### The need for him & the conception

- **Tārakāsura's boon** — the asura Tāraka (or, in the Tamil cycle, **Sūrapadman** with brothers
  Siṃhamukha and Tāraka) wins from Brahmā that he can be slain *only by a true son of Śiva* — counting on
  the fact that the ascetic, widowed (post-Satī) Śiva would never father one. (Skanda Purāṇa; Śiva Purāṇa)
- **Kāma's sacrifice & Pārvatī's penance** — to restart Śiva's householder life, the devas send **Kāma**
  to fire desire at the meditating Śiva; Śiva burns Kāma to ash with his third eye, but Pārvatī's long
  *tapas* eventually wins him. (Śiva Purāṇa — see [`shiva-myth-cycle.md`](shiva-myth-cycle.md))
- **The seed too potent to bear** — Śiva's seed (*tejas*) is so fiery that no womb can hold it. **Agni**
  (sometimes as a dove/bird, or receiving it from the gods) carries it, then transfers it to **Gaṅgā**,
  who, unable to bear it, deposits it in a thicket of **Śara (Saravaṇa) reeds**. (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva
  §§224–232; Śiva Purāṇa)

### The six-fold birth (*Ṣaṇmukha*)

- **Born in the reeds** — in the Saravaṇa reed-bed the seed becomes **six infants**, each in a lotus.
  The six **Kṛttikās** (the Pleiades star-maidens) find and suckle them — whence the name **Kārtikeya**
  ("son of the Kṛttikās"). When Pārvatī embraces all six at once they fuse into one child with **six
  faces and twelve arms** (**Ṣaṇmukha / Śaṇmukha**); the six faces also explain the six Kṛttikā mothers
  and the six abodes. (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva; Skanda Purāṇa; Śiva Purāṇa)
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva — the older account):* Skanda is born of **Agni and Svāhā** (Svāhā
    impersonating six of the seven Ṛṣi-wives to unite with Agni), making Agni the father; the link to
    Śiva is secondary in this archaic layer. The slandered Ṛṣi-wives become the Kṛttikā constellation. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (the Gaṅgā strand):* because Gaṅgā bore the seed he is **Gāṅgeya**; because of the reeds,
    **Sāravaṇabhava**. (Skanda Purāṇa)
  - *Variant (Tamil Kanda Purāṇam):* Murugan springs from **six sparks of fire from Śiva's third eye**,
    carried over the Saravaṇa pool, raised by the Kārttikai maidens, then merged by Pārvatī — a Śaiva
    re-reading that keeps Śiva as sole father. (Kanda Purāṇam, Kacciyappa Civācāriyar) [regional tradition]

### The slaying of the demon & generalship

- **Made general of the gods** — even as a boy Skanda is anointed **Devasenāpati**, commander of the
  celestial army, by Indra (whose own position he eclipses); he is armed by Śiva with the **Vel** (the
  divine spear, an embodiment of Pārvatī's Śakti). (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva; Skanda Purāṇa)
- **The killing of Tārakāsura** — Skanda leads the devas and slays Tāraka with the Vel, fulfilling his
  birth-purpose and ending the asura's terror of the three worlds. (Skanda Purāṇa; Matsya Purāṇa)
  - *Variant (Tamil — Sūrapadman):* the great enemy is **Sūrapadman** of Vīramaheśvaram; Murugan hurls
    the Vel and splits him in two — the halves become his **peacock (mayil)** vāhana and his **rooster
    (cēval)** banner-emblem, the demon thus serving the god he fought. (Kanda Purāṇam) [regional tradition]
- **Vāhana & weapons** — the **peacock** (Paravāṇi) as mount, the **Vel** as primary weapon, the rooster
  on his flag; a string of names: **Subrahmaṇya, Kumāra, Guha** (the hidden one), **Mahāsena**.

### Marriages

- **Devasenā (Deivānai)** — given to him by Indra as a reward / by the gods, daughter of Indra (or of
  Viṣṇu, adopted by Indra); his "army-bride," wedded at Tiruparaṅkuṉṟam after the demon-war. (Skanda
  Purāṇa; Kanda Purāṇam)
- **Vaḷḷi** — daughter of a Veḍḍa (tribal hunter) chieftain of the Tamil hills, whom Murugan woos in
  disguise (aided by Gaṇeśa, who appears as a wild elephant to frighten her into Murugan's arms); his
  "love-bride." Devasenā and Vaḷḷi are made co-wives — in the Tamil telling both are daughters of Viṣṇu
  (Amṛtavaḷḷi / Sundaravaḷḷi) reborn. (Kanda Purāṇam; Tamil tradition) [regional tradition]

### The Tamil Murugan & the six abodes

- **Arupaḍai Vīḍu (the six war-camps/abodes)** — the six canonical Murugan temples of Tamil Nadu, each
  tied to an episode, traditionally pilgrimaged in order: **Tiruparaṅkuṉṟam** (Madurai — marriage to
  Devasenā after the demon-war), **Tiruchendur** (sea-shore — the battle against the asuras), **Palani /
  Paḻani** (the renouncer-boy with the herb-paste *daṇḍāyudhapāṇi* form, after the mango-race sulk),
  **Swāmimalai** (where boy-Skanda teaches the meaning of **Praṇava / Oṃ** to his father Śiva, becoming
  *Swāminātha*, "teacher of the lord"), **Tiruttaṇi** (anger calmed, after wedding Vaḷḷi), and
  **Paḻamuṭircōlai** (the forest hill, abundance / Vaḷḷi associations). (Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭai, Nakkīrar;
  Kanda Purāṇam) [regional tradition]
- **Praṇava-teaching (Swāmimalai)** — the child detains even Brahmā for failing to explain Oṃ, imprisons
  him, briefly takes over creation, and then expounds the syllable to Śiva himself — the *guru* even of
  his father. (Kanda Purāṇam; Skanda Purāṇa) [regional tradition]

### The estrangement & northern decline

- **The sulk that sent him south** — losing the mango/fruit race to Gaṇeśa (Part I), the proud Kārtikeya
  abandons Kailāsa and settles in the Tamil south, a charter-myth for why Skanda-worship faded in the
  north (where he remains chiefly **Subrahmaṇya**) but flourishes as **Murugan** in the south and among
  the Tamil diaspora. (Skanda Purāṇa; Tamil tradition) [regional tradition]
- *Variant:* in some northern lore Kārtikeya remains an austere celibate (which is why women traditionally
  avoid certain of his shrines), whereas the Tamil Murugan is the amorous husband of Vaḷḷi — a north/south
  divergence in his very character. [scholarly]
