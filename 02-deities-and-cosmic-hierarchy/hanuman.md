# Hanuman — Vanara Devotee, Chiranjivi, Rudravatara

> Cosmic address: [Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md) → individual deity. Hanuman acts in the [Treta Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) (Ramayana) and survives into the [Dvapara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and [Kali Yugas](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) as one of the immortal [Chiranjivi](#chiranjivi--the-deathless-one).

Hanuman (also Anjaneya, Maruti, Bajrangbali, Pavanaputra) is the foremost vanara (forest-dweller) ally of [Rama](dashavatara.md), the paradigm of bhakti (devotion), and among the most widely worshipped deities of living Hinduism. He sits at the meeting point of two great traditions: in the Vaishnava framing he is the perfect *devotee* of Vishnu-as-Rama; in the Shaiva framing he is an *avatara of Shiva himself* (the 11th Rudra). This file catalogs both, plus his birth cycle, his Sundara Kanda feats, his Mahabharata role, his iconographic forms, and his bhakti cult.

## Birth & Childhood (Anjaneya Cycle)

- **Parentage — Anjana, Kesari, and Vayu** - Born to the apsara-turned-vanari Anjana (cursed to monkey form) and the vanara chief Kesari; conceived through the agency of Vayu, the wind god, making Hanuman *Vayuputra/Pavanaputra* (son of the wind). (Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda 66; Uttara Kanda 35–36)
  - *Variant (Valmiki, Kishkindha 66):* Vayu approaches Anjana non-physically (*manasa*) and grants the son; Hanuman is Kesari's legal son but Vayu's direct son.
  - *Variant (Shiva Purana, Rudravatara framing):* Anjana performs penance to Shiva; Shiva vows his 11th Rudra form will be born as her son, and Vayu carries that divine power (or Shiva's seed/essence) into her womb. See [Rudravatara](#the-rudravatara-claim-shaiva).
  - *Variant (Eknath / Maharashtrian bhakti):* the *Maruti* lineage emphasizes Vayu's wind-essence; Hanuman as Vayu's son links him to *prana* (breath/life-force).
- **The name "Hanuman" — the struck jaw** - As an infant, mistaking the rising sun for a ripe fruit, he leapt skyward to swallow it. Indra struck him with the *vajra* (thunderbolt) on the jaw, casting him dead to earth with a broken jaw; "Hanu" = jaw, hence *Hanuman*, "one of the [prominent/struck] jaw." (Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda; Ramcharitmanas)
  - *Variant:* in some tellings Rahu (or the swallowing of the sun coinciding with an eclipse) provokes Indra's strike.
- **Vayu's wrath & the gods' boons** - Enraged at his son's death, Vayu withdrew all air from the worlds, suffocating creation. To placate him the devas revived Hanuman and each granted a boon: Indra — invulnerability to the vajra; Brahma — invincibility / freedom from Brahma's weapon; Vishnu, Surya, Varuna, Yama, Kubera, Vishvakarma, Shiva — strength, longevity, and powers, making him effectively immortal and supremely strong. (Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda 36; Shiva Purana)
- **The boon-amnesia (curse of forgetting his powers)** - As a mischievous child Hanuman tormented sages; they cursed him to *forget his own immense powers* until reminded of them at the destined moment. This is why Jambavan must remind him of his strength before the leap to Lanka. (Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha/Sundara Kanda; widely in Ramcharitmanas)
- **Education under Surya** - Hanuman studies the scriptures, grammar, and arts directly from Surya (the sun-god) as guru, running backward across the sky to face him; becomes a master of Sanskrit grammar (*navavyakarana*) and the shastras. (Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda; later tradition)

## The Ramayana Role (Treta Yuga)

For the full narrative arc see the [Treta Yuga file](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md). Hanuman's key acts:

- **Minister of Sugriva** - Serving the exiled vanara king Sugriva at Rishyamukha; it is Hanuman who first meets Rama and Lakshmana and brokers the Rama–Sugriva alliance, impressing Rama with his flawless speech. (Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda 3–4)
- **The search for Sita** - Dispatched south with Angada's party; reaching the ocean's edge, the despairing vanaras learn from Sampati that Sita is in Lanka. Jambavan reminds Hanuman of his forgotten powers, and he resolves to leap the sea. (Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda 63–67)

### Sundara Kanda — the "Beautiful Book," Hanuman's own canto

The fifth book of the Valmiki Ramayana centers almost entirely on Hanuman; it is recited as a self-contained devotional text.

- **The leap across the ocean** - Expanding to colossal size, Hanuman springs from Mount Mahendra and bounds ~100 yojanas to Lanka. En route he is tested: Mainaka (golden mountain) offers rest; the naga-mother Surasa swallows him (he shrinks and escapes); the shadow-catching demoness Simhika seizes his shadow and he kills her. (Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara Kanda 1)
- **Scaling Lanka & finding Sita** - Shrinking to cat-size, he infiltrates Ravana's city by night, searches the palace and Ashoka Vatika, and finally finds Sita captive beneath a shimshapa tree, refusing Ravana's advances. (Sundara Kanda 2–15)
- **Revealing himself to Sita** - He recites Rama's story and presents Rama's signet ring as a token; offers to carry Sita back, which she refuses (only Rama must rescue her, to uphold his honor). She gives him her *chudamani* (crest-jewel) for Rama. (Sundara Kanda 31–40)
- **Destroying the Ashoka grove & confronting Ravana** - To provoke a reckoning, Hanuman uproots the grove, slays Aksha (Ravana's son) and many demons, and lets himself be captured by Indrajit's Brahmastra (honoring Brahma's boon). Brought before Ravana, he delivers a warning. (Sundara Kanda 41–51)
- **The burning of Lanka** - Ravana sets Hanuman's tail ablaze; Hanuman breaks free and, leaping roof to roof, sets the golden city aflame, then dips his burning tail in the sea. (Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara Kanda 52–54)
  - *Variant (worry for Sita):* Hanuman momentarily fears the fire reached Sita, but learns she is unharmed — by her chastity / Agni's protection the fire did not burn her quarter.
- **Return with news** - He recrosses to the mainland, roars in triumph, and reports Sita's location and message to Rama, restoring the prince's hope. (Sundara Kanda 57–66)

### Yuddha Kanda — war feats

- **Fetching the Sanjivani / Dronagiri mountain** - When Lakshmana (and in some passages much of the army) is felled by Indrajit's weapons, Hanuman flies to the Himalayas for the *sanjivani* (life-restoring herb) on Mount Dronagiri; unable to identify the single herb, he uproots and carries the *entire mountain* back to Lanka. (Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 74)
  - *Variant:* he makes this trip twice; on the return he is briefly struck down over Ayodhya by Bharata, who mistakes him for a demon, then is honored on recognizing him. (Ramcharitmanas / later tradition)
- **Carrying Rama and Lakshmana on his shoulders** - In battle Hanuman bears the brothers aloft against Ravana and Indrajit. (Yuddha Kanda)
- **Rescue from Patala — the Ahiravana episode** - Ravana's sorcerer-brother Ahiravana abducts the sleeping Rama and Lakshmana to the netherworld (Patala) to sacrifice them to Mahamaya/Bhadrakali; Hanuman descends, discovers Ahiravana's life is split among five lamps in five directions, assumes his **five-faced (Panchamukha)** form to extinguish all five at once, and slays him. (Not in Valmiki's core text; from later/regional Ramayanas — *Adbhuta Ramayana*, Krittivasi & Bengali traditions, *Mairavana-charita*) [late text]

## Iconographic Forms

- **Panchamukha Hanuman (five-faced)** - Five faces — Hanuman (east), Narasimha (south), Garuda (west), Varaha (north), Hayagriva (sky/zenith) — with fifteen eyes and ten arms bearing weapons; originates in the Ahiravana rescue. Largely a Tantra-tradition form, attested in iconography from roughly the 15th century CE. (Tantric texts; *Sritattvanidhi*) [late text]
  - *Variant (face attributions):* lists of which face points which direction vary by text and temple.
- **The heart-bearing image (Rama in the chest)** - The beloved image of Hanuman tearing open his chest to reveal Rama and Sita enthroned in his heart — proof that Rama dwells within him — dramatizes total bhakti. (Ramcharitmanas devotional tradition; iconography; [folk tradition] in origin)
- **Other forms** - *Veera/Vira Hanuman* (warrior, mace raised); *Dasa Hanuman* (humble servant, hands folded, before Rama); *Suryamukhi / flying Hanuman bearing the mountain*; *Bala Hanuman* (child reaching for the sun). (temple iconography)

## The Mahabharata Role (Dvapara Yuga)

As a Chiranjivi, Hanuman lives on into the next age and intersects the [Mahabharata](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md).

- **Brother of Bhima** - Both Hanuman and Bhima are sons of Vayu (Hanuman in Treta via Anjana, Bhima in Dvapara via Kunti), hence half-brothers. (Mahabharata, Vana Parva 146–150)
- **The tail-lifting humility lesson** - In the Gandhamadana forest, Bhima — proud of his strength — demands an aged "monkey" lying across his path move its tail aside. Bhima cannot budge the tail with either hand or both arms; humbled, he recognizes Hanuman, who teaches him humility and the dharma of the ages. (Mahabharata, Vana Parva 146–150)
- **Boon to roar in battle** - Hanuman promises to aid the Pandavas; he will sit on Arjuna's banner and amplify the Pandava war-cries to terrify the enemy. (Mahabharata, Vana Parva)
- **Kapidhvaja — the monkey-bannered chariot** - Hanuman resides on the flag of Arjuna's chariot throughout the Kurukshetra war, protecting it (in tradition, absorbing the recoil of celestial weapons so the chariot is spared); hence Arjuna's epithet *Kapidhvaja* ("monkey-bannered"). (Mahabharata, Bhishma Parva; Bhagavad Gita context)
  - *Note:* the [Bhagavad Gita](../04-deep-dives/) is spoken on this very chariot; Hanuman is the unseen presence on its standard.

## The Rudravatara Claim (Shaiva)

- **11th Rudra / avatara of Shiva** - In Shaiva tradition (notably the Shiva Purana), when Vishnu was to descend as Rama, Shiva resolved to serve him on earth and took birth as Hanuman — counted as the 11th of the eleven Rudras. Thus Hanuman *is* Shiva incarnate. (Shiva Purana, Shatarudra Samhita; Skanda Purana tradition)
  - *Variant (transmission of the seed):* Shiva's tejas/seed is carried by Vayu (and in some versions via the *amrita* / divine pudding shared at Dasharatha's putrakameshti yajna) into Anjana's womb, linking Hanuman simultaneously to Shiva and Vayu.
- **Vaishnava counter-framing (devotee, not deity-incarnate)** - The dominant Ramananda/Tulsidas Vaishnava bhakti stream frames Hanuman not as a god incarnating but as the supreme *dasa* (servant-devotee) of Rama, whose greatness lies entirely in surrender to Rama. Both framings coexist in popular worship.
  - *Reconciliation (common popular view):* "Shiva became the devotee of Vishnu" — Hanuman embodies the unity of Shaiva and Vaishnava streams (Hari-Hara harmony).

## Chiranjivi — the Deathless One

- **Immortality boon from Sita / Rama** - Sita (and in tellings Rama) blesses Hanuman to live as long as Rama's story is told on earth; he is to remain through the Kali Yuga. (Ramayana tradition; Ramcharitmanas)
- **Among the seven (or eight) Chiranjivi** - Hanuman is listed among the immortals — commonly Ashwatthama, Mahabali, Vyasa, Hanuman, Vibhishana, Kripacharya, Parashurama (and sometimes Markandeya/Jambavan). See the broader cosmology in [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md). (Chiranjivi shloka tradition; Puranic)
- **Present wherever Rama is praised** - Folk belief holds Hanuman silently attends every recitation of the Ramayana / Rama-katha. [folk tradition]

## Bhakti Cult & Living Worship

- **Hanuman Chalisa (Tulsidas)** - A 40-verse (chalisa) Awadhi hymn by Goswami Tulsidas (16th c. CE), the single most-recited Hanuman text; praises his strength, learning, devotion, and protective power (e.g., the *vajra-deha*, leaping the sun, crossing the ocean). (Tulsidas, *Hanuman Chalisa*; companion to the *Ramcharitmanas*) [late text — 16th c.]
- **Ramcharitmanas (Tulsidas)** - Tulsidas's Awadhi Ramayana (1574 CE) crystallized North Indian Hanuman bhakti, foregrounding Hanuman's role; the *Sundarkand* of the Manas is recited for protection. [late text]
- **Maruti / Anjaneya regional traditions** - In Maharashtra, Samarth Ramdas (17th c.) established Maruti temples linking Hanuman to strength, celibacy (brahmacharya), and civic vigor; in South India *Anjaneya* worship is widespread, often Panchamukha. (regional bhakti history) [scholarly]
- **Vows & observance** - Tuesday and Saturday are Hanuman's days; offerings of sindoor (vermilion, recalling his smearing himself red out of love for Sita), oil, and *boondi/laddoo*; **Hanuman Jayanti** marks his birth (date varies by region — Chaitra Purnima in the north; other dates in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, etc.). [folk tradition / regional]
- **Functions in popular devotion** - Remover of fear and obstacles, guardian against evil spirits and Saturn's (Shani's) afflictions, patron of wrestlers and strength, and exemplar of selfless service.
