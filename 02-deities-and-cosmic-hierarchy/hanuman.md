# Hanuman — Vanara Devotee, Chiranjivi, Rudravatara

> Cosmic address: [Deities & Cosmic Hierarchy](README.md) → individual deity. Hanuman acts in the [Treta Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) (Ramayana) and survives into the [Dvapara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and [Kali Yugas](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) as one of the immortal [Chiranjivi](#chiranjivi--the-deathless-one).

Hanuman (also Anjaneya, Maruti, Bajrangbali, Pavanaputra) is the foremost vanara (forest-dweller) ally of [Rama](dashavatara.md), the paradigm of bhakti (devotion), and among the most widely worshipped deities of living Hinduism. He sits at the meeting point of two great traditions: in the Vaishnava framing he is the perfect *devotee* of Vishnu-as-Rama; in the Shaiva framing he is an *avatara of Shiva himself* (the 11th Rudra). This file catalogs both, plus his birth cycle, his Sundara Kanda feats, his Mahabharata role, his iconographic forms, and his bhakti cult.

---

## Birth & Childhood (Anjaneya Cycle)

- **Parentage — Anjana, Kesari, and Vayu** - Born to the apsara-turned-vanari Anjana (cursed to monkey form) and the vanara chief Kesari; conceived through the agency of Vayu, the wind god, making Hanuman *Vayuputra/Pavanaputra* (son of the wind). (Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda 66; Uttara Kanda 35–36)
  - *Variant (Valmiki, Kishkindha 66):* Vayu approaches Anjana non-physically (*manasa*) and grants the son; Hanuman is Kesari's legal son but Vayu's direct son.
  - *Variant (Shiva Purana, Rudravatara framing):* Anjana performs penance to Shiva; Shiva vows his 11th Rudra form will be born as her son, and Vayu carries that divine power (or Shiva's seed/essence) into her womb. See [Rudravatara](#the-rudravatara-claim-shaiva).
  - *Variant (Eknath / Maharashtrian bhakti):* the *Maruti* lineage emphasizes Vayu's wind-essence; Hanuman as Vayu's son links him to *prana* (breath/life-force).
- **Rahu's grievance and the struck jaw** - The infant, mistaking the rising sun for a ripe fruit, leapt skyward to swallow it — on the very day of the conjunction, when **Rahu** had come for the same orb. Finding "a second Rahu" at his prey, Rahu carried the complaint to **Indra**, who rode out on **Airavata**; the child, seeing the white elephant, sprang for that too, and Indra loosed the *vajra*. It broke the boy's left jaw and cast him lifeless on a mountain. This is **core Valmiki, not a variant reading**: Rahu, the eclipse-day setting, the Airavata lunge and the shattered jaw are all narrated in Uttara Kanda 35 as Agastya tells Rama the vanara's origin. (Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda 35)
- **The naming** - At the boon-ceremony that follows, Indra himself gives the name: because his own thunderbolt shattered the child's *hanu* (jaw), the boy shall be called **Hanuman**, "one of the [struck] jaw." (Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda 36)
- **Vayu's wrath & the boon-ceremony — the itemized roster** - Enraged, Vayu withdrew into a cave with his son and held the winds out of the worlds, so that no creature could breathe or void; the gods came to the wind-god and Brahma restored the child's life. Brahma then called on the assembled powers to endow him, and the grants are **given one by one in the text**: **Indra** — invulnerable to the vajra; **Surya (Martanda)** — a hundredth part of his own effulgence, and mastery of the shastras and of speech; **Varuna** — proof against his noose and his waters; **Yama** — freedom from disease and from death in battle; **Kubera** — his mace shall never wound him; **Shiva** — his shafts withheld; **Vishvakarma** — proof against all celestial weapons, and long life; **Brahma** — immunity from his own weapons. (Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda 36)
  - *Note (who is NOT in the roster):* **Vishnu grants nothing** in Valmiki's ceremony, and neither does **Agni** — the two names most often added to the list in retellings. Hanuman's tail not burning is **not** a birth-boon: it is Sita's prayer to Agni answered in the moment, at the burning of Lanka, with Vayu blowing cool alongside the flame — Hanuman himself attributes it to "the kindness of Sita, the power of Rama, and the friendship of my father." (Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara Kanda 53)
- **The curse of forgetting — core text, with a release clause** - Drunk on the boons, the boy harried the forest hermitages, and the **rishis born in the lines of Bhrigu and Angiras** cursed him: by their curse he would **remain unaware of his own strength for a long time — but would wield it in full the moment he was reminded of it**. This is **core Valmiki Uttara Kanda 36**, not folk devotional embroidery; the release-on-reminder clause is stated in the curse itself, which is why the reminding works. Jambavan's rousing of him at the southern shore is equally core text. (Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda 36; Kishkindha Kanda 65–66)
  - *Variant (Ramcharitmanas):* Tulsidas keeps the curse and the forgetting but places the reminding differently within his narrative; only the *placement*, not the mechanic, is his own.
- **Education under Surya** - Hanuman studies the scriptures, grammar, and arts directly from Surya (the sun-god) as guru, running backward across the sky to face him; becomes a master of Sanskrit grammar (*navavyakarana*) and the shastras. (Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda 36; later tradition)
- **Two Valmiki birth-narrations, not one** - The epic tells the birth **twice**, and the two accounts do not agree. **Kishkindha Kanda 66** (Jambavan's rousing speech) gives the compact version: Anjana, Kesari, Vayu's *manasa* begetting, the leap at the sun, Indra's bolt, Vayu's strike against the worlds, and a short set of boons. **Uttara Kanda 35–36** (Agastya to Rama) gives the long version, and adds Rahu, Airavata, the itemized eight-god roster, Indra's naming, the Surya tutelage and the Bhrigu–Angiras curse. The leap-distance, the birthplace (Uttara puts his parents on **Sumeru**), and the boon-count all differ between them. The corpus records both; they are not to be merged. (Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda 66; Uttara Kanda 35–36)

## The Ramayana Role (Treta Yuga)

For the full narrative arc see the [Treta Yuga file](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md). Hanuman's key acts:

- **Minister of Sugriva** - Serving the exiled vanara king Sugriva at Rishyamukha; it is Hanuman who first meets Rama and Lakshmana and brokers the Rama–Sugriva alliance. (Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda 3–4)
- **The speech-certificate** - Rama's aside to Lakshmana after that first meeting is the epic's formal warrant for Hanuman's learning: only one who has mastered the **Rig, Yajur and Sama** and studied grammar entire could speak so; the delivery is unhurried, unhesitating, undissonant, pitched in the middle register — and *"though much is said by him, not a single word has gone amiss."* A king with such an envoy, Rama says, succeeds in every undertaking. (Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda 3)
- **The search for Sita** - Dispatched south with Angada's party; reaching the ocean's edge, the despairing vanaras learn from Sampati that Sita is in Lanka. Jambavan reminds Hanuman of his forgotten powers, and he resolves to leap the sea. (Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda 63–67)

### Sundara Kanda — the "Beautiful Book," Hanuman's own canto

The fifth book of the Valmiki Ramayana centers almost entirely on Hanuman; it is recited as a self-contained devotional text.

- **The leap across the ocean** - Expanding to colossal size, Hanuman springs from Mount Mahendra and bounds ~100 yojanas to Lanka. En route he is tested: Mainaka (golden mountain) offers rest; the naga-mother Surasa swallows him (he shrinks and escapes); the shadow-catching demoness Simhika seizes his shadow and he kills her. (Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara Kanda 1)
- **Scaling Lanka & finding Sita** - Shrinking to cat-size, he infiltrates Ravana's city by night, searches the palace and Ashoka Vatika, and finally finds Sita captive beneath a shimshapa tree, refusing Ravana's advances. (Sundara Kanda 2–15)
- **Revealing himself to Sita** - He recites Rama's story and presents Rama's signet ring as a token; offers to carry Sita back, which she refuses (only Rama must rescue her, to uphold his honor). She gives him her *chudamani* (crest-jewel) for Rama. (Sundara Kanda 31–40)
- **His own account of himself** - Pressed by Sita to say who he is, Hanuman gives the epic's only first-person statement of his parentage: he is the son of **Kesari**, the vanara who went from Mount Malyavan to Gokarna and, at the prayer of gods and seers, slew the demon **Shambasadana** at a shrine by the sea; and he is the son of **Vayu** by begetting. The passage is genuine Valmiki, and is the sole textual home of the Kesari–Shambasadana deed — it sits **outside** the two birth-accounts (Kishkindha 66, Uttara 35–36), which is why it is easily mistaken for later tradition. (Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara Kanda 35) — cf. [Vanaras & Rikshas](../08-beings-and-bestiary/vanaras-and-rikshas.md).
- **Destroying the Ashoka grove & confronting Ravana** - To provoke a reckoning, Hanuman uproots the grove, slays Aksha (Ravana's son) and many demons, and lets himself be captured by Indrajit's Brahmastra (honoring Brahma's boon). Brought before Ravana, he delivers a warning. (Sundara Kanda 41–51)
- **The burning of Lanka** - Ravana sets Hanuman's tail ablaze; the fire does not burn him — Sita prays to Agni for his welfare, and Vayu blows cool along the flame. Hanuman breaks free and, leaping roof to roof, sets the golden city aflame, sparing **Vibhishana's** house alone, and comes to rest on Trikuta. (Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara Kanda 52–54)
  - *Note (quenching the tail in the sea):* the famous image of Hanuman dousing his burning tail in the ocean is **popular and iconographic tradition**, not the Southern text — sarga 54 closes on Trikuta, and sarga 55 opens with his dread that the fire may have reached Sita. [folk / iconographic]
  - *Variant (worry for Sita):* Hanuman momentarily fears the fire reached Sita, but learns she is unharmed — by her chastity / Agni's protection the fire did not burn her quarter.
- **Return with news** - He recrosses to the mainland, roars in triumph, and reports Sita's location and message to Rama, restoring the prince's hope. (Sundara Kanda 57–66)

### Yuddha Kanda — war feats

- **The first herb-flight — the unnamed mountain** - When Indrajit's **brahmastra**, loosed unseen from the sky after his oblation (Yuddha 73), lays Rama, Lakshmana and the host senseless, **Jambavan** sends Hanuman to the Himalaya for four herbs — *mrita-sanjivani*, *vishalyakarani*, *savarnyakarani* and *sandhani*. The herb-mountain lies among the northern peaks, **between Rishabha and golden Kailasa**; Valmiki gives it **no proper name**. Unable to pick out the herbs as they hide themselves from him, Hanuman uproots and carries back the **entire summit**. (Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 74)
- **The second herb-flight — Oshadhi / Mahodaya** - A distinct, later errand, and the one the epic *does* name: after **Ravana's** Maya-forged *shakti* fells Lakshmana, the physician **Sushena** sends Hanuman to the mountain **Oshadhi**, also called **Mahodaya**, for the same four herbs; again he brings the peak entire, and Sushena draws the herbs himself. (Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 101) — the two flights are two events, not two tellings of one.
  - *Note (the name "Dronagiri"):* **Dronagiri is not a Valmiki place-name anywhere in the epic.** It belongs to later and regional tradition, which attaches it to the first, unnamed mountain; the corpus keeps it as tradition's name, not the text's. The herb-roster, the two mountains and the "Dronagiri" question in full: [Ramayana Objects & Tokens §V](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/ramayana-objects-and-tokens.md); the mountains from the geography side: [Meru, Kailasa & the Cosmic Mountains](../06-sacred-geography/meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md).
  - *Variant (Ramcharitmanas / later tradition):* on the return flight he is struck down over Ayodhya by Bharata, who mistakes him for a demon, then is honored on recognizing him.
- **Carrying Rama and Lakshmana on his shoulders** - When Ravana enters the field in his chariot and the brothers fight on foot, Hanuman bears **Rama** aloft on his shoulders so that the fight is met at the chariot's height — the origin of the *Hanuman as Rama's vahana* iconography. In the same sarga he lifts the wounded **Lakshmana** in his arms and carries him back to Rama, a weight no other could raise. (Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 59)
- **Bharata's reward at Nandigrama** - Sent ahead from the Pushpaka to break the news of Rama's return, Hanuman is rewarded by **Bharata** with a hundred thousand cows, a hundred prime villages, and **sixteen maidens of noble family** for wives — the epic's largest single grant to him, and a detail retellings almost always drop. (Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 125.43–45)
- **Sita's necklace and the ten virtues** - At the coronation Rama tells Sita to bestow her pearl necklace on whomever she is pleased with and in whom ten named qualities are ever present — strength, valour, fame, skill, capability, conduct, prudence, courtesy, resolution and prowess; she looks over the assembly and puts it on Hanuman. The ten virtues are Rama's wording, the choice hers. (Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 128.82) — **not** the occasion of the deathlessness grant; see [Chiranjivi](#chiranjivi--the-deathless-one).
- **Rescue from Patala — the Ahiravana episode** - Ravana's sorcerer-brother Ahiravana abducts the sleeping Rama and Lakshmana to the netherworld (Patala) to sacrifice them to Mahamaya/Bhadrakali; Hanuman descends, discovers Ahiravana's life is split among five lamps in five directions, assumes his **five-faced (Panchamukha)** form to extinguish all five at once, and slays him. Absent from Valmiki entirely; its **earliest documented home is the Krittivasi Ramayana** (15th c. Bengali), from which it spreads into the *Mairavana-charita* and the wider regional Rama-katha. (Krittivasi Ramayana and Bengali tradition; later *Mairavana-charita* and *Adbhuta Ramayana* strands) [late text]

## Iconographic Forms

- **Panchamukha Hanuman (five-faced)** - Five faces — Hanuman (east), Narasimha (south), Garuda (west), Varaha (north), Hayagriva (sky/zenith) — with fifteen eyes and ten arms bearing weapons; originates in the Ahiravana rescue. Largely a Tantra-tradition form, attested in iconography from roughly the 15th century CE. (Tantric texts; *Sritattvanidhi*) [late text]
  - *Variant (face attributions):* lists of which face points which direction vary by text and temple.
- **The heart-bearing image (Rama in the chest)** - The beloved image of Hanuman tearing open his chest to reveal Rama and Sita enthroned in his heart — proof that Rama dwells within him — dramatizes total bhakti. (Ramcharitmanas devotional tradition; iconography; [folk tradition] in origin)
- **Other forms** - *Veera/Vira Hanuman* (warrior, mace raised); *Dasa Hanuman* (humble servant, hands folded, before Rama); *Suryamukhi / flying Hanuman bearing the mountain*; *Bala Hanuman* (child reaching for the sun). (temple iconography)

## The Mahabharata Role (Dvapara Yuga)

As a Chiranjivi, Hanuman lives on into the next age and intersects the [Mahabharata](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md).

- **Brother of Bhima** - Both Hanuman and Bhima are sons of Vayu (Hanuman in Treta via Anjana, Bhima in Dvapara via Kunti), hence half-brothers. (Mahabharata, Vana Parva 146–150)
- **The tail-lifting humility lesson** - In the Gandhamadana forest, Bhima — proud of his strength — demands an aged "monkey" lying across his path move its tail aside. Bhima cannot budge the tail with either hand or both arms; humbled, he recognizes Hanuman, who teaches him humility and the dharma of the ages. (Mahabharata, Vana Parva 146–150)
- **Boon to roar in battle** - Hanuman promises to aid the Pandavas; he will sit on Arjuna's banner and amplify the Pandava war-cries to terrify the enemy. (Mahabharata, Vana Parva)
- **Kapidhvaja — the monkey-bannered chariot** - Hanuman resides on the flag of Arjuna's chariot throughout the Kurukshetra war, protecting it (in tradition, absorbing the recoil of celestial weapons so the chariot is spared); hence Arjuna's epithet *Kapidhvaja* ("monkey-bannered"). (Mahabharata, Bhishma Parva; Bhagavad Gita context)
  - *Note:* the [Bhagavad Gita](../04-deep-dives/) is spoken on this very chariot; Hanuman is the unseen presence on its standard.

## The Rudravatara Claim (Shaiva)

- **11th Rudra / avatara of Shiva** - In Shaiva tradition (notably the Shiva Purana), when Vishnu was to descend as Rama, Shiva resolved to serve him on earth and took birth as Hanuman — counted as the 11th of the eleven Rudras. Thus Hanuman *is* Shiva incarnate. (Shiva Purana, Shatarudra Samhita; Skanda Purana tradition)
  - *Variant (transmission of the seed):* Shiva's tejas/seed is carried by Vayu (and in some versions via the *amrita* / divine pudding shared at Dasharatha's putrakameshti yajna) into Anjana's womb, linking Hanuman simultaneously to Shiva and Vayu.
- **Vaishnava counter-framing (devotee, not deity-incarnate)** - The dominant Ramananda/Tulsidas Vaishnava bhakti stream frames Hanuman not as a god incarnating but as the supreme *dasa* (servant-devotee) of Rama, whose greatness lies entirely in surrender to Rama. Both framings coexist in popular worship.
  - *Reconciliation (common popular view):* "Shiva became the devotee of Vishnu" — Hanuman embodies the unity of Shaiva and Vaishnava streams (Hari-Hara harmony).

## Chiranjivi — the Deathless One

- **Rama's grant at the Mahaprasthana** - The firm primary locus is **Uttara Kanda 108**, where Rama, about to leave the world, issues his last commands. To Hanuman: *"As long as my story is told in the world, so long mayest thou be happy"* — and Hanuman answers that so long as that history circulates, life will remain in his body. It is **Rama's** grant, not Sita's, and it is made at the departure, not at the Lanka victory: **Yuddha Kanda 128** — where later tradition often places it — contains only Sita's gift of her pearl necklace. (Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda 108)
- **The other two terms granted in the same scene** - Rama fixes the spans of his other long-lived companions at the same audience: **Vibhishana** is to hold Lanka *as long as the moon and the sun shine on the earth*, and **Jambavan** (with **Mainda** and **Dvivida**) is to dwell on earth *until the Kali Yuga begins* — three different terms, three different companions, all in one sarga. Sugriva, having already installed **Angada**, follows Rama instead of remaining. (Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda 108)
- **The Sita-blessing version** - The widely-told form in which **Sita** confers the deathlessness is later devotional tradition rather than Valmiki's text; the corpus keeps it as such. (Ramcharitmanas and later Rama-katha tradition)
- **Among the seven (or eight) Chiranjivi** - Hanuman is listed among the immortals — commonly Ashwatthama, Mahabali, Vyasa, Hanuman, Vibhishana, Kripacharya, Parashurama (and sometimes Markandeya/Jambavan). See the broader cosmology in [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md). (Chiranjivi shloka tradition; Puranic)
- **Present wherever Rama is praised** - Folk belief holds Hanuman silently attends every recitation of the Ramayana / Rama-katha. [folk tradition]

## Bhakti Cult & Living Worship

- **Hanuman Chalisa (Tulsidas)** - A 40-verse (chalisa) Awadhi hymn by Goswami Tulsidas (16th c. CE), the single most-recited Hanuman text; praises his strength, learning, devotion, and protective power (e.g., the *vajra-deha*, leaping the sun, crossing the ocean). (Tulsidas, *Hanuman Chalisa*; companion to the *Ramcharitmanas*) [late text — 16th c.]
- **Ramcharitmanas (Tulsidas)** - Tulsidas's Awadhi Ramayana (1574 CE) crystallized North Indian Hanuman bhakti, foregrounding Hanuman's role; the *Sundarkand* of the Manas is recited for protection. [late text]
- **Maruti / Anjaneya regional traditions** - In Maharashtra, Samarth Ramdas (17th c.) established Maruti temples linking Hanuman to strength, celibacy (brahmacharya), and civic vigor; in South India *Anjaneya* worship is widespread, often Panchamukha. (regional bhakti history) [scholarly]
- **Vows & observance** - Tuesday and Saturday are Hanuman's days; offerings of sindoor (vermilion, recalling his smearing himself red out of love for Sita), oil, and *boondi/laddoo*; **Hanuman Jayanti** marks his birth (date varies by region — Chaitra Purnima in the north; other dates in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, etc.). [folk tradition / regional]
- **Functions in popular devotion** - Remover of fear and obstacles, guardian against evil spirits and Saturn's (Shani's) afflictions, patron of wrestlers and strength, and exemplar of selfless service.
