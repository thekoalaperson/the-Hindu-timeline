# Bahuchara Mata — Gujarati Shakta Goddess and Patron of the Hijra Community

> Deities-and-hierarchy node. **Bahucharā Mātā** (also **Bahuchar**, **Becharā Mā**, **Bālā Triśūla-dhāriṇī**) is a major **Gujarati Śākta folk goddess (lok-devī)** and **kuladevī (clan-mother)** of western India, enshrined at **Becharājī** in Mehsana district, and famed above all as the **patron deity of the hijra community**. Like **[Khodiyār](khodiyar-mata.md)**, she is a **Cāraṇ-born śakti** read as a manifestation of **[Ādi-Śakti / the great Devī](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)**, but she is a distinct goddess with her own seat, mount and cult. ## 1. Two Clocks — Who Bahuchara Mata Is

Bahucharā is a **Gujarati lok-devī / kuladevī** with a thin legendary-historical kernel and a thick, living devotional cult; her "scriptural clock" runs only through her equation with the **[great Devī / Ādi-Śakti](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)**, not through any Sanskrit text that names "Bahucharā."

- **Scriptural clock** — Bahucharā has **no Vedic or Purāṇic footprint under that name**; her theological legitimacy rests on the Cāraṇ doctrine that the great **[Devī](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)** takes birth (*āvataraṇa*) in Cāraṇ households as the *āī* ("mother"), so she is read as a localized form of **Ādi-Śakti / Durgā / Bālā**. [folk] [late text]
- **Historical / legendary clock** — her origin-legend is set in **Cāraṇ Gujarāt** and turns on the *tragu* (ritual self-mutilation) of a Cāraṇ woman; her temple-cult at **Becharājī** is attested in recent centuries, the present shrine traditionally rebuilt in the **18th century** under Gaekwad / Maratha patronage. The dating is devotional-traditional, not documentary. [folk] [disputed]
- **What she governs** — protection of **clan and lineage** (as kuladevī), **fertility, childbirth and the welfare of children**, and — most distinctively — the **sanction and protection of the hijra / transgender community** and of those who change or transcend gender. [regional]
- **The theological engine** — a **Cāraṇ-born śakti** whose central legend is an act of *tragu* and a **curse of impotence liftable only by worship in female guise**, fusing the lok-devī incarnation pattern with a goddess who governs **manhood, its loss, and its transcendence**. [folk] [scholarly]

## 2. Names and Epithets (catalog)

- **Bahucharā / Bahucharā Mātā** — her principal cult-name; popularly linked to the Cāraṇ woman **Bahucharā** (with sisters Bahot and others in some tellings) whose *tragu* founds the cult. [folk]
- **Becharā Mā / Bechrājī Mā** — the everyday Gujarati form; the temple-town **Becharājī (Bahucharājī)** in **Mehsāṇā district** takes its name from her. [regional]
- **Bālā / Bālā Triśūla-dhāriṇī** — "the Maiden bearing the trident," her form as a youthful warrior-goddess. [regional]
- **Murga-vāhinī / the rooster-rider** — descriptive epithet from her **rooster (kukkuṭa) mount** (§4). [regional]
- **Āī / Āī Śrī** — "the Mother," the Cāraṇ honorific applied to the lineage of god-bearing Cāraṇ women (cf. **Āvaḍ**, **Mōgal**, **[Khodiyār](khodiyar-mata.md)**) to which she is reckoned. [folk]

## 3. The Origin-Legend (the *tragu* and the curse, in order)

The defining Bahucharā narrative is a Cāraṇ tale of a caravan attacked by a bandit, a woman's *tragu*, and a curse of impotence that becomes the charter of her whole gender-transgressive cult; recensions differ, the through-line is constant.

- **The Cāraṇ caravan** — the Cāraṇ woman **Bahucharā** (with sisters / kinswomen, in a travelling Cāraṇ party) is journeying when the band is **set upon by the marauder Bāpiyā (Bāpiyo)**, a local bandit / outlaw chief. (Cāraṇ folk tradition) [folk]
- **The threat of dishonour** — Bāpiyā attacks the caravan and **threatens the women with abduction / molestation**; the Cāraṇ have no warrior-escort to defend their honour. (Cāraṇ folk tradition) [folk]
- ***Tragu* — the self-mutilation** — rather than submit, Bahucharā invokes the **Cāraṇ right of *tragu*** (ritual self-wounding / self-immolation as a sacred curse-weapon) and **cuts off her own breast(s) / mutilates herself**, turning her shed blood into a death-curse upon the assailant. (Cāraṇ folk tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **The curse of impotence** — dying by *tragu*, Bahucharā **curses Bāpiyā with impotence / loss of manhood (becoming a *napuṃsaka*)** — he is unmanned for laying violent hands on a Cāraṇ woman. (Cāraṇ folk tradition) [folk]
- **The lifting of the curse — worship in female guise** — Bāpiyā can be **released from impotence only by worshipping Bahucharā as a woman** — dressing and living in female guise and offering devotion at her shrine; submission to the goddess **in a female mode** is the sole remedy. This clause makes Bahucharā the goddess who **sanctions the surrender of manhood** and grounds her patronage of those who renounce or transcend male gender. (Cāraṇ folk tradition) [folk] [scholarly]
- **Apotheosis and the seat at Becharājī** — Bahucharā is recognized as an *āvataraṇa* of the great **[Devī](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)**; her *sthāna* at **Becharājī** (Mehsāṇā) becomes her great pilgrimage temple. (sthala-tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant (sthala-mahatmya, Becharājī tradition):* some local tellings instead centre an aniconic seat under a **vaṛkhalī / sacred tree** discovered by a devotee, the goddess revealing herself there; the *tragu*-and-curse legend and the tree-shrine legend coexist in the temple lore. [folk] [regional]

## 4. Iconography — the Rooster-Rider

- **The rooster (kukkuṭa) vāhana** — Bahucharā's defining emblem is her **cock / rooster mount (kukkuṭa)**, unusual among Hindu goddesses; she is shown **seated on or accompanied by a rooster**, an emblem of dawn, virility and martial readiness. [regional] [folk]
- **The Bālā warrior form** — she is iconographically a **youthful Durgā-type maiden (Bālā)** — multi-armed in temple images, bearing **triśūla (trident), sword, *abhaya* and other attributes**, red-robed; in her own tradition she is marked by the **rooster** and the *tragu* legend. [regional]
- **The breast / *tragu* motif** — her self-mutilation is recalled in cult imagery and gesture; the goddess who **cut off her own breast** presides over the loss and transcendence of the body's gendered marks — a thread that ties her to her hijra devotees. [folk] [scholarly]
- **Aniconic and *yantra* forms** — the Becharājī seat is venerated also as a **bāla-yantra / aniconic stone and tree-seat**, the figural image being a Sanskritizing overlay on an older folk *sthāna*. [folk]
- **Not Brahmanical in origin** — like **[Khodiyār](khodiyar-mata.md)** and the other Cāraṇ *āīs*, Bahucharā sits **outside Sanskrit temple-orthodoxy**: her authority is the **Cāraṇ *sākhī*, the *tragu* charter, the kuladevī bond and the Becharājī seat**, retrofitted to **[Ādi-Śakti / Durgā](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)** theology. [scholarly]

## 5. Temple and Pilgrimage (the living cult)

- **Becharājī (Bahucharājī), Mehsāṇā** — her **principal temple** stands at **Becharājī** in **Mehsāṇā district, Gujarāt**, north-west of Ahmedabad; it is one of the **three great Śakti-pīṭha-type pilgrimage centres of Gujarat** popularly grouped with **Ambājī** and **Mahākālī of Pāvāgaḍh**. [regional]
- **The 18th-century shrine** — the present main temple is traditionally said to have been **built / rebuilt in the 18th century** under **Gaekwad (Maratha) patronage** (the figure **Mānājī Rāo Gaekwad** is named in temple lore), consolidating an older folk seat into a major institution. [regional] [disputed]
- **Festival calendar** — **Caitra and Āśo (Āśvina) Navarātri**, **Caitra Sud Caudaś / Pūrṇimā** and her appearance-days draw mass pilgrimage, with **garbā, *jot* (lamp) and *havan* worship**; her *melā* (fairs) are major Gujarati pilgrim events. [regional] [folk]
- **A pīṭha of all-Gujarat reach** — by temple-scale and pilgrim-numbers Bahucharā ranks with **Ambājī, [Khodiyār](khodiyar-mata.md) and Hingḷāj** as a leading goddess of the Gujarat zone, her cult **living and recent**, richly attested in folk song and temple-building, not in scripture. [regional]

## 6. Patron of the Hijra Community (her signature role)

- **The kuladevī of the hijras** — Bahucharā is the **chief patron and tutelary goddess of the hijra (transgender / "third-gender") community of Gujarat and much of India**; the *tragu* curse — release from impotence only through **worship in female guise** — is read as the **divine charter** for those who renounce male gender. [regional] [scholarly]
- **The emasculation rite (*nirvāṇa*)** — in traditional hijra practice the **ritual emasculation / castration (*nirvāṇa*)** by which a *hijra* is "reborn" is performed **under the invocation of Bahucharā Mātā**, the goddess held to give the strength to undergo it and to bless the transformed devotee; the act is framed as **surrender of manhood to the Mother**. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Devotion and pilgrimage** — hijra *gharānās* and individuals make **pilgrimage to Becharājī**, offer worship, sing her praises, and invoke her in their **badhāī (blessing-songs at births and weddings)**, claiming her sanction to **bless fertility and bestow boons of progeny** — a power earned precisely by their renunciation of generative manhood. [regional] [scholarly]
- **The goddess of gender-transcendence** — Bahucharā thus governs **manhood, its loss, and its transcendence**: she who **unmanned Bāpiyā** and **cut off her own breast** presides over a community defined by the crossing of gender, making her one of the most theologically distinctive goddesses of western India. [scholarly]
- **Distinct from Khodiyār and the wider Śākta field** — Bahucharā shares the **Cāraṇ-born lok-devī milieu** of **[Khodiyār](khodiyar-mata.md)** (crocodile-rider, kuladevī of Saurāṣṭra), but is a **separate goddess** — different mount (rooster vs crocodile), different seat (Becharājī, Mehsāṇā vs Galdharā/Matel, Saurāṣṭra) and a **unique hijra patronage** Khodiyār does not carry. Her gender-transgressive devotee cult invites comparison with **[Reṇukā-Yellammā](renuka-yellamma.md)** and her *jogati/jogatī* devotees, though the legends are wholly distinct. [regional] [scholarly]

> **Two clocks.** Bahucharā carries **no scriptural yuga-date**. The *tragu*-and-curse legend is Cāraṇ oral *sākhī*, and Bahucharā as a *worshipped goddess* is a **kali-historical** lok-devī / Śakti-pīṭha cult — the Becharājī seat, the Gaekwad-era temple and the hijra patronage attested in recent centuries, not in Veda or Purāṇa. Her equation with the Purāṇic **[Devī / Ādi-Śakti / Bālā](devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)** is a **Sanskritizing overlay**, kept on a separate clock from the Cāraṇ mother herself.
