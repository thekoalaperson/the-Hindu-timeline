# The Saptarishi — The Seven Sages Across the Manvantaras

> Deities-and-hierarchy node. The **Saptarishi** ("seven seers") are the brahmana-pole of the cosmic administration that is re-constituted every *manvantara* — the counterpart to the Manu, the Indra, and the manvantara-avatar. For the full administrative schema and the per-manvantara seer-lists see [../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md](../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md); for the cosmic clock see [../00-time-and-cosmology/units-of-time.md](../00-time-and-cosmology/units-of-time.md). ## 1. What the Saptarishi Are

- **The seven seers** - A fixed *office* of seven supreme rishis who anchor each manvantara: they preserve and re-promulgate the **Vedas** after the yuga-decline, father the brahmana *gotras* (clan-lineages), and serve as the standing council of dharma alongside the Manu and Indra. (Vishnu Purana 3.1-2; Bhagavata Purana 8.13)
- **Mind-born sons of Brahma** - In the dominant Puranic creation account, the original Saptarishi are *manasaputras* — "mind-born sons" emitted from Brahma's mind/body to begin procreation — and are simultaneously titled **Prajapatis** ("lords of progeny"), the seeders of all subsequent beings. (Manu Smriti 1.34-35; Vishnu Purana 1.7; Bhagavata Purana 3.12) — see [trimurti-and-tridevi.md](./trimurti-and-tridevi.md) and [devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md](./devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
- **They CHANGE each manvantara** - Like the Indra-office and the Manu-office, the seership rotates: each of the 14 manvantaras has its **own** set of seven sages. The "Saptarishi" is therefore a renewable cosmic post, not a single permanent committee. (Vishnu Purana 3.1-2; Bhagavata Purana 8.13) — full per-manvantara lists in [../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md §4](../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md).
- **Brahmarshi rank** - The Saptarishi are the archetypal **Brahmarshis** (the highest grade of rishi, above Rajarshi and Maharshi); attaining "Brahmarshi" is precisely what makes one fit for the office, as the Vishvamitra arc dramatizes (§4). (Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda; Mahabharata)
- **Naimisha / oral transmission** - The seers are the human terminus of the Vedic *shruti* chain: what Brahma "sees," the rishis "hear" and transmit. (See [../90-literature-corpus/shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md](../90-literature-corpus/shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md).)

## 2. The Composition — ALL Variant Lists by Source

There is **no single canonical seven.** Different strata of literature name different sets; the two best-known are the Vedantic (Brihadaranyaka) list of historical hymn-lineages and the Puranic/astronomical list of Brahma's mind-born sons. All major variants are cataloged below.

- **Puranic / astronomical set (most common today)** - **Marichi, Atri, Angiras, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu, Vasishtha** — the seven mind-born Prajapatis, and the names mapped onto the seven stars of Ursa Major. (Rigveda asterism tradition; Vishnu Purana 1.7; Mahabharata, Brihat Samhita)
  - *Variant (Mahabharata / Brihat Samhita ordering):* Angiras, Atri, Kratu, Marichi, Pulaha, Pulastya, Vasishtha — the same seven mind-born sons, listed alphabetically/differently. (Mahabharata; Varahamihira, Brihat Samhita)
- **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad (2.2.4 / 2.2.6)** - **Atri, Bharadvaja, Gautama, Jamadagni, Kashyapa, Vasishtha, Vishvamitra** — a set of *historical Vedic clan-founders* rather than Brahma's mind-sons. This is also essentially the **current (Vaivasvata) manvantara** roster. (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 2.2)
- **Jaiminiya Brahmana (2.218-221)** - **Agastya, Atri, Bharadvaja, Gautama, Jamadagni, Vasishtha, Vishvamitra** — substitutes **Agastya** for Kashyapa. (Jaiminiya Brahmana)
- **Krishna Yajurveda (sandhya-vandana mantras)** - **Angiras, Atri, Bhrigu, Gautama, Kashyapa, Kutsa, Vasishtha** — notably includes **Bhrigu** and the otherwise-rare **Kutsa**. (Taittiriya / Krishna Yajurveda recitation lists) [scholarly]
- **Gopatha Brahmana (1.2.8)** - lists **eight** rather than seven: Vasishtha, Vishvamitra, Jamadagni, Gautama, Bharadvaja, Gungu, Agastya, Kashyapa. (Gopatha Brahmana) [scholarly]
- *Variant (Bhrigu / Daksha as alternates):* Several lists swap in **Bhrigu** (the Bhargava patriarch, §4) and some early enumerations of Brahma's mind-sons include **Daksha** among the Prajapatis, so depending on the count Daksha and Bhrigu drift in and out of the "seven." The wider Prajapati group is variously given as 7, 9, or 10 (e.g., adding Bhrigu, Daksha, Narada, Sanaka). (Manu Smriti 1.35; Bhagavata Purana 3.12; Mahabharata) [disputed]
- *Note (Vishvamitra the anomaly):* Vishvamitra is the one seer **born a Kshatriya** who forced his way into the brahmana seership by penance — uniquely, he is a Saptarishi who was *not* a mind-born brahmana son of Brahma. (Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 51-65) (See §4.)

### The CURRENT (Vaivasvata, 7th) manvantara Saptarishi

- **Bhagavata Purana 8.13** - **Kashyapa, Atri, Vasishtha, Vishvamitra, Gautama, Jamadagni, Bharadvaja.**
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 3.2 ordering):* Vasishtha, Kashyapa, Atri, Jamadagni, Gautama, Vishvamitra, Bharadvaja — same seven, different sequence.
  - *Variant (with Agastya/Bhrigu):* some recitation traditions substitute **Agastya** or **Bhrigu** for Kashyapa in the current set, matching the Jaiminiya/Yajurveda lists above. (cross-text note) [disputed]
- For past and future manvantara rosters (Svarochisha, Uttami, Tamasa, Raivata, Chakshusha; and future Savarni with Galava & Parashurama), see [../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md §4](../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md).

## 3. The Saptarishi in the Sky — Ursa Major & Arundhati

- **Saptarishi Mandala = Ursa Major (Big Dipper)** - The seven bright stars of the Great Bear are identified one-to-one with the seven sages; the asterism is named *Saptarishi* in Indian astronomy, an identification already implicit in the Rigveda (the "seven seers" fixed in the northern sky). (Rigveda; Surya Siddhanta; Brihat Samhita)
- **Star-to-sage mapping** - The standard correspondence assigns the Puranic seven (Marichi, Vasishtha, Angiras, Atri, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu) to the Dipper's stars; mappings differ slightly by source, but **Vasishtha is always the star Mizar (ζ Ursae Majoris)** in the handle. (Brihat Samhita; traditional astronomy)
- **Arundhati — Vasishtha's wife — the faint companion star** - **Arundhati** (the chaste wife of Vasishtha, herself counted among ideal *pativratas*) is identified with **Alcor (80 Ursae Majoris)**, the faint star that orbits beside Mizar. The pair Mizar–Alcor (Vasishtha–Arundhati) is a true visual/binary double. (Hindu astronomy tradition; Ursa Major)
  - **Arundhati-darshana marriage rite** - In the Hindu wedding the groom shows the bride the Arundhati–Vasishtha pair as the model of inseparable, faithful union (and as a traditional eyesight/clarity test). [folk tradition] (marriage-ritual usage)
- **Dhruva (Pole Star) and the precession story** - The Saptarishi wheel around **Dhruva** (Polaris, the fixed pole-star, a devotee turned eternal); their slow apparent motion underpins the legendary **"Saptarishi era"** calendar (a reckoning in which the sages "advance" through the nakshatras over centuries). (Vishnu Purana; Brihat Samhita; Kalhana's Rajatarangini for the era) [scholarly]

## 4. Capsule Roles & Signature Episodes

The seven (plus the close associates Bhrigu and Agastya) carry many of the foundational myth-cycles. One dense line per sage / episode.

- **Vasishtha — the priest of the Solar dynasty** - *Kulaguru* (family priest) of the Ikshvaku / Suryavamsha kings, including Rama; keeper of the wish-cow **Kamadhenu/Nandini/Shabala**; husband of **Arundhati**; one of the most-invoked Rigvedic hymn-authors (RV Mandala 7). (Rigveda 7; Valmiki Ramayana; Vishnu Purana) — see [../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
- **The Vasishtha–Vishvamitra rivalry** - King **Vishvamitra**, a Kshatriya, covets Vasishtha's cow Kamadhenu; she conjures an army (Pallavas, Shakas, Yavanas, Dravidas, etc. from her body) that routs his forces — proving tapas > arms. Humiliated, Vishvamitra renounces his throne and undertakes millennia of penance to rise from Rajarshi to **Brahmarshi**, eventually winning the title from Vasishtha himself. (Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 51-65; Mahabharata, Adi Parva)
  - **Death of Vasishtha's sons** - In the feud's bitter phase, Vishvamitra causes king **Kalmashapada** (possessed by a rakshasa) to devour all of Vasishtha's hundred sons; the grieving Vasishtha attempts suicide but is preserved. (Mahabharata, Adi Parva; Valmiki Ramayana)
  - **Trishanku's heaven** - When Vasishtha and his sons refuse to send king **Trishanku** bodily to heaven, Vishvamitra does so by sheer tapas; Indra hurls Trishanku back, and Vishvamitra freezes him mid-fall and begins building a *counter-heaven* (and a second set of stars/Brahma) — exhausting his austerities and forcing him to start over. (Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 57-60)
  - *Variant (reconciliation):* the epics close the cycle with the two seers reconciled, Vishvamitra finally hailed "Brahmarshi" by Vasishtha. (Mahabharata; Ramayana)
- **Vishvamitra — beyond the rivalry** - Composer of the **Gayatri Mantra** (RV 3.62.10); guru who takes the boy Rama and Lakshmana to slay Tataka and guard his sacrifice (launching the Ramayana); father of **Shakuntala** by the apsara **Menaka**, who breaks his penance. (Rigveda 3; Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda; Mahabharata, Adi Parva — Shakuntala) — see [../04-deep-dives/](../04-deep-dives/).
- **Atri & Anasuya** - The sage **Atri** and his supremely chaste wife **Anasuya**: when the Trimurti come to test her chastity disguised as mendicants demanding to be fed while she is nude, she turns them into three infants; the three gods are then reborn as her sons — **Dattatreya (Vishnu), Chandra/Soma (Brahma), and Durvasa (Shiva)**. (Bhagavata Purana; Markandeya Purana; Ramayana, Anasuya episode)
  - *Note:* via **Soma/Chandra**, Atri is the ancestral root of the **Chandravamsha** (Lunar dynasty). (Vishnu Purana 4.6) — see [../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
- **Bhrigu kicks Vishnu** - Tasked with judging which of the Trimurti is supreme, **Bhrigu** insults each in turn; finding Vishnu asleep, he **kicks Vishnu in the chest**, and Vishnu responds only by gently massaging the sage's foot — winning the test as the most forbearing (the origin myth of Vishnu's *Shrivatsa* mark and Lakshmi's pique). (Bhagavata Purana 10.89; Padma Purana)
  - *Variant (Bhrigu's curse on Lakshmi/Shiva):* in some tellings Lakshmi, offended that her chest-dwelling was struck, curses brahmanas to poverty; Bhrigu in turn or Shiva react variously — retellings differ. (Padma Purana; sthala-puranas) [disputed]
  - *Note:* Bhrigu heads the **Bhargava** line; he and his descendants (Shukra, Chyavana, Parashurama's Jamadagni line) make Bhrigu a frequent alternate member of the Saptarishi. (Mahabharata; Bhagavata Purana)
- **Agastya drinks the ocean** - When the Asuras (the Kalakeyas) hide in the sea after harassing the gods, **Agastya drinks the entire ocean dry** in one sip so the gods can destroy them — afterward the ocean must be refilled (linked to the Ganga-descent cycle). (Mahabharata, Vana Parva 104-105)
  - **Agastya humbles the Vindhyas** - The **Vindhya range** grows skyward to block the sun out of jealousy of Meru; Agastya, traveling south, asks the mountains to bow so he may pass and "stay low until I return" — and never returns, permanently flattening them; this myth marks Agastya as the great **rishi who carried Vedic culture south** (revered across Tamil tradition as founder of Tamil grammar). (Mahabharata, Vana Parva; South Indian / Tamil tradition) [folk tradition]
  - *Note:* Agastya is the **southern star Canopus** (Agastya), whose heliacal rising is ritually observed; he is also a chiranjivi (long-lived sage). (Brihat Samhita)
- **Kashyapa — father of beings** - The Prajapati **Kashyapa**, by his thirteen wives (daughters of Daksha — Aditi, Diti, Danu, etc.), fathers the **Adityas (devas), Daityas, Danavas, Nagas, birds, and most living classes** — the universal patriarch of the Vaivasvata creation. (Vishnu Purana 1.15, 1.21; Mahabharata, Adi Parva) — see [devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md](./devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md). Kashmir (*Kashyapa-mira*) is traditionally named for him.
- **Angiras — the Atharva/fire line** - **Angiras**, source of the **Angirasa** hymns and (with Atharvan) of the Atharvaveda tradition; ancestor of **Brihaspati** (guru of the devas) and the **Bharadvaja/Gautama** Angirasa clans. (Rigveda; Atharvaveda; Mahabharata)
- **Pulastya — root of the Rakshasas (and the Puranas)** - **Pulastya**, grandfather of **Ravana, Kubera, and Vibhishana** (through his son Vishravas); also the sage to whom **Parashara** the Puranic narrator is linked, making Pulastya a key transmitter of Purana lore. (Vishnu Purana 1.7-8; Ramayana, Uttara Kanda)
- **Pulaha & Kratu — the quieter Prajapatis** - **Pulaha** (father of various creatures and sages) and **Kratu** (in some accounts father of the **Valakhilyas**, the 60,000 thumb-sized ascetic sages) round out the seven mind-born sons; both appear mainly in genealogical and creation contexts. (Vishnu Purana 1.7, 1.10; Bhagavata Purana 4.1)
- **Marichi — eldest mind-son** - **Marichi**, usually named *first* of the mind-born sons; father of **Kashyapa** and thus grandfather of devas and asuras alike — the genealogical apex of the Vaivasvata creation. (Vishnu Purana 1.7; Bhagavata Purana 3.12)
- **Gautama, Jamadagni, Bharadvaja (the current-set clan-fathers)** - **Gautama** (husband of **Ahalya**, cursed to stone and freed by Rama; founder of the Nyaya-linked Gautama gotra); **Jamadagni** (father of **Parashurama**, the axe-wielding 6th avatar — see [dashavatara.md](./dashavatara.md)); **Bharadvaja** (great Vedic seer, host of Rama in the Ramayana, linked to the Bharadvaja gotra and Ayurveda/Dhanurveda traditions). (Ramayana; Mahabharata; Vishnu Purana)

## 5. Why They Matter to the Timeline

- The Saptarishi are the **brahmana axis** of the renewable cosmic order: at every manvantara turnover (see [../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md](../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md)) a fresh seven re-anchor the Vedas, mirroring the new Manu (royal/legal axis) and new Indra (divine-political axis).
- In the **Matsya flood**, Vaivasvata Manu carries "the seven sages, the seed of all creatures, and the Vedas" across the pralaya — the Saptarishi are literally the cargo that re-seeds knowledge for our humanity. (Matsya Purana; Bhagavata Purana 8.24) — see manvantara-system.md §5.
- Through their *gotras* they are the **genealogical and ritual root of living brahmana lineages**, making them the one cosmic institution with an unbroken thread into recorded history. See [../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md).
