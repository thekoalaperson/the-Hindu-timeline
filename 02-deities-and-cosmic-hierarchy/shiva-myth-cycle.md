# The Śiva Myth-Cycle — Cosmic Deeds of Mahādeva

> Where this sits in the hierarchy: `02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/` — a deep expansion of the Śiva section (§4) of [`trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](trimurti-and-tridevi.md). That node fixes Śiva's place on the cosmic clock (the tamas-aspect destroyer/dissolver who ends each Kalpa, consort Pārvatī, seat Kailāsa); **this file** catalogues the major Śaiva myth-episodes — each a dense, source-attributed event with all sectarian and Purāṇic variants. The recurring frame is **Śaiva-primacy**: the transcendent Sadāśiva/Maheśvara is above the Trimūrti, and these deeds repeatedly stage Brahmā and Viṣṇu as Śiva's subordinates. For the cosmic-time framing of the destruction these deeds embody, see [`../00-time-and-cosmology/kalpa-and-pralaya.md`](../00-time-and-cosmology/kalpa-and-pralaya.md).

---

## 1. Cosmological / Transcendent Manifestations

- **Liṅgodbhava — the pillar of fire (jyotirliṅga origin)** — when Brahmā and Viṣṇu quarrel over supremacy, an infinite pillar of fire/light (Liṅga) erupts; whoever finds its end is supreme. Brahmā (as **haṃsa**) flies up, Viṣṇu (as **Varāha**) digs down; neither finds the limit. Viṣṇu honestly concedes; Brahmā falsely claims success → cursed (no worship). Establishes Śiva as the unbounded Absolute and the liṅga as his aniconic form. (*Śiva Purāṇa Vidyeśvara-saṃhitā; Liṅga Purāṇa; Kūrma Purāṇa; Vāyu Purāṇa*) [foundational Śaiva-primacy myth] — see the fuller summary and the ketakī/cow variants at [`trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](trimurti-and-tridevi.md) §4; **this file does not duplicate it**, only places it as the cosmological head of the cycle.
  - *Doctrinal note:* the date here is "outside time" — the liṅga is the unmanifest Brahman prior to and underlying every Kalpa, not an event within the current one.

- **Ardhanārīśvara — the half-male/half-female form** — Śiva fuses with Pārvatī into one body split down the centre (right male, left female), expressing that Śiva (puruṣa/consciousness) and Śakti (prakṛti/energy) are inseparable; without Śakti, Śiva is *śava* (a corpse). (*Śiva Purāṇa; Liṅga Purāṇa; Skanda Purāṇa*) — the Śaiva ↔ Śākta hinge.
  - *Variant (origin):* Brahmā, finding his mind-born creation could not multiply, prays to Śiva, who appears as Ardhanārīśvara and separates out the female principle so sexual generation can begin. (*Liṅga Purāṇa; Śiva Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (sage Bhṛṅgi):* the devotee Bhṛṅgi insists on circumambulating only Śiva, not Pārvatī; Śiva takes the Ardhanārīśvara form so Bhṛṅgi cannot separate them — Bhṛṅgi becomes an insect/bores between them, and is reduced to skin and bone. (*Śaiva sthala-purāṇa / folk tradition*)

- **Dakṣiṇāmūrti — the silent south-facing guru** — Śiva as the supreme teacher seated under a banyan, expounding jñāna, yoga, music, and śāstra through **silence (cinmudrā)** to the four aged Kumāras (Sanaka et al.), who appear as youths to the youthful-yet-ancient teacher. The ultimate truth is taught wordlessly. (*Śiva Purāṇa; later Śaiva/Advaita tradition; Śaṅkara's Dakṣiṇāmūrti Stotra*)

- **Mahākāla — Śiva as Time-the-devourer** — Śiva as **Great Time/Death**, who consumes all beings and even Brahmā at the great dissolution; the form enshrined at Ujjain's **Mahākāleśvara** jyotirliṅga (the only south-facing/dakṣiṇāmukha liṅga). At mahāpralaya he reabsorbs the cosmos. (*Śiva Purāṇa; Liṅga Purāṇa; Mahābhārata*) — see [`../00-time-and-cosmology/units-of-time.md`](../00-time-and-cosmology/units-of-time.md) for Kāla as cosmic time.

- **Naṭarāja & the Ānanda Tāṇḍava — the cosmic dance of bliss** — Śiva as **Lord of Dance** performs the **Ānanda Tāṇḍava** ("dance of bliss") in the golden hall (Cit-Sabhā) of **Cidambaram**, the symbolic centre of consciousness; the dance enacts the **pañcakṛtya (five acts)** by which Śiva runs all existence:
  1. **Sṛṣṭi** (creation) — the upper-right hand's **ḍamaru** drum sounds primal sound/time;
  2. **Sthiti** (preservation) — the lower-right **abhaya-mudrā** ("fear not");
  3. **Saṃhāra** (dissolution) — the upper-left hand's **agni** (fire) of destruction;
  4. **Tirobhāva** (concealment/veiling) — the planted right foot crushing the dwarf **Apasmāra** (ignorance/forgetfulness), the bondage of māyā;
  5. **Anugraha** (grace/liberation) — the raised left foot and the gesture pointing to it, refuge for the soul.
  (*Cidambaram Naṭarāja tradition; Śaiva Siddhānta theology; Tirumular's Tirumantiram; Naṭarāja bronze iconography, Cōḻa period [scholarly]*) — the dance is the destruction-and-renewal of [`../00-time-and-cosmology/kalpa-and-pralaya.md`](../00-time-and-cosmology/kalpa-and-pralaya.md) rendered as art.
  - *Variant (Rudra Tāṇḍava / Saṃhāra Tāṇḍava):* the **fierce/violent** dance of destruction performed at the world's end (and in cremation grounds), contrasted with the blissful Ānanda Tāṇḍava — the same dance read as terror vs. grace. (*Śaiva tradition*)
  - *Variant (the dance-contest origin):* the form is won in a dance-duel against **Kālī** (or against the demoness in the Tāraka-forest myth, where Śiva subdues heretical sages) — when Śiva raises his leg in the **ūrdhva-tāṇḍava** pose, Kālī, bound by modesty, cannot match it and concedes. (*Tamil Śaiva sthala-purāṇa; Cidambaram māhātmya [late text/folk]*)
  - *Scholarly note:* the iconic four-armed Naṭarāja bronze crystallises in the Pallava–Cōḻa Tamil south (c. 7th–10th c. CE), later than the Purāṇic strata; the "cosmic dancer" reading is largely a south-Indian Śaiva Siddhānta development [scholarly].

## 2. The Ocean-Churning & Sustaining Deeds

- **Hālāhala / Nīlakaṇṭha — drinking the world-poison** — during the **Samudra-manthana** (churning of the milk-ocean), the serpent Vāsuki disgorges the all-destroying **Hālāhala** poison; to save creation Śiva drinks it, and Pārvatī clutches his throat so it cannot pass into his body, leaving it lodged there — hence **Nīlakaṇṭha**, "the blue-throated." (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa; Mahābhārata Ādi Parva; Śiva Purāṇa*) — the churning itself is catalogued from the Vaiṣṇava side in [`trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](trimurti-and-tridevi.md) §3 and (deep) under the Samudra-Manthana deep-dive in [`../04-deep-dives/`](../04-deep-dives/).
  - *Variant (origin of the poison):* the Hālāhala arises from Vāsuki's fanged mouth as he is wrenched back and forth; in some tellings it is the first thing to emerge, threatening both devas and asuras alike, who flee to Śiva. (*Bhāgavata 8.7*)
  - *Variant (Śaiva framing):* the deed is read as proof that Śiva, not Viṣṇu, is the real saviour of the churning — Viṣṇu organises the churn, but only Śiva can absorb its lethal by-product. (*Śiva Purāṇa* tilt)

- **Gaṅgādhara — bearing the descent of Gaṅgā** — when King Bhagīratha's penance brings the celestial **Gaṅgā** down to earth to redeem the ashes of Sagara's 60,000 sons, her fall would shatter the world; Śiva catches her in his matted locks (jaṭā) and releases her in a tamed stream. (*Rāmāyaṇa Bāla Kāṇḍa; Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Mahābhārata*) — links the Ikṣvāku/Sūryavaṃśa line; see [`../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md`](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
  - *Variant:* Gaṅgā, proud, descends with full force intending to sweep Śiva to the netherworld; he angrily traps her entirely in his hair so she vanishes, releasing her only after Bhagīratha's further penance — a humbling of Gaṅgā's pride. (*Rāmāyaṇa; Skanda Purāṇa*)

## 3. Demon-Slaying & Time-Conquering Deeds

- **Tripurāntaka — destroyer of the three flying cities** — the three asura brothers **Tārakākṣa, Vidyunmālī, and Kamalākṣa** (sons of Tāraka), after penance to Brahmā, receive three indestructible flying cities (**Tripura**) built by the asura-architect **Maya** — one of gold (heaven), one of silver (sky/air), one of iron (earth) — destructible only when all three align on a single instant, by a single arrow. After a millennium of misrule, Śiva mounts a **cosmic chariot** and looses one arrow that incinerates all three at the aligned moment. (*Śiva Purāṇa Rudra-saṃhitā; Liṅga Purāṇa; Matsya Purāṇa; Mahābhārata Karṇa Parva*) [Śaiva-primacy: every other god becomes a mere component of Śiva's weapon-system]
  - *The chariot/weapon (canonical inventory):* **Earth (Bhūmi)** = chariot body; **Sun and Moon** = the two wheels; **Brahmā** = charioteer; **Mount Meru** = the bow; **Vāsuki** (serpent) = bowstring; **Viṣṇu** = the arrow-shaft; **Agni** = arrowhead; **Vāyu** = the fletching feathers; the four Vedas = the horses. (*Mahābhārata Karṇa Parva; Śiva Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (Padma Purāṇa):* the three brothers are named Tārakākṣa, Kamalākṣa, and **Vidyunmālī**, with the third sometimes called **Vīryavāna**; the boon is explicitly that they cannot be killed except by the single aligned arrow. (*Padma Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (the smile that burns):* in some tellings Śiva does not even need to release the arrow fully — a mere **smile (or laugh)** of Maheśvara reduces the cities to ash once aligned; emphasises that the elaborate weapon is theatre, the power is Śiva's will. (*Śaiva tradition*)
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu-as-bull / the deluding monk):* before the war Viṣṇu, as a heretic teacher (or a bull/Buddha-like preacher), seduces the Tripura asuras away from dharma to make them vulnerable. (*Śiva Purāṇa; Matsya Purāṇa*)

- **Kāmadahana — the burning of Kāma** — the gods, needing a son of Śiva to slay the invincible demon **Tārakāsura**, send **Kāmadeva** (the love-god) to fire his flower-arrow at the meditating Śiva and kindle desire for **Pārvatī**; the disturbed Śiva opens his **third eye** and reduces Kāma to ash. At the pleas of Kāma's widow **Rati** (and Pārvatī), Śiva later restores him — but **bodiless (Ananga)**, surviving only as the felt mind-state of love. (*Śiva Purāṇa Rudra-saṃhitā; Matsya Purāṇa; Kālidāsa's Kumārasaṃbhava*) — the union it enables produces **Kārttikeya/Skanda**, the slayer of Tārakāsura.
  - *Variant (resurrection):* Kāma is reborn in body as **Pradyumna**, son of Kṛṣṇa and Rukmiṇī, in the Dvāpara age. (*Bhāgavata; Harivaṃśa*) — cross-link [`../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md`](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md).
  - *Festival link:* the episode underlies **Holi / Kāma-dahana**, especially in the south. [living tradition]

- **Kālāntaka & Mārkaṇḍeya — conquering Death (Yama) for a boy-devotee** — the sage Mṛkaṇḍu's son **Mārkaṇḍeya** is fated to die at sixteen. A perfect Śiva-devotee, on his appointed day he clings to the **liṅga**. When **Yama** flings his noose to drag the boy's soul, the noose loops the liṅga itself; Śiva bursts forth as **Kālāntaka** ("Ender of Death/Time"), kicks/strikes Yama down (in some tellings slaying him outright), and grants Mārkaṇḍeya **deathless eternal youth** at sixteen. (*Śiva Purāṇa; Skanda Purāṇa; Liṅga Purāṇa; Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa context*) — the theological seat of the **Mahā-Mṛtyuñjaya** ("great death-conquering") mantra (*Ṛgveda 7.59.12, the Tryambaka mantra*).
  - *Variant (mantra-source):* the Mahā-Mṛtyuñjaya is taught to Mārkaṇḍeya by **Brahmā** (or by his father Mṛkaṇḍu) beforehand, which is what makes him invulnerable. (*Śaiva tradition*)
  - *Variant (Yama restored):* because Yama is necessary to the cosmic order, Śiva revives him after the lesson; "Kālāntaka" thus means master-of-death, not its permanent abolition. (*Śiva Purāṇa*)

- **Bhairava — the severing of Brahmā's fifth head & the Kāpālika penance** — Brahmā, with five heads, grows arrogant (the fifth head speaks insolently / claims supremacy / lusts after his own daughter). From his fury Śiva manifests the terrifying **Kāla Bhairava** (born of his third eye/breath), who shears off the offending fifth head with a fingernail — leaving Brahmā four-headed. The sin of **brahmahatyā** (Brahmin-/Brahmā-slaying) clings to Bhairava as the **skull (kapāla)** fused to his palm; he must wander as a naked mendicant (**Bhikṣāṭana / Kapālin**), begging with the skull-bowl, until the sin is expiated. The skull finally drops only at **Kāśī/Vārāṇasī** (at Kapāla-mocana tīrtha), making Kāśī the supreme place of liberation. (*Śiva Purāṇa Śatarudra-saṃhitā; Skanda Purāṇa; Matsya Purāṇa*) [Śaiva-primacy myth — Śiva disciplines the creator-god himself] — cross-link the same event told from Brahmā's side in [`trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](trimurti-and-tridevi.md) §2.
  - *Variant (trigger):* the cause is variously (a) Brahmā's pride after the Liṅgodbhava lie, (b) the fifth head's disrespect to Śiva, or (c) Brahmā coveting his daughter Śatarūpā/Sandhyā. (*Śiva Purāṇa; Skanda Purāṇa; Matsya Purāṇa* differ)
  - *Variant (origin of Kāpālika sect):* the myth is the charter for the **Kāpālika / Kālāmukha** ascetics, who imitate Bhairava with skull-bowl, ash, and cremation-ground sādhana. (*Śaiva tantric tradition [scholarly]*)
  - *Variant (Bhairava as guardian):* Kāla Bhairava becomes the eternal **kotwāl (watchman) of Kāśī**; pilgrims must obtain his leave. [living tradition]

- **Gajāsura-saṃhāra (Gajāntaka) — slaying the elephant-demon** — the elephant-demon **Gajāsura** (son of Mahiṣāsura), boon-emboldened, torments the gods and devotees at Vārāṇasī; Śiva impales him on his trident, dances the fierce dance upon/within him, and flays his hide, wearing the elephant-skin as a garment — hence **Kṛttivāsas**, "clad in hide." (*Śiva Purāṇa Yuddha-/Rudra-saṃhitā; Liṅga Purāṇa; Kūrma Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (the boon at death):* the dying Gajāsura begs that Śiva forever wear his skin and remain present where he fell; granted at the **Kṛttivāseśvara** shrine, Vārāṇasī. (*sthala-purāṇa*)

- **Andhakāsura-vadha — slaying the blind/darkness demon** — **Andhaka** ("the blind one"), born of Śiva's own sweat-drop (or adopted by Hiraṇyākṣa), lusts after Pārvatī, not knowing she is in effect his mother. Śiva impales him on the trident and holds him aloft until his pride/lust is burned away; because each drop of Andhaka's blood spawns a clone, the **Mātṛkās / Saptamātṛkā** (in some texts created with Viṣṇu's help) drink the blood before it falls. The chastened Andhaka becomes the gaṇa-leader **Bhṛṅgi**. (*Matsya Purāṇa; Kūrma Purāṇa; Liṅga Purāṇa; Padma Purāṇa; Śiva Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (the Mātṛkās):* the blood-drinking goddesses are spawned by Śiva himself (or by the assembled gods), prefiguring the Raktabīja motif of the [`Devī Māhātmya`](trimurti-and-tridevi.md) §7. (*Matsya Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (redemption):* rather than killed, Andhaka is converted into a devotee and made chief of Śiva's hosts — a "salvation through impalement." (*Kūrma/Liṅga Purāṇa*)

- **Jālandhara-vadha — slaying the ocean-born demon & the Vṛndā deception** — **Jālandhara**, an immensely powerful asura born of Śiva's own fiery glance fallen into the ocean, becomes invincible through the chastity (pativratā-power) of his wife **Vṛndā**. To defeat him, **Viṣṇu** assumes Jālandhara's form and tricks Vṛndā into breaking her chastity; her power broken, Śiva slays Jālandhara with a cakra/discus spun from his own toe. The betrayed Vṛndā curses Viṣṇu (→ the **śālagrāma** stone / the Tulasī plant) and immolates herself. (*Śiva Purāṇa Rudra-saṃhitā Yuddha-khaṇḍa; Padma Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (Vṛndā = Tulasī):* Vṛndā is reborn as the sacred **Tulasī** plant, beloved of Viṣṇu — folding a Vaiṣṇava etiology into a Śaiva victory. (*Padma Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (origin of Gaṇeśa's beheading link / regional):* some regional tellings tie Jālandhara's energy or weapons into later episodes; treated as [folk tradition].

- **Vīrabhadra & the destruction of Dakṣa's sacrifice** — after **Satī** self-immolates at her father **Dakṣa's** yajña (where Śiva was insulted), the grief-maddened Śiva tears a lock of his hair and creates the colossal **Vīrabhadra** (with the goddess **Bhadrakālī**), who storms the sacrifice, scatters the gods, and **beheads Dakṣa** (later restored with a goat's head). Satī's scattered limbs become the **Śakti Pīṭhas**. (*Śiva Purāṇa; Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Kālikā Purāṇa; Vāyu Purāṇa*) — bridges into [`trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](trimurti-and-tridevi.md) §7 (the Śakti spectrum); listed here as a core Rudra-fury deed.
