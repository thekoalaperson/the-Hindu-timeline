# Forms of Viṣṇu — Vyūhas, Keśava Forms & Aspects

> Node in [`02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](README.md). A completeness-sweep roster of the **non-avatāra manifestations** of Viṣṇu (Nārāyaṇa / Hari) — the emanation-theology of the **Pāñcarātra** and **Vaikhānasa** Āgamas, as systematized by Śrī Vaiṣṇavism. Where the [Daśāvatāra](dashavatara.md) catalogs Viṣṇu's *descents into the world* (yuga-keyed), this file catalogs his *modes of being*: the **5 categories** (Para, Vyūha, Vibhava, Antaryāmin, Arcā), the **4 Vyūhas** (Vāsudeva–Saṅkarṣaṇa–Pradyumna–Aniruddha) and their secondary expansions, the **24 Keśava mūrtis** (the *Caturviṃśati-mūrti* of the *ācamana*), and the great iconographic/composite forms (Nārāyaṇa on Śeṣa, Hayagrīva, Mohinī, Dhanvantari, Viśvarūpa, Vaikuṇṭha-Caturmūrti, Hari-Hara). The 24-mūrti roster is **closed and canonical**; the Vibhava and Arcā categories are **open-ended** [flag] — Vibhava overlaps the (innumerable) avatāra list and Arcā multiplies with every consecrated temple image.

---

## Doctrine: the five aspects (*pañca-prakāra*) of the One

Pāñcarātra theology (formalized by **Yāmuna** and **Rāmānuja** for [Śrī Vaiṣṇavism](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md)) holds that the supreme Brahman — Viṣṇu-Nārāyaṇa — is *single* but graciously accessible in **five descending modes**, so that the soul may reach him at whatever level it can. (*Lakṣmī Tantra*; *Ahirbudhnya Saṃhitā*; Rāmānuja)

- **1. Para (परा)** — the **transcendent supreme**: Viṣṇu in [Vaikuṇṭha](../06-sacred-geography/README.md) seated on Ananta-Śeṣa with Śrī, possessing the full **six guṇas** (*ṣāḍguṇya*) undivided. Invisible to the unliberated; the goal of the *muktas*. (*Pāñcarātra*; *Lakṣmī Tantra*)
- **2. Vyūha (व्यूह)** — the **fourfold cosmic emanation** (*caturvyūha*) for the work of creation, sustenance and dissolution; each Vyūha foregrounds a pair of the six guṇas. The proper subject of this file's §A.
- **3. Vibhava (विभव)** — the **incarnatory descents** (avatāra) into the worlds across the yugas: Matsya, Kūrma, Rāma, Kṛṣṇa, etc. — fully cataloged at [Daśāvatāra](dashavatara.md). The *Ahirbudhnya Saṃhitā* lists **39 vibhavas**; the *Bhāgavata* calls them innumerable. [open-ended]
- **4. Antaryāmin (अन्तर्यामिन्)** — the **indwelling inner controller**, the *paramātman* seated in the heart of every being (*Bṛhadāraṇyaka* 3.7's "inner ruler") and in every atom; the object of yogic interiorization. (*Upaniṣads*; Pāñcarātra)
- **5. Arcā (अर्चा)** — the **consecrated icon** (*arcāvatāra*): Viṣṇu's gracious self-confinement into a temple *mūrti* or *śālagrāma* so the embodied devotee may serve him directly (e.g. Raṅganātha at Śrīraṅgam, Veṅkaṭeśvara at Tirumala, Jagannātha at Purī). The most accessible mode; central to *prapatti* (surrender). [open-ended — multiplies per temple] (Vaikhānasa & Pāñcarātra Āgamas)

> The five form a **ladder of accessibility**, Para being hardest and Arcā easiest to approach — the theological charter for temple worship in Śrī Vaiṣṇavism. (Rāmānuja; Vedānta Deśika, *Pāñcarātra-rakṣā*)

## A. The Four Vyūhas (*Caturvyūha*)

The primary emanations from Para-Vāsudeva, each a complete deity yet foregrounding two of the **six divine guṇas** — *jñāna* (knowledge), *aiśvarya* (sovereignty), *śakti* (potency), *bala* (strength), *vīrya* (virility/immutability), *tejas* (splendour). Each presides over a cosmic function, a direction, a Veda, and (in later mapping) a tattva of the evolving cosmos. (*Ahirbudhnya Saṃhitā*; *Lakṣmī Tantra*; *Sātvata Saṃhitā*)

- **Vāsudeva (वासुदेव)** - The first and supreme Vyūha, identical in essence with Para; possesses all six guṇas in fullness, foregrounding **jñāna + bala**. Source of the other three. Associated with the soul/*cit*, the East, white colour, and (iconographically) holding all four āyudhas. Equated with **Kṛṣṇa** as the indwelling deity. (*Pāñcarātra*)
- **Saṅkarṣaṇa (सङ्कर्षण)** - Second emanation from Vāsudeva; foregrounds **jñāna + bala**, governs the **dissolution (saṃhāra)** of the cosmos and is the lord of the *jīva*-principle and the ego's withdrawal. Identified with **Balarāma** (Ananta-Śeṣa). Animal: lion; direction: South. Expounds dharma. (*Ahirbudhnya Saṃhitā*)
- **Pradyumna (प्रद्युम्न)** - Third emanation, from Saṅkarṣaṇa; foregrounds **aiśvarya + vīrya**, presides over **creation (sṛṣṭi)** and is the source of *manas* (mind) and *buddhi*. Identified with **Pradyumna, son of Kṛṣṇa** (himself a rebirth of Kāma). Animal: makara/deer; direction: West. (*Lakṣmī Tantra*)
- **Aniruddha (अनिरुद्ध)** - Fourth emanation, from Pradyumna; foregrounds **śakti + tejas**, presides over **sustenance/protection (sthiti)** and the manifest world, source of *ahaṃkāra* (ego) and the elements. Identified with **Aniruddha, grandson of Kṛṣṇa**. Animal: boar; direction: North; the Vyūha closest to the material world and to the *vibhava* avatāras. (*Ahirbudhnya Saṃhitā*)

  - *Variant (guṇa-pairing):* sources differ on which two guṇas each Vyūha foregrounds; a common scheme assigns Vāsudeva = *jñāna+bala*, Saṅkarṣaṇa = *jñāna+bala* (withdrawal), Pradyumna = *aiśvarya+vīrya*, Aniruddha = *śakti+tejas*. The principle (each = 2 of 6) is stable; the exact pairing varies by Saṃhitā. [disputed]
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata identification):* the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (and Gauḍīya theology) reads the four as the **Vṛṣṇi heroes** — Vāsudeva (Kṛṣṇa), Saṅkarṣaṇa (Balarāma), Pradyumna (his son), Aniruddha (his grandson) — historicizing the Vyūhas into the Yādava clan worshipped at Mathurā. The **Heliodorus pillar** (c. 110 BCE) and Mora-well inscription attest early *vṛṣṇi-vīra* / *caturvyūha* worship. [historical]
  - *Sub-Vyūhas (Vyūhāntara):* each primary Vyūha emanates **three secondary forms** (12 total), which are precisely the first 12 of the Keśava mūrtis below — e.g. from Vāsudeva: Keśava, Nārāyaṇa, Mādhava; from Saṅkarṣaṇa: Govinda, Viṣṇu, Madhusūdana; from Pradyumna: Trivikrama, Vāmana, Śrīdhara; from Aniruddha: Hṛṣīkeśa, Padmanābha, Dāmodara. (*Pāñcarātra*; *Padma Purāṇa*)

## B. The 24 Keśava Mūrtis (*Caturviṃśati-mūrti*)

The twenty-four-fold expansion (3 from each Vyūha = 12, doubled in some schemes, or the canonical 24 of the *ācamana*) — distinguished iconographically **only** by the *order in which the four āyudhas* (**śaṅkha** conch, **cakra** discus, **gadā** mace, **padma** lotus) are held in the four hands. These are the **24 names of the daily *ācamana*** (sipping-purification) and the *sandhyāvandana*, recited so that the body is sanctified limb by limb. The standard sequence (*Padma Purāṇa*, *Rūpamaṇḍana*, Vaiṣṇava Āgamas):

- **Keśava (केशव)** - "Lord of fine hair" / slayer of demon Keśin; first and head of the list, presides over the month Mārgaśīrṣa. Hands (one common scheme): padma–śaṅkha–cakra–gadā.
- **Nārāyaṇa (नारायण)** - "He who dwells on / is the abode of the waters (nāra)"; the primal form reclining on the cosmic ocean. Month: Pauṣa.
- **Mādhava (माधव)** - "Consort of Mā (Lakṣmī)" / lord of spring (madhu); month: Māgha. (Also a name of Kṛṣṇa.)
- **Govinda (गोविन्द)** - "Finder/protector of cows / of the earth (go)"; the cowherd aspect; month: Phālguna.
- **Viṣṇu (विष्णु)** - "The all-pervader" (root *viṣ*); the integral name within the list; month: Caitra.
- **Madhusūdana (मधुसूदन)** - "Slayer of the demon Madhu" (with Kaiṭabha); month: Vaiśākha. See [Hayagrīva](#d-the-great-iconographic--composite-forms) below.
- **Trivikrama (त्रिविक्रम)** - "He of the three strides"; the cosmic-spanning form of the [Vāmana](dashavatara.md) descent; month: Jyeṣṭha.
- **Vāmana (वामन)** - "The dwarf"; the brahmin-boy aspect who begs three paces from Bali; month: Āṣāḍha. Cf. [Daśāvatāra §Vāmana](dashavatara.md).
- **Śrīdhara (श्रीधर)** - "Bearer of Śrī (Lakṣmī)" on his chest (the *śrīvatsa*); month: Śrāvaṇa.
- **Hṛṣīkeśa (हृषीकेश)** - "Lord of the senses (hṛṣīka)"; the inner controller of the indriyas; month: Bhādrapada.
- **Padmanābha (पद्मनाभ)** - "Lotus-naveled"; from whose navel-lotus Brahmā and the cosmos arise; month: Āśvina. (Form enshrined at Tiruvanantapuram.)
- **Dāmodara (दामोदर)** - "Bound at the belly by a rope (dāma-udara)"; the child Kṛṣṇa tied to the mortar by Yaśodā; month: Kārttika. Closes the **first twelve** (the *māsa*-presiding "12 Ādityas of the year" identification).
- **Saṅkarṣaṇa (सङ्कर्षण)** - The Vyūha-name re-entering the list as the 13th mūrti; the Balarāma-principle of withdrawal.
- **Vāsudeva (वासुदेव)** - The supreme Vyūha-name as the 14th mūrti; "son of Vasudeva / indweller of all."
- **Pradyumna (प्रद्युम्न)** - 15th; the creative Vyūha re-listed.
- **Aniruddha (अनिरुद्ध)** - 16th; the sustaining Vyūha re-listed. (Mūrtis 13–16 = the four Vyūhas themselves entering the 24-fold.)
- **Puruṣottama (पुरुषोत्तम)** - "The supreme person" of *Gītā* 15.16–18, transcending kṣara and akṣara; enshrined as Jagannātha at Purī.
- **Adhokṣaja (अधोक्षज)** - "He beyond sense-perception" / "born below the axle"; the unfathomable aspect.
- **Narasiṃha (नरसिंह)** - "Man-lion"; the fierce protector aspect, here a *mūrti* of the list (distinct from but echoing the [Narasiṃha avatāra](dashavatara.md)).
- **Acyuta (अच्युत)** - "The infallible / unfallen"; he who never deviates from his nature.
- **Janārdana (जनार्दन)** - "He who agitates / is implored by people"; refuge of the world.
- **Upendra (उपेन्द्र)** - "The younger Indra" / Indra's benefactor; the Vāmana-born brother of Indra (Aditi's son).
- **Hari (हरि)** - "The tawny one / remover (of sins and saṃsāra)"; the integral Vaiṣṇava name.
- **Kṛṣṇa (कृष्ण)** - "The dark / all-attractive"; the 24th and culminating mūrti, the *pūrṇa* form. Closes the list.

  - *Variant (order & membership):* the sequence above follows the common South-Indian *ācamana* order (Keśava…Kṛṣṇa). The *Padma Purāṇa* and various Āgamas reorder mūrtis 13–24 and some lists substitute **Hayagrīva**, **Vyāsa** or **Hṛṣīkeśa** placements; the *first twelve* (Keśava→Dāmodara) are everywhere stable as the **12 month-/Āditya-presiding** forms. [disputed — order varies]
  - *Variant (āyudha permutation):* each mūrti is defined by a distinct arrangement of conch-discus-mace-lotus across the 4 hands. With 4 objects in 4 hands there are 24 permutations — exactly matching the 24 mūrtis, a deliberate iconographic mnemonic. Sculptural manuals (*Rūpamaṇḍana*, *Aparājitapṛcchā*) give the canonical assignment; minor regional variation exists. (*Śilpa* texts)
  - *Variant (number):* a longer list of **39 mūrtis** appears in some Saṃhitās (the 24 plus 15 further names); and the *ācamana* itself sometimes recites only the **first 12** plus *praṇava*. [late text]

## C. Nārāyaṇa on Śeṣa & the resting/cosmic postures

- **Śeṣaśāyī / Anantaśayana Nārāyaṇa** - Viṣṇu reclining (*śayana*) in *yoga-nidrā* upon the thousand-hooded serpent **Ananta-Śeṣa** floating on the [Kṣīra-sāgara](../06-sacred-geography/README.md) (Ocean of Milk), with **Lakṣmī** at his feet and **Brahmā** on the navel-lotus — the **Para/resting** form between cosmic cycles. The icon at Śrīraṅgam (Raṅganātha) and Tiruvanantapuram (Padmanābha). (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.8)
- **Padmanābha (creation posture)** - From the reclining Nārāyaṇa's navel springs the lotus bearing **Brahmā**, who begins secondary creation — the cosmogonic frame of every *kalpa*. See [Brahmā's life](../README.md). (*Bhāgavata* 3.8)
- **Yoga-Nārāyaṇa / Sthānaka–Āsana–Śayana** - Āgamic threefold posture taxonomy of any Viṣṇu icon: **standing (sthānaka)**, **seated (āsana)**, **reclining (śayana)** — each further classed as *yoga* (meditative), *bhoga* (enjoying), *vīra* (heroic), *abhicārika* (fierce/sorcerous). (*Vaikhānasa Āgama*; *Śilparatna*)

## D. The great iconographic & composite forms

Aspect-forms not in the standard 24/Daśāvatāra lists but central to Vaiṣṇava worship and myth:

- **Hayagrīva (हयग्रीव)** - The **horse-headed** form, brilliant white, who recovers the **Vedas** stolen by the demons **Madhu and Kaiṭabha** from Brahmā (or slays the asura Hayagrīva who stole them); worshipped as the **god of knowledge and learning**, esp. by Vaikhānasas, Śrī Vaiṣṇavas and [Mādhvas](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md) (Vedānta Deśika's *Hayagrīva Stotra*). Listed among the 24 avatāras in *Bhāgavata* 10.40. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa*; *Hayagrīva Upaniṣad*; *Devī Bhāgavata*)
  - *Variant:* in the *Devī Bhāgavata* the slain demon is also named **Hayagrīva** — Viṣṇu's own head is severed and replaced with a horse's by the Goddess's design, then he slays his demonic namesake. [disputed — myth doubling]
- **Mohinī (मोहिनी)** - The **only female form** of Viṣṇu; the enchantress who, after the **[Samudra-manthana](dashavatara.md)**, beguiles the asuras and distributes the **amṛta** to the devas alone. Holds conch + discus (upper hands) and the nectar-pot (lower). Later beguiles **Śiva** (begetting **Ayyappa / Hari-Hara-putra**) and the demon **Bhasmāsura**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.8–9, 8.12; *Mahābhārata*, Ādi Parva)
  - *Variant:* the *Devī Bhāgavata* tradition disputes Mohinī's avatāra-status, subordinating the enchantment to the Goddess. [disputed]
- **Dhanvantari (धन्वन्तरि)** - The **divine physician**, who rises from the churned ocean bearing the **pot of amṛta**, the conch, the *Sudarśana-cakra* and a medicinal leech; founder/patron of **Āyurveda**, worshipped on Dhanteras. Counted as the 12th of the *Bhāgavata*'s 22 avatāras. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.3, 8.8; *Garuḍa Purāṇa*)
- **Viśvarūpa (विश्वरूप)** - The **cosmic all-form** (*viśva-rūpa-darśana*) revealed by **Kṛṣṇa** to **Arjuna** in **[Bhagavad-Gītā ch. 11](../90-literature-corpus/README.md)** — infinite mouths, eyes and arms containing all worlds, devas, and the warring armies being devoured by Time. The supreme theophany; also shown to Yaśodā, Brahmā and the Kaurava court. (*Bhagavad Gītā* 11; *Mahābhārata*, Udyoga Parva — the embassy theophany; *Bhāgavata* 10.7)
- **Vaikuṇṭha-Caturmūrti (वैकुण्ठ चतुर्मूर्ति)** - The **four-faced, eight-armed** icon (esp. of **Kashmir**, c. 8th–10th c.): central **human** face (East), **lion/Narasiṃha** (South), **boar/Varāha** (North), and fierce **demonic/Kapila** face (West, rear). Read either as the avatāra-faces (Nara–Narasiṃha–Varāha–Kapila) or as the **four Vyūhas** (Vāsudeva–Saṅkarṣaṇa–Pradyumna–Aniruddha) — uniting §A and the avatāras in one image. (*Viṣṇudharmottara Purāṇa*; Pāñcarātra)
  - *Variant:* the simpler **three-faced Vaikuṇṭha** (man–lion–boar, omitting the rear demonic face) is the common sculptural type; the fierce fourth face is described in text but rarely carved. [iconographic]
- **Hari-Hara (हरिहर)** - The **composite half-Viṣṇu / half-Śiva** icon — Viṣṇu's right half (cakra, yellow, *kirīṭa* crown) fused with Śiva's left (triśūla, matted *jaṭā*, half-third-eye) — asserting the **non-difference of the two great gods** (a Smārta / harmonizing theology). Also called **Śaṅkara-Nārāyaṇa**. Their son is **Ayyappa / Śāstā** (via Mohinī). See [Trimūrti](trimurti-and-tridevi.md) and [Śiva forms](shiva-avatars-and-forms.md). (*Skanda Purāṇa*; *Vāmana Purāṇa*; iconography of Hālebīḍu, Lepakshi)
- **Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa (लक्ष्मीनारायण)** - The conjugal form, Viṣṇu with **Śrī-Lakṣmī** on his lap or left — the *Para* couple of Vaikuṇṭha; theological core of Śrī Vaiṣṇavism (Śrī as eternal mediatrix, *puruṣakāra*). See [Tridevī](trimurti-and-tridevi.md). (*Lakṣmī Tantra*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*)

## E. Other named expansions & related forms

- **The 39 Vibhavas** - The *Ahirbudhnya Saṃhitā*'s extended descent-list (Padmanābha, Dhruva, Ananta, Śaktyātman, Madhusūdana, Vidyādhideva, Kapila, Viśvarūpa, Vihaṅgama, Krodātman, Vaḍavāvaktra, Dharma, Vāgīśvara, Ekārṇavaśāyin, Kamaṭheśvara/Kūrma, Varāha, Narasiṃha, Amṛtaharaṇa, Śrīpati, Kāntātman, Rāhujit, Kālanemighna, Pārijātahara, Lokanātha, Śāntātman, Dattātreya, Nyagrodhaśāyin, Ekaśṛṅgatanu/Matsya, Vāmanadeha, Trivikrama, Nara, Nārāyaṇa, Hari, Kṛṣṇa, Rāma×3 [Paraśu-, Rāma-Rāghava, Bala-Rāma], Vedavid/Kalki, Pātālaśayana). Many overlap the [Daśāvatāra](dashavatara.md). [open-ended roster; numbering varies] (*Ahirbudhnya Saṃhitā* ch. 5)
- **Antaryāmin / Pāramātmika forms** - In *yoga* and Pāñcarātra, the indwelling Viṣṇu measured "a thumb's-length" (*aṅguṣṭha-mātra*) in the heart-lotus; the **Kṣīrodaśāyī Viṣṇu** (Milk-ocean indweller of each universe) and **Garbhodakaśāyī** and **Kāraṇodakaśāyī** (Mahā-Viṣṇu of the *Kāraṇa* ocean) are the three *puruṣa-avatāras* of Gauḍīya theology — cosmic levels of the indwelling supersoul. (*Bhāgavata*; Gauḍīya *Sandarbhas*)
- **Garuḍa-vāhana & Lakṣmī-Hayagrīva** etc. — vehicle- and consort-paired processional forms multiply in the **Arcā** category at each temple. [open-ended]

> **Open-ended flag:** only the **24 Keśava mūrtis** (and the 4 Vyūhas) form a *closed* canonical roster. The Vibhava list (39 / innumerable) and the Arcā forms (one or more per temple) are by doctrine **unbounded**, "like rivulets from an inexhaustible lake" (*Bhāgavata* 1.3.26).
