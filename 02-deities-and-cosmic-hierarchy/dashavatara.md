# Daśāvatāra — The Ten Avatars of Vishnu

> Node in `02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`. The Daśāvatāra is the canonical descent-sequence of Vishnu (the Preserver) across the four yugas of the **current 28th Mahayuga** of the [7th Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/). It is the spine that links the cosmic clock to the recorded event-files: each avatar manifests when *dharma* declines, fulfilling the promise of *Bhagavad Gita* 4.7–8 — "Whenever dharma decays, I manifest myself." This file maps each avatar to its yuga and catalogs the major **variant lists** (the 22/24-avatar Bhagavata schema; Buddha vs Balarama vs Krishna as the 9th).

---

## The core doctrine (avatāra-vāda)

- **Avatāra** = "descent" (Skt. *ava-tṝ*, "to cross down"). Vishnu descends into the created order to restore *dharma*, protect the *sādhu* (righteous), and destroy the *duṣkṛt* (evildoers). (*Bhagavad Gita* 4.7–8; *Bhagavata Purana* 1.3)
- **"Innumerable" descents:** the *Bhagavata Purana* (1.3.26) stresses *avatārā hy asaṅkhyeyāḥ* — "the incarnations of Hari are innumerable, like rivulets from an inexhaustible lake." The fixed lists (10 / 22 / 24) are therefore selections of the *prādhānika* (principal) avatars, not an exhaustive census.
- **Classification:** later theology (esp. Pāñcarātra / Sri Vaishnava) subdivides descents into *pūrṇa* (full, e.g. Krishna/Rama), *aṃśa* (partial), *āveśa* (empowerment, e.g. Parashurama, Buddha in some readings), and *guṇa/yuga/manvantara/līlā* avatars. [theological systematization, post-Puranic]
- **Yuga mapping (standard frame):** first 4 in [Satya Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/01-satya-yuga.md); next 3 in [Treta Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md); 8th–9th in [Dvapara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md); 10th (Kalki) at the close of [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md). The sequence is often read as an evolutionary arc: aquatic → amphibian → mammal → man-beast → dwarf-man → full man — a parallel noted since the 19th c. [modern interpretive gloss]

## SATYA YUGA — avatars 1–4

### 1. Matsya (मत्स्य) — the Fish
- **The cosmic deluge & rescue of the Vedas.** Vishnu as a great fish warns **Manu** (Vaivasvata / Satyavrata) of the coming *pralaya* (flood), tells him to build a boat and load the seeds of all life and the seven sages (*saptarṣi*); Matsya tows the boat tied to his horn through the deluge to safety. Marks the start of our Manvantara. (*Shatapatha Brahmana* 1.8.1 — earliest version, with Manu but no demon; *Matsya Purana*; *Bhagavata Purana* 8.24)
  - *Variant (Matsya & Bhagavata Puranas):* Matsya additionally slays the demon **Hayagriva** (or "Shankhasura"), who had stolen the Vedas from a sleeping Brahma, and restores the scriptures. The boat is tied to Matsya's horn with the serpent **Vasuki/Shesha** as rope.
  - *Variant (Mahabharata, Vana Parva):* the flood hero is **Manu** explicitly; the fish grows monstrously large, demanding ever-bigger vessels — no Vedas-theft subplot.
  - *Variant (early Brahmana layer):* Matsya is a form of **Prajapati / Brahma**, not yet identified with Vishnu — the Vishnu-attribution is the later Puranic overlay. [text-historical note]
- *See* [event file in satya/](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/01-satya-yuga.md).

### 2. Kurma (कूर्म) — the Tortoise
- **Samudra Manthana (churning of the ocean of milk).** Devas and asuras churn the cosmic ocean for *amṛta* (nectar of immortality), using Mount Mandara as churning-rod and the serpent Vasuki as rope. The mountain begins to sink; Vishnu becomes a giant tortoise and supports it on his back as a pivot. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.7–8; *Vishnu Purana* 1.9; *Mahabharata*, Adi Parva)
  - Linked sub-emanations during the churning: **Dhanvantari** (physician-god bearing the nectar pot) and **Mohinī** (Vishnu's enchantress form, who tricks the asuras out of the nectar) — both counted as separate avatars in the 22/24 lists below.
  - *Variant (early layer):* in the *Shatapatha Brahmana* the tortoise is **Prajapati**, again pre-Vishnu attribution. [text-historical note]

### 3. Varaha (वराह) — the Boar
- **Lifting the Earth from the cosmic ocean.** The demon **Hiranyaksha** drags the earth-goddess **Bhūdevī (Prithvi)** down into the cosmic waters (Rasātala / Pātāla). Vishnu takes a colossal boar form, dives in, slays Hiranyaksha (after a 1,000-year duel), and raises the earth on his tusks. (*Bhagavata Purana* 3.13, 3.18–19; *Vishnu Purana* 1.4; *Varaha Purana*)
  - *Variant (Shatapatha Brahmana / early):* the boar **Emūṣa** raises the earth as a cosmogonic act, identified with Prajapati — no Hiranyaksha demon. The demon-slaying narrative is the Puranic development. [text-historical note]
  - *Variant (Vaishnava theology):* "Yajña-Varaha" — the boar's body parts are allegorized as components of the Vedic sacrifice (*yajña*). (*Bhagavata Purana* 3.13.35ff)
  - **Earliest epigraphic Dashavatara:** the 7th-c. Ādivarāha cave at Mahabalipuram centers this form. [historical art-dating]

### 4. Narasimha (नरसिंह) — the Man-Lion
- **Slaying Hiranyakashipu & saving Prahlada.** The asura king **Hiranyakashipu** (brother of Hiranyaksha) wins a boon making him un-killable by man or beast, indoors or out, day or night, on ground or in air, by no weapon. He persecutes his devotee-son **Prahlada**. Vishnu bursts from a pillar as half-man-half-lion (neither man nor beast), at twilight (neither day nor night), on the threshold (neither in nor out), on his lap (neither ground nor air), and disembowels the demon with his claws (no weapon). (*Bhagavata Purana* 7.2–10; *Vishnu Purana* 1.16–20)
  - *Variant (Shaiva texts):* the fierce, uncontrollable post-kill Narasimha is subdued only by **Shiva** as **Sharabha** (a part-bird, part-lion beast) — a sectarian counter-narrative asserting Shiva's supremacy. (*Linga Purana*, *Sharabha Upanishad*) [Shaiva variant — contested by Vaishnavas]
  - *Variant (regional, Andhra/Telangana):* expanded cycle of nine Narasimha forms (*Nava-Narasimha*) and the consort **Chenchu Lakshmi** marriage legend. [folk/regional tradition]

## TRETA YUGA — avatars 5–7

### 5. Vamana (वामन) — the Dwarf
- **Trivikrama: the three world-spanning strides.** The benevolent but over-mighty asura king **Bali** (Prahlada's grandson) conquers the three worlds. Vishnu appears as a dwarf brahmin boy and begs Bali for only "three paces of land." Bali grants it; Vamana swells to cosmic size (**Trivikrama**) and covers earth with one stride, heaven with the second; for the third he places his foot on Bali's head, pushing him to the netherworld (Sutala/Pātāla). (*Rig Veda* 1.154 — the three strides of Vishnu, the seed myth; *Bhagavata Purana* 8.15–23; *Vishnu Purana*)
  - Bali is granted lordship of Pātāla and a boon of annual return — basis of the **Onam** festival (Kerala). [living regional tradition]
  - *Variant (Rig Vedic):* the *tri-vikrama* is purely cosmogonic (Vishnu measuring the cosmos) with **no dwarf and no Bali** — the dwarf-deception is the later Puranic narrative frame. [text-historical note]

### 6. Parashurama (परशुराम) — Rama-with-the-Axe
- **Extermination of the kshatriyas, 21 times.** The brahmin-warrior, son of sage **Jamadagni**, receives an axe (*paraśu*) from Shiva. When King **Kartavirya Arjuna** (Sahasrarjuna) steals Jamadagni's wish-cow Kamadhenu and kills the sage, Parashurama annihilates the tyrant and then the entire kshatriya class twenty-one times over, filling five lakes with their blood at Samanta-panchaka. (*Mahabharata*, Vana Parva; *Bhagavata Purana* 9.15–16; *Vishnu Purana*)
  - Considered a **chiranjivi** (immortal); reappears across yugas — clashes with Rama (breaking Shiva's bow), and teaches Bhishma, Drona, Karna in the Dvapara age. He is an **āveśa-avatāra** (empowered descent) in classification, hence he coexists with later avatars.
  - *Regional tradition:* reclaims **Konkan/Kerala** coast (Parashurama Kshetra) by hurling his axe to make the sea recede. [folk/regional]

### 7. Rama (राम) — the Ideal King (Maryada Purushottama)
- **The Ramayana.** Prince of Ayodhya (Ikshvaku/Solar dynasty), exiled 14 years; his wife **Sita** is abducted by the rakshasa king **Ravana** of Lanka; allied with **Hanuman** and the vanara army, Rama builds a bridge (Rama Setu) to Lanka, slays Ravana, and recovers Sita. Embodies *dharma* and ideal kingship. (*Valmiki Ramayana*; *Bhagavata Purana* 9.10–11; *Adhyatma Ramayana*; *Ramcharitmanas* — Tulsidas, devotional vernacular)
  - *Variant traditions of the Ramayana:* Jain (Paumacariya — Rama as non-violent, Lakshmana kills Ravana), Buddhist (Dasaratha Jataka), and SE Asian (Ramakien, Reamker) retellings differ on key episodes. [cross-tradition variants]
  - In the **22/24-avatar list**, Rama is counted and **Balarama + Krishna** are *also* counted separately (see below).
- *See* [Treta event files](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md).

## DVAPARA YUGA — avatar 8 (and 9, by variant)

### 8. Krishna (कृष्ण) — the Purna Avatar / Svayam Bhagavan
- **The Mahabharata & Bhagavad Gita.** Born in the Yadava/Vrishni clan to Devaki & Vasudeva; slays his tyrant uncle **Kamsa**; lifts **Govardhana** hill; the **Rasa-lila** with the gopis of Vrindavan; charioteer and counselor to **Arjuna** at Kurukshetra, where he delivers the **Bhagavad Gita**. His departure (3102 BCE) marks the **start of Kali Yuga**. (*Mahabharata*; *Bhagavata Purana* 10–11; *Harivamsa*; *Vishnu Purana* 5)
  - **Theological status:** in Gaudiya Vaishnavism, Krishna is **Svayam Bhagavan** — not an avatar *of* Vishnu but the very *source* of all avatars (*kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam*, *Bhagavata* 1.3.28). In most other lists he is the 8th avatar.
  - *See* [Dvapara event files](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md).

### 9. Balarama (बलराम) — Krishna's elder brother *(in many Vaishnava lists)*
- **Avatar of Shesha / the plow-bearer.** Elder brother of Krishna, wielder of plow (*hala*) and mace; regarded as the incarnation of the cosmic serpent **Shesha (Ananta)**. Slays the asuras **Dhenuka** and **Pralamba**; drags the **Yamuna** river with his plow. (*Bhagavata Purana* 10; *Vishnu Purana*)
  - Counted as the **9th avatar** in: Smarta, Sri Vaishnava, and Shaiva-Siddhanta lists (with Krishna at 8); and as **8th** with Krishna at 9 in some North-Indian Bhagavata orderings.

> **THE 9TH-AVATAR QUESTION — variant lists side by side:**
>
> | Tradition / source | 8th | 9th |
> |---|---|---|
> | North-Indian Bhagavatism, ISKCON, Sadh Vaishnava | **Krishna** | **Buddha** |
> | Smarta, Sri Vaishnava, Shaiva Siddhanta | **Balarama** | **Krishna** |
> | Gaudiya Vaishnavism | **Balarama** | **Buddha** |
> | Ādivarāha inscription, Mahabalipuram (7th c.) | **Balarama** | **Buddha** *(Krishna omitted)* |
> | Govardhan Math (Odisha) | **Balarama** | **Jagannātha** |
> | Warkari sampradaya (Maharashtra/Goa) | **Krishna** | **Vithoba** |
>
> Sources naming the **Krishna–Buddha** ordering: *Agni Purana* 49; *Garuda Purana* 1.86.10–11; *Linga Purana* 2.48.31–32; *Naradiya Purana* 4.119.14–19; *Padma Purana* 7.66, 9.229; *Varaha Purana* 4.2–3, 48.17–22; *Skanda Purana*, Reva Khanda 151.

### 9 (alt). Buddha (बुद्ध) — *(in the Krishna-as-8th lists; appears in Kali Yuga, not Dvapara)*
- **The deluder / the compassionate one.** Identified with Gautama Buddha (or a distinct "Buddha"); born in the **Kīkaṭa** (Magadha) country to **Añjana** (per *Bhagavata*). Two opposed readings of his *purpose*:
  - *Reading A (anti-Vedic / "deluder"):* Vishnu takes ascetic form to **delude the asuras** away from the Vedic path so the devas can defeat them; he leads "those envious of theists" astray. (*Bhagavata Purana* 1.3.24; *Agni Purana*; *Vishnu Purana* 3.17–18, the "Mayamoha" delusion narrative)
  - *Reading B (compassion / ahimsa):* Vishnu incarnates to **stop Vedic animal sacrifice** out of compassion for all beings. (Jayadeva's *Gita Govinda*, Dashavatara-stotra, 12th c. — "nindasi yajña-vidher… sadaya-hṛdaya darśita-paśu-ghātam"). [devotional reinterpretation]
  - *Note:* in the 22-avatar Bhagavata list, **Buddha is the 21st** (a Kali-Yuga descent), not the 9th (he becomes the 23rd in the extended 24-avatar list). Historical inclusion: by the 8th c. CE Buddha appears as an avatar across several Puranas. [historical text-dating]
  - *See* [Kali Yuga node](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).

## KALI YUGA — avatar 10 (future)

### 10. Kalki (कल्कि) — the Final Destroyer-Restorer
- **The apocalyptic horseman.** At the close of [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) (≈ 426,873 years from now, per the 432,000-yr Kali span), Vishnu will be born as **Kalki**, son of **Vishnuyaśas**, in the village of **Shambhala**. Riding the white horse **Devadatta** and wielding a blazing sword, he destroys the wicked, ends the age of vice, and inaugurates the next **Satya Yuga**, restoring dharma to all four legs. (*Bhagavata Purana* 1.3.25, 12.2; *Kalki Purana*; *Vishnu Purana* 4.24; *Agni Purana*)
  - His preceptor is **Parashurama** (the 6th avatar, a chiranjivi); he marries **Padmavati**; defeats the demon **Kali** (personification of the age). (*Kalki Purana*)
  - *Variant timing (Sri Yukteswar school):* in the *Holy Science* (1894) 24,000-yr cycle, Kali Yuga ended ~1700 CE and we are in ascending Dvapara — recasting Kalki's "arrival" entirely. [non-canonical / modern variant; see [framework constants](../00-time-and-cosmology/)]

## VARIANT LISTS — beyond the ten

### The 22-Avatar list (*Bhagavata Purana* 1.3, lila-avatars)
In order as enumerated in *Bhagavata* Canto 1 Ch. 3: (1) the **Four Kumaras**, (2) **Varaha**, (3) **Narada**, (4) **Nara-Narayana**, (5) **Kapila**, (6) **Dattatreya**, (7) **Yajña**, (8) **Rishabha**, (9) **Prithu**, (10) **Matsya**, (11) **Kurma**, (12) **Dhanvantari**, (13) **Mohini**, (14) **Narasimha**, (15) **Vamana**, (16) **Parashurama**, (17) **Vyasa**, (18) **Rama**, (19) **Balarama**, (20) **Krishna**, (21) **Buddha**, (22) **Kalki**. (*Bhagavata Purana* 1.3.6–25)

### The 24-Avatar list
The same enumeration is commonly extended to **24** by adding **Hamsa** (the swan, expounder of knowledge) and **Hayagriva** (the horse-headed restorer of the Vedas), and/or by counting the four Kumaras and Nara-Narayana as individuals. Buddha is then the 23rd. (*Bhagavata Purana* traditional commentary; *Panchratra*)

### Other counts
- **39 avatars** are listed in the *Pañcarātra* / Sri Vaishnava enumerations (adding forms like Adi-Murti, Lakshmi-Narasimha, etc.). [theological]
- The doctrine's bottom line remains *Bhagavata* 1.3.26: the descents are *innumerable*; fixed lists are pedagogical, not closed.
