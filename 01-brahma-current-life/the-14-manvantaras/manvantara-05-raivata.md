# Manvantara 5 — Raivata (Past)

> Hierarchy: Shveta-Varaha Kalpa (current Day of Brahma) → 5th of 14 Manvantaras → presided over by **Raivata Manu**. Long elapsed; follows [Tamasa (4th)](./manvantara-04-tamasa.md), precedes [Chakshusha (6th)](./manvantara-06-chakshusha.md) and the current [Vaivasvata (7th)](./manvantara-07-vaivasvata/). Up: [the 14 Manvantaras README](./README.md). Each Manvantara ≈ 306,720,000 human years (71 Mahayugas + 1 sandhya).

## Overview

The Raivata Manvantara is the third and last of the three eras (Tamasa, Raivata, Chakshusha) that the Puranas group as a near-set, often born of the same Priyavrata line. The Puranic record for this era is principally a list — Manu, Indra, deva-classes, Saptarishis, sons, avatar — with one celebrated narrative attached: the **time-dilation tale of Kakudmi/Raivata and his daughter Revati**, which (in the dominant reading) belongs to a *human-dynasty* Raivata rather than to Raivata Manu himself. That ambiguity, and the cross-manvantara anachronism it produces, is the most interesting feature of this era and is flagged below.

- **Raivata Manu** — fifth Manu, presiding sovereign of mankind for this era. Said to be the **brother of the 4th Manu, [Tamasa](./manvantara-04-tamasa.md)**, both reckoned of the Priyavrata line. (chronological placement: 5th manvantara of the present Kalpa, c. 4 manvantaras + ~1.2 billion human years into the Day of Brahma) (*Vishnu Purana* 3.1; *Bhagavata Purana* 8.5)
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana 5.1.28; Brahmanda Purana):* Raivata is named explicitly as a **son of Priyavrata** and brother of Tamasa. (per wisdomlib *Puranic Encyclopaedia*, "Raivata-manu")
  - *Variant (epithet):* the era and Manu are also called **Raivata** after the name-stem *Revata*; the homonymy with King Kakudmi-Raivata of Kushasthali (see below) is the root of the chronological confusion.

## Key Office-Holders

- **Indra of this Manvantara: Vibhu** — the Indra (king of the devas) for the Raivata era. (*Vishnu Purana* 3.1; *Bhagavata Purana* 8.5)
  - *Variant (older Puranic recensions):* the Indra is named **Vibhu / Vipashchita** ("the all-knowing"); some lists render it **Vibhus** (plural form). [variant spelling]
- **Classes of devas (gods):** four groups, each said to contain fourteen gods — **Amitabhas, Bhutarayas (Abhutarajas / Bhutarajas), Vaikunthas,** and **Sumedhas** (group names differ across recensions). (*Vishnu Purana* 3.1; *Bhagavata Purana* 8.5) [variant]
  - *Note:* the deva-class **Vaikunthas** shares its name with the avatar **Vaikuntha** of this same era (below) — the *Bhagavata* says the avatar was born "along with the great gods known as Vaikunthas," tying the deva-group and the incarnation together. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.5)
- **Saptarishis (seven sages):** **Hiranyaroma, Vedashri (Vedasiras), Urddhabahu, Vedabahu, Sudhaman (Sudama), Parjanya,** and **Mahamuni.** (*Vishnu Purana* 3.1; *Bhagavata Purana* 8.5)
  - *Variant (Bhagavata recension via wisdomlib):* list given as **Hiranyaroma, Vedasiras, Urdhvabahu, Devabahu, Sudhaman, Parjanya, Mahamuni** — substituting **Devabahu** for Vedabahu. [variant]
  - *Variant (Brahmanda Purana):* "**Devabahu** and others were the seven sages," who "served as calf to milk the earth" (the standard Puranic image of the sages aiding cosmic order). [variant]
  - *Variant (HinduPad / popular lists):* **Devabahu, Subahu, Parjanya, Somapa, Muni, Hiranyaroma,** and **Saptashva.** [variant] [folk]
- **Sons of Raivata Manu:** **Bali, Vindhya** and others, the eldest named **Arjuna** (not the Mahabharata Arjuna). (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.5; *Vishnu Purana*)
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana, Wilson):* sons given as **Balabandhu, Susambhavya, Satyaka** and other kings. [variant]
  - *Variant (HinduPad):* ten sons — Varuna, Tattvadarshi, Chitiman, Havyapa, Kavi, Mukta, Nirutsuka, Sattva, Vimoha, Prakashaka. [variant] [folk]

## Vishnu Avatar of the Era

- **Vaikuntha** — Vishnu's manifestation for the Raivata Manvantara. Per the *Bhagavata Purana*, **Lord Vaikuntha was born of his own accord, together with the great gods called Vaikunthas**, and — **at the request of his consort the goddess Rama (Lakshmi)** — he created the supreme realm known as **Vaikuntha** (Vishnu's eternal abode) to please her. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.5)
  - *Variant (secondary / popular):* some retellings name the mother as a goddess "Vikuntha" rather than identifying her with Lakshmi/Rama; the *Bhagavata* itself ties the boon-request to Rama. (secondary: Wikipedia "Manu") [late/secondary attribution]
  - *Note:* this is one of only a handful of avatars in the manvantara-cycle (alongside Vaivasvata-era avatars; cf. the avatar lists under [the 14 Manvantaras README](./README.md)) where the Purana actually narrates *why* the form appeared, rather than merely naming it.

## The Revati & Kakudmi Time-Dilation Tale

The single famous narrative connected to the "Raivata" name is the story of **King Kakudmi (also called Raivata)** and his daughter **Revati** — celebrated in modern writing as an ancient depiction of *relativistic time dilation.* In the Puranic genealogies this Kakudmi-Raivata is a king of the **Sharyati / Anarta line** of the *human dynasties*, not Raivata Manu the manvantara-sovereign; the shared name is what links the tale to this page. [disputed — see anachronism note]

- **Kakudmi seeks a husband for Revati** — Kakudmi, ruler of the city **Kushasthali** (later **Dvaraka**, in the Anarta region), believes no earthly suitor is worthy of his peerless daughter and travels with her to **Brahmaloka** (Brahma's realm) to ask Brahma directly. (chronological placement: at the *end* of the Vaivasvata-era genealogies, i.e. millions of years *after* Raivata Manu's own manvantara) (*Bhagavata Purana* 9.3.27–36; *Vishnu Purana* 4.1)
- **The wait during the Gandharva song** — they arrive while Brahma is absorbed in a musical performance by the Gandharvas **Hāhā and Hūhū**; Kakudmi waits politely for it to finish. (*Bhagavata Purana* 9.3)
- **Brahma's revelation of elapsed time** — when Kakudmi names the suitors he had in mind, Brahma laughs and explains that **twenty-seven chaturyugas (mahayugas) have passed on Earth** during the brief wait — roughly **116 million human years** — so the proposed bridegrooms, their sons, and entire dynasties are long dead. (*Bhagavata Purana* 9.3) [scholarly: cited as an early literary instance of time-dilation]
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 4.1):* the same episode is told more briefly; the elapsed span is given as a large but loosely specified number of yugas rather than a precise 27 chaturyugas. [variant]
- **Brahma's counsel — marry Revati to Balarama** — Brahma advises Kakudmi that the worthy husband now living is **Balarama** (Baladeva, elder brother of [Krishna](../../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md), an incarnation of Shesha), then ruling at the same Dvaraka that Kushasthali had become. (*Bhagavata Purana* 9.3)
- **The plough that shrank a bride** — Revati, born in an earlier yuga, is far **taller** than people of Balarama's age; Balarama taps her on the shoulder (or head) with the tip of his **plough (hala)**, his signature weapon, reducing her to the stature of his own era so the marriage can proceed. (*Bhagavata Purana* 9.3; *Vishnu Purana* 4.1)
  - *Variant (folk / regional):* some retellings have Balarama press her down to "normal" height by his strength rather than specifically the plough-tip. [folk] [variant]

### Anachronism note [disputed]

- **The cross-manvantara problem:** Revati's marriage to Balarama occurs in the **[Vaivasvata (7th) Manvantara](./manvantara-07-vaivasvata/)** — in the Dvapara Yuga at the very end of the present cycle's recorded human history — yet King Kakudmi shares the name **Raivata** with the Manu *two manvantaras earlier.* Read naively, this puts a daughter of the 5th-manvantara sovereign into the lap of Krishna's era. The Puranic genealogies resolve it: **Kakudmi-Raivata is a Sharyati-line king of the Vaivasvata manvantara**, and the *time-dilation* itself (27 chaturyugas in Brahmaloka) is precisely the device that telescopes his "earlier" generation forward into Balarama's age. The link to *this* page is therefore by name-homonymy and by the time-dilation theme, not by literal placement of the wedding in the Raivata Manvantara. [disputed] [scholarly]
- **Why it still belongs here:** the tale is the chief narrative carrying the "Raivata" name, and several popular sources file it under the Raivata Manvantara; cataloguing it here (with the anachronism flagged) is honest to both the texts and the confusion they generate.

## Notes & Status

- Apart from the Revati/Kakudmi episode (which strictly belongs to the human-dynasty record of the Vaivasvata era), this Manvantara is fully **past** and lightly documented: the Puranas give essentially the office-holder lists above (Manu, Indra, four deva-classes, Saptarishis, sons, avatar) with notable name-variants between the *Vishnu*, *Bhagavata*, and *Brahmanda* recensions. No independent epic-scale narrative is set *within* the Raivata Manvantara itself; the record here is genuinely sparse, and this page catalogs the variants rather than inventing events.
