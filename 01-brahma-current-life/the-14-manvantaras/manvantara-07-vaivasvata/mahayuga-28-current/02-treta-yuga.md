# Tretā Yuga — Events

> Hierarchy: Brahma's current life → Shveta-Varaha Kalpa → 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara → **28th Mahāyuga** → **Tretā Yuga** (the 2nd quarter, 1,296,000 human years, dharma on **3 legs**, 75% virtue). Sits between [Satya Yuga](./01-satya-yuga.md) and [Dvāpara Yuga](./03-dvapara-yuga.md). This is the yuga of **Rāma** and the **Rāmāyana** — the spine of the entire age.

Orientation note: Treta sees Vishnu incarnate three times within this Mahāyuga (Vāmana, Paraśurāma, Rāma — avatāras 5, 6, 7 of the Daśāvatāra). It is the great age of the [Sūryavaṃśa / Ikshvāku dynasty](../../../../03-lineages-vamsha/) of Ayodhyā and the apex of ritual sacrifice (yajña), which is itself the dominant dharma-mode of Treta.

---

## Vishnu Avatāras of Treta Yuga

- **Vāmana (the Dwarf) — 5th avatāra** — Vishnu born as a dwarf brāhmaṇa son of Kashyapa and Aditi, to subdue the asura-king **Bali** (grandson of Prahlāda), who had conquered the three worlds. At Bali's sacrifice Vāmana begs **three paces of land**; granted, he grows cosmic (Trivikrama), covers earth with one stride and heaven with the second, then sets his third foot on Bali's head, pushing him to **Pātāla** (the netherworld), which Bali is allowed to rule. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.15–23; Vishnu Purāṇa; Vāmana Purāṇa)
  - *Variant (yuga-placement):* Bhāgavata and most lists place Vāmana in **Treta**; some traditions place him at the Satya–Treta junction. Bali is promised he will become **Indra in the next (8th, Sāvarṇi) Manvantara** and is counted among the immortals (chiranjīvi). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa)
  - *Variant:* Vishnu grants Bali the boon that he (Vishnu) will guard his door in Pātāla; this is woven into Vaishnava devotional readings of Bali as a model bhakta. The Onam festival of Kerala commemorates Bali's annual return. [folk/regional tradition]

- **Paraśurāma (Rāma-with-the-axe) — 6th avatāra** — Vishnu born as the brāhmaṇa-warrior son of sage **Jamadagni** and **Renukā** (lineage of Bhṛgu). Receives a divine axe (paraśu) from Shiva. (Mahābhārata; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.15–16; Vishnu Purāṇa)
  - **Kārtavīrya Arjuna & Kāmadhenu** — the thousand-armed Haihaya king Kārtavīrya Arjuna steals/seizes the wish-cow **Kāmadhenu (Surabhi)** / her calf from Jamadagni's hermitage; Paraśurāma slays him. In revenge Kārtavīrya's sons kill the unarmed Jamadagni. (Mahābhārata; Bhāgavata Purāṇa)
  - **Twenty-one-fold kṣatriya annihilation** — Enraged at his father's murder, Paraśurāma vows to rid the earth of the kṣatriya warrior-class **twenty-one times** (the surviving widows each time bore new offspring, hence the repetition); said to have filled five lakes at **Samanta-pañchaka (Kurukshetra)** with their blood, later offering oblations to his ancestors there. (Mahābhārata, Vana/Shanti Parvan; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.16)
  - **Renukā's beheading** — at Jamadagni's command Paraśurāma beheads his own mother (for a momentary lapse of mind), then asks a boon to restore her to life. (Mahābhārata; Bhāgavata Purāṇa)
  - **Bridge to later yugas** — Paraśurāma is a chiranjīvi (immortal); he appears again in the Rāmāyana (confronting Rāma after the bow-breaking) and in the Mahābhārata (guru to Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Karṇa) — explicitly linking Treta to [Dvāpara](./03-dvapara-yuga.md). (Mahābhārata; Rāmāyana, Bāla Kāṇḍa)
  - *Variant (regional/folk):* Paraśurāma reclaims **Konkan / Kerala (Paraśurāma-kshetra)** from the sea by hurling his axe, creating the western coast — a land-creation legend not in the core epics. [folk tradition]

- **Rāma (Rāmacandra) — 7th avatāra** — the central event-chain of the entire yuga; see the dedicated section below.

## RĀMA / THE RĀMĀYANA — full event chain

> Frame text = **Vālmīki Rāmāyana** (7 Kāṇḍas; oldest extant, ~5th–4th c. BCE in core). Major retellings catalogued at the end. Rāma is the prince of **Ayodhyā**, of the [Ikshvāku/Sūryavaṃśa line](../../../../03-lineages-vamsha/).

### Bāla Kāṇḍa — birth & boyhood
- **Putra-kāmeṣṭi yajña & divine birth** — King **Daśaratha** of Ayodhyā, childless by his three queens (Kausalyā, Kaikeyī, Sumitrā), performs a son-granting sacrifice (with sage **Ṛṣyaśṛṅga**); pāyasa (sacred porridge) from the fire yields four sons: **Rāma** (Kausalyā), **Bharata** (Kaikeyī), **Lakṣmaṇa & Śatrughna** (Sumitrā). (Vālmīki, Bāla Kāṇḍa)
- **Viśvāmitra's request & demon-slaying** — sage **Viśvāmitra** takes Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa to guard his sacrifice; Rāma kills the rākṣasī **Tāṭakā**, receives celestial weapons (astras), and routs **Mārīca** (flung far by an arrow) and slays **Subāhu**. (Vālmīki, Bāla Kāṇḍa)
- **Ahalyā's liberation** — Rāma's foot/presence restores **Ahalyā** (wife of Gautama, cursed to stone for Indra's deception) to human form. (Vālmīki, Bāla Kāṇḍa)
- **Sītā svayaṃvara & breaking Shiva's bow** — at **Mithilā**, King **Janaka** offers his daughter **Sītā** (found in a furrow, ayonijā — not womb-born) to whoever can string **Shiva's bow (Pināka)**; Rāma lifts and breaks it; marries Sītā. The other three brothers marry Janaka's/Kushadhvaja's daughters (Ūrmilā, Māṇḍavī, Śrutakīrti). (Vālmīki, Bāla Kāṇḍa)
- **Paraśurāma confrontation** — on the journey home Paraśurāma challenges Rāma over the broken bow; Rāma effortlessly strings Paraśurāma's Vishnu-bow, draining his power — the 6th avatāra cedes to the 7th. (Vālmīki, Bāla Kāṇḍa)

### Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa — the exile
- **Kaikeyī's two boons & the exile decree** — on the eve of Rāma's coronation, the maid **Mantharā** poisons Kaikeyī's mind; Kaikeyī invokes two old boons from Daśaratha: **Bharata** to be crowned, **Rāma** exiled **14 years** to the forest. (Vālmīki, Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa)
- **The forest departure** — Rāma accepts without protest; **Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa** insist on accompanying him. Daśaratha dies of grief (recalling the curse of Śravaṇa-kumāra's blind parents). (Vālmīki, Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa)
- **Bharata's refusal & the sandals** — Bharata, away at his uncle's, returns, refuses the throne, pursues Rāma to **Chitrakūṭa**, and rules as regent in Rāma's name with Rāma's **pāḍukā (sandals)** on the throne at Nandigrāma. (Vālmīki, Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa)

### Aranya Kāṇḍa — the forest & abduction
- **Daṇḍaka forest, sages, demon-slaying** — Rāma clears the forest of rākṣasas; encounters **Virādha**, **Agastya** (gifts of bow, quiver, sword), and **Jaṭāyu** (the vulture-king, Daśaratha's friend). (Vālmīki, Aranya Kāṇḍa)
- **Śūrpaṇakhā mutilated** — Rāvaṇa's sister **Śūrpaṇakhā** lusts after Rāma/Lakṣmaṇa; spurned and attacking Sītā, Lakṣmaṇa cuts off her nose and ears. (Vālmīki, Aranya Kāṇḍa)
- **Khara & Dūṣaṇa's army destroyed** — Śūrpaṇakhā's brothers attack with 14,000 rākṣasas; Rāma annihilates them, prompting Śūrpaṇakhā to incite Rāvaṇa. (Vālmīki, Aranya Kāṇḍa)
- **The golden deer & abduction of Sītā** — **Mārīca**, disguised as a golden deer, lures Rāma away; mimicking Rāma's voice, he draws Lakṣmaṇa off too (the "Lakṣmaṇa-rekhā" appears in later retellings, not core Vālmīki); **Rāvaṇa**, disguised as an ascetic, abducts **Sītā** to **Lankā**. **Jaṭāyu** fights Rāvaṇa and is mortally wounded, telling Rāma the direction before dying. (Vālmīki, Aranya Kāṇḍa)
  - *Variant (Lakṣmaṇa-rekhā):* the protective line Lakṣmaṇa draws around Sītā is a **later/regional addition** (prominent in Rāmcaritmānas and folk versions), absent from critical Vālmīki. [late/regional]
  - *Variant (Māyā-Sītā / Chāyā-Sītā):* in **Adhyātma Rāmāyana**, **Kūrma Purāṇa**, and **Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa**, the real Sītā is hidden with Agni and an illusory "shadow Sītā" is abducted — preserving her purity and pre-explaining the later fire-ordeal. (Adhyātma Rāmāyana; Kūrma Purāṇa)

### Kishkindhā Kāṇḍa — the vānara alliance
- **Alliance with Sugrīva; Vālī slain** — Rāma meets **Hanumān** and the exiled vānara-prince **Sugrīva**; allies with him and kills Sugrīva's brother **Vālī** (Bālī) — controversially, with an arrow shot from concealment — restoring Sugrīva to the throne of **Kishkindhā**. (Vālmīki, Kishkindhā Kāṇḍa)
  - *Variant (the Vālī ethics debate):* the "unfair" killing of Vālī is a perennial moral crux; epics and commentaries (and Jain/Buddhist retellings) treat it variously. [disputed within tradition]
- **The search for Sītā** — vānara armies dispatched to the four directions; the southern party learns from **Sampāti** (Jaṭāyu's brother) that Sītā is in Lankā across the ocean. (Vālmīki, Kishkindhā Kāṇḍa)

### Sundara Kāṇḍa — Hanumān's leap
- **Hanumān crosses to Lankā** — leaps the ocean, finds Sītā in the **Aśoka-vāṭikā**, gives Rāma's ring, refuses to carry her back (so Rāma may win her honourably), burns Lankā with his tail after being captured, and returns with Sītā's crest-jewel (chūḍāmaṇi). (Vālmīki, Sundara Kāṇḍa) — the most independently-recited book.

### Yuddha Kāṇḍa — the war
- **Setu-bandha (the bridge)** — vānaras (led by **Nala**) build a causeway to Lankā over the ocean (which Rāma threatens with arrows until it yields). (Vālmīki, Yuddha Kāṇḍa)
- **Vibhīṣaṇa defects** — Rāvaṇa's righteous brother **Vibhīṣaṇa** joins Rāma after counselling Rāvaṇa to return Sītā. (Vālmīki, Yuddha Kāṇḍa)
- **The great battle & key deaths** — fall of **Kumbhakarṇa** (Rāvaṇa's giant brother), **Indrajit/Meghanāda** (slain by Lakṣmaṇa after the Brāhmī sacrifice is disrupted), and others. Lakṣmaṇa felled by Indrajit's weapon is revived when **Hanumān** fetches the **Sañjīvanī** herb (carrying the whole **Droṇagiri** mountain). (Vālmīki, Yuddha Kāṇḍa)
- **Rāvaṇa slain** — Rāma kills **Rāvaṇa** (the ten-headed Lankā-king) with the **Brahmāstra** (the Āditya-hṛdaya hymn taught by Agastya precedes it). (Vālmīki, Yuddha Kāṇḍa)
- **Agni-parīkṣā (fire ordeal)** — Sītā proves her chastity by entering fire; Agni returns her unharmed. (Vālmīki, Yuddha Kāṇḍa)
  - *Variant:* in **Adhyātma Rāmāyana** and others the fire-ordeal is the moment the **shadow-Sītā** is consumed and the real Sītā restored. (Adhyātma Rāmāyana)
- **Return & coronation (Rāma-rājya)** — flight home in the **Puṣpaka-vimāna**; Rāma crowned at Ayodhyā, inaugurating the ideal reign (**Rāma-rājya**). (Vālmīki, Yuddha Kāṇḍa) — celebrated as **Dīpāvalī/Vijayadaśamī** in later tradition.

### Uttara Kāṇḍa — the aftermath
- **Sītā's banishment** — a washerman's gossip about Sītā's time in Lankā prompts Rāma to exile the pregnant Sītā to **Vālmīki's hermitage**, where she bears twins **Lava** and **Kuśa**. (Vālmīki, Uttara Kāṇḍa)
- **Lava-Kuśa & the Aśvamedha** — the boys, taught the Rāmāyana by Vālmīki, recite it at Rāma's horse-sacrifice and capture the sacrificial horse, confronting their father. (Vālmīki, Uttara Kāṇḍa)
- **Sītā's return to the earth** — asked again to prove her purity, Sītā calls on her mother **Bhūmi (Earth)**, who opens and receives her. (Vālmīki, Uttara Kāṇḍa)
- **Rāvaṇa's backstory & Rāma's departure** — Uttara Kāṇḍa narrates Rāvaṇa's genealogy and conquests, and finally Rāma's renunciation, walking into the **Sarayū** to return to Vaikuṇṭha as Vishnu. (Vālmīki, Uttara Kāṇḍa)
  - *Variant (canonicity):* the **Uttara Kāṇḍa** (and arguably the Bāla Kāṇḍa) are widely held by scholars to be **later additions** to the original epic; the Sītā-banishment is omitted or altered in many retellings. [late text / disputed]

### Catalogued Rāmāyana variants
- **Vālmīki Rāmāyana** (Sanskrit, base text) — stresses Rāma's **human/heroic** nature; he learns of his divinity only late.
- **Adhyātma Rāmāyana** (within Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, ascribed to Vyāsa) — strongly **Advaita/devotional**; Rāma = supreme Brahman, **Sītā = Māyā**; introduces the **Chāyā-Sītā**; chief inspiration for Tulsīdās. (Adhyātma Rāmāyana)
- **Rāmcaritmānas** (Tulsīdās, 16th c., Awadhi) — the North-Indian devotional standard; Rāma fully divine; popularizes the **Lakṣmaṇa-rekhā**; typically **omits the harsh Sītā-banishment** of Uttara Kāṇḍa.
- **Kamba Rāmāyanam / Irāmāvatāram** (Kamban, Tamil, ~12th c.) — major Tamil devotional retelling.
- **Dasaratha Jātaka** (Buddhist) — Rāma as a **Bodhisattva**; notably Rāma and Sītā are depicted as **siblings who later marry**; far shorter, no Lankā war emphasis. (Buddhist canon)
- **Paumacariya / Paumacariyam** (Vimalasūri, Jain, Prakrit, ~earliest non-Vālmīki) — rationalist polemic: all characters are **mortals**, not gods; **Rāvaṇa is a noble Vidyādhara**, not a demon; **Lakṣmaṇa (not Rāma) kills Rāvaṇa**; Rāma (Padma) is non-violent and attains liberation. Sītā renounces as a **Jain ascetic** after abandonment and is reborn in heaven. (Jain tradition)
- **Regional/SE-Asian retellings** — Bengali **Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇ**, Oriya, Telugu, Kannada versions; **Ramakien** (Thai), **Hikayat Seri Rama** (Malay), **Reamker** (Khmer), **Phra Lak Phra Lam** (Lao), **Kakawin Rāmāyaṇa** (Javanese) — each with local emendations to actors and episodes. [regional traditions]

## Other Suryavaṃśa / Ikshvāku kings & key Treta narratives

- **Bhagīratha & the Descent of Gangā (Gangāvataraṇa)** — the most famous non-Rāma Treta event. King **Sagara** of Ayodhyā performs an Aśvamedha; **Indra** steals the horse and hides it near sage **Kapila** in Pātāla. Sagara's **60,000 sons** dig down, disturb Kapila, and are reduced to ash by his eyes. Generations later **Bhagīratha** does immense penance to bring the celestial **Gangā** down to liberate their souls; **Shiva** breaks the river's fall in his hair (Gangādhara); Gangā flows to Pātāla and redeems the ancestors. Gangā gains the epithet **Bhāgīrathī**. (Rāmāyana, Bāla Kāṇḍa; Vishnu Purāṇa; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.8–9; Mahābhārata)
  - *Variant (yuga-placement):* Bhagīratha is an ancestor of Rāma in the same Ikshvāku line, placing this within Treta; some retellings narrate it as embedded story-within-story in the Rāmāyana. (Rāmāyana, Bāla Kāṇḍa)
- **Hariścandra the truthful** — Ikshvāku king who, to honor a debt/promise to sage **Viśvāmitra**, surrenders his kingdom, sells his wife **Śaivyā** and son **Rohitāśva**, and becomes a **caṇḍāla's servant at the cremation ground**; tested to the extreme, his truthfulness restores all and elevates him to heaven. (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa; Aitareya Brāhmaṇa [earlier Śunaḥśepa-linked form]; Devī Bhāgavata)
  - *Variant:* the older **Aitareya Brāhmaṇa** version centers on Hariścandra's son and the near-sacrifice of **Śunaḥśepa**; the famous "sold-to-a-caṇḍāla" trial is the later **Purāṇic/Mārkaṇḍeya** elaboration popularized in drama and film. [text-layer variant]
- **Other Ikshvāku/Sūryavaṃśa figures of the era** — **Raghu** (eponym of the **Raghuvaṃśa**, sung by Kālidāsa), **Aja** (Daśaratha's father), **Dilīpa** (cow-service to Nandinī for a son), **Ambarīṣa** (the great Vishnu-devotee tested by Durvāsā and the Sudarśana-cakra), **Triśaṅku** (Viśvāmitra's stranded heaven). Most are recounted as the deep ancestry of Rāma. (Vishnu Purāṇa; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9; Raghuvaṃśa)
  - *Note:* the **Mahābali/Vāmana** episode (above) is also placed by Bhāgavata within Treta, intertwining the asura and Sūryavaṃśa threads of the age.

## Yuga-character notes (Treta)
- **Dharma stands on 3 legs (75%)** — the bull of Dharma (Vṛṣa) has lost one leg since [Satya](./01-satya-yuga.md); virtue still dominant but ritual effort now required to maintain it.
- **Dominant mode = yajña (sacrifice/ritual)** — kings sustain dharma via great sacrifices (Aśvamedha, Rājasūya); the deva-asura tension is overt (Vāmana–Bali). (Vishnu Purāṇa; Bhāgavata Purāṇa)
- **Transition into Dvāpara** — Paraśurāma's immortality and the cliff-hanger of the Sūryavaṃśa carry the narrative forward; see [Dvāpara Yuga](./03-dvapara-yuga.md) (Kṛṣṇa, Mahābhārata).
