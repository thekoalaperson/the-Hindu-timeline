# Kali Yuga — Scriptural Record (Current)

> **Where this sits:** Brahma's current life → Shveta-Varaha Kalpa → 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara → 28th Mahayuga → **4th and final yuga: Kali**. We are HERE. This node covers the Kali Yuga purely from the **scriptural / prophetic** angle (Puranas, Mahabharata). For datable events of the historical period, see [recorded-history.md](./recorded-history.md). For the preceding age, see [../03-dvapara-yuga.md](../03-dvapara-yuga.md).

---

## 1. The Frame — When, How Long, How Reckoned

- **Duration:** 1,000 deva-years + 100 (sandhya) + 100 (sandhyamsa) = 1,200 deva-years × 360 = **432,000 human years** — the shortest yuga, 1/10 of a Mahayuga (Vishnu Purana 1.3; Bhagavata 3.11; Surya Siddhanta 1.15-20).
- **Start epoch:** **3102 BCE** (traditionally 17/18 Feb, midnight, Ujjain meridian), reckoned from **Krishna's departure** from the earth, which the Puranas place at the exact moment Kali "fully" entered (Bhagavata 1.15.36; Vishnu Purana 5.38).
- **Elapsed (2026 CE):** 3102 + 2026 = **5,128 years**. **Remaining:** ~426,872 years.
- **Dharma symbolism:** the **bull of Dharma (Vrisha) now stands on one leg** — only 1/4 of virtue remains, falling steadily toward zero by the yuga's end (Bhagavata 1.16-17, where Earth and Dharma lament as a cow and bull; Manusmriti 1.81-86).
  - *Variant (Aryabhatiya):* same 3102 BCE epoch, but the four yugas are treated as **equal quarters** of 1,080,000 years each (no 4:3:2:1 sandhya structure) — Kali = Dvapara in length.
  - *Variant (Sri Yukteswar, The Holy Science 1894):* the million-year figures are a misreading; Kali lasted only ~2,400 years and **already ended ~1700 CE** — we are now in *ascending Dvapara Yuga*. [non-canonical / modern]

## 2. Symptoms & Decline Predictions (the Kali-dharma)

Catalogued chiefly in **Bhagavata Purana 12.2-3**, **Vishnu Purana 6.1**, **Linga Purana 1.40**, **Mahabharata Vana Parva 188-190** (Markandeya's prophecy):

- **Universal diminution (Bhagavata 12.2.1):** religion, truthfulness, cleanliness, forbearance, mercy, lifespan, bodily strength and memory all "decrease day by day."
- **Lifespan collapses (Bhagavata 12.2.9):** maximum human life falls to **50 years** by mid-age, and far less by the end (Mahabharata gives 16 at the nadir).
- **Wealth = worth (Bhagavata 12.2.2):** riches alone judged the mark of good birth, conduct and virtue.
- **Power = justice (Bhagavata 12.2.2):** law applied only according to one's strength; the strong prey on the weak.
- **Marriage by mere attraction (Bhagavata 12.2.3):** unions formed by superficial lust, dissolved at whim; gender judged only by sexual prowess.
- **Sham religion (Bhagavata 12.2.4-6):** the Vedic path forgotten; "religion" becomes mostly atheistic display. A **brahmana recognized only by his thread**, a sannyasi by externals; sacred status bought, not earned.
- **Holiness by externals:** mere bathing in water (not inner purity) taken as piety; beauty judged by hairstyle (Bhagavata 12.2.5-6).
- **Predatory kings (Bhagavata 12.2.7-8):** rulers become thieves and bandits; subjects flee to hills and forests to live on roots, leaves, wild honey, meat.
- **Ecological & economic ruin (Bhagavata 12.2.9):** drought, famine, crushing taxation; cities depopulated.
- **Social inversion:** all varnas sink toward shudra conduct; cow and brahmana unprotected; servants abandon fallen masters and vice versa (Vishnu Purana 6.1; Linga Purana 1.40).
- *Variant emphasis (Mahabharata, Vana Parva 188):* Markandeya stresses **mleccha (barbarian) kings**, child-bearing girls, 16-year lifespans, men eating one another, and the earth yielding fruit only in deserted shrines — a starker apocalyptic register than the Bhagavata's social satire.
- *Variant emphasis (Linga & Vayu Puranas):* foreground **adharmic rulers (Shaka, Yavana, Huna, Abhira)** as the literal political face of decline, bridging prophecy to the historical-dynasty lists below.
- **The one consolation (Bhagavata 12.3.51-52) — "Kaler dosha-nidhe":** though Kali is "an ocean of faults," it holds a single great virtue — **mere chanting of God's name (nama-sankirtana)** liberates a soul, a feat requiring vast austerity in earlier ages. (Echoed in Vishnu Purana 6.2; the cornerstone of bhakti / Gaudiya tradition.)

## 3. The Kali Yuga Kings — Puranic Dynasty Prophecy

Uniquely, the Puranas (Bhagavata 12.1; Vishnu Purana 4.24; Vayu, Matsya, Brahmanda) **prophesy named dynasties** of Magadha to follow the Mahabharata-war Barhadratha line — i.e. the texts foretell what historians treat as recorded history. Cast as future ("will rule") because the Puranas frame themselves as spoken in Dvapara. See [recorded-history.md](./recorded-history.md) for the historical/dated counterpart.

- **Barhadratha line ends (Bhagavata 12.1.2-3):** the last king **Puranjaya (Ripunjaya)** is assassinated by his minister **Shunaka**, who enthrones his own son.
- **Pradyota dynasty** — **5 kings, 138 years** (Bhagavata 12.1.3-4): Pradyota, Palaka, Vishakhayupa, Rajaka, Nandivardhana.
- **Shishunaga dynasty** — **10 kings, 360 years** (Bhagavata 12.1.5-9): Shishunaga, Kakavarna, Kshemadharma, Kshatraujas/Kshetrajna, **Vidhisara (Bimbisara)**, **Ajatashatru**, Darbhaka, Ajaya, Nandivardhana, **Mahanandi**.
- **Nanda dynasty** — **Mahapadma + 8 sons, ~100 years** (Bhagavata 12.1.10-11): Mahapadma, born of Mahanandi by a shudra woman, called the **"exterminator of all kshatriyas"** (Sarva-kshatrantaka); his line styled the first non-kshatriya empire.
- **Maurya dynasty** — founded when **a brahmana [Chanakya/Kautilya] uproots the Nandas** and enthrones **Chandragupta** (Bhagavata 12.1.12). Line: Chandragupta, Varisara (Bindusara), **Ashokavardhana (Ashoka)**, Suyashas, Sangata, Shalishuka, Somasharma, Shatadhanva, Brihadratha — **~137 years** (Vishnu Purana 4.24 gives 10 Mauryas).
- **Shunga dynasty** — **10 kings, ~112 years** (Bhagavata 12.1.15-17): founded by **Pushyamitra**, who slays the last Maurya; then Agnimitra, Sujyeshtha, Vasumitra, Bhadraka, Pulinda, Ghosha, Vajramitra, Bhagavata, Devabhuti.
- **Kanva dynasty** — **4 kings, 45 years** (Bhagavata 12.1.18-20): Vasudeva (slays last Shunga), Bhumitra, Narayana, Susharma.
- **Andhra / Satavahana dynasty** — **30 kings, ~456 years** (Bhagavata 12.1.21-28): begun by **Balin (Simuka)**, who slays the last Kanva; includes Krishna, Shri-Shantakarna, Paurnamasa, etc.
- **Late "mleccha" rulers (Bhagavata 12.1.29-37; Vishnu Purana 4.24):** thereafter **Abhiras (7), Gardabhins (10), Kankas/Shakas (16), Yavanas (8), Tusharas/Turushkas (14), Gurundas/Murundas (13), Maunas/Hunas (11)** — explicitly low-born, short-lived, irreligious kings; many ruled *contemporaneously*, which the texts compress into a single succession.
- **The nadir king — Vishvasphurji (Bhagavata 12.1; Vishnu Purana 4.24):** a king (also called Puranjaya) who **abolishes caste**, shows no reverence to Vedas or brahmanas, and reduces the twice-born to outcaste status — the symbolic floor of the dynastic prophecy.
  - *Variant (Vayu / Matsya / Brahmanda Puranas):* give **differing king counts and regnal years** for the same dynasties (e.g. varying Maurya and Andhra totals) and add region-specific lines (Kosala, Vidisha, the Naga kings) — no two Purana lists agree exactly; this is a major textual-variant cluster.
  - *Variant (Bhavishya Purana) [late text]:* extends the prophetic king-list far past antiquity to name medieval and even quasi-historical modern rulers; widely regarded as interpolated and **non-canonical for chronology**.

## 4. The End of Kali — Kalki, the Tenth Avatar

The terminal prophecy, most fully in **Kalki Purana**, with core verses in **Bhagavata 12.2.16-24** and **Vishnu Purana 4.24 & 6.1-2**:

- **Kalki's advent (Bhagavata 12.2.18-19; Vishnu Purana 4.24):** when adharma reaches its limit, **Vishnu incarnates as Kalki** in the village of **Shambhala**, son of the brahmana **Vishnuyasha** (mother **Sumati** per Kalki Purana).
- **His arms (Bhagavata 12.2.19-20; Kalki Purana):** mounts the swift white horse **Devadatta** (a form of Garuda), bears a **blazing sword** (gifted by Shiva), gem-hilted; in Kalki Purana also a parrot **Shuka** who knows past/present/future.
- **The cleansing (Bhagavata 12.2.19-20; Vishnu Purana 4.24):** he slaughters the **mleccha and dasyu kings/robbers** by the millions, exterminating the wicked who have lost all spiritual sense.
- **The dawn of the new age (Bhagavata 12.2.23-24; Vishnu Purana 4.24):** with the destroyers gone, **the minds of survivors become spontaneously pure** ("clear as crystal"); these become the **seed-population** of the next age, begetting offspring who follow Krita-yuga dharma.
- **Astronomical trigger (Bhagavata 12.2.24; Vishnu Purana 4.24):** **Satya/Krita Yuga returns when the Moon, Sun, and Jupiter all enter the asterism Pushya (Tishya) together** — the cosmic clock resets. See [../01-satya-yuga.md](../01-satya-yuga.md) for the next age (note: that node may describe the *current* 28th-cycle Satya; the *next* Satya begins a new Mahayuga).
- **Restorers of dharma (Bhagavata 12.2.37-38; Vishnu Purana 4.24):** two immortals — **Devapi** (of the Puru/lunar line) and **Maru** (of the Ikshvaku/solar line) — who survived all four yugas in meditation at the village **Kalapa**, re-emerge to **re-found the kshatriya dynasties** and teach proper dharma.
- *Variant (Kalki Purana, the dedicated text):* expands a full epic — Kalki's marriages (to **Padma**/Lakshmi and Rama), wars against **Koka and Vikoka**, defeat of the demon **Kali** personified, and pilgrimage; gives Kalki a long earthly reign before ascending. [late, sectarian Vaishnava]
- *Variant (Mahabharata, Vana Parva 190):* the destroyer at age's end is named **Pramiti / Kalki** — a "brahmin-warrior" who, *aided by the brahmanas*, annihilates the barbarians; the Kalki-avatar identity is less developed here than in the Puranas.
- *Variant (Buddhist & Shambhala tradition):* the village name **Shambhala** is shared with the Kalachakra Buddhist prophecy of a final king **Rudra Chakrin** defeating mleccha forces — a notable cross-tradition parallel. [comparative / non-Hindu]

## 5. Dissolution & the Cosmic Reset

- **Not a universal pralaya:** the end of *this* Kali Yuga is a **yuga-junction (yuga-sandhi)**, not the dissolution of the world. After it, the **28th Mahayuga completes** and the **29th begins with a fresh Satya Yuga** within the same Vaivasvata Manvantara (Vishnu Purana 6.3; Bhagavata 12.4).
- **Larger dissolutions still distant (Bhagavata 12.4):**
  - **Naimittika (occasional) pralaya** comes only at the end of **Brahma's day** (after all 14 Manvantaras), 1,000 Mahayugas hence.
  - **Prakritika (elemental) pralaya** at the end of Brahma's 100-year life.
  - **Nitya (constant)** and **Atyantika (final liberation)** dissolutions are continuous/individual.
- **The wheel turns on:** after the next Satya, the yuga cycle repeats — 43 more Mahayugas remain in this Manvantara, then 7 more Manvantaras complete the Kalpa.
