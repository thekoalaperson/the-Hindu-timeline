# Manvantara 6 — Cākṣuṣa (Past)

> Hierarchy: Brahma's current life → Śveta-Varāha Kalpa → **6th of the 14 Manvantaras** (the one immediately before ours). Presided over by Cākṣuṣa Manu. See [the current 7th](./manvantara-07-vaivasvata/), the [preceding 5th — Raivata](./manvantara-05-raivata.md), and [overview of all 14](../). Per [framework constants](../../../00-time-and-cosmology/), each manvantara ≈ 71 mahāyugas + 1 sandhyā ≈ 306,720,000 years.

The last completed manvantara before Vaivasvata (ours). The Purāṇas treat it densely because it lies just behind our own horizon: its Manu's lineage feeds directly into the Pṛthu cycle, and its Nara–Nārāyaṇa and Dattātreya births are among the most-cited "before our time" events. As with all manvantaras, the office-holder lists (Indra, sages, deva-classes) vary by recension; all attested variants are cataloged below.

## The Presiding Manu — Cākṣuṣa

- **Cākṣuṣa Manu** — the sixth Manu, named for the deity **Cakṣus** (the personified faculty of sight), whose son he is in the standard derivation; his manvantara takes its name from him (Viṣṇu Purāṇa III; Bhāgavata Purāṇa). (Reigns through the whole 6th manvantara.)
- **Lineage / genealogy** — descends from **Dhruva** (the pole-star ascetic of the [Svāyambhuva](./manvantara-01-svayambhuva.md) line): Dhruva → Śiṣṭi (by Śambhu) → **Ripu** (by Succhāyā) → Ripu m. Bṛhatī → **Cakṣus / Cākṣuṣa** → Manu by **Puṣkariṇī**, daughter of Vīraṇa Prajāpati (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa; Puranic Encyclopaedia). His descendant **Puru** → **Aṅga** → **Vena** → the emperor **Pṛthu**, tying this Manu's house to the earth-flattening Pṛthu cycle (see [02-... deities / Pṛthu](../../../) where catalogued). [genealogy]
- **Previous-birth legend (Ānanda):** before becoming Manu he lived as **Ānanda**, son of the sage **Anamitra**. By accident — a cat carried him off and switched him for the infant son of **King Vikrānta** — he was raised as a royal heir; on learning the truth (at his sacred-thread rite) he confessed to the king and withdrew to the forest for **tapas**, whereupon **Brahmā** blessed him to be reborn as the sixth Manu (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa; echoed in Bhāgavata-tradition). [folk / etiological]
  - *Variant (name):* some accounts give the penitent's name simply as a sage **Ānanda** "blessed by Brahmā to become a Manu," without the cat-and-king frame. [account differs by text]

## Sons of Cākṣuṣa Manu

The Manu's sons seed the next ruling dynasties; the lists differ sharply between the two principal Purāṇas:

- *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa / Mārkaṇḍeya — 10 sons by wife **Naḍvalā**, daughter of Prajāpati Vairāja):* **Kuru, Puru, Śatadyumna, Tapasvī, Satyavān, Śuci, Agniṣṭoma, Atirātra, Sudyumna, Abhimanyu.** (Puru carries the line to Aṅga/Pṛthu.)
- *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa — 12 sons by **Naḍvalā**):* **Puru, Kutsa, Trita, Dyumna, Satyavān, Ṛta, Vrata, Agniṣṭoma, Atirātra, Pradyumna, Śibi, Ulmuka.**
- *Variant (Bhāgavata, "headed by" formula):* sons "headed by **Puru, Pūru(ṣa), Sudyumna**." [variant — summary vs. full list]
- *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa, popular retellings):* the Manu has **ten sons, of whom Ruru is the most prominent.** [variant — "Ruru" appears for "Kuru" in some chains]

## Cosmic Officers of this Manvantara

### Indra (king of the devas)
- **Manojava** (Bhāgavata Purāṇa).
- *Variant:* **Mantradruma** (Viṣṇu Purāṇa III). The two are sometimes harmonised as one figure under both titles.
- *Variant (recensional spellings):* **Manoja / Manojapa / Camūmān** in other recensions. [variant]

### Class(es) of devas (deva-gaṇas)
- **Āpyas** — the headline deva-class of this manvantara (Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa).
- *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa, fuller list of god-groups):* five classes — **Ādya, Prabhūta (Pṛthuga), Ṛbhu (Ṛtu), Pṛthaga, Lekha** (each said to number eight), so ~forty gods total. [lists differ across Purāṇas]
- *Variant (HinduPad / popular):* prominent deities **Lekha, Ṛbhu, Pṛthagbhūta, Varimūla, Divau.** [folk spelling-variant of the above]

### Saptarṣis (the Seven Sages)
The single most variant-heavy item; at least three distinct heptads circulate:
- *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa, primary):* **Sumedhas, Virajas, Haviṣmat, Uttama, Madhu, Abhināman, Sahiṣṇu.**
- *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa, alternate enumeration):* **Hiraṇyaromā, Vedaśrī, Ūrdhvabāhu, Vedabāhu, Sudhāman, Parjanya, Mahāmuni.**
- *Variant (HinduPad / popular):* **Bhṛgu, Sudhāman, Virāj, Viṣṇu, Nārada, Vivasvān, Abhimānī.**
- *Variant (one web-encyclopaedia chain):* **Haviṣmān, Prāṇa, Dākṣa (Vāmadeva), Tapasvī, Sutapa, Jyotiṣmān, Haviṣmat.** [late/secondary]

### Avatāra of Viṣṇu
- **Ajita** — Viṣṇu manifests as Ajita to uphold dharma in this period (Bhāgavata Purāṇa). Some texts even gloss "Cākṣuṣa's true name" as Ajita, conflating Manu and avatāra. [disputed]
- *Variant:* the avatāra is named **Vaikuṇṭha** (alternate Bhāgavata frame, born of Vikuṇṭhā). [variant]

## Events, Stories & Figures of the Cākṣuṣa Age

The Purāṇas anchor several "deep past, but well before our era" births and deeds in this manvantara:

- **Birth of Nara and Nārāyaṇa** — the twin sage-incarnations of Viṣṇu, born as sons of **Dharma** (and Mūrti/Ahiṃsā), appear in the Cākṣuṣa manvantara (Bhāgavata Purāṇa). They are the perpetual ascetics of Badarikāśrama; Nārāyaṇa later reincarnates as Kṛṣṇa and Nara as Arjuna in the [Mahābhārata](../../../) era of our own age. (During the 6th manvantara.) [links the two great ascetics to a pre-Vaivasvata origin]
- **The Atri–Anasūyā triad** — from the chaste **Anasūyā** (wife of sage **Atri**) the Trimūrti take partial birth in this age: **Brahmā as Soma/Candra (the Moon)**, **Viṣṇu as Dattātreya**, and **Śiva as Durvāsas** (Mārkaṇḍeya / Puranic Encyclopaedia). Dattātreya becomes the great guru-avatāra; Durvāsas the irascible curse-bearing sage who recurs across later epic chronology. (During the 6th manvantara.) [major avatāra-origin anchored here]
  - *Variant:* the births of Candra, Dattātreya, and Durvāsas are placed by other texts in differing kalpas/manvantaras; the Cākṣuṣa placement follows the Mārkaṇḍeya tradition. [chronology disputed across texts]
- **Ajita and the Churning of the Ocean of Milk (Kṣīra-sāgara manthana)** — in the Bhāgavata frame the avatāra **Ajita** of this manvantara is the form that bears **Mount Mandara** as the tortoise **Kūrma** and distributes the **amṛta** at the devas' churning; thus some readings locate (a) churning at the close of / within the Cākṣuṣa age. (Late in the 6th manvantara — text-dependent.) [disputed: the churning is variously dated; see Kūrma in 03-/04- avatāra material]
- **The Pṛthu prelude** — although the **Vena → Pṛthu** earth-milking epic is usually narrated in our own era, the dynastic root (Cākṣuṣa → Puru → Aṅga → Vena) is planted here, so the Cākṣuṣa lineage is the genealogical bridge into the Vaivasvata-age kings. (Lineage seeded in the 6th manvantara; deeds in/after the 7th.) [genealogical bridge]

## Transition into Our Age (Vaivasvata)

- This manvantara closed at the end of its **sandhyā** (twilight). In the Purāṇic frame each manvantara-juncture brings a partial dissolution and the installation of a new Manu, new Indra, new sages, and new deva-classes; the renewal at the close of Cākṣuṣa installed **Vaivasvata Manu** (Śrāddhadeva / son of Vivasvān-Sūrya) as the **7th and current Manu**. (End of the 6th → start of the 7th manvantara.)
- The **Matsya (great flood)** episode — Viṣṇu's fish-avatāra rescuing Vaivasvata Manu — is narrated at this very junction, i.e. the deluge that ushers in our manvantara is sometimes framed as the dissolution closing the Cākṣuṣa age. (At the Cākṣuṣa–Vaivasvata sandhyā — text-dependent.) See [Manvantara 7 — Vaivasvata](./manvantara-07-vaivasvata/) and Matsya in the avatāra material. [boundary event, chronology text-dependent]

## On the Sparseness of the Record

Beyond the Nara–Nārāyaṇa and Atri-triad births, the lineage chain, and the office-holder lists, the Purāṇas preserve **little continuous narrative** set squarely "during" the Cākṣuṣa reign — most named events are origin-points (births/lineages) or boundary events (the churning, the flood) whose dating to this manvantara is itself text-dependent. The richness here is genealogical and onomastic (who held which cosmic office), not a sustained event-history. This is typical of the pre-Vaivasvata manvantaras, which the texts render mostly as enumerations.
