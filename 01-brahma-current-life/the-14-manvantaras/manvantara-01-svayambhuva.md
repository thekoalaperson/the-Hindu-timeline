# Manvantara 1 — Svāyambhuva (Past)

> First of the 14 Manvantaras of the current Kalpa (Shveta-Varaha). Ruled by **Svāyambhuva Manu**, the mind-born son of Brahmā. We are now 6 Manvantaras later, in the 7th ([Vaivasvata](manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)). It is the **richest-documented past Manvantara**: most of the early genealogical and "creation" narratives of the Purāṇas (Dhruva, Pṛthu, the Prachetas, Dakṣa's daughters) are set here, at the dawn of the present Kalpa just after the Varāha rescue of the Earth.
> Parent: [the-14-manvantaras](./README.md) · Up to [01-brahma-current-life](../../README.md)

## Cosmic Address
- **Manvantara 1 of 14** · Manu: **Svāyambhuva** · Indra of the era: **Yajña** · Devas: **Yāmas** (and Tuṣitas, per some texts) · ~306,720,000 yrs (1 Manvantara). See [../../00-time-and-cosmology](../../00-time-and-cosmology) for the clock.
- This Manvantara opens immediately after the **Varāha avatāra** lifts the submerged Earth at the dawn of the [Shveta-Varaha Kalpa](../../README.md) — hence the entire present Kalpa is named for that boar (*Varāha-kalpa*). The Svāyambhuva creation is therefore the "first morning" of Brahmā's current day.

## Officers of the Era (the 5 manvantara-constants)
Each Manvantara is defined by its Manu, its Indra, its class(es) of gods (gaṇa-devatā), its seven sages (saptarṣi), and the Manu's sons (the seed-kings). For Svāyambhuva:
- **Manu** — **Svāyambhuva** ("self-born / born of the Self-existent"), mind-born son of Brahmā (*Viṣṇu P.* 1.7; *Bhāgavata P.* 3.12).
- **Indra** — **Yajña**, son of Manu's daughter Ākūti by ṛṣi Ruci; an aṃśa (partial descent) of Viṣṇu who holds the throne of the devas in this age (*Bhāgavata P.* 4.1, 8.1). One of the rare cases where the Indra-post is filled by an incarnation of Viṣṇu himself.
- **Devas (gaṇa-devatā)** — the **Yāmas** (Yāma gods), twelve sons of Yajña and his consort **Dakṣiṇā** (*Bhāgavata P.* 4.1).
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu/Vāyu P.):* the class of gods of this era is given simply as the **Yāmas**; some lists also name the **Tuṣitas** here, though most assign the Tuṣitas to the 2nd ([Svārociṣa](manvantara-02-svarochisha.md)) Manvantara [disputed across recensions].
- **Saptarṣi (Seven Sages)** — **Marīci, Atri, Aṅgiras, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu, Vasiṣṭha** — i.e. the original Prajāpati mind-sons of Brahmā themselves serve as the first septet of sages (*Viṣṇu P.*; *Bhāgavata P.* 8.1). See [../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md).
  - *Note:* later Manvantaras have different saptarṣi rosters; in Svāyambhuva the sages and the prajāpatis coincide, marking the freshness of creation.
- **Manu's sons (seed-kings)** — **Priyavrata** and **Uttānapāda**, from whom the first royal dynasties descend (*Viṣṇu P.* 1.7; *Bhāgavata P.* 3.12).

## Key Events & Stories (chronological within the era)

### A. The first humans and the founding family
- **Creation of Svāyambhuva Manu & Shatarūpā** — Brahmā divides his own body into a male and a female half; the male becomes **Svāyambhuva Manu**, the female **Shatarūpā** ("hundred-formed"). They are treated as the first human couple and progenitors of mankind (*Manusmṛiti* 1; *Viṣṇu P.* 1; *Bhāgavata P.* 3–4). *(Opens the Manvantara, just after Varāha.)*
  - *Variant (Matsya/Brahma P.):* Shatarūpā is Brahmā's own daughter (identified with Sarasvatī / Vāc / Sāvitrī), and Brahmā's infatuation with her — for which he sprouts extra heads to keep her in view, and Śiva later strikes one off — is a noted, theologically awkward episode [disputed; the incest motif is muted or rationalized in Vaiṣṇava retellings]. See [../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).
  - *Variant (count of children):* most texts give **2 sons + 3 daughters**; a few recensions enumerate additional sons (e.g. Prasūti is sometimes listed as a daughter married out, sometimes the daughters number differently) [late-text drift].
- **Children of Manu** — Two sons, **Priyavrata** and **Uttānapāda**; three daughters, **Ākūti**, **Devahūti**, **Prasūti** (*Bhāgavata P.* 3.12, 4.1; *Viṣṇu P.* 1.7).
  - **Ākūti** m. ṛṣi **Ruci** → twins **Yajña** (the era's Indra, a Viṣṇu aṃśa) and **Dakṣiṇā** (who becomes Yajña's wife); their sons are the twelve **Yāma** gods.
  - **Devahūti** m. Prajāpati **Kardama** → nine daughters (wed to the nine prajāpatis incl. Marīci, Atri, Aṅgiras, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu, Bhṛgu, Vasiṣṭha, Atharvan) and one son, **Kapila** — the Sāṃkhya teacher and a Viṣṇu avatāra, who delivers the *Devahūti-putra Gītā* to his mother (*Bhāgavata P.* 3.24–33). See [../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
  - **Prasūti** m. Prajāpati **Dakṣa** → daughters who become the mothers of cosmos and dharma (incl. **Satī**, **Aditi**, **Diti**, **Danu**, and the personified virtues Śraddhā, Maitrī, etc.). This is the **first Dakṣa** of the era. See the full unpacking at [../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md).

### B. The two royal lines
- **Priyavrata's line (the elder son)** — initially a reluctant ascetic, Priyavrata is persuaded by Brahmā to take up kingship; he marries **Kāmyā** (= Barhiṣmatī), daughter of Kardama, and fathers **ten sons + two daughters**; the sons include **Āgnīdhra** and **Medhātithi** among ten "jyotiṣmat" princes (*Viṣṇu P.* 2.1; *Bhāgavata P.* 5.1). *(Mid-Manvantara dynastic founding.)*
  - **Chariot-furrows of Priyavrata** — driving his chariot around the Earth seven times to make a second sun's worth of daylight, his wheel-ruts carve out the **seven oceans** and demarcate the **seven dvīpas** (continents) of cosmic geography (*Bhāgavata P.* 5.1). See [../../06-sacred-geography](../../06-sacred-geography). This is the Purāṇic origin-myth of the *sapta-dvīpa* world map.
  - *Continuation [late, set in Vaivasvata]:* the Bhāgavata's famous **Ṛṣabha → Bharata → Jaḍa-Bharata** parable descends from Priyavrata via Āgnīdhra and Nābhi — but the Bhāgavata frames it in the present (Vaivasvata) era, not here. Catalogued at [../../04-deep-dives/rishabha-bharata-jada.md](../../04-deep-dives/rishabha-bharata-jada.md) [chronology disputed between texts].
- **Uttānapāda's line (the younger son)** — from which spring **Dhruva** and, several generations on, **Pṛthu** (below). Uttānapāda has two queens, the favored **Suruci** (mother of **Uttama**) and the neglected **Sunīti** (mother of **Dhruva**) (*Bhāgavata P.* 4.8).

### C. Dhruva — the boy who became the pole star
- **Dhruva's penance** — the small son **Dhruva**, pushed off his father's lap by the proud queen Suruci, vows to win a station higher than any. Counseled by **Nārada**, he performs extreme tapas in the **Madhuvana** forest, mastering breath until the worlds tremble (*Bhāgavata P.* 4.8–4.12; *Viṣṇu P.* 1.11–12).
- **Viṣṇu's boon & the fixing of the Pole** — Viṣṇu appears, touches Dhruva's cheek with His conch, and grants him **Dhruva-loka / Dhruva-pada** — the immovable celestial point around which all stars and the saptarṣi wheel — to last a full Kalpa (*Bhāgavata P.* 4.9, 4.12). The North Star is thereby a person, the model bhakta. *(One of the earliest events of human dynastic time.)*
  - *Variant:* in some retellings the boon-step is Dhruva's elevation **above the Three Worlds** and even above his own grandfather; the "pole star" identification is the standard but is sometimes left implicit in *Viṣṇu P.*
  - *Sequel:* Dhruva's later war with the **Yakṣas** (after his brother Uttama is slain), halted by the intervention of the **Manu Svāyambhuva** himself, and Dhruva's final ascent on a celestial vehicle alongside his mother Sunīti (*Bhāgavata P.* 4.10–12).

### D. Vena's tyranny & Pṛthu's milking of the Earth
- **The fall of the line into Vena** — generations after Dhruva (via **Aṅga**, who weds **Sunīthā**, daughter of **Mṛtyu/Death**), the tyrant **Vena** is born; he bans all yajña and worship, claiming "I alone am Hari, the enjoyer of sacrifice." Dharma collapses; the sages kill him with sanctified blades of kuśa-grass and *huṃ*-mantras (*Bhāgavata P.* 4.13–14; *Viṣṇu P.* 1.13).
- **The churning of Vena's corpse & birth of Pṛthu** — from the churned thigh of dead Vena emerges the dwarf **Niṣāda** (taking out the sin); then from the churned arms arise **Pṛthu** and his consort **Arcis** — Pṛthu being a direct aṃśa-avatāra of Viṣṇu, the first consecrated **rājan** (king) (*Bhāgavata P.* 4.15).
- **The milking of the Earth** — when the famished Earth, in cow-form, hides her herbs and grains, Pṛthu pursues her with drawn bow; she yields, and is "milked" — Pṛthu using **Svāyambhuva Manu** as the calf and his own cupped hands as the pail — drawing out grain so beings can be fed. Earth is thereafter **Pṛthivī**, "she who belongs to Pṛthu," and he levels the land for cultivation, towns and trade (*Bhāgavata P.* 4.17–18; *Viṣṇu P.* 1.13). Full treatment: [../../04-deep-dives/prithu-prithvi.md](../../04-deep-dives/prithu-prithvi.md).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata):* not Pṛthu alone but **every order of beings** milks the Earth-cow in turn — devas (calf Indra, vessel of gold), ṛṣis (calf Bṛhaspati), pitṛs, nāgas, asuras, yakṣas, plants — each with its own calf, milker and pail, drawing the sustenance proper to its kind. This is the Purāṇa's etiology for the whole food-chain.
  - **Pṛthu's 100 horse-sacrifices & clash with Indra** — Indra repeatedly steals Pṛthu's sacrificial horse (in various ascetic disguises), nearly provoking Pṛthu to kill him before Brahmā brokers peace; later the **four Kumāras** and **Viṣṇu (as Janārdana)** instruct Pṛthu, who delivers a celebrated discourse before retiring to the forest with Arcis (*Bhāgavata P.* 4.19–23).

### E. The Prachetas — the closing of the Svāyambhuva genealogy
- **The ten Prachetas** — descending from Pṛthu through **Antardhāna** and **Prācīnabarhis**, the **ten Prachetas** brothers undertake 10,000 years of underwater tapas, receiving from Viṣṇu the boon to be progenitors of mankind (*Viṣṇu P.* 1.14–15; *Bhāgavata P.* 4.24–30).
  - **The Rudra-Gīta** — Śiva (Rudra) appears to the Prachetas as they begin their austerity and teaches them the hymn of self-realization, the **Rudra-Gīta / Rudra-saṃhitā stotra** (*Bhāgavata P.* 4.24).
  - **Quarrel with the trees & Marṣiā/Māriṣā** — emerging to find the Earth overgrown with trees, the Prachetas nearly burn the forests; **Soma (the Moon)** mediates and gives them in marriage the tree-born maiden **Māriṣā** (Marṣiā), from whom is born **Dakṣa** — the *re-born* Dakṣa (the "second Dakṣa"), who will carry the prajāpati function into later ages (*Viṣṇu P.* 1.15; *Bhāgavata P.* 4.30). This dovetails the line back into the Dakṣa-cycle of [sati-daksha-and-parvati](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md).
