# Prāṇa & the Vital Airs

> Cross-cutting doctrine within [05-concepts-and-dharma](./README.md) of *The Hindu Timeline*; back to the [master index](../README.md). **Prāṇa** — the life-force that animates body and cosmos — and its anatomy: the five major *prāṇas*, five *upa-prāṇas*, the *prāṇamaya-kośa*, and the discipline of *prāṇāyāma*. Companion to [yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md) (which treats *prāṇāyāma* as a limb of *aṣṭāṅga*), [atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md](./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md) (the self the airs serve), and the subtle-body material in [../04-deep-dives/yoga-sutras-of-patanjali.md](../04-deep-dives/yoga-sutras-of-patanjali.md).

*Prāṇa* (root *pra* + *an*, "to breathe forth") is at once the breath, the vital energy, and the cosmic life-principle. The [Vedas](../90-literature-corpus/shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) already deify *Prāṇa*; the [Upanishads](../90-literature-corpus/shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) make it the contest-winner among the faculties and the thread of life; later yoga and Tantra map it into a precise functional anatomy of "winds" (*vāyus*) coursing the subtle body. This file catalogs the life-force concept, the fivefold and tenfold breaths, the energy-sheath, and breath-discipline, recording variant rosters and framings across texts and lineages. Two clocks apply where dating matters: scripture's self-claims vs. academic dating [scholarly].

---

## 1. Prāṇa as Life-Force

- **Prāṇa — the cosmic & bodily life-principle** - The animating energy underlying breath, sensation, mind, and motion; without it the body is inert. Conceived both microcosmically (the breath in a being) and macrocosmically (the life of the universe). (Ṛgveda; Atharvaveda; Upanishads.)
- **The Prāṇa-hymn of the Atharvaveda** - AV 11.4 is a sustained hymn glorifying *Prāṇa* as a deity — lord of all that breathes, present in the wind, the rain, the trees, the foetus; "homage to Prāṇa in whose control is this All." Earliest extended deification of breath-as-cosmic-power. (Atharvaveda 11.4.)
- **The contest of the faculties (prāṇa-vidyā)** - A recurring Upanishadic parable: the senses (speech, sight, hearing, mind) each leave the body to prove their primacy; the body survives each loss but collapses when *Prāṇa* prepares to depart — proving breath the chief (*śreṣṭha*) and support of all. (Chāndogya Upanishad 5.1; Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upanishad 6.1; Praśna Upanishad 2.)
- **Prāṇa in the Praśna Upanishad** - The text built around six questions on breath: where *prāṇa* arises, how it divides into the five airs, how it relates to *ātman* and *OM*; it presents *prāṇa* as born of the *ātman* and distributed by *deva*-like sub-functions. Single most systematic early source. (Praśna Upanishad.)
- **Prāṇa and Brahman** - Several Upanishads identify *prāṇa* closely with *Brahman* itself (the worship of breath as the absolute), making the vital force a bridge between the physical and the ultimate. See [atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md](./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md). (Kauṣītaki Upanishad; Chāndogya Upanishad.)
  - *Variant (Sāṃkhya/Yoga framing):* *prāṇa* is a function of the subtle body (*sūkṣma-śarīra* / *liṅga-śarīra*) and the inner instrument (*antaḥkaraṇa*), not identical with pure consciousness (*puruṣa*); it belongs to *prakṛti*. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Vedānta/cosmic):* the macro-*prāṇa* is *Hiraṇyagarbha* / *sūtrātman* — the thread-self binding all beings — while individual breaths are its localizations. [late text]
- **Prāṇa likened to the five-hooded serpent** - Because the life-air functions in a fivefold way (the five *prāṇas* below), it is traditionally compared to a five-hooded serpent. (Purāṇic / Vaiṣṇava commentarial imagery.) [folk]

## 2. The Five Major Prāṇas (Pañca-Prāṇa / Pañca-Vāyu)

The single life-force differentiates into five functional "winds," each with a seat, a direction of flow, and governed organs. The canonical roster appears in the Praśna and later yoga texts.

- **Prāṇa (the inward / upward forward air)** - The primary breath; seated in the chest/heart, governing inhalation and the intake of all that sustains. The Praśna locates the embodied *prāṇa* in eye, ear, mouth, and nose (the faculties of reception). Direction: inward and upward (chest-to-throat). (Praśna Upanishad 3; later yoga texts.)
- **Apāna (the downward / outward air)** - The "down-breath"; seated in the pelvis/lower abdomen, ruling the organs of excretion and generation, elimination, and exhalation. Direction: downward and outward. (Praśna Upanishad 3.)
- **Samāna (the equalizing / middle air)** - Seated at the navel; governs **digestion and assimilation**, kindling the digestive fire (*jaṭharāgni*) and balancing *prāṇa* and *apāna*. Direction: lateral/equalizing between the upper and lower airs. (Praśna Upanishad 3; Bṛhadāraṇyaka.)
- **Udāna (the up-breath / ascending air)** - Seated in the throat and head; governs speech, the upward movement of energy, and — critically — the **departure of the soul at death**, leading the being upward, downward, or to rebirth according to merit. Also linked to growth, will, and ascent to higher states. (Praśna Upanishad 3.7; Bṛhadāraṇyaka.)
- **Vyāna (the pervading / circulating air)** - Distributed throughout the whole body; governs circulation, the distribution of nutrients and energy, and coordination of movement; "moves in all these." Direction: diffuse/all-directional. (Praśna Upanishad 3.)
  - *Mnemonic correspondence (yogic):* often mapped to elements/directions — *prāṇa* (forward, chest), *apāna* (down, pelvis), *samāna* (in/contracting, navel), *udāna* (up, throat), *vyāna* (out/pervading, periphery). [scholarly]
  - *Variant (ordering/seat differences):* texts vary on exact seats and on whether *samāna* or *vyāna* is named "fourth" vs "fifth"; the Praśna's order (prāṇa–apāna–samāna–vyāna–udāna) differs from the common modern listing (prāṇa–apāna–samāna–udāna–vyāna). [disputed]
  - *Variant (Bhagavad Gītā):* the Gītā references the *prāṇa*/*apāna* pair and their offering into one another as a form of *yajña* and *prāṇāyāma* (Bhagavad Gītā 4.29; 5.27; see [../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md](../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md)).
  - *Variant (Ayurvedic):* classical Āyurveda (Caraka, Suśruta) keeps the same five *vāyu* names as sub-types of *vāta doṣa*, with seats and functions broadly parallel but framed medically rather than soteriologically. [scholarly]

## 3. The Five Upa-Prāṇas (Minor Vital Airs)

The secondary or "sub-breaths" handle reflexive, involuntary bodily acts. The roster is standard in Haṭha-yoga and later compendia; it is largely **post-Upanishadic** systematization. [late text]

- **Nāga** - The cause of **belching and hiccups**; expels agitated air upward from the stomach. (Yoga-yājñavalkya; Haṭha tradition.)
- **Kūrma ("tortoise")** - Governs **blinking and the opening/closing of the eyelids**, keeping the eyes moist and protected and enabling vision. (Yoga tradition.)
- **Kṛkara (Kṛkala)** - The cause of **sneezing and coughing** (clearing the respiratory passages), and associated with hunger and thirst; assists respiration. (Yoga tradition.)
- **Devadatta ("god-given")** - Governs **yawning** and the deep involuntary intake of breath, releasing fatigue and drowsiness. (Yoga tradition.)
- **Dhanañjaya ("wealth-conqueror")** - **Pervades the whole body**, related to nourishment, swelling/decomposition; said to remain even after death until the body decays, and (in some accounts) to open and close the heart valves. (Yoga tradition.)
  - *Variant (function swaps):* sources differ on which sub-breath governs hunger/thirst vs. sneezing — sometimes Kṛkara is given hunger and Devadatta sleep/yawning; assignments are **not uniform** across manuals. [disputed]
  - *Variant (rosters):* some texts list only the five major airs and omit the *upa-prāṇas*; the "ten winds" scheme (5 + 5) is a later harmonization most fully stated in texts like the *Yoga-yājñavalkya* and *Gheraṇḍa Saṃhitā*. [late text] [scholarly]
  - *Open roster note:* spellings (Kṛkara/Kṛkala/Kūrkala) and minor-air counts vary; treat the tenfold list as a tradition-stabilized convention, not a single fixed canon. [flag]

## 4. The Prāṇamaya-Kośa (Vital-Energy Sheath)

The *prāṇas* constitute one of the five "sheaths" (*kośas*) wrapping the *ātman*, taught first in the Taittirīya Upanishad. Cross-link the self-doctrine in [atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md](./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md).

- **The Pañca-Kośa doctrine (Taittirīya Upanishad)** - The self is wrapped in five concentric sheaths from gross to subtle, each "interior to and finer than" the last; realization proceeds by seeing through them to the *ātman*. (Taittirīya Upanishad, Brahmānanda-valli; conventionally dated to mid-1st millennium BCE [scholarly].)
- **Annamaya-kośa — the food sheath** - The outermost: the physical body, made of and sustained by food (*anna*). (Taittirīya Upanishad 2.1.)
- **Prāṇamaya-kośa — the vital-air sheath** - Interior to the body: the sheath of *prāṇa* and the five airs, animating the physical form; "the gods breathe after breath, and men, and beasts; for breath is the life of beings." The bridge between gross body and mind. (Taittirīya Upanishad 2.2–2.3.)
- **Manomaya-kośa — the mind sheath** - The sensory-emotional mind (*manas*) and the ritual/intentional mind; processes perception and volition. (Taittirīya Upanishad 2.3–2.4.)
- **Vijñānamaya-kośa — the intellect/knowledge sheath** - Discriminative intelligence (*buddhi*/*vijñāna*), the agent of discernment and the "doer" of action. (Taittirīya Upanishad 2.4–2.5.)
- **Ānandamaya-kośa — the bliss sheath** - The innermost, subtlest sheath of bliss (*ānanda*), nearest to — though, in Advaita, still distinct from — the *ātman*/*Brahman* it veils. (Taittirīya Upanishad 2.5.)
  - *Variant (Advaita Vedānta):* Śaṅkara and the *Tattva-bodha* treat the five *kośas* as objects of negation (*neti neti*) — the *ātman* is the witness "not any of these sheaths," including the *prāṇamaya*. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Yoga/Tantra):* the *prāṇamaya-kośa* is correlated with the subtle body's *nāḍī* network and *cakras*, making it the operative field for *prāṇāyāma* and kuṇḍalinī work (see §5 and [yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md)). [late text]

## 5. Prāṇāyāma (Regulation of the Breath / Vital Air)

*Prāṇāyāma* = *prāṇa* + *āyāma* ("extension/restraint") — the disciplined regulation of breath to steady mind and rouse/redirect the vital airs. It is the **fourth limb** of Patañjali's *aṣṭāṅga* (see [yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md)).

- **Patañjali's definition** - After *āsana* is steady, *prāṇāyāma* is "the cessation of the movement of inhalation and exhalation" (*tasmin sati śvāsa-praśvāsayor gati-vicchedaḥ*), regulated by place, time, and number, becoming long and subtle. (Yoga Sūtras 2.49–2.51.) It thins the veil over the inner light and fits the mind for *dhāraṇā* (2.52–2.53).
- **The phases of the breath cycle** - **Pūraka** (inhalation), **Kumbhaka** (retention), **Rechaka** (exhalation), and (in some lists) **Śūnyaka** (empty retention). Patañjali names the operations as external, internal, and suppressed/restrained movements; a fourth (*kevala* / spontaneous) transcends the prior three (Yoga Sūtras 2.50–2.51).
- **Kumbhaka — retention as the heart of the practice** - The Haṭha tradition treats retention as primary: "when the breath is retained, the mind becomes steady." The *Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā* (II) catalogs **eight kumbhakas** (*sūrya-bhedana, ujjāyī, sītkārī, śītalī, bhastrikā, bhrāmarī, mūrcchā, plāvinī*). (Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā 2.44–2.77.) [late text]
- **Nāḍī purification** - *Prāṇāyāma*'s aim in Haṭha is to cleanse the **72,000 nāḍīs** (energy channels) so *prāṇa* can enter the central **suṣumnā**; the lunar **iḍā** (left) and solar **piṅgalā** (right) are balanced via *nāḍī-śodhana* (alternate-nostril breathing). (Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā ch. 2.) Cross-link the subtle-body map in [yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md) §2.
- **Bandhas & control of the airs** - The three "locks" — *mūla-bandha* (root), *uḍḍiyāna-bandha* (abdominal), *jālandhara-bandha* (throat) — are used to seal and redirect *prāṇa* and *apāna*, uniting them at the navel to drive energy upward into *suṣumnā*. (Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā 3.) [late text]
- **Prāṇāyāma as inner sacrifice (Gītā)** - The Gītā reframes breath-control as *yajña*: offering the in-breath into the out-breath and vice versa, restraining their courses (Bhagavad Gītā 4.29). See [../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md](../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md).
  - *Variant (Patañjala vs. Haṭha emphasis):* Patañjali subordinates *prāṇāyāma* to meditative absorption (a mental aid), whereas Haṭha texts make it a physiological lever to force kuṇḍalinī and *suṣumnā*; classical "rāja yoga" treats Haṭha as preparatory (HYP 1.1–2). [scholarly]
  - *Variant (ratios):* traditional *pūraka : kumbhaka : rechaka* ratios (e.g., 1:4:2) and counts vary by lineage; the *Gheraṇḍa Saṃhitā* and *Śiva Saṃhitā* give differing techniques and cautions. [folk] [late text]
  - *Caution motif:* Haṭha manuals warn that ungraded *prāṇāyāma* (especially forceful retention) can disturb the airs and cause illness — practice is to be staged and ideally guru-guided. (HYP 2.16.) [folk]
