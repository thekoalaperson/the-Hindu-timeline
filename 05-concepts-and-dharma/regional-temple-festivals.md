# Regional Temple Festivals & Jatras (beyond the pan-Hindu calendar)

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Concepts & Dharma](README.md) → **Regional Temple Festivals & Jatras**. The *localized* festival layer — temple *utsavas*, processional *jatras/yatras*, deity-possession rites and harvest/new-year *sankranti* feasts that are tied to a **specific temple, goddess, or region** rather than to the pan-Hindu lunar calendar catalogued elsewhere.

Puri)](../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md), [51 Shakti Peethas](../06-sacred-geography/51-shakti-peethas.md), [Sthala Puranas of Major Temples](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md)

This file catalogues the **regional / temple-bound** festival stratum that [festivals-and-vratas.md](festivals-and-vratas.md) deliberately leaves out: the great processional *yatras* and South-Indian temple *brahmotsavas*, the Kerala *poorams* and *theyyam*, the Tamil Murugan *kavadi* and goddess-wedding *utsavas*, the Goddess *jatras* of the Deccan, the Shakta menstruation-fair of the Northeast, Bengal's year-end Gajan–Charak austerity cycle, and the solar harvest / new-year cluster. Two distinctions run throughout, per project policy: (1) **scripture-date vs. observance-date** — most charter-myths are Puranic [traditional], while the *attested practice* of these regional festivals is usually medieval-to-modern (kingly foundations of the 16th–19th c. recur strikingly); (2) **deity-charter vs. agrarian/folk substrate** — many are harvest or fertility rites later mapped onto a Puranic deity. Where a festival has multiple origin-stories all are catalogued as nested variants. Rosters of participating deities/temples (Kullu's "300+ devtas," Kerala's many *kavus*) are **open-ended** and flagged.

## I. The great processional Yatras — gods on chariots & palanquins

- **Jagannath Rath Yatra (Puri, Odisha)** - the chariot festival on *Ashadha Shukla Dvitiya* (Jun–Jul) when **Jagannath** (a form of Krishna/Vishnu), his elder brother **Balabhadra** and sister **Subhadra** — plus the **Sudarshana Chakra** — are pulled in three towering wooden *rathas* from the [Jagannath temple](../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) ~3 km to the **Gundicha Temple**, residing a week before the return (*Bahuda Yatra*). One of the four [Char Dham](../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md). Deity = Jagannath/[Krishna](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md). (Wikipedia, *Ratha Yatra (Puri)*; Britannica)
  - *Variant (Indradyumna / Skanda Purana charter):* King **Indradyumna**, instructed in a dream, recovers Krishna's imperishable remains (or a sacred *daru*/log washed ashore) and has **Vishvakarma** carve the three deities from neem (*daru-brahma*); the carving was left unfinished (hence the deities' stub-armed form). [traditional] (Skanda Purana tradition; Om Spiritual Shop)
  - *Variant (Subhadra's wish):* the yatra commemorates **Subhadra's** desire to visit **Dvaraka**/her natal home, her brothers escorting her — alt., Jagannath's own annual wish to visit his birthplace. [folk]
  - *Variant (Nabakalebara):* every **12–19 years** (when an *Adhika Ashadha* falls) the wooden bodies are ritually replaced — the "new embodiment," the *Brahma-padartha* (soul-substance) transferred in secret. [traditional]
  - *Note:* the chariot-procession gave English the word "juggernaut." [scholarly]
  - *Prelude — Chandan Yatra / Chandana Yatra:* the **21-day sandal-paste boat festival** opening the Jagannath ritual year on *Akshaya Tritiya* (Apr–May), when the **representative deities** — **Madanmohan** (the *chalanti pratima* of Jagannath) with **Sri Devi** and **Bhu Devi**, and the **Pancha-Pandavas** (five Shiva *lingas*) — are rowed on decorated boats (*chapas*) on the **Narendra tank** and anointed with cooling **sandal-paste** (*chandana*) against the Odishan summer heat; the same Akshaya Tritiya day begins the building of the chariots for the **Rath Yatra**. This is the **first of the bathing-and-anointing overtures** to the chariot festival catalogued above. [traditional] (Wikipedia, *Chandan Yatra*; Jagannath Temple Puri)
  - *Prelude — Snana Yatra / Snana Purnima (Deva-snana Purnima):* on **Jyeshtha Purnima** (May–Jun) the principal **Jagannath, Balabhadra and Subhadra** are brought to the open **Snana Bedi** (bathing altar) for the **Mahasnana**, a public ceremonial bath poured from **108 pots** of scented water — held to be the deities' "birthday" bath; on this day they are given the **Gajavesha** (*Gaja-anana*), the **elephant-form (Ganesha) darshan**. After the bath the deities ritually **"fall ill" with fever** (*jvara*) and pass into the **Anavasara / Anasara** seclusion — kept out of public view for ~15 days, treated by the *daita* servitors with herbal remedies (*phuluri tela* etc.) — reappearing only at the **Nava-yauvana / Netrotsava** darshan just before the **Rath Yatra**, completing the **bathe → sicken → seclude → re-emerge** arc that the chariot festival climaxes. [traditional] (Wikipedia, *Snana Yatra*, *Anavasara*; Jagannath Temple Puri)
- **Kullu Dussehra (Himachal Pradesh)** - a 7-day *deva-jatra* in the Kullu valley **beginning** on Vijayadashami (when North-Indian Dussehra ends — see [festivals-and-vratas.md](festivals-and-vratas.md#i-autumn--sharad-cluster)); **300+ village devtas/devis** are carried in palanquins to Dhalpur maidan to pay homage to **Raghunath ji** (Rama), the valley's sovereign deity. (Wikipedia, *Kullu Dussehra*; utsav.gov.in)
  - *Variant (Raja Jagat Singh, 17th c.):* as penance for a curse, King **Jagat Singh** installed an idol of **Raghunath** (brought from Ayodhya) as the valley's true king and ruled as his regent — the festival's historical foundation. [scholarly]
  - *Note:* the village-deity roster is **open-ended** and varies yearly; **Hadimba** (Bhima's consort, the valley's grandmother-goddess) is the senior devi whose nod opens the rath. [folk]
- **Bastar Dussehra (Chhattisgarh)** - claimed the **world's longest festival (~75 days)**, with **no Ravana-dahan**; instead it honours the tribal guardian-goddess **Danteshwari** (a [Shakti Peetha](../06-sacred-geography/51-shakti-peethas.md) deity) and the clan goddess **Maoli**, drawing hundreds of forest deities to Jagdalpur. Deity = Danteshwari/Devi. (Outlook; Deccan Chronicle; Village Square)
  - *Variant (Kakatiya/Chalukya foundation, ~15th–16th c.):* instituted by the Bastar ruling house; its rites (*Kachhan Gaadi*, *Rath Parikrama*, *Muria Darbar*) are a **tribal–royal compact**, not the Rama–Ravana myth. [scholarly]
- **Gangaur (Rajasthan)** - 16/18-day spring *jatra* from the day after Holi through *Chaitra Shukla Tritiya*; images of **Gauri** (Parvati) and **Isar** (Shiva) are dressed and processed, celebrating marital fidelity, harvest and conjugal blessedness; unmarried girls pray for good husbands, married women for their spouses' long life. Deity = Gauri/[Parvati](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md). (Wikipedia, *Gangaur*; utsav.gov.in)
  - *Variant (Gauri returns to Kailasa):* the procession enacts **Gauri** visiting her natal home and being escorted back to **Shiva**, her farewell on the final day mirroring a bride's *vidai*. [traditional]
- **Tirumala-Tirupati Brahmotsavam (Salakatla Brahmotsavam, Andhra)** - the great **nine-day Vaishnava *utsavam*** of **Venkateshwara** (Balaji, a form of Vishnu) at [Tirumala](../06-sacred-geography/tirumala-tirupati-venkateshwara.md), said to have been **founded by Brahma himself** (hence *Brahma-utsava*) — the **paradigm of the South Indian *brahmotsava* form** replicated across Vaishnava temples; the processional *utsava-murti* **Malayappa Swami** is taken out daily on a sequence of *vahanas* (mounts), drawing millions on Garuda Seva day. Deity = Venkateshwara/Vishnu. (Wikipedia, *Tirumala Venkateswara Temple*; TTD)
  - *Variant (Venkatachala Mahatmya / Varaha Purana charter):* **Brahma** is said to have first performed the *utsavam* on Venkatachala hill to thank Vishnu for descending as Venkateshwara in the **Kali Yuga**; the rite follows **Vaikhanasa / Pancharatra Agama** prescription (*ankurarpana* sprouting-rite, *dhvajarohana* flag-hoisting opening it). [traditional]
  - *Note (vahana sequence):* the daily mounts run **Pedda Sesha → Chinna Sesha → Hamsa (swan) → Simha (lion) → Muthyapu Pandiri (pearl-canopy) → Kalpavriksha → Sarvabhupala → Mohini → Garuda Seva (the climactic eagle-mount) → Hanumanta → Gaja (elephant) → Surya-prabha & Chandra-prabha (sun and moon discs) → swarna ratham (golden chariot) →** culminating in **Rathotsavam** (the great wooden *ratha*), the *Aswa* (horse) *vahana* and the **Chakra-snanam** (the Sudarshana disc's purificatory bath at the *Pushkarini* tank, *avabhrutha*). The roster is regionally **fixed at Tirumala** but the order varies in derivative temple brahmotsavas. [traditional]

## II. Kerala — Poorams, Pongala & the dancing gods

- **Thrissur Pooram (Kerala)** - the "Pooram of all poorams," held on the *Pooram* asterism in *Medam* (Apr–May) and centred on the **Vadakkunnathan** ([Shiva](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md)) temple; two factions — **Paramekkavu** and **Thiruvambadi** Bhagavathi temples — lead rival processions of ~30 caparisoned elephants, climaxing in *Kudamattam* (the rhythmic exchange of ornamental parasols) and *panchavadyam* drumming. Presiding deity = Shiva (Vadakkunnathan); processional deities = the two Bhagavathis (Devi). (Wikipedia, *Thrissur Pooram*; *Vadakkunnathan Temple*)
  - *Variant (Sakthan Thampuran, ~1796):* founded by **Raja Rama Varma "Sakthan Thampuran"** of Cochin after the smaller temples were barred from the Aarattupuzha Pooram; he organized the ten temples around Vadakkunnathan into a unified secular spectacle. [scholarly]
  - *Note (Kerala pooram cluster):* Thrissur is the apex of a wider **pooram** tradition — **Aarattupuzha Pooram** (claimed oldest, the "gathering of gods"), **Peruvanam Pooram**, **Nenmara Vallanghy Vela**, **Chinakkathoor**, **Uthralikavu** — an **open-ended** regional roster of temple *vela/pooram* festivals built on elephant pageantry. [folk]
- **Attukal Pongala (Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala)** - on the *Pongala* day (full moon of *Makaram–Kumbham*, Feb–Mar) **millions of women** cook *pongala* (sweet rice-jaggery porridge) in earthen pots on makeshift hearths over ~5–7 km around the **Attukal Bhagavathy** temple, offering it to **Attukal Amma** — a **Guinness-recorded** women-only gathering (4M+). Deity = Attukal Bhagavathy (Devi/Bhadrakali). (Wikipedia, *Attukal Pongala*; keralafolklore.com)
  - *Variant (Kannagi charter):* Attukal Amma is identified with **Kannagi**, heroine of the Tamil epic *Silappatikaram*, who burned Madurai in vengeance; the temple is "Sabarimala of women." [traditional]
- **Theyyam / Kaliyattam (North Malabar — Kannur, Kasaragod)** - not a calendar festival but a **deity-possession ritual season** (roughly *Thulam* to *Edavam*, Oct–Jun) performed in *kavus* (sacred groves) and family shrines; after 8–10 hours of song (*thottam*) and elaborate costume/makeup, the placing of the *mudi* (headdress) marks the **god entering the dancer**, who is then venerated as the living deity across caste lines. (Wikipedia, *Theyyam*; Kerala Tourism)
  - *Variant (open-ended pantheon):* there are **400+ named theyyams** — among them **Muthappan** (a Shiva–Vishnu hunter-god of Parassinikkadavu), **Gulikan** (a Yama/Kala aspect), **Pottan Theyyam** (a casteless Shiva who rebukes caste), **Vishnumoorthi** (Narasimha), and many *veera* (hero) and *amma* (goddess) theyyams; the roster is regionally **open-ended** and includes deified ancestors, heroes and folk deities. [folk]
  - *Note:* theyyam preserves a **pre-Brahmanical / Dravidian substrate** of village-deity and ancestor worship later syncretized with Puranic figures. [scholarly]

## IIb. Tamil Nadu — Murugan, the goddess-wedding & the festival of lamps

- **Thaipusam (Tamil Nadu & diaspora)** - the most globally visible **Murugan** ([Kartikeya/Skanda](../06-sacred-geography/arupadai-veedu-murugan.md)) festival, on the **Pusam** asterism (*Pushya nakshatra*) of the Tamil month **Thai** (Jan–Feb), when the moon stands in Pushya; a penitential pilgrimage of vow-fulfilment marked by the carrying of the **kavadi** (an arched burden-frame) and ascetic body-piercing — *vel*-skewers through cheek and tongue, hooks in the back — by devotees in trance after long fasting. Deity = Murugan. (Wikipedia, *Thaipusam*; Britannica)
  - *Variant (Parvati's Vel / Surapadma charter):* the day commemorates **Parvati** giving **Murugan** the **Vel** (divine lance) with which he slew the *asura* **Surapadma / Soorapadman** at [Tiruchendur](../06-sacred-geography/arupadai-veedu-murugan.md) — the *vel*-piercing re-enacting the weapon's victory. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Idumban / origin of the kavadi):* the sage **Agastya** sent his disciple **Idumban** to carry two hills (**Sivagiri** and **Saktigiri**) south on a shoulder-pole; at **Palani** the boy-Murugan (as the renunciate **Dandayudhapani**) sat on one hill and could not be moved, and slew Idumban in the ensuing fight before reviving him — the shoulder-pole becoming the prototype **kavadi**, and Idumban the guardian whose shrine pilgrims salute first. [traditional]
  - *Note (mass pilgrimage & diaspora):* the great Thaipusam centres are **Palani** and **Tiruchendur** (two of the [Arupadai Veedu](../06-sacred-geography/arupadai-veedu-murugan.md) "six abodes"), and across the Tamil diaspora the festival is huge at **Batu Caves (Malaysia)**, **Singapore**, **Mauritius** and the Caribbean — among the largest Hindu gatherings outside India. [scholarly]
- **Madurai Chithirai Thiruvizha (Tamil Nadu)** - the **month-long Chithirai festival** at the **Madurai Meenakshi** ([Meenakshi Amman](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md)) temple (Apr–May), enacting the **Meenakshi Tirukalyanam** — the celestial **wedding of the fish-eyed goddess Meenakshi** to **Shiva as Sundareswarar**, the goddess first crowned as **Pandya sovereign** before her marriage; fused with a Vaishnava strand drawing **over a million** in a rare Shaiva–Vaishnava civic confluence. Presiding deities = Meenakshi (Devi) + Sundareswarar (Shiva); processional = Kallazhagar (Vishnu). (Wikipedia, *Chithirai Thiruvizha*, *Meenakshi Temple*; Britannica)
  - *Variant (Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam / Halasya Mahatmya charter):* the wedding is one of Shiva's **64 *tiruvilaiyadal*** (sacred sports) at Madurai; Meenakshi is born from the Pandya king's *yajna* with three breasts (the third vanishing when she meets Shiva), conquers the directions, and weds Sundareswarar who reigns over Madurai. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Kallazhagar / Alagar crossing):* **Kallazhagar** (Vishnu, the **bride's brother**) sets out from **Alagar Koil** to give away Meenakshi, but **arrives late** and, finding the wedding done, turns back at the river **Vaigai** — his entry into the river (*Alagar's Vaigai crossing*) is the festival's great Vaishnava climax, knitting the two sects into one civic event. [traditional]
- **Karthigai Deepam (Tamil Nadu)** - the ancient Tamil **festival of lamps** (held in Tamil reckoning to **predate Diwali**), on the **Krittika** asterism of the month **Karthikai** (Nov–Dec), observed with rows of **agal** (earthen) lamps; it climaxes in the giant ghee-beacon **Maha / Bharani Deepam** lit atop **Arunachala** ([Tiruvannamalai](../06-sacred-geography/pancha-bhuta-sthalams.md), the *Agni* fire-element *sthalam*), re-enacting **Shiva's infinite pillar of fire** (*Lingodbhava*). Deity = Shiva (Arunachaleswarar); also Kartikeya. (Wikipedia, *Karthikai Deepam*; Britannica)
  - *Variant (Lingodbhava / pillar of fire):* when **Brahma** and **Vishnu** disputed supremacy, Shiva appeared as an **endless column of fire** whose top and base neither could find; Arunachala hill **is** that fiery *linga*, and the hilltop beacon is its annual manifestation. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Krittika / six mothers):* the *Krittika* asterism ties the day to **Kartikeya/Murugan**, nursed by the **six Pleiades-mothers** (*Krittikas*) and thereby six-faced (*Shanmukha*); the lamps also honour his birth-star. [traditional]
- **Panguni Uthiram (Tamil Nadu)** - the great **day of divine marriages**, on the **Uttara-phalguni (Uthiram)** asterism at the **full moon of Tamil Panguni** (Mar–Apr), when a striking cluster of celestial weddings is solemnized across South Indian temples on a single day: **Shiva–Meenakshi/Parvati**, **Murugan–Deivanai**, **Rama–Sita**, and **Andal–Ranganatha** (the *Vaishnava bride-saint* united with [Ranganatha](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md)); also one of the **two great kavadi days** (with **Thaipusam**) at the [Murugan abodes](../06-sacred-geography/arupadai-veedu-murugan.md). Deities = Shiva+Parvati / Murugan+Deivanai / Rama+Sita / Andal+Ranganatha. (Wikipedia, *Panguni Uthiram*; Tamil temple calendars)
  - *Variant (Tirukalyanam convergence):* the day's significance is that **Uttara-phalguni**, ruled by **Bhaga/Aryaman**, is the *nakshatra* of marital union — hence the deities' *tirukalyanam* (sacred weddings) are clustered on it; at **Tiruvannamalai** and the **Arupadai Veedu** temples it is a major *kavadi* and float (*teppam*) occasion. [traditional]
- **Skanda Shashti / Soorasamharam (Tamil Nadu)** - a **six-day fast** in the Tamil month **Aippasi** (Oct–Nov, from new moon to the sixth lunar day, *Shashthi*), the foremost **Murugan** war-festival, peer to **Thaipusam**; it climaxes in the **Soorasamharam** — the dramatic **seashore re-enactment of Murugan slaying the *asura* Surapadma** (*Soorapadman*) — staged most famously at **Tiruchendur** ([Arupadai Veedu](../06-sacred-geography/arupadai-veedu-murugan.md)), followed the next day by the **Tirukalyanam**, Murugan's wedding to **Deivanai**. Among the largest Tamil Murugan festivals. Deity = Murugan. (Wikipedia, *Skanda Sashti*, *Soorasamharam*; Tiruchendur Murugan Temple)
  - *Variant (Surapadma / the peacock & cock charter):* **Surapadma**, split in two by the **Vel**, is transformed — one half becoming Murugan's **peacock mount** (*mayil*) and the other his **cock-banner** (*seval kodi*) — so the *asura* is not annihilated but absorbed into the god's insignia; the six days re-enact the six-day war. [traditional]
  - *Note (Kanda Sashti Kavasam):* the festival is bound up with the recitation of the **Kanda Sashti Kavasam**, a popular protective hymn to Murugan. [folk]
- **Vaikuntha Ekadashi / Adhyayana Utsava (Srirangam, Tirupati & the Divya Desams)** - the foremost **Sri Vaishnava temple-utsava** of **Margazhi** (Dec–Jan), when the **Paramapada Vasal** (the *gate of Vaikuntha* / "heaven's-gate," normally shut all year) is **opened** for devotees to pass through behind the processional Vishnu; it falls within the **20-day Adhyayana Utsava** ("festival of recitation") — **Pagal Pathu** (the ten *daytime* days) and **Ra Pathu** (the ten *night* days) of chanting the **Nalayira Divya Prabandham** (the 4,000 Tamil hymns of the [Alvars](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md)) before the deity, climaxing in the **Iyarpa Sattrumurai** (concluding recital) when Vishnu honours **Nammalvar** in the *Moksha*-granting **Nammalvar Moksham**. Deity = Ranganatha / Venkateshwara (Vishnu). (Wikipedia, *Vaikuntha Ekadashi*, *Adhyayana Utsavam*; Srirangam Temple)
  - *Variant (Vishnu opens Vaikuntha):* on **Mokshada/Vaikuntha Ekadashi** the gates of **Vaikuntha** are held to stand open and the devout who pass the **Paramapada Vasal** attain liberation — the *temple* enactment of the *heaven's-gate* myth. [traditional]
  - *Distinction:* this is the **temple-utsava** dimension (Paramapada Vasal + Adhyayana recitation at the Divya Desams) of the same lunar day whose **pan-Hindu *vrata*** — *Mokshada / Vaikuntha Ekadashi* fasting — is catalogued in [festivals-and-vratas.md](festivals-and-vratas.md); the *vrata* is observed everywhere, the **gate-opening utsava** is the distinctive **Sri Vaishnava / Tamil** form. [scholarly]
  - *Cross-link:* peer Tamil temple-utsavas in this file — **Thaipusam**, **Skanda Shashti** and **Madurai Chithirai Thiruvizha** — and the [Arupadai Veedu](../06-sacred-geography/arupadai-veedu-murugan.md) Murugan abodes.

## III. The Deccan Goddess — Telangana's flower & food jatras

- **Bonalu (Telangana)** - a *Mother-Goddess* thanksgiving in *Ashadha* (Jul–Aug) when women carry **bonam** (cooked rice with milk/jaggery in decorated pots) on their heads to temples of **Mahankali / Yellamma / Pochamma**; features the *Pothuraju* (the goddess's fierce brother-protector) and *Rangam* (oracular trance prophecy). Deity = Mahankali (Devi). (Wikipedia, *Bonalu*; Pragyata)
  - *Variant (1813 plague charter):* a Hyderabad military battalion at **Ujjain** vowed to the Mahakali there that they would install her idol if she lifted a **plague** back home; on her deliverance they founded the **Ujjaini Mahakali** temple — origin of the **Golconda/Lal Darwaza Bonalu** cycle. [scholarly]
- **Bathukamma (Telangana)** - a 9-day **floral** festival in *Ashvayuja* (Sep–Oct), overlapping Navaratri and ending the day before Dussehra; women build conical tiered **flower-stacks** ("Bathukamma," *come-alive Mother-Goddess*) honouring **Gauri/Maha Gauri**, dancing in circles around them before immersing them in water. Deity = Gauri (Devi). (CSIS; MyTelangana)
  - *Variant (Sati / Gauri):* the name invokes the Goddess "come alive" — variously linked to **Gauri** as life-giver and to a princess legend; primarily an **agrarian–monsoon fertility** rite mapped onto the Devi. [folk]
  - *Cross-link:* Bathukamma's Navaratri timing places it alongside the pan-Hindu [Navaratri / Durga Puja](festivals-and-vratas.md#i-autumn--sharad-cluster) cluster as its distinctively Telangana regional form.

## IV. Shakta, Shaiva & Surya fairs of the East/Northeast

- **Ambubachi Mela (Kamakhya, Guwahati, Assam)** - a 4-day Tantric *mela* in *Ahar* (mid-Jun) marking the **annual menstruation of the goddess Kamakhya**; the [Kamakhya Shakti Peetha](../06-sacred-geography/51-shakti-peethas.md) (where Sati's *yoni* fell) closes its doors for 3 days while the Earth-Mother is impure-yet-fertile, reopening on the 4th to vast crowds of Tantrics, *sadhus* and *aghoris*; the red-stained *raktavastra* cloth is distributed as *prasada*. Deity = Kamakhya (Devi/Shakti). (Wikipedia, *Ambubachi Mela*; OpIndia)
  - *Variant (Sati's yoni):* uniquely among festivals it **sacralizes menstruation** as cosmic fertility — a Shakta counter-narrative to menstrual taboo. [scholarly]
- **Chhath Puja (Bihar, Jharkhand, E-UP, Nepal)** - a rigorous 4-day *vrata* (Oct–Nov, *Kartik Shukla Shashthi*) of fasting, river-standing and offering *arghya* to the **setting and rising Sun** ([Surya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md)) and **Chhathi Maiya** (Surya's sister/Usha, protector of children); one of the few festivals worshipping the **setting** sun. Deity = Surya + Chhathi Maiya. (Wikipedia, *Chhath*; Britannica; Sahapedia)
  - *Variant (Vedic Surya-worship):* traced to Rigvedic solar hymns; the *vratis* recall sages who worshipped Surya for vitality and healing. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Priyavrata / Karna / Draupadi):* alt. charters — King **Priyavrata** (son of Svayambhuva Manu) restored a stillborn child via Chhathi Maiya; **Karna** (Surya's son) instituted sun-worship; the **Pandavas/Draupadi** performed it to regain their kingdom. [folk]
- **Gajan & Charak Puja (Bengal)** - the Bengali **year-end Shaiva austerity cycle**, filling the closing days of **Chaitra** and climaxing on **Chaitra Sankranti** (~Apr 13–14, the last day of the Bengali year, eve of *Pohela Boishakh* — the solar clock of section V); ordinary householders take **temporary renunciation** as **sannyasis / bhoktas** — sacred thread of the vow, fasting, celibacy, sleeping on the ground, alms-begging — to serve **Shiva** through the year's turn. Deity = Shiva (also Nil; also Dharma Thakur, below). (Wikipedia, *Gajan (festival)*, *Charak Puja*; Banglapedia)
  - *Variant (Shiva's wedding charter):* the sannyasis are held to become **Shiva's *ganas* and *bhuta-preta*** (goblin retinue) attending his **year-end wedding to Gauri / Harakali** — the austerities are the wedding-party's service, and the festival's songs are the wedding-songs. [folk]
  - *Charak (hook-swinging) & ban-piercing:* on the Sankranti day a stripped **Charak tree** (pole) is raised and vow-takers **swing suspended from iron hooks passed through the flesh of the back**, alongside ***ban-phonra*** (iron skewers through tongue, cheeks or sides), fire-walking and thorn-jumping — ordeal-worship offered for boons of children, cure and harvest; the hook-swinging was **suppressed by the colonial Bengal government (1865)** yet persists in places. [folk] [scholarly]
  - *Shiber Gajan (folk-drama):* gajan troupes perform song-dramas of **Shiva as a poor Bengali peasant** — his ploughing, his poverty, his quarrels and his **rustic marriage** with Gauri — the high god domesticated into the agrarian household; kin to the **Gambhira** masked-performance tradition of Malda. [folk] [regional]
  - *Nil Puja / Nil Shashthi:* on the **eve of the Sankranti** Bengali women **fast until the evening lamp-offering to Nil (Nilakantha Shiva)** for the **welfare of their children**, while roving *nil-sannyasis* carry the Nil icon door-to-door singing of **Nil's marriage** — the domestic-vrata strand of the same cycle. [folk] [regional]
  - *Variant (Dharma-er Gajan):* the identical gajan complex — ascetic *bhaktas*, *ghat* rites, hook-swinging, *ban-phonra* — is also performed for the aniconic folk god **[Dharma Thakur](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dharma-thakur.md)** in the western Rarh (typically later, *Baisakh–Jyaishtha*), with a **Dom (non-Brahmin) priesthood**; which cult's gajan is the older template is debated. [folk] [scholarly] [disputed]
  - *Note (two clocks):* the charter is the timeless Shiva-wedding myth [traditional]; the attested festival is medieval-to-modern Bengal, its Charak ordeals documented — and legislated against — in the colonial record. [scholarly]

## V. The Solar New-Year & Harvest cluster (Sankranti family)

These share a single astronomical anchor — the **Sun's zodiacal entry** (*sankranti*) — so they fall on fixed solar dates (~Apr 13–15 for the Mesha/Aries family; mid-Jan for the Makara/Capricorn family), unlike the lunar festivals of [festivals-and-vratas.md](festivals-and-vratas.md). See [Units of Time / sankranti](../00-time-and-cosmology/units-of-time.md).

- **Pongal / Thai Pongal (Tamil Nadu)** - a 4-day **harvest** festival from the last day of *Margazhi* into *Thai* (mid-Jan), coinciding with **Makara Sankranti** and the start of **Uttarayana** (the Sun's northward journey); thanksgiving to **Surya** and to cattle. Days: *Bhogi* (bonfire of the old), *Thai Pongal* (boiling-over of new rice as prosperity-omen), *Mattu Pongal* (cattle-honoring), *Kaanum Pongal* (kin-visiting). Deity = Surya. (Wikipedia, *Pongal*; Britannica)
  - *Cross-link:* the pan-Indian Makara Sankranti family (Lohri, Uttarayan, Magh Bihu, Makar Sankranti) — see [festivals-and-vratas.md](festivals-and-vratas.md).
- **Gudi Padwa / Ugadi / Yugadi (Maharashtra; Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra)** - the **lunisolar New Year** on *Chaitra Shukla Pratipada* (Mar–Apr); in Maharashtra a **Gudi** (bamboo pole topped with a bright cloth, neem, garland and inverted vessel) is raised for victory/prosperity; in the South **Ugadi** is marked by *Ugadi pachadi* (six-taste paste, life's mixed flavours) and *Panchanga-shravanam* (year-almanac reading). Deity/charter = Brahma's creation. (Wikipedia, *Gudi Padwa*; prokerala)
  - *Variant (Brahma creates time):* the day **Brahma created the universe and began *kala* (time)** — *Yuga-adi*, "beginning of the age"; see [Cosmology / creation](../00-time-and-cosmology/README.md). [traditional]
  - *Variant (Rama / Shalivahana):* alt. charters — **Rama's** coronation/return, or **Shalivahana's** victory and the start of the *Shalivahana Shaka* era; the Gudi as a **dhvaja** (banner of triumph). [folk]
- **Vishu (Kerala)** - the **Malayalam solar New Year** at **Mesha Sankranti** (mid-Apr); centred on the *Vishukkani* — an auspicious arrangement (golden *konna* flowers, rice, fruit, gold, a Krishna icon and mirror) that must be the **first sight** on waking, setting the year's fortune. Deity = Vishnu/Krishna (commonly via Guruvayur/Sabarimala). (hinduinside.com; Wikipedia, *Vaisakhi*-family)
- **Baisakhi / Vaisakhi (Punjab)** - **Mesha Sankranti** (~Apr 13–14) **harvest** + Hindu solar New Year, anchored to the Sun's entry into **Aries**; for Sikhs also the 1699 founding of the **Khalsa** by Guru Gobind Singh [Sikh tradition]. Deity = solar/agrarian; pan-regional thanksgiving. (Wikipedia, *Vaisakhi*; Sanskriti)
- **Bohag / Rongali Bihu (Assam)** - the chief of Assam's **three Bihus**, at **Mesha Sankranti**; a 7-day spring/New-Year feast with **Goru Bihu** (cattle-worship), *Bihu* dance, and *pitha/laru* feasting — an **agrarian** rite lightly mapped onto solar reckoning. Deity = agrarian/solar (cattle & fertility focus). (realshepower; hinduinside.com)
  - *Note (Bihu trio):* **Bohag/Rongali** (spring, Apr), **Kati/Kongali** (autumn, austere), **Magh/Bhogali** (winter harvest, feasting) — the three are seasonal, only Bohag being the New Year. [regional]
- **Cross-regional note:** the Mesha-Sankranti New Year is observed under many names — **Puthandu** (Tamil), **Pohela Boishakh** (Bengal), **Pana Sankranti** (Odisha), **Jur Sital** (Mithila), **Vishu** (Kerala) — an **open-ended** family sharing one solar trigger. [scholarly]
- **River Pushkaram / Pushkaralu (Deccan & South India)** - a **12-yearly river-bathing fair** anchored not to the Sun but to **Brihaspati (Jupiter)**: each of **twelve sacred rivers** is worshipped for ~**12 days** when **Jupiter transits into that river's corresponding zodiacal sign** (*rashi*) — the festival **begins on the day of Jupiter's entry** (*Pushkara* commencement). The **Godavari Pushkaram** (Jupiter in **Simha/Leo**) and **Krishna Pushkaram** (Jupiter in **Kanya/Virgo**) draw crowds **rivalling the Kumbh Mela**, with mass *snana* (ritual bathing), *tarpana* for ancestors, and riverbank fairs. (Wikipedia, *Pushkaram*; Andhra/Telangana temple endowments)
  - *Variant (Pushkara / Brihaspati charter):* the *deva* **Pushkara** — granted by **Shiva** (var. **Brahma**) the power to **sanctify waters** — was sought by **Brihaspati**; the two agreed that **Pushkara would enter a river to charge it with full *tirtha*-power for twelve days whenever Brihaspati moved into the river's *rashi***, so that the bathing-merit of that river peaks on its Pushkaram. [traditional]
  - *Note (12-river ↔ 12-rashi mapping):* the twelve rivers paired to the twelve signs are conventionally — **Mesha**→Ganga, **Vrishabha**→Narmada, **Mithuna**→Saraswati, **Karka**→Yamuna, **Simha**→**Godavari**, **Kanya**→**Krishna**, **Tula**→Kaveri, **Vrishchika**→Bhima, **Dhanu**→Tapi/Pushkaravahini, **Makara**→Tungabhadra, **Kumbha**→Sindhu, **Mina**→Pranhita (the roster of minor rivers varies by tradition and is **open-ended**). [traditional]
  - *Note (Adi & Antya Pushkaram):* the **first 12 days** of Jupiter's transit are the **Adi Pushkaram** (most auspicious opening) and the **last 12 days** before Jupiter leaves the sign are the **Antya Pushkaram** (closing) — so each river's cycle is book-ended by these two especially meritorious windows. [traditional]
  - *Frame:* a **Jupiter-anchored cyclic bathing-fair** standing alongside the **solar/Sankranti** harvest cluster above and the **lunar/Kumbh Mela** *parva* — three different astronomical clocks (Sun, Jupiter, Sun-Moon-Jupiter conjunction) each generating a great Indian bathing tradition. [scholarly]

## VI. Patterns across the regional layer

- **Kingly foundations recur** - Thrissur Pooram (Sakthan Thampuran, ~1796), Kullu Dussehra (Jagat Singh, 17th c.), Bastar Dussehra (Bastar house), Jagannath (Indradyumna myth + Ganga/Gajapati patronage) — many regional festivals are **medieval-to-early-modern royal institutions**, distinct from their ancient charter-myths. [scholarly]
- **Temple-foundation & sovereignty charters (South)** - the Tamil/Andhra *utsavas* fuse deity-myth with **kingship of the deity over the city**: Meenakshi crowned **Pandya sovereign** before her wedding, Venkateshwara's **Brahma-founded** *brahmotsavam*, Arunachala-as-fire-*linga* — the temple-foundation layer (god-as-king of the *sthala*) is as load-bearing as the cosmic myth, per file policy. [scholarly]
- **Goddess + protector pairing** - Bonalu's *Pothuraju*, Bastar's clan goddesses, theyyam's *amma* figures: the Deccan/Malabar village-Goddess complex pairs a fierce mother with a male guardian — a substrate older than Puranic Devi theology. [scholarly]
- **Agrarian core, Puranic veneer** - Pongal, Bihu, Bathukamma, Baisakhi are at root **harvest/fertility** rites later attached to Surya, Gauri or Brahma; the two-clock distinction (folk substrate vs. scriptural charter) is sharpest here. [scholarly]
- **Open-ended rosters [flagged]** - the participating-deity lists for Kullu (300+ devtas), theyyam (400+ forms), and the Kerala pooram/vela circuit are regionally variable and not closed; counts above are indicative, not canonical. [folk]
