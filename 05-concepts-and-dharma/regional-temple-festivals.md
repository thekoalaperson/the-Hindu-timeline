# Regional Temple Festivals & Jatras (beyond the pan-Hindu calendar)

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Concepts & Dharma](README.md) → **Regional Temple Festivals & Jatras**. The *localized* festival layer — temple *utsavas*, processional *jatras/yatras*, deity-possession rites and harvest/new-year *sankranti* feasts that are tied to a **specific temple, goddess, or region** rather than to the pan-Hindu lunar calendar catalogued elsewhere.

Puri)](../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md), [51 Shakti Peethas](../06-sacred-geography/51-shakti-peethas.md), [Sthala Puranas of Major Temples](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md)

This file catalogues the **regional / temple-bound** festival stratum that [festivals-and-vratas.md](festivals-and-vratas.md) deliberately leaves out: the great processional *yatras*, the Kerala *poorams* and *theyyam*, the Goddess *jatras* of the Deccan, the Shakta menstruation-fair of the Northeast, and the solar harvest / new-year cluster. Two distinctions run throughout, per project policy: (1) **scripture-date vs. observance-date** — most charter-myths are Puranic [traditional], while the *attested practice* of these regional festivals is usually medieval-to-modern (kingly foundations of the 16th–19th c. recur strikingly); (2) **deity-charter vs. agrarian/folk substrate** — many are harvest or fertility rites later mapped onto a Puranic deity. Where a festival has multiple origin-stories all are catalogued as nested variants. Rosters of participating deities/temples (Kullu's "300+ devtas," Kerala's many *kavus*) are **open-ended** and flagged.

---

## I. The great processional Yatras — gods on chariots & palanquins

- **Jagannath Rath Yatra (Puri, Odisha)** - the chariot festival on *Ashadha Shukla Dvitiya* (Jun–Jul) when **Jagannath** (a form of Krishna/Vishnu), his elder brother **Balabhadra** and sister **Subhadra** — plus the **Sudarshana Chakra** — are pulled in three towering wooden *rathas* from the [Jagannath temple](../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md) ~3 km to the **Gundicha Temple**, residing a week before the return (*Bahuda Yatra*). One of the four [Char Dham](../06-sacred-geography/char-dham-and-chota-char-dham.md). Deity = Jagannath/[Krishna](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md). (Wikipedia, *Ratha Yatra (Puri)*; Britannica)
  - *Variant (Indradyumna / Skanda Purana charter):* King **Indradyumna**, instructed in a dream, recovers Krishna's imperishable remains (or a sacred *daru*/log washed ashore) and has **Vishvakarma** carve the three deities from neem (*daru-brahma*); the carving was left unfinished (hence the deities' stub-armed form). [traditional] (Skanda Purana tradition; Om Spiritual Shop)
  - *Variant (Subhadra's wish):* the yatra commemorates **Subhadra's** desire to visit **Dvaraka**/her natal home, her brothers escorting her — alt., Jagannath's own annual wish to visit his birthplace. [folk]
  - *Variant (Nabakalebara):* every **12–19 years** (when an *Adhika Ashadha* falls) the wooden bodies are ritually replaced — the "new embodiment," the *Brahma-padartha* (soul-substance) transferred in secret. [traditional]
  - *Note:* the chariot-procession gave English the word "juggernaut." [scholarly]
- **Kullu Dussehra (Himachal Pradesh)** - a 7-day *deva-jatra* in the Kullu valley **beginning** on Vijayadashami (when North-Indian Dussehra ends — see [festivals-and-vratas.md](festivals-and-vratas.md#i-autumn--sharad-cluster)); **300+ village devtas/devis** are carried in palanquins to Dhalpur maidan to pay homage to **Raghunath ji** (Rama), the valley's sovereign deity. (Wikipedia, *Kullu Dussehra*; utsav.gov.in)
  - *Variant (Raja Jagat Singh, 17th c.):* as penance for a curse, King **Jagat Singh** installed an idol of **Raghunath** (brought from Ayodhya) as the valley's true king and ruled as his regent — the festival's historical foundation. [scholarly]
  - *Note:* the village-deity roster is **open-ended** and varies yearly; **Hadimba** (Bhima's consort, the valley's grandmother-goddess) is the senior devi whose nod opens the rath. [folk]
- **Bastar Dussehra (Chhattisgarh)** - claimed the **world's longest festival (~75 days)**, with **no Ravana-dahan**; instead it honours the tribal guardian-goddess **Danteshwari** (a [Shakti Peetha](../06-sacred-geography/51-shakti-peethas.md) deity) and the clan goddess **Maoli**, drawing hundreds of forest deities to Jagdalpur. Deity = Danteshwari/Devi. (Outlook; Deccan Chronicle; Village Square)
  - *Variant (Kakatiya/Chalukya foundation, ~15th–16th c.):* instituted by the Bastar ruling house; its rites (*Kachhan Gaadi*, *Rath Parikrama*, *Muria Darbar*) are a **tribal–royal compact**, not the Rama–Ravana myth. [scholarly]
- **Gangaur (Rajasthan)** - 16/18-day spring *jatra* from the day after Holi through *Chaitra Shukla Tritiya*; images of **Gauri** (Parvati) and **Isar** (Shiva) are dressed and processed, celebrating marital fidelity, harvest and conjugal blessedness; unmarried girls pray for good husbands, married women for their spouses' long life. Deity = Gauri/[Parvati](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md). (Wikipedia, *Gangaur*; utsav.gov.in)
  - *Variant (Gauri returns to Kailasa):* the procession enacts **Gauri** visiting her natal home and being escorted back to **Shiva**, her farewell on the final day mirroring a bride's *vidai*. [traditional]

## II. Kerala — Poorams, Pongala & the dancing gods

- **Thrissur Pooram (Kerala)** - the "Pooram of all poorams," held on the *Pooram* asterism in *Medam* (Apr–May) and centred on the **Vadakkunnathan** ([Shiva](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md)) temple; two factions — **Paramekkavu** and **Thiruvambadi** Bhagavathi temples — lead rival processions of ~30 caparisoned elephants, climaxing in *Kudamattam* (the rhythmic exchange of ornamental parasols) and *panchavadyam* drumming. Presiding deity = Shiva (Vadakkunnathan); processional deities = the two Bhagavathis (Devi). (Wikipedia, *Thrissur Pooram*; *Vadakkunnathan Temple*)
  - *Variant (Sakthan Thampuran, ~1796):* founded by **Raja Rama Varma "Sakthan Thampuran"** of Cochin after the smaller temples were barred from the Aarattupuzha Pooram; he organized the ten temples around Vadakkunnathan into a unified secular spectacle. [scholarly]
  - *Note (Kerala pooram cluster):* Thrissur is the apex of a wider **pooram** tradition — **Aarattupuzha Pooram** (claimed oldest, the "gathering of gods"), **Peruvanam Pooram**, **Nenmara Vallanghy Vela**, **Chinakkathoor**, **Uthralikavu** — an **open-ended** regional roster of temple *vela/pooram* festivals built on elephant pageantry. [folk]
- **Attukal Pongala (Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala)** - on the *Pongala* day (full moon of *Makaram–Kumbham*, Feb–Mar) **millions of women** cook *pongala* (sweet rice-jaggery porridge) in earthen pots on makeshift hearths over ~5–7 km around the **Attukal Bhagavathy** temple, offering it to **Attukal Amma** — a **Guinness-recorded** women-only gathering (4M+). Deity = Attukal Bhagavathy (Devi/Bhadrakali). (Wikipedia, *Attukal Pongala*; keralafolklore.com)
  - *Variant (Kannagi charter):* Attukal Amma is identified with **Kannagi**, heroine of the Tamil epic *Silappatikaram*, who burned Madurai in vengeance; the temple is "Sabarimala of women." [traditional]
- **Theyyam / Kaliyattam (North Malabar — Kannur, Kasaragod)** - not a calendar festival but a **deity-possession ritual season** (roughly *Thulam* to *Edavam*, Oct–Jun) performed in *kavus* (sacred groves) and family shrines; after 8–10 hours of song (*thottam*) and elaborate costume/makeup, the placing of the *mudi* (headdress) marks the **god entering the dancer**, who is then venerated as the living deity across caste lines. (Wikipedia, *Theyyam*; Kerala Tourism)
  - *Variant (open-ended pantheon):* there are **400+ named theyyams** — among them **Muthappan** (a Shiva–Vishnu hunter-god of Parassinikkadavu), **Gulikan** (a Yama/Kala aspect), **Pottan Theyyam** (a casteless Shiva who rebukes caste), **Vishnumoorthi** (Narasimha), and many *veera* (hero) and *amma* (goddess) theyyams; the roster is regionally **open-ended** and includes deified ancestors, heroes and folk deities. [folk]
  - *Note:* theyyam preserves a **pre-Brahmanical / Dravidian substrate** of village-deity and ancestor worship later syncretized with Puranic figures. [scholarly]

## III. The Deccan Goddess — Telangana's flower & food jatras

- **Bonalu (Telangana)** - a *Mother-Goddess* thanksgiving in *Ashadha* (Jul–Aug) when women carry **bonam** (cooked rice with milk/jaggery in decorated pots) on their heads to temples of **Mahankali / Yellamma / Pochamma**; features the *Pothuraju* (the goddess's fierce brother-protector) and *Rangam* (oracular trance prophecy). Deity = Mahankali (Devi). (Wikipedia, *Bonalu*; Pragyata)
  - *Variant (1813 plague charter):* a Hyderabad military battalion at **Ujjain** vowed to the Mahakali there that they would install her idol if she lifted a **plague** back home; on her deliverance they founded the **Ujjaini Mahakali** temple — origin of the **Golconda/Lal Darwaza Bonalu** cycle. [scholarly]
- **Bathukamma (Telangana)** - a 9-day **floral** festival in *Ashvayuja* (Sep–Oct), overlapping Navaratri and ending the day before Dussehra; women build conical tiered **flower-stacks** ("Bathukamma," *come-alive Mother-Goddess*) honouring **Gauri/Maha Gauri**, dancing in circles around them before immersing them in water. Deity = Gauri (Devi). (CSIS; MyTelangana)
  - *Variant (Sati / Gauri):* the name invokes the Goddess "come alive" — variously linked to **Gauri** as life-giver and to a princess legend; primarily an **agrarian–monsoon fertility** rite mapped onto the Devi. [folk]
  - *Cross-link:* Bathukamma's Navaratri timing places it alongside the pan-Hindu [Navaratri / Durga Puja](festivals-and-vratas.md#i-autumn--sharad-cluster) cluster as its distinctively Telangana regional form.

## IV. Shakta & Surya fairs of the East/Northeast

- **Ambubachi Mela (Kamakhya, Guwahati, Assam)** - a 4-day Tantric *mela* in *Ahar* (mid-Jun) marking the **annual menstruation of the goddess Kamakhya**; the [Kamakhya Shakti Peetha](../06-sacred-geography/51-shakti-peethas.md) (where Sati's *yoni* fell) closes its doors for 3 days while the Earth-Mother is impure-yet-fertile, reopening on the 4th to vast crowds of Tantrics, *sadhus* and *aghoris*; the red-stained *raktavastra* cloth is distributed as *prasada*. Deity = Kamakhya (Devi/Shakti). (Wikipedia, *Ambubachi Mela*; OpIndia)
  - *Variant (Sati's yoni):* uniquely among festivals it **sacralizes menstruation** as cosmic fertility — a Shakta counter-narrative to menstrual taboo. [scholarly]
- **Chhath Puja (Bihar, Jharkhand, E-UP, Nepal)** - a rigorous 4-day *vrata* (Oct–Nov, *Kartik Shukla Shashthi*) of fasting, river-standing and offering *arghya* to the **setting and rising Sun** ([Surya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md)) and **Chhathi Maiya** (Surya's sister/Usha, protector of children); one of the few festivals worshipping the **setting** sun. Deity = Surya + Chhathi Maiya. (Wikipedia, *Chhath*; Britannica; Sahapedia)
  - *Variant (Vedic Surya-worship):* traced to Rigvedic solar hymns; the *vratis* recall sages who worshipped Surya for vitality and healing. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Priyavrata / Karna / Draupadi):* alt. charters — King **Priyavrata** (son of Svayambhuva Manu) restored a stillborn child via Chhathi Maiya; **Karna** (Surya's son) instituted sun-worship; the **Pandavas/Draupadi** performed it to regain their kingdom. [folk]

## V. The Solar New-Year & Harvest cluster (Sankranti family)

These share a single astronomical anchor — the **Sun's zodiacal entry** (*sankranti*) — so they fall on fixed solar dates (~Apr 13–15 for the Mesha/Aries family; mid-Jan for the Makara/Capricorn family), unlike the lunar festivals of [festivals-and-vratas.md](festivals-and-vratas.md). See [Units of Time / sankranti](../00-time-and-cosmology/units-of-time.md).

- **Pongal / Thai Pongal (Tamil Nadu)** - a 4-day **harvest** festival from the last day of *Margazhi* into *Thai* (mid-Jan), coinciding with **Makara Sankranti** and the start of **Uttarayana** (the Sun's northward journey); thanksgiving to **Surya** and to cattle. Days: *Bhogi* (bonfire of the old), *Thai Pongal* (boiling-over of new rice as prosperity-omen), *Mattu Pongal* (cattle-honoring), *Kaanum Pongal* (kin-visiting). Deity = Surya. (Wikipedia, *Pongal*; Britannica)
  - *Cross-link:* the pan-Indian Makara Sankranti family (Lohri, Uttarayan, Magh Bihu, Makar Sankranti) — see [festivals-and-vratas.md](festivals-and-vratas.md).
- **Gudi Padwa / Ugadi / Yugadi (Maharashtra; Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra)** - the **lunisolar New Year** on *Chaitra Shukla Pratipada* (Mar–Apr); in Maharashtra a **Gudi** (bamboo pole topped with a bright cloth, neem, garland and inverted vessel) is raised for victory/prosperity; in the South **Ugadi** is marked by *Ugadi pachadi* (six-taste paste, life's mixed flavours) and *Panchanga-shravanam* (year-almanac reading). Deity/charter = Brahma's creation. (Wikipedia, *Gudi Padwa*; prokerala)
  - *Variant (Brahma creates time):* the day **Brahma created the universe and began *kala* (time)** — *Yuga-adi*, "beginning of the age"; see [Cosmology / creation](../00-time-and-cosmology/README.md). [traditional]
  - *Variant (Rama / Shalivahana):* alt. charters — **Rama's** coronation/return, or **Shalivahana's** victory and the start of the *Shalivahana Shaka* era; the Gudi as a **dhvaja** (banner of triumph). [folk]
- **Vishu (Kerala)** - the **Malayalam solar New Year** at **Mesha Sankranti** (mid-Apr); centred on the *Vishukkani* — an auspicious arrangement (golden *konna* flowers, rice, fruit, gold, a Krishna icon and mirror) that must be the **first sight** on waking, setting the year's fortune. Deity = Vishnu/Krishna (commonly via Guruvayur/Sabarimala). (hinduinside.com; Wikipedia, *Vaisakhi*-family)
- **Baisakhi / Vaisakhi (Punjab)** - **Mesha Sankranti** (~Apr 13–14) **harvest** + Hindu solar New Year, anchored to the Sun's entry into **Aries**; for Sikhs also the 1699 founding of the **Khalsa** by Guru Gobind Singh [Sikh tradition]. Deity = solar/agrarian; pan-regional thanksgiving. (Wikipedia, *Vaisakhi*; Sanskriti)
- **Bohag / Rongali Bihu (Assam)** - the chief of Assam's **three Bihus**, at **Mesha Sankranti**; a 7-day spring/New-Year feast with **Goru Bihu** (cattle-worship), *Bihu* dance, and *pitha/laru* feasting — an **agrarian** rite lightly mapped onto solar reckoning. Deity = agrarian/solar (cattle & fertility focus). (realshepower; hinduinside.com)
  - *Note (Bihu trio):* **Bohag/Rongali** (spring, Apr), **Kati/Kongali** (autumn, austere), **Magh/Bhogali** (winter harvest, feasting) — the three are seasonal, only Bohag being the New Year. [regional]
- **Cross-regional note:** the Mesha-Sankranti New Year is observed under many names — **Puthandu** (Tamil), **Pohela Boishakh** (Bengal), **Pana Sankranti** (Odisha), **Jur Sital** (Mithila), **Vishu** (Kerala) — an **open-ended** family sharing one solar trigger. [scholarly]

## VI. Patterns across the regional layer

- **Kingly foundations recur** - Thrissur Pooram (Sakthan Thampuran, ~1796), Kullu Dussehra (Jagat Singh, 17th c.), Bastar Dussehra (Bastar house), Jagannath (Indradyumna myth + Ganga/Gajapati patronage) — many regional festivals are **medieval-to-early-modern royal institutions**, distinct from their ancient charter-myths. [scholarly]
- **Goddess + protector pairing** - Bonalu's *Pothuraju*, Bastar's clan goddesses, theyyam's *amma* figures: the Deccan/Malabar village-Goddess complex pairs a fierce mother with a male guardian — a substrate older than Puranic Devi theology. [scholarly]
- **Agrarian core, Puranic veneer** - Pongal, Bihu, Bathukamma, Baisakhi are at root **harvest/fertility** rites later attached to Surya, Gauri or Brahma; the two-clock distinction (folk substrate vs. scriptural charter) is sharpest here. [scholarly]
- **Open-ended rosters [flagged]** - the participating-deity lists for Kullu (300+ devtas), theyyam (400+ forms), and the Kerala pooram/vela circuit are regionally variable and not closed; counts above are indicative, not canonical. [folk]
