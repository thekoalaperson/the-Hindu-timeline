# The Eighteen Tamil Siddhars (Pathinen Siddhar) & the Siddha Tradition

> Cross-cutting doctrine within [05-concepts-and-dharma](./README.md) of *The Hindu Timeline* — the **South-Indian Tamil Śaiva esoteric lineage** (*Patinen Siddhar*) and the body-immortality science they founded. Back to the [master index](../README.md).
> A parallel to the North-Indian Nāth-yogins; see the subtle-body map in [chakras-and-kundalini.md](./chakras-and-kundalini.md), the rāja/haṭha context in [yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md), the lineage frame in [../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md), and the cult-sites in [regional-temple-festivals.md](./regional-temple-festivals.md).

The **Siddhars** (Tamil *cittar*, from Skt. *siddha*, "perfected one") are a class of Tamil Śaiva mystic-physician-alchemists who pursued **deathlessness of the body** (*kāya-siddhi*) rather than escape from it — they sought to transmute the gross body into an imperishable "golden" body through yoga, alchemy (*irasavātam* / *rasavāda*), and the universal salt-elixir **muppu**, and they founded **Siddha medicine** (*Siddha Vaidya*), South India's indigenous medical system. Their canonical college is the **Patinenmar Siddhar** — "the Eighteen Siddhars" — headed by **Agastya** (Tamil *Agattiyar*). The two clocks apply sharply here: the lineage frames its founders as **deathless adepts still alive in the Himalayas / Potikai hills across yugas**, while academically the Siddhar poetic corpus is dated **c. 6th–18th c. CE**, with most alchemical texts late-medieval. [scholarly]

## 1. Who the Siddhars Are — and Are Not

- **Definition** — a *cittar* is one who has attained *citti*/*siddhi* (perfection, occult mastery): control over the eight *aṣṭamā-siddhi* powers, mastery of *prāṇa*, and the **deathless body**. They wrote in Tamil, not Sanskrit, in deliberately cryptic "twilight" verse (*paripāṣai*, sandhā-language). (Tamil Śaiva tradition.)
- **Anti-caste, anti-ritual** — the Siddhars are famed for **iconoclasm**: scorn for temple ritualism, caste, idolatry and brahminical orthodoxy, an inward Śiva-as-the-Self (*civam*), and blunt social satire (esp. Pāmpāṭṭi-Cittar, Civavākkiyar). (Siddhar songs.) [folk]
- **Distinct from the 84 Mahāsiddhas** — the Tamil Eighteen are a **separate roster** from the Buddhist/tantric **84 Mahāsiddhas** (Caurāṅgi, Lūipa, etc.), though figures and motifs cross over. [scholarly]
- **Distinct from, yet parallel to, the Nāths** — the Siddhar project (haṭha-yoga + alchemy + immortality) mirrors the North-Indian **Nātha-siddha** order of Matsyendra/Gorakṣa; the two are sister "Siddha-cult" movements, and some names overlap (a "Gorakkar"/Korakkar sits on the Tamil list). [scholarly]
- **Internal vs external alchemy** — Siddhar alchemy works on **two registers at once**: literal mercury–sulphur–salt processing (transmuting base metal and the body) *and* an inner yogic alchemy where the "mercury" is *bindu*/semen and the fire is kuṇḍalinī — the verses code both. (Bogar 7000; Tamil alchemical corpus.)

## 2. The Roster — the Eighteen (Patinenmar Siddhar)

The list of eighteen is **conventional and open-ended**: "eighteen" functions as a sacred totality (cf. the eighteen *purāṇas*, eighteen days of the war), and competing manuscript lists name different adepts. Never treat any one roster as fixed.

- **Agattiyar (Agastya)** — the **head of the eighteen**, "foremost of the Siddhars" (*muppāṭ-cuṭar*); the Vedic ṛṣi reborn as father of Tamil grammar, medicine and yoga; teacher of Bogar, Tirumūlar and others; said to dwell deathless in the **Potikai (Pothigai) hills**. (Agattiyar corpus; Tamil tradition.)
- **Tirumūlar** — yogin-poet, author of the **Tirumantiram**; counted among the 63 Nāyaṉārs *and* the Siddhars; the doctrinal bridge between Siddhar yoga and Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta. (Tirumantiram.)
- **Bogar (Pōkar)** — the great alchemist; reputed sculptor of the **Palani Murugan navapāṣāṇa image** from nine poisons; author of **Bogar 7000**; legend sends him to China (as "Bo-Yang"/Lao-Tzu in folk syncretism). (Bogar 7000.) [folk]
- **Koṅkaṇar (Koṅkaṇa-Siddhar)** — alchemist and disciple/associate of Bogar; a large *Koṅkaṇar* corpus on rasavāda and muppu is attributed to him.
- **Pāmpāṭṭi-Cittar** ("the snake-charmer Siddhar") — author of the refrain-songs *Āṭu pāmpē* ("Dance, O serpent!") — the "serpent" being **kuṇḍalinī**; famed iconoclast and satirist. (Pāmpāṭṭic-Cittar Pāṭalkaḷ.)
- **Karuvūrār (Karuvūr Tēvar)** — Siddhar associated with the **Bṛhadīśvara (Tanjore) temple's** consecration under Rāja Rāja Cōḻa; alchemy and Śaiva hymnody attributed to him.
- **Pataññ-cali-Cittar (Patañjali)** — the Siddhar list claims the yoga-sūtra **Patañjali** as one of the eighteen, linking the Tamil yoga of breath to the rāja-yoga canon (see [yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md)). [disputed]
- **Sundarānandar** — Siddhar-yogin, pupil in the Agastya line; verse on yoga and kāya-kalpa attributed to him.
- **Other names that recur on the lists** — *Tirumūlar, Korakkar/Gorakkar, Cattaimuni (Sattaimuni), Vālmīki (Vānmīkanār), Nandi(-tēvar), Macca-muni (Matsyendra), Idaikkāṭṭu-Cittar, Kamala-muni, Kuthambai, Aluga(nār), Pulastya(r), Pulipāṇi, Dhanvantari, Rāmadevar (Yacob), Kūrmamuni, Cattanāthar.* (Various manuscript lists.)
  - *Variant (the "standard" temple list):* one widely-circulated eighteen runs **Nandi devar, Agattiyar, Tirumūlar, Korakkar, Konkanar, Sattaimuni, Vanmikar, Ramadevar, Bogar, Pulipani, Idaikkadar, Kamalamuni, Macchamuni, Karuvurar, Sundaranandar, Kuthambai, Pambatti, Dhanvantari.** [regional]
  - *Variant (jīva-samādhi shrines):* each Siddhar is venerated at a **jīva-samādhi** (living-tomb) shrine — e.g. Bogar & Agattiyar at **Palani**, Karuvūrār at **Karuvai/Tanjore**, Pāmpāṭṭi at **Marutamalai**, Sundarānandar at **Maḍurai/Kuṭāl** — pilgrimage anchors of the cult. (Temple tradition; cf. [regional-temple-festivals.md](./regional-temple-festivals.md).) [regional]
  - *Variant (overlapping totals):* alongside the eighteen, traditions count **9 Nava-Siddhar**, and lists of *Pulastya, Vyāsa, Vālmīki* etc. blur the Siddhars with the purāṇic ṛṣis. [disputed]

## 3. The Siddha System They Founded

### Siddha Medicine (*Siddha Vaidya*)

- **Indigenous Tamil medicine** — the **Siddha system** is South India's classical medicine, parallel and partly prior to Āyurveda's southern reach; it shares the *tridoṣa* (*vātam–pittam–kapham*), *pañca-bhūta* and *sapta-dhātu* frame but is distinguished by heavy **mineral/metallic and mercurial pharmacy**. (Siddha Vaidya tradition; cf. [ayurveda-and-the-science-of-life.md](./ayurveda-and-the-science-of-life.md).)
- **Agastya as founder-physician** — Agattiyar is named the **father of Siddha medicine**; foundational medical texts (*Agattiyar Vaittiyam*, *Agattiyar 2000*, *Pūrṇa-sūtram*) are ascribed to him. (Agattiyar corpus.)
- **Metallic & mineral materia medica** — Siddha pharmacy specialises in processed **metals and minerals** (*loham*), sulphur, mercury (*irasam*), arsenic, and the calcined ash-medicines **centūram / pārpam / cuṇṇam** — products of the same alchemy that targets bodily immortality. (Siddha pharmacopoeia.)
- **Pulse & eight-fold diagnosis** — diagnosis by **nāḍi (pulse-reading)** plus the *eṇ-vakai* eight examinations (tongue, complexion, voice, eye, touch, stool, urine, pulse). (Siddha clinical tradition.)

### Tamil Alchemy — *Rasavāda* & *Muppu*

- **Rasavāda (*irasavātam*)** — the science of **transmuting base metals to gold** and, by the same operations, fixing and immortalising the body; mercury (*pārada/irasam*) is the central "living metal," wedded to sulphur (the Śiva–Śakti chemistry). (Bogar 7000; Koṅkaṇar.)
- **Muppu — the "universal salt"** — the Siddhars' secret **three-salt elixir** (the "philosopher's stone" of Tamil alchemy), a closely-guarded preparation said to fix mercury, transmute metals, and confer the deathless body; its exact composition is deliberately concealed in the verses. (Siddhar alchemical corpus.) [disputed]
- **Navapāṣāṇa** — the **nine-poison/nine-mineral** amalgam; Bogar's **Palani Daṇḍāyutapāṇi (Murugan)** image is said to be cast from it, so that its abhiṣeka-water becomes medicinal. (Palani temple tradition.) [folk]

### Kāya-kalpa, Yoga & Immortality

- **Kāya-kalpa (*kāyakaṟpam*)** — rejuvenation regimens (herbo-mineral *kalpa* preparations, breath-control, sealing the body's "gates") to halt ageing and extend life across yugas; the medical face of the immortality quest. (Siddhar kāya-kalpa texts.)
- **Vāci-yoga / prāṇāyāma** — the Siddhar core practice: **vāci** (breath, *vāyu*) discipline to reverse the breath, still the mind, and unite the vital airs — the Tamil idiom of the same haṭha-yoga in [chakras-and-kundalini.md](./chakras-and-kundalini.md). (Tirumantiram; Siddhar songs.)
- **Kuṇḍalinī & the inner serpent** — the rising serpent-power (Pāmpāṭṭi's "dancing snake") through the centres to the crown is the soteriological goal — *kāya-siddhi* and *jīvan-mukti* in one. (Tirumantiram; Pāmpāṭṭi.)
- **Aṣṭamā-siddhi** — the eight occult attainments (*aṇimā, mahimā, laghimā, garimā, prāpti, prākāmya, īśitva, vaśitva*) are byproducts the Siddhars are said to wield but warned not to crave. (Siddhar/yoga tradition.)
- **Jīva-samādhi, not death** — a perfected Siddhar does not "die" but enters **conscious living entombment** (*jīva-samādhi*), remaining present at the shrine — the cult's logic of deathlessness. (Temple tradition.) [folk]

## 4. The Technical Poetic Corpus

- **Tirumantiram** — Tirumūlar's ~3,000-verse Tamil classic; the **tenth Tirumuṟai** of the Śaiva canon; the earliest systematic Tamil work on yoga, mantra, kuṇḍalinī and Śaiva Siddhānta — the doctrinal cornerstone of Siddhar thought. (Tirumantiram.) [scholarly]
- **Bogar 7000** (*Pōkar Ēḻāyiram*) — Bogar's vast verse-treatise on alchemy, muppu, kāya-kalpa, mantra and yoga — the flagship alchemical text of the lineage. (Bogar 7000.) [late text]
- **The Agattiyar / Agastya corpus** — a huge body of Tamil texts under Agastya's name on medicine, alchemy, yoga and jñāna: *Agattiyar 2000 / 1200 / 12000*, *Agattiyar Paripūraṇam*, *Pūrṇa-sūtram*, etc. — much of it pseudonymous accretion across centuries. (Agattiyar corpus.) [disputed]
- **The Cittar Pāṭalkaḷ ("Siddhar songs")** — the popular devotional-philosophical lyric corpus: **Civavākkiyar**, **Pāmpāṭṭi-Cittar**, **Paṭṭiṉattār**, **Kuthambai-Cittar**, **Idaikkāṭṭu-Cittar** — terse, satirical, mystical. (Anthologised Siddhar songs.) [folk]
- **Codes & secrecy** — the corpus is written in deliberate **enigmatic code** (*paripāṣai*) — mercury for semen, "woman" for kuṇḍalinī, salts named by riddle — guarding the practical alchemy from the uninitiated. (Siddhar literary convention.)

## 5. Place in Tamil Śaiva Spirituality

- **Within Śaivism, beyond orthodoxy** — the Siddhars are an **antinomian wing of Tamil Śaivism**: Śiva-centred and yoga-driven like Śaiva Siddhānta (Tirumūlar straddles both), but anti-ritual and anti-caste in tone — a tension never resolved into a single church. [scholarly]
- **The Murugan connection** — strong links to **Murukaṉ/Kārttikeya** (Palani, the navapāṣāṇa icon, Bogar's shrine) tie the lineage to the Tamil Skanda cult. [regional]
- **A southern mirror of the Nāths** — chronologically and structurally the Tamil Siddha-cult parallels the Nātha-yogin movement of the north; both crystallise the medieval **Siddha synthesis** of haṭha-yoga, tantra, alchemy and immortality. Sampradāya context: [../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md). [scholarly]
- **Two clocks** — *scriptural*: Agastya and the eighteen are deathless adepts active from the Tretā/Dvāpara into the present Kali age; *historical*: the datable corpus runs **c. 6th c. (Tirumantiram, contested) through the 15th–18th c.** alchemical texts, with "eighteen" a retrospective canon. [scholarly]
