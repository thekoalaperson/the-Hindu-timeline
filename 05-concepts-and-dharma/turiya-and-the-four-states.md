# Turīya & the Four States

> **Where this sits:** A core doctrine of the *concepts-and-dharma* layer. The Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad maps the *whole of consciousness* — and with it the syllable AUM and the identity of ātman and Brahman — onto four states (*avasthā*) and four "quarters" (*pāda*) of the Self. Shankara called Māṇḍūkya alone, with Gauḍapāda's verses, sufficient for liberation.
> Parent: [../README.md](../README.md) · Sibling: [./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md](./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md) · See also: [./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md), [./purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md](./purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md), [../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/) (for AUM as symbol), and the Upaniṣad catalog in [../90-literature-corpus/](../90-literature-corpus/).

The **Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad** (Atharvaveda; 12 mantras, the shortest of the principal Upaniṣads) opens "AUM — this whole world is that syllable" (*om ityetad akṣaram idaṃ sarvam*, mantra 1) and declares "all this is Brahman; this Self is Brahman" (*sarvaṃ hyetad brahma, ayam ātmā brahma*, mantra 2). It then analyzes the Self as having **four quarters** (*so'yam ātmā catuṣpāt*), correlating each with a state of consciousness and with a component of AUM. **Gauḍapāda's Kārikā** (the *Māṇḍūkya Kārikā / Āgama-śāstra*, c. 6th–7th c. CE) is the oldest extant systematic Advaita commentary woven into it. This file catalogs the four states, the AUM correspondence, the seven characteristics of Turīya, the Kārikā's structure, and the variant readings (including *turīyātīta*).

---

## 1. The fourfold Self — overview (Māṇḍūkya mantras 2–7)

- **The Self has four quarters** - *catuṣpāt*: three are the ordinary experiential states (each an *avasthā*); the fourth, Turīya, is their non-dual ground. The states are also named by their presiding consciousness — Vaiśvānara, Taijasa, Prājña, and (the unnamed) Turīya (Māṇḍūkya 2–7).
- **Three bodies / three sheaths mapping** - Waking = gross body (*sthūla-śarīra*); dream = subtle body (*sūkṣma-śarīra*); deep sleep = causal body (*kāraṇa-śarīra*); Turīya = beyond all three bodies, the witness (*sākṣin*). (Vedāntic synthesis; cf. *Pañcakośa* of Taittirīya, treated separately.)
- **Two ways the "four" is read** - As *four states in sequence* (developmental/meditative ladder), **or** as *three states plus their permanent substratum* (Turīya is not a fourth item in the series but that which underlies and witnesses the other three). Advaita strongly favors the latter (see §6).

## 2. Jāgrat — the waking state (mantra 3)

- **Vaiśvānara ("common to all men")** - The first quarter (*prathamaḥ pādaḥ*); consciousness turned **outward** (*bahiṣ-prajña*), cognizing gross objects (*sthūla-bhuk*, "enjoyer of the gross"). AUM-syllable: **A** (mantra 9). (Māṇḍūkya 3.)
- **Seven limbs (*sapta-aṅga*)** - Vaiśvānara is figured as the cosmic person whose body is the universe: heaven = head, sun = eye, air = breath, fire = mouth/heart, space = trunk/abdomen, water = bladder/kidneys, earth = feet — drawn from the Vaiśvānara-vidyā of Chāndogya 5.18.2. (Māṇḍūkya 3.)
- **Nineteen mouths (*ekonaviṃśati-mukha*)** - The nineteen instruments of experience: 5 sense-organs (*jñānendriya*), 5 organs of action (*karmendriya*), 5 vital airs (*prāṇa, apāna, vyāna, udāna, samāna*), and 4 inner faculties (*antaḥkaraṇa*) — manas, buddhi, ahaṅkāra, citta. (Māṇḍūkya 3; same nineteen carried into mantras 4–5.)
  - *Variant (count of antaḥkaraṇa):* some commentators reduce the inner faculty to three (manas, buddhi, ahaṅkāra) and still reach nineteen by other groupings; the "four-fold antaḥkaraṇa" is Shankara's standard reckoning.
- **A = ādi / āpti** - The matrā **A** is glossed as *āpti* ("obtaining, pervading") and *ādimattva* ("being first"): the waking/gross pervades and is first, as A is the first sound and pervades all speech (Māṇḍūkya 9).

## 3. Svapna — the dream state (mantra 4)

- **Taijasa ("the luminous / fiery one")** - The second quarter; consciousness turned **inward** (*antaḥ-prajña*), cognizing the subtle, self-illumined mental impressions (*pravivikta-bhuk*, "enjoyer of the subtle/exquisite"). Named *taijasa* because it shines by its own light, mind being made of *tejas*. AUM-syllable: **U** (mantra 10). (Māṇḍūkya 4.)
- **Same seven limbs, nineteen mouths** - Identical apparatus to waking, but operating on internally projected objects built from *vāsanā* (latent impressions) — the dreamer is the sole maker of the dream-world (cf. Kārikā 2.1–10 on dream as illustration of the world's unreality).
- **U = utkarṣa / ubhayatva** - Matrā **U** glossed as *utkarṣa* ("exaltation, superiority") and *ubhayatva* ("intermediateness") — dream stands between waking and deep sleep, as U stands between A and M (Māṇḍūkya 10).

## 4. Suṣupti — deep dreamless sleep (mantras 5–6)

- **Prājña ("the cognitional / the wise")** - The third quarter; the state where the sleeper "desires no desire, sees no dream" (*na kañcana kāmaṃ kāmayate, na kañcana svapnaṃ paśyati*), become an **undifferentiated mass of consciousness** (*prajñāna-ghana*), full of bliss (*ānanda-maya*), enjoying bliss (*ānanda-bhuk*). AUM-syllable: **M** (mantra 11). (Māṇḍūkya 5.)
- **The "doorway" verse (mantra 6)** - Prājña is "Lord of all" (*sarveśvara*), "the all-knowing" (*sarvajña*), "the inner controller" (*antaryāmin*), "the womb / source and dissolution of all beings" (*yoniḥ sarvasya, prabhavāpyayau hi bhūtānām*). In cosmic register Prājña ↔ **Īśvara**, Taijasa ↔ **Hiraṇyagarbha**, Vaiśvānara ↔ **Virāṭ**. (Māṇḍūkya 6; Vedāntic micro-/macro-cosmic mapping.)
- **Bliss but not liberation** - The bliss of suṣupti is the *covering* of ignorance (*ājñāna/āvaraṇa*), not realization: ignorance (the "seed," *bīja-bhāva*) persists, which is why one wakes still ignorant. Turīya alone is *seedless*. (Kārikā 1.12–13.)
- **M = miti / apīti** - Matrā **M** glossed as *miti* ("measure") and *apīti* ("merging, dissolution") — in deep sleep the prior two states merge, as A and U merge into M, and the whole word is measured out and reabsorbed (Māṇḍūkya 11).

## 5. Turīya — the fourth (mantra 7)

- **"The fourth" (*caturtha / turīya*)** - Mantra 7 defines it by negation: **not** inward-cognizing, **not** outward-cognizing, **not** both, **not** a mass of cognition (*na prajñāna-ghana*), **not** cognizing, **not** non-cognizing. (Māṇḍūkya 7.)
- **The seven characteristics (from mantra 7)** - Turīya is: (1) *adṛṣṭa* — unseen / imperceptible; (2) *avyavahārya* — beyond empirical dealing / transaction; (3) *agrāhya* — ungraspable; (4) *alakṣaṇa* — without distinguishing marks; (5) *acintya* — unthinkable; (6) *avyapadeśya* — indescribable / unnameable; (7) the positive triad capping the verse — *śāntaṃ śivam advaitam* ("tranquil, auspicious/benign, non-dual"). It is "the essence of the cognition of the one Self" (*ekātma-pratyaya-sāra*), into which the world is resolved (*prapañcopaśama*). **"That is the Self; that is to be known"** (*sa ātmā, sa vijñeyaḥ*). (Māṇḍūkya 7.)
- **A-mātra — the soundless fourth** - The whole syllable AUM, undivided, is Turīya; specifically it is **amātra** ("measureless / partless"), the **silence after the M**, *avyavahārya* and *prapañcopaśama* — "without an element, beyond all dealings, the cessation of phenomena, auspicious and non-dual" (Māṇḍūkya 12). The three sounds dissolve into the silence as the three states dissolve into Turīya.
- **He who knows it merges his self in the Self** - The closing promise: one who knows AUM thus "enters the Self by the self" (*ātmanā ātmānaṃ praviśati*) (Māṇḍūkya 12).
- **Neither cause nor effect** - Whereas Viśva (waking) and Taijasa (dream) are conditioned by both cause and effect, and Prājña by cause alone (the seed of ignorance), **Turīya is conditioned by neither**: it is the unborn, the seer of all, ever-free (Kārikā 1.11). The knower "sees neither sleep nor dream in Turīya" (Kārikā 1.14).

## 6. Gauḍapāda's Kārikā — structure & key doctrines

- **Four prakaraṇas (chapters)** - (1) **Āgama-prakaraṇa** — the scriptural exposition, woven verse-by-verse with the Upaniṣad's mantras (29 kārikās); (2) **Vaitathya-prakaraṇa** ("On Unreality/Falsity") — the world's status as dream-like, *vaitathya* (38); (3) **Advaita-prakaraṇa** ("Non-duality") — establishing non-dualism (48); (4) **Alātaśānti-prakaraṇa** ("Quenching of the Firebrand") — the *alāta-cakra* (whirling firebrand) image: the apparent circle of fire is unreal, so too the apparent multiplicity of consciousness (100). (Gauḍapāda, *Māṇḍūkya Kārikā*.)
  - *Variant / dispute [scholarly]:* The 4th chapter uses notably Buddhist vocabulary (*saṃvṛti/paramārtha*, *dharma*, "Buddha"); some scholars (Vidhushekhara Bhattacharya and others) read heavy Mahāyāna (esp. Madhyamaka/Yogācāra) influence, and a few question single authorship or full Vedāntic orthodoxy of chapter 4. Traditional Advaita treats all four as one work by one Gauḍapāda, paramaguru of Shankara. [disputed]
  - *Variant (citation custom):* Vedāntic teachers often cite only chapters 1–3 as doctrinally authoritative scripture-commentary, treating chapter 4 as supplementary dialectic. [scholarly]
- **Ajātivāda — the doctrine of non-origination** - Gauḍapāda's central thesis: nothing is ever really born (*na kaścij jāyate*); creation, the jīva, the world — none truly arises. What appears to arise is *māyā*; only the unborn non-dual Brahman is real (Kārikā 1.16–18, 3.48, 4.71). Turīya is precisely this unborn ground.
- **Asparśa-yoga — "the yoga of no-contact"** - The supreme yoga in which consciousness touches/contacts nothing (no subject–object relation), free of all *spṛśa*; "the yoga hard for all yogis to attain" (*durdarśam sarva-yogibhiḥ*), it is Turīya realized directly, fear-free and non-dual (Kārikā 3.39, 4.2). Contrast with Patañjali's *saṃprajñāta* stages — see [./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md).
- **The two unrealities, one reality** - "The world is *māyā*; non-duality alone is the supreme reality" (Kārikā 1.17). The three states are mutually negating and so unreal *as states*; what persists through and beyond them is alone real.

## 7. Turīyātīta — "beyond the fourth"

- **Turīyātīta in Kashmir Śaivism** - The non-dual Trika system adds a **fifth state**, *turyātīta* ("transcending the fourth"), sometimes equated with the void (*śūnya*) and full liberation (*jīvanmukti*) — the bliss of Turīya now stabilized and saturating even the waking, dream and sleep states. (Kashmir Śaiva texts; e.g. tradition around Abhinavagupta / Kṣemarāja.) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (seven turīyas):* Kashmir Śaivism further grades Turīya into stages, from *nimīlanā-samādhi* (eyes-closed, inward absorption) to *unmīlanā-samādhi* (eyes-open, the non-dual flooding the objective world). Turīyātīta is the culmination where the distinction collapses.
- **Advaita's caution on the term** - Strict Advaitins note Māṇḍūkya itself uses only "the fourth" (*caturtha/turīya*) and not *turīyātīta*; since Turīya is already the partless Absolute, a state "beyond" it is, taken literally, a category error. The term is admitted only **pedagogically** — to dissolve the misimpression that Turīya is one state among four. (Advaita exegesis.)
- **Ramana Maharṣi's reading** - "Turīya is only another name for the Self." It is not a fourth state alongside the three but the ever-present awareness of all three. "Fourth" indicates *that which differs from* the three states and their witness; once grasped, even "fourth" is dropped, which is what *turīyātīta* points to — there is, finally, only the Self, no states at all. (Ramana, *Talks / Maharshi's Gospel*.) [late text]

## 8. Comparative table — the correspondences

- **Quarter 1** - State *jāgrat* (waking) · consciousness **Vaiśvānara** (cosmic: Virāṭ) · body gross · object gross (*sthūla*) · matrā **A** · "enjoyer of the gross."
- **Quarter 2** - State *svapna* (dream) · consciousness **Taijasa** (cosmic: Hiraṇyagarbha) · body subtle · object subtle (*pravivikta*) · matrā **U** · "enjoyer of the subtle."
- **Quarter 3** - State *suṣupti* (deep sleep) · consciousness **Prājña** (cosmic: Īśvara) · body causal · object bliss-with-ignorance-seed (*ānanda*) · matrā **M** · "enjoyer of bliss."
- **Quarter 4** - State **Turīya** (the fourth) · consciousness = the Self / pure witness (no cosmic counterpart; it is the ground of all three) · no body · no object (non-dual) · **amātra** (silence) · *śāntaṃ śivam advaitam*.

## 9. Cross-links & related concepts

- **Ātman = Brahman** - Turīya is the locus of the mahāvākya *ayam ātmā brahma* ("this Self is Brahman," Māṇḍūkya 2, the Atharvavedic great saying) — see [./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md](./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md) §3.
- **Yoga & samādhi** - Turīya is often correlated with *nirvikalpa samādhi*; *asparśa-yoga* contrasts with Patañjali's eight limbs — see [./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md).
- **Mokṣa** - Realizing Turīya as one's own Self is liberation (*jīvanmukti*) — see [./purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md](./purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md).
- **AUM / praṇava** - The syllable-symbol whose three sounds + silence carry this whole scheme — cross-ref the symbols section [../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/).

> **Open rosters / caveats:** The exact enumeration of the "nineteen mouths" and "seven limbs" varies slightly by commentator; the seven-state grading of Turīya in Kashmir Śaivism is a developed esoteric scheme not all sub-schools accept; and the authorship/Buddhist-influence question on Kārikā chapter 4 remains scholarly-disputed. Verse numbering of Māṇḍūkya (12 mantras) and Kārikā follows the Shankara recension.
