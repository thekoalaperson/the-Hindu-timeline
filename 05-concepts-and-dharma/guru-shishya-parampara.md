# Guru-Śiṣya Paramparā

> Cross-cutting doctrine within [05-concepts-and-dharma](./README.md) of *The Hindu Timeline*. The **teacher-disciple transmission** — how sacred knowledge (and spiritual potency) is handed down living-mouth to living-ear through unbroken chains (*paramparā*) belonging to schools (*sampradāya*): the guru's nature and role, *dīkṣā* (initiation), the great lineages, *guru-bhakti*, the festival of Guru Pūrṇimā, and the cautions. Back to the [master index](../README.md).
> Companion files: [The Three Mārgas](the-three-margas.md) (the paths the guru transmits), [Varṇa, Āśrama & the Saṃskāras](varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md) (the *brahmacarya* studentship stage and *upanayana* rite that ground the relationship), [Ātman, Brahman & the Six Darśanas](atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md) (the realization the guru points to), [Śruti — Vedas & Upaniṣads](../90-literature-corpus/shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) (the oral matrix in which the tradition formed).

The phrase names a *mechanism* more than a doctrine: Hindu knowledge — Vedic recitation, ritual, philosophy, yoga, music, dance, martial and craft arts — was for most of its history transmitted not primarily through books but through a personal, initiatory bond between a *guru* (teacher) and a *śiṣya* (disciple, also *cela*/*chela*, *antevāsin*, *śrāvaka*). *Paramparā* ("uninterrupted row/succession") is the resulting chain across generations; *sampradāya* ("that which is handed over") is the doctrinal community that the chain constitutes and validates. To "belong" to a tradition is, classically, to have received *dīkṣā* from a living guru who stands in such a chain. The two clocks apply: tradition projects its chains back to the gods or to primordial sages (a *scriptural/devotional* genealogy), while historians can date the human founder-*ācāryas* only from roughly the 8th century CE onward (an *academic* clock). This file is the comparative-doctrinal node; for the texts that expound it see the [literature corpus](../90-literature-corpus/README.md).

## 1. Vocabulary — Guru, Śiṣya, Paramparā, Sampradāya

- **Guru (गुरु)** - "weighty one" — etymologically the *guru* is "heavy" (cf. Latin *gravis*) with spiritual gravity/authority; the teacher who removes ignorance. By far the most-quoted gloss splits the word: **gu** = "darkness," **ru** = "the remover" → "remover of darkness." (Source: Advayatāraka Upaniṣad, verse 16; popularized via the Guru Gītā.)
  - *Variant (gloss):* a parallel folk-etymology reads **gu** = darkness, **ru** = **light** → the guru as "the light that dispels darkness." Both are *spiritual* etymologies, not the literal Sanskrit derivation (which is simply "heavy/venerable"). [folk]
- **Śiṣya (शिष्य)** - "one to be instructed/disciplined" (from √śās, "to instruct/govern"); the disciple who has formally accepted a guru. Synonyms/registers: *cela*/*chela* (follower), *antevāsin* ("one who dwells near/at the end [of the teacher's house]"), *vidyārthin* (student). (Source: Sanskrit lexical tradition.)
- **Ācārya (आचार्य)** - a teacher who not only instructs but *embodies* and establishes right conduct (*ācāra*) — typically the founding or presiding teacher of a sampradāya (e.g. Śaṅkar-ācārya, Rāmānuj-ācārya). A subset distinction: the *ācārya* gives the full teaching; the *upādhyāya* teaches a part for a fee. (Source: Manusmṛti 2.140–141, which ranks ācārya, upādhyāya, guru.)
- **Paramparā (परम्परा)** - "one-after-another, uninterrupted series"; the lineage as a *chain of transmission*, each link receiving from the previous and passing to the next. The Bhagavad Gītā (4.1–3) supplies the charter image: the yoga was handed down *evaṃ paramparā-prāptam* ("thus received by succession") from Vivasvān → Manu → Ikṣvāku, was lost over time, and is re-spoken by Kṛṣṇa to Arjuna. (Source: Bhagavad Gītā 4.1–3; see [../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md](../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md).)
- **Sampradāya (सम्प्रदाय)** - "that which is handed over / tradition"; the doctrinal community + its body of teaching, transmitted *through* a paramparā. A sampradāya may maintain *gurukulas*, and be seated at a *maṭha* (monastery), *ākhāṛā* (ascetic regiment), or temple. (Source: Wikipedia *Sampradaya*; Hinduism Today.)
- **Gurukula (गुरुकुल)** - "the guru's household/family"; the residential school where the *brahmacārin* lived with the teacher, serving him while studying — the institutional form of the relationship in the Vedic-classical period. See [varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md](varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md) on the *brahmacarya* āśrama.
- **Satguru / Sadguru (सद्गुरु)** - the "true guru": a *realized* (often *jīvanmukta*) master, held to be identical with Brahman, distinguished from a merely competent instructor. The Guru Gītā tradition reserves the highest reverence for the satguru, who "cuts asunder the disciple's bondage by the sword of knowledge." (Source: Sivananda/Divine Life Society, *Guru Tattva*; Guru Gītā.) [late text]

## 2. The Guru's Role and Necessity

- **Indispensability of a living teacher** - The dominant view across schools: scripture is opaque without a guru to *unlock* it; self-study (*svādhyāya*) alone risks error. The Upaniṣadic image is foundational — *upa-ni-ṣad* itself means "sitting down near" the teacher to receive the secret teaching. (Source: classical etymology of *Upaniṣad*; Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 1.2.12, which directs the seeker to "approach, fuel in hand" a guru learned in scripture and established in Brahman — *tad-vijñānārthaṃ sa gurum evābhigacchet*.)
  - *Variant (jñāna-marga caution):* even within knowledge-traditions a strand holds that the guru can only *point*; final realization is the disciple's own — "the guru opens the door, you must enter." [scholarly]
- **What the guru does** - (a) gives the *teaching* (Veda, mantra, śāstra, technique); (b) gives *dīkṣā*, formal initiation that admits the disciple to the lineage; (c) transmits *spiritual potency* directly (see §3, *śaktipāta*); (d) models conduct (*ācāra*); (e) destroys the disciple's doubts and ego over time. (Source: Hinduism Today; Pāñcarātra / Śaiva dīkṣā literature.)
- **Reciprocal duty: śuśrūṣā and service** - The disciple owes obedience, service (*sevā*), and *śuśrūṣā* ("the desire to hear/serve"); the guru owes the full teaching withheld from none qualified. The Taittirīya Upaniṣad's convocation address (*śīkṣā-vallī* 1.11) frames the ethical contract — "speak the truth, follow dharma… let there be no neglect of study and teaching." (Source: Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.11.)
- **Guru as more than human** - Devotional traditions elevate the guru to the status of God, or above: the Guru Gītā declares *guru is Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and Maheśvara; the guru is the supreme Brahman itself — salutations to that guru* (*gurur brahmā gurur viṣṇur…*). (Source: Guru Gītā, embedded in the Skanda Purāṇa.) [late text]
  - *Variant (Kabīr / sant tradition):* the famous couplet places the guru *above* God — when guru and Govinda (God) both stand before me, whom do I bow to first? I bow to the guru, who showed me Govinda. [folk] [late text]

## 3. Dīkṣā — Initiation

- **Dīkṣā (दीक्षा)** - the formal initiation by which a guru admits a disciple to the path/lineage; typically a one-to-one rite involving transmission of a mantra and the undertaking of a spiritual discipline. The classical derivation pairs *dī* (giving — of divine knowledge) with *kṣa* (destruction — of sin/ignorance). (Source: Tantric/Pāñcarātra literature; Wikipedia *Diksha*.)
- **Mantra-dīkṣā** - the most common form: the guru whispers a *guru-mantra* / *iṣṭa-mantra* into the disciple's ear; this mantra becomes the seed of the entire subsequent practice and is kept secret. (Source: Tantric tradition; krama-dīkṣā begins here.)
- **The three modes of transmission (sight, touch, word)** - dīkṣā may be conferred by the guru's *gaze* (*cākṣuṣī/dṛk*), *touch* (*sparśa*), or *word/mantra* (*vāc/mantra*) — the classic triad, often illustrated by analogy: the tortoise (sight), the fish (touch), the bird (word/thought) nourishing their young. (Source: Tantric/Śākta texts; widely cited.)
- **The five Tantric dīkṣās** - Tantra enumerates five: **samaya-dīkṣā** (by ritual), **sparśa-dīkṣā** (by touch, without ritual), **vāg-dīkṣā** (by word/mantra), **śāmbhavī-dīkṣā** (arising from mere perception of the guru), and **māno-dīkṣā** (performed in the mind/at a distance). (Source: Tantra, via Wikipedia *Diksha*.)
  - *Variant (Śākta fourfold):* Śākta texts describe four — **Kriyāvatī**, **Varṇamayī**, **Kalāvatī**, **Vedhamayī** — the *Kriyāvatī* form having the guru draw the disciple's *caitanya* (consciousness) into himself, purify it, and return it. (Source: Devī Tantra literature, wisdomlib.) [late text]
  - *Variant (Pāñcarātra Vaiṣṇava):* the Pāñcarātra system structures dīkṣā as a graded sacrament marking entry to Viṣṇu-worship, with *samaya*, *viśeṣa*, and *nirvāṇa* dīkṣās at ascending levels. (Source: ExoticIndia, *Diksha in Pancharatra*.)
- **Śaktipāta (शक्तिपात) — "descent of power"** - the direct transmission of spiritual energy from guru (or the divine) to disciple, by word, look, thought, or touch; understood as an act of *grace* (*anugraha*) that cannot be forced or willed by the recipient. Central to Kashmir Śaivism and Siddha/Śākta yoga. (Source: Wikipedia *Shaktipata*; Kashmir Śaivism.)
  - *Variant (intensities, Kashmir Śaivism):* graded by force — e.g. *tīvra-tīvra* ("super-supreme grace") producing immediate identity with Śiva and liberation; *tīvra-madhya* ("supreme-medium") making the recipient self-illumined without needing further external guru. The full scheme has nine grades (three intensities × three). (Source: Kashmir Śaivism, via Wikipedia *Shaktipata*.) [scholarly]
- **Relation to the saṃskāras** - For the *dvija* ("twice-born"), the Vedic gateway to study is the **upanayana** ("leading near [to the teacher]"), the sacred-thread investiture that begins *brahmacarya* and confers eligibility to receive the *Gāyatrī mantra* — the saṃskāric ancestor of later sectarian dīkṣā. See [varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md](varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md). Tantric/bhakti dīkṣā, by contrast, is frequently held open across varṇa and gender. [scholarly]

## 4. Paramparā & Sampradāya — The Chains

- **The principle of validation** - A teaching's authority rests partly on its unbroken pedigree; a guru "outside" any paramparā is suspect, and reciting the lineage (*guru-paramparā* / *guru-vandana*) at the start of study/ritual affirms the chain. (Source: pan-Hindu practice; Gītā 4.2 on the lineage being "lost.")
- **The Advaita / Daśanāmī line** - Ādi Śaṅkara (trad. 8th c. CE) is held to descend, through Gauḍapāda and Govinda Bhagavatpāda, ultimately from Nārāyaṇa via Brahmā and Vasiṣṭha; he organized renunciate monks into the **Daśanāmī** ("ten-named") orders and founded four cardinal *maṭhas* (Śṛṅgeri, Purī, Dvārakā, Jyotir/Badrī) each headed by a *Śaṅkarācārya*. The opening of the lineage hymn invokes *nārāyaṇaṃ padmabhuvaṃ vasiṣṭhaṃ…*. (Source: Advaita maṭha tradition.) See [atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md](atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md).
- **The four Vaiṣṇava sampradāyas (Catuḥ-sampradāya)** - the orthodox quartet, each tracing to a divine originator and crystallizing in a human *ācārya*. A Padma Purāṇa verse warns that mantras taken *outside* a recognized sampradāya are fruitless (*sampradāya-vihīnā ye mantrās te niṣphalā matāḥ*). (Source: Padma Purāṇa, widely cited in Gauḍīya literature.) [late text]
  - **Śrī (Lakṣmī) Sampradāya** — originator: **Lakṣmī/Śrī**; human ācārya: **Rāmānuja** (11th–12th c.); philosophy: *Viśiṣṭādvaita* (qualified non-dualism). (Source: Vaiṣṇava sampradāya tradition.)
  - **Brahma Sampradāya** — originator: **Brahmā** (taught by Kṛṣṇa within the heart); ācārya: **Madhva** (1238–1317 CE); philosophy: *Dvaita* (dualism). The Gauḍīya (Caitanya) lineage affiliates here as the **Brahma-Mādhva-Gauḍīya Sampradāya**.
  - **Rudra Sampradāya** — originator: **Rudra/Śiva**; ācāryas: **Viṣṇusvāmī** and **Vallabha** (1479–1531); philosophy: *Śuddhādvaita* (pure non-dualism).
  - **Kumāra (Sanaka) / Nimbārka Sampradāya** — originators: the **Four Kumāras** (Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanātana, Sanatkumāra); ācārya: **Nimbārka** (trad. early, dating disputed); philosophy: *Dvaitādvaita* (dualistic non-dualism, *bhedābheda*).
  - *Variant (datings disputed):* the *traditional* dates and even the divine-origin claims (especially Nimbārka's antiquity and the Gauḍīya affiliation to Madhva) are contested by scholars; treat the divine genealogies as devotional and the human dates as approximate. [disputed] [scholarly]
- **Śaiva and Śākta lineages** - parallel chains: the **Nātha** sampradāya (Matsyendranātha → Gorakhnātha) of Haṭha yoga; the **Śaiva Siddhānta** ācārya-paramparā of the Tamil South; the **Vīraśaiva/Liṅgāyat** tradition (Basava, 12th c.); the **Kashmir Śaiva** transmission (Vasugupta → Somānanda → Utpaladeva → Abhinavagupta → Kṣemarāja); and Śākta/Śrīvidyā guru-lineages. (Source: respective sectarian traditions.) See [Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md), [Devī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md).
- **The Daśaśloki / paramparā recitation** - many lineages preserve a versified guru-list recited before practice, anchoring the present teacher to the chain and ultimately to the deity. (Source: sectarian liturgy.)
- **Modern global lineages** - 19th–20th c. movements export the model: Ramakrishna → Vivekananda (Ramakrishna Math); the Yogananda/Kriyā lineage (Mahāvatār Bābājī → Lahiri Mahasaya → Sri Yukteswar → Yogananda); Sivananda → the Divine Life Society; the Gauḍīya-derived **ISKCON** (Bhaktisiddhānta → Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda). (Source: respective organizations.) [late text]

## 5. Guru-Bhakti & the Guru Gītā

- **Guru-bhakti (गुरुभक्ति)** - loving devotion and total surrender to the guru, treated as a discipline in its own right and the precondition for the teaching to "take." The Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad supplies the proof-text most cited for it: *"yasya deve parā bhaktir yathā deve tathā gurau"* — "to the great soul who has supreme devotion to God, and to the guru as to God, the truths of the teaching shine forth" (Śvetāśvatara 6.23). (Source: Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 6.23.)
- **The Guru Gītā** - a stand-alone hymn of (commonly) 182 verses, framed as Śiva instructing Pārvatī on the guru's nature, embedded in the **Skanda Purāṇa**; the central liturgical text of guru-worship in many lineages (recited daily in, e.g., the Siddha Yoga and Divine Life traditions). It is the source of *gurur brahmā gurur viṣṇuḥ* and of the *gu/ru* etymology. (Source: Guru Gītā / Skanda Purāṇa.) [late text]
- **Pādukā / guru-pūjā** - worship of the guru's *sandals* (*pādukā*) or feet (*pāda-sevana*, one of the navadhā-bhakti modes, see [the-three-margas.md](the-three-margas.md)) as a focus when the guru is absent or deceased; the *guru-paramparā* honored through the *pīṭha* (seat) and its successor. (Source: pan-Hindu devotional practice.)
- **Ekalavya — the absent guru** - the Mahābhārata's archer who, refused tuition by Droṇa, learns by worshipping a clay image of him and surpasses Droṇa's own students — then surrenders his right thumb as *guru-dakṣiṇā* (the teacher's fee) when Droṇa demands it. The story is read as both the supreme model of guru-devotion *and* a critique of caste-bound gate-keeping. (Source: Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva; see [../03-itihasa-the-epics/mahabharata-overview.md](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/README.md) if present.) [disputed]
- **Guru-dakṣiṇā** - the disciple's offering to the teacher on completing study — not a fixed fee but whatever the guru asks; the Ekalavya episode is its darkest instance, the ideal being a freely given honoring. (Source: Mahābhārata; Dharmaśāstra on completion of study.)

## 6. Guru Pūrṇimā (Vyāsa Pūrṇimā)

- **The festival** - observed on the **full-moon day of Āṣāḍha** (June–July) as the day to honor one's guru; equally called **Vyāsa Pūrṇimā** because it marks the (traditional) birth of **Vyāsa**, the sage who divided the single Veda into four and composed the Mahābhārata and Purāṇas — archetype of the guru-as-transmitter. (Source: Britannica; Wikipedia *Guru Purnima*.) For Vyāsa see [../90-literature-corpus/shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md](../90-literature-corpus/shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md).
- **Why the Āṣāḍha full moon** - the Āṣāḍha pūrṇimā is held to radiate peak spiritual energy, the guru's blessing being most potent and the disciple most receptive; it also opens the four-month *cāturmāsya* retreat traditionally begun by ascetics on this day. (Source: festival tradition; Kevala Yoga.)
- **Date (2026)** - most pañcāṅgas place it on **Wednesday, 29 July 2026** (Āṣāḍha Śukla Pūrṇimā tithi running c. 18:18 on 28 Jul to c. 20:05 on 29 Jul); the festival is lunar and shifts yearly, and regional almanacs differ (some list 30 July 2026, and a few earlier-Āṣāḍha reckonings put it c. 10–11 July). (Source: 2026 pañcāṅga sources — Prokerala, Drik/AstroSight.) [disputed] Cross-link the festivals node: [festivals-and-vratas.md](festivals-and-vratas.md).
- **Cross-tradition observance** - Buddhists mark it as the day the Buddha gave his first sermon at Sārnāth; Jains honor it as the day Mahāvīra took his first disciple (Indrabhūti Gautama). (Source: Wikipedia *Guru Purnima*.)
  - *Variant (deity foci):* many Hindus also direct the day's *pūjā* to Śiva (the *Ādi Guru* / Dakṣiṇāmūrti) and to Gaṇeśa. The form **Dakṣiṇāmūrti** — Śiva as the silent, south-facing teacher instructing sages by stillness — is the iconographic "first guru." (Source: Britannica; Śaiva iconography.)

## 7. Variants, Disputes & Cautions

- **Two clocks on the lineages** - tradition extends every major paramparā back to a deity or primordial ṛṣi (devotional genealogy); historians can verify the human founder-ācāryas only from c. 8th c. CE (Śaṅkara) onward, and several "ancient" links (e.g. the Gauḍīya claim to the Madhva line) are reconstructions. Keep the scriptural and academic genealogies distinct. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **"Mantra outside a sampradāya is fruitless"** - the Padma Purāṇa dictum is invoked to *require* lineage affiliation; critics note it also functions to police orthodoxy and exclude unaffiliated teachers. Its force is contested across sects. [disputed] [late text]
- **Caste, gender, and access** - Vedic *upanayana*-gated study was historically restricted to *dvija* males; bhakti, Tantra, and modern movements widened or abolished these gates, admitting women and all varṇas to dīkṣā — a major fault line in how "the" tradition is described. [scholarly]
- **The "self" vs "living guru" question** - traditions disagree whether a living, embodied guru is strictly necessary: some hold realization can come from a deceased master's grace, from the *iṣṭa-devatā* directly, or (rarely) without any external guru (the *tīvra-tīvra śaktipāta* case); the mainstream insists on a living teacher. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Risk of guru-abuse** - the very intensity of *guru-bhakti* and unconditional obedience that the tradition prizes is also its danger; modern critics and several documented scandals warn that the model can shield exploitation, and reformers stress *viveka* (discernment) in choosing a guru — classical texts themselves enjoin *testing* a prospective guru (and disciple) before initiation. [scholarly]
- **Etymology cautions** - the *gu = darkness / ru = remover (or light)* glosses are *spiritual* etymologies from the Advayatāraka Upaniṣad / Guru Gītā, not the linguistic derivation of the word (literally "heavy, venerable"). Present them as devotional, not philological. [folk]
- **Open-ended rosters** - the *sampradāya* and *maṭha* lists here are representative, not exhaustive: sub-lineages, regional paramparās, and modern offshoots run into the hundreds and resist a closed catalog. [open roster]
