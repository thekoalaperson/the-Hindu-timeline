# Festivals & Vratas — Origins Mapped to Myth

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Concepts & Dharma](README.md) → **Festivals & Vratas — Origins Mapped to Myth**. The living calendar: how the great festivals (*utsava*) and vows (*vrata*) re-enact specific events on the timeline — births of avatars, deaths of asuras, churning-of-the-ocean episodes — each ritually replayed on its *tithi* every year.
>
> Parent: [Concepts & Dharma](README.md) · Up to: [Master Index](../README.md) · Related: [Purusharthas & Karma](purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md) · [Varna, Ashrama & Samskaras](varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md) · [Units of Time / tithi & jyotisha](../00-time-and-cosmology/units-of-time.md) · [Kumbh Mela & Pilgrimage Cycles](../06-sacred-geography/kumbh-mela-and-pilgrimage-cycles.md)

A **festival** (*utsava*, *parva*) is a calendrical commemoration; a **vrata** is a votive observance — fast, vow, *puja*, and often a *katha* (recitation of the charter-story) — undertaken for a specific fruit (*phala*). Both are dated by the **luni-solar Hindu calendar** (*tithi* within a *paksha* of a lunar month) or, for solar festivals, by **sankranti** (the Sun's zodiacal transit) — see [Units of Time](../00-time-and-cosmology/units-of-time.md). The distinctive feature catalogued below is that nearly every major festival is a **dramatization of a datable timeline-event**: it sits not only in the ritual year but at a specific point in the [yuga](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md)–[manvantara](../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md) chronology. Most charter-myths are **Puranic** (1st millennium CE compilation of older matter) [traditional]; the festivals' *attested practice* is generally medieval-to-modern, so scripture-date and observance-date must be kept distinct.

## I. Autumn / Sharad cluster — the great victories of good over evil

- **Navaratri** ("nine nights") - nine-night autumn (*Sharad/Ashvin*) festival of the **Goddess**, climaxing on the tenth day; the goddess's nine-night battle with the buffalo-demon **Mahishasura**, slain on the last night. Mapped to the [Devi Mahatmya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md) episode in which Durga is forged from the combined *tejas* of the [Trimurti and all devas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md). (Britannica, *Navratri*; Wikipedia, *Navaratri*)
  - *Variant (Sharad vs. four navaratris):* tradition counts **four** seasonal navaratris (Chaitra/Vasanta, Ashadha, Sharad, Magha/Pausha), of which **Sharad Navaratri** is the most observed and **Chaitra (Vasanta) Navaratri** the second, ending in [Rama Navami](#v-vishnu--avatar-birthdays). [traditional]
  - *Variant (Bengal — Durga Puja):* in Bengal/Assam/Odisha the same days are **Durga Puja**, foregrounding Durga's return to her natal home and her Mahishasura victory, with elaborate *pandals* and immersion (*visarjan*). [regional]
  - *Variant (Gujarat — Garba/Dandiya):* foregrounds devotional circle-dance before the *ghatasthapana* pot-altar. The **garbo** — a perforated earthen lamp-pot cradling a flame, danced around in concentric circles — is read as the **cosmic womb** (*garbha*) and the **Devi's** indwelling *shakti*/light; the **Dandiya Raas** stick-dance echoes **Krishna's** *raas-lila* circle with the *gopis* ([Krishna Lila](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md)). See the fuller temple-festival treatment in [regional temple festivals](regional-temple-festivals.md). [regional]
- **Dussehra / Vijayadashami** ("tenth [day], victory-tenth") - the tenth day capping Navaratri; **two** charter-events fused. (Britannica, *Navratri*; Wikipedia, *Navaratri*; Sri Somesvara Temple)
  - *Variant (North — Rama-Ravana):* commemorates **Rama** (7th [dashavatara](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)) slaying the ten-headed **Ravana** to recover Sita — the climax of the [Yuddha Kanda](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md); celebrated as **Ramlila** with the burning of Ravana effigies. Treta Yuga event. [traditional]
  - *Variant (East/South — Durga-Mahishasura):* marks **Durga's** beheading of Mahishasura and the close of Durga Puja. [regional]
  - *Variant (Mahabharata gloss):* the day the **Pandavas** retrieved their hidden weapons from the *shami* tree, ending their incognito year ([Virata Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/04-virata-parva.md)); *Shami-puja* and *Apta-leaf* exchange derive from this. [folk]
- **Ayudha Puja / Astra Puja** ("worship of weapons/tools") - chiefly **Maha Navami** (9th day of Navaratri, eve of Vijayadashami); weapons, tools, vehicles, machines, instruments, and books are worshipped as forms of the **Goddess** (Saraswati in the South, Durga in the East), cleaned, decked, and rested for the day. (Britannica; Wikipedia, *Ayudha Puja*)
  - *Variant (Durga's arming):* charter-linked to **Durga** being armed by the devas — each god yielding his weapon — for the war on **Mahishasura** ([Devi Mahatmya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)). [traditional]
  - *Variant (Pandavas' shami tree):* tied to the **Pandavas** recovering their weapons hidden in the *shami* tree at the close of their incognito year ([Virata Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/04-virata-parva.md)) — the same Shami-puja strand noted under Vijayadashami. [folk]
  - *Note (distinctness):* Ayudha Puja (autumn, Goddess-keyed) is distinct from **Vishwakarma Puja** (the artisans' festival of the divine architect, below in Section VI), though both honor implements of craft. [scholarly]
- **Diwali / Deepavali** ("row of lamps") - five-day festival of lights around **Kartik Amavasya** (new moon), the year's most pan-Hindu festival; no single charter — a stack of timeline-events keyed to consecutive *tithis*. (National Geographic, *Diwali*; Wikipedia, *Diwali*; Devdutt Pattanaik; Google Arts & Culture)
  - *Variant (North — Rama's return):* the main night marks **Rama, Sita and Lakshmana** returning to **Ayodhya** after 14 years' exile and the Ravana-war ([Uttara Kanda](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md)); Ayodhya lit lamps to guide them. Treta Yuga. [traditional]
  - *Variant (South — Naraka Chaturdashi):* day before Amavasya marks **Krishna** (with Satyabhama) slaying the asura **Narakasura** of Pragjyotishapura and freeing 16,000 captives — referenced in the **Vishnu Purana** and **Harivamsha**; Dvapara Yuga. Main "Deepavali" day in Tamil Nadu, Andhra, Telangana, Kerala. [traditional] (National Geographic)
  - *Variant (Lakshmi Puja):* Amavasya night as the worship of **Lakshmi**, who (in one strand) emerged from the [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md) and is invoked for prosperity. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Bali Pratipada / Bali Padyami):* the fourth day commemorates **Vamana** (5th avatar) pushing **Bali** to the netherworld — and, in Kerala/Maharashtra, Bali's permitted yearly return; see Onam below. [regional]
  - *Variant (Yama / ancestors):* a strand links the lamps to **Yama** and the honoring of *pitr* (ancestors). [folk] (Devdutt Pattanaik)
  - *Variant (Bhai Dooj / Bhau Beej — the closing fifth day):* **Yama Dwitiya / Bhratri Dwitiya**, *Kartik Shukla Dvitiya* (2nd), the pentad's final day; **Yama** (lord of death) visits his sister **Yamuna** (the river-goddess), who welcomes, *tilaks*, and feasts him — moved, Yama decrees that any brother thus honored by a sister on this day escapes **Naraka**. Sisters apply *tilak* and pray for brothers' long life; cf. the [Raksha Bandhan](#vii-kinship--marital-vratas--the-womens-vows) brother-sister vrata. [traditional] (Drik Panchang)

## II. Spring cluster — Holi and the burning of evil and desire

- **Vasant / Basant Panchami** (also **Sri Panchami**) - *Magha Shukla Panchami* (5th); the Vedic onset of spring (*vasanta*) and the festival of **Saraswati**, goddess of learning, music, and speech. (Wikipedia, *Vasant Panchami*; Drik Panchang)
  - *Variant (Saraswati's manifestation):* celebrated as **Saraswati's birthday/appearance** — in one strand **Brahma** brought her forth to give voice, melody, and order to a silent creation; the day's foremost worship is of the [goddess Saraswati](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saraswati.md), books and instruments laid before her. [traditional]
  - *Variant (aksharabhyasa):* the auspicious day for a child's first writing of letters (*aksharabhyasa* / *vidyarambha*), placed on the tongue and traced in grain or sand. [traditional]
  - *Variant (seasonal / regional):* yellow (mustard-bloom) dress, **kite-flying** (esp. North India and Punjab), and the lighting/erecting of the **Holika** pyre forty days before — Vasant Panchami traditionally opens the run-up to [Holi](#ii-spring-cluster--holi-and-the-burning-of-evil-and-desire). [regional]
- **Holi** - spring full-moon (*Phalguna Purnima*) festival of colors, preceded by the **Holika Dahan** bonfire on the eve; the most famous origin is the burning of the demoness **Holika**. (Britannica, *Holi*; Wikipedia, *Holi*; History.com)
  - *Variant (Holika-Prahlada):* **Hiranyakashipu**, the asura father of the Vishnu-devotee **Prahlada**, sends his fire-immune sister **Holika** to burn the boy on her lap; she burns instead and Prahlada is spared — prefiguring the **Narasimha** [avatar](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md). The Holika bonfire enacts good's victory over evil. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Radha-Krishna / Braj):* the play of color originates in **Krishna** smearing color on **Radha** and the *gopis* in Vrindavan ([Krishna Lila](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md)); the devotional heart of Braj Holi. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Lathmar Holi, Barsana):* women of Radha's natal village **Barsana** beat the men of Krishna's **Nandgaon** with staves (*lath*) — a localized Braj enactment. [regional/folk]
  - *Variant (South — Kamadahana):* in Tamil Nadu and the South the festival recalls **Shiva** burning **Kama** (the love-god) to ash with his third eye; the bonfire is *Kama Dahanam*, devotion triumphing over desire — see [Shiva myth cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md). [regional]

## III. Shaiva festivals — Shiva and the Goddess

- **Maha Shivaratri** ("great night of Shiva") - the *Chaturdashi* (14th) of the dark fortnight of Magha/Phalguna; an all-night vigil and fast for **Shiva**, the year's foremost Shaiva *vrata*. Multiple charters. (Britannica, *Maha-shivaratri*; Hindu American Foundation)
  - *Variant (Lingodbhava):* the **Linga Purana** ties the night to the appearance of the infinite **jyotirlinga** (pillar of light) whose top and bottom **Brahma** and **Vishnu** could not find — Shiva's formless infinity; basis of the [12 Jyotirlingas](../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md). [traditional/late text]
  - *Variant (Halahala):* the night Shiva drank the **halahala** poison thrown up by the [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md), holding it in his throat (**Nilakantha**) to save creation; devotees keep vigil as he did. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Shiva-Parvati wedding):* celebrated as the night of **Shiva's marriage to Parvati** — see [Sati, Daksha & Parvati](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md). [traditional]
- **Kartik Purnima / Dev Diwali** ("the gods' Diwali"; also **Tripurari / Tripuri Purnima**) - *Kartik Shukla Purnima* (full moon); a great Shaiva (and cross-sectarian) full-moon *parva* with a stack of charters. (Wikipedia, *Kartik Purnima*; Drik Panchang)
  - *Variant (Tripurantaka — primary Shaiva charter):* the day **Shiva** as **Tripurantaka** destroyed the three flying cities (*Tripura*) of the asuras with a single arrow of the **Pinaka** bow — hence *Tripurari/Tripuri Purnima* — see the [Tripurāntaka form](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-avatars-and-forms.md) (*Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra Saṃhitā*; *Mahābhārata, Karṇa Parva*) and cf. [Tripurasura at Bhimashankar](../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md). [traditional]
  - *Variant (Dev Deepawali):* "the Diwali of the gods" — kept fifteen days after Diwali, most spectacularly on the **ghats of [Varanasi/Kashi](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md)**, where the gods are said to descend to bathe in the Ganga and lakhs of lamps (*deepa*) are lit on the riverfront steps. [traditional/regional]
  - *Variant (Matsya / Vaishnava strand):* tied in one Vaishnava reckoning to the appearance of **Matsya**, Vishnu's first [avatar](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md). [traditional]
  - *Variant (Kartikeya's birth):* held to be the birthday of **Kartikeya/Skanda** — see [Ganesha & Kartikeya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md). [traditional]
  - *Variant (Bhishma Panchak / Pushkar):* closes the five-day **Bhishma Panchak** fast (Prabodhini Ekadashi to Kartik Purnima) and coincides with the great **[Pushkar](../06-sacred-geography/pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md) fair and Brahma bath**, when pilgrims bathe at the Brahma-tirtha. [traditional/regional]
- **Nag Panchami** - *Shravana Shukla Panchami* (5th); worship of the **Nagas** (serpent-deities), milk offered at snake-holes and to serpent-images. (HinduPad, *Nag Panchami Vrat Katha*; iloveindia)
  - *Variant (Janamejaya's sarpa-yajna):* commemorates the sage **Astika** halting King **Janamejaya's** snake-sacrifice (*sarpa-satra*) and saving the serpent race — the [Adi Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md) frame-story of the Mahabharata. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Krishna-Kaliya):* in Braj/western strands, recalls **Krishna** subduing the serpent **Kaliya** in the Yamuna ([Krishna Lila](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md)). [regional/folk]

## IV. Avatar birthdays — Vishnu's descents on the timeline

- **Rama Navami** - *Chaitra Shukla Navami* (9th), capping **Chaitra (Vasanta) Navaratri**; birthday of **Rama**, 7th [dashavatara](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md), son of Dasharatha of Ayodhya — the opening of the [Bala Kanda](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md). Treta Yuga. (Wikipedia, *Navaratri*)
- **Krishna Janmashtami** - *Bhadrapada (Shravana in Amanta reckoning) Krishna Ashtami* (8th of the dark fortnight), at midnight; birth of **Krishna**, 8th avatar, in Mathura's prison to Devaki and Vasudeva — see [Krishna Lila](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md). Dvapara Yuga; falls within the *Chaturmas* holy period. (Wikipedia/Chaturmas)
- **Govardhan Puja / Annakut** ("mountain of food") - *Kartik Shukla Pratipada*, day after Diwali; **Krishna** lifting **Govardhan Hill** on one finger to shelter Vrindavan from **Indra's** rains, teaching worship of nature over Indra — **Bhagavata Purana**; mountains of food (*annakut*) offered. Dvapara Yuga. (Eshwar Bhakti, *Govardhan Puja*)
- **Vamana / Onam** - Kerala's harvest festival (*Thiruvonam*, Shravana asterism, month of *Chingam*); the annual return of the just asura-king **Mahabali**, whom **Vamana** (5th avatar) had pushed to *Patala* after the three-strides boon. Mahabali is permitted one yearly visit to his people, who keep his memory with *pookalam* and feast. Cross-references the Diwali **Bali Pratipada** strand. (Kerala Tourism, *Mahabali legend*; Wikipedia, *Onam*)
- **Narasimha Jayanti** - *Vaishakha Shukla Chaturdashi*; appearance of **Narasimha** (man-lion, 4th avatar) to slay **Hiranyakashipu** and save **Prahlada** — the same asura-lineage as Holi above. [traditional]

## V. Ganesha, Hanuman, and the divine sons

- **Ganesh Chaturthi / Vinayaka Chaturthi** - *Bhadrapada Shukla Chaturthi* (4th, Aug–Sep); birth/installation of **Ganesha**, dissolved in water (*visarjan*) after 1.5–10 days. (Britannica; theadvitya; Isha)
  - *Variant (birth-myth):* **Parvati** forms Ganesha from her body's paste/turmeric; **Shiva** beheads the boy guarding her door and restores him with an elephant's head — see [Ganesha & Kartikeya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md). [traditional]
  - *Variant (scribe of the Mahabharata):* Ganesha as the scribe to whom **Vyasa** dictated the [Mahabharata](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/README.md) — invoked at the festival as lord of letters and beginnings. [traditional]
  - *Variant (public festival):* the modern mass *sarvajanik* form was popularized in Maharashtra by **Lokmanya Tilak** (1893) — a datable [historical](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md) layer atop the Puranic myth. [scholarly]
- **Hanuman Jayanti** - birthday of **Hanuman**, the *vanara* devotee of Rama and a [chiranjivi](../04-deep-dives/the-chiranjivis.md); a partial incarnation of **Shiva/Vayu** — see [Hanuman](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md). Treta Yuga origin. (Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (calendar):* dated to **Chaitra Purnima** in the North, but to **margazhi Amavasya** (Dec–Jan) in Tamil Nadu and to other dates in Maharashtra/Karnataka. [regional]
- **Kartikeya / Skanda Shashthi** - sixth-day festival (esp. Tamil Nadu) for **Kartikeya/Murugan**, marking his slaying of the asura **Surapadma** — see [Ganesha & Kartikeya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md). [regional]

## VI. Solar / harvest festivals — the Sun's transit

- **Makar Sankranti** - the **Sun's transit into Makara (Capricorn)** (~14 Jan), one of the few **solar-fixed** Hindu festivals; marks **Uttarayana**, the Sun's auspicious northward course, and the winter harvest. Dedicated to **Surya** — see [Navagraha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md). (Britannica; Wikipedia, *Makar Sankranti / Uttarayana*)
  - *Variant (Bhishma):* the day **Bhishma**, on his bed of arrows, chose to die — he had the boon of *icchamrityu* and waited for Uttarayana before releasing his life ([Anushasana Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/13-anushasana-parva.md)). Dvapara Yuga. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Sankranti-Sankarasura):* Puranic legend of the goddess **Sankranti** slaying the demon **Sankarasura**. [folk/late text]
  - *Variant (regional names):* **Pongal** (Tamil Nadu, 4-day), **Lohri** (Punjab, eve), **Uttarayan/Kite festival** (Gujarat), **Maghi** (North), **Bihu** (Assam, as Magh Bihu). [regional]
- **Ratha Saptami** (also **Surya Jayanti / Magha Saptami**) - *Magha Shukla Saptami* (7th); the **Sun-god's** birthday and awakening, when **Surya's** seven-horsed **chariot** (*ratha*) turns decisively northward — a solar new-beginning. Ritual dawn bathing with **arka** (*Calotropis*) leaves placed on the head and shoulders; see [Surya / Navagraha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md). (Drik Panchang; Wikipedia, *Ratha Saptami*) [traditional]
- **Vishwakarma Puja / Jayanti** - the artisans' and craftsmen's festival of **Vishvakarma**, the **divine architect** and maker of the gods' weapons, chariots, and cities; tools, machines, and workshops are worshipped. Dated to **Kanya Sankranti** (~17 Sep, the Sun's transit into Virgo) in the East (Bengal, Odisha, Assam, Bihar), with a separate **Magha** observance elsewhere — see [Vishvakarma & Tvashtar](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishvakarma-and-tvashtar.md). Distinct from the autumn Goddess-keyed **Ayudha Puja** (Section I). (Wikipedia, *Vishwakarma Puja*; Drik Panchang) [traditional/regional]

## VII. Kinship & marital vratas — the women's vows

- **Raksha Bandhan** - *Shravana Purnima*; a sister ties a protective thread (*rakhi*) on her brother's wrist. Multiple charters. (Amar Chitra Katha; Bablarakhi)
  - *Variant (Bali-Lakshmi):* **Lakshmi**, disguised as a poor woman, ties a thread on **Bali** (whom Vishnu was guarding in Patala) to win back her husband — links to the Vamana/Onam cycle. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Indra-Indrani):* **Indrani** tied a sacred thread on **Indra** before his war with the asuras, ensuring victory — an early *raksha* charter. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Krishna-Draupadi):* **Draupadi** bound her torn *sari* on **Krishna's** bleeding finger; he vowed lifelong protection — repaid at the *vastra-haran* ([Sabha Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md)). [folk]
- **Vat Savitri / Vat Purnima** - married women fast and circumambulate a **banyan (vata) tree**, tying thread, for the husband's long life; enacts **Savitri** compelling **Yama** to restore her husband **Satyavan** — the [Savitri-Satyavan](../04-deep-dives/savitri-satyavan.md) *upakhyana* of the [Vana Parva](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md). (Wikipedia, *Savitri Vrata / Vata Purnima*; Dharmayug)
  - *Variant (calendar):* observed on **Jyeshtha Amavasya** in North India but on **Jyeshtha Purnima** (Vat Purnima) in Maharashtra/Gujarat/South. [regional]
- **Karva Chauth** - *Kartik Krishna Chaturthi* (4th of the dark fortnight); married women fast from dawn to moonrise for the husband's longevity, breaking the fast on sighting the moon through a sieve. Charter-stories include the tale of **Queen Veeravati** and a Mahabharata gloss in which **Draupadi** kept the fast on Krishna's advice for the **Pandavas**. Chiefly North/Northwest Indian. [traditional/folk] [regional]
- **Teej** (Hariyali / Hartalika / Kajari) - monsoon vows for marital welfare honoring **Parvati's** reunion with **Shiva** after her austerities — see [Sati, Daksha & Parvati](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md). [regional]

## VIII. Periodic and lunar vratas — the recurring observances

- **Ekadashi** - the **eleventh** *tithi* of each fortnight (≈24/year), a fast sacred to **Vishnu**; abstention from grains for spiritual purification and to "wake" or "sleep" Vishnu at the bounds of *Chaturmas*. (Wikipedia/Chaturmas)
  - *Variant (named Ekadashis):* each has its own *katha* — e.g. **Vaikuntha (Mokshada) Ekadashi** (the day the [Bhagavad Gita](../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md) was spoken), **Devshayani** (Vishnu's cosmic sleep begins) and **Prabodhini/Devuthani** (Vishnu wakes), bounding the four-month *Chaturmas*. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Ajamila / Ekadashi-mahatmya):* the **Padma Purana** personifies *Ekadashi* as a power born to slay the demon **Mura**; cf. the deliverance theme of [Ajamila](../04-deep-dives/ajamila.md). [late text]
- **Tulsi Vivah** ("Tulsi's wedding") - the ceremonial marriage of the sacred basil **Tulsi** (the deified **Vrinda**) to **Vishnu** in his **Shaligram** (sacred-stone) form, performed from **Prabodhini (Devuthani) Ekadashi** through **Kartik Purnima**; it marks **Vishnu waking** from his four-month *Chaturmas* sleep and so opens the auspicious **wedding season**. (Wikipedia, *Tulsi Vivah*; Drik Panchang)
  - *Variant (Vrinda–Jalandhara origin):* the **Padma Purana** charter — **Vrinda**, the chaste wife of the asura **Jalandhara**, whose virtue made him invincible; Vishnu broke it by taking Jalandhara's form, and the cursing/blessing Vrinda became the **Tulsi** plant ever wed to Vishnu — see [Padma Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/padma-purana.md). [traditional/late text]
- **Pradosha** - the *Trayodashi* (13th) twilight vrata for **Shiva**, tied to the [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md): the gods propitiated Shiva at *pradosha* after he drank the halahala. [traditional]
- **Sankashti / Vinayaka Chaturthi** - the *Chaturthi* (4th) of each fortnight, monthly fasts to **Ganesha**. [traditional]
- **Purnima vratas (Satyanarayana)** - full-moon worship of **Vishnu as Satyanarayana** with the **Skanda Purana** *katha*; performed on any auspicious Purnima. [traditional]

## IX. The guru and the sages

- **Guru Purnima / Vyasa Purnima** - *Ashadha Purnima*; honors the **guru** lineage, dated to the birth (and the day of Veda-division) of **Veda Vyasa**, the **Adi Guru** who split the one Veda into four and composed the [Mahabharata](../04-deep-dives/mahabharata/README.md) and Puranas — see the [Saptarishi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md) and [literature corpus](../90-literature-corpus/README.md). Opens the *Chaturmas* monastic retreat. (Britannica, *Guru Purnima*; Wikipedia, *Vyasa*; pujayagna)
  - *Variant (Buddhist):* the same full moon marks the **Buddha's** first sermon at Sarnath; observed as Guru Purnima across Indic traditions. [traditional]
- **Akshaya Tritiya** - *Vaishakha Shukla Tritiya*; an "imperishable" (*akshaya*) auspicious day with stacked charters — the birth of **Parashurama** (6th avatar), the descent of the **Ganga**, the start of **Treta Yuga** in one reckoning, and the day **Vyasa** began dictating the Mahabharata to **Ganesha**. [traditional] (cf. [yugas](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md))

## Notes on dating & method

- **Two clocks.** Each festival has a **mythic timeline-date** (the [yuga](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md)/[manvantara](../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md) of its charter-event — Treta for Rama-festivals, Dvapara for Krishna-festivals, cosmogonic/Manthana for Shaiva ones) and a **calendar-date** (its annual *tithi* or *sankranti*). These are independent and both are noted above. [traditional]
- **Charter vs. practice [scholarly].** The Puranic charter-myth is usually older than the festival's *attested mass observance*; some forms are demonstrably modern (e.g. Tilak's public Ganesh Chaturthi, 1893) — see [recorded history](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md).
- **Variant policy.** Where a festival carries multiple incompatible charters (Diwali, Holi, Maha Shivaratri, Raksha Bandhan), all are catalogued as attributed; regional and calendar (Amavasya vs. Purnima; Amanta vs. Purnimanta month reckoning) variants are tagged [regional].
