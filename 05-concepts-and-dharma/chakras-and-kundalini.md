# Chakras & Kuṇḍalinī

> Cross-cutting doctrine within [05-concepts-and-dharma](./README.md) of *The Hindu Timeline* — the **subtle-body map and serpent-energy soteriology** that underlies the tantric and haṭha-yoga streams. Back to the [master index](../README.md).
> See the practice context in [yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md) (§2, Kuṇḍalinī/Laya Yoga), the textual catalog in [../90-literature-corpus/agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md](../90-literature-corpus/agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md), and the Shakta theology in [../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md).

The chakra–kuṇḍalinī complex maps the human body as a **microcosm of the cosmos** (*piṇḍa–brahmāṇḍa* identity): a network of subtle channels (*nāḍī*) carrying vital wind (*prāṇa*), strung along a central axis on which lie energy-wheels (*cakra*), with the divine feminine power (*kuṇḍalinī-śakti*) sleeping coiled at the base. Liberation in this idiom is the **ascent of Śakti through the central channel to reunite with Śiva at the crown**, dissolving (*laya*) individuality into pure consciousness. NB the **two clocks**: scripturally this is the body's eternal anatomy revealed by Śiva to the Goddess; academically the standardised seven-wheel serpent scheme is a **medieval tantric systematisation (c. 8th–16th c. CE)** with no full counterpart in the Veda or early Upaniṣads. [scholarly]

---

## 1. The Subtle Body (*sūkṣma-śarīra*) & the Nāḍīs

- **The three bodies** - Vedāntic/tantric anatomy nests three sheaths: *sthūla-śarīra* (gross physical), *sūkṣma-śarīra* (subtle/astral, where prāṇa, mind, nāḍīs and chakras operate), and *kāraṇa-śarīra* (causal). The chakra system is an organ of the **subtle body**, not dissectible in the gross body. (Vedānta; tantric/haṭha texts.)
- **Prāṇa & the five vāyus** - The life-wind animating the subtle body, subdivided into five *vāyus* — *prāṇa*, *apāna*, *samāna*, *udāna*, *vyāna* (plus five "minor" winds in some lists). Haṭha yoga's aim is to still and unite *prāṇa* (upward) with *apāna* (downward) to force prāṇa into the central channel. (Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā; Upaniṣads.)
- **72,000 nāḍīs** - The standard tantric count of channels radiating through the subtle body; some texts give other figures (e.g. *Śiva Saṃhitā* 350,000; *Gheraṇḍa Saṃhitā* and others vary). Of these, three are primary. (Haṭha texts; tantric Āgamas.)
  - *Variant (count):* totals of **72,000 / 80,000 / 200,000 / 350,000** appear across texts; the roster is open-ended and schematic rather than fixed. [disputed]
- **Iḍā — the lunar channel** - Runs on the **left**, terminating at the left nostril; cooling, feminine, *candra*/moon, associated with the right brain hemisphere and the Ganges (*Gaṅgā*) in the body-as-pilgrimage map. (Tantric & haṭha sources; named *Śaśī* "moon" in the *Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa*.)
- **Piṅgalā — the solar channel** - Runs on the **right**, terminating at the right nostril; heating, masculine, *sūrya*/sun, left brain hemisphere, the Yamunā in the body-map. (Tantric & haṭha sources; named *Mihira* "sun" in the *Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa*.)
  - *Note:* iḍā and piṅgalā spiral around the central channel, crossing at the chakras (the caduceus-like image); breath naturally alternates dominance between the two nostrils — *svara yoga* tracks this cycle.
- **Suṣumnā — the central channel** - The axial *brahma-nāḍī* running up the spinal column from *mūlādhāra* to *sahasrāra*; normally closed/dormant. Kuṇḍalinī rises only here. Identified with the hidden Sarasvatī (*triveṇī* confluence at the *ājñā*) and, microcosmically, with Mount Meru / the world-axis. (Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa; Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā.)
  - *Variant (inner channels):* later texts nest finer channels **inside** suṣumnā — *vajrā/vajriṇī*, *citriṇī*, and the innermost *brahma-nāḍī* — through which kuṇḍalinī actually threads. (Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa.) [late text]

## 2. The Seven Chakras (the standard scheme)

The dominant model (from the *Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa* via Avalon/Woodroffe's 1919 *The Serpent Power*, which fixed the modern image) lists six chakras along suṣumnā plus the transcendent crown. Each is visualised as a lotus with a fixed petal-count (the petals bear Sanskrit syllables), a *bīja* (seed-mantra), a *tattva* (element), a presiding deity and *śakti*, and a *yantra*-geometry. Counts and attributions vary by text — see §3.

- **Mūlādhāra — root, base of spine/perineum** - 4-petal crimson lotus; element **earth (pṛthivī)**, bīja **LAṂ**, yellow square yantra; deity child-Brahmā + śakti Ḍākinī; survival, grounding. **Kuṇḍalinī sleeps coiled here**, three-and-a-half times around the *svayambhu-liṅga*. (Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa.)
  - *Etymology note:* the *Kubjikāmata Tantra* (c. 10th c.) calls the base simply *ādhāra* ("support"); *mūla-* ("root") is a later prefix. [scholarly]
- **Svādhiṣṭhāna — sacral, genitals/lower abdomen** - 6-petal vermilion lotus; element **water (āp)**, bīja **VAṂ**, white crescent-moon yantra; deity Viṣṇu + śakti Rākiṇī; emotion, pleasure, creativity, the unconscious. (Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa.)
- **Maṇipūra — "city of jewels," navel/solar plexus** - 10-petal blue lotus; element **fire (tejas/agni)**, bīja **RAṂ**, red triangle yantra; deity Rudra + śakti Lākiṇī; will, power, digestion, transformation. (Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa.)
- **Anāhata — "unstruck (sound)," heart** - 12-petal lotus; element **air (vāyu)**, bīja **YAṂ**, smoky-grey hexagram (*ṣaṭkoṇa*) yantra; deity Īśa + śakti Kākinī; love, compassion, balance. Seat of the *jīvātman* and the inner "unstruck" sound (*anāhata-nāda*). (Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa.)
- **Viśuddha — "pure," throat** - 16-petal smoky-purple lotus; element **ether/space (ākāśa)**, bīja **HAṂ**, white circle yantra; deity Sadāśiva + śakti Śākinī; speech, truth, expression. (Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa.)
- **Ājñā — "command," between/behind the eyebrows** - 2-petal white lotus (petals = HAṂ + KṢAṂ, the syllables of iḍā/piṅgalā); the **"third eye,"** seat of *manas*/mind; deity Paramaśiva as the *itara-liṅga* + śakti Hākinī; the bīja **OṂ**. Here iḍā, piṅgalā and suṣumnā are said to **merge** (inner triveṇī). Transcends the five gross elements (*tattvātīta*). (Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa.)
- **Sahasrāra — "thousand-petalled," crown / above the head** - The thousand-petalled lotus, **outside the chakra series proper** (above suṣumnā's mouth, the *brahmarandhra*). Abode of **Paramaśiva**; the goal of kuṇḍalinī's ascent, where Śakti reunites with Śiva (*Śiva–Śakti-sāmarasya*), producing the nectar (*amṛta*) of liberation. Beyond element, color, sound. (Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa.)
  - *Variant (between ājñā and sahasrāra):* many tantras insert subtle stations — **bindu/candra, nāda, śakti, vyāpinī, samanā, unmanā**, and a *soma/lalāṭa* (forehead) chakra and *manas/talu* (palate) chakra — graded ascents that the popular seven-wheel chart omits. (Tantric corpus.) [late text]

## 3. System Variants — How Many Chakras?

The "seven chakras" are a modern default; classical tantra is far more plural. Catalog of attested schemes:

- *Variant (six — ṣaṭ-cakra):* the heading of the very source-text — *Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa* = "description of the **six** chakras" — counts mūlādhāra→ājñā as six and treats **sahasrāra as the transcendent goal, not a seventh wheel**. The popular "seven" simply adds the crown to the list. (Pūrṇānanda, *Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa*, 1577.)
- *Variant (six in the Kubjikā tradition):* the **Kubjikāmata Tantra** (c. 10th c. CE) is among the earliest to record a six-chakra column; the Kaula/Kubjikā streams are a historical seedbed of the system. [scholarly]
- *Variant (five — Kubjikā/Kaula sub-system):* a **five-chakra** scheme is documented within the Kubjikāmata corpus (studied by Heilijgers-Seelen), showing the count was unsettled even within one lineage. [scholarly]
- *Variant (six in the Netra Tantra):* the **Netra Tantra** describes six chakras; the seven-wheel version stabilised only a few centuries later. [scholarly]
- *Variant (eight):* the **Kaulajñāna-nirṇaya** (attributed to Matsyendranātha) describes an **eight-chakra** system. [scholarly]
- *Variant (twelve):* the **Vijñāna-bhairava Tantra** (and other Trika/Kashmir Śaiva sources) reference up to **twelve** stations along the central channel. [scholarly]
- *Variant (nine):* some later/Nāth and folk syntheses teach **nine chakras**, adding stations such as *sūrya/soma* and *lalāṭa* above ājñā. [folk]
- *Variant (six Upaniṣadic ṣaṭ-cakra):* the **Yoga-cūḍāmaṇi**, **Yoga-śikhā** and other Yoga Upaniṣads (late, c. 13th–16th c.) give six-chakra meditations — the chakra system entering the "Upaniṣad" genre after the tantras. [late text]
- *Variant (academic dating):* the Veda, principal Upaniṣads and Patañjali's *Yoga Sūtras* lack the chakra-and-serpent map; the standardised system is a **medieval tantric/haṭha development** (Tantras c. 8th c. onward; haṭha synthesis 11th–15th c.; Western fixation via Woodroffe 1919). [scholarly]

## 4. Kuṇḍalinī-Śakti — the Coiled Serpent Power

- **Kuṇḍalinī defined** - *Kuṇḍalinī* ("she who is coiled," from *kuṇḍala* "ring/coil") — the cosmic feminine energy (Śakti) present in the individual as a dormant force coiled **three-and-a-half times** around the base *liṅga* at mūlādhāra, her mouth covering the gate of suṣumnā. She is the Goddess (Devī/Pārvatī) within the body. (Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa; Śakta tantras.)
  - *Variant (mythic identity):* identified variously as **Kuṇḍalinī = Pārvatī/Durgā/Kālī**, or as the serpent **Ananta/Śeṣa** in microcosm, or as the goddess **Kubjikā** (the "crooked/coiled one") in the Kubjikā tradition. [folk]
- **Awakening (*kuṇḍalinī-jāgaraṇa*)** - Roused by combined prāṇāyāma, mūla-bandha, mantra, mudrā and guru's grace (*śaktipāta*); the woken serpent uncoils and **rises through suṣumnā**, piercing each chakra in turn until she reaches sahasrāra. (Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā 3; Śiva Saṃhitā.)
- **Union & liberation** - At the crown, **Śakti merges with Śiva**; the resulting bliss-nectar (*amṛta*) floods the body and consciousness dissolves into non-dual awareness — the tantric *mokṣa* / *jīvanmukti*. (Tantric & haṭha corpus.)
- **Descent & integration** - In some schools kuṇḍalinī, having reached sahasrāra, **descends** redistributing the awakened state back through the chakras, integrating realisation into embodied life rather than remaining in absorption. (Tantric teaching.) [folk]
- **Hazards [folk/clinical]** - Traditional and modern accounts warn of premature/forced awakening ("kuṇḍalinī syndrome": heat, involuntary movements *kriyā*, psychological disturbance) without purified nāḍīs or a guru — hence haṭha's insistence on preparation. [folk]

## 5. The Three Granthis (Knots)

Three "knots" in suṣumnā bind prāṇa to the lower, middle and higher orders of attachment and must be pierced for kuṇḍalinī to pass. Named for the *trimūrti*.

- **Brahma-granthi — lower knot** - At/around **mūlādhāra–svādhiṣṭhāna**; binds to the physical, survival, sensual attachment and *tamas*. Pierced first; associated with *mūla bandha*. (Haṭha texts; Śiva Saṃhitā.)
- **Viṣṇu-granthi — middle knot** - Between **maṇipūra and anāhata** (heart region); binds to emotion, relationships, ego-assertion and *rajas*. Associated with *uḍḍīyāna bandha*. (Haṭha texts.)
- **Rudra-granthi — upper knot** - Between **viśuddha and ājñā** (third-eye region); binds to subtle attachment, psychic powers (*siddhi*) and intellectual identity; final knot before union at sahasrāra. Associated with *jālandhara bandha*. (Haṭha texts.)
  - *Variant (locations):* texts disagree on exact placements — Brahma-granthi is variously at mūlādhāra alone or spanning mūlādhāra–svādhiṣṭhāna; Rudra-granthi at ājñā itself in some accounts. [disputed]

## 6. Kuṇḍalinī- / Haṭha-Yoga Method

The practical toolkit for purifying the nāḍīs and rousing the serpent (overlaps [yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md) §2):

- **Ṣaṭkarma — six cleansings** - *Dhauti, basti, neti, trāṭaka, nauli, kapālabhāti* — purify the body before serious prāṇāyāma. (Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā 2.)
- **Āsana & Prāṇāyāma** - Steady posture plus breath-retention (*kumbhaka*, esp. *nāḍī-śodhana* alternate-nostril) to balance iḍā/piṅgalā and force prāṇa into suṣumnā. (HYP 1–2.)
- **Bandhas — locks** - *Mūla bandha* (root), *uḍḍīyāna bandha* (abdominal), *jālandhara bandha* (throat) — the *tri-bandha*, paired with the three granthis (see §5). (HYP 3.)
- **Mudrās & the great seals** - *Mahāmudrā, mahābandha, mahāvedha, khecarī, vajrolī, śakticālana* ("stirring the serpent") — techniques HYP credits with directly raising kuṇḍalinī. (HYP 3.)
- **Mantra & bīja** - Internal repetition of chakra bīja-syllables and **OṂ**; mantra as the sonic key to each wheel. (Tantric/mantra yoga; cf. yoga file §2.)
- **Laya & nāda** - Dissolution (*laya*) of mind by absorption in the inner *nāda* (unstruck sound, rising at anāhata) until consciousness merges at sahasrāra. (HYP 4 — *Nāda-anusandhāna*.)

## 7. Primary & Key Sources (text-clock)

- **Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā** - Svātmārāma, 15th c. CE; 4 chapters / ~389 ślokas; the standard haṭha manual (ṣaṭkarma, āsana, prāṇāyāma, mudrā/bandha, nāda) — frames haṭha as preparation for rāja yoga. [late text]
- **Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa** - Pūrṇānanda, 1577 CE (a section of his *Śrī-tattva-cintāmaṇi*); the single most influential chakra text — full petal/bīja/deity/yantra descriptions; basis of Woodroffe's *The Serpent Power*. [late text]
- **Śiva Saṃhitā** - c. 17th c. (or earlier); haṭha-tantric, detailing nāḍīs, chakras, kuṇḍalinī and yogin-types. [late text]
- **Gheraṇḍa Saṃhitā** - c. 17th c.; the *sapta-aṅga* ("seven-limb") *ghaṭastha* yoga (note: seven limbs, not Patañjali's eight). [late text]
- **Kubjikāmata Tantra** - c. 10th c. CE; root tantra of the Kubjikā/Kaula tradition; among the earliest to record the chakra column. [scholarly date]
- **Goraksha Śataka / Goraksha Paddhati** - Nāth-tradition (Gorakṣanātha lineage), pre-HYP; early chakra/kuṇḍalinī teaching. [folk lineage]
- **Yoga Upaniṣads** - *Yoga-cūḍāmaṇi*, *Yoga-śikhā*, *Dhyānabindu*, *Yoga-kuṇḍalinī* etc. (late, c. 13th–16th c.); carry the ṣaṭ-cakra into the Upaniṣadic genre. [late text]
- **Western transmission** - Sir John Woodroffe ("Arthur Avalon"), *The Serpent Power* (1919) — translated the *Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa* and fixed the seven-rainbow-chakra image now globally dominant. [scholarly]
