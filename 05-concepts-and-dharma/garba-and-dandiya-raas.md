# Garba & Dandiya Rāsa — Gujarat's Navarātri Circle-Dance of the Goddess

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Concepts & Dharma](README.md) → **Garba & Dandiya Rāsa**. The distinctively **Gujarati / Western-Indian** form of the pan-Hindu nine-night Goddess festival — a mass-participatory **circle-dance around a perforated lamp-pot (the *garbo*)** read as the cosmic womb (*garbha*) holding the indwelling *śakti* of the Devī.
>

**Garbā** (Gujarati *garbo / garbā*) and **Dāṇḍiyā Rāsa** are the two paired dances of the **nine-night Navarātri** of Gujarat — the largest such festival in Western India and, by participation, **one of the world's largest religious dance events**, drawing tens of thousands nightly in stadium-scale *grounds* across the state and the Gujarati diaspora. Both are danced for the **Goddess** — **Ambā / Ambājī / Mātājī** (Durgā, Mahā-Śakti) — over the *Āśvina Śukla* nine nights of **Sharad Navarātri** (Sep–Oct). As elsewhere in the corpus, **scripture-date and observance-date are kept distinct**: the *charter* is the Purāṇic Shakta substrate (the *Devī Māhātmya*; the Bhāgavata *rāsa-līlā*), while the *attested mass-festival* in its present stadium form is largely **medieval-to-modern**, Gujarati and folk-rooted, and only recently codified [scholarly].

---

## I. The *garbo* — the perforated lamp-pot as cosmic womb

- **Garbo / Garbā-dīp** — the eponymous object: a **perforated earthen pot** (*garbo*, "womb-pot") pierced with holes and holding a **lit lamp (*dīpa*) within**, often topped with a coconut or a betel-areca, set at the centre of the dance-ground or carried on the head; the lamp-light streaming through the holes is read as the **divine light of the Devī shining out through the body / the cosmos**. The word *garbā* derives from **Sanskrit *garbha*** ("womb"), via *garbha-dīpa* ("womb-lamp"). [traditional]
  - *Variant (garbha-śakti / cosmic-womb reading):* the pot is the **womb of creation (*garbha*)** and the inner lamp the **indwelling *śakti*** — the single life-flame of the Goddess animating the universe; dancers circling it enact the **created world revolving around the unmoved generative centre**. [traditional]/[scholarly]
  - *Variant (concentric circles):* the dance proceeds in **concentric rings** (*maṇḍala*) turning around the central *garbo* or a Devī image — a **circumambulation (*pradakṣiṇā*)** of the Goddess, the moving body of devotees mirroring cyclic time and the wheel of creation. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (the woman as garbha):* in folk reading the lamp-pot carried on the head equates the **female body itself with the *garbha***, the festival foregrounding the **generative feminine** — danced traditionally and overwhelmingly by women, men joining especially in the *dāṇḍiyā* form. [folk]
  - *Note (Garbī, the male/devotional form):* a distinct older Gujarati genre, the **Garbī** — devotional *garbā*-songs (notably those of the poet-saint **Dayārām**, 18th–19th c., often Kṛṣṇa-themed) — sits beside the women's *garbā* round the lamp; the two share the name but the song-repertoire differs. [regional]

## II. The Shakta Navarātri substrate — the *Devī Māhātmya* charter

- **Sharad Navarātri** — the nine nights of the **Goddess** on which the whole festival sits: the worship of **Durgā / Ambā** and her victory over the buffalo-demon, the pan-Hindu autumn cluster catalogued in [Festivals & Vratas](festivals-and-vratas.md#i-autumn--sharad-cluster); Gujarat's *garbā/dāṇḍiyā* is its **regional dance-form**, as Bengal's *pandal*-Durgā Pūjā and Mysore's *Dasara* are theirs. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Devī Māhātmya / Durgā Saptaśatī charter):* the scriptural charter is the **Devī Māhātmya** (the *Caṇḍī / Durgā Saptaśatī*, embedded in the *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa*), in which the **Goddess slays Mahiṣāsura** the buffalo-demon (and Madhu-Kaiṭabha, Śumbha-Niśumbha) — the nine nights re-enacting her war; the text presents the Devī as the **primordial *śakti*, the womb-energy (*prakṛti / mūla-prakṛti*)** from which all gods draw power, directly underwriting the *garbha-śakti* reading of the *garbo*. [traditional]
  - *Variant (nine forms / Navadurgā):* the nine nights are mapped to the **Nava-durgā** (Śailaputrī … Siddhidātrī), a night per form; in Gujarat the presiding face is **Ambā / Ambājī**, the great mother-goddess. [traditional]/[regional]
  - *Variant (śakti-pīṭha substrate):* Ambājī (below) is counted in tradition among the **Śakti Pīṭhas** (the *heart* of Satī fell there in some lists) — tying Gujarat's Navarātri to the pan-Indian Goddess geography catalogued in [51 Śakti Pīṭhas](../06-sacred-geography/51-shakti-peethas.md). [regional]/[disputed]

## III. Dāṇḍiyā Rāsa — the stick-dance and the Kṛṣṇa *rāsa-līlā* echo

- **Dāṇḍiyā Rāsa** — the **paired stick-dance**: dancers in two facing rings strike **short decorated sticks (*dāṇḍiyā*)** against a partner's in rhythmic, accelerating patterns, rotating so each dancer meets every other — a **martial-playful** dance distinct from the flowing women's *garbā*, danced by men and women together. [traditional]/[regional]
  - *Variant (Kṛṣṇa's rāsa-līlā charter):* tradition reads *dāṇḍiyā/rāsa* as an echo of **Kṛṣṇa's *rāsa-līlā*** with the **gopīs** of Vraja — the clashing sticks standing for the playful "mock-combat" of the *rāsa*, and the circle for the ring in which Kṛṣṇa multiplied himself to partner each gopī; the substrate is **Bhāgavata Purāṇa X.29–33** (the *rāsa* on the autumn full-moon). This **ties the Goddess and Kṛṣṇa threads together** in one festival. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Rāsa / Rāso lineage):* "Rāsa" names an old Gujarati–Vrindāvana circle-dance genre (*rāsa-līlā*, *rāso*); the **Tālarāsa** (clapping) and **Daṇḍarāsa** (stick) are classical *rāsa* sub-types named in dance treatises, the *dāṇḍiyā* being the surviving popular Daṇḍarāsa. [scholarly]
  - *Note (two rāsas, kept distinct):* this Kṛṣṇa-rāsa echo in *dāṇḍiyā* is a **separate strand** from the actual **Mahā-Rāsa night** of [Sharad Pūrṇimā](sharad-purnima-kojagari-lakshmi-puja.md#iii-the-mahā-rāsa--kṛṣṇas-circle-dance-in-braj) (the full moon ~days after Navarātri ends); both are *autumn* circle-dances on the Bhāgavata charter but they are **distinct occasions** — the Navarātri *dāṇḍiyā* is Goddess-keyed, the Pūrṇimā Mahā-Rāsa Kṛṣṇa-keyed. Catalogued separately per file policy. [scholarly]

## IV. The major centres — Ambājī, Ahmedabad, Vadodara

- **Ambājī (Banaskāṇṭhā, North Gujarat)** — the great **temple of Ambā Mātā**, the festival's spiritual heart; the shrine famously houses **no anthropomorphic image** but the **Śrī Yantra (*Viṣa-yantra*)** as the Goddess's aniconic form, and the **Bhādarvī Pūnam** (Bhādrapada full-moon) fair draws vast pilgrim crowds — Ambā/Ambājī being the **Mātājī** invoked across the *garbā* grounds. [regional] (Ambaji temple tradition)
- **Ahmedabad & Vadodara (Baroda)** — the great **urban festival centres**: Vadodara is renowned for the **United Way of Baroda *garbā*** and other stadium-scale grounds (tens of thousands of dancers nightly), while Ahmedabad, Surat and Rajkot host mass commercial and community *garbā/dāṇḍiyā* nights — the **modern stadium form** of the festival. [regional]
- **The Mātājī / temple tradition** — at root a **temple- and household-Goddess** rite: *garbā* is danced before a **Mātājī** image or the *garbo*-lamp, with *āratī*, *prasāda* and *jāgaraṇa* (vigil); the stadium event is the public efflorescence of a domestic and temple devotion to the **Mother**. [regional]/[folk]

## V. Scale & recognition — one of the world's largest dance festivals

- **Mass participation** — the Gujarat Navarātri *garbā/dāṇḍiyā* is among the **largest participatory religious-dance events on earth**, with single grounds hosting tens of thousands and the whole state dancing nightly for nine nights, plus huge diaspora celebrations (UK, USA, Canada, East Africa). [scholarly]
- **UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (2023)** — **"Garba of Gujarat"** was **inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2023** (18th session, Botswana, Dec 2023) — India's 15th ICH inscription — recognizing the *garbā* round the lamp-pot as a living **devotional, communal and unifying** tradition. [scholarly] (UNESCO ICH listing, 2023)
- **Frame (regional form of a pan-Hindu festival)** — *garbā/dāṇḍiyā* is the **distinctively Gujarati / Western-Indian face of Sharad Navarātri**, standing beside Bengal's [Durgā Pūjā](festivals-and-vratas.md#i-autumn--sharad-cluster), the Mysore [Dasara](regional-temple-festivals.md), Telangana's [Bathukamma](regional-temple-festivals.md#iii-the-deccan-goddess--telanganas-flower--food-jatras) and North India's [Rāmlīlā/Dussehra](festivals-and-vratas.md#i-autumn--sharad-cluster) — **one Goddess-festival, many regional bodies**. [scholarly]

## VI. Patterns across Garbā & Dāṇḍiyā

- **The lamp-pot as the hinge** — the entire complex pivots on the **garbo**: *garbha* (womb) + *dīpa* (lamp) → the **cosmic womb holding the Goddess's light**, danced around in circles; the etymology *is* the theology. [scholarly]
- **Two charters braided** — **Shakta** (Devī Māhātmya / Mahiṣāsura victory, the *garbha-śakti*) and **Vaiṣṇava** (Bhāgavata *rāsa-līlā*, the gopīs' ring) meet in one festival — the *garbā* round the Goddess-lamp, the *dāṇḍiyā* echoing Kṛṣṇa's rāsa — **kept distinct, not fused**. [scholarly]
- **Two clocks** — the Purāṇic **charter** (Devī Māhātmya, Bhāgavata) is ancient; the **attested stadium festival** is medieval-to-modern Gujarati folk practice newly codified (2023 UNESCO) — the scripture-date vs. observance-date distinction again. [scholarly]
- **Feminine-generative core** — danced traditionally by **women** around the womb-pot, foregrounding the **generative feminine *śakti*** beneath the later martial Durgā charter — a folk-fertility substrate under the Purāṇic veneer. [scholarly]/[folk]
