# Bhakti-Rasa & the Bhāvas — Rūpa Gosvāmī's Devotional Aesthetics

> Doctrinal node within [05-concepts-and-dharma](./README.md) of *The Hindu Timeline*. The **science of devotional emotion**: how the Gauḍīya theologian **Rūpa Gosvāmī** (16th c. CE, Kali Yuga — [scholarly] dating) took the *rasa* theory of the [Nāṭya Śāstra](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/natya-shastra-and-the-classical-arts.md) and re-engineered it so that **bhakti itself becomes the supreme rasa**, tasted in five primary *bhāvas* toward Kṛṣṇa. Two clocks: the *theory* is documented 16th-century history; its *object* — the [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md) of Vraja — sits scripturally in Dvāpara Yuga (and, for Gauḍīyas, eternally in Goloka, outside yuga-time altogether).
> Companions: [The Three Mārgas](the-three-margas.md) (bhakti as a path among paths), [Nimbārka, Vallabha & Caitanya](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md) (the sampradāya context), [Rāsa-Līlā](../04-deep-dives/rasa-lila.md) (the paradigm event of the highest rasa).

---

## 1. From Stage-Rasa to Bhakti-Rasa — the Genealogy

- **The rasa-sūtra of Bharata** — the *Nāṭya Śāstra* (c. 200 BCE–200 CE [scholarly]) defines *rasa* ("taste, flavor") as aesthetic relish arising when a stable emotion is cooked by its causes and effects: *"vibhāvānubhāva-vyabhicāri-saṃyogād rasa-niṣpattiḥ"* (NŚ 6). Eight rasas on eight *sthāyi-bhāvas* (stable emotions); a ninth, **śānta**, added later — the *secular* nava-rasa scheme is cataloged at [natya-shastra-and-the-classical-arts.md](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/natya-shastra-and-the-classical-arts.md). (Nāṭya Śāstra 6–7)
- **Bhakti demoted by the poeticians** — classical Sanskrit poetics generally refused bhakti the rank of rasa: devotion to a deity (*deva-viṣayā rati*) was classed a mere *bhāva* (subordinate feeling), reducible to śānta or to śṛṅgāra's machinery — the position associated with the mainstream *alaṃkārika* line (e.g. Mammaṭa's *Kāvyaprakāśa*, 11th c.). Abhinavagupta (Kashmir, c. 1000 CE) treated bhakti as an auxiliary of śānta-rasa. [scholarly]
- **Vedāntic warrant** — the devotional aestheticians anchor the whole project in *śruti*: **"raso vai saḥ"** — "He (Brahman) is rasa; attaining this rasa one becomes blissful" (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.7). God is not the *object* of taste only but taste itself.
- **Vopadeva's precedent** — the *Muktāphala* of Vopadeva with Hemādri's commentary (13th c., Devagiri court [scholarly]) had already sorted the devotees of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa under the nava-rasa headings — an acknowledged forerunner of Rūpa's system. [scholarly]
- **Caitanya's commission at Prayāga (1516 CE)** — Caitanya Mahāprabhu instructs **Rūpa Gosvāmī** for ten days at the Daśāśvamedha-ghāṭ, Prayāga, "empowering" him with the seed of bhakti-rasa doctrine and dispatching him to Vṛndāvana. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya-līlā 19) [late text — hagiography written c. 1615]
- **Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu ("Ocean of the Nectar of Devotional Rasa")** — Rūpa completes the treatise at Vṛndāvana in **Śaka 1463 = 1541 CE** (the text's own colophon). Its architecture is an ocean's map: four **vibhāgas** (Eastern, Southern, Western, Northern divisions) each subdivided into **laharīs** ("waves"). Its opening verse crowns Kṛṣṇa **akhila-rasāmṛta-mūrti** — "the embodiment of the nectar of all rasas." (Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu [BRS] 1.1.1; colophon)
- **The great inversion** — Rūpa's move: **bhakti is not a bhāva under some rasa; Kṛṣṇa-rati (love for Kṛṣṇa) is itself the sthāyi-bhāva**, and when combined with its vibhāvas, anubhāvas, sāttvikas and vyabhicārīs it matures into **bhakti-rasa**, of which the worldly rasas are pale reflections. The definition of the topmost devotion: *"anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyaṃ jñāna-karmādy-anāvṛtam ānukūlyena kṛṣṇānuśīlanaṃ bhaktir uttamā"* — cultivation of Kṛṣṇa, favorable, free of other desire, uncovered by jñāna or karma. (BRS 1.1.11)
- **Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi** — Rūpa's companion volume devoted entirely to the highest rasa, **madhura** (the "brilliant sapphire" of conjugal love), with its heroine-typology and the escalating stages of prema (§5). (Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi)
- **The proof-text of many tastes** — one Bhāgavata verse does heavy lifting: at Kaṃsa's arena Kṛṣṇa appears *differently to each beholder* — a thunderbolt to the wrestlers, the best of males to the women, death to Kaṃsa, the Absolute to the yogīs, their own child to the elders. One God, refracted through the beholder's bhāva. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.43.17)

## 2. The Five Sthāyi-Bhāvas — Primary Devotional Rasas

Rūpa's Western Division sets out **five direct (mukhya) rasas**, each grounded in a distinct flavor of *rati* toward Kṛṣṇa, arranged in an ascending ladder of intimacy — each higher rung *containing* the qualities of those below. (BRS, Western Division / paścima-vibhāga)

- **Śānta — tranquil adoration** — peaceful absorption in Kṛṣṇa as the Absolute/Paramātman; awe without active service. Exemplars: the four Kumāras, and the self-satisfied sages drawn to Kṛṣṇa's qualities. In Gauḍīya ranking it is the *threshold* rasa — real, but lacking the possessive "my-ness" (*mamatā*) of the higher moods. (BRS 3.1)
  - *Variant (is śānta a bhakti-rasa at all?):* since śānta lacks active service, some Gauḍīya discussion treats it as marginal — devotion proper begins with dāsya; Rūpa retains it as the first of five. [disputed]
- **Dāsya (prīti) — servitude** — love as reverent service to Kṛṣṇa the master; the servant's mood of Vraja attendants (Raktaka, Patrī) and, in the wider Vaiṣṇava frame, of Hanumān toward Rāma. Adds *sevā* (active service) to śānta's stillness. (BRS 3.2)
- **Sakhya (preyas) — friendship** — love between equals; the cowherd boys Śrīdāmā, Subala, Madhumaṅgala who wrestle Kṛṣṇa, ride his shoulders, share his lunch — and Arjuna, whose friendship frames the [Bhagavad Gītā](../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md). Adds *viśrambha* (intimate trust that forgets God's godhood). (BRS 3.3)
- **Vātsalya — parental love** — love flowing *downward*: Kṛṣṇa as one's child, to be fed, protected, scolded. Exemplars: **Nanda and Yaśodā**, who bind the Absolute to a mortar for stealing butter (the *Dāmodara-līlā* — see [krishna-balya-lila.md](../04-deep-dives/krishna-balya-lila.md)). Adds the sense of being Kṛṣṇa's *guardian*. (BRS 3.4; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.9)
- **Madhurya (śṛṅgāra / ujjvala) — conjugal love** — the amorous love of the **gopīs**, with **Rādhā** supreme; the total self-gift in which every other mood is contained. Its paradigm event is the **[rāsa-līlā](../04-deep-dives/rasa-lila.md)** (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.29–33, the *rāsa-pañcādhyāyī*). Treated summarily in BRS 3.5 and exhaustively in the *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*.
- **The seven secondary (gauṇa) rasas** — Rūpa's Northern Division admits laughter (*hāsya*), wonder (*adbhuta*), heroism (*vīra*), compassion/sorrow (*karuṇa*), anger (*raudra*), fear (*bhayānaka*) and disgust (*bībhatsa*) as *indirect* devotional rasas — real but only when serving a primary mood (Yaśodā's fear for Kṛṣṇa, the wrestlers' vīra) — totaling **twelve** bhakti-rasas, with rules for which rasas are friendly (*maitrī*) or hostile (*vaira*) in combination, and the flaw of incompatible mixture (*rasābhāsa*). (BRS, Northern Division / uttara-vibhāga)

## 3. The Anatomy of a Rasa — Vibhāva & Uddīpana in Kṛṣṇa-Līlā

Rūpa's Southern Division transposes Bharata's dramaturgical apparatus, term for term, onto the devotee's inner life. (BRS, Southern Division / dakṣiṇa-vibhāga)

- **Vibhāva (excitants)** — split into **ālambana** (the "support"): **Kṛṣṇa as viṣaya** (object of love) and the **devotee as āśraya** (vessel of love); and **uddīpana** (stimulants): everything that fans remembrance of him. (BRS 2.1)
- **Uddīpanas of Vraja** — the catalog is concrete Kṛṣṇa-līlā furniture: the **flute-song** (which called the gopīs to the rāsa), the **peacock feather**, the buffalo-horn, his footprints, the **kadamba tree**, the **Yamunā**, **Govardhana hill**, the dust of Vraja, his names, his devotees. For the aesthetician, the whole sacred geography of [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md) is a bank of devotional triggers. (BRS 2.1, uddīpana section)
- **Anubhāva (ensuing acts)** — the outward actions that express the awakened emotion: dancing, rolling on the ground, singing, loud crying, stretching the limbs. (BRS 2.2)
- **Sāttvika-bhāvas (involuntary transformations)** — eight bodily signs of genuine ecstasy: **stambha** (stupor), **sveda** (sweat), **romāñca** (horripilation), **svara-bheda** (broken voice), **vepathu** (trembling), **vaivarṇya** (pallor), **aśru** (tears), **pralaya** (swoon). Gauḍīya hagiography reads all eight in Caitanya's own body. (BRS 2.3; Caitanya-caritāmṛta) [late text for the hagiographic application]
- **Vyabhicāri / sañcāri-bhāvas (transient emotions)** — the **thirty-three** flickering states (despondency, jubilation, envy, remembrance, drowsiness…) inherited from Nāṭya Śāstra 7, which pass like waves across the stable emotion and deepen its taste. (BRS 2.4; Nāṭya Śāstra 7)
- **The chemistry** — when Kṛṣṇa-rati (the sthāyi) combines with these four ingredient-classes in a heart purified by practice, it is relished as **bhakti-rasa**; the same materials in a mundane heart produce only the shadow-rasas of the theater. (BRS 2.5)

## 4. The Ladder — Sādhana → Bhāva → Prema

Rūpa's Eastern Division grades devotion into three phases, with a nine-step staircase. (BRS, Eastern Division / pūrva-vibhāga)

- **Sādhana-bhakti** — devotion as *practice*: discipline performed with the senses to awaken what is innate. Rūpa lists **sixty-four aṅgas** (limbs), beginning with taking shelter of a guru; the last five are singled out as extraordinarily potent: **association with sādhus, nāma-kīrtana (chanting the Name), hearing the Bhāgavata, residing in Mathurā-maṇḍala, and serving the mūrti with faith** — each able, in even slight contact, to produce bhāva. (BRS 1.2.90–92)
- **The nine stages** — *"ādau śraddhā tataḥ sādhu-saṅgo 'tha bhajana-kriyā…"* — **śraddhā** (faith) → **sādhu-saṅga** (holy company) → **bhajana-kriyā** (taking up practice) → **anartha-nivṛtti** (clearing of impediments) → **niṣṭhā** (steadiness) → **ruci** (taste) → **āsakti** (attachment) → **bhāva** (first dawn of ecstatic emotion) → **prema** (pure love). (BRS 1.4.15–16)
  - *Variant (Madhurya-kadambinī):* Viśvanātha Cakravartī (c. 1700 CE) devotes a whole treatise to walking this staircase stage by stage, including a taxonomy of the *anarthas* to be cleared. [late text]
- **Bhāva-bhakti** — the "sprout" (aṅkura) of prema: rati softening the heart, marked by symptoms such as forbearance, not wasting time, detachment, absence of pride, eagerness, taste for chanting. (BRS 1.3)
- **Prema-bhakti** — the ripened fruit: love dense, possessive, unbreakable even by cause for rupture. For the Gauḍīyas prema is the **fifth puruṣārtha**, *above mokṣa* — the position argued at length in Jīva Gosvāmī's *Prīti-sandarbha* (later 16th c.). (BRS 1.4; Prīti-sandarbha)
- **Beyond prema — the intensities** — in madhura-rasa alone, prema keeps escalating: **sneha → māna → praṇaya → rāga → anurāga → bhāva → mahābhāva**, the last (love at the pitch of madness, *divyonmāda*) possible only in **Rādhā** and her circle. (Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi, sthāyi-bhāva chapter)
  - *Variant (Caitanya as the taster):* Gauḍīya theology holds Caitanya to be Kṛṣṇa himself assuming **Rādhā's bhāva and luster** precisely to taste mahābhāva from the inside. (Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Ādi-līlā 1.5–6) [late text]

## 5. Vaidhī vs Rāgānugā — and Mañjarī-Bhāva

- **Vaidhī bhakti** — practice propelled by **scriptural injunction** (*vidhi*) and fear of omission: the ordinary regulated path, its motor being obedience. (BRS 1.2.6ff)
- **Rāgātmikā bhakti** — the *spontaneous*, thirst-like love **inherent in the eternal residents of Vraja** — Yaśodā's motherhood, Subala's friendship, the gopīs' passion. Not attainable by rule, and not itself a practice: it is the model. (BRS 1.2.272ff)
- **Rāgānugā bhakti** — practice that **follows (anugā) the rāgātmikā** exemplars: the sādhaka selects a Vraja resident whose mood attracts them, and serves "in the wake" of that resident — outwardly performing the sādhana limbs, **inwardly, in a meditatively conceived perfected body (siddha-deha), serving the līlā** alongside one's chosen exemplar. Two forms: *kāmānugā* (following the amorous mood) and *sambandhānugā* (following kinship moods). (BRS 1.2.294–296)
- **Mañjarī-bhāva** — the distinctive Gauḍīya refinement of kāmānugā: aspiring not to be a gopī-lover of Kṛṣṇa directly but a **mañjarī** — an adolescent handmaid of **Rādhā** — who relishes the Divine Couple's union vicariously, serving without seeking Kṛṣṇa's touch for herself; the "sweetness of service to the servant." Kavi-karṇapūra's *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* (1576 CE) identifies the Vṛndāvana Gosvāmīs with eternal mañjarīs — Rūpa himself as **Rūpa-mañjarī**. [late text]
  - *Variant (ekādaśa-bhāva):* later Gauḍīya initiation manuals (paddhatis of Gopāla-guru Gosvāmī and Dhyānacandra) formalize the guru's bestowal of an **eleven-fold siddha identity** (name, form, age, dress, service, group, etc.) for līlā-meditation — a codification not found in Rūpa's own text. [late text]
  - *Variant (gaura-nāgara-vāda):* a Bengali current from Narahari Sarakāra of Śrīkhaṇḍa directed madhura-bhāva at **Caitanya himself** as the amorous hero — rejected by the mainstream Gauḍīya line. [regional] [disputed]
- **Svakīyā vs parakīyā** [disputed] — is the gopīs' love wedded (*svakīyā*) or paramour-love (*parakīyā*, love across the boundary of marriage, prized as the most intense)? The *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi* exalts parakīyā in the līlā; Jīva Gosvāmī's commentaries were read as resolving it into svakīyā at the ultimate level, while later commentators (Viśvanātha Cakravartī's line) champion parakīyā — a controversy that ran to formal debate within the sampradāya. (Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi; commentarial tradition) [disputed] [scholarly]

## 6. Parallels — Other Grammars of Divine Emotion

- **Vallabha's Puṣṭimārga bhāvas** — the parallel Kṛṣṇa-sect of [Vallabhācārya](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md) organizes *sevā* of the child Śrīnāthjī overwhelmingly in **vātsalya-bhāva** (the devotee as Yaśodā-like caretaker: feeding, dressing, lulling the deity through the day's eight darśanas), with madhurya as an inner register — grace (*puṣṭi*) rather than aesthetic ripening being the engine. The Aṣṭachāp poets, above all [Sūrdās](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/surdas.md), are the vātsalya canon in Braj-bhāṣā verse. [scholarly]
- **Tamil love-modes — the older stream** — centuries before Rūpa, the [Āḻvārs](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/alvars-and-nayanars.md) poured devotion into the **akam** (interior/love-poetry) conventions of the [Sangam canon](../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md): Nammāḻvār speaking in a woman's voice (*nāyakī-bhāva*) pining for Viṣṇu, and [Āṇḍāḷ](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/andal.md)'s bridal mysticism (*Nācciyār Tirumoḻi*) — a functional anticipation of madhurya-rasa in a wholly different poetics; on the Śaiva side, Māṇikkavācakar's *Tirukkōvaiyār* casts Śiva as the akam lover. [scholarly] [regional]
- **The nava-rasa vs bhakti-rasa boundary** — the classical dance-and-drama world eventually absorbed the devotional turn: later aestheticians admit **bhakti** and **vātsalya** among candidate additions to the nine (see [natya-shastra-and-the-classical-arts.md](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/natya-shastra-and-the-classical-arts.md)), while the Gauḍīya line runs the assimilation the other way — the secular nine become shadows of the twelve devotional rasas. [disputed — 8 vs 9 vs 11 rasas]
- **Within the mārga scheme** — in the map of paths, this whole file is the interior engineering of **bhakti-mārga** at its Gauḍīya maximum: where [the-three-margas.md](the-three-margas.md) asks *which* path saves, Rūpa asks what the saved actually *taste*. Prema, not mokṣa, is the summit.
