# The Paths of Yoga & Patanjali's Eight Limbs

> Cross-cutting doctrine within [05-concepts-and-dharma](./) of *The Hindu Timeline*. Yoga as **lived practice and soteriology** — the disciplines a soul actually undertakes to reach *moksha*. For the text-catalog entry on Yoga as one of the six *darshanas* (its place among Samkhya, Nyaya, etc.) see [../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md); for the doctrinal goal itself see [purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md](purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md) and [atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md](atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md).

Yoga (root *yuj*, "to yoke / union") names both an end (liberating disjunction of spirit from matter, or union with the divine) and the full toolkit of disciplines that get there. The word is older than any single system: the [Vedas](../90-literature-corpus/shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) use it for "yoking," the *Katha* and *Shvetashvatara Upanishads* give the first technical descriptions of meditative yoga, the [Bhagavad Gita](../04-deep-dives/bhagavad-gita.md) systematises the great *margas* (paths), and Patanjali's *Yoga Sutras* fix the classical eight-limbed scaffold. This file catalogs the principal paths and then walks Patanjali's *ashtanga* limb by limb, recording variant framings across traditions.

## 1. The Principal Margas (Paths) to Moksha

The classical scheme of "four yogas" (popularised by Vivekananda, but rooted in the Gita) maps four temperaments onto four routes — all said to converge on the same liberation.

- **Karma Yoga — the path of action** - Acting in fulfilment of *dharma* while renouncing attachment to the *fruits* (*phala*) of action; work offered as sacrifice rather than for reward. Krishna's core counsel to Arjuna: *"karmany evadhikaras te ma phaleshu kadachana"* — "you have a right to action alone, never to its fruits" (Bhagavad Gita 2.47; theme of chs. 3, 5). Suited to the active temperament.
  - *Variant (Gita):* **nishkama karma** (desireless action) vs **sannyasa** (renunciation of action) are reconciled — the Gita argues acting without attachment surpasses mere abstention from action (Gita 5.2).
- **Bhakti Yoga — the path of devotion** - Loving surrender (*prapatti*) and single-minded devotion to a personal deity (Ishvara, Krishna, Vishnu, Shiva, Devi); the deity's grace carries the devotee across. Krishna declares the devotee "dearest" and promises deliverance to those who surrender all (Gita 9, 12, 18.66 *sarva-dharman parityajya*). Suited to the emotional/devotional temperament.
  - *Variant (Bhagavata / Vaishnava):* the **navadha bhakti** (nine modes — hearing, chanting, remembering, serving, worshipping, bowing, servitude, friendship, self-offering) codified in the Bhagavata Purana (7.5.23). See [../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md).
  - *Variant (Shaiva/Shakta):* parallel devotional streams toward Shiva ([../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)) and the Goddess ([../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md)).
- **Jnana Yoga — the path of knowledge** - Liberation by discriminative wisdom (*viveka*): direct realisation that *avidya* (ignorance) binds, and that the *atman* is identical with / distinct-yet-dependent on *Brahman*. Method = *shravana* (hearing), *manana* (reflection), *nididhyasana* (deep meditation). Rooted in the [Upanishads](../90-literature-corpus/shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) and Advaita Vedanta; the Gita treats it in chs. 2, 4, 13. Suited to the contemplative/intellectual temperament.
- **Raja Yoga / Dhyana Yoga — the path of meditation** - The "royal" path of stilling mind and senses through concentrated meditation; in later usage essentially Patanjali's eight-limbed system (see §3). The Gita's ch. 6 (*Dhyana Yoga* / *Atma-samyama Yoga*) gives a compact meditation manual — posture, gaze, breath, the steadied mind "like a lamp in a windless place" (Gita 6.19).
  - *Note:* "Raja Yoga" as a label for the Yoga Sutras is medieval; the *Hatha Yoga Pradipika* explicitly frames hatha as preparation **for** raja yoga (HYP 1.1–2). [scholarly]

- **The Gita's wider list** - Beyond the famous four, the Gita's 18 chapters are each titled a "yoga" (e.g. *Vishada*, *Sankhya*, *Vibhuti*, *Vishvarupa-darshana*, *Kshetra-kshetrajna*, *Gunatraya-vibhaga*, *Moksha-sannyasa* yoga) — signalling that the entire dialogue is a graded curriculum of yogas (Bhagavad Gita, chapter colophons).

## 2. The Tantric / Subtle-Body Streams

These overlap the four margas but center on *prana*, the subtle body, and energy rather than ethics or knowledge alone. Cross-link: [../90-literature-corpus/agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md](../90-literature-corpus/agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md).

- **Hatha Yoga — the forceful/physical path** - Discipline of the body and breath (*ha* "sun" + *tha* "moon", uniting the solar/lunar currents) to purify the nadis and rouse kundalini, **as preparation for raja yoga**. Locus classicus: the *Hatha Yoga Pradipika* of Svatmarama (15th c. CE), 4 chapters / ~389 shlokas covering *shatkarma* (six cleansings), *asana*, *pranayama*, *mudra*/*bandha*, and *nada* (HYP). [late text]
  - *Variant (Nath lineage):* HYP traces hatha to **Adinatha (Shiva), Matsyendranatha, and Gorakshanatha**, listing ~35 *siddha* masters; the Nath / Gorakhnath tradition is the historical seedbed (HYP 1.4–9). [folk tradition]
  - *Variant (earlier texts):* pre-HYP hatha manuals include the *Goraksha Shataka*, *Shiva Samhita*, and *Gheranda Samhita* (the last giving a *sapta-anga* "seven-limb" *ghatastha* yoga rather than eight). [scholarly]
- **Mantra Yoga — the path of sacred sound** - Liberation via repetition (*japa*) of *mantras* and seed-syllables (*bija*), supremely **OM/AUM**. The *Mandukya Upanishad* maps AUM's three sounds + silence onto waking/dream/deep-sleep/*turiya*; Patanjali identifies OM as the *vachaka* (designator) of Ishvara, its *japa* a direct means to absorption (Yoga Sutras 1.27–28). [Mandukya Upanishad; Yoga Sutras]
- **Kundalini / Laya Yoga — the path of dissolution** - Awakening the dormant *kundalini-shakti* (coiled serpent-energy) at the *muladhara* and raising it through the **central channel (sushumna)**, piercing the *chakras*, to merge Shakti with Shiva at the *sahasrara* (crown), dissolving (*laya*) mind into pure consciousness.
  - *Subtle-body framework:* **72,000 nadis**, of which three are primary — *ida* (lunar, left), *pingala* (solar, right), *sushumna* (central); the classic **seven chakras** (muladhara, svadhishthana, manipura, anahata, vishuddha, ajna, sahasrara) (HYP; tantric agamas). [late text]
  - *Variant (scholarly):* the full seven-chakra serpent scheme is a relatively **late, tantric-era systematisation**; Vedic and early Upanishadic texts lack it, and chakra counts/locations vary widely across tantras (e.g. *Sat-Cakra-Nirupana*). [scholarly]
- **Tantra Yoga** - Broader ritual-soteriological systems (Shaiva, Shakta, Vaishnava agamas/tantras) using *mantra*, *yantra*, *nyasa*, *puja*, and (in left-hand *vamachara*) transgressive rites, treating the body as microcosm of the cosmos. Source: the Agama/Tantra corpus (see literature node above).

## 3. Patanjali's Ashtanga — The Classical Eight-Limbed Yoga

The *Yoga Sutras of Patanjali* (~196 sutras in 4 *padas*: *Samadhi*, *Sadhana*, *Vibhuti*, *Kaivalya*; compiled c. 2nd c. BCE – 4th c. CE) crystallise classical Yoga as the practical twin of [Samkhya](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md) metaphysics, adding a theistic anchor (Ishvara). The standard commentary is the *Yoga Bhashya* attributed to **Vyasa** (c. 4th–5th c. CE), with Vachaspati Mishra's *Tattvavaisharadi* gloss.

- **The defining sutra** - *"yogash chitta-vritti-nirodhah"* — "Yoga is the cessation/stilling of the fluctuations (*vrittis*) of the mind-stuff (*chitta*)" (Yoga Sutras 1.2). Then *"tada drashtuh svarupe 'vasthanam"* — "then the Seer abides in its own nature" (1.3); otherwise the Seer is identified-with its fluctuations (1.4).
  - *The five vrittis (1.5–11):* right knowledge (*pramana*), error (*viparyaya*), conceptualisation (*vikalpa*), sleep (*nidra*), memory (*smriti*) — to be stilled by practice (*abhyasa*) and dispassion (*vairagya*) (1.12).

### The metaphysical frame (Samkhya + Ishvara)
- **Purusha & Prakriti** - Liberation = the discriminative isolation of **Purusha** (pure witness-consciousness) from **Prakriti** (matter, including mind/*buddhi*/*ahamkara*). The 25-*tattva* Samkhya scheme underlies the system. (Note: much of this vocabulary — *purusha*, *prakriti*, *buddhi*, *ahamkara* — is sparse in Patanjali himself but pervasive in Vyasa's *Bhashya*. [scholarly])
- **The five kleshas (afflictions)** - *avidya* (ignorance — the root), *asmita* (egoism), *raga* (attachment), *dvesha* (aversion), *abhinivesha* (clinging to life/fear of death) (Yoga Sutras 2.3–9).
- **Kriya Yoga** - The "yoga of action" prelude: **tapas** (austerity), **svadhyaya** (self-study/scriptural recitation), **Ishvara-pranidhana** (surrender to the Lord) — practised to attenuate the kleshas and cultivate samadhi (2.1–2).
- **Ishvara** - A special, ever-free Purusha "untouched by afflictions, actions, and their fruits," the supreme teacher unbounded by time, **designated by OM** (1.23–28). *Ishvara-pranidhana* is itself a sufficient path to samadhi (1.23, 2.45).
  - *Variant (reading):* Patanjali's Ishvara is **not a creator-god** but a perfect model-soul and object of devotion — a "theism" grafted onto Samkhya's atheism; this is Yoga's *bhakti* element embedded in the Sutras. [scholarly]

### The eight limbs (ashtanga, Yoga Sutras 2.28–3.3)
The first five are *bahiranga* (external aids); the last three, collectively **samyama**, are *antaranga* (internal).

- **1. Yama — restraints (the five great vows)** - **ahimsa** (non-violence), **satya** (truthfulness), **asteya** (non-stealing), **brahmacharya** (continence), **aparigraha** (non-possessiveness) (2.30). Called the *mahavrata*, "great vow," binding regardless of birth, place, time, or circumstance (2.31).
- **2. Niyama — observances** - **shaucha** (purity), **santosha** (contentment), **tapas** (austerity), **svadhyaya** (self-study), **Ishvara-pranidhana** (surrender to the Lord) (2.32). [Note tapas/svadhyaya/Ishvara-pranidhana also = Kriya Yoga.]
- **3. Asana — posture** - *"sthira-sukham asanam"* — a seat that is **steady and comfortable**, mastered by relaxing effort and meditating on the infinite (2.46–48). In Patanjali a *meditation seat* only; the elaborate postural repertoire belongs to later hatha texts.
- **4. Pranayama — breath regulation** - Control of inhalation/exhalation/retention after posture is mastered; thins the veil over the "inner light" and makes the mind fit for *dharana* (2.49–53).
- **5. Pratyahara — withdrawal of the senses** - The senses disengage from their objects and "follow the mind," like bees following the queen — mastery over the senses (2.54–55).
- **6. Dharana — concentration** - Binding the mind to a single point/object/region (*desha-bandhash chittasya*) (3.1).
- **7. Dhyana — meditation** - Unbroken, sustained flow of attention toward that object (3.2).
- **8. Samadhi — absorption** - The object alone shines, "as if empty of its own form," subject–object distinction collapsing (3.3).
  - **Samyama** - Dharana + dhyana + samadhi practised together on one object; its mastery yields the *vibhutis* / *siddhis* (extraordinary powers catalogued in Pada 3) — which Patanjali warns are *obstacles* to the higher goal (3.4–5, 3.37, 3.51).

### Kinds of samadhi & the goal
- **Samprajnata samadhi (cognitive/"seeded" absorption)** - Absorption still accompanied by an object, graded as *vitarka* (gross reasoning), *vichara* (subtle reflection), *ananda* (bliss), *asmita* (pure I-am-ness) (Yoga Sutras 1.17). Also termed *sabija* ("with seed," 1.46).
- **Asamprajnata samadhi (supra-cognitive/objectless absorption)** - Beyond all cognitive content, leaving only latent impressions (*samskara*); cultivated by supreme dispassion (1.18). Its highest reach is **nirbija** ("seedless") samadhi, where even the samskaras are burned and nothing is left to germinate (1.51).
- **Kaivalya — "aloneness," liberation** - The final state: Purusha, having discriminated itself fully from Prakriti, abides in its own nature; the *gunas*, their purpose served, dissolve back into their source (Yoga Sutras 4.34, the closing sutra). Yoga's *moksha* = isolation/independence of consciousness, not (as in Advaita) merger into Brahman. [scholarly]

## 4. The Two-Patanjalis Question

- **Disputed identity** - Tradition (esp. King Bhoja's *Rajamartanda*) equates the *Yoga Sutra*'s Patanjali with the **grammarian Patanjali** author of the *Mahabhashya* (commentary on Panini), and a medical writer — "one who purifies speech (grammar), mind (yoga), and body (medicine)." Most modern scholars reject the identification on linguistic and chronological grounds, dating the *Mahabhashya* to ~2nd c. BCE and treating the *Yoga Sutras*' author as a distinct, possibly later, compiler. [disputed] [scholarly] (Already flagged in [../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md).)
- **Composite-text view** - Some scholars argue the Sutras themselves weave together earlier strata (a *kriya-yoga* layer, an *ashtanga* layer, a *siddhi*/Pada-3 layer), so "Patanjali" may be an editor of inherited material rather than a single original author. [scholarly]
