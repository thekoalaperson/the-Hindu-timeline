# The 25 Tattvas of Sāṃkhya

> **Where this sits:** A cross-cutting doctrine of the *concepts-and-dharma* layer — the oldest systematic Indian account of *what reality is made of* and *how it evolves*. Sāṃkhya supplies the metaphysical scaffolding that Yoga, Tantra, Ayurveda, and the Purāṇic cosmogonies all borrow.
> Parent: [../README.md](../README.md) · Sibling concepts: [Ātman, Brahman & the Six Darśanas](atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md) · [Yoga — Paths & Patañjali](yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md) · See also the cosmogonic emanation in [../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md](../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md) and the Yoga Sūtra deep-dive in [../04-deep-dives/yoga-sutras-of-patanjali.md](../04-deep-dives/yoga-sutras-of-patanjali.md).

**Sāṃkhya** ("enumeration, reckoning") is the dualist, originally **non-theistic** darśana that catalogs reality into exactly **25 principles (*tattvas*)**. It is the classical *atheistic* school of the six — it posits no creator-god (*Īśvara*); the cosmos arises from the proximity of two ultimate, beginningless realities: **Puruṣa** (pure consciousness, plural and inert) and **Prakṛti** (unconscious primordial matter, active and one). Liberation (*kaivalya*) is the discriminative realization that Puruṣa is *not* Prakṛti. The locus classicus is the **Sāṃkhya-Kārikā of Īśvarakṛṣṇa** (c. 350–450 CE [scholarly dating]); tradition traces the system to the sage **Kapila** (an *avatāra* of Viṣṇu in the Bhāgavata, who teaches it to his mother Devahūti). [two clocks]

## 1. The two ultimate realities

- **Puruṣa (पुरुष) — pure consciousness / the Self** - The witness (*sākṣin*); changeless, attributeless, inactive, beyond the three guṇas. It does nothing and is affected by nothing — it merely *illuminates*. Neither cause nor effect (SK 3) (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 17–19).
  - *Puruṣa is plural:* Sāṃkhya argues for a **multiplicity** of selves (against Vedānta's single Brahman), since births, deaths, and faculties are distributed individually (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 18). [point of contrast with Advaita — see [Ātman, Brahman & the Six Darśanas](atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md)]
  - *The five proofs of Puruṣa:* (a) aggregates exist *for another*, (b) there must be a witness of the guṇas, (c) there must be an experiencer, (d) there must be one who seeks liberation, (e) the contrast principle (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 17).
- **Prakṛti / Pradhāna / Avyakta (प्रकृति) — primordial unmanifest matter** - The single, eternal, unconscious, uncaused root of all objectivity; "the cause that is not an effect" (*mūla-prakṛti*). Active, productive, pervasive, but blind — it evolves *for the sake of* Puruṣa (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 3, 11).
  - *The three guṇas — Prakṛti's "strands":* Prakṛti is the equilibrium of three constituents whose disturbance *is* manifestation:
    - **Sattva** — luminosity, lightness, harmony, intelligence, pleasure (*prīti*); buoyant and illuminating.
    - **Rajas** — activity, motion, passion, pain (*aprīti*); the dynamic mover.
    - **Tamas** — inertia, heaviness, concealment, delusion, indifference (*viṣāda*); the restraining, veiling principle (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 12–13).
  - *Satkāryavāda — the effect pre-exists in the cause:* Sāṃkhya's signature causal theory — the effect (the world) is *already present* latently in Prakṛti and is merely *manifested*, not newly created (*asad-akaraṇāt*, "the non-existent cannot be made"). Five arguments given (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 9). [contrast Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika *asatkāryavāda*]

## 2. The chain of evolution (pariṇāma)

When the equilibrium of the guṇas is disturbed (by Puruṣa's mere proximity, *sannidhi*), Prakṛti unfolds in a fixed sequence. **Mahat, ahaṃkāra, and the five tanmātras are both effects and causes; the eleven indriyas + manas and the five mahābhūtas are effects only; Prakṛti is cause only; Puruṣa is neither** (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 3).

- **(1) Mahat / Buddhi (महत् / बुद्धि) — the great one / intellect** - The first evolute; cosmic intelligence at the universal scale, individual discernment (*adhyavasāya*, "determination/judgement") at the personal scale. Seat of the eight *bhāvas* (virtue/vice, knowledge/ignorance, dispassion/attachment, power/weakness) (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 23).
- **(2) Ahaṃkāra (अहंकार) — the I-maker / ego** - The principle of individuation and self-reference ("I", "mine"); the pivot from which the chain *bifurcates* by which guṇa dominates (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 24).
  - *Sāttvika ahaṃkāra (vaikārika):* produces **manas + the 11 indriyas** (the "luminous" psychic set).
  - *Tāmasa ahaṃkāra (bhūtādi):* produces the **five tanmātras** (and through them the gross elements).
  - *Rājasa ahaṃkāra (taijasa):* supplies the *energy* enabling both lines to evolve (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 25).
  - *Variant (school dispute):* some hold the tanmātras spring from manas/buddhi rather than from tāmasa ahaṃkāra directly — the *vaikṛta* vs *tāmasa* derivation debated by later commentators (Vācaspati's *Tattva-Kaumudī* vs Vijñānabhikṣu's *Sāṃkhya-Pravacana-Bhāṣya*). [scholarly]

### The eleven indriyas + the inner organ

- **(3) Manas (मनस्) — mind** - The "central" inner instrument: coordinates sensation, deliberates (*saṃkalpa-vikalpa*), and is counted as *both* a sense and an action organ (*ubhayātmaka*) (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 27).
  - *The antaḥkaraṇa (inner instrument):* manas + ahaṃkāra + buddhi together form the threefold inner organ; with the ten outer indriyas they make the **thirteen-fold instrument** (*trayodaśa-karaṇa*) (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 32–33).
- **The five jñānendriyas (buddhīndriyas) — organs of knowledge:**
  - **(4) Śrotra** — hearing (ear). **(5) Tvak / Sparśana** — touch (skin). **(6) Cakṣus** — sight (eye). **(7) Rasana / Jihvā** — taste (tongue). **(8) Ghrāṇa** — smell (nose).
- **The five karmendriyas — organs of action:**
  - **(9) Vāk** — speech (mouth). **(10) Pāṇi** — grasping (hands). **(11) Pāda** — locomotion (feet). **(12) Pāyu** — excretion (anus). **(13) Upastha** — generation/pleasure (genitals).

### The five tanmātras — subtle elements ("mere-that")

The "subtle, undifferentiated" objects of sense — the essences from which the gross elements condense (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 38).

- **(14) Śabda-tanmātra** — sound-essence → produces ether.
- **(15) Sparśa-tanmātra** — touch-essence → produces air.
- **(16) Rūpa-tanmātra** — form/colour-essence → produces fire.
- **(17) Rasa-tanmātra** — taste-essence → produces water.
- **(18) Gandha-tanmātra** — smell-essence → produces earth.

### The five mahābhūtas — gross elements (effects only)

Each great element is *accumulative*, carrying the qualities of the subtler ones before it (ether = sound; air = sound+touch; fire = +form; water = +taste; earth = all five) (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 22, 38).

- **(19) Ākāśa** — ether/space (quality: sound).
- **(20) Vāyu** — air (sound, touch).
- **(21) Tejas / Agni** — fire (sound, touch, form).
- **(22) Ap / Jala** — water (+ taste).
- **(23) Pṛthivī** — earth (+ smell; all five).

> **The arithmetic of 25:** Puruṣa (1) + Prakṛti (1) + the 23 evolutes = **25 tattvas**. The 23 = Mahat (1) + Ahaṃkāra (1) + Manas (1) + 10 indriyas + 5 tanmātras + 5 mahābhūtas.
> *Variant numbering (24 vs 25):* some popular expositions count "**24 tattvas of Prakṛti**" (the unmanifest Prakṛti + its 23 evolutes) and list Puruṣa separately as the 25th, witness apart from the 24-fold field — same content, different bookkeeping (echoed in *kṣetra/kṣetrajña*, Bhagavad Gītā 13). [folk/popular framing]

## 3. The bodies, transmigration, and the path to kaivalya

- **The liṅga-śarīra (subtle body)** - The transmigrating vehicle = the 18-fold subtle body (buddhi + ahaṃkāra + manas + 10 indriyas + 5 tanmātras), which migrates life to life carrying the *bhāvas* until liberation; it dissolves only at kaivalya (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 39–40).
- **Bondage is mistaken identity** - Suffering (the threefold *duḥkha*: *ādhyātmika*, *ādhibhautika*, *ādhidaivika*) arises because Puruṣa wrongly identifies with buddhi's modifications — a "reflection" confusion, not real contact (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 1, 20).
- **Liberation by discriminative knowledge (*viveka-khyāti*)** - Release comes not by ritual or devotion but by *jñāna*: the decisive insight "I am not [Prakṛti], nothing is mine, there is no I" (*nāsmi, na me, nāham*) (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 64).
- **The dancer simile** - Once seen, Prakṛti withdraws "like a dancer who ceases when the audience has watched"; she does not perform again for that liberated Puruṣa (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 59, 61).
- **Kaivalya (कैवल्य) — "aloneness/isolation"** - The goal: Puruṣa abiding in its own pure, witnessing nature, utterly disentangled from the guṇas — no merger into a deity, no cosmic union, simply the cessation of false association (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 68).
  - *Jīvanmukti:* by inertia (*saṃskāra*) the body persists awhile after liberating knowledge "like a potter's wheel spinning on after the push"; at death, final, absolute kaivalya (*videha-kaivalya*) supervenes (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 67–68).

## 4. Theistic vs atheistic Sāṃkhya, and the Yoga relation

- **Classical Sāṃkhya is nirīśvara (god-less)** - Īśvarakṛṣṇa's system explains the cosmos by Prakṛti's teleology alone; arguments against a creator-god appear in later texts (a perfect god needs nothing; an imperfect one is no god) (Sāṃkhya-Sūtra 1.92–95, attributed to Kapila [late/uncertain dating]).
  - *Variant (seśvara / theistic Sāṃkhya):* the Mahābhārata, the Purāṇas (esp. the **Bhāgavata's Kapila-gītā**), and the **Yoga** school graft Īśvara onto the same 25 tattvas — Yoga adds *Īśvara* as a special, ever-free Puruṣa (Yoga Sūtra 1.24), making it "Sāṃkhya with a god." [see [Yoga — Paths & Patañjali](yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md)]
- **Sāṃkhya supplies the metaphysics; Yoga supplies the method** - The two are the classic "twin" darśanas: Yoga accepts the tattva-enumeration wholesale and adds the eightfold practical discipline (*aṣṭāṅga*) to *achieve* the discrimination Sāṃkhya describes. [cross-ref [Yoga Sūtras deep-dive](../04-deep-dives/yoga-sutras-of-patanjali.md)]

## 5. The Śaiva / Tantra extension — 36 tattvas

Kashmir Śaivism and the Tantric traditions **adopt all 25 Sāṃkhya tattvas intact** but reject its dualism and god-lessness, *adding 11 higher principles above Prakṛti* to map the descent of a single divine consciousness (Śiva) into matter. The result is a **non-dual, idealist** hierarchy of 36, grouped into three tiers (Tantric digests; Abhinavagupta's *Tantrāloka*; *Tattva-Saṅgraha*). [late text / Tantra]

- **Śuddha tattvas (pure, 1–5) — the divine order, above māyā:**
  - **(1) Śiva** — pure "I" consciousness (*prakāśa*), being-light.
  - **(2) Śakti** — the divine power (*vimarśa*), self-reflective awareness; the dynamic pole of Śiva.
  - **(3) Sadāśiva** — first stirring of "I am this," with *I-ness* dominant (*icchā*, will).
  - **(4) Īśvara** — "this-am-I," with *this-ness* (objectivity) dominant (*jñāna*, knowledge).
  - **(5) Śuddhavidyā / Sadvidyā** — balanced "I am this," the threshold of pure-impure (*kriyā*, action).
- **Śuddhāśuddha tattvas (pure-impure, 6–12) — māyā and the kañcukas (the "five cloaks"):**
  - **(6) Māyā** — the veiling power that splits the One into many and conceals divinity (here a *real* limiting principle, not Advaita's illusion).
  - **The five kañcukas — contractions of Śiva's infinitude into a limited subject:**
    - **(7) Kalā** — limited *agency/efficacy* (vs Śiva's omnipotence).
    - **(8) Vidyā** — limited *knowledge* (vs omniscience).
    - **(9) Rāga** — limited *desire/attachment* (vs all-fullness, *pūrṇatva*).
    - **(10) Kāla** — *time*, sequence (vs eternity).
    - **(11) Niyati** — *causal/spatial restriction*, destiny (vs all-pervasiveness).
  - **(12) Puruṣa** — the contracted, individuated soul (*aṇu*) wrapped in māyā and the kañcukas.
- **Aśuddha tattvas (impure, 13–36) — the 24 of Prakṛti onward:** identical in name and order to Sāṃkhya's: **(13) Prakṛti, (14) Buddhi, (15) Ahaṃkāra, (16) Manas, (17–21) the 5 jñānendriyas, (22–26) the 5 karmendriyas, (27–31) the 5 tanmātras, (32–36) the 5 mahābhūtas.**

- **Key reinterpretations vs Sāṃkhya:**
  - *Puruṣa is many but Prakṛti is now plural too* in some Śaiva schemes (one Prakṛti per soul), unlike Sāṃkhya's single cosmic Prakṛti. [school variant]
  - *The whole 36 is the self-display of one consciousness* — Śiva *becomes* the universe (*ābhāsavāda*, "doctrine of appearances"), reversing Sāṃkhya's irreducible Puruṣa/Prakṛti split.
  - *Variant (64-tattva counts):* some Āgamic and Śākta lists expand further (adding *parā* principles above Śiva, or sub-dividing), yielding 36+ schemes; the standard Trika number remains **36** (*Mālinīvijaya*, *Svacchanda Tantra*). [disputed / tradition-specific]
  - *Variant (Śaiva Siddhānta):* the dualist South-Indian Śaiva Siddhānta also uses 36 tattvas but keeps *paśu* (soul), *pati* (Śiva), and *pāśa* (bondage) ontologically distinct — a non-monistic reading of the same map. [school variant]

## 6. Quick comparison table

| Tier | Sāṃkhya (25) | Śaiva / Trika (36) |
|---|---|---|
| Above matter | Puruṣa (consciousness), Prakṛti (matter) — two ultimates | Śiva, Śakti, Sadāśiva, Īśvara, Śuddhavidyā + Māyā + 5 kañcukas + Puruṣa |
| Psychic | Mahat/Buddhi, Ahaṃkāra, Manas | same (under Prakṛti) |
| Faculties | 5 jñānendriyas + 5 karmendriyas | same |
| Subtle/gross | 5 tanmātras + 5 mahābhūtas | same |
| Theology | non-theistic (classical); plural Puruṣas | non-dual idealism; one Śiva-consciousness |
| Goal | kaivalya (isolation of Puruṣa) | recognition (*pratyabhijñā*) of identity with Śiva |
