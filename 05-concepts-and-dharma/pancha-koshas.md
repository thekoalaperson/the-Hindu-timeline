# The Pañca Kośas

> **Where this sits:** A dedicated doctrine-file of the *concepts-and-dharma* layer. The [Atman–Brahman](./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md) file maps *what the Self is*; this file maps the **five concentric sheaths (kośa)** that veil it, and the **neti-neti** discrimination (*viveka*) that strips them away to reveal the Ātman.
> Parent: [../README.md](../README.md) · See also: [Atman, Brahman & the Mahavakyas](./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md) · [Yoga Paths & Patanjali](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md) · [Purusharthas, Karma, Samsara & Moksha](./purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md)

The **pañca-kośa** ("five sheaths") model is the principal Vedāntic anatomy of the human person: five nested layers — *anna* (food), *prāṇa* (vitality), *manas* (mind), *vijñāna* (intellect), *ānanda* (bliss) — each enclosing the next, all of them concealing the innermost **Ātman**, which is identical with **Brahman**. The locus classicus is the **Brahmānanda Valli (Ānanda Valli)**, the second chapter of the **Taittirīya Upaniṣad** (Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda), at *Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.1–2.5*; the **Bhṛgu Valli** (3.1–3.6) restates it as a teaching from sage **Varuṇa** to his son **Bhṛgu**. The suffix **-maya** carries a double sense — "made/composed of" *and* "abundant in/pervaded by" — so *annamaya* = "the sheath consisting of / abounding in food."

---

## 1. Frame — sheaths, bodies, and the goal

- **The kośa as veil** - Each sheath (*kośa*, lit. "scabbard, treasury, cocoon") is **anātman** ("not-Self"), a modification of *prakṛti*, subject to origination and destruction; the Self is what the sheaths *hide*, never what they *are* (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.1–2.5; Śaṅkara's *Bhāṣya*).
- **Nesting ("sheath-within-sheath")** - The five are concentric, gross to subtle: *annamaya* outermost, then *prāṇamaya*, *manomaya*, *vijñānamaya*, *ānandamaya* innermost — "other than and within" (*anyo'ntara ātmā*) each prior one (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.2–2.5). Common later illustration: layers of an onion, or scabbards within a sword's sheath.
- **The three bodies (śarīra-traya) overlay** - The kośas map onto the Vedāntic three-body scheme: **sthūla śarīra** (gross body) = annamaya; **sūkṣma/liṅga śarīra** (subtle body) = prāṇamaya + manomaya + vijñānamaya; **kāraṇa śarīra** (causal body) = ānandamaya (Vedāntasāra of Sadānanda; Pañcadaśī 1).
- **The three states (avasthā-traya) overlay** - Waking (*jāgrat*) operates the gross/annamaya; dream (*svapna*) the subtle sheaths; deep sleep (*suṣupti*) is the field of the causal/ānandamaya, where bliss is "fully manifested" though Self is still veiled by ignorance (Māṇḍūkya correlation; Pañcadaśī).
- **The purpose** - The model is **soteriological, not anatomical**: it is a ladder for *viveka* (discrimination), each rung negated ("not this") until only the witnessing Ātman remains (see §4).

## 2. The five sheaths in sequence (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.1–2.5)

The Upaniṣad describes each sheath in an avian/anthropomorphic figure — the *puruṣa-vidha* ("man-shaped") image — with five "limbs" (*pañcāṅga*): **śiras** (head), **dakṣiṇa pakṣa** (right wing/side), **uttara pakṣa** (left wing/side), **ātman/madhya** (trunk, self), and **puccha** = **pratiṣṭhā** (tail = foundation/support). Each sheath's *puccha* is the tail that "supports" it and points toward the deeper sheath.

- **1. Annamaya-kośa — the food-sheath** - The gross physical body, "born of food, growing by food, returning to food (earth)"; *anna* (matter) is "the eldest-born of beings" and the medicine of all (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.1–2.2). Limbs: head, right side, left side, trunk, the **earth (pṛthivī)** as the tail/support. Corresponds to the gross body / waking state.
- **2. Prāṇamaya-kośa — the vital-air sheath** - The life-force animating the food-body: the five vital airs (*prāṇa, apāna, vyāna, udāna, samāna*) and the five organs of action (*karmendriya*); "the gods breathe by it, as do men and beasts; it is the life of beings" (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.2–2.3). Limbs: *prāṇa* (head), *vyāna* (right), *apāna* (left), **ākāśa** (space) as trunk, **earth (pṛthivī)** as tail/support. The annamaya is "filled by" the prāṇamaya. Part of the subtle body / dream state.
- **3. Manomaya-kośa — the mind-sheath** - The lower mind (*manas*) of perception, emotion, doubt, volition (*saṅkalpa-vikalpa*), with the five organs of knowledge (*jñānendriya*); seat of "I" and "mine," generator of desire and the felt sense of duality (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.3–2.4). Limbs drawn from the Veda itself: the **Yajus** (head), the **Ṛk** (right), the **Sāman** (left), **instruction/ādeśa** (trunk), the **Atharvāṅgiras** (tail/support). The verse here adds the famous *"yato vāco nivartante…"* — "whence words turn back, together with the mind, unable to reach **It** [Brahman-bliss]; one who knows that bliss fears nothing" (2.4.1). Subtle body / dream.
- **4. Vijñānamaya-kośa — the intellect-sheath** - The higher mind: *buddhi* (discriminative intellect) and *ahaṃkāra* (ego-sense) with the organs of knowledge; the faculty of **faith (śraddhā), truth (satya), yoga, and reason** — "vijñāna performs sacrifice and performs all acts; the gods worship vijñāna as Brahman" (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.4–2.5). Limbs: **śraddhā** (head), **ṛta** (right), **satya** (left), **yoga** (trunk), **mahas** (the great, tail/support). It is the **agent of transmigration** — the determining "knower/doer" that carries karma; subtlest of the three subtle sheaths.
- **5. Ānandamaya-kośa — the bliss-sheath** - The innermost, causal sheath of *ānanda*; "filled with" the prior; closest reflection of the Self, experienced purely in deep sleep and in absorption (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.5). Its five limbs are graded joys: **priya** (the joy of seeing a loved object — head), **moda** (the joy of gaining it — right), **pramoda** (the fullest delight — left), **ānanda** (bliss itself — trunk), and **Brahman** as the **puccha pratiṣṭhā** — the *tail that is the support of all*, i.e. Brahman as the final ground beneath even the bliss-sheath (2.5).
  - *Critical note:* Even ānandamaya is still a **kośa** and so *not* the Ātman itself — it is bliss *reflected in* and *limited by* ignorance (the causal body), not the unconditioned bliss-nature of Brahman. The verse's move from "ānanda" (the sheath) to "Brahman" (the *puccha pratiṣṭhā*) is precisely the step *beyond* the fifth sheath (Śaṅkara, *Taittirīya Bhāṣya*). [scholarly]

## 3. The Ānanda-mīmāṃsā — the calculus of bliss (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.8)

A distinctive appendix to the bliss-sheath: a graded "measurement of bliss" (*ānanda-mīmāṃsā*) showing all relative joys as fractions of Brahman's infinite bliss, each rung **one hundred-fold** the last.

- **The base unit** - One unit = the bliss of a noble, healthy, learned, resolute young man who possesses the whole earth full of wealth (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.8.1).
- **The ascending hundred-fold ladder** - ×100 at each step through: human-gandharvas (*manuṣya-gandharva*) → divine-gandharvas (*deva-gandharva*) → the *pitṛs* (ancestors in their long-enduring world) → the *ājāna-deva* (gods by birth) → the *karma-deva* (gods by ritual merit) → the *devas* → **Indra** → **Bṛhaspati** → **Prajāpati** → **Brahmā / Brahman** (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.8.1–4).
- **The refrain and resolution** - At each rung: "*…and of one who is free from desire (akāmahata)*" — the desireless knower already shares the higher bliss. The chapter concludes that the bliss of Brahmā and the bliss of "the man learned in the Veda, free from desire" are **one and the same** — relative joys are quantitative fractions; Brahman's bliss is qualitatively infinite and attained by *knowledge*, not accumulation (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.8.4–2.9.1; "*yato vāco nivartante*" recurs at 2.9).

## 4. Neti-neti and the kośa-viveka — the path inward

- **The discriminating method (pañcakośa-viveka)** - The seeker successively **identifies, then dis-identifies** with each sheath: "I am not the food-body (it is born and dies), not the vital airs (they sleep), not the mind (it doubts), not the intellect (it is an instrument and agent), not even the bliss-sheath (it is intermittent, veiled by ignorance)." What *witnesses* all five, present in waking, dream, and deep sleep, is the **Ātman** (Vivekacūḍāmaṇi 154–210; Pañcadaśī 3, *Pañcakośa-viveka*).
- **Neti neti ("not this, not this")** - The apophatic engine of the discrimination: every sheath, being objectifiable, is negated as "not the Self," for the Self is the un-objectifiable subject behind all (Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.3.6, 4.4.22; applied to the kośas by Śaṅkara). See the fuller treatment in [Atman, Brahman & the Mahavakyas §1](./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md).
- **The "five-sheathed" Self of the Vivekacūḍāmaṇi** - Śaṅkara (attrib.) likens the Ātman to a king's son lost among hunters, or a lamp within five enclosing globes: the light (consciousness) is mistaken for the globes (sheaths) until viveka separates them (Vivekacūḍāmaṇi 149–155).
- **The Bhṛgu Valli ascent (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 3.1–3.6)** - Varuṇa gives Bhṛgu the definition "*that from which beings are born, by which they live, and into which they enter at death — desire to know That; That is Brahman*" (3.1). Through *tapas*, Bhṛgu realizes Brahman successively as **anna**, then **prāṇa**, then **manas**, then **vijñāna**, and finally **ānanda** (3.2–3.6) — climbing the same five rungs that the Ānanda Valli descends, and concluding "Brahman is bliss" (*ānando brahmeti vyajānāt*, 3.6).
- **Where the kośas dissolve** - In *samādhi* / *jīvanmukti* the sheaths are not destroyed but seen-through: known as superimpositions (*adhyāsa*) on the one witnessing consciousness, they no longer bind. This is the meeting-point with the *aṣṭāṅga* discipline — see [Yoga Paths & Patanjali](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md).

## 5. Variants, parallels, and disputed readings

- **Ānanta vs. Ānanda in the opening (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.1.1)** - The defining verse reads "*satyaṃ jñānam anantaṃ brahma*" ("Brahman is reality, knowledge, infinity"); Paul Deussen and others note an alternative tradition reading **ānanda** ("bliss") for **ananta** ("infinite"), harmonizing with *sat-cit-ānanda* and with the chapter's bliss-climax. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Sāyaṇa / standard):* *ananta* ("infinite") is the received text; bliss enters via the ānandamaya sheath, not the opening triad.
- **Status of the ānandamaya-kośa — Brahman or sheath?** - A classic Vedāntic dispute centered on *Brahma Sūtra* 1.1.12–19 (*ānandamaya-adhikaraṇa*):
  - *Variant (Advaita / Śaṅkara):* Ānandamaya is **a sheath, NOT Brahman**; it is bliss conditioned by ignorance (causal body). Brahman is the *puccha pratiṣṭhā* "tail/support" *beyond* it. The *-maya* suffix signals modification, hence finitude. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Viśiṣṭādvaita / Rāmānuja & some pre-Śaṅkara readers):* Ānandamaya **is Brahman** — the supreme Self of infinite bliss; here *-maya* connotes "abundance/fullness," not modification. Rāmānuja reads the *adhikaraṇa* to identify the bliss-sheath with the Lord. [scholarly]
- **Number and naming of sheaths** - The fivefold list is fixed in the Taittirīya; later schemata vary:
  - *Variant (yogic / tantric usage):* The same five are rendered "physical / energy or vital / mental / wisdom or intellect / bliss" bodies, often correlated with cakras and the three guṇas in modern Haṭha- and Integral-yoga literature. [late text][folk]
  - *Variant (some texts collapse or expand):* A few popular expositions fold ānandamaya into the causal body and speak of "three bodies, five sheaths"; others add the "witness" (*sākṣī*) as a notional sixth beyond the five — strictly the Ātman, not a kośa. [folk]
- **Mapping to faculties (antaḥkaraṇa)** - The inner instrument's four functions are distributed across the subtle sheaths: *manas* (mind) → manomaya; *buddhi* (intellect) + *ahaṃkāra* (ego) → vijñānamaya; *citta* (memory-store) is variously assigned — a point on which commentators differ. [scholarly]
- **Parallel models** - The five-kośa scheme parallels but is distinct from: the **three guṇas** (sattva/rajas/tamas), the **24 tattvas** of Sāṃkhya (which the sheaths roughly subsume), and the **cakra/nāḍī** maps of tantra. Open-ended cross-system correlation tables in modern wellness literature are interpretive and post-classical — flagged as such. [folk]
