# The Three Guṇas

> **Where this sits:** A core doctrine of the *concepts-and-dharma* layer — the three "strands" (*guṇa* literally = thread/strand/quality) that compose all of material nature (*prakṛti*) and, by their shifting proportions, generate every body, mind, mood, food, and deed. Where [atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md](./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md) maps *consciousness* (puruṣa/atman/Brahman), this file maps *matter* and its qualitative texture.
> Parent: [../README.md](../README.md) · Foundations: the Sāṃkhya source-text in [../90-literature-corpus/darshanas/samkhya-karika.md](../90-literature-corpus/darshanas/samkhya-karika.md) · Liberation context: [./purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md](./purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md) and [./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md).

The doctrine of the three *guṇas* is one of the most pervasive ideas in Hindu thought: it begins as a technical Sāṃkhya cosmology (the constituents of unmanifest matter), is taken up by Yoga, and is broadcast to the wider tradition by the **Bhagavad Gītā**, which devotes three consecutive chapters (14, 17, 18) to *classifying* the whole field of human life — faith, food, sacrifice, austerity, charity, knowledge, action, agent, intellect, resolve, and happiness — into a *sāttvika / rājasa / tāmasa* triad. The practical and soteriological payoff is **guṇātīta** / **trigunātīta**: transcending the strands altogether.

Two clocks note: guṇa theory is a *doctrinal* schema (no scriptural date); academic dating places the classical Sāṃkhya formulation in the *Sāṃkhya-Kārikā* of Īśvarakṛṣṇa (c. 350–450 CE [scholarly]), though guṇa language is far older (Upaniṣads, Mokṣadharma of the Mahābhārata).

## 1. The Guṇas as Constituents of Prakṛti (Sāṃkhya basis)

- **Prakṛti as triguṇa** - Primordial unmanifest matter (*mūla-prakṛti* / *pradhāna*) is not inert "stuff" plus qualities; it *is* the three guṇas in their nature. The guṇas are the very fabric of materiality, not properties added to it (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 11). See [../90-literature-corpus/darshanas/samkhya-karika.md](../90-literature-corpus/darshanas/samkhya-karika.md).
- **Equilibrium = unmanifest; disequilibrium = manifestation** - In *prakṛti* unmanifest, the three are held in perfect balance (*sāmyāvasthā*); creation (*sarga*) begins only when that equilibrium is disturbed and one guṇa dominates, setting off the evolution of *mahat/buddhi → ahaṃkāra → manas, senses, elements* (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 22, on tattva-evolution).
- **Defining functions (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 12–13)** - The guṇas are defined by what they *do*:
  - **Sattva** - *prakāśa* (illumination/light), *prīti* (pleasure, joy); buoyant and light (*laghu*), luminous (*prakāśaka*).
  - **Rajas** - *pravṛtti* (activity, urging, motion), *duḥkha* (pain, restlessness); stimulating (*upaṣṭambhaka*), mobile (*cala*).
  - **Tamas** - *niyama* (restraint) / *avaraṇa* (veiling, obstruction), *viṣāda* (delusion, indifference, dejection); heavy (*guru*), enveloping (*varaṇaka*).
- **Mutual dependence and dominance** - None of the three ever exists in isolation; they "overpower, support, produce, and consort with one another" (*anyonya-abhibhava-āśraya-janana-mithuna-vṛtti*, Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 12). In any manifest thing all three are present, with one dominant and the others subordinate.
- **Analogy of the lamp** - The three cooperate for a single purpose (the experience and release of *puruṣa*) the way wick, oil, and flame cooperate to give light (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 13).
- **Why prakṛti is inferred to be triguṇa** - Sāṃkhya argues from effect to cause: since manifest products show pleasure/pain/delusion (*sukha-duḥkha-moha*), their material cause must contain the corresponding sattva/rajas/tamas (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 14–15, *satkāryavāda*, "the effect pre-exists in the cause").
- **Colours (symbolic)** - A traditional colour-coding (esp. Śvetāśvatara/ Mahābhārata streams): **sattva = white** (*śukla/śveta*), **rajas = red** (*rakta/lohita*), **tamas = black/dark** (*kṛṣṇa*). Cf. the "one unborn female, red-white-black" of Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 4.5, read by commentators as prakṛti's three guṇas. [late text reading]

## 2. Psychology of the Guṇas

- **They condition body, mind, and intellect** - Because *buddhi* (intellect), *ahaṃkāra* (ego), and *manas* (mind) are themselves evolutes of prakṛti, they too are woven of the guṇas; one's character is the running proportion of the three (Bhagavad Gītā 14.5).
- **Sattva binds by attachment to happiness and knowledge** - It is pure and luminous, yet still *binds* the embodied self by tying it to *sukha* (joy) and *jñāna* (knowledge) — a golden chain, but a chain (Gītā 14.6).
- **Rajas binds by attachment to action** - Born of craving and attachment (*tṛṣṇā-saṅga*), it fetters the self through restless activity and its fruits (Gītā 14.7).
- **Tamas binds by negligence, sloth, and sleep** - Born of ignorance (*ajñāna-ja*), it deludes all beings and binds through *pramāda* (heedlessness), *ālasya* (laziness), and *nidrā* (sleep) (Gītā 14.8).
- **Their characteristic "pulls"** - "Sattva attaches one to happiness, rajas to action, and tamas, veiling knowledge, attaches to negligence" (Gītā 14.9).
- **They wax and wane against each other** - At different times one guṇa rises by suppressing the other two; predominance is dynamic, not fixed (Gītā 14.10).
- **Diagnostic signs of dominance** - When all the gates of the body are lit by *knowledge*, sattva is ascendant (14.11); *greed, restless enterprise, and craving* mark rising rajas (14.12); *darkness, inertia, heedlessness, delusion* mark rising tamas (14.13).
- **The guṇas determine rebirth** - Dying in sattva → birth among the pure/wise (the worlds of the knowers); dying in rajas → rebirth among the action-attached; dying in tamas → birth in deluded/lower wombs (Gītā 14.14–15, 14.18). This links guṇa-psychology directly to *karma* and *saṃsāra* — see [./purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md](./purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md).
- **Fruits of each** - The fruit of good action is sāttvika and pure; the fruit of rajas is pain; the fruit of tamas is ignorance (Gītā 14.16).

## 3. The Threefold Classification — Gītā Chapters 14, 17, 18

The Gītā's signature move is to take every domain of life and sort it into three. Catalogued below by domain. (Together these chapters are sometimes called the *guṇa-vibhāga* teaching.)

### 3a. Faith (śraddhā) — Chapter 17
- **Faith follows one's nature** - "The faith of each is according to his own *sattva* (innate disposition); a person *is* his faith" (Gītā 17.3). Thus faith itself is threefold.
- **Sāttvika faith** - Worship directed to the *devas* (gods) (Gītā 17.4).
- **Rājasa faith** - Worship directed to *yakṣas* and *rākṣasas* (Gītā 17.4).
- **Tāmasa faith** - Worship directed to *pretas* (ghosts) and *bhūtas* (spirits) (Gītā 17.4). Extreme self-torturing austerity done in delusion is also condemned as demonic (17.5–6).

### 3b. Food (āhāra) — Chapter 17.7–10
- **Sāttvika food** - Increases longevity, purity, strength, health, joy; juicy, soft/oily (*snigdha*), substantial, agreeable; promotes clarity (Gītā 17.8).
- **Rājasa food** - Bitter, sour, salty, very hot, pungent, dry, burning; causes pain, grief, disease (Gītā 17.9).
- **Tāmasa food** - Stale, tasteless, putrid, leftover (*yāta-yāma*), and impure/polluted (*ucchiṣṭa*) (Gītā 17.10). (Popular gloss: food cooked more than ~3 *yāmas*/watches before eating; the "3 hours" framing is a modern paraphrase. [folk])

### 3c. Sacrifice / Yajña — Chapter 17.11–13
- **Sāttvika yajña** - Offered as a duty (*vidhi-diṣṭa*), without desire for reward, with the mind fixed only on the rightness of offering (Gītā 17.11).
- **Rājasa yajña** - Offered with an eye to fruit (*phala*) or for ostentation/display (*dambha*) (Gītā 17.12).
- **Tāmasa yajña** - Without scriptural sanction, without distributed food, without mantras, gifts, or faith (Gītā 17.13).

### 3d. Austerity / Tapas — Chapter 17.14–19
- **The three bodies of tapas** - of body (*śārīra*: worship, purity, non-violence — 17.14), of speech (*vāṅmaya*: truthful, beneficial, non-agitating words, recitation — 17.15), of mind (*mānasa*: serenity, gentleness, silence, self-control — 17.16).
- **Sāttvika tapas** - Practised with supreme faith, by the desireless, for no fruit (Gītā 17.17).
- **Rājasa tapas** - Done for respect, honour, reverence, or display; unstable and impermanent (Gītā 17.18).
- **Tāmasa tapas** - Done with deluded self-torture, or to harm/destroy another (Gītā 17.19).

### 3e. Charity / Dāna — Chapter 17.20–22
- **Sāttvika dāna** - Given as a duty, to a worthy recipient, at the right place and time, expecting no return (Gītā 17.20).
- **Rājasa dāna** - Given grudgingly, or expecting a return or future reward (Gītā 17.21).
- **Tāmasa dāna** - Given at the wrong place/time, to unworthy persons, without respect, with contempt (Gītā 17.22).
- **Om Tat Sat** - The chapter closes with the threefold designation of Brahman invoked at the start of all sacrifice, charity, and austerity to perfect them (Gītā 17.23–28). Cross-ref the Mahāvākyas/Brahman in [./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md](./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md).

### 3f. Renunciation/Knowledge/Action — Chapter 18 (the great sorting)
- **Tyāga (renunciation) of three kinds** - Giving up duty from delusion is tāmasa (18.7); giving it up from fear of trouble is rājasa (18.8); doing one's duty while renouncing attachment and fruit is sāttvika (18.9–10).
- **Jñāna (knowledge)** - Sāttvika: sees the one imperishable being in all (18.20); rājasa: sees only manifold separate entities (18.21); tāmasa: clings irrationally to one part as if it were the whole (18.22).
- **Karma (action)** - Sāttvika: prescribed, unattached, desireless, without like/dislike (18.23); rājasa: done with craving, egoism, great effort (18.24); tāmasa: undertaken in delusion, ignoring consequence, loss, and capacity (18.25).
- **Kartṛ (agent)** - Sāttvika: free of attachment/ego, resolute, energetic, unmoved by success or failure (18.26); rājasa: passionate, greedy, violent, impure, swayed by joy and grief (18.27); tāmasa: undisciplined, vulgar, obstinate, deceitful, lazy, procrastinating (18.28).
- **Buddhi (intellect)** - Sāttvika: knows action and inaction, bondage and freedom, what to do and what to fear (18.30); rājasa: confuses dharma and adharma (18.31); tāmasa: takes adharma to be dharma, sees all things upside-down (18.32).
- **Dhṛti (resolve/firmness)** - Sāttvika: steadies mind, breath, and senses through unwavering yoga (18.33); rājasa: holds to duty, pleasure, wealth, craving the fruit (18.34); tāmasa: never lets go of sleep, fear, grief, despair, conceit (18.35).
- **Sukha (happiness)** - Sāttvika: like poison at first but nectar in the end, born of self-knowledge — "the serenity of one's own mind" (18.37); rājasa: nectar at first, poison in the end, from sense–object contact (18.38); tāmasa: deluding from start to finish, born of sleep, sloth, heedlessness (18.39).
- **Universality claim** - "There is no being on earth or among the gods in heaven free from these three guṇas born of prakṛti" (Gītā 18.40) — and the very division of *varṇa* duties is said to follow guṇa-and-action (18.41). Compare the social application in [./varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md](./varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md).

## 4. The Trigunātīta Goal — Transcending the Strands

- **The aim is to go beyond all three** - Even sattva binds; liberation (*mokṣa*) is not "maximizing sattva" but *crossing past* the guṇas to immortality (*amṛta*) (Gītā 14.20). See [./purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md](./purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md).
- **Arjuna's question** - "By what marks is one who has crossed the three guṇas known? How does he act? How does he cross them?" (Gītā 14.21) — the Gītā answers with a portrait of the **guṇātīta**.
- **Marks of the guṇātīta (Gītā 14.22–25)** - He neither hates illumination, activity, and delusion when they arise nor longs for them when absent; sits *unconcerned* (*udāsīna-vat*), unshaken, knowing "it is only the guṇas that act"; equal in pleasure and pain, in a clod, a stone, and gold; equal toward the dear and the unwanted; firm; equal in blame and praise, honour and dishonour, friend and foe; and has abandoned all undertakings (*sarvārambha-parityāgī*).
- **The means: unswerving bhakti** - "One who serves Me with unwavering devotion (*avyabhicāreṇa bhakti-yogena*), transcending these guṇas, is fit to become Brahman" (Gītā 14.26) — devotion is the bridge across the strands. Compare the disciplines in [./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md).
- **Brahman's foundation** - Krishna is declared the abode (*pratiṣṭhā*) of the immortal, imperishable Brahman, of eternal dharma, and of absolute bliss (Gītā 14.27).
- **Sāṃkhya path to the same end** - In Sāṃkhya, *kaivalya* (isolation) comes when *buddhi* discriminates puruṣa from prakṛti; prakṛti then "ceases to dance" before the seen puruṣa, and the play of the guṇas stops for that self (Sāṃkhya-Kārikā 59–68). Liberation here is *discriminative knowledge* (*viveka-khyāti*), not devotion.
- **Yoga path** - In Patañjali, the final *kaivalya* is "the return of the guṇas to their source, void of any aim for the puruṣa" — i.e., the guṇas resolve back into prakṛti (Yoga-Sūtra 4.34). See [./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md](./yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md).

## 5. Variants Across Schools

- *Variant (classical Sāṃkhya):* Guṇas are **real, objective constituents** of an independent, uncreated *prakṛti*; the cosmos is their self-transformation (*pariṇāma*); puruṣa (pure consciousness) is wholly *outside* the guṇas, never an agent. Liberation = discrimination, not transcendence-by-grace. [scholarly basis: Sāṃkhya-Kārikā]
- *Variant (Yoga / Patañjala):* Accepts the Sāṃkhya guṇa-realism but adds **Īśvara** and treats the guṇas under three further "stages" (*viśeṣa*, *aviśeṣa*, *liṅga-mātra*, *aliṅga* — YS 2.19). The *vṛttis* of the mind and even the kleśas are guṇa-functions; *kaivalya* = guṇa-resolution (YS 4.34).
- *Variant (Bhagavad Gītā / theistic synthesis):* Guṇas are "born of prakṛti" but **prakṛti is the Lord's own lower nature** (*apara-prakṛti*, Gītā 7.4–5); they emanate from and are governed by Krishna, who is *himself* untouched by them (Gītā 7.12–13, "they are in Me, I am not in them"). Transcendence is via *bhakti*.
- *Variant (Advaita Vedānta / Śaṅkara):* Prakṛti with its guṇas = **māyā**, the beginningless power of *nirguṇa* Brahman; the guṇas have empirical (*vyāvahārika*) reality only and dissolve in *jñāna*. "Saguṇa" Brahman/Īśvara is Brahman *associated* with māyā's guṇas; the absolute is *nirguṇa* (attributeless). See §1–2 of [./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md](./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md).
  - *Sub-note:* Hence Advaita distinguishes the *guṇas of prakṛti* (sattva/rajas/tamas) from "*guṇa*" in the sense of *attribute*; *nirguṇa* Brahman lacks the latter entirely.
- *Variant (Viśiṣṭādvaita / Rāmānuja):* The material world (*prakṛti* with its guṇas) is the **body of Brahman** (Viṣṇu); the guṇas are real and god-dependent. Distinct from the *kalyāṇa-guṇas* (auspicious *attributes*) which the supreme Brahman eternally possesses — a deliberately different sense of "guṇa." [late text: Śrī-Bhāṣya]
- *Variant (Dvaita / Madhva):* Prakṛti and its guṇas are real and eternally dependent on Viṣṇu; souls themselves are graded, and (in some readings) have an innate guṇa-character determining their eligibility. [late text]
- *Variant (Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika):* Does **not** use the sattva/rajas/tamas triad as world-constituents at all; "*guṇa*" is one of its seven categories (*padārtha*) — a list of 24 specific *qualities* (colour, taste, number, conjunction, pleasure, pain, etc.) inhering in substances. A genuinely different doctrine sharing the word. See [../90-literature-corpus/darshanas/vaisheshika-sutras.md](../90-literature-corpus/darshanas/vaisheshika-sutras.md) and [../90-literature-corpus/darshanas/nyaya-sutras.md](../90-literature-corpus/darshanas/nyaya-sutras.md).
- *Variant (Mahābhārata Mokṣadharma / Pre-classical):* Earlier strata enumerate guṇas with shifting metaphysics (sometimes more than three; guṇas as evolutes rather than constituents), reflecting "proto-Sāṃkhya" before Īśvarakṛṣṇa fixed the system. [scholarly: open-ended; rosters vary]
- *Variant (Purāṇic cosmogony):* The three guṇas are personified/correlated with the **Trimūrti** — rajas→Brahmā (creation), sattva→Viṣṇu (preservation), tamas→Śiva/Rudra (dissolution) — a common but not universal mapping; some texts assign sattva to Viṣṇu and place Brahmā under rajas, Śiva under tamas. [folk/late text; mapping varies by text] Cross-ref [../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).
