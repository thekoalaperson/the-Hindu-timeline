# Nāstika Darśanas & the Āstika-Nāstika Frame — Cārvāka, Buddhism, Jainism, the Ājīvikas

> **Where this sits:** The structural *head* of the darśana map in the *concepts-and-dharma* layer — the missing heterodox half of Indian philosophy. The orthodox six are cataloged in [atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md](./atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md) and [../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md); the epistemology that sorts them sits in [the-pramanas.md](./the-pramanas.md). This file fulfils the dangling "see heterodox schools" cross-reference and maps the three (or four) **nāstika** systems that *reject* Vedic authority.
> Parent: [../README.md](../README.md) · Sibling concepts: [.](.)

The Indian philosophical field is split by one criterion, and it is **not theism**. A darśana is **āstika** ("affirmer") if it accepts the **Veda as a valid means of knowledge** (*śabda-pramāṇa*, see [the-pramanas.md](./the-pramanas.md)), and **nāstika** ("denier") if it rejects Vedic authority. Two parallel streams feed the field: the **brāhmaṇa** stream of Vedic ritualists and the **śramaṇa** stream of renouncer-ascetics (Buddhist, Jain, Ājīvika) that grew up alongside and against it. The two-clocks distinction matters acutely here: the orthodox tradition narrates these schools through a **scriptural clock** (founders as deluders, as Tīrthaṅkaras across cosmic time), while the **historical clock** dates their human founders to the 6th–5th c. BCE śramaṇa ferment of Magadha.

---

## 1. The Āstika-Nāstika Frame Itself

- **The criterion is Vedic authority, not God** — *āstika* = accepts the Veda as *pramāṇa*; *nāstika* = denies it. The decisive consequence: non-theistic schools can be **orthodox**, and soul-affirming schools can be **heterodox**. (Standard doxographic usage)
  - **Non-theistic yet āstika** — classical **Sāṃkhya** is *nirīśvara* (no creator-God) and **Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā** treats the Veda as authorless and impersonal, needing no God — yet both are orthodox because they revere the Veda (see [the-samkhya-tattvas.md](./the-samkhya-tattvas.md)).
  - **Soul-affirming yet nāstika** — **Jainism** affirms an eternal *jīva* (soul) and rebirth, yet is heterodox because it rejects the Veda. So "ātma-vāda" does not make a school āstika.
- **Manusmṛti 2.11** — gives the law-book definition: *nāstiko veda-nindakaḥ* — "a nāstika is one who reviles / disparages the Veda." This fixes the term on **scriptural authority**, not on belief in deities. (*Manusmṛti* 2.11)
- **The six-vs-three grid** — six orthodox (*ṣaḍ-darśana*: Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta) against three classical heterodox: **Cārvāka, Bauddha, Jaina**. (Standard enumeration)
  - *Variant (wider "sixteen darśanas" schema):* Mādhava-Vidyāraṇya's **Sarva-darśana-saṅgraha** (14th c.) surveys *sixteen* systems, opening with **Cārvāka** as the lowest rung and ascending to Advaita as the highest — heterodox and orthodox ranked on one ladder. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Jain doxography):* the **Ṣaḍ-darśana-samuccaya** of Haribhadra lists *six* systems including Buddhism, Jainism, and Lokāyata, treating the field differently again. [late text]

## 2. Cārvāka / Lokāyata — the Materialist School

The one fully **materialist** darśana; admits **pratyakṣa (perception) alone** as pramāṇa (see [the-pramanas.md](./the-pramanas.md), §2, Cārvāka = 1).

- **Bhūta-caitanya / deha-ātma-vāda** — the body is a compound of **four elements** (*bhūta*: earth, water, fire, air; ether often dropped as imperceptible), and **consciousness is an emergent property** of that compound, arising as intoxicating power arises from fermented ingredients that lack it singly. The self *is* the living body; at death, consciousness ends. (*Sarva-darśana-saṅgraha* ch. 1)
- **Svabhāva-vāda** — events arise from the intrinsic **nature** of things (or chance), **not from karma** or a moral cosmic order; no unseen *adṛṣṭa* links act to fruit.
- **Denials** — rejects **karma, rebirth, mokṣa, God (Īśvara), and the Veda**; the supersensible is unknowable by perception, hence denied. Priestcraft and ritual are condemned as the livelihood of the cunning. (*Sarva-darśana-saṅgraha* ch. 1)
- **Hedonist ethics** — the famous maxim: *yāvaj jīvet sukhaṃ jīvet, ṛṇaṃ kṛtvā ghṛtaṃ pibet* — "while you live, live happily; borrow money and drink ghee" — pleasure (*kāma*) the only rational aim, even if mixed with pain. (Attributed verses, via *Sarva-darśana-saṅgraha*)
- **The lost Bṛhaspati-sūtra** — the school's root text, ascribed to **Bṛhaspati**, survives only in **fragments quoted by opponents**; the whole positive corpus is reconstructed from hostile citations. [disputed — almost no Cārvāka text survives in its own voice]
- **Jayarāśi's Tattvopaplavasiṃha** — "The Lion that Devours All Categories" (8th c.), an extant skeptical work often linked to Lokāyata, which undermines the validity of *every* pramāṇa — a more radical skepticism than the standard one-pramāṇa portrait. [scholarly]
- **Epic attestations** —
  - *Variant (Rāmāyaṇa):* the brahmin **Jābāli** counsels Rāma with materialist arguments — there is no afterlife, ritual for the dead is futile — and Rāma rebukes him; a *nāstika* voice put in the mouth of an advisor. (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa 108–109)
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata):* **Cārvāka** appears as a **rākṣasa** disguised as a brahmin who reviles Yudhiṣṭhira after the war and is destroyed by the assembled brahmins — a polemical personification, not a philosopher. (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva 38–39)

## 3. The Ājīvikas — the Third Great Śramaṇa Movement

The **fourth** heterodox system, usually dropped from the standard "three" but historically a major rival of Buddhism and Jainism.

- **Makkhali Gosāla** — the founder/leading teacher (a contemporary and former associate of Mahāvīra), heading the Ājīvika ascetic order. (Buddhist and Jain sources)
- **Niyati-vāda (strict fatalism)** — the core doctrine: all events are fixed by **niyati** (destiny/necessity); there is **no free will, no efficacy of effort (*puruṣakāra*), and no karma that one can alter**. Every being runs a predetermined course through 8,400,000 great-aeons of transmigration, after which liberation comes automatically — exertion changes nothing. This determinism is the school's signature. (Reported in *Sāmaññaphala Sutta*)
- **Buddhist attestation** — the **Sāmaññaphala Sutta** (*Dīgha Nikāya* 2) puts Gosāla's niyati doctrine in the mouth of one of the "six heretical teachers" surveyed for King Ajātaśatru. (*Dīgha Nikāya* 2)
- **Jain attestation** — the **Bhagavatī Sūtra** narrates Gosāla's career and his quarrel and final break with Mahāvīra at length. (*Bhagavatī Sūtra* / *Viyāhapannatti*)
- **Mauryan patronage** — the Ājīvikas enjoyed royal support under **Bindusāra** (reputedly), and **Aśoka** and his grandson **Daśaratha** dedicated the **Barābar and Nāgārjunī cave** dwellings to Ājīvika ascetics — the school's main surviving epigraphic footprint before its slow extinction. (Barābar/Nāgārjunī cave inscriptions) [historical]

## 4. Jainism — the 24 Tīrthaṅkaras

Heterodox by the Vedic criterion, yet a complete soul-affirming, karma-realist system. Its sacred history is a **set of 24 Tīrthaṅkaras** ("ford-makers") who reveal the path each in their cosmic descending half-cycle.

- **The 24 as a roster** — a fixed list spanning vast cosmic time; the first two and last two anchor it:
  - **1. Ṛṣabha / Ādinātha** — the first ford-maker, a primordial culture-hero who also appears in the Hindu corpus as **Ṛṣabhadeva**, an avatāra of Viṣṇu in the *Bhāgavata* (8th of the 22) — a genuine **Hindu-Jain overlap** (see [../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.3–6; Jain *Ādi-purāṇa*)
  - **23. Pārśvanātha** — the 23rd, traditionally c. 9th–8th c. BCE, the earliest Tīrthaṅkara most scholars treat as likely **historical**. [scholarly]
  - **24. Mahāvīra (Vardhamāna)** — the 24th and last of this cycle, the **historical founder/reformer** of the order (c. 599–527 BCE by tradition; 6th–5th c. BCE academically), contemporary of the Buddha. [historical]
- **The 63 śalākāpuruṣas** — Jain "universal history" frames the Tīrthaṅkaras within **63 illustrious beings** (*śalākāpuruṣa*): 24 Tīrthaṅkaras + 12 *Cakravartins* (universal emperors) + 9 *Baladevas* + 9 *Vāsudevas* + 9 *Prativāsudevas* — a parallel mytho-historical scheme to the Hindu yuga/avatāra frame. (Jain tradition)
- **Source texts** —
  - *Variant (Śvetāmbara canon):* the **Kalpa-sūtra** (Bhadrabāhu) narrates the lives of the Tīrthaṅkaras, especially Mahāvīra and Pārśva. (*Kalpa-sūtra*)
  - *Variant (Digambara):* **Jinasena's Ādi-purāṇa** (9th c.) gives the great life of Ṛṣabha. (*Ādi-purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (encyclopedic):* **Hemacandra's Triṣaṣṭi-śalākā-puruṣa-carita** (12th c.) narrates all **63** great beings in one work. (*Triṣaṣṭi-śalākā-puruṣa-carita*)

## 5. Buddhism — and the Paurāṇic-vs-Historical Buddha

The middle heterodox school; its **two-pramāṇa logical tradition** (Dignāga–Dharmakīrti) is mapped in [the-pramanas.md](./the-pramanas.md). Here the focus is the *reconciliation problem*: how a rejecter of the Veda became a Hindu **avatāra**.

- **The historical Gautama** — Siddhārtha Gautama, c. 6th–5th c. BCE, a Magadhan śramaṇa whose teaching denies the Veda's authority and the permanent self (*anātman*) — squarely nāstika on the Manusmṛti criterion. [historical]
- **Absorption as Viṣṇu's 9th avatāra** — by c. 8th c. CE the Buddha is folded into the **Daśāvatāra** (see [../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)) — the scriptural clock overwriting the historical one.
- **The Māyāmoha "deluder" rationale** — the *polemical* reading: Viṣṇu takes a deluding form (**Māyāmoha**) to lead the *asuras* (or the unworthy) away from the Veda so that, abandoning their dharma, they lose their power and are destroyed. The Buddha-descent is thus a *deliberate deception* of the enemies of the gods. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 3.17–18)
  - *Variant (conciliatory reading):* the *Bhāgavata* names the Buddha-descent compassionately — *buddho nāmnāñjana-sutaḥ* — born to **Añjana** in the **Kīkaṭa** (Magadha) country, to "delude the enemies of the gods" but in a softened, honoured register. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.3.24)
  - *Variant (identity question):* whether this avatāra **is** the historical Gautama or a **distinct "Kīkaṭa Buddha"** is itself disputed; the Purāṇic Buddha's parentage (son of Añjana) does not match Gautama's (son of Śuddhodana). [disputed — see dashavatara.md §9-alt]
- **Polemical-vs-conciliatory function** — the avatāra device does double duty: it lets orthodoxy **absorb** a rival (claiming the Buddha for Viṣṇu) while simultaneously **discrediting** Buddhism (his teaching was a deliberate delusion meant for the unworthy). [scholarly]
