# Śrāddha & the Pitṛ Rites

> Cross-cutting doctrine and ritual of ancestor-worship in Sanātana Dharma. Sits under [05-concepts-and-dharma](README.md) → master index [../README.md](../README.md). The *pitṛ-yajña* (debt to the ancestors) is one of the three constitutive debts (*ṛṇa*) every dvija is born owing, alongside the debt to the gods (*deva-ṛṇa*) and to the seers (*ṛṣi-ṛṇa*); see [varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md](varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md).
>
> "You are here": this is the ritual machinery by which a deceased person is moved from a hungry, dangerous ghost (*preta*) into a settled, beneficent ancestor (*pitṛ*) — the post-mortem counterpart to the [samskaras](varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md) of the living. Antyeṣṭi (cremation) is the *last* samskara of the body; śrāddha is the *ongoing* maintenance of the soul.

**Śrāddha** (śrāddha, "that which is done with *śraddhā* / faith") = the offerings of food (*piṇḍa*), water (*tarpaṇa*), and gifts (*dāna*) made by descendants to nourish and sustain the dead in *pitṛ-loka*. The whole complex rests on a transactional cosmology: the dead literally depend on the living for sustenance, and the living depend on the dead for blessings, progeny, and protection. Failure to perform śrāddha leaves the ancestor starving and the lineage afflicted (*pitṛ-doṣa*). Locus classicus: **Manusmṛti 3.122–286** (the longest single treatment); **Garuḍa Purāṇa, Pretakhaṇḍa / Pretakalpa**; the *gṛhya-sūtras* (Āśvalāyana, Pāraskara, Baudhāyana); and the medieval digests (*Śrāddhakalpa*, *Dharmasindhu*, *Nirṇayasindhu*).

## I. THE PITṚS & PITṚ-LOKA — who and where the ancestors are

- **The pitṛs ("fathers")** - The collective of departed ancestors who have completed the post-death rites and attained settled status. Not gods, not ghosts — a distinct class of beings inhabiting *pitṛ-loka*, dependent on descendant offerings yet able to grant boons (long life, progeny, prosperity). Worshipped facing **south** (the *dakṣiṇa* / Yama's direction) with the sacred thread worn over the right shoulder (*prācīnāvīti*) — the inverse of deva-worship.
- **Pitṛ-loka** - The "world of the fathers," classically located in the lunar / southern celestial region (associated with **Yama** and the moon, *candra*). Distinct from *deva-loka* (the bright "way of the gods," *devayāna*) — the dead who do rites travel the *pitṛ-yāna* ("way of the fathers"), the lunar path of return/rebirth, per **Chāndogya Upaniṣad 5.10** and **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 6.2** (the two-paths / *pañcāgni-vidyā* doctrine). See [../00-time-and-cosmology/brahmanda-and-bhuvana-kosha.md](../00-time-and-cosmology/brahmanda-and-bhuvana-kosha.md) and [../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
- **Pitṛ time-scale** - The ancestors run on a different clock: **one day of the pitṛs ≈ one (lunar) month of humans**, the moon's dark half being their day and the bright half their night (or vice versa per source). This anchors the *monthly* amāvāsyā śrāddha rhythm. Cf. the nested cosmic clocks of [../00-time-and-cosmology/units-of-time.md](../00-time-and-cosmology/units-of-time.md).
- **The three-generation rule (sapiṇḍa group)** - Śrāddha is owed to **three ascending generations**: father (*pitṛ*), grandfather (*pitāmaha*), great-grandfather (*prapitāmaha*) — and their wives in the maternal line. Beyond three generations the dead merge into the undifferentiated mass of *nāndīmukha* / remote ancestors honoured only collectively (Manusmṛti 3.221; gṛhya-sūtras).
- **The divine forms of the three** - The three generations are invoked as manifestations of three classes of Vedic deity: father = **Vasu** form, grandfather = **Rudra** form, great-grandfather = **Āditya** form (per the *śrāddha* manuals and Tamil-brahmin death-ritual tradition). This identifies the personal dead with the cosmic deva-classes, raising them above mere ghosts.
- **The Viśvedevas** - The "All-Gods" are invoked as *presiding witnesses* and guardians of every śrāddha, protecting the offering from rākṣasa interference; a separate piṇḍa/seat is given them. Their presence is what makes the rite efficacious rather than a mere meal.
- **The primordial Pitṛs (devatā-class)** - Beyond one's own forebears, the texts name *primal* ancestor-races who are themselves objects of worship:
  - **Agniṣvāttas, Barhiṣads, Somapas, Ājyapas, Sukālins** - the classical seven (or several) classes of *divine* pitṛs, variously the mind-born sons of Brahmā / the ancestors of gods, brahmanas, kshatriyas, vaishyas, and shudras respectively (Manusmṛti 3.194–201; Viṣṇu, Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇas). The *Agniṣvāttas* (those who took no fire) and *Barhiṣads* (those who used sacred grass) are the most cited.
  - **Svadhā** - personified offering-call/consort of the pitṛs; *svadhā* is to the ancestors what *svāhā* is to the gods — the ritual exclamation that delivers the oblation. Often called daughter of Dakṣa, wife of the pitṛs (Purāṇic).
  - *Variant (Purāṇic rosters):* the names, number (often seven, sometimes more), and varna-correlations of the divine pitṛ-classes vary across **Manusmṛti, Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Vāyu Purāṇa, Matsya, Brahmāṇḍa**; this is an open-ended roster — treat specific lists as text-dependent. [late text]

## II. THE PRETA → PITṚ TRANSITION — the most important arc

The dead do not become ancestors automatically. At death the soul is a **preta** ("the departed / gone-forth") — a disembodied, *bodiless and hungry* spirit with no settled place, restless and potentially dangerous to the living. The entire post-death ritual program exists to *build it a new subtle body* and *promote* it from preta to pitṛ. This is the doctrinal heart of the topic.

- **Antyeṣṭi (cremation, the "last sacrifice")** - The corpse is offered into fire as a final oblation (the body itself the offering). Death-rites proper begin from cremation; see [varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md](varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md) for antyeṣṭi as the 16th samskara. The soul is now a *preta* with no body to inhabit.
- **Daśa-gātra / the ten-day piṇḍas** - From day 1 to day 10 after cremation, a **daily piṇḍa** is offered. Each day's piṇḍa builds one *limb* of the preta's new subtle body: day 1 the head, then neck, shoulders, heart, back, navel, hips/loins, thighs, knees/feet, and on the 10th the appetite/digestive capacity is completed (Garuḍa Purāṇa). Without these the preta has no vehicle and cannot eat or travel.
  - *Variant (limb-allocation):* the exact body-part assigned to each of the ten days varies slightly between Garuḍa Purāṇa recensions and regional manuals. [folk/late text]
- **Aśauca (death-pollution)** - The bereaved family is ritually impure for a fixed period (classically **10 days** for brahmanas, longer for lower varnas per Manusmṛti 5.59ff; in practice often 13). The impurity bars normal worship and is lifted at the end of the cycle.
- **Ekādaśāha / 11th-day rites** - On the 11th day purificatory and preparatory rites (vṛṣotsarga — release of a bull; gifts, *dāna* of bed, vessels, the deceased's necessities to brahmanas) equip the preta for the onward journey.
- **Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa — the decisive rite** - On the **12th day** (classically; often telescoped to the 12th/13th), the *single* preta-piṇḍa is divided and *merged* into the three piṇḍas of the father, grandfather, and great-grandfather — the act that **converts preta into pitṛ** and admits it to the sapiṇḍa fellowship. Only after sapiṇḍīkaraṇa is the deceased addressed as a *pitṛ* and worshipped in Vasu form; the great-great-grandfather is simultaneously bumped out into the remote ancestors. This is the formal "merger" that gives the rite its name (*sa-piṇḍa-karaṇa*, "making to share the piṇḍa").
  - *Variant (timing):* śāstra prescribes the 12th month (a full year of monthly *māsika* śrāddhas, then sapiṇḍīkaraṇa at year's end), but **Kali-Yuga abridgement** allows it on the 12th *day* — both are catalogued in the digests. Practice today overwhelmingly uses the 12th/13th day.
  - *Variant (Garuḍa Purāṇa journey):* in parallel with the piṇḍa-physiology, the GP narrates the preta's **one-year journey to Yama-loka** — crossing the river **Vaitaraṇī**, passing 16 cities/stations, arriving on the 11th–12th month — the māsika piṇḍas being the food that sustains it en route. See [../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/narakas-and-afterlife.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/narakas-and-afterlife.md).
- **Māsika & ūnamāsika śrāddhas** - The monthly memorial śrāddhas for the first year (and the "deficient-month" *ūnamāsika* offered at fractional intervals) feed the traveling preta until it reaches its destination and is enrolled among the pitṛs.
- **Sa-preta danger** - Until promoted, a neglected preta may become a malevolent ghost (*bhūta*, *piśāca*) — those who died *aputra* (sonless), by violence, suicide, or without rites are especially at risk of becoming wandering spirits; cf. the bestiary of the restless dead in [../08-beings-and-bestiary/](../08-beings-and-bestiary/).

## III. THE CORE OFFERINGS — piṇḍa-dāna & tarpaṇa

- **Piṇḍa-dāna** - Offering of **piṇḍas**: balls of cooked rice (sometimes barley/wheat flour, *piṇyāka*) kneaded with black sesame (*tila*), ghee, honey, and water. The piṇḍa is the *food-body* of the ancestor — both nourishment and, in the death-rites, the literal material of the new subtle body. Three piṇḍas for the three generations are standard; offered with *svadhā* and *darbha* grass, facing south.
- **Tila (black sesame)** - Indispensable; said to ward off rākṣasas and please the pitṛs. The phrase *tila-tarpaṇa* (sesame-water libation) is near-synonymous with the rite. Sesame's origin from Viṣṇu's sweat / its purificatory power is cited in the Purāṇas.
- **Tarpaṇa** - Libations of **water mixed with sesame and barley/rice**, poured from the cupped hands through the *pitṛ-tīrtha* (the part of the palm between thumb and index finger, the ancestors' "ford") to "satiate" (*√tṛp*, "to satisfy") gods, sages, and ancestors in turn. Performed daily by the orthodox (*nitya-tarpaṇa*), and centrally during Pitṛ-Pakṣa and at tīrthas. The water carries the offering down the *pitṛ-tīrtha* of the hand specifically toward the dead.
- **Darbha / kuśa grass and the south** - Sacred grass forms the seat and the *pavitra* (ring) worn; the whole rite is oriented **south** with the thread reversed (*prācīnāvīti*) — the spatial grammar that marks it as ancestral rather than divine.
- **Brāhmaṇa-bhojana & dāna** - Feeding brahmanas (who "stand in" for the pitṛs and Viśvedevas — the food eaten by the brahmana reaches the ancestor) and giving *dāna* (cows, gold, land, sesame, the deceased's goods) are integral; the brahmana's satisfaction *is* the ancestor's satisfaction.
- **Crow-feeding (kāka-bali)** - A portion of the offering is set out for **crows**, believed to be messengers/embodiments of the pitṛs; the ancestors are thought to come in crow-form, and a crow eating the offering signals acceptance. [folk] Central to the popular emotional life of Pitṛ-Pakṣa.
- **Variant offering-substances** - Beyond rice: barley, milk, fruit, honey, certain vegetables, and — pleasingly to the pitṛs for longer durations — particular meats/fish in the older Dharmaśāstra (Manu 3.267–272 lists what satisfies the ancestors for how long). Later/bhakti-influenced and regional practice tends toward vegetarian offering. [variant by text/region/era]

## IV. TYPES OF ŚRĀDDHA — the taxonomy

The digests enumerate many (often "twelve") classes; the load-bearing distinctions:

- **Nitya śrāddha** - The *obligatory daily/regular* offering to all ancestors (often minimal — water, or food to a brahmana/cow/crow).
- **Naimittika / Ekoddiṣṭa śrāddha** - "Occasioned, aimed at one (*eka-uddiṣṭa*)" — for a *single, recently deceased* person, before sapiṇḍīkaraṇa. Distinctively: **no three generations and no Viśvedevas are invoked**, only the one preta — because that soul is not yet a pitṛ. The death-cycle rites (daśa-gātra etc.) are ekoddiṣṭa in character.
- **Pārvaṇa / Parvaṇa śrāddha** - The "festival/parvan" śrāddha to the **three generations together**, with Viśvedevas — the *standard* ancestral śrāddha, performed on the death-anniversary tithi, on amāvāsyā, and during Pitṛ-Pakṣa. Three (or six, incl. maternal line) piṇḍas.
- **Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa śrāddha** - The merger rite (§II) — technically a fourth/transitional type bridging ekoddiṣṭa into pārvaṇa.
- **Ābhyudayika / Nāndīmukha śrāddha** - **Auspicious / "joyful-faced"** śrāddha performed *before* happy events (marriage, upanayana, birth, housewarming) to seek ancestral blessing — proof the pitṛs are sources of *prosperity*, not only objects of mourning. Offered facing east (not south), with the thread normal — the inverse posture of mournful śrāddha.
- **Tīrtha śrāddha** - Performed at a sacred *tīrtha* (Gayā, Kāśī, Prayāga, Kurukṣetra, the Godāvarī, riverbanks) for special potency.
- **Goṣṭhī, Śuddhi, Karmāṅga, Daivika, Puṣṭi, Kāmya śrāddhas** etc. - Further specialized types (for a gathering, for purification, as a subsidiary limb of another rite, for desire-fulfilment) listed in **Manusmṛti / Yājñavalkya-smṛti** and the digests. [open-ended roster — counts and names vary by digest]

## V. PITṚ-PAKṢA / MAHĀLAYA — the great annual season

- **Pitṛ-Pakṣa** - The "fortnight of the ancestors": the **dark half (kṛṣṇa-pakṣa) of the lunar month of Bhādrapada / Āśvina** (Sept–Oct), 15–16 lunar days dedicated to feeding all the pitṛs, who are believed to descend to the human world for this period. Also called **Mahālaya Pakṣa, Apara Pakṣa, Soḷaśrāddha ("sixteen śrāddhas"), Kanāgat, Jitiyā, Pitru Pokkho**. Regional reckoning differs: in the *amānta* (south/west) calendar it is the dark half of Bhādrapada; in the *pūrṇimānta* (north) reckoning the same fortnight falls in Āśvina — same days, different month-name. See [../00-time-and-cosmology/hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md](../00-time-and-cosmology/hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md).
- **Tithi-matching** - Each ancestor is ideally offered śrāddha on **the lunar tithi corresponding to their death-tithi** within the fortnight (e.g. one who died on a *navamī* is honoured on *navamī* of Pitṛ-Pakṣa). Special days: **Avidhavā Navamī** (9th, for married women who predeceased their husbands), and tithis for those who died by accident/weapon (offered on *Ghāyala Caturdaśī*).
- **Mahālaya / Sarva-Pitṛ Amāvāsyā** - The concluding **new-moon day** ("Mahālaya Amāvāsyā," *sarva-pitṛ* = "all the ancestors"). On it, śrāddha is offered to **all** ancestors at once — and crucially, per the **Garuḍa Purāṇa**, it is the catch-all day for those who **do not know the death-date**, missed the proper tithi, or whose forebears died at unknown times. The most important single tarpaṇa-day of the year; the day the visiting pitṛs depart. In 2026 Mahālaya Amāvāsyā falls on **2 October**; it also marks the transition into Devī-Pakṣa / the start of [Navarātri](festivals-and-vratas.md).
- **Compulsory for the son** - The śrāddha (esp. by the eldest son) is treated as a *non-optional duty* securing the ancestor's heavenly station; the desire for a son in Hindu thought is bound up with the need for someone to perform these rites (the *putra* who "saves from the *put* hell," a folk-etymology in Manu/Mahābhārata). Daughters/grandsons/other relatives may substitute per later and modern allowance. [variant — increasingly inclusive in modern practice]

## VI. GAYĀ ŚRĀDDHA — the supreme ancestral tīrtha

- **Gayā (Bihar)** - The preeminent place on earth for piṇḍa-dāna; offering at Gayā is held to grant **final liberation (mukti) to the ancestors** and to *discharge the pitṛ-debt permanently* — after a Gayā-śrāddha, annual śrāddha is (per many authorities) no longer strictly required. Extolled in **Garuḍa, Viṣṇu, and Vāyu Purāṇas** ("*Gayāyāṁ piṇḍadānena putrasya putratā*" — by offering piṇḍa at Gayā a son fulfills the very meaning of sonship). Dedicated file: [../06-sacred-geography/gaya-pitru-tirtha.md](../06-sacred-geography/gaya-pitru-tirtha.md).
- **The Gayāsura legend** - Gayāsura was a *daitya* of extreme piety to Viṣṇu whose body became so holy that anyone touching it was instantly liberated — threatening to *empty hell and abolish karma*. The gods asked him to sacrifice his body; Viṣṇu pressed him down with his foot (some versions: via Brahmā's yajña performed on his body, pinned by the *Dharmaśilā* stone). Dying, Gayāsura asked the boon that **his body become an eternal tīrtha where any ancestral offering grants the dead liberation** — granted by Viṣṇu. His body became the land of Gayā.
  - *Variant (who subdues him):* in some Purāṇic tellings the gods perform a great *yajña* on Gayāsura's recumbent body to keep him still and a stone (Dharmaśilā) is placed on him by Brahmā; in others Viṣṇu directly plants his foot. [variant by Purāṇa]
- **Viṣṇupada Temple** - Marks the spot, enshrining a **40-cm "footprint of Viṣṇu"** (*Viṣṇupāda*) incised in basalt — the foot that subdued Gayāsura. The principal site of Gayā piṇḍa-dāna. See [../06-sacred-geography/gaya-pitru-tirtha.md](../06-sacred-geography/gaya-pitru-tirtha.md) and the foot-print/pāda motif in [../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/).
- **The Gayā piṇḍa-circuit** - Classical Gayā-śrāddha (*Gayā-śrāddha* / *tri-pākṣika*) involves offering at a sequence of *vedis* (altars/ghats) over multiple days — the Phalgu river, Akṣaya-vaṭa (the "imperishable banyan" where the Phalgu-vat blesses the offerer), Prapitāmaheśvara, Brahma-sarovar and others. The **Phalgu river** itself figures in a Rāmāyaṇa-linked legend (Sītā's curse making it run underground) tied to ancestral offering.
- **Why Gayā "ends" the obligation** - Because Gayā grants the ancestors *gati* (a final good destination / liberation), the recurring sustenance-debt is settled — the transactional logic completed once and for all. [scriptural; some authorities still recommend continuing annual rites out of devotion]

## VII. PITṚ-DOṢA & the consequences of neglect

- **Pitṛ-doṣa ("ancestral affliction")** - The doctrine that *unsatisfied or improperly-sent-off ancestors* cause misfortune in the living lineage: childlessness, repeated miscarriage, failure, illness, family discord, obstacles to marriage. Diagnosed astrologically (afflictions to the 9th house, Sun/Rāhu/Ketu, *pitṛ-kāraka* in jyotiṣa); see [../00-time-and-cosmology/jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md](../00-time-and-cosmology/jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md). [folk/jyotiṣa — not a Vedic category as such]
- **Remedies** - Performance of overdue śrāddha, **Nārāyaṇa-bali** and **Tripiṇḍī-śrāddha** (special expiatory rites for pretas who died badly or were denied rites), tarpaṇa, Gayā-śrāddha, feeding, and tīrtha-pilgrimage. The *Nārāyaṇa-bali* specifically addresses unnatural death (suicide/accident/violence) where the soul is stuck as a preta.
- **The "five great sacrifices"** - Daily *pitṛ-yajña* (a morsel/water to ancestors) is one of the **pañca-mahāyajñas** every householder owes, alongside deva-, bhūta-, manuṣya-, and brahma-yajña — see [../90-literature-corpus/yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md](../90-literature-corpus/yajnas-and-vedic-rituals.md). The ancestral debt is thus a *daily*, not merely annual, obligation.

## VIII. SCRIPTURE vs. ACADEMIC DATING — the two clocks

- **Scriptural / traditional view** - The pitṛ-rites are *sanātana* (beginning-less), instituted by the primal Pitṛs and Manu, taught in śruti (the *pañcāgni-vidyā* of Chāndogya/Bṛhadāraṇyaka) and codified by Manu and the gṛhya-sūtras as eternal dharma.
- **Academic / historical view** - [scholarly] The *piṇḍapitṛyajña* is already in the **Black Yajurveda / Brāhmaṇas / Śrauta-sūtras** (~1000–600 BCE) as a śrauta rite; the domestic, three-generation *piṇḍa*-śrāddha is systematized in the **gṛhya-sūtras** (~600–300 BCE) and elaborated in **Manusmṛti** (~200 BCE–200 CE). The full *preta → pitṛ* journey-cosmology with Vaitaraṇī, Yama-loka stations, and Gayā-supremacy is a **Purāṇic** development (esp. **Garuḍa Purāṇa Pretakhaṇḍa**, ~mid-1st millennium CE and later). The two clocks: tradition reads one continuous eternal institution; scholarship reads a layered evolution from Vedic ancestor-oblation to medieval Purāṇic eschatology.
