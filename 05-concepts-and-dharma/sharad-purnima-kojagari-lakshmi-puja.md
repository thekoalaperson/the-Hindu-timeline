# Sharad Purnima / Kojagari Lakshmi Puja

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Concepts & Dharma](README.md) → **Sharad Pūrṇimā / Kojāgarī Lakṣmī Pūjā**. The first autumn full-moon (*Āśvina/Kārtika Śukla Pūrṇimā*) — the brightest moon of the year — kept across India as a *vrata* night with three distinct charters the corpus engages elsewhere but never ties to this *tithi*: Lakṣmī roaming to ask **"Ko jāgarti?"** ("who is awake?"); the moon **dripping amṛta** onto the *kheer* set under its beams; and, in Braj, **Kṛṣṇa's Mahā-Rāsa** with the *gopīs*.
>

**Sharad Pūrṇimā** (also **Kojāgarī Pūrṇimā**, **Kaumudī Pūrṇimā**, **Rāsa Pūrṇimā**, **Kumāra Pūrṇimā** in Odisha) is the **full moon of *Āśvina*** (Sept–Oct), the first *pūrṇimā* after [Sharad Navarātri](festivals-and-vratas.md#i-autumn--sharad-cluster--the-great-victories-of-good-over-evil) and roughly twenty days before [Diwali](festivals-and-vratas.md#i-autumn--sharad-cluster--the-great-victories-of-good-over-evil) — to which it stands as the **autumn (full-moon) counterpart** of Diwali's [Lakṣmī Pūjā](festivals-and-vratas.md#i-autumn--sharad-cluster--the-great-victories-of-good-over-evil) on the Kārtika new moon. It marks the close of the monsoon (the lunar month that opens the *Sharad* season) and the year's clearest, most luminous night — the charter-stack below all hangs on that exceptional moonlight (*kaumudī*). As across the calendar, **scripture-date and observance-date are kept distinct**: the charters are Purāṇic [traditional]/[late text], while the *attested vrata* in its regional forms is largely medieval-to-modern.

---

## I. Kojāgarī Lakṣmī Pūjā — "who is awake?"

- **Kojāgara / Kojāgarī Vrata** ("ko jāgarti" = "who is awake?") - the night **Lakṣmī**, goddess of wealth and fortune, is said to **descend and roam the earth** asking *"Ko jāgarti?"* — and to **bless with prosperity whoever she finds awake** (*jāgaraṇa*, the all-night vigil). Devotees keep vigil, light lamps, play dice (*pāśā/caupaṛ*), and worship Lakṣmī; the very name **Kojāgarī** is contracted from the question. Charter linked to the **Skanda** and **Brahmavaivarta** Purāṇa *vrata*-matter on the *Kaumudī/Kojāgara* night. [traditional]/[late text]
  - *Variant (Bengal — Kojāgarī Lakṣmī Pūjā):* the **principal Lakṣmī Pūjā of Bengal** — kept on Sharad Pūrṇimā rather than on Diwali night (as in the north/west) — with the *alpanā* (rice-paste floor art), the *jhāpi*/winnow, owl (*pecaka*, Lakṣmī's *vāhana*) and sheaf-of-paddy iconography of the harvest goddess. [regional]
  - *Variant (Odisha — Kumāra Pūrṇimā):* kept as **Kumāra Pūrṇimā**, the birthday of **Kārttikeya/Kumāra** (see [Ganesha & Kartikeya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md)); unmarried girls observe a dawn-to-moonrise fast and the **Gaja-Lakṣmī Pūjā**, worshipping both the war-god and the goddess of fortune. [regional]
  - *Variant (Mithilā / Assam):* in **Mithilā** (Bihar) and Assam the night is likewise the great **Kojāgarā Lakṣmī** vigil, paired with games of chance whose winnings are taken as a token of the goddess's favour for the year. [regional]
  - *Variant (the dice / Indra strand):* the dice-play is glossed in one strand as Lakṣmī (or Pārvatī) playing dice with her consort, the gambler's luck standing in for the goddess's coming year of fortune; some texts seat **Indra and Indrāṇī** worship on this night. [folk]

## II. The amṛta-moon and the *kheer* under the moonbeams

- **Amṛta-varṣā** ("the rain of nectar") - the belief that on this single night the **moon's rays carry *amṛta*** (nectar) and a special cooling, curative potency — the moon being the cup of *soma/amṛta* drained and refilled by the gods. Worshippers set out **rice-*kheer*** (*pāyasa*, milk-and-rice pudding) in open vessels **under the moonbeams overnight**, to be eaten at dawn as **cooling, medicinal prasāda**. [folk]
  - *Variant (Manthana / Candra link):* the charter rides directly on the corpus's [Samudra Manthana](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md) and [Candra/Soma](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/chandra-soma.md) material — the **moon (Candra) rose from the churning of the ocean** alongside **Lakṣmī** and the **amṛta** itself, so the year's brightest moon is read as the *amṛta*-bearing **Soma-vessel** at its fullest. The *kheer*-under-moonlight rite enacts the moon shedding nectar on the earth. [traditional]/[folk]
  - *Variant (Āyurvedic gloss):* the cooling moon-soaked *kheer* is taken as a **post-monsoon Pitta-pacifier**, the night marking the body's seasonal shift from monsoon damp to autumn dryness — see the *ṛtu* (seasonal-regimen) logic of [Āyurveda](ayurveda-and-the-science-of-life.md). The moon (*Candra/Soma*) is the cool, watery, *Kapha-Soma* luminary, hence its nectarine, restorative reading. [folk]/[scholarly]
  - *Variant (eye-medicine / longevity strand):* some traditions gaze at the moon through a needle's eye or take the moon-charged milk as a tonic for the eyes and longevity, the *amṛta* being the elixir of deathlessness. [folk]

## III. The Mahā-Rāsa — Kṛṣṇa's circle-dance in Braj

- **Mahā-Rāsa / Rāsa-Pūrṇimā** ("the great *rāsa*"; *rāsa-līlā*) - in Braj/Vṛndāvana, Sharad Pūrṇimā is the **night of the *rāsa-līlā***: summoned by **Kṛṣṇa's flute** on the **autumn full-moon night**, the **gopīs** leave their homes to dance the *rāsa*; Kṛṣṇa **multiplies himself** to partner each one in a circle, the theological heart of *bhakti-rasa*. The Bhāgavata sets the dance explicitly on this *śarat-pūrṇimā* moon (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* X.29–33; cf. *Harivaṃśa*). Dvāpara-Yuga event — see [Krishna Līlā § Rāsa-līlā](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md). [traditional]
  - *Variant (the lengthened "night of Brahmā"):* tradition holds Kṛṣṇa **stretched this one night to the length of a *night of Brahmā*** (a *kalpa*) so the *rāsa* could be danced in full — one reason the *tithi* itself is exalted; see the cosmological [night-of-Brahmā](../00-time-and-cosmology/units-of-time.md) measure. [traditional]/[folk]
  - *Variant (Vraja temple observance):* Vṛndāvana, Mathurā and the Braj temples keep a **Śarad-Pūrṇimā rāsa** with *rāsa-līlā* enactments and Kṛṣṇa swung/worshipped under the full moon; continuous with the swing-and-dance Vaiṣṇava *līlā* repertoire (cf. [Swing & Float Temple Festivals](swing-and-float-temple-festivals.md)). [regional]
  - *Note (distinct charter):* the Mahā-Rāsa night is a **separate strand** from the Lakṣmī and amṛta-moon charters above — Vaiṣṇava-devotional rather than fortune-/harvest-keyed — but all three sit on the same exceptionally bright *tithi*; kept separate here per file policy. [scholarly]

## IV. Kaumudī Mahotsava — the classical autumn moon-festival

- **Kaumudī Mahotsava** ("the great festival of moonlight"; *kaumudī* = moonlight) - the **classical-period autumn full-moon festival** of moonlight and revelry, named in Sanskrit literature (it lends its title to the *Kaumudī-mahotsava* play attributed to **Vijjikā/Kiśoravidyā**, and is referenced in dramatic and *nibandha* sources) — the courtly ancestor-form of the Sharad/Kaumudī Pūrṇimā keeping, when cities lit lamps and held nocturnal festivities under the *kaumudī*. The name survives in **Kaumudī Pūrṇimā** for this *tithi*. [scholarly]/[disputed]
  - *Note (two clocks):* Kaumudī Mahotsava anchors the **observance-date** layer — an *attested* classical-to-medieval moonlight festival — beneath the Purāṇic **charter** layer (Lakṣmī's "ko jāgarti", the amṛta-moon, the rāsa). The bright autumn moon is the **shared natural fact**; the goddess, the nectar and the dance are the charters later laid over it. [scholarly]

## V. Patterns across the Sharad-Pūrṇimā night

- **One *tithi*, three charters** - Lakṣmī's vigil (**fortune/harvest**), the amṛta-*kheer* (**moon/medicine**) and the Mahā-Rāsa (**Kṛṣṇa-bhakti**) are *distinct, non-merged* charters sharing the *Āśvina Pūrṇimā* moon — catalogued separately, never fused. [scholarly]
- **Autumn counterpart to Diwali Lakṣmī Pūjā** - the **full-moon** Lakṣmī worship of *Sharad Pūrṇimā* (esp. Bengal) is the eastern analogue of the **new-moon** [Lakṣmī Pūjā of Diwali](festivals-and-vratas.md#i-autumn--sharad-cluster--the-great-victories-of-good-over-evil) kept in the north and west — same goddess, opposite lunar phase, twenty days apart. [scholarly]
- **The brightest moon as the hinge** - every charter exploits the year's clearest moonlight: Lakṣmī can *see* who is awake, the moon is full enough to *drip nectar*, and the *rāsa* needs the silver autumn night — the natural fact (post-monsoon clear sky, full *Soma*-moon) carries the symbolism. [scholarly]
- **Harvest / seasonal substrate** - the night closes the monsoon and opens *Sharad*; the paddy-sheaf Lakṣmī, the cooling *kheer* and the new-rice *pāyasa* all betray a **first-harvest / seasonal-shift substrate** beneath the Purāṇic veneer — the two-clock (folk substrate vs. scriptural charter) distinction again. [scholarly]
- **Regional name-roster [flagged]** - Kojāgarī (Bengal/Mithilā), Kumāra Pūrṇimā (Odisha), Rāsa/Śarad Pūrṇimā (Braj), Kaumudī Pūrṇimā (classical/pan-Indian) are regional faces of **one *tithi***; the list is indicative, not closed. [folk]
