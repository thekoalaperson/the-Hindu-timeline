# Āyurveda — The Science of Life, the Tridoṣa & the Caraka/Suśruta Tradition

> Cross-cutting doctrine within [05-concepts-and-dharma](./README.md) of *The Hindu Timeline*; back to the [master index](../README.md). **Āyurveda** (*āyus* "life" + *veda* "knowledge") — the canonical Upaveda of medicine and one of the great indigenous Hindu knowledge-systems. This file treats the **doctrinal core** (the tridoṣa, pañca-mahābhūta body, sapta-dhātu, agni/āma, prakṛti), the **eight branches** (*aṣṭāṅga*), the **two founding schools** (Caraka/Ātreya vs. Suśruta/Dhanvantari), Vāgbhaṭa's synthesis, and the Brahmā→Dhanvantari transmission myth. Companion to the Upaveda genealogy in [../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md), the Dhanvantari churning-myth in [../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md), the breath-anatomy it shares in [./prana-and-the-vital-airs.md](./prana-and-the-vital-airs.md), and the element/tattva metaphysics it inherits in [./the-samkhya-tattvas.md](./the-samkhya-tattvas.md).

**Āyurveda** is the "science of (long) life" — at once a medical system, a philosophy of body–mind balance, and the **Upaveda of the Atharvaveda** (with a minority parentage in the Ṛgveda). It builds its physiology on Sāṃkhya's **pañca-mahābhūta** and gathers it into the famous **three-doṣa (tridoṣa)** humoral theory. Two clocks apply: the system frames itself as **beginningless (*śāśvata*) knowledge** revealed by Brahmā at the dawn of creation and handed down through gods and sages; academically, the surviving *saṃhitās* are redacted compositions of roughly the last centuries BCE through the mid-first millennium CE [scholarly].

## 1. The Doctrinal Core

### The Tridoṣa (Three Humors) — the central concept

- **The three doṣas** — the body and its functions are governed by three dynamic "faults/humors": **Vāta** (wind/movement), **Pitta** (bile/transformation), **Kapha** (phlegm/cohesion). Health is their dynamic equilibrium (*sāmya*); disease is their disturbance (*vaiṣamya*). This is the single most central Āyurvedic concept and the organizing frame of diagnosis and therapy. (Caraka Saṃhitā, Sūtrasthāna 1; Suśruta Saṃhitā, Sūtrasthāna 21; Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya, Sūtrasthāna 1.)
- **Vāta (vāyu)** — the principle of **movement**: derived from *ākāśa* + *vāyu*; governs respiration, circulation, nervous impulse, excretion, and all motion; cold, dry, light, mobile. The "king of the doṣas" — when deranged it drives the other two. Its five sub-types (*prāṇa, udāna, samāna, vyāna, apāna*) mirror the yogic vital airs — see [./prana-and-the-vital-airs.md](./prana-and-the-vital-airs.md). (Suśruta, Sū. 21; Caraka, Sū. 12.)
- **Pitta** — the principle of **transformation**: derived from *tejas/agni* (+ water); governs digestion, metabolism, body heat, vision, intellect, complexion; hot, sharp, light, oily. Five sub-types (*pācaka, rañjaka, sādhaka, ālocaka, bhrājaka*). (Suśruta, Sū. 21; Caraka, Sū. 12.)
- **Kapha (śleṣman)** — the principle of **cohesion & structure**: derived from *jala* (+ earth); governs lubrication, immunity, stability, growth, and the bulk of the tissues; heavy, cold, oily, stable. Five sub-types (*kledaka, avalambaka, bodhaka, tarpaka, śleṣaka*). (Suśruta, Sū. 21; Caraka, Sū. 12.)
  - *Variant (doṣa counts):* Suśruta and the surgical school weight **rakta (blood)** so heavily as a near-fourth factor that some read Suśruta as a "four-humor" frame; the Caraka/internal-medicine school keeps the strict **three**. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (mental doṣas):* the **manasa-doṣa** scheme of *sattva–rajas–tamas* (the three guṇas — see [./the-samkhya-tattvas.md](./the-samkhya-tattvas.md)) governs the mind alongside the bodily tridoṣa, giving a parallel psychic humoral theory. (Caraka, Śārīrasthāna.)

### The body's material basis & subsystems

- **Pañca-mahābhūta — the five-element body** — every bodily substance is a compound of the five gross elements **(ākāśa, vāyu, agni/tejas, ap/jala, pṛthivī)** taken from Sāṃkhya cosmology; the doṣas are themselves element-combinations. The macrocosm–microcosm axiom: *"as in the universe, so in the person"* (*loka-puruṣa-sāmya*). (Caraka, Śā. 5; see [./the-samkhya-tattvas.md](./the-samkhya-tattvas.md).)
- **Sapta-dhātu — the seven tissues** — the body is built of seven successively refined tissues, each nourishing the next: **rasa** (plasma/lymph) → **rakta** (blood) → **māṃsa** (muscle) → **medas** (fat) → **asthi** (bone) → **majjā** (marrow/nerve) → **śukra** (reproductive essence). The final refinement yields **ojas**, the subtle essence of vitality and immunity. (Suśruta, Sū. 14–15; Caraka, Cikitsāsthāna 15.)
- **Trimala — the three excretions** — *purīṣa* (faeces), *mūtra* (urine), *sveda* (sweat); their proper formation and elimination is a health marker. (Suśruta, Sū. 15.)
- **Agni & āma — digestive fire and toxins** — **agni** (esp. the central *jaṭharāgni*) cooks food into tissue and is the linchpin of health; weak or disordered agni leaves **āma**, an undigested toxic residue that clogs channels (*srotas*) and seeds disease. "All disease begins with disordered agni." (Caraka, Cikitsā. 15; Sū. 28.)
- **Srotas — the bodily channels** — the network of vessels/channels through which *doṣas*, *dhātus*, and wastes flow; their blockage (*srotorodha*) or over-flow underlies pathology. (Caraka, Vimānasthāna 5.)

### Prakṛti — individual constitution

- **Prakṛti (constitution)** — each person is born with a fixed ratio of the three doṣas, set at conception, defining a lifelong **type**: *vāta*, *pitta*, *kapha*, dual combinations, or balanced (*sama*). Diagnosis, diet, and therapy are tailored to it — a foundational personalization principle. (Caraka, Vimāna. 8, Śā. 8; Suśruta, Śā. 4.) [not to be confused with Sāṃkhya's cosmic *prakṛti*]
- **Triguṇa mental prakṛti** — a parallel psychic typology of *sāttvika, rājasika, tāmasika* temperaments grades mental constitution. (Caraka, Śā. 4.)

## 2. The Aṣṭāṅga — Eight Branches of Āyurveda

Āyurveda systematizes itself into **eight clinical limbs** (*aṣṭāṅga*), the canonical division by which the whole science is defined. (Suśruta, Sū. 1; Caraka, Sū. 30; Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya, Sū. 1.)

- **Kāya-cikitsā** — internal/general medicine (the Caraka school's heartland).
- **Śalya-tantra** — surgery (extraction of foreign bodies; the Suśruta school's heartland).
- **Śālākya-tantra** — ENT & ophthalmology (diseases above the clavicle).
- **Kaumāra-bhṛtya** — pediatrics, obstetrics & midwifery.
- **Agada-tantra** — toxicology (poisons, bites, antidotes).
- **Bhūta-vidyā** — psychiatry / "demonology" (mental disorders, spirit-affliction).
- **Rasāyana** — rejuvenation & longevity therapeutics.
- **Vājīkaraṇa** — aphrodisiacs & reproductive medicine.

## 3. The Two Foundational Schools (Sampradāyas)

Classical Āyurveda crystallized into **two great lineages**, never to be merged: the **medical** Ātreya/Caraka tradition and the **surgical** Dhanvantari/Suśruta tradition.

- **The Ātreya–Caraka school (kāya-cikitsā / internal medicine)** — traced to the sage **Ātreya Punarvasu**, whose teaching was set down by his pupil **Agniveśa** as the *Agniveśa-tantra*, later **redacted by Caraka** (and completed by **Dṛḍhabala**) as the **Caraka Saṃhitā** — the foundational text of internal medicine, emphasizing diagnosis, the tridoṣa, ethics of the physician, and pharmacology. (Caraka Saṃhitā.)
- **The Dhanvantari–Suśruta school (śalya / surgery)** — traced to **Divodāsa Dhanvantari**, king of Kāśī and incarnation of the divine physician, who taught **Suśruta**; preserved as the **Suśruta Saṃhitā**, the foundational surgical text — describing instruments (*yantra/śastra*), incisions, cataract couching, plastic/rhinoplasty repair, and detailed anatomy from cadaver study. (Suśruta Saṃhitā, Sū. 1.)
  - *Distinction:* Caraka leads in **internal medicine and doctrine**; Suśruta leads in **surgery and surgical anatomy** — the two are complementary, not rival readings of one canon. The pair plus Vāgbhaṭa form the **Bṛhat-trayī** ("great triad") of Āyurvedic classics. [scholarly]
- **Vāgbhaṭa's synthesis** — the physician **Vāgbhaṭa** (c. 6th–7th c. CE [scholarly]) harmonized the Caraka and Suśruta traditions into the **Aṣṭāṅga Saṅgraha** (prose+verse) and its condensed verse digest the **Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya** — among the most-studied Āyurvedic texts, third member of the Bṛhat-trayī. (Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya; Aṣṭāṅga Saṅgraha.)
  - *Variant (one vs two Vāgbhaṭas):* tradition and scholarship debate whether the *Saṅgraha* and *Hṛdaya* are by one author or two (an "elder" and "younger" Vāgbhaṭa). [disputed]
  - *Later digests (Laghu-trayī):* the medieval **Mādhava Nidāna** (diagnostics), **Śārṅgadhara Saṃhitā** (pharmaceutics), and **Bhāvaprakāśa** (materia medica) form the "lesser triad." [late text]

## 4. The Brahmā→Dhanvantari Transmission Myth

The system's mythic frame makes Āyurveda **revealed, beginningless knowledge** descending from the creator through gods to humans — the scriptural clock, distinct from textual dating above. (Caraka, Sū. 1; Suśruta, Sū. 1.)

- **Brahmā the origin** — Brahmā recalls/recites Āyurveda (as a part or upāṅga of the Atharvaveda) at the start of creation, in 100,000 verses and 1,000 chapters. (Suśruta, Sū. 1; Caraka, Sū. 1.)
- **The divine relay** — Brahmā → **Dakṣa Prajāpati** → the **Aśvins** (the twin divine physicians) → **Indra**; from Indra the knowledge passes to the human/sage world. (Caraka, Sū. 1; Suśruta, Sū. 1.)
  - *Variant (Caraka's descent):* Indra → **Bharadvāja** (who seeks it to relieve human suffering) → **Ātreya Punarvasu** → **Agniveśa** and fellow disciples → **Caraka's** redaction. (Caraka Saṃhitā, Sū. 1.)
  - *Variant (Suśruta's descent):* Indra → **Dhanvantari** incarnate as **Divodāsa**, king of Kāśī → **Suśruta** and fellow pupils. (Suśruta Saṃhitā, Sū. 1.) [the surgical lineage]
- **Dhanvantari at the Churning** — the divine physician **Dhanvantari** rises from the **Samudra Manthana** (churning of the ocean) bearing the pot of **amṛta** (nectar of immortality); he is hailed as the deva of medicine and, in Vaiṣṇava reckoning, an *aṃśa-avatāra* of Viṣṇu and patron of Āyurveda (worshipped on **Dhanteras**). Full episode: [../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md](../04-deep-dives/samudra-manthana.md). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.8; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.9.)
  - *Variant (two Dhanvantaris):* the Purāṇic ocean-born Dhanvantari and the historical/teaching **Divodāsa Dhanvantari** of Kāśī are sometimes distinguished as separate figures, sometimes as one re-incarnate. [disputed]
- **Atharvaveda as parent** — the surgical and medical hymns/charms of the **Atharvaveda** (against fevers, wounds, poisons; healing herbs) are taken as Āyurveda's Vedic root; Suśruta explicitly calls Āyurveda an **upāṅga of the Atharvaveda**. (Atharvaveda; Suśruta, Sū. 1.)
  - *Variant (Ṛgveda parentage):* some Upaveda lists attach Āyurveda to the **Ṛgveda** instead — see the catalog in [../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md). [regional]
