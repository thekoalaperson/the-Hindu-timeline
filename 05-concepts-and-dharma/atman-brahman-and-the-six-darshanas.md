# Atman, Brahman & the Mahavakyas — Vedanta and the Schools

> **Where this sits:** A cross-cutting doctrine of the *concepts-and-dharma* layer — the metaphysical core that underlies the whole cosmic hierarchy. The cosmos (kalpas, manvantaras, yugas) is the *play of Brahman*; this file asks *what that Brahman is, and what the individual self (atman) is in relation to it.*
> Parent: [../README.md](../README.md) · Sibling concepts: [../05-concepts-and-dharma/](.) · See also the Upanishadic creation accounts in [../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md](../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md) and the darshana catalog in [../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md).

This is the central metaphysical inquiry of Vedanta (*Uttara Mimamsa*): the nature of **Brahman** (the Absolute), of **atman** (the self), and of their relation. The Upanishads assert the identity; the *Brahma Sutras* of Badarayana condense the assertion into aphorism; and the great commentators (*bhashyakaras*) read those same aphorisms in radically divergent ways, producing the schools cataloged below. The cosmogonic Upanishadic passages themselves are treated separately — see the cross-link above — so this file does **not** re-narrate creation; it maps the doctrine.

## 1. Brahman — the Absolute

- **Brahman as the ground of all** - The single, infinite, self-existent reality from which all proceeds and into which all dissolves; "the One without a second" (*ekam evadvitiyam*) (Chandogya Upanishad 6.2.1).
- **Sat-Chit-Ananda** - Brahman characterized as Being-Consciousness-Bliss (*satyam jnanam anantam brahma*, "Brahman is reality, knowledge, infinity") (Taittiriya Upanishad 2.1).
- **Neti neti ("not this, not this")** - The apophatic method: Brahman is approached by negating every limiting predicate, since no description can contain it (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 2.3.6, 4.4.22).
- **Nirguna Brahman** - Brahman *without* attributes — the formless, transcendent, indescribable Absolute beyond name, form, and qualification.
- **Saguna Brahman / Ishvara** - Brahman *with* attributes — the personal Lord, creator-sustainer-dissolver, the object of devotion and meditation.
  - *Variant (Advaita / Shankara):* Nirguna is the higher truth (*paramarthika*); Saguna Brahman/Ishvara is real only at the empirical level (*vyavaharika*), Brahman conditioned by maya. The two are not two Brahmans but one reality at two levels of viewing.
  - *Variant (Vishishtadvaita / Ramanuja):* The "nirguna" texts deny only *defiling* qualities (*heya-gunas*); the supreme Brahman (= Vishnu/Narayana) is essentially **saguna**, possessing infinite auspicious attributes (*kalyana-gunas*). A truly attributeless reality is rejected.
  - *Variant (Dvaita / Madhva):* Brahman is Vishnu, fully personal and saguna; nirguna means free of *material* (prakritic) qualities only.
- **Brahman as material and efficient cause** - Unlike systems needing a separate maker and stuff, Vedanta holds Brahman is *both* the material cause (*upadana*) and efficient cause (*nimitta*) of the world (Brahma Sutra 1.4.23, *prakrti-adhikarana*).

## 2. Atman — the Self

- **Atman as the innermost self** - Not body, breath, mind, or ego, but the witnessing consciousness underlying them; "the Self pervading within and without" (Mundaka Upanishad 2.1.4; Katha Upanishad).
- **The chariot analogy** - The atman is the rider; the body the chariot; intellect the charioteer; mind the reins; senses the horses; sense-objects the roads (Katha Upanishad 1.3.3–4).
- **Atman as deathless** - Unborn, eternal, unslain when the body is slain — the teaching Krishna gives Arjuna (Katha Upanishad 1.2.18–19; Bhagavad Gita 2.20).
- **Jiva (the embodied self)** - Atman as it appears bound, individuated, transmigrating under karma. Whether jiva and Atman are ultimately one is the question that *defines* the schools (see §6).

## 3. The Four Mahavakyas — the "Great Sayings"

Tradition identifies four declarations, one drawn from each Veda, taken as the condensed essence of Upanishadic revelation. Each is also classed by function: *lakshana* (defining Brahman), *anubhava* (the seeker's realization), *upadesha* (the teacher's instruction), *anusandhana* (meditative application).

- **Prajnanam Brahma** — "Consciousness is Brahman" - The *lakshana-vakya* defining Brahman as pure awareness (Aitareya Upanishad 3.3 / 3.1.3, **Rigveda**).
- **Aham Brahmasmi** — "I am Brahman" - The *anubhava-vakya*, the realized seeker's own recognition of identity (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 1.4.10, **Yajurveda**).
- **Tat Tvam Asi** — "That Thou Art" - The *upadesha-vakya*, the teacher (Uddalaka) instructing the pupil (Shvetaketu) nine times over (Chandogya Upanishad 6.8.7 and refrain through 6.16, **Samaveda**).
- **Ayam Atma Brahma** — "This Self is Brahman" - The *anusandhana-vakya*, identifying the very Self one meditates on with Brahman (Mandukya Upanishad 2, **Atharvaveda**).
  - *Variant (Advaita reading):* These declare **literal identity** — the apparent jiva *is* the one Brahman; "Tat Tvam Asi" means tat = tvam exactly, the difference being illusory (Shankara's *Upadesha Sahasri*).
  - *Variant (Vishishtadvaita reading):* Ramanuja parses "Tat Tvam Asi" as *coordinate predication* (*samanadhikaranya*): "That [Brahman, whose body the soul is] art Thou" — affirming inseparable relation, not bare identity.
  - *Variant (Dvaita reading):* Madhva reads such texts as affirming *similarity/dependence*, even glossing some as "*atat tvam asi*" ("Thou art **not** That"), preserving eternal soul–God difference.

## 4. The Five Koshas — sheaths over the Self

The atman is "covered" by five concentric sheaths (*pancha-kosha*), gross to subtle, peeled back in self-inquiry (Taittiriya Upanishad 2.1–5):

- **Annamaya kosha** — the "food" sheath, the physical body built of and sustained by food.
- **Pranamaya kosha** — the "vital-energy" sheath of breath/prana animating the body.
- **Manomaya kosha** — the "mind" sheath of *manas* and the sensory faculties; seat of doubt and impulse.
- **Vijnanamaya kosha** — the "intellect/discernment" sheath of *buddhi*, ego, and the will that knows and decides.
- **Anandamaya kosha** — the "bliss" sheath, the subtlest covering, experienced in deep joy and approaching (but not being) the Self itself.
  - Mapping to the **three bodies** (*sharira-traya*): Annamaya = gross body (*sthula-sharira*); Pranamaya+Manomaya+Vijnanamaya = subtle body (*sukshma/linga-sharira*); Anandamaya = causal body (*karana-sharira*).

## 5. The Three States and Turiya — the Mandukya analysis

The Mandukya Upanishad (12 verses, **Atharvaveda**) maps consciousness onto the syllable **AUM** and four states (*avastha*):

- **Jagrat (waking)** — sound *A*; consciousness turned outward to the gross world; the cosmic correlate *Vaishvanara/Virat*.
- **Svapna (dream)** — sound *U*; consciousness turned inward to the subtle world of impressions; the correlate *Taijasa/Hiranyagarbha*.
- **Sushupti (deep sleep)** — sound *M*; undifferentiated mass of consciousness, bliss without objects; the correlate *Prajna/Ishvara*.
- **Turiya ("the fourth")** — the *silence* after AUM; not a state among the three but the unconditioned witness underlying all of them — pure, non-dual Atman; *Ayam Atma Brahma* applies here (Mandukya 7).
  - *Elaboration (Gaudapada's Mandukya Karika):* Gaudapada (Shankara's parama-guru) develops *ajata-vada* — the doctrine that nothing is ever truly born; the world is like a dream, and Turiya alone is real. [scholarly: the *Karika* is the earliest systematic Advaita text.]

## 6. The Vedanta schools — variant relations of atman to Brahman

All accept the *prasthana-traya* (the threefold canon: Upanishads, [Brahma Sutras](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md), Bhagavad Gita); they differ entirely in *how* they read it.

- **Advaita (non-dualism)** — *Shankara* (c. 8th c. CE), *Brahma Sutra Bhashya* - Atman **is** Brahman, wholly and without remainder. Plurality and the world are *vyavaharika* (empirically real, ultimately superimposed by **maya/avidya**); on liberation the jiva does not *go* anywhere — it *realizes* it was always Brahman. Moksha = knowledge (*jnana*).
- **Vishishtadvaita (qualified non-dualism)** — *Ramanuja* (1017–1137 CE), *Sri Bhashya* - Brahman is one but *qualified* (*vishishta*) by real internal distinctions: sentient souls (*cit*) and insentient matter (*acit*) form the **body of Brahman** (= Vishnu/Narayana). Souls are real, eternal, distinct *parts* — never identical to, never separable from, the Lord. Moksha = loving service in eternal communion; **bhakti** and *prapatti* (surrender) are the means.
- **Dvaita (dualism / Tattvavada)** — *Madhva* (1238–1317 CE), *Brahma Sutra Bhashya* - Five eternal differences (*pancha-bheda*): God–soul, God–matter, soul–soul, soul–matter, matter–matter are all real. Brahman (Vishnu) is wholly independent (*svatantra*); souls are eternally dependent (*paratantra*) and **graded** — even predestined, some to perpetual bondage (*tamo-yogyas*). Moksha = eternal bliss in God's presence, never merger.
- **Bhedabheda (difference-and-non-difference)** — *Bhaskara* (c. 9th c.), and earlier *Bhartriprapancha* - The soul is *both* different and non-different from Brahman, simultaneously and really; like waves and ocean, or sparks and fire. A tradition more than a single school; precursor to the later Vaishnava systems.
  - *Variant — Dvaitadvaita (Svabhavika-bhedabheda):* *Nimbarka* (c. 13th c.) — soul and world are naturally (*svabhavika*) both distinct from and dependent on Krishna; devotion to Radha-Krishna is central.
- **Shuddhadvaita (pure non-dualism)** — *Vallabha* (1479–1531 CE) - Brahman = Krishna is the *pure* (suddha) non-dual reality; souls and world are *real* emanations of Brahman, **not** products of maya (hence "pure" — undefiled by an illusion-principle). Path of grace, *Pushti-marga* ("the way of divine nourishment/grace").
- **Achintya-bhedabheda (inconceivable difference-and-non-difference)** — *Chaitanya* (1486–1534 CE); systematized by the *Gosvamis* of Vrindavana - The soul is **inconceivably** (*achintya*) at once one with and different from Krishna; the relation transcends logic and is grasped only through devotion. Basis of Gaudiya Vaishnavism. [The relation is held to be beyond rational resolution by design.]

### Contrast: Samkhya's dualism (not a Vedanta school)

- **Purusha and Prakriti** - Samkhya (one of the six [orthodox darshanas](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md)) is uncompromisingly **dualist**: plural eternal **purushas** (pure passive conscious selves) and a single **prakriti** (unconscious primal matter of three gunas) are *both* fully real and independent (Samkhya Karika of Ishvarakrishna).
- **No Brahman, classically no Ishvara** - Classical Samkhya is *nirishvara* (non-theistic): liberation (*kaivalya*, "isolation") is the purusha's discriminative realization that it is *not* prakriti — not union with any Absolute.
- **Where Vedanta diverges** - Vedanta refuses two ultimate realities: prakriti is re-read as **maya**, dependent on Brahman, not co-eternal with consciousness. Brahman is the single material-and-efficient cause; purusha-plurality is denied (or, in Advaita, reduced to one). Shankara's *Brahma Sutra Bhashya* explicitly refutes Samkhya's independent *pradhana* (Brahma Sutra 1.1.5–11, the *ikshaty-adhikarana*).
- **Bridge in the Gita** - The Bhagavad Gita absorbs Samkhya vocabulary (purusha, prakriti, gunas) but subordinates prakriti to the *Purushottama* (supreme person), making it Brahman's own lower nature (Bhagavad Gita 7.4–7, ch. 13–14).

## 7. Quick comparison table

| School | Founder/Bhashya | Atman–Brahman relation | Status of world | Moksha |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Advaita | Shankara | Identical | Maya (empirically real) | Knowledge; realize identity |
| Vishishtadvaita | Ramanuja | Part / body of Brahman | Fully real | Bhakti; eternal communion |
| Dvaita | Madhva | Eternally different | Fully real | Grace; bliss near God |
| Bhedabheda | Bhaskara/Nimbarka | Both, really | Real | Devotion + knowledge |
| Shuddhadvaita | Vallabha | Non-dual, no maya | Real emanation | Grace (Pushti) |
| Achintya-bhedabheda | Chaitanya | Inconceivably both | Real | Loving devotion |
| Samkhya (contrast) | Ishvarakrishna | No Brahman; purusha≠prakriti | Prakriti fully real | Kaivalya (isolation) |
