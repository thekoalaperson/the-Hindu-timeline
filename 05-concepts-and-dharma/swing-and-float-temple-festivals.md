# Swing & Float Temple Festivals — Dola, Jhulan & Teppotsavam

> Cosmic address: [The Hindu Timeline](../README.md) → [Concepts & Dharma](README.md) → **Swing & Float Temple Festivals**. Two processional temple-*utsava* genres the corpus has not yet catalogued: the **swing** (*dolā/hindola/jhūlana*) festivals, in which the deity is rocked on a flower-decked swing, and the **float** (*teppam/teppotsavam*) festivals, in which the *utsava-mūrti* is rowed around the temple tank on an illuminated raft. Both are *deity-procession* forms (movement of the god through space) rather than calendar *vratas* (fast-and-vow), and so sit beside — not inside — the pan-Hindu calendar.
>

This file catalogues two **processional temple-utsava genres** that the corpus treats nowhere despite covering the chariot *yatras*, the Kerala *poorams* and the South-Indian *brahmotsava* in [regional-temple-festivals.md](regional-temple-festivals.md): the **swing utsavas** (Dolā and Jhūlana) of the Vaiṣṇava world, and the **float utsava** (*teppotsavam*) of South-Indian Āgama temples. Two project distinctions run throughout: (1) **scripture-date vs. observance-date** — the swing-charters are anchored in the **Bhāgavata** Kṛṣṇa-līlā substrate and Purāṇic matter [traditional], while the *attested temple practice* is medieval-to-modern (the Vṛndāvana and tank-temple liturgies as we have them are largely post-15th-c.); (2) **deity-charter vs. agrarian/monsoon substrate** — both genres ride a seasonal undertow (Dolā at the spring full moon, Jhūlana in the monsoon, Teppotsavam in the cool post-monsoon tank-fill), a fertility/agrarian layer later mapped onto Kṛṣṇa, Venkaṭeśwara or the Goddess. These are **deity-in-motion** rites; cf. the chariot and palanquin *yatras* catalogued next door.

## I. Dolā Yātrā / Dol Pūrṇimā — the spring swing (eastern India & Braj)

- **Dolā Yātrā / Dol Pūrṇimā** ("the swing procession"; Odia *Dolā*, Bengali *Dol*) - the **Vaiṣṇava swing festival** on *Phālguna Pūrṇimā* (the spring full moon, Feb–Mar — the same *tithi* as [Holi](festivals-and-vratas.md#ii-spring-cluster--holi-and-the-burning-of-evil-and-desire)), when **Kṛṣṇa** as **Dolagovinda / Dolānanda** (with **Rādhā**, or with Lakṣmī) is set on a decorated **swing-palanquin** (*vimāna/dolā*) and rocked while devotees throw colored powder (*abīra/phāgu*). It is the **Vaiṣṇava counterpart to north-Indian Holi**, foregrounding the deity's swing rather than the Holikā bonfire. Deity = Kṛṣṇa/Jagannāth/[Kṛṣṇa Līlā](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md). (Wikipedia, *Dol Purnima / Dol Jatra*; Drik Panchang) [traditional]
  - *Variant (Odisha — Jagannāth Dolā):* in Odisha the *cālanti pratimā* (movable form) of Jagannāth, **Dolagovinda / Madanmohan**, is processed and swung; the **Dolā** is one of the named festivals of the Puri Jagannāth ritual year, the deity carried to a **dolā-maṇḍapa** in town and rocked over several days (*Dolā* through *Pūrṇimā*). [regional]
  - *Variant (Bengal — Dol/Gauḍīya):* in Bengal the day is **Dol Pūrṇimā / Dol Jātrā**, when **Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa** images are swung amid *abīr* and *kīrtana*; the **Gauḍīya** tradition doubles it as the **appearance day (birth) of Caitanya Mahāprabhu** (1486 CE), held to be a joint *avatāra* of Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa — so Bengali Vaiṣṇavas keep it as **Gaura-pūrṇimā** as much as a swing-festival. [regional/scholarly]
  - *Variant (Braj — Dāūjī / Holī-tide swing):* across Braj/Vṛndāvana the spring full moon's swing-play merges with the **Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa color-play** of [Braj Holī](festivals-and-vratas.md#ii-spring-cluster--holi-and-the-burning-of-evil-and-desire); the swing here is the *spring* swing, distinct from the monsoon Jhūlana below. [regional]
  - *Note (distinct from pan-Hindu Holi):* Dolā shares the *tithi* with Holi but is a **distinct rite** — a deity *swing-procession*, not the Holikā-Dahan bonfire / Hiraṇyakaśipu-Prahlāda charter catalogued under [Holi in festivals-and-vratas.md](festivals-and-vratas.md#ii-spring-cluster--holi-and-the-burning-of-evil-and-desire). Both are kept separate here per file policy. [scholarly]

## II. Jhūlana Yātrā / Hindolā — the monsoon swing (Vṛndāvana & the Gauḍīya–Puṣṭimārg world)

- **Jhūlana Yātrā / Hindolā Utsava** ("the swing festival"; *jhūlana* = swing, *hindolā* = cradle-swing) - the **monsoon swing festival**, run for several days up to *Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā* (the *Rakṣā Bandhan* full moon, Jul–Aug), when **Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa** are placed on **flower-decked swings** (*jhūlana/hindolā*) inside the temple and gently rocked by priests and devotees amid monsoon greenery, *kīrtana* and seasonal *malhār* music. Rooted in the **Bhāgavata** Kṛṣṇa-līlā image of the divine couple swinging in the Vṛndāvana groves during the rains. Deity = Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa/[Kṛṣṇa Līlā](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md). (Wikipedia, *Jhulan Yatra / Hindola*; ISKCON / Krishna.com) [traditional]
  - *Variant (Vṛndāvana — Bāṅke Bihārī):* at the **Bāṅke Bihārī** temple, Vṛndāvana, the swing is the festival's epicentre — the otherwise-veiled deity is brought to a silver/gold *hindolā* (the famous **svarṇa-hindolā**, golden swing) for the *jhūlana* days; one of Braj's most thronged *darśanas*. [regional]
  - *Variant (Gauḍīya / ISKCON):* the **Gauḍīya** and worldwide **ISKCON** calendar keeps **Jhulan Yatra** as a five-day festival ending on Balarāma's appearance day (*Balarāma Pūrṇimā*, = Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā), Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa swung nightly on decorated *jhūlanas* in temples across the globe. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Puṣṭimārg — Hindolā/Jhūlanotsava):* in the **Vallabha Puṣṭimārg** (Nāthdwārā, the *Śrīnāthjī* haveli, and the *havelis* of the seven *svarūpas*), **Hindolā** is celebrated through **Śrāvaṇa**, **Śrīnāthjī** placed daily on richly varied *hindolās* (flower, fruit, mirror, *paṭ* swings) — a hallmark of the *sevā* (graded service) aesthetic of that *sampradāya*. [regional]
  - *Note (distinct from Dolā):* Jhūlana is the **monsoon (Śrāvaṇa)** swing; Dolā the **spring (Phālguna)** swing — distinct festivals on distinct *tithis*, often conflated in casual usage but kept separate here. The swing-on-water boat festivals (Puri **Candana Yātrā**) are a *third*, related, water-procession form catalogued under [regional-temple-festivals.md](regional-temple-festivals.md#i-the-great-processional-yatras--gods-on-chariots--palanquins). [scholarly]

## III. Float Festival / Teppotsavam — the temple-tank raft procession (South India)

- **Teppotsavam / Teppam / Theppam** ("float festival"; Tamil *teppakkuḷam* = float-tank; Telugu *teppotsavam*) - the pan-South-Indian **temple-tank raft procession**, an **Āgama temple-utsava** in which the **processional deity** (*utsava-mūrti*) is taken from the sanctum onto an **illuminated, garlanded float/raft** (*teppam*) and rowed in circuits around the **temple tank** (*teppakkuḷam/puṣkariṇi*) over several evenings, lamps mirrored in the water. It is a **water-procession genre** parallel to the land-borne *brahmotsava* *vāhana* sequence; the spread is **both Vaiṣṇava and Śaiva**, on a shared agrarian / post-monsoon tank-fill substrate. (Wikipedia, *Float Festival*, *Teppakulam*; temple calendars) [traditional/regional]
  - *Variant (Madurai — Meenakshi Teppam):* the most famous, on the great **Vāṇḍiyūr (Mariamman) Teppakkuḷam** tank, when **Mīnākṣī** and **Sundareśwara** (the Goddess and Śiva, see [sthala-purāṇas](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md)) are floated on a decorated raft around the central island shrine on the *Thai* (Jan–Feb) full moon — popularly tied to the **birth-anniversary of Tirumalai Nāyak**, the 17th-c. ruler who had the tank dug. Śaiva–Śākta. [regional]
  - *Variant (Tirumala/Tirupati — Teppotsavam):* the five-day float festival of **Venkaṭeśwara** (with consorts **Śrī Devī, Bhū Devī** and **Sītā-Rāma, Kṛṣṇa**) on the **Svāmi Puṣkariṇi** at [Tirumala](../06-sacred-geography/tirumala-tirupati-venkateshwara.md), the *utsava-mūrti* **Malayappa Swāmi** rowed on the lamp-lit *teppam* — a Vaiṣṇava **Vaikhānasa-Āgama** float-utsava distinct from the great nine-day **Brahmotsavam** (catalogued in [regional-temple-festivals.md](regional-temple-festivals.md#i-the-great-processional-yatras--gods-on-chariots--palanquins)). Vaiṣṇava. [regional]
  - *Variant (Mylapore — Kapālīśwarar Theppam):* in Chennai, the **Kapālīśwarar** (Śiva) temple's float festival on the Mylapore temple tank, the processional deities floated over three nights in the Tamil month of *Thai* — a metropolitan Śaiva instance of the genre. [regional]
  - *Note (Āgama genre, not a single feast):* Teppotsavam is a **standard item of the Āgama utsava-repertoire** (alongside *brahmotsava*, *dhvajārohaṇa*, the *vāhana* mounts and the *avabhṛtha/tīrthavāri* tank-bath) replicated at countless South-Indian temples on locally fixed dates — an **open-ended roster**, the three above being only the best-known. [scholarly]

## IV. Patterns across the swing & float layer

- **Deity-in-motion, not deity-fasting** - unlike the *vratas* of [festivals-and-vratas.md](festivals-and-vratas.md), these are **processional utsavas**: the rite is the *movement* of the god (rocking on a swing, rowing on a tank), continuous with the chariot/palanquin *yatras* of [regional-temple-festivals.md](regional-temple-festivals.md). [scholarly]
- **Two swings, two seasons** - **Dolā** (Phālguna, spring full moon) and **Jhūlana/Hindolā** (Śrāvaṇa, monsoon) are *distinct* festivals on distinct *tithis*, both Vaiṣṇava and both rooted in the [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md) swing-imagery — frequently conflated, kept separate here. [scholarly]
- **Float festivals are an Āgama genre** - Teppotsavam is a *category* of temple-utsava (the water counterpart of the *vāhana* procession), spread across **both Vaiṣṇava and Śaiva** temples — Madurai (Śaiva-Śākta), Tirumala (Vaiṣṇava), Mylapore (Śaiva). [scholarly]
- **Agrarian / monsoon substrate, Purāṇic veneer** - the swing answers the rains and the tank-float the post-monsoon fill: a **fertility/water substrate** later mapped onto Kṛṣṇa, Venkaṭeśwara or the Goddess — the two-clock (folk substrate vs. scriptural charter) distinction again. [scholarly]
- **Royal-foundation layer (South)** - like Thrissur Pooram and Tirumala Brahmotsavam, the Madurai float-tank is an **early-modern royal work** (Tirumalai Nāyak, 17th c.), the rite's *attested form* medieval-to-modern even where the deity-myth is ancient. [scholarly]
- **Open-ended rosters [flagged]** - the temples keeping a *teppotsavam* and the *havelis*/temples keeping a *hindolā* run to dozens each; the instances above are indicative, not a closed list. [folk]
