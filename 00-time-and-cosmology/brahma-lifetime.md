# Brahmā's Lifetime (Mahākalpa)

> Where this sits: the outermost measurable shell of the cosmic clock. One [Day of Brahmā](./kalpa-and-pralaya.md) (a Kalpa) is 4.32 billion years; 360 day+night pairs make a Brahmā-year; 100 Brahmā-years make his full lifetime — the **Mahākalpa** (a.k.a. **Parārdha-pair / Para**), **311.04 trillion human years**. This file maps that whole span, where *we* are inside it (51st year, 1st day, [Shveta-Vārāha Kalpa](./kalpa-and-pralaya.md)), and what happens when it ends. Below this node sits the current Kalpa → [the 14 Manvantaras](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/).

---

## 1. The Architecture of Brahmā's Life

- **The full lifespan = 100 Brahmā-years = 1 Mahākalpa = 311.04 trillion human years** — the largest period that carries a personal creator-deity. Beyond it lies only unmanifest Prakṛti / Parabrahman. (Vishnu Purana 6.3; Bhagavata Purana 3.11, 12.4; Surya Siddhanta frame)
  - Derivation: 8.64 billion (one day+night of Brahmā) × 360 (days in a Brahmā-year) × 100 (years) = 311,040,000,000,000 ≈ **311.04 trillion**.
- **Para / Parārdha** — the lifespan splits into two halves of 50 Brahmā-years each; each half is one **Parārdha** (~155.52 trillion years). The two together = **one Para**. (Bhagavata Purana 3.11.34–36; Vishnu Purana 6.3)
  - *Note (terminology):* "Parārdha" literally = "half of a Para" / also a number (10¹⁷). The completed first half is the **Pūrva-Parārdha**; the half now in progress is the **Dvitīya / Uttara-Parārdha**.
- **Nested counting unit chain** (each level = personal deity's tenure):
  - 1 **Kalpa** = a Day of Brahmā = 1000 Mahāyugas = 14 Manvantaras + 15 Sandhyas = **4.32 billion yrs** → see [kalpa-and-pralaya.md](./kalpa-and-pralaya.md)
  - 1 **Night of Brahmā** = equal Kalpa of dissolution = 4.32 billion yrs (naimittika pralaya; Brahmā sleeps)
  - 1 **Brahmā-day+night** = 8.64 billion yrs
  - 360 of those = 1 **Brahmā-year** ≈ 3.1104 trillion yrs
  - 100 Brahmā-years = **Mahākalpa / Brahmā's lifetime** = 311.04 trillion yrs

## 2. "You Are Here" — Brahmā's Current Age

- **First Parārdha complete:** 50 Brahmā-years (~155.52 trillion yrs) have fully elapsed. Brahmā is now in the **51st year** — the start of his **second/uttara Parārdha**. (Bhagavata Purana 3.11.36; standard reckoning)
- **First day of the 51st year:** the current Kalpa is the **Shveta-Vārāha Kalpa** ("White Boar," named for Viṣṇu's Varāha avatāra lifting the Earth at this day's dawn). It is the **1st day of Brahmā's 51st year**. (Vishnu Purana 1.4; Bhagavata Purana 3.13)
  - *Variant (Padma Purana et al.):* some lists name the *first* Kalpa of creation the **Brāhma Kalpa**, and place Shveta-Vārāha as the *current* day — the two are reconciled by saying we are early in the second half of his life, not literally his first-ever day. [reconciliation note]
- **Within this Kalpa:** 6 Manvantaras elapsed; we are in the **7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara**, **28th Mahāyuga** of its 71, **Kali Yuga**, ~5,127 years in (as of 2026 CE). See [../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/).
- **So: roughly half of Brahmā's entire life is over.** He has ~50 more Brahmā-years (~155.52 trillion human yrs) to live.

## 3. The Four Pralayas (Dissolution by Scale)

Hindu cosmology nests *four* dissolutions; Brahmā's death corresponds to the third. (Vishnu Purana 6.3–4; Bhagavata Purana 12.4; Bhagavad Gita 8.17–19 for the day/night layer)

- **Nitya Pralaya (constant/perpetual)** — the ceaseless death of individual beings; ordinary mortality, every moment. (Vishnu Purana 6.3; Bhagavata 12.4.35)
- **Naimittika Pralaya (occasional / "Brāhma")** — at the end of each Day of Brahmā (Kalpa, 4.32 billion yrs). The three lower worlds (bhūr, bhuvar, svar) burn in the **Saṃvartaka fire**, then drown under **Saṃvartaka rain-clouds**; creation is withdrawn into Brahmā as he sleeps through his Night, to be re-emitted at the next dawn. Beings of the higher lokas survive. (Vishnu Purana 6.3; Bhagavata 12.4.6–13; Gita 8.18–19) → detailed in [kalpa-and-pralaya.md](./kalpa-and-pralaya.md)
- **Prākṛtika Pralaya (elemental / "Mahā-pralaya")** — at the end of Brahmā's **entire 100-year life**. This is the dissolution treated below in §4. Everything — including Brahmā himself and all lokas up to Satyaloka — collapses back into Prakṛti. (Vishnu Purana 6.4; Bhagavata 12.4.15–22)
- **Ātyantika Pralaya (absolute / final)** — not cosmic but individual: **mokṣa**, the jīva's permanent liberation from rebirth through true knowledge of Brahman; no return to saṃsāra. (Vishnu Purana 6.3; Bhagavata 12.4.34–36)
  - *Variant emphasis (Vedanta / Bhagavata):* some teachers rank ātyantika as the "highest" pralaya because it alone is irreversible for the liberated soul, whereas the cosmic three always recur.

## 4. Mahā-pralaya — The End of Brahmā's Life (Prākṛtika Dissolution)

When the 100 Brahmā-years complete, the **Great Dissolution** unwinds creation in *reverse order of emanation* — each tattva (cosmic element/principle) reabsorbed into its subtler source until only undifferentiated Prakṛti, and beyond it Parabrahman, remains. (Vishnu Purana 6.4; Bhagavata Purana 12.4.15–26)

- **Hundred-year drought & the twelve suns** — a drought of 100 (human-scale, intensified) years parches everything; the sun's rays multiply (often **seven or twelve suns**) and drink up all water, then scorch the three worlds to ash. (Bhagavata Purana 12.4.6–9; Vishnu Purana 6.3 — partly shared with the naimittika sequence, escalated here)
- **Saṃvartaka fire ascends** — fire driven by the wind (and from Śeṣa/Saṅkarṣaṇa's mouths in Vaishnava accounts) consumes the lower worlds and rises toward the higher lokas. (Bhagavata 12.4.9–10; Vishnu Purana 6.3)
- **The cosmic deluge** — colossal clouds (Saṃvartaka, Niḷaya, etc.) rain for a vast span, flooding the cosmos into a single shoreless ocean. (Vishnu Purana 6.4; Matsya Purana)
- **Sequential reabsorption of the tattvas (the distinctive prākṛtika stage):** water dissolves earth's quality → fire dissolves water → air dissolves fire → ether (ākāśa) dissolves air → the tanmātras and ahaṅkāra (ego) dissolve ether → mahat (cosmic intellect) absorbs ahaṅkāra → **Prakṛti absorbs mahat**. Each gross element merges into the subtle source from which it arose. (Vishnu Purana 6.4; Bhagavata 12.4.15–21)
- **Even the lokas and their lords dissolve** — unlike the Night of Brahmā, here the upper worlds (Maharloka, Janaloka, Tapoloka, **Satyaloka — Brahmā's own abode**) are destroyed. Brahmā himself, his life complete, is reabsorbed. (Bhagavata 12.4.4, 12.4.22; Vishnu Purana 6.4)
- **Equilibrium of the guṇas** — sattva, rajas, tamas return to perfect balance in unmanifest Prakṛti; "creation" ceases to exist as form. Only the witnessing Brahman / Mahāviṣṇu remains, beyond time. (Vishnu Purana 6.4; Bhagavata 12.4.22–26)
  - *Variant (Vaishnava, Bhagavata/Gaudīya):* the cosmos re-enters the body of **Mahā-Viṣṇu (Kāraṇodakaśāyī Viṣṇu)**, lying on the Causal Ocean; a new Brahmā is later born from the lotus at his navel to begin the next Mahākalpa. (Bhagavata 2.10.46; 3.11.40)
  - *Variant (Shaiva):* at the great dissolution all returns into **Śiva / Sadāśiva**, who alone is the un-dissolved (the "Mahā-saṃhāra-mūrti"); a new creative cycle issues from him. (Linga Purana, Shiva Purana)
  - *Variant (Shākta):* the ground that remains and re-emits is **Mahādevī / Ādi-Parāśakti**; Prakṛti is herself the Goddess, into whom even Brahmā-Viṣṇu-Śiva dissolve. (Devi Bhagavata Purana, Devi Mahatmya frame)

- **What comes after:** the Mahā-pralaya endures for a span equal to Brahmā's life (another 311.04 trillion yrs of un-manifestation, per several accounts), after which a **new Brahmā** is generated and the entire Mahākalpa cycle begins anew. Time and creation are thus beginningless and endless; this Brahmā is one in an infinite series. (Bhagavata 3.11.40–42; Vishnu Purana 6.4)
  - *Variant (Manusmṛti 1.73–74):* frames the alternation of Brahmā's waking/sleeping as the rhythm by which "the immortal" creates and reabsorbs, without dwelling on a terminal heat-death — emphasis on perpetual recurrence.

## 5. Many Brahmās, Many Universes (Variant Cosmologies)

Several traditions insist that *this* Brahmā is not unique — there are innumerable Brahmās across innumerable universes, and an infinite succession of them across time.

- **Brahmā-vimohana-līlā ("The Bewilderment of Brahmā")** — the Brahmā of *our* universe, doubting child-Kṛṣṇa, steals Kṛṣṇa's cowherd boys and calves; Kṛṣṇa expands into identical duplicates of them all for a year. Brahmā, returning, sees that creation never missed them — and is then shown **countless universes, each with its own Brahmā** of differing numbers of heads (4, 6, 10, 20, 100, 1000…) according to that universe's size. He repents his pride. (Bhagavata Purana 10.13–14)
  - Theological point: each Brahmā is a finite delegated creator; the size of his head-count scales with his universe — establishing Kṛṣṇa/Viṣṇu as the unbounded source. (Bhagavata 10.14; Brahma Saṃhitā 5.40–48)
- **Innumerable universes from Mahā-Viṣṇu's breath** — universes (brahmāṇḍas) emanate like bubbles / pores with each exhalation of Mahā-Viṣṇu; each is sealed in **seven concentric layers** (earth, water, fire, air, ether, total energy/mahat, false ego), each tenfold the prior. A Brahmā is born in each. (Bhagavata Purana 2.5; 3.11; Brahma Saṃhitā 5.11–13)
  - *Variant (Gaudīya Vaishnava synthesis):* the lifespan of *one* Mahā-Viṣṇu's exhalation = the life of all those Brahmās; even Brahmā's 311-trillion-year life is "less than a moment" for Mahā-Viṣṇu. (Brahma Saṃhitā 5.48; Chaitanya Charitamrita)
- **The Markandeya account** — the sage Mārkaṇḍeya, blessed with longevity beyond a Kalpa, witnesses the deluge between cosmic days and floats on the cosmic ocean, glimpsing the infant (Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa) who contains all worlds — narrative evidence of repeated dissolutions and re-creations. (Mahabharata, Vana Parva; Bhagavata 12.8–10)
- **Multiple-Brahmā framing in Indra's pride story** — in the tale where an ant-procession is revealed to be a line of former Indras, the larger frame likewise asserts a beginningless succession of Brahmās and Indras across cycling universes. (Brahma Vaivarta Purana; popularized via "Indra and the Ants")
  - *Variant note:* this story is most famously preserved in the Brahmavaivarta and in modern retellings; the "ants = past Indras/Brahmās" detail is a late/Puranic elaboration. [late text]
