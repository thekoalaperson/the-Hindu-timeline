# Bila-svarga and the Seven Pātālas (The Subterranean Paradises)

> Where this sits: a cosmography node of `00-time-and-cosmology/`. The seven **pātālas** that hang below the earth-disc are *not* hells: the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (5.24) brands them collectively **bila-svarga** ("the heaven of caverns / subterranean heaven") and rates them *superior* to the upper Svarga. This file states the inversion doctrine, the serpent-gem illumination, the timeless luxury of the inhabitants, the three-tier vertical map that keeps bila-svarga pointedly distinct from the punitive **narakas** below it, and a per-region roster. For the egg that contains all of it see [The Brahmāṇḍa & Bhuvana-kośa](./brahmanda-and-bhuvana-kosha.md); for the dissolution that ends it see [Kalpa & Pralaya](./kalpa-and-pralaya.md); for the "no aging / no time" claim against real durations see [Units of Time](./units-of-time.md).

---

## 1. The inversion doctrine — caverns that out-rank heaven

The defining and counter-intuitive claim: the *lower* worlds are *better* than the *upper* ones.

- **Bila-svarga = "the heaven of holes."** Śukadeva names the seven realms below the earth **bila-svarga** — *bila* ("cavern, hole, burrow") + *svarga* ("heaven") — and says that in them "there is a greater enjoyment of sense gratification than in the higher planetary systems," because their splendour, wealth, and luxuries surpass even those of the upper heavens. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.7–8)
- **Why "superior."** The denizens are **Daityas, Dānavas, Yakṣas, Rākṣasas, Nāgas** and their consorts who live in continuous, undisturbed enjoyment — opulent cities, gardens, groves, lakes, and palaces built by the demon-architect Maya — with none of the upheaval, rivalry, or eventual fall that marks Indra's Svarga. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.8–10)
- **Nārada's praise — the canonical articulation.** The *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* puts the inversion in the mouth of the sage **Nārada**: returning from the nether regions he tells the gods that **Pātāla is far more delightful than Svarga (Indra's heaven)** — its serpent-jewels, beautiful Dānava and Daitya women, fragrant air, and constant pleasures have no equal above. This speech is the classic textual proof-text for treating the lower worlds as paradises. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.5, Wilson trans.)
- **Read as theology, not geography.** The doctrine is a deliberate moral reversal: sense-pleasure is *richest* exactly where liberation is *furthest* — the asuras' paradise is a gilded trap, not a goal. The same texts that praise bila-svarga subordinate all of it to *mokṣa*. [scholarly]

## 2. Perpetual illumination — no sun, no moon, only serpent-gems

There is no solar light below the earth-disc; the *Bhāgavata* solves the problem with the **crest-jewels of the great serpents**.

- **No sun or moon, yet not dark.** Although the rays of the sun and moon do not reach the subterranean worlds, darkness is **dispelled by the glow of the gems (maṇi) on the hoods of the great Nāgas** — the cavern-heaven is self-lit by serpent-jewellery. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.9–10)
- **Daylight without time.** Because the light is constant and there is no sun to rise or set, the inhabitants experience **no day–night cycle and no seasons** — one reason the passage of time is imperceptible there (see §3). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.10)
- **Vāsuki and the Nāgas.** The serpent-kings who furnish this light — **Vāsuki** foremost, with Takṣaka, Kuhaka, and the many-hooded sons of Kadru — dwell in the lowest tiers; their jewels are the cosmos's subterranean "sun." (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.29–31)

## 3. The inhabitants — no aging, no disease, no felt time

- **Bodies that do not decay.** Those who live in bila-svarga "do not become old," are **free from disease, fear of death, anxiety, and wrinkles or grey hair**; their complexion stays youthful and their strength undiminished. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.13)
- **Time runs unfelt.** They pass their lives in continuous enjoyment of fine food, drink, and bathing, and **do not perceive the passage of time** — "they live a long time without any disturbance," ageless and unaware of the years sliding by. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.13–14)
  - *Two-clock caution:* "no felt time" is a claim *inside* the cosmographic clock — a description of subjective changelessness, not a suspension of the kalpic schedule. Bila-svarga still dissolves with the rest of the egg at pralaya (§5); its inhabitants are long-lived, not eternal. Compare the durations in [Units of Time](./units-of-time.md). [scholarly]
- **Built by Maya.** The cities, gardens, and pleasure-palaces are the work of **Maya Dānava**, the asura master-architect, whose sorcery furnishes the realms with illusory mansions, jewelled halls, and artificial gardens. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.10, 31)

## 4. The three-tier vertical map — and why bila-svarga ≠ naraka

The Purāṇas stack the cosmos in three named tiers; bila-svarga is the **middle-lower** band, *above* the hells.

- **(a) Upper heavens (ūrdhva-loka)** — the seven upper worlds (Bhū → Satya) along Meru's axis, Svarga among them; the realm of devas and ascended souls. Catalogued in [Devas, Asuras & the 14 Lokas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
- **(b) The earth-disc (Bhū-maṇḍala)** — the lotus-disc of seven dvīpas at the egg's waist; the human plane. See [The Brahmāṇḍa & Bhuvana-kośa](./brahmanda-and-bhuvana-kosha.md).
- **(c) The subterranean paradises (bila-svarga)** — the **seven pātālas**, beginning ~70,000 yojanas below the earth, each region 10,000 yojanas thick, stacked downward. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.7; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.5)
- **The narakas are a *fourth*, lower thing.** Below all seven pātālas — above the Garbhodaka ocean at the very floor of the egg, in the **southern** quarter of Yama — lie the **28 narakas**, the hells of *punishment*. The *Bhāgavata* keeps these textually and geographically separate from bila-svarga: the pātālas are splendid Nāga-and-asura paradises; the narakas are torture-realms. **Never conflate the two.** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.26.5) — catalogued in [Narakas & Afterlife](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/narakas-and-afterlife.md).

## 5. The seven pātālas, named — the *Bhāgavata* roster (5.24)

In descending order, each with its ruling tenant:

- **Atala** — realm of **Bala**, son of Maya Dānava. By a yawn he produced three kinds of bewitching women (*svairiṇī*, *kāmiṇī*, *puṃścalī*) who enchant any man who enters. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.16)
- **Vitala** — domain of **Śiva as Hāṭakeśvara** with his consort Bhavānī, attended by **Gaṇas**; from their union flows the river **Hāṭakī**, whose gold (*hāṭaka*) the inhabitants wear as ornaments. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.17–18)
- **Sutala** — kingdom of the virtuous Daitya emperor **Bali (Mahābali)**, grandson of Prahlāda, installed here by **Vāmana/Trivikrama** after he surrendered the three worlds; Viṣṇu himself stands guard at his gate. The most honoured of the pātālas. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.18–21; 8.22) — see [Daśāvatāra: Vāmana](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Talātala** — realm of the Dānava architect **Maya** himself, lord of sorcery (*māyā*), spared and protected by **Śiva (Tripurāri)** after the burning of the three cities. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.27)
- **Mahātala** — abode of the **many-hooded Nāgas**, the serpent sons of Kadru — Kuhaka, Takṣaka, Kāliya, Suṣeṇa and their kin — who sport with their families, ever fearful of Garuḍa. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.28)
- **Rasātala** — dwelling of the **Daityas and Dānavas** called **Paṇis** — the **Nivāta-kavacas, Kāleyas, Hiraṇya-puravāsīs** — perpetual enemies of the devas, hiding in holes like serpents, cowed only by the goddess Saramā's incantation. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.29)
- **Pātāla** (a.k.a. **Nāgaloka**) — the lowest tier, kingdom of the Nāga-king **Vāsuki** and serpents **Śaṅkha, Kulika, Mahāśaṅkha, Dhanañjaya** and others, many-hooded and gem-crested; below it (~30,000 yojanas) reclines **Ananta-Śeṣa**, bearing the worlds on a single hood. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24.29–31; Śeṣa at 5.25)
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 2.5):* lists the seven as **Atala, Vitala, Nitala, Gabhastimat, Mahātala, Sutala, Pātāla** — **Nitala** and **Gabhastimat** replace the *Bhāgavata*'s Talātala/Rasātala, and the sequence differs. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.5, Wilson) [disputed: nomenclature unsettled across Purāṇas]
  - *Variant (other lists):* some Purāṇas give **Tala, Atala, Vitala, Nitala, Talātala, Mahātala, Sutala**, reflecting genuinely unstable nomenclature; only the *count* (seven) is constant. (Loka traditions) [disputed]
  - *Note (Padma Purāṇa):* the *Pātāla-khaṇḍa* of the **Padma Purāṇa** uses Pātāla as the setting for narrative episodes (e.g. the **Pātāla-Rāmāyaṇa**, Rāma's pursuit of the asura Mahīrāvaṇa/Ahirāvaṇa into the netherworld) rather than re-cataloguing the cosmography — bila-svarga as *stage* rather than *map*. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Pātāla-khaṇḍa) [late text] — cf. [Hanumān: the Ahirāvaṇa episode](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md).

## 6. How it nests (synthesis)

- **Container** → all seven pātālas sit in the **lower half of the [Brahmāṇḍa](./brahmanda-and-bhuvana-kosha.md)**, above the Garbhodaka ocean, below the [earth-disc](./brahmanda-and-bhuvana-kosha.md).
- **Above** → the upper Svarga that bila-svarga *out-ranks* — the inversion of §1.
- **Below** → the **28 [narakas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/narakas-and-afterlife.md)**, a separate punitive tier, *not* part of bila-svarga.
- **Light** → no sun/moon; the **serpent-gems** of Vāsuki and the Nāgas (§2).
- **Time** → "no felt aging," but still bound to the [kalpa and its pralaya](./kalpa-and-pralaya.md); long-lived, not eternal (§3, [Units of Time](./units-of-time.md)).
