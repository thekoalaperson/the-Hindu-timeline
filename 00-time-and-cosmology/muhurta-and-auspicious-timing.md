# Muhūrta & the Auspicious / Inauspicious Windows of the Day

> Hierarchy location: `00-time-and-cosmology/` — the *electional* face of the clock. [`units-of-time.md`](units-of-time.md) defines the **muhūrta as a unit** (1/30 of a day-night ≈ 48 min); [`hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md`](hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md) stamps each day with its five limbs; this file catalogs **which slices of the day are fit or unfit for action** — the 30 named muhūrtas, Brahma-muhūrta and Abhijit, the Rāhu-kāla/Yamagaṇḍa/Gulika weekday grid, Choghaḍiyā, and the gaṇḍānta/pañcaka/bhadrā cautions that drive marriage and saṃskāra timing. Up-link: [section README](README.md).

**Muhūrta** carries a double sense: (a) a fixed **unit** — 2 ghaṭikā = 48 minutes, 30 to the ahorātra — and (b) an **elected moment**, the "right time" chosen from the pañcāṅga for a wedding, journey, or rite. The electional science (*muhūrta-śāstra*) is one of the three classical limbs of jyotiṣa (see [`jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md`](jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md)). Its grammar is simple: every day contains named good windows (Brahma-muhūrta, Abhijit), named bad windows keyed to the weekday (Rāhu-kāla, Yamagaṇḍa, Gulika-kāla), and larger periodic hazards (gaṇḍānta joints, pañcaka star-runs, the bhadrā karaṇa) that an almanac-reader steers around.

## 1. The 30 muhūrtas of the ahorātra

- **The count** — one ahorātra (day + night) = **30 muhūrtas** of 48 minutes each; a year of 360 such days = **10,800 muhūrtas**, a number the Brāhmaṇa texts prize for its correspondence to the bricks of the fire-altar and the syllables of the Ṛgveda. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa X.4.2; cf. [`units-of-time.md`](units-of-time.md) where all four unit-chains converge on 30 muhūrtas = 1 day.)
- **Named, not just numbered** — Vedic and later muhūrta literature assigns each of the 30 a **presiding name/deity**: 15 to daylight (sunrise→sunset), 15 to night. The roster below is the one compiled in modern muhūrta manuals from the **Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa** lists (TB III.10.1 names sets of muhūrtas in the Sāvitra-cayana context); spellings and one-to-one deity assignments **vary by compilation** [disputed]. (Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa III.10.1; Wikipedia *Muhurta*.)
  - **Day muhūrtas 1–15:** Rudra, Āhi, Mitra, Pitṛ, Vasu, Vārāha, Viśvedevā, **Vidhi/Abhijit (8th — midday)**, Satamukhī, Puruhūta, Vāhinī, Naktanakarā, Varuṇa, Aryaman, Bhaga.
  - **Night muhūrtas 16–30:** Girīśa, Ajapāda, Ahir-Budhnya, Puṣya, Aśvinī, Yama, Agni, Vidhātṛ, Kaṇḍa, Aditi, Jīva/Amṛta, Viṣṇu, Dyumadgadyuti, **Brāhma (29th — the pre-dawn Brahma-muhūrta)**, Samudram.
  - *Variant (auspiciousness grading):* manuals grade each of the 30 as śubha or aśubha (e.g. Rudra, Āhi, Pitṛ, Yama inauspicious; Mitra, Abhijit, Brāhma auspicious), but the gradings differ between compilations and are not fixed in the Vedic source [disputed] [late text].
- **Elastic in practice** — because daylight ≠ 12 hours except at equinox, working pañcāṅgas divide the *actual* sunrise-to-sunset span into 15 equal day-muhūrtas (and the night likewise), so a "48-minute" muhūrta stretches or shrinks with the season; the 48-minute figure is the mean. (Standard pañcāṅga practice; Drik Panchang.)

### 1a. Brahma-muhūrta — the creator's pre-dawn window
- **Definition** — the **penultimate (29th) muhūrta of the night**: the window beginning **2 muhūrtas (96 min) before sunrise and ending 1 muhūrta (48 min) before sunrise** (e.g. ≈ 4:24–5:12 am for a 6:00 sunrise). Named for Brahmā/Brahman; held to be when sattva predominates in the atmosphere and mind. (Muhūrta-śāstra convention; Wikipedia *Brahmamuhurta*.)
- **Dharmaśāstra injunction** — *"brāhme muhūrte budhyeta"* — one should **wake in the Brahma-muhūrta** and reflect on dharma and artha. (Manusmṛti 4.92)
- **Āyurvedic injunction** — the healthy person should **rise in the Brahma-muhūrta** "to protect his life-span" (*brāhme muhūrta uttiṣṭhet svastho rakṣārtham āyuṣaḥ*). (Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya, Sūtrasthāna 2.1; the same rule appears in dinacaryā chapters of the Suśruta tradition.)
- **Use** — prescribed for waking, ablution, japa, Vedic study, meditation and yoga — *not* an electional window for worldly contracts; it is a daily observance rather than a chosen muhūrta.
- *Variant (definition):* some modern usage loosely calls the whole last 1½ hours before dawn "Brahma-muhūrta"; the śāstric definition is the specific 29th muhūrta ending 48 min before sunrise [folk].

### 1b. Abhijit muhūrta — the victorious midday window
- **Definition** — the **8th of the 15 day-muhūrtas**, centred on **local solar noon** (±24 min; e.g. ≈ 11:36 am–12:24 pm for a 6–6 day). Treated as **universally auspicious**: it is said to "conquer" (abhi-√ji) most minor flaws of the day, and serves as the default muhūrta when no better election is available. (Muhūrta-śāstra convention; see the fuller treatment under [`nakshatras.md`](nakshatras.md) §3.)
- **Relation to the star Abhijit** — the daily *muhūrta* is named after, but computed independently of, the 28th nakṣatra **Abhijit** (Vega) — the supernumerary star that the **Mahābhārata (Vana/Āraṇyaka Parva)** describes as having slipped from the sky while contesting seniority with Rohiṇī, narrated in the Skanda cycle. (Mahābhārata III; [`nakshatras.md`](nakshatras.md).)
- **Exceptions** — tradition **excludes Wednesdays** from Abhijit's blanket auspiciousness, and most manuals do **not** allow Abhijit alone to sanction a **marriage**, which demands full election (§4) [folk] [disputed]. (Muhūrta manuals; Drik Panchang.)

## 2. The weekday grid — Rāhu-kāla, Yamagaṇḍa, Gulika-kāla

The daytime (sunrise→sunset) is divided into **8 equal parts** (mean 1½ hours each). Three of the eight are struck out daily, their position rotating with the weekday. All three are ruled by **shadow-beings**: Rāhu the eclipse-node, Yama's "poison-knot" (yama-gaṇḍa), and **Gulika/Māndi, the "son of Śani"** — one of the upagrahas already rostered in [`jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md`](jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md). These windows are **absent from the classical siddhāntas**; they are a later, pan-Indian almanac practice, observed with special rigor in **South India** [late text] [regional]. (Drik Panchang; Wikipedia *Rahukaalam*.)

The grid (segment of 8, with mean clock-times for a 6:00–18:00 day):

| Weekday | **Rāhu-kāla** | **Yamagaṇḍa** | **Gulika-kāla** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sunday | 8th (4:30–6:00 pm) | 5th (12:00–1:30) | 7th (3:00–4:30) |
| Monday | 2nd (7:30–9:00 am) | 4th (10:30–12:00) | 6th (1:30–3:00) |
| Tuesday | 7th (3:00–4:30 pm) | 3rd (9:00–10:30) | 5th (12:00–1:30) |
| Wednesday | 5th (12:00–1:30 pm) | 2nd (7:30–9:00) | 4th (10:30–12:00) |
| Thursday | 6th (1:30–3:00 pm) | 1st (6:00–7:30) | 3rd (9:00–10:30) |
| Friday | 4th (10:30–12:00) | 7th (3:00–4:30) | 2nd (7:30–9:00) |
| Saturday | 3rd (9:00–10:30 am) | 6th (1:30–3:00) | 1st (6:00–7:30) |

- **Rāhu-kāla (Rāhu-kālam)** — the strongest daily prohibition: **no new undertaking, journey, purchase or auspicious rite is begun** in it (work already begun may continue). Because it is anchored to *actual* sunrise/sunset, its clock-time shifts daily; the table's times are means only. Especially binding in Tamil, Kerala, Karnataka and Andhra practice [regional]. (Drik Panchang; Wikipedia *Rahukaalam*.)
  - *Variant (worship inversion):* Rāhu-kāla is deliberately **chosen** for propitiating Rāhu himself, and in a well-known regional cult, for **Durgā worship on Tuesdays/Fridays** (Rahu-kala Durga worship) [regional] [folk].
- **Yamagaṇḍa (Yamaghaṇṭa-kāla)** — "Yama's knot": work begun in it is said to **end in destruction/death of the purpose** (kārya-hāni); avoided above all for **journeys**. Linked in upagraha schemes to the death-god Yama. [late text] (Drik Panchang.)
- **Gulika-kāla (Māndi's hour)** — ruled by **Gulika/Māndi, son of Śani** (see the naga-named upagraha bullet in [`jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md`](jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md)). Its signature belief: **whatever is done in Gulika-kāla repeats** — hence it is shunned for funerals and illness-related acts (which one does not want repeated), while a minority usage *elects* it for acts one wishes to multiply (savings, medicine-taking) [folk]. (Drik Panchang; muhūrta manuals.)
- **Durmuhūrta** — additionally, one or two of the 30 named muhūrtas are voided each day by weekday rule (e.g. Rudra on Sunday); pañcāṅgas print these as *durmuhūrtam* alongside the three kālas above [late text]. (Drik Panchang.)

## 3. Choghaḍiyā — the western-Indian eight-fold day [regional]

- **The system** — dominant in **Gujarat, Rajasthan and Marwari mercantile usage**: day (sunrise→sunset) and night (sunset→sunrise) are each cut into **8 choghaḍiyās** of ~4 ghaṭīs (mean ≈ 1½ h; the name = *cho* "four" + *ghaḍiyā* "ghaṭīs"). Used above all for **travel and business openings**, where a full muhūrta election is impractical. (Drik Panchang — Choghadiya; Wikipedia *Choghadiya*.)
- **The seven names and their planetary lords** — **Udvega** (Sun — bad, agitation), **Cara/Chala** (Venus — good, esp. travel), **Lābha** (Mercury — gain, good), **Amṛta** (Moon — best), **Kāla** (Saturn — bad; but elected for wealth-accumulation by some [folk]), **Śubha** (Jupiter — good), **Roga** (Mars — bad, illness/conflict).
- **The rotation rule** — the **first choghaḍiyā of the day belongs to the weekday's lord** (Sunday opens with Udvega/Sun, Monday with Amṛta/Moon, Tuesday with Roga/Mars, Wednesday with Lābha/Mercury, Thursday with Śubha/Jupiter, Friday with Cara/Venus, Saturday with Kāla/Saturn), and the sequence then follows the **horā order of planets** (Sun→Venus→Mercury→Moon→Saturn→Jupiter→Mars, repeating), so the **8th choghaḍiyā always repeats the 1st**; the night series continues the same cycle. (Drik Panchang — Choghadiya.)
- **Interaction with the kāla grid** — practice treats a good choghaḍiyā as **overridden by Rāhu-kāla**: even an Amṛta window is dropped if Rāhu-kāla overlaps it [folk] [regional].

## 4. Periodic hazards — gaṇḍānta, pañcaka, bhadrā

- **Gaṇḍānta ("knot-end")** — the three **junctions where a water sign meets a fire sign**, which are simultaneously nakṣatra junctions: **Revatī→Aśvinī (Mīna→Meṣa), Āśleṣā→Maghā (Karka→Siṃha), Jyeṣṭhā→Mūla (Vṛścika→Dhanus)**. The last portion of the first star and first portion of the next (commonly the last/first quarters, or a degree-band around the joint) are **spiritually "knotted" and inauspicious** for beginnings; a **birth** in gaṇḍānta (esp. Mūla/Āśleṣā/Jyeṣṭhā joints) traditionally calls for **śānti rites** performed by the parents. Threefold in the manuals: **tithi-gaṇḍānta** (junction of pūrṇā and nandā tithis: 5th→6th, 10th→11th, 15th→1st), **nakṣatra-gaṇḍānta** (the three star-joints), **lagna-gaṇḍānta** (the same sign-joints rising). (Bṛhat Parāśara Horā Śāstra, birth-in-Mūla śānti chapters [late text]; muhūrta manuals; cross-list at [`nakshatras.md`](nakshatras.md) §4.)
- **Pañcaka ("the fivefold")** — the run of **five nakṣatras from Dhaniṣṭhā (its latter half) through Śatabhiṣaj, Pūrva-Bhādrapadā, Uttara-Bhādrapadā and Revatī** — i.e. the Moon's transit of **Kumbha and Mīna**, ≈5 days each sidereal month. Prohibited works during pañcaka [folk] [regional]: **roof-laying/house-thatching, buying a cot/bed, collecting fuel or wood, journeying south** (Yama's direction), and above all **cremation** — a death in pañcaka is believed to draw **four more deaths** in the family unless the rite is corrected by cremating **effigies (of kuśa/darbha or flour)** alongside the body, a funerary remedy attributed to the **Garuḍa Purāṇa** in practice [folk] [disputed].
  - *Variant (five kinds by weekday):* almanacs subdivide by the weekday on which the pañcaka begins — **Roga-pañcaka** (Sunday), **Rāja-pañcaka** (Monday), **Agni-pañcaka** (Tuesday), **Mṛtyu-pañcaka** (Saturday), **Cora-pañcaka** (Friday), with Wednesday/Thursday starts held harmless — a purely almanac-level elaboration [folk] [late text].
- **Bhadrā (Viṣṭi karaṇa)** — the **7th movable karaṇa** (half-tithi) of the pañcāṅga cycle (roster in [`hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md`](hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md) §1), recurring **8 times each lunar month** and universally treated as **inauspicious for beginnings**; specific rites are *calendar-locked against it* — **Holikā-dahana must not be lit, and rakhi must not be tied, while Bhadrā prevails**. (Muhūrta manuals; Drik Panchang.)
  - **Personification** — Bhadrā is mythologized as a **daughter of Sūrya and Chāyā, sister of Śani**, born so fierce that she devoured whatever rite she attended, until **Brahmā assigned her a fixed seat in the karaṇa-cycle as Viṣṭi**, where her times could be known and avoided — a frame-story retold in astrological lore [late text] [folk].
  - **The three-world rule** — a floating verse of the Muhūrta-Cintāmaṇi tradition locates Bhadrā by the **Moon's sign**: Moon in Karka, Siṃha, Kumbha or Mīna → Bhadrā dwells in **martya-loka (earth) — fully harmful**; Moon in Meṣa, Vṛṣabha, Mithuna or Vṛścika → she is in **svarga**; in Kanyā, Tulā, Dhanus or Makara → in **pātāla** — and *"only where she dwells does she destroy the work"*, so heaven- and netherworld-Bhadrās are treated as tolerable on earth [late text] [disputed]. (Muhūrta-Cintāmaṇi tradition.)

## 5. Electional basics — marriage and the saṃskāras

- **The election stack** — a full muhūrta for a saṃskāra clears, in order: the **year** (Jupiter/Venus visible, not an adhika-māsa), the **month/season**, the **tithi**, the **weekday**, the **nakṣatra**, the **yoga/karaṇa**, and finally the **lagna** rising at the chosen minute (*lagna-śuddhi*, with the 7th/8th houses clean for marriage). The canonical manuals: **Bṛhat Saṃhitā** of Varāhamihira (electional chapters; 6th c. CE [scholarly]), **Muhūrta-Cintāmaṇi** of Rāma Daivajña (1600 CE [scholarly]), **Muhūrta-Mārtaṇḍa** of Nārāyaṇa Daivajña (16th c. [scholarly]), and in the south **Kālaprakāśikā**.
- **Vivāha (marriage) nakṣatras** — the manuals admit chiefly **Rohiṇī, Mṛgaśīrṣa, Maghā (not its 1st quarter), Uttara-Phalgunī, Hasta, Svātī, Anurādhā, Mūla (not its 1st quarter), Uttarāṣāḍhā, Uttara-Bhādrapadā, Revatī**; full star-gradings are in [`nakshatras.md`](nakshatras.md). (Muhūrta-Cintāmaṇi, vivāha-prakaraṇa.)
- **Closed seasons** — no marriages during: **Cāturmāsya** (Viṣṇu asleep, Devaśayanī→Devotthāna Ekādaśī); **adhika-māsa** (the intercalary month, devotionally special but electionally void — see [`hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md`](hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md) §4); **Kharamāsa/Dhanurmāsa & Mīnamāsa** (Sun in Dhanus or Mīna — the *malamāsa* of northern practice [regional]); and while **Guru or Śukra is combust** (*gurvasta, śukrāsta* — Jupiter/Venus invisible near the Sun), a hard stop in all schools. (Muhūrta manuals; Drik Panchang.)
- **Other saṃskāras** — **upanayana** is preferred in **Uttarāyaṇa** (the Sun's auspicious northward half-year — see [`hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md`](hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md) §3c); tonsure (cūḍākaraṇa), first feeding (annaprāśana) and gṛha-praveśa each carry their own tithi/nakṣatra tables in the same manuals; the pattern, not the roster, is the invariant.
- **Godhūli-lagna** — the "cow-dust hour" at sunset is accepted, especially in northern and folk practice, as an **all-purpose fallback marriage window** when a computed lagna is unavailable [folk] [regional]. (Muhūrta manuals.)

## 6. Two clocks — how old is all this?

- **Scriptural frame** — the 30-muhūrta day and the deity-named muhūrtas belong to the Veda itself (Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa, Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa) and are treated by tradition as timeless features of the ahorātra, valid in every yuga.
- **Academic frame [scholarly]** — the layer-dates: 30-muhūrta reckoning in the **Brāhmaṇas c. 900–700 BCE**; muhūrta as a working unit in the **Vedāṅga Jyotiṣa** (traditionally dated by its internal solstice to c. 1400–1200 BCE, academically placed mid–1st millennium BCE [disputed]); the **Manusmṛti** Brahma-muhūrta rule c. 200 BCE–200 CE; systematic electional astrology from **Varāhamihira (c. 550 CE)**; the mature manuals **16th–17th c. CE** (Muhūrta-Mārtaṇḍa, Muhūrta-Cintāmaṇi 1600 CE); and the **Rāhu-kāla/Yamagaṇḍa/Gulika grid and Choghaḍiyā** unattested in the classical siddhāntas — late, regional almanac practice of uncertain (likely second-millennium CE) origin [late text] [scholarly].
