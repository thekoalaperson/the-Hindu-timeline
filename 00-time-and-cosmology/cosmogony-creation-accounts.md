# Cosmogony — Accounts of Creation

> Where this sits: `00-time-and-cosmology/` — the foundational node. Before the cosmic clock starts ticking (yugas, manvantaras, kalpas), the texts ask *how the cosmos arose at all*. Hindu tradition gives not one answer but many, layered chronologically (Vedic → Upanishadic → Puranic) and sectarianly (Vaishnava / Shaiva / Shakta). This file catalogs ALL major accounts as distinct, source-attributed variants. For the resulting time-frame see [the cosmic clock](./README.md) and [the kalpa structure](../01-brahma-current-life/).

A key conceptual point: most accounts describe **cyclic re-creation** (the cosmos is re-projected each Day of Brahma after dissolution/pralaya), not an absolute one-time origin. The Puranas distinguish:
- **Sarga** — primary creation: the unfolding of the cosmos out of unmanifest *prakriti* at the start of Brahma's life.
- **Pratisarga / Visarga** — secondary creation: the repeated re-manifestation within each kalpa by Brahma (mind-born sons, lineages, beings).

## I. Vedic Hymn Accounts (oldest layer)

- **Nasadiya Sukta — the Hymn of Creation** (RV 10.129). The most philosophically radical account: begins *"Then there was neither existence (sat) nor non-existence (asat); neither air nor sky beyond."* No death, no immortality, no day/night. **"That One" (Tad Ekam) breathed windless, by its own impulse**; apart from it nothing was. Darkness hidden by darkness, an undifferentiated salila (water/flux). Through **tapas (heat / cosmic ardor)** "that One" arose; then **kama (desire), the first seed of mind (manas)**, came upon it — the bond linking being to non-being, discerned by sages searching in their hearts. (Rigveda, Mandala 10 — late stratum) [agnostic / non-theistic]
  - *Distinctive ending:* the gods came **after** creation, so who truly knows its origin? *"He who is its overseer in the highest heaven — he surely knows, or perhaps he knows not."* Widely read as one of the earliest expressions of cosmological agnosticism. (RV 10.129.6–7)
  - *Variant emphasis (Upanishadic gloss):* later Vedanta reads "That One" as **Brahman**, the impersonal absolute, retrofitting a monistic creation onto the hymn's open question. (Brihadaranyaka, Chandogya Upanishads)

- **Hiranyagarbha Sukta — the Golden Womb/Embryo** (RV 10.121). A **golden germ (Hiranyagarbha)** arose in the beginning, born as the **sole lord of all that exists**, sustaining earth and heaven. Each verse but the last asks the refrain *"Kasmai devaya havisha vidhema?"* — "To which god shall we offer worship?" — naming the creator only obliquely as **Prajapati** ("the lord of creatures") in the closing verse. The germ floats on the primordial waters and becomes the cosmic egg / source of the world. (Rigveda 10.121)
  - *Continuity note:* Hiranyagarbha is later identified with **Brahma** (Puranas) and with **Prajapati / Brahman** (Brahmanas, Upanishads); it is the conceptual bridge from the Vedic "golden embryo" to the Puranic cosmic egg (Brahmanda). (Manusmriti 1.5–9; Vishnu Purana)

- **Purusha Sukta — creation by cosmic sacrifice** (RV 10.90). The cosmos is produced by the **dismemberment of the Cosmic Man (Purusha)** in a primal *yajna* performed by the gods. Purusha has a thousand heads, eyes, feet; he pervades the earth and extends ten fingers beyond; he is *"all that has been and all that will be."* The gods sacrifice him; from his parts the world is fashioned:
  - From his **mind → the moon**, **eye → the sun**, **mouth → Indra and Agni**, **breath → wind (Vayu)**, **navel → the atmosphere**, **head → the heavens**, **feet → the earth**, **ear → the directions**.
  - The **four varnas** issue from him: **Brahmana from the mouth, Kshatriya from the arms, Vaishya from the thighs, Shudra from the feet** — the classic (and much-debated) charter of the social order. (Rigveda 10.90)
  - *Variant (Atharvaveda / Taittiriya):* expanded Purusha hymns recur (e.g. AV 19.6; Taittiriya Aranyaka 3), with variation in which limb yields which entity. [text variant]

- **Brahmanaspati / Asat→Sat account** (RV 10.72). The gods (devas) are born; **"existence was born from non-existence" (asatah sad ajayata)**; the worlds are produced by Brahmanaspati like a smith forging. Daksha is born of Aditi and Aditi of Daksha (mutual generation — a deliberate paradox). (Rigveda 10.72) [archaic; contradicts the Nasadiya Sukta's refusal to derive sat from asat — catalog both]

- **Cosmic waters / Apah and the embryo** (RV 10.82, Nasadiya echoes; Brahmana cosmogonies). The undifferentiated **primeval waters** carry a floating embryo from which the creator emerges — a motif feeding directly into both Hiranyagarbha and the lotus-from-navel account. (Rigveda; Shatapatha Brahmana)

## II. Brahmanic / Upanishadic Accounts

- **Prajapati as creator-by-tapas / self-multiplication.** Prajapati, alone and afraid, desires *"May I be many, may I propagate"*; he performs tapas and emits the worlds, creatures, and the Vedas; he is exhausted ("emptied out") by creating and is reconstituted by sacrifice. (Shatapatha Brahmana; Taittiriya Brahmana)
- **Atman / Brahman as sole reality (Upanishadic monism).** *"In the beginning this was Self (Atman) alone"* — the Self, lonely, desires a second, splits into male and female (husband/wife), and from their union all beings arise; the One becomes the many through *nama-rupa* (name and form). (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 1.4) 
  - *Variant (Chandogya 6.2):* *"In the beginning this was Being (Sat) alone, one only, without a second"* — explicitly **rejecting** the Nasadiya/RV 10.72 idea that being came from non-being. The Sat wills "May I be many," producing fire, water, food in sequence. [direct doctrinal contradiction — catalog both]
  - *Variant (Taittiriya 2.7):* creation proceeds out of *ananda* (bliss); from Atman → space → air → fire → water → earth → plants → food → man.
  - *Variant (Aitareya Upanishad 1):* Atman creates the worlds, then the cosmic Purusha, from whom the organs and their presiding deities emerge.

## III. Vaishnava Account — Brahma from the Lotus from Vishnu's Navel

- **Nabhi-kamala / lotus-born Brahma.** During the cosmic night (pralaya), **Vishnu (as Narayana) reclines on the serpent Shesha (Ananta) upon the causal ocean (Kshira-sagara / Ekarnava)**. At the dawn of creation a **lotus springs from Vishnu's navel**, and upon it sits **Brahma**, the active agent of creation who then projects the worlds. Vishnu is thus the ultimate source; Brahma the secondary, delegated creator. (Bhagavata Purana esp. 3.8; Vishnu Purana; Matsya Purana; Mahabharata)
  - *Madhu–Kaitabha episode:* two demons born from Vishnu's ear-wax threaten the newborn Brahma / steal the Vedas; Vishnu slays them — framing the order of creation against primordial chaos. (Mahabharata; Devi Mahatmya 1; Bhagavata Purana)
  - *Variant (Brahma's bewilderment):* Brahma, born on the lotus, does not know his own origin; he descends the lotus-stalk seeking its root, fails, performs tapas, and is granted vision of Vishnu — establishing Vishnu's supremacy. (Bhagavata Purana 3.8–9)
  - *Variant (egg on the lotus):* some accounts place the **cosmic egg (Brahmanda)** atop the navel-lotus, with Brahma forming inside it (synthesis of the lotus and egg motifs). (Puranic syncretic versions)
  - *Cross-note:* the **periodicity** — Brahma emerges to create at the start of each Day of Brahma after Shiva/Vishnu's dissolution — anchors this to [the kalpa cycle](../01-brahma-current-life/).

## IV. The Cosmic Egg — Brahmanda

- **Brahma-from-the-golden-egg (Hiranyagarbha → Brahmanda).** The creator's seed becomes a **golden egg** floating on the cosmic waters; it incubates for a divine year (or a thousand ages). Brahma develops within and the egg splits: **its upper shell → the heavens (Svarga), lower shell → the earth (Bhumi), the space between → the sky/atmosphere (Antariksha)**; the membranes/fluids become mountains, clouds, oceans, rivers. (Manusmriti 1.5–13; Vishnu Purana 1.2; Markandeya, Brahmanda, Vayu, Linga Puranas)
  - The Brahmanda is enclosed in **seven concentric sheaths** — water, fire, air, ether (akasha), ahamkara (ego-principle), mahat (cosmic intellect), and pradhana/avyakta (unmanifest primordial matter) — each tenfold the previous. (Vishnu Purana 1.2; Bhagavata Purana 3.26)
  - *Variant (Manu):* the Self-existent (Svayambhu) first creates the waters, deposits seed in them which becomes the golden egg; **he himself is born within it as Brahma**, then divides the egg. (Manusmriti 1.8–13)
  - *Variant (Chandogya 3.19):* the **non-existent (asat) becomes existent**, turns into an egg, lies a year, splits into a **silver half (earth) and golden half (sky)** — an early egg-cosmogony from the cosmic Sun-egg. [text variant — egg from asat]

## V. Shaiva Account — Shiva / Sadashiva as Source

- **Shiva as the supreme cause; Brahma and Vishnu as his agents.** In Shaiva texts the creator (Brahma), preserver (Vishnu), and destroyer (Rudra) are all subordinate emanations of **the supreme Shiva**, who alone creates, sustains, destroys, conceals, and reveals (the **pancha-kritya**, five acts). (Shiva Purana; Linga Purana; Kurma Purana)
  - **Lingodbhava — the infinite pillar of light.** Brahma and Vishnu dispute who is supreme; an **endless column of fire (jyotirlinga)** appears. Vishnu (as boar) burrows down, Brahma (as swan) flies up, neither finds its end; Shiva emerges from the linga as the true source of both. (Shiva Purana; Linga Purana) — the origin myth of the **linga** as the formless cosmic source.
  - *Tantric Shaiva cosmology (Kashmir Shaivism / Shaiva Siddhanta):* creation is **emanation (abhasa) of the 36 tattvas** from the absolute. The hierarchy descends: **Parashiva / Anuttara (the unconditioned absolute) → Shiva-tattva and Shakti-tattva → Sadashiva → Ishvara → Shuddha-vidya**, then through maya into the impure tattvas down to earth. **Sadashiva** is the stage where the first stirring of "I-this" cognition (the seed of objectivity) arises — the cosmos is Shiva's self-recognition (pratyabhijna), not a material act. (Tantraloka of Abhinavagupta; Pratyabhijna texts; Shaiva Siddhanta agamas) [philosophical/tantric variant]
  - *Variant (Sadashiva-murti):* the five faces of Sadashiva (Sadyojata, Vamadeva, Aghora, Tatpurusha, Ishana) correspond to the five elements / five acts and generate the cosmic order. (Shaiva agamas)

## VI. Shakta Account — Devi / Shakti as Primordial

- **The Goddess as the ultimate creatrix and Brahman.** In Shaktism the **Mahadevi (Adi Parashakti)** is the supreme reality and the creative power; Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva owe their existence and capacity to act to **her**. Without Shakti, Shiva is *shava* (a corpse) — consciousness is inert without power. (Devi Mahatmya / Durga Saptashati; Devi Bhagavata Purana; Devi Upanishad)
  - **Yoganidra / Mahamaya awakens the cosmos.** At the start of a cycle Vishnu lies in yogic sleep on the causal ocean; the demons Madhu and Kaitabha arise; **the Goddess as Yoganidra** withdraws from Vishnu so he can wake and slay them — the Goddess is the power that drives even Vishnu's creative role. (Devi Mahatmya 1)
  - **Devi creates the Trimurti.** The Devi Bhagavata Purana has the Supreme Goddess bring forth **Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva themselves** and assign them their cosmic functions, then grant each a consort-shakti (Saraswati, Lakshmi, Parvati/Kali) who are her own portions. (Devi Bhagavata Purana 3)
  - *Tantric Shakta cosmology:* creation as the Goddess's **self-expansion through stages of involution** (e.g. eight stages), her *spanda* (vibration) projecting the tattvas; she is both the unmanifest (Para) and the manifest universe. (Shakta tantras; Devi Bhagavata) [tantric variant]
  - *Variant (Lalita Mahatmya):* **Lalita Tripurasundari** manifests from the cosmic fire of the gods' sacrifice and creates/orders the worlds. (Brahmanda Purana — Lalitopakhyana)

## VII. Puranic Sequential Creation — Sarga & the Categories of Creation

The Vishnu Purana (1.2–5) and Bhagavata Purana give an ordered, philosophical unfolding from unmanifest *prakriti* via the Samkhya tattvas, then a graded series of creations. This is the spine onto which the manvantara/yuga clock attaches.

- **Primary unfolding (Prakrita sarga).** From Vishnu's will, **pradhana/prakriti** (unmanifest matter) is disturbed; the gunas (sattva, rajas, tamas) lose equilibrium; there emerge in sequence: **mahat (cosmic intellect) → ahamkara (ego) → the tanmatras (subtle elements) and the senses → the five gross elements (mahabhutas)** → the cosmic egg (Brahmanda), within which Vishnu as Brahma begins active creation. (Vishnu Purana 1.2; Bhagavata Purana 3.5, 3.26 — Samkhya scheme)

- **The nine creations (sargas)** of the Vishnu Purana (1.5) — three Prakrita (elemental) + five Vaikrita (derived) + one mixed:
  1. **Mahat-tattva** (the intellect / Mahat) — *Prakrita*.
  2. **Bhuta-sarga (tanmatra)** — the subtle elements / principle of egoism — *Prakrita*.
  3. **Indriya / Vaikarika** — the sense-organs — *Prakrita* (sometimes counted with #2).
  4. **Mukhya-sarga** — immovable things (plants, minerals) — *Vaikrita*.
  5. **Tiryak-srotas** — animals ("horizontal current") — *Vaikrita*.
  6. **Urdhva-srotas** — celestials/devas ("upward current") — *Vaikrita*.
  7. **Arvak-srotas** — humans ("downward current") — *Vaikrita*.
  8. **Anugraha-sarga** — the creation of grace/sentiment (beings of the four kinds re: dharma) — *Vaikrita/mixed*.
  9. **Kaumara-sarga** — the creation of the Kumaras (the eternal, ageless mind-born ascetics) — *mixed*. (Vishnu Purana 1.5; Bhagavata Purana 3.10)
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana 3.10):* lists **ten creations** — six by Brahma (mahat, ego, elements, senses, devas, etc.) plus three Prakrita plus the Kaumara — with somewhat different grouping/ordering than the Vishnu Purana. [count variant: 9 vs 10]
  - *Variant (Brahma's failed first attempts):* Brahma's initial emanations are unproductive; he creates in stages, repeatedly dissatisfied, finally succeeding — the order of attempts differs across Puranas. (Bhagavata Purana 3.12)

## VIII. Secondary Creation — Brahma's Mind-Born Sons, Rudra, and the Prajapatis

Once the framework exists, **Brahma populates it** (visarga / pratisarga). This generates the [lineages (vamsha)](../03-lineages-vamsha/) that the historical timeline rests on.

- **The mind-born sons (Manasaputras).** Brahma, desiring to create, first produces sons **from his mind** (not body). The standard nine (Vishnu Purana 1.7): **Bhrigu, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu, Angiras, Marichi, Daksha, Atri, Vasishtha** — the **Brahmarshis / Prajapatis**, progenitors of all beings. (Vishnu Purana 1.7; Manusmriti 1.34–35)
  - *Variant counts:* texts list **7, 9, 10, or more** mind-born sons.
    - *Seven (Saptarishi):* Marichi, Atri, Angiras, Pulaha, Kratu, Pulastya, Vasishtha. (Mahabharata; various Puranas)
    - *Ten Prajapatis:* the above nine **+ Narada** (or + others; lists vary by text). (Bhagavata Purana; Hindu tradition)
    - *Additional mind-born:* **Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanatana, Sanatkumara** (the four/Kumaras), **Narada**, **Dharma**, **Kardama**, and others appear depending on text. (Bhagavata Purana 3.12)

- **The Kumaras refuse to procreate.** Brahma's first sons, the four **Sanaka-kumaras** (eternal celibate child-sages), reject creation and remain renunciant — frustrating Brahma's plan to populate the world. (Bhagavata Purana 3.12; Vishnu Purana 1.7) — the **Kaumara-sarga** of the nine creations above.

- **Birth of Rudra from Brahma's anger.** Enraged at the Kumaras' refusal, Brahma's brow (forehead), darkened with frowns, emits **Rudra** — half male, half female. Brahma bids him divide; the male part becomes **eleven Rudras**, the female the **shaktis/goddesses**. (Vishnu Purana 1.7; Bhagavata Purana 3.12)
  - *Variant:* Rudra is told to create but produces only beings like himself (immortal, fierce); Brahma stops him and assigns creation of mortals to the Prajapatis instead. (Shiva-leaning Puranas) [sectarian inversion of the Shaiva account where Shiva is supreme]

- **Brahma splits into male and female — Svayambhuva Manu and Shatarupa.** Brahma divides his body into two halves: the male becomes **Svayambhuva Manu** (the first Manu), the female **Shatarupa**; their union begins the human/dynastic line. (Vishnu Purana 1.7; Manusmriti 1.32) — links to [Manvantara 01: Svayambhuva](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/).
  - Their children: sons **Priyavrata** and **Uttanapada**; daughters whose progeny seed further lineages.

- **Daksha and the population of the worlds.** **Daksha Prajapati** (a mind-born son / and reborn son of the Prachetas in a later cycle) fathers numerous daughters who, married to the sages and gods, become the mothers of **devas, asuras, animals, plants, nagas, and all living kinds**. His **daughters married to Kashyapa (Aditi, Diti, Danu, etc.)** generate the Adityas (gods), Daityas, Danavas (demons), and more — the great branching of beings. (Vishnu Purana 1.15, 1.21; Bhagavata Purana 6.6)
  - *Variant:* the number of Daksha's daughters varies — commonly **24** (married to Dharma, the sages, etc.) and/or **60** (assigned to Kashyapa, the moon, etc.). [count variant]
  - *Daksha's two creations:* "creation by will" (mind-born offspring) first; when insufficient, Daksha resorts to **sexual generation (maithuni srishti)** — marking the shift to the present mode of procreation. (Vishnu Purana 1.15)

- **The presiding cosmic functionaries.** Brahma also establishes the **Manus, Indras, Saptarishis, gods, and ancestors** appropriate to each manvantara — the recurring "officers" of cosmic time. (Vishnu Purana 3.1–2; Bhagavata Purana 8) — see [the 14 manvantaras](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/).

## IX. Synthesis — How the Variants Relate

- **Layered, not contradictory (traditional harmonization):** the Vedic hymns describe the *unmanifest absolute and first impulse*; the Upanishads identify it as Brahman/Atman; the Puranas narrate the *staged material unfolding* and *who governs it*. Each sect (Vaishnava/Shaiva/Shakta) names its supreme deity as the unmanifest source and casts the Trimurti as that deity's functional aspects.
- **Genuine doctrinal tensions (catalog, don't resolve):** *sat-from-asat* (RV 10.72, Chandogya 3.19) **vs** *sat-only / no-derivation* (Chandogya 6.2, Nasadiya Sukta); **agnostic** origin (Nasadiya) **vs** confident theistic origin (Purusha Sukta, Puranas); sectarian supremacy disputes (Vaishnava lotus-Brahma vs Shaiva Lingodbhava vs Shakta Devi-creates-Trimurti).
- **The hand-off to the timeline:** every account converges on **Brahma as the operative creator of the current kalpa**, whose Day begins the manvantara/yuga sequence. From here the clock starts — see [Brahma's current life](../01-brahma-current-life/) and [the cosmic time units](./README.md).
