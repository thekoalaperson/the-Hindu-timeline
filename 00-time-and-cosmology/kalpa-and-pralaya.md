# Kalpa, Day/Night of Brahmā & the Dissolutions (Pralaya)

> Where this sits: a core-spine node of `00-time-and-cosmology/`. The **kalpa** is the largest unit before Brahmā's own lifespan — one Day of Brahmā. This file defines the kalpa, its mirror-image Night (`pralaya`), the **four kinds of dissolution**, what dissolves vs. persists, and the naming of notable kalpas. For the units below the kalpa see `./yugas-and-mahayuga.md` and `./manvantara.md`; for the cosmic address see the FRAMEWORK CONSTANTS §4.

---

## 1. The Kalpa = One Day of Brahmā

- **Kalpa defined** — 1 kalpa = 1000 mahāyugas = 14 manvantaras + 15 sandhyās (junctures) = **4,320,000,000 years (4.32 billion)**. This is exactly one **Day of Brahmā** (*Brahmā-divasa*). (Viṣṇu Purāṇa I.3; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.11; [Kalpa (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpa_(time)))
  - Arithmetic check: 14 × 71 mahāyugas = 994; + 15 sandhyās (each = 1 Satya Yuga = 0.4 mahāyuga) = 994 + 6 = **1000 mahāyugas**. The 15 sandhyās bracket the 14 manvantaras (one before each, one after the last). (Viṣṇu Purāṇa I.3)
- **Day + Night** — Brahmā's Night (*Brahmā-rātri*) equals his Day: another 4.32 billion years. One full *ahorātra* of Brahmā = **8.64 billion years**. 360 such days = 1 Brahmā-year; 100 Brahmā-years = his lifespan = **311.04 trillion years** (a *Mahā-kalpa* / *parārdha*-pair). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.11; FRAMEWORK CONSTANTS §3)
- **"Kalpa" as both Day and Night** — many texts use *kalpa* for the Day and *pralaya* (or *kalpa-anta*, "kalpa's end") for the Night; some treat a kalpa as the Day-plus-Night pair. Read each Purāṇa's usage in context. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.11; [Pralaya (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pralaya))
- **What runs inside one kalpa** — exactly 14 Manus reign in succession; we are in the 7th (Vaivasvata). At the close of the 14th Manu's reign the Day ends and the Night (naimittika pralaya) falls. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa I.3; see `./manvantara.md`)

## 2. Pralaya — The Four Types of Dissolution

The standard four-fold scheme (Viṣṇu Purāṇa VI; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 12.4): **Nitya, Naimittika, Prākṛtika, Ātyantika**. They are ordered by scale, from the moment-to-moment to the absolute.

- **Nitya Pralaya (constant / perpetual dissolution)** — the ceaseless, moment-by-moment dying of all beings; the continuous decay worked by Time (*Kāla*) on every body from Brahmā downward. Imperceptible like the slow drift of the planets. Nothing cosmic resets — it is the everyday death and flux of the manifest world. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa VI.3; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 12.4.38)
- **Naimittika Pralaya (occasional / incidental dissolution)** — the **Night of Brahmā**, falling at the end of each kalpa (every 4.32 billion years). Caused by *nimitta* (an "occasion" — Brahmā's sleep), hence "occasional." The three lower worlds burn and flood; the universe is withdrawn into Nārāyaṇa, who sleeps on Śeṣa until the next Day dawns and creation resumes. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa VI.3; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 12.4.4; [4 Types of Pralaya — yantrachants](https://yantrachants.com/4-types-of-pralaya-in-the-vishnu-purana/))
- **Prākṛtika Pralaya (elemental / primordial dissolution)** — the **end of Brahmā's entire life** (after 100 Brahmā-years ≈ 311.04 trillion years). Not just the worlds but the very *building blocks* dissolve: the whole Brahmāṇḍa (cosmic egg) collapses back into undifferentiated **Prakṛti** (primordial nature), in the exact reverse order of creation. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa VI.4; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 12.4.15–22)
- **Ātyantika Pralaya (absolute / final dissolution)** — **mokṣa**: the individual soul's permanent merger with Brahman/Nārāyaṇa through liberating knowledge. The ego (*ahaṃkāra*) is destroyed, saṃsāra ends for that being forever. The only "dissolution" that is personal, voluntary, and irreversible. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa VI.5; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 12.4.34–36)

## 3. What Dissolves & What Persists

### At Naimittika Pralaya (Night of Brahmā)
- **The fire-and-flood sequence** — at the kalpa's close there is no rain for 100 years; drought and famine; the sun's seven rays become **seven suns** (or the fire **Saṃvartaka**) that drink the oceans and scorch the three worlds (Bhūr, Bhuvar, Svar) to ash; then torrential clouds (*Saṃvartaka* clouds) flood everything into a single cosmic ocean. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa VI.3; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 12.4.6–13)
- **What dissolves** — Bhūloka (earth), Bhuvarloka (atmosphere), Svargaloka (heaven): the three lower worlds. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa VI.3)
- **What persists** — the four higher worlds **Maharloka, Janaloka, Tapoloka, Satyaloka** survive (the sages and liberated beings retreat upward to Janaloka). Prakṛti persists; matter is not annihilated, only beings cannot be sustained in the manifest regions. Nārāyaṇa persists, sleeping on **Śeṣa** — whose name means both "the end" and "the remainder / what is left over" after the naimittika pralaya. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 12.4.4; [Pralaya (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pralaya))
  - *Variant (Śiva Purāṇa):* the **lower ten realms** are destroyed in naimittika pralaya, while the **higher four** (Satya, Tapas, Jana, Mahar) are preserved — a slightly different count of where the dividing line falls. (Śiva Purāṇa; [Pralaya (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pralaya))

### At Prākṛtika Pralaya (end of Brahmā's life)
- **Reverse-order elemental collapse** — each element loses its defining quality and merges into its cause: **earth** loses fragrance → merges into **water**; water loses fluidity → into **fire**; fire loses form/colour → into **air (vāyu)**; air loses touch → into **ether (ākāśa)**; ether (sound) → into **ahaṃkāra (ego)**; the senses → into ahaṃkāra; ahaṃkāra → into **mahat (cosmic intellect)**; mahat and the three guṇas → into **Prakṛti**. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa VI.4; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 12.4.15–22)
- **What dissolves** — everything manifest, including Brahmā himself, the higher worlds, and the seven causal principles (mahat, ahaṃkāra, five tanmātras). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 12.4)
- **What persists** — **Prakṛti** reabsorbed into, and resting in, **Puruṣa / Brahman**; the Supreme alone remains, unmanifest, until a new Brahmā and a new cosmic Day arise. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa VI.4)

### At Ātyantika Pralaya
- **What dissolves** — individual ego/ignorance for the liberated soul only. **What persists** — pure consciousness, identity with Brahman; no return to saṃsāra. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa VI.5)

## 4. Naming of Notable Kalpas

- **Current kalpa: Śveta-Vārāha Kalpa ("White Boar")** — the **1st day of Brahmā's 51st year**; named for Viṣṇu's **Varāha (boar)** avatāra who lifted the earth at this kalpa's dawn. We are presently in it. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa I.4; [Śveta-Vārāha Kalpa — Wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/shveta-varaha-kalpa); FRAMEWORK CONSTANTS §4)
- **Previous kalpa: Pādma Kalpa ("Lotus")** — the **last day of Brahmā's 50th year**; named for the lotus that sprang from Viṣṇu's navel bearing Brahmā. The Padma Purāṇa narrates it. (Padma Purāṇa; [Hindu Cosmology — shivohampath](https://www.shivohampath.com/post/hindu-cosmology-explained-yugas-kalpas-manvantaras-the-cyclical-universe))
- **The 30 kalpas of a Month of Brahmā** — the Śiva Purāṇa (2.4.13) enumerates 30 named kalpas constituting one month of Brahmā. Recorded names include: (1) Śveta, (2) Nīlalohita, (3) Vāmadeva, (4) Rathantara, (5) Raurava, (6) Deva, (7) Bṛhat, (8) Kandarpa, (9) Sadya, (10) Īśāna, (11) Tamaḥ, (12) Sārasvata, (13) Udāna, (14) Gāruḍa, (15) Kaurma, (16) Nārasiṃha, (17) Samāna, … (remaining names vary by recension). (Śiva Purāṇa 2.4.13; [Śveta-Vārāha Kalpa — Wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/shveta-varaha-kalpa))
  - *Variant (naming conventions):* kalpas are named in different texts after the dominant avatāra, deity, element, or first creation of that Day — hence "Vārāha" (boar), "Pādma" (lotus), "Brāhma," "Kaurma" (tortoise), "Nārasiṃha" (man-lion). Lists and orderings differ across Purāṇas; treat any single sequence as one recension, not the canon. (Various Purāṇas; [Pralaya (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pralaya))

## 5. Cross-Tradition Variants & Disputed Points

- **Naimittika vs. *Brāhma* pralaya naming** — some texts call the Night-of-Brahmā dissolution *Brāhma pralaya* (because it is timed to Brahmā's sleep) reserving *naimittika* as a synonym; the substance is identical. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 12.4)
- **Three-fold vs. four-fold schemes** — a few sources collapse Nitya into ordinary flux and count only three "cosmic" pralayas (naimittika, prākṛtika, ātyantika); the four-fold list (Viṣṇu Purāṇa) is the standard catalog. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa VI; [THE CONCEPT OF PRALAYA IN THE VIṢṆU PURĀṆA (IJCRT)](https://ijcrt.org/papers/IJCRT2011209.pdf))
- **Boundary of destruction (Śiva vs. Bhāgavata)** — see §3: Bhāgavata draws the survival line at Maharloka (three worlds burn, four survive); Śiva Purāṇa frames it as ten lower realms destroyed, four upper preserved. [disputed count]
- **Mārkaṇḍeya's witness** — the sage **Mārkaṇḍeya**, granted vision of the pralaya, sees the cosmic flood and the infant Kṛṣṇa/Nārāyaṇa floating on a banyan leaf — the narrative anchor for "what persists." [folk/devotional framing] (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 12.8–10; Mahābhārata, Vana Parva)
