# The Kālachakra — the Wheel of Time & Time as Deity

> Where this sits: **[00-time-and-cosmology](./README.md)** ([master index](../README.md)) — the **synthesis node** for the whole section. The other files in this folder each describe *one rung* of the time-ladder; this file shows how every rung is a single revolving **wheel (cakra)**, and how that wheel is itself a divine principle — **Kāla**, time-as-God. Read this *after* [units-of-time](./units-of-time.md), [yugas-and-mahayuga](./yugas-and-mahayuga.md), [kalpa-and-pralaya](./kalpa-and-pralaya.md), [brahma-lifetime](./brahma-lifetime.md), [manvantara-system](./manvantara-system.md) and [jyotisha-and-the-zodiac](./jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md): it stitches them together.

Hindu time is not a line but a **wheel**, and the wheel is not a thing but a **deity**. Two ideas converge here. (1) *Mechanism*: every duration — from the **truti** (~29.6 µs) to the **para** (Brahmā's full 311.04-trillion-year life) — is one turning of a smaller or larger cog in a single nested machine, the **kālachakra**, "wheel of time." (2) *Person*: that wheel is a face of the Supreme — **Kāla**, Time itself, named as a primary *form/potency of Viṣṇu*, wielded as his disc-weapon (**Sudarśana / Kālachakra**), and worshipped in Śaiva idiom as **Mahākāla**, the Great Time that devours even the gods. The Gītā's `kālo'smi` is the hinge between the two: the cosmic person *is* the cosmic clock.

This file is deliberately a **cross-reference / synthesis node** — it re-states figures established elsewhere only as needed and points back to the dedicated files for the arithmetic.

## 1. The wheel as one nested mechanism (truti → para)

- **The single turning wheel** — all Hindu durations are concentric rotations of one device; finishing one revolution *is* beginning the next, so there is no "edge" to time, only larger gears (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.11; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.3). The metaphor is explicit: the recurring ages "turn like a wheel" (Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva).
- **The cogs, smallest to largest** — each is a fixed multiple of the one below; the full arithmetic lives in the linked files:
  - **truti → ... → ahorātra (day)** — the atomic-to-daily chain; base instant ≈ 29.6 µs (see [units-of-time](./units-of-time.md), §1).
  - **ahorātra → māsa → varṣa (human year)** — the everyday calendar wheel (see [hindu-calendar-and-panchanga](./hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md)).
  - **deva-varṣa** — the master conversion: **1 divine year = 360 human years**, the ratio from which every higher figure descends (Bhāgavata 3.11; Sūrya-Siddhānta 1.13–14).
  - **yuga → mahāyuga (caturyuga)** — 4:3:2:1, summing to 12,000 deva-yr = **4,320,000 human yr** (see [yugas-and-mahayuga](./yugas-and-mahayuga.md)).
  - **manvantara** — 71 mahāyugas + sandhyā ≈ 306.72 M human yr; 14 per kalpa (see [manvantara-system](./manvantara-system.md)).
  - **kalpa** — 1000 mahāyugas = 1 Day of Brahmā = **4.32 billion human yr**, closed by a *pralaya* (see [kalpa-and-pralaya](./kalpa-and-pralaya.md)).
  - **Brahmā's life (para / mahākalpa)** — 100 Brahmā-years = **311.04 trillion human yr**, then a *mahā-pralaya* (see [brahma-lifetime](./brahma-lifetime.md)).
  - **para → the next Brahmā** — even the demiurge is consumed and re-emitted; only Kāla/Viṣṇu persists across the turn (Bhāgavata 3.11.38–42).
- **Why "wheel" and not "ladder"** — the units are usually *recited* as a ladder (multiplication), but they *behave* as a wheel: each level **resets and repeats**, and the repetition is what makes the whole eternal. The ladder is the gearing; the wheel is the motion.
- **Time as the cosmos's *measure*, not a separate stuff** — in Bhāgavata 3.11 Maitreya tells Vidura that time is known only through the *movement of bodies* (the Sun is the great time-keeper); time has no perceptible atom of its own — it is inferred from the trasareṇu (mote) crossing a sunbeam (Bhāgavata 3.11.1–5). Thus the wheel is literally read off the sky → see [jyotisha-and-the-zodiac](./jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md).

## 2. Kāla as cosmic principle and impersonal devourer

- **Kāla = "time" and "death"** — the same Sanskrit word carries both; to be *in* time is to be mortal, so Time is the universal destroyer by definition (Kāla is a standing epithet of **Yama**, lord of death) (Atharvaveda 19.53–54; lexical tradition).
- **Atharvaveda Kāla-sūkta (19.53–54)** — the oldest hymn personifying Time as a *first principle*: Kāla is a horse with seven reins / a thousand eyes, who "drew forth" the worlds, the waters, even Prajāpati; "Kāla created yonder heaven, Kāla created these earths." Here Time is nearly the creator-godhead itself — earlier than its later subordination under Viṣṇu/Śiva. [late-Vedic]
- **Kāla as a primary *form* of Viṣṇu** — the Viṣṇu Purāṇa names **four primary forms** of the Supreme: *Pradhāna* (unmanifest matter), *Puruṣa* (spirit), *Vyakta* (manifest substance), and **Kāla** (time) — time is the *agitator* that disturbs the equilibrium of the other three and starts a cycle of creation (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.2).
- **Kāla as Viṣṇu's impelling potency** — in the Bhāgavata, Time is "the controlling form of the Supreme," the unseen power by which the Lord stirs *prakṛti* into creation and by which he later withdraws it; God pervades the world internally as the Indwelling Self (*antaryāmin*) and externally **as Time** (Bhāgavata 3.11; 3.26).
- **The impersonal devourer of the epics** — across the Mahābhārata, Rāmāyaṇa and Bhāgavata, *kāla* recurs as an impersonal, irresistible force: "Time cooks all beings," "Time is the cause," "it is Time that ripens and Time that destroys"; kings and heroes fall not to enemies but to Time (Mahābhārata, esp. Śānti & Strī Parvas).
- **Kāla and asymmetry** — note that the *wheel* is symmetric (it turns forever) but each *swallowing* (pralaya) is a real annihilation of forms; see §7 for this tension between eternal recurrence and the finality of dissolution.

## 3. Kāla as weapon: the Sudarśana / the time-disc of Viṣṇu

- **Sudarśana Chakra = Kāla-chakra** — Viṣṇu's spinning discus is explicitly glossed as the **"wheel of time"**: a flaming, 108-/many-spoked disc whose rotation *is* the turning of the ages; its alias in the texts is **Kāla-cakra** (Mahābhārata; Bhāgavata; Viṣṇu Purāṇa). The god holds Time in his hand as a weapon.
- **Solar / seasonal reading** — the disc's spokes are read as the divisions of the year (ṛtus, months, the 12 ādityas), tying the hand-weapon directly to the saṃvatsara-wheel of §5 and the zodiac of §6 (Purāṇic iconographic tradition).
- **Function** — as a weapon it beheads (e.g. Rāhu, the demon who stole amṛta), and as Time it "beheads" every form at its appointed moment; the two senses are one. The disc is thus the **personal-agency face** of the otherwise impersonal kāla of §2.
  - *Cross-link:* the Rāhu/Ketu episode and the eclipse-nodes are detailed in [nakshatras](./nakshatras.md) and [jyotisha-and-the-zodiac](./jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md).

## 4. Mahākāla — Śiva as the Great Time that devours time

- **Mahākāla = "Great Time / Great Death"** — a Śaiva name for the Supreme as the devourer who outlasts even Kāla; the *bahuvrīhi* mahā + kāla is read both as "great time" and as "beyond time," because Śiva consumes Time itself at the *mahā-pralaya* (Śaiva Purāṇas; lexical tradition).
- **Kāla as a *form of Śiva*** — in Śaiva idiom Time manifests as **Kāla-Bhairava** and as **Kālāgni-Rudra** (the "fire of time," the cosmic conflagration that ends a kalpa); both are the destroying-Time aspect of Śiva (Śiva Purāṇa; Liṅga Purāṇa).
- **Śiva who slays Kāla/Yama** — the **Kālāntaka / Mārkaṇḍeya** myth: when Yama (Kāla) comes for the boy-devotee Mārkaṇḍeya, Śiva bursts from the liṅga and kills Death itself, earning the name *Kālāntaka* ("ender of Time/Death"). Here the deity is *above* the wheel rather than identical with it. (Śiva Purāṇa; Mārkaṇḍeya tradition).
- **Mahākāleśvara** — the Ujjain *jyotirliṅga* enshrines this Mahākāla; Ujjain was also the prime meridian (zero-longitude) of classical Indian astronomy, neatly fusing the **god of Time** with the **reference point for measuring time** (temple + Sūrya-Siddhānta tradition). [folk/devotional fusion]
- **Sectarian variant** — Vaiṣṇava texts make **Kāla a potency of Viṣṇu** (§2–3); Śaiva texts make **Time a face of Śiva / something Śiva transcends** (this section). Both agree Time is divine; they differ on *which* deity owns or supersedes it. [disputed — sectarian]

## 5. The saṃvatsara-cakra — the 60-year Jovian wheel

- **What it is** — a named **60-year cycle** of years (*saṃvatsaras*), a human-scale "small kālachakra" used liturgically and astrologically; each year has a proper name and a presiding quality, and after the 60th the wheel repeats (Sūrya-Siddhānta; Jyotiṣa tradition).
- **The Jovian basis** — a *saṃvatsara* is a **Jovian year**: the time **Bṛhaspati (Jupiter)** takes to transit one rāśi by mean motion (~1 year), so **5 orbits of Jupiter (5 × 12 = 60)** complete the cycle; equivalently it tracks the **Jupiter–Saturn return** (~12 and ~30 yr periods reconciling near 60 yr) (Sūrya-Siddhānta; Samvatsara tradition).
- **The 60 names** — running **Prabhava → ... → Kṣaya** (Akṣaya), then repeating; the current run began with Prabhava in **1987 CE** and closes ~**2046 CE** (2026 sits within it).
- **Division among the Trimūrti** — the 60 are split into three groups of 20: **Prabhava→Vyaya → Brahmā**; **Sarvajit→Parābhava → Viṣṇu**; the final 20 → **Śiva** — making the human 60-year wheel a miniature of the cosmic creation–maintenance–dissolution cycle (Jyotiṣa/Purāṇic tradition).
  - *Variant (naming/era):* a parallel **Bṛhaspati-cakra** numbering can fall ~12 years out of step with the southern (Telugu/Kannada) list because of how dropped (*kṣaya*) Jovian years are handled; rosters and start-years differ by region. [disputed — regional]
- **Cross-link:** the saṃvatsara is the top of the everyday calendar wheel — see [hindu-calendar-and-panchanga](./hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md) for how it nests with the lunar month and tithi.

## 6. The wheel and the sky — zodiac, precession, the cosmic clock-face

- **The zodiac as the visible dial** — because time is read from celestial motion (§1), the **rāśi-cakra** (12-sign zodiac) and the **nakṣatra** belt are literally the *face of the kālachakra*; the Sun's circuit marks the year, the Moon's the month (see [jyotisha-and-the-zodiac](./jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md) and [nakshatras](./nakshatras.md)).
- **Sūrya-Siddhānta precession** — the text models the equinoxes' shift; it gives a rate near **54″/year** and (distinctively) describes a **libration / trepidation** — a to-and-fro *oscillation* of the equinox rather than the modern uniform precession (Sūrya-Siddhānta 3).
  - *Variant (libration figures):* the asterism-circle is said to swing **27° east then 27° west** (a 108° sweep), librating **600 times per mahāyuga** — i.e. precession is itself periodic, *another wheel within the wheel*. [scholarly — disputed reconstruction]
- **Ayanāṃśa** — the gap between tropical and sidereal zodiacs accumulated by precession; its size is the running "odometer" of this slow wheel and underlies the sidereal basis of Indian astrology (Jyotiṣa tradition; see [jyotisha-and-the-zodiac](./jyotisha-and-the-zodiac.md)).
- **Nested rotation, all the way up** — day (Earth's spin) ⊂ month (Moon) ⊂ year (Sun) ⊂ 12-yr & 60-yr (Jupiter/Saturn) ⊂ precession (~25,800-yr modern / oscillatory in S-S) ⊂ yuga/mahāyuga: every astronomical period is one more concentric cog of the same kālachakra.

## 7. The Gītā theophany — `kālo'smi`, where wheel and deity become one

- **The verse (Bhagavad-Gītā 11.32)** — at the climax of the **Viśvarūpa** (universal-form) vision, Kṛṣṇa declares **"kālo'smi lokakṣayakṛt pravṛddho"** — *"I am Time, the mighty world-destroyer, here come to annihilate the worlds."* All the warriors are *already* slain by Time; Arjuna is only its instrument (BG 11.32–33).
- **Why it is the synthesis** — this single line fuses the two halves of this file: the **impersonal wheel** of §1–2 and the **personal deity** of §3–4 are revealed as *the same reality* — the cosmic person *is* the destroying clock. Creation, preservation and destruction are all "the whole process" shown in one body (BG 11; Rāmānuja & Śaṅkara commentaries).
- **Theological reading** — death is not an interruption of the divine plan but *part of God*; mortality is universal and human agency operates inside Time's constraint (commentarial consensus; Madhva, Rāmānuja, Śaṅkara on BG 11.32).
- **Reception note** — the verse is the one J. Robert Oppenheimer recalled at the 1945 Trinity test ("Now I am become Death/Time, the destroyer of worlds"), the best-known modern citation of the kāla theophany. [modern reception]

## 8. Eternal recurrence vs. the asymmetry of pralaya

- **The recurrence (symmetry)** — the wheel turns *forever*; Kali is followed by a new Satya, every kalpa is followed by another, even Brahmā's death is followed by a new Brahmā. In the strong reading, the *same* events recur each cycle (the same Manus, the same ṛṣis, the same broad pattern) — a Hindu "eternal return" (Mahābhārata Śānti Parva; Purāṇic manvantara-lists, see [manvantara-system](./manvantara-system.md)).
- **The asymmetry (decline + real loss)** — yet *within* a cycle time is **not** symmetric: dharma declines monotonically Satya→Kali (4:3:2:1), and each *pralaya* is a genuine annihilation of forms, not a mere phase. The arrow of decay runs one way even though the wheel runs in a circle (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.3; Bhāgavata 12.2–3 on the Kali decline).
- **How the two reconcile** — the standard resolution: the **wheel is eternal and symmetric at the macro scale**, but **each turn is directional (decline) at the meso scale**, and **only the seed/persisting principle (Kāla–Viṣṇu, or the liberated soul) escapes the wheel entirely** at *mokṣa*. The point of the whole cosmology is thus to *step off* the kālachakra, not to ride it (Gītā 8; Bhāgavata 3.11.38–42).
  - *Variant (degree of recurrence):* texts differ on whether each kalpa is *identical* (strong recurrence) or merely *similar in pattern* (weak recurrence); the manvantara rosters are given as fixed for *this* kalpa but the next kalpa's names can differ. [disputed] See open-roster flags in [manvantara-system](./manvantara-system.md).
