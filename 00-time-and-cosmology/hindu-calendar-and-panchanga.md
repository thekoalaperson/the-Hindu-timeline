# The Hindu Calendar & the Pañcāṅga

> Hierarchy location: `00-time-and-cosmology/` — the "civil clock" companion to [`units-of-time.md`](./units-of-time.md). Where Units-of-Time builds the cosmic ladder (truti → kalpa), this file builds the **human-scale, observational calendar**: how a day, a month, a year and an era are actually reckoned, named and used to fix ritual dates. Up-link: [section README](./README.md). The luni-solar machinery here is what every festival, vrata, śrāddha and muhūrta in the later sections silently depends on.

The Hindu calendar is **luni-solar**: months are tied to the Moon's phases, the year is anchored to the Sun's path, and the two are forcibly reconciled by **intercalation** (adhika-māsa). Its daily expression is the **pañcāṅga** ("five limbs") — a five-fold almanac read each morning to know the lunar day, the weekday, the Moon's star, and two derived luni-solar angles. The system is **regionally plural**: the same date can carry different month-names, era-numbers and month-boundaries (amānta vs pūrṇimānta) across India. This file catalogs the limbs, the months, the seasons, the ayanas, the eras, the 60-year cycle, and the recipe by which festival dates fall out.

---

## 1. The Pañcāṅga — the five limbs (pañca-aṅga)

The pañcāṅga is the daily almanac. Its name is literally **pañca (five) + aṅga (limb)**. Each limb is an astronomical quantity computed for a given place and instant (usually local sunrise); the five together "stamp" the day. Four of the five derive from the **relative longitudes of Sun and Moon**; only the weekday is independent. (Source: [Panchangam — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchangam); [Britannica — Hindu calendar](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Hindu-calendar).)

- **Tithi** (lunar day) — the limb that *defines the Hindu date*. One tithi = the time for the **Moon–Sun elongation to increase by 12°**; since the synodic month is 360° of elongation, there are **30 tithis per lunar month**. Tithis are unequal in clock-length (≈19–26 hr) because the Moon's speed varies. Numbered 1–15 within each fortnight (paksha). The tithi "current at sunrise" governs the civil date; this causes **tithi-vṛddhi** (a tithi spanning two sunrises, repeated) and **tithi-kṣaya** (a tithi skipped, never touching a sunrise). The 30 tithis: Pratipadā (1), Dvitīyā (2), Tṛtīyā (3), Caturthī (4), Pañcamī (5), Ṣaṣṭhī (6), Saptamī (7), Aṣṭamī (8), Navamī (9), Daśamī (10), Ekādaśī (11), Dvādaśī (12), Trayodaśī (13), Caturdaśī (14), and the fifteenth — **Pūrṇimā** (full moon) closing Śukla pakṣa, **Amāvāsyā** (new moon) closing Kṛṣṇa pakṣa. (Source: [Panchangam — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchangam); [DharmaRenaissance — Five Limbs](https://blog.dharma-renaissance.org/spiritual-insight/master-the-panchanga-a-complete-proven-guide-to-hindu-timekeepings-five-limbs/).)
  - *Special tithi names:* **Pūrṇimā** = full moon (Moon–Sun elongation 180°→ end), **Amāvāsyā** = new moon (elongation reset to 0°); Caturthī, Ekādaśī, Trayodaśī, Caturdaśī carry the heaviest vrata-load (e.g. Ekādaśī fasting).

- **Vāra** (weekday) — the **seven-day week**, the one limb independent of Sun–Moon angle (it is a fixed planetary cycle). Each day is ruled by a graha (see [`02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/) for the Navagraha). The seven: **Ravivāra/Bhānuvāra** (Sun, Sunday), **Somavāra/Induvāra** (Moon, Monday), **Maṅgalavāra/Bhaumavāra** (Mars, Tuesday), **Budhavāra** (Mercury, Wednesday), **Guruvāra/Bṛhaspativāra** (Jupiter, Thursday), **Śukravāra/Bhṛguvāra** (Venus, Friday), **Śanivāra/Sthiravāra** (Saturn, Saturday). The Hindu vāra runs **sunrise-to-sunrise**, not midnight-to-midnight. (Source: [Panchangam — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchangam).)

- **Nakṣatra** (lunar mansion) — the asterism the **Moon occupies** at the reckoning instant. The ecliptic is divided into **27 nakṣatras of 13°20′ each** (sometimes 28, inserting Abhijit). The Moon traverses one nakṣatra in ≈ one day, completing 27 in a sidereal month (≈27.3 days). Foundational to **muhūrta** (electional timing), birth-star (janma-nakṣatra) and many festivals (e.g. Onam = Śravaṇa/Thiruvonam; Kṛṣṇa Janmāṣṭamī links Rohiṇī). Full roster of the 27 (with Abhijit as a 28th [disputed]) is catalogued in this section's deep-dives; the count itself is the calendrical point here. (Source: [Panchangam — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchangam); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Hindu-calendar).)
  - *Variant (27 vs 28):* The standard pañcāṅga uses **27** equal nakṣatras; the **28th, Abhijit** (overlapping Uttarāṣāḍha–Śravaṇa), is retained in some muhūrta and older Vedic listings [disputed]/[late text].

- **Yoga** (luni-solar sum) — a derived angle: the **sum of the longitudes of Sun and Moon**, divided into **27 equal parts of 13°20′**. (Note: tithi uses the *difference* of longitudes; yoga uses the *sum*.) The 27 yogas run **Viṣkambha, Prīti, Āyuṣmān, Saubhāgya, Śobhana, Atigaṇḍa, Sukarman, Dhṛti, Śūla, Gaṇḍa, Vṛddhi, Dhruva, Vyāghāta, Harṣaṇa, Vajra, Siddhi, Vyatīpāta, Varīyān, Parigha, Śiva, Siddha, Sādhya, Śubha, Śukla, Brahma, Aindra (Māhendra), Vaidhṛti.** Certain yogas (e.g. **Vyatīpāta, Vaidhṛti**) are inauspicious; **Amṛta-siddhi** and similar nakṣatra+vāra combinations are sought for auspicious work. (Source: [Panchangam — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchangam); [DharmaRenaissance](https://blog.dharma-renaissance.org/spiritual-insight/master-the-panchanga-a-complete-proven-guide-to-hindu-timekeepings-five-limbs/).)

- **Karaṇa** (half-tithi) — **half of a tithi**: the time for the Moon–Sun elongation to grow by **6°**. There are **2 karaṇas per tithi → 60 karaṇas per lunar month**. They cycle through **11 names**: 7 "movable" (cara) repeating 8 times, and 4 "fixed" (sthira) occurring once each per month. The **4 fixed**: **Śakuni, Catuṣpada, Nāga, Kiṃstughna** (clustered around amāvāsyā). The **7 movable**: **Bava, Bālava, Kaulava, Taitila, Gara (Garaja), Vaṇija, Viṣṭi (Bhadrā)** — of which **Viṣṭi/Bhadrā is strongly inauspicious** and work is avoided during it. (Source: [Panchangam — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchangam); [DharmaRenaissance](https://blog.dharma-renaissance.org/spiritual-insight/master-the-panchanga-a-complete-proven-guide-to-hindu-timekeepings-five-limbs/).)

> **Reading the five together:** A pañcāṅga line such as *"Śukla Pratipadā, Somavāra, Aśvinī nakṣatra, Viṣkambha yoga, Bava karaṇa"* fully specifies the ritual character of the day and is the basis for choosing a **muhūrta** (auspicious window).

## 2. The lunar month, the paksha, and amānta vs pūrṇimānta

- **Synodic (lunar) month — māsa** = one full cycle of Moon phases, **≈29.53 days**, divided into **30 tithis**. Twelve such months ≈ **354 days** — about **11 days short** of the solar year, the deficit that forces intercalation (§4). (Source: [Hindu calendar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_calendar).)
- **Paksha** (fortnight, ≈15 tithis) — each māsa splits into two:
  - **Śukla pakṣa** — the **bright/waxing** half, ending in **Pūrṇimā** (full moon).
  - **Kṛṣṇa pakṣa** — the **dark/waning** half, ending in **Amāvāsyā** (new moon).
- **Where the month begins — two reckonings:**
  - **Amānta** ("ending in amāvāsyā") — month runs **new moon → new moon**. Dominant in **South India, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Karnataka, Andhra, Tamil Nadu, Kerala** (the southern/western bloc). (Source: [ShriAstroTime — Amanta vs Purnimanta](https://shriastrotime.com/blogs/amanta-vs-purnimanta-understanding-the-two-hindu-lunar-month-systems-in-india).)
  - **Pūrṇimānta** ("ending in pūrṇimā") — month runs **full moon → full moon** (i.e. day after pūrṇimā to next pūrṇimā). Dominant in **North India** (Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, the Hindi belt). (Source: [ShriAstroTime](https://shriastrotime.com/blogs/amanta-vs-purnimanta-understanding-the-two-hindu-lunar-month-systems-in-india); [Hindu calendar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_calendar).)
  - *Consequence (festival drift):* The two systems **agree on the śukla-pakṣa half** of any month but **disagree on the kṛṣṇa-pakṣa half by one month-name**. The kṛṣṇa-pakṣa that is, e.g., *Kṛṣṇa Caitra* in the pūrṇimānta north is *Kṛṣṇa Phālguna* in the amānta south. This is why a festival like **Kṛṣṇa Janmāṣṭamī** is "Bhādrapada Kṛṣṇa Aṣṭamī" in one scheme and "Śrāvaṇa Kṛṣṇa Aṣṭamī" in the other — same *day*, different *month-label*. (Source: [ShriAstroTime](https://shriastrotime.com/blogs/amanta-vs-purnimanta-understanding-the-two-hindu-lunar-month-systems-in-india).)

## 3. The twelve months, the six ṛtus, and the two ayanas

### 3a. The 12 lunar months (māsa)
Each lunar month is **named for the nakṣatra in which the full moon falls**. (Source: [Hindu calendar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_calendar); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Hindu-calendar).)

- **Caitra** — ≈ Mar–Apr; first month of the lunar year; **Caitra Śukla Pratipadā = New Year** (Gudi Padwa / Ugadi / Chaitra Navaratri begins). Ṛtu: Vasanta.
- **Vaiśākha** — ≈ Apr–May; Akṣaya Tṛtīyā, Buddha Pūrṇimā. Ṛtu: Vasanta.
- **Jyeṣṭha** — ≈ May–Jun; Vat Savitri, Ganga Dussehra. Ṛtu: Grīṣma.
- **Āṣāḍha** — ≈ Jun–Jul; Ratha Yātrā, Guru Pūrṇimā, Devaśayanī Ekādaśī (start of Cāturmāsya). Ṛtu: Grīṣma.
- **Śrāvaṇa** — ≈ Jul–Aug; Raksha Bandhan, Nāga Pañcamī, the Śrāvaṇa Mondays (Śaiva). Ṛtu: Varṣā.
- **Bhādrapada** (Bhādra/Proṣṭhapada) — ≈ Aug–Sep; Gaṇeśa Caturthī, Janmāṣṭamī (amānta), Pitṛ-pakṣa (śrāddha fortnight). Ṛtu: Varṣā.
- **Āśvina** (Aśvin) — ≈ Sep–Oct; Śāradīya Navarātri, Durgā Pūjā, Vijayadaśamī (Dussehra). Ṛtu: Śarad.
- **Kārttika** — ≈ Oct–Nov; Dīpāvalī, Govardhana, Bhai Dooj, Kārttika Pūrṇimā, Devotthāna (end of Cāturmāsya). Ṛtu: Śarad.
- **Mārgaśīrṣa** (Agrahāyaṇa) — ≈ Nov–Dec; Gītā Jayantī, Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī. Ṛtu: Hemanta.
- **Pauṣa** — ≈ Dec–Jan; often "blank" of festivals (in some traditions inauspicious for weddings); Makar Sankranti falls at its end (solar). Ṛtu: Hemanta.
- **Māgha** — ≈ Jan–Feb; Vasanta Pañcamī, Māgha Mela / Kumbh bathing, Mahā Śivarātri (Māgha/Phālguna kṛṣṇa). Ṛtu: Śiśira.
- **Phālguna** — ≈ Feb–Mar; Holī (Phālguna Pūrṇimā), Holikā Dahana; last month of the lunar year. Ṛtu: Śiśira.

### 3b. The 6 ṛtus (seasons) — two months each
**Vasanta** (spring: Caitra–Vaiśākha), **Grīṣma** (summer: Jyeṣṭha–Āṣāḍha), **Varṣā** (monsoon: Śrāvaṇa–Bhādrapada), **Śarad** (autumn: Āśvina–Kārttika), **Hemanta** (pre-winter: Mārgaśīrṣa–Pauṣa), **Śiśira** (winter: Māgha–Phālguna). (Source: [Hindu calendar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_calendar).)

### 3c. The two ayanas (solstice-to-solstice half-years)
The solar year splits into two **ayanas** by the Sun's declination:
- **Uttarāyaṇa** — the Sun's **northward** course (winter→summer solstice), ≈ **Makara Saṅkrānti to Karka Saṅkrānti**. Considered auspicious; the *devayāna* ("path of the gods"); Bhīṣma famously waited for it to die. (Source: [Hindu calendar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_calendar).)
- **Dakṣiṇāyana** — the Sun's **southward** course (summer→winter solstice). The *pitṛyāna* ("path of the ancestors"); Cāturmāsya and Pitṛ-pakṣa fall here.
  - *Variant (sidereal drift):* Because most Hindu calendars are **sidereal (nirayana)**, Makar Sankranti now falls ≈ **14 January**, no longer at the actual tropical solstice (≈21 Dec) — a ~23–24° precessional gap (the **ayanāṃśa**). The *tropical* solstice and the *calendrical* uttarāyaṇa have drifted apart by ~3 weeks. [scholarly]

### 3d. The 12 solar months (rāśi / saṅkrānti)
Parallel to the lunar months, the **solar calendar** (used in Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Bengal, Assam, Odisha, Punjab) tracks the Sun through the **12 zodiacal signs (rāśi)**. The Sun's entry into a sign is a **saṅkrānti**; the time the Sun spends in one rāśi is a **solar month** (≈30–31 days). The 12 rāśis: **Meṣa, Vṛṣabha, Mithuna, Karka, Siṃha, Kanyā, Tulā, Vṛścika, Dhanus, Makara, Kumbha, Mīna.** Solar New Year (Meṣa Saṅkrānti, ≈14 Apr) = **Vaisakhi / Puthandu / Pohela Boishakh / Vishu / Bihu**. (Source: [Hindu calendar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_calendar); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Hindu-calendar).)

## 4. Adhika-māsa (intercalation) and kṣaya-māsa (suppression)

The lunar year (≈354 d) lags the solar year (≈365.25 d) by ≈11 days. Left uncorrected the months would drift through the seasons (as in the purely lunar Islamic calendar). The fix keeps the luni-solar calendar **season-locked**: (Source: [Adhika-masa — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adhika-masa); [PanchangBodh — Adhika Masa](https://panchangbodh.com/adhika-masa-guide).)

- **Adhika-māsa** ("extra month"; also **Mala-māsa, Puruṣottama-māsa, Adhik-mās**) — an **intercalary lunar month inserted ≈ every 32½ months (~every 2.7 years)**, so that ~7 such months occur in 19 years (a Metonic-like ratio). **Rule:** when a lunar month contains **no solar saṅkrānti** — i.e. **two new moons (amānta) fall within a single solar month**, with no Sun-sign-entry between them — that month is doubled. The **first** copy is the *adhika* (intercalary) month; the **second** is the *nija/śuddha* (true) month. Both carry the same name (e.g. "Adhika Śrāvaṇa" then "Nija Śrāvaṇa"). Adhika-māsa is held **devotionally special** (dedicated to Viṣṇu as Puruṣottama → "Puruṣottama-māsa") but is generally **avoided for auspicious saṃskāras** (weddings, upanayana). (Source: [Adhika-masa — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adhika-masa); [PanchangBodh](https://panchangbodh.com/adhika-masa-guide).)
- **Kṣaya-māsa** ("decayed / dropped month") — the **rare opposite**: when **two saṅkrāntis fall within a single lunar month** (the Sun crosses two sign-boundaries in one short lunar month, possible near the Sun's perigee when lunar months are shortest), that month-name is **suppressed/deleted**. Occurs only a few times per century; the **1982–83** kṣaya was recent, the **next ≈ Jan 2124**. A kṣaya is always **compensated by two adhika-māsas** in the same year, so the year still has 13 lunar months. (Source: [Hindu calendar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_calendar).)

## 5. Era reckonings (saṃvat) — counting the years

A Hindu year carries an **era number (saṃvat)** counting from an epoch. Multiple eras run in parallel across regions; the same Gregorian year maps to different saṃvat numbers. (Source: [Samvatsara — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samvatsara); [Hindu calendar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_calendar).)

- **Vikrama Saṃvat (Vikrami era)** — epoch **57/58 BCE**, attributed to the legendary **Vikramāditya of Ujjain** (after victory over the Śakas). Dominant in **North & West India, Nepal** (Nepal's official era). Conversion: **CE + 57 ≈ Vikrama** (in the early-year months). Begins (in most northern usage) on **Caitra Śukla Pratipadā**; the Gujarati Vikrama year begins on **Kārttika Śukla** (Diwali). (Source: [Samvatsara — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samvatsara); [GeoTimeDate](https://geotimedate.org/articles/panchang/hindu-year-or-samvatsara).)
- **Śaka Saṃvat (Śālivāhana Śaka era)** — epoch **78 CE**, traditionally tied to **Gautamīputra Sātakarṇi / Śālivāhana**. Conversion: **CE − 78 ≈ Śaka**. Dominant in the **Deccan & South**; adopted as the **Indian National Calendar (1957)**, whose civil year begins at **Caitra 1 ≈ 22 March** (21 Mar in leap years). (Source: [Samvatsara — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samvatsara); [Hindu calendar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_calendar).)
- **Kali Yuga era (Kali-saṃvat / Kaliyugābda)** — epoch **3102 BCE** (traditionally **17/18 February 3102 BCE**, the death of Kṛṣṇa / onset of Kali Yuga; see [`yugas-and-mahayuga.md`](./yugas-and-mahayuga.md)). Conversion: **CE + 3101 ≈ Kali year**. Used in **pañcāṅgas, horoscopes and astronomical (siddhānta) reckoning** rather than civic life. This is the era that links the everyday calendar back to the cosmic yuga-clock. (Source: [Samvatsara — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samvatsara).)
- *Other regional eras [for completeness]:* **Kollam (Malayalam) era** (epoch 825 CE, Kerala); **Bengali San** (epoch 593 CE); **Saptarṣi/Laukika era** (Kashmir); **Gupta era** (319 CE); **Harṣa era** (606 CE) [scholarly]. Roster is **open-ended** — dozens of dynastic/regional eras existed. (Source: [Hindu calendar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_calendar).)

## 6. The 60-year Jovian cycle (ṣaṣṭi-saṃvatsara cakra)

Distinct from the era-count is the **named 60-year cycle**. Here a **saṃvatsara** = a "Jovian year," originally indexed to **Jupiter's position** (Jupiter ≈12-yr orbit, Saturn ≈30-yr orbit; their cycle's least-common-multiple ≈ **60 years**, after which both planets return near their starting sidereal positions). Each of the 60 years has a proper **name** (you "are" the year you were born in). The cycle's 60 names divide into three **vimśatikas (groups of 20)** assigned to **Brahmā (1–20), Viṣṇu (21–40), Śiva/Rudra (41–60)**. (Source: [Samvatsara — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samvatsara); [CircleOfDivineAstrology](https://circleofdivineastrology.com/samvatsara-the-jupiter-years-you-are-the-year-you-are-born-in/).)

**The 60 saṃvatsaras (standard order, beginning Prabhava):**

- **Brahma vimśatika (1–20):** 1 Prabhava, 2 Vibhava, 3 Śukla, 4 Pramoda(ūta), 5 Prajāpati, 6 Aṅgirasa, 7 Śrīmukha, 8 Bhāva, 9 Yuva(n), 10 Dhātṛ, 11 Īśvara, 12 Bahudhānya, 13 Pramāthī, 14 Vikrama, 15 Vṛṣa(pra), 16 Citrabhānu, 17 Subhānu (Svabhānu), 18 Tāraṇa (Dhāraṇa), 19 Pārthiva, 20 Vyaya.
- **Viṣṇu vimśatika (21–40):** 21 Sarvajit, 22 Sarvadhārī, 23 Virodhī, 24 Vikṛti, 25 Khara, 26 Nandana, 27 Vijaya, 28 Jaya, 29 Manmatha, 30 Durmukhī (Durmukha), 31 Hevilambī (Hemalambī), 32 Vilambī, 33 Vikārī, 34 Śārvarī (Śarvari), 35 Plava, 36 Śubhakṛt, 37 Śobhakṛt (Śobhana), 38 Krodhī, 39 Viśvāvasu, 40 Parābhava.
- **Rudra/Śiva vimśatika (41–60):** 41 Plavaṅga, 42 Kīlaka, 43 Saumya, 44 Sādhāraṇa, 45 Virodhakṛt, 46 Paridhāvī, 47 Pramādīca (Pramādī), 48 Ānanda, 49 Rākṣasa, 50 Nala (Anala), 51 Piṅgala, 52 Kālayukti (Kālayukta), 53 Siddhārthī, 54 Raudra (Raudrī), 55 Durmati, 56 Dundubhi, 57 Rudhirodgārī, 58 Raktākṣī (Raktākṣa), 59 Krodhana, 60 Akṣaya.

(Source: [HinduPad — 60 Year Names](https://hindupad.com/hindu-year-names-60-years-names-in-hinduism/); [Samvatsara — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samvatsara).)

- *Variant (cycle start — disputed):* The **siddhānta-jyotiṣa** tradition is said to begin the cycle from **Vijaya** (running to **Nandana**), whereas **Varāhamihira** begins from **Prabhava**, and "since Varāhamihira's time" the **Prabhava-start** has been the accepted standard [scholarly]. (Source: [Samvatsara — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samvatsara).)
- *Variant (North vs South application):* In **South India** the 60 names are applied **uniformly** to the lunar year. In **North India** the named-cycle usage is weaker / the **Jovian (Bārhaspatya) sixty-year** reckoning can drift by a *kṣaya* (a Jovian year occasionally dropped to keep pace), so the northern and southern current-year names can differ by one. [scholarly]
- *Spelling note:* Names vary across Sanskrit/Telugu/Tamil/Kannada usage (e.g. Pramoda ~ Pramoduta, Hevilambi ~ Hemalambi, Pramadica ~ Pramadi). Variants are normal pronunciation/transliteration differences, not different years.

## 7. How festival dates are computed

A festival is fixed not to a Gregorian date but to a **calendrical coordinate** — typically *(month, paksha, tithi)*, sometimes *(solar month, nakṣatra)*, occasionally *(saṅkrānti)*. The pañcāṅga then locates that coordinate each year. (Source: [Hindu calendar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_calendar); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Hindu-calendar).)

- **Tithi-based (most common)** — defined by *(māsa, pakṣa, tithi)*. E.g. **Mahā Śivarātri** = Phālguna (pūrṇimānta) / Māgha (amānta) Kṛṣṇa Caturdaśī; **Rāma Navamī** = Caitra Śukla Navamī; **Dīpāvalī** = Kārttika Amāvāsyā; **Holī** = Phālguna Pūrṇimā. The festival falls on the **day the prescribed tithi is "live" at the relevant moment** (sunrise for most; the specific *vyāpti* rule — e.g. tithi at sunrise, at moonrise, at midnight, or at aparāhṇa — is festival-specific and is the chief reason **pañcāṅgas disagree on a date by ±1 day** in vṛddhi/kṣaya years).
- **Nakṣatra-based** — fixed to *(solar/lunar month + nakṣatra)*. E.g. **Onam** = Śravaṇa (Thiruvonam) nakṣatra in Siṃha solar month; **Janmāṣṭamī** weighs Rohiṇī nakṣatra alongside Aṣṭamī tithi.
- **Saṅkrānti-based (solar)** — fixed to the **Sun's sign-entry**. E.g. **Makar Saṅkrānti** (Sun → Makara, ≈14 Jan), **Meṣa Saṅkrānti** New Year (≈14 Apr). These barely move year-to-year (sidereal solar dates) — the only Hindu festivals roughly Gregorian-stable.
- **Why dates "move" in Gregorian terms:** because the lunar year is ~11 days shorter, a tithi-fixed festival **regresses ~11 days each year**, then **jumps forward ~19 days** in an adhika-māsa year — keeping it inside its season. Two pañcāṅgas can also differ because of **(a)** amānta vs pūrṇimānta month-labelling, **(b)** the tithi-vyāpti rule chosen, **(c)** the **ayanāṃśa** value used (Lahiri / Raman / KP schools), and **(d)** the **reference location** (sunrise/sunset are place-dependent). The festival roster is **open-ended** and region-specific; the *computation method*, not a fixed list, is the invariant. [scholarly]
