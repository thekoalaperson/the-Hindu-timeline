# Tulsīdās — the Rāma-bhakta of Vārāṇasī

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](README.md) — the biographical home of **Gosvāmī Tulsīdās** (trad. 1532–1623 CE), the Awadhi/Braj poet-saint of Mughal-era Vārāṇasī who anchors the **saguṇa Rāma-bhakti** wing of the north-Indian movement mapped in [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md). His masterwork the *Rāmcaritmānas* keeps its own canonical text-home at [`../04-deep-dives/ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md`](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md); this file covers the **life, hagiography, full oeuvre and legacy**. Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md); master index [`../README.md`](../README.md).
> Two clocks: the *man* sits in **Kali-Yuga documented history** (16th–17th c. CE [scholarly]; cross-ref [`recorded-history.md`](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md)); his *subject* — Rāma — belongs to **Tretā Yuga** of the current 28th Mahāyuga ([`../04-deep-dives/ramayana/README.md`](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/README.md)). ## Who he was

- **Gosvāmī Tulsīdās (Tulasīdāsa)** — Vaiṣṇava poet-saint and renunciant of **Vārāṇasī (Kāśī)**, the supreme voice of **saguṇa Rāma-bhakti** in north India: devotion to **Rāma as saguṇa Parabrahman** — the attributeless absolute made graspable in the person of the prince of Ayodhyā. He stands to the Rāma-wing of the bhakti field as [Sūrdās](surdas.md) and [Mīrābāī](mirabai.md) stand to the Kṛṣṇa-wing, opposite the nirguṇa sants of [Kabīr's](kabir.md) line — the doctrinal map is drawn in [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Tulsidas*; Britannica, *Tulsidas*)
- **Languages** — he wrote in **two vernaculars, not Sanskrit**: **Awadhi** (the *Rāmcaritmānas*, *Janakī-maṅgala*, *Rāmalalā-nahachū*…) and **Braj-bhāṣā** (*Vinaya-patrikā*, *Kavitāvalī*, *Gītāvalī*, *Kṛṣṇa-gītāvalī*) — a deliberate bhakti-egalitarian choice that drew the hostility of Kāśī's Sanskrit establishment (below). (Source: Wikipedia, *Tulsidas*) [scholarly]
- **Vālmīki reborn [folk]** — hagiography remembers him as a **reincarnation of Vālmīki**, born again in Kali Yuga to sing Rāma's deeds in the people's tongue; the claim appears in Nābhādās's *Bhaktamāl* and is elaborated in later sectarian lives. (Source: *Bhaktamāl*; *Mūla Gosāīṃ Carita*) [folk]

## Dating, birth and sources — the two clocks [disputed]

- **Traditional dates** — **b. V.S. 1589 (1532 CE), d. V.S. 1680 (1623 CE)**; the death is fixed by the tradition-couplet *"saṃvat solaha sau asī, asī gaṅga ke tīra / śrāvaṇa śuklā saptamī, tulasī tajyo śarīra"* — "in Saṃvat 1680, at Asī on the Gaṅgā's bank, on Śrāvaṇa śukla 7, Tulsī left the body." (Source: Wikipedia, *Tulsidas*; tradition-couplet) [traditional]
  - *Variant [disputed]:* several older traditions put the birth at **V.S. 1554 (1497 CE)** or **1511 CE**, yielding an improbable 126-year life; most modern scholarship prefers **c. 1532** and treats the longer span as hagiographic rounding. [scholarly]
- **Birthplace [disputed]** — most commonly **Rājāpur** (Bāndā district, on the Yamunā, in present-day Uttar Pradesh); rival claims name **Sūkarakṣetra / Soron** (Kasganj) and other sites. Parents are given as **Ātmārām Dūbe** and **Hulsī**, a Sarayūpārīṇa Brāhmaṇa family. (Source: Wikipedia, *Tulsidas*) [disputed]
- **The hagiographic sources [late text]** — almost all life-detail comes from (a) the terse stanza on Tulsī in **Nābhādās's *Bhaktamāl*** (a near-contemporary devotee-roster, c. 1600, later expanded by Priyādās's commentary, 1712), and (b) the **Mūla Gosāīṃ Carita** and *Gosāīṃ Carita*, full lives whose received texts scholars regard as **substantially later than they claim to be**. Tulsī's own works add scattered autobiographical lines (*Kavitāvalī*, *Vinaya-patrikā*, the *Mānas* prologue). (Source: Wikipedia, *Tulsidas*; Lutgendorf, *The Life of a Text*) [scholarly]
- **Autobiographical anchors [scholarly]** — the poet himself says he **heard the Rāma-story from his guru at Sūkarakheta** (*Mānas*, Bāl-kāṇḍa: *maiṃ puni nija gura sana sunī kathā so sūkarakheta*), and in the *Kavitāvalī* describes his childhood destitution — orphan-poverty so sharp that "even Ajāmila's sins were fewer," begging his food door to door. (Source: *Rāmcaritmānas*, Bāl-kāṇḍa; *Kavitāvalī*, Uttara-kāṇḍa)

## The hagiographic life [folk]/[late text]

- **The inauspicious birth (Rāmbolā)** — born under the **Abhuktamūla** portion of Mūla nakṣatra (a configuration held to doom the father), after twelve months in the womb, with all thirty-two teeth, and speaking **"Rāma"** as his first word — hence the childhood name **Rāmbolā** ("he who uttered Rāma"). The frightened parents gave the infant away; the servant **Cuniyā** raised him until her death, leaving him a beggar-orphan of about five. (Source: *Mūla Gosāīṃ Carita*; *Vinaya-patrikā* alludes to parental abandonment) [late text]
- **Adoption by the guru** — the wandering Rāmānandī ascetic **Narharidās (Naraharyānanda)** found the orphan, initiated him, gave him the name **Tulsīdās**, and recited to him the Rāma-story at **Sūkarakṣetra (Soron)** — the one episode the poet himself corroborates (above). Later study is placed at Vārāṇasī under **Śeṣa Sanātana**, covering grammar, the Vedas and darśanas. (Source: *Mūla Gosāīṃ Carita*; Wikipedia, *Tulsidas*) [late text]
- **The Ratnāvalī episode — the turning of the life [folk]** — the pivot of the hagiography: Tulsī, married to **Ratnāvalī** and infatuated with her, follows her across the swollen river to her father's house (the telling has him gripping a floating corpse as a raft and a snake as a rope, unseeing in his passion). Ratnāvalī rebukes him: *"asthi carma maya deha mama, tā meṃ aisī prīti / taisī jo śrī rāma mahaṃ, hota na tau bhava-bhīti"* — "this body of mine is bone and skin; had you such love for Rāma, you would have no fear of rebirth." Stung awake, Tulsī **renounces household life on the spot** and leaves for Prayāg and Kāśī as a renunciant. (Source: *Mūla Gosāīṃ Carita*; Priyādās on *Bhaktamāl*) [folk]
  - *Variant [scholarly]:* the marriage-and-renunciation tale appears only in the later hagiographies; some scholars note Tulsī's own verses never mention a wife, and treat Ratnāvalī as a didactic legend built on his vairāgya. [disputed]
- **The preta and the leper — Hanumān darśana [oral tradition]** — Tulsī daily poured his leftover water at the root of a tree, unknowingly satisfying a **preta** (thirsty ghost), who in gratitude revealed how to find Hanumān: *he attends every Rāma-kathā, arriving first disguised as a leper and leaving last.* Tulsī seized the leper's feet after his own kathā at Vārāṇasī; **Hanumān revealed himself** and, at Tulsī's plea for the sight of Rāma, directed him to **Citrakūṭ** ([`../06-sacred-geography/chitrakoot-kamadgiri.md`](../06-sacred-geography/chitrakoot-kamadgiri.md)). (Source: *Mūla Gosāīṃ Carita*; Lutgendorf, *Hanuman's Tale*) [oral tradition]
- **The darśana at Citrakūṭ [oral tradition]** — at Citrakūṭ two princely horsemen passed Tulsī, who failed to recognize them as **Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa**; Hanumān promised a second chance. Next morning at the ghāṭ, as Tulsī ground sandal-paste, Rāma himself came as a boy and asked for a tilaka; Hanumān, fearing Tulsī would miss him again, took a parrot's form and sang the famous dohā — *"citrakūṭa ke ghāṭa para bhaī santana kī bhīra / tulasīdāsa candana ghisaiṃ tilaka deta raghubīra"* — "on Citrakūṭ's ghāṭ the saints thronged; Tulsīdās grinds sandal, Raghuvīra himself takes the tilaka." (Source: *Mūla Gosāīṃ Carita*; tradition-dohā) [oral tradition]
- **Old age and the aching arm** — late hagiography and his own verse record afflictions in his last years at Vārāṇasī — an intense **pain in the arm (bāhu-pīḍā)**, answered by the **Hanumān Bāhuka**, his petition-poem to Hanumān for healing; the *Kavitāvalī* also laments an **epidemic ravaging Kāśī**. He died at **Asī Ghāṭ on the Gaṅgā, V.S. 1680 (1623 CE)** per the death-couplet above. (Source: *Hanumān Bāhuka*; *Kavitāvalī*; tradition-couplet) [traditional]

## Mughal-era Vārāṇasī — context and court legends

- **Historical setting [scholarly]** — Tulsī's productive life spans the reigns of **Akbar (1556–1605) and Jahāngīr (1605–1627)**: a north India of consolidated Mughal power, vigorous vernacular literary culture, and Vārāṇasī ([`../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md`](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md)) as the Sanskrit-orthodox capital against which his vernacular project was measured. (Source: Britannica, *Tulsidas*; Lutgendorf, *The Life of a Text*) [scholarly]
- **The Ṭoḍar Mal connection** — tradition links Tulsī in friendship with **Ṭoḍar Mal**, Akbar's revenue minister (or, in other readings, a Vārāṇasī landholder of that name); a dohā **mourning Ṭoḍar's death** is attributed to Tulsī. (Source: *Dohāvalī* attribution; Wikipedia, *Tulsidas*) [disputed]
- **The emperor and the monkeys [oral tradition]** — a popular legend has "the emperor" (named as Akbar or Jahāngīr in different tellings) summon Tulsī to Delhi to perform a miracle; Tulsī refuses — *"I know nothing but Rāma's name"* — and is imprisoned, whereupon **hordes of monkeys ransack the court** until the emperor releases him and begs pardon. In a widespread modern telling Tulsī composes the **forty verses of the Hanumān Cālīsā during forty days of this imprisonment** — an aetiology the [Cālīsā's own file](../04-deep-dives/stotras/hanuman-chalisa.md) treats with the same caution. (Source: popular hagiography; Lutgendorf, *Hanuman's Tale*) [oral tradition] [disputed]
- **The paṇḍits of Kāśī and the Viśvanātha test [folk]** — the Sanskrit establishment, scandalized that scripture had been sung in Awadhi, harassed the poet (thieves sent for the *Mānas* manuscript are met by two armed guards — Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa themselves — watching the poet's hut). To settle the text's standing, the *Mānas* was **laid beneath the Sanskrit scriptures overnight in the Viśvanātha sanctum**; by morning it lay **on top**, endorsed *satyaṃ śivaṃ sundaram* with Śiva's own signature. (Source: *Mūla Gosāīṃ Carita*; Priyādās on *Bhaktamāl*) [folk]
- **The Mīrā correspondence [disputed]** — a cherished tradition has [Mīrābāī](mirabai.md) write to Tulsī asking whether she may abandon persecuting kin for Kṛṣṇa; his reply is identified with the *Vinaya-patrikā* pada *"jāke priya na rāma-vaidehī, tajiye tāhi koṭi bairī sama, jadyapi parama sanehī"* — "abandon whoever holds not Rāma and Vaidehī dear, though they be your dearest, as one abandons ten million enemies." Chronology makes the exchange doubtful; the tradition is recorded, not endorsed. (Source: *Vinaya-patrikā*; popular tradition) [disputed]

## The oeuvre — twelve works beyond and around the Mānas

Tradition attributes about a dozen securely-held works (six "major," six "minor") plus a penumbra of attributed hymns; the split runs Awadhi (narrative) vs Braj (lyric). (Source: Wikipedia, *Tulsidas*) [scholarly]

- **Rāmcaritmānas** — the Awadhi epic of Rāma in seven kāṇḍas, begun **Rāma-navamī V.S. 1631 (1574 CE) at Ayodhyā** ([`../06-sacred-geography/ayodhya.md`](../06-sacred-geography/ayodhya.md)) and finished in about two years and seven months across Ayodhyā, Kāśī and Citrakūṭ. **Canonical home: [`ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md`](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md)** — structure, theology, manuscript history and its debt to the [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md) are treated there, not here. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramcharitmanas*) [scholarly]
- **Vinaya-patrikā ("The Petition of Humility")** — Braj-bhāṣā, ~279 padas: a **formal petition (patrikā) filed in Rāma's court** against the tyranny of Kali Yuga, delivered through Hanumān, with opening hymns to Gaṇeśa, Śiva, Pārvatī, Gaṅgā and the divine court before the plea proper; the summit of his **vinaya** (penitent-humility) voice and, with the *Mānas*, his most theologically weighty work. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vinaya Patrika*; Allchin's translation) [scholarly]
- **Kavitāvalī** — Braj-bhāṣā poems in kavitta/savaiyā meters, arranged in seven kāṇḍas mirroring the *Mānas*; contains his sharpest **autobiographical stanzas** — the beggar-orphan childhood, the taunts of his critics, and the defiant *"māṅgi ke khaibo, masīta ko soibo"* ("I'll beg my food and sleep in a mosque — nothing to take, nothing to give") — plus a grim portrait of Kali-age Kāśī in its Uttara-kāṇḍa. (Source: *Kavitāvalī*, Uttara-kāṇḍa; Allchin's translation) [scholarly]
- **Dohāvalī** — a collection of **~573 free-standing dohās/sorṭhās** on Rāma-nāma, ethics and devotion; some couplets are shared with the *Mānas* and *Rāmājñā-praśna*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Tulsidas*) [scholarly]
- **Gītāvalī and Kṛṣṇa-gītāvalī** — Braj song-cycles: the *Gītāvalī* (~328 padas) sings Rāma's life as rāga-set lyric in seven kāṇḍas; the *Kṛṣṇa-gītāvalī* (61 padas) shows the Rāma-poet singing **Kṛṣṇa** — the bhramara-gīta and Braj themes — evidence against any sectarian wall between the two saguṇa streams (cf. [Sūrdās](surdas.md)). (Source: Wikipedia, *Tulsidas*) [scholarly]
- **The minor works** — **Barvai Rāmāyaṇa** (a miniature Rāma-story in barvai meter), **Janakī-maṅgala** and **Pārvatī-maṅgala** (Awadhi wedding-songs of Sītā's and Pārvatī's marriages), **Rāmalalā-nahachū** (a folk nahachū song for child-Rāma's rite), **Rāmājñā-praśna** (an augury/praśna text on Rāma's will), **Vairāgya-sandīpanī** ("Kindling of Detachment," on the sant's nature). (Source: Wikipedia, *Tulsidas*) [scholarly]
- **Hanumān Cālīsā — the attribution question** — the forty-verse Hanumān hymn, arguably the **most-recited devotional text of modern north India**, is traditionally Tulsī's (the signature-verse names Tulsīdās, and tradition ties it to the imprisonment legend above); the attribution rests on tradition and internal signature rather than early manuscript proof, and some scholars leave it open. Full treatment at [`../04-deep-dives/stotras/hanuman-chalisa.md`](../04-deep-dives/stotras/hanuman-chalisa.md); on the deity, see [`../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Hanuman Chalisa*; Lutgendorf, *Hanuman's Tale*) [disputed]
- **Hanumān Bāhuka and the Hanumān hymns** — the *Hanumān Bāhuka* (petition for the healing of his arm) and the Sanskrit-Braj *Hanumānāṣṭaka/Bajraṅg Bāṇ* penumbra attributed with varying confidence; Tulsī is the single greatest source of Hanumān's early-modern devotional ascent. (Source: Lutgendorf, *Hanuman's Tale*) [scholarly]

## Place in the Rāmānandī stream

- **Lineage** — tradition places Tulsī in the **Rāmānandī Sampradāya**: his guru Narharidās is counted in the succession from **Rāmānanda** (the Gangetic organizer of Rāma-bhakti, hinge of the whole northern movement — see [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md#rāmānanda--the-root-of-the-north-indian-movement)), itself doctrinally downstream of the [Rāmānuja / Śrī-Vaiṣṇava line](four-vaishnava-sampradayas.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Tulsidas*, *Ramananda*) [traditional]
  - *Caveat [scholarly]:* the guru-chain linking Tulsī to Rāmānanda is a **sectarian reconstruction**; Tulsī names Narharidās but never charts the fuller lineage, and modern scholars treat the Rāmānandī claim on him as retrospective (if theologically apt).
- **Theology** — Tulsī's Rāma is **saguṇa Parabrahman**: the *Mānas* repeatedly asserts the identity of the formless nirguṇa and the embodied saguṇa (*"aguna saguna dui brahma sarūpā"*), resolving the sant-era dispute by subordinating both to bhakti and to the **Name of Rāma**, which he ranks above even Rāma's form. He is also notably **Śaiva-Vaiṣṇava irenic**: Śiva narrates the *Mānas* frame-story, and Tulsī's Kāśī works honor Viśvanātha — a deliberate healing of the sectarian split. (Source: *Rāmcaritmānas*, Bāl-kāṇḍa; *Vinaya-patrikā*) [scholarly]

## Legacy — Rāmlīlā, Saṅkaṭ Mocan, and the Rāma-public

- **The Rāmlīlā [traditional]** — tradition credits Tulsīdās (with his disciple **Megha Bhagat**) with instituting the **Rāmlīlā** — the open-air autumn enactment of Rāma's story with the *Mānas* as its recited script — at Vārāṇasī; the form spread across the Hindi belt, its grandest survival the month-long **Rāmnagar Rāmlīlā** patronized by the Kāśī Narēś (established 19th c.). UNESCO proclaimed **"Ramlila, the traditional performance of the Ramayana"** a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity (2005; inscribed on the Representative List 2008). (Source: UNESCO ICH, *Ramlila*; Lutgendorf, *The Life of a Text*) [traditional]/[scholarly]
- **Saṅkaṭ Mocan and the ghāṭs [traditional]** — the **Saṅkaṭ Mocan Hanumān temple** of Vārāṇasī is traditionally held to stand where Tulsī received Hanumān's darśana, and to have been founded by him; **Tulsī Ghāṭ** (adjacent to Asī Ghāṭ, where he died) preserves his residence, and relics of the poet are shown there. (Source: temple tradition; Lutgendorf, *Hanuman's Tale*) [traditional]
- **The vernacular scripture** — the *Mānas* became "the Bible of north India" [scholarly, Growse's phrase], the most-read work of the Hindi world, recited in pārāyaṇa and akhaṇḍa-pāṭha like a Veda — the fullest vindication of the bhakti wager that scripture belongs in the people's tongue; its text-history and reception live in [`ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md`](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md). (Source: Growse, tr. *The Rámáyana of Tulsi Dás*; Lutgendorf, *The Life of a Text*) [scholarly]
- **The proverb of the poets** — the Braj literary field ranked him in the proverb *"Sūra sūra, Tulasī sasī"* — "Sūr is the sun, Tulsī the moon" (the Aṣṭachāp poets the stars) — pairing him permanently with [Sūrdās](surdas.md) at the summit of early-modern Hindi verse. (Source: Hindi literary tradition) [folk]
