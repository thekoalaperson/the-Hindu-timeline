# Gorā Kumbhār (Goroba the Potter-Saint)

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](./README.md) (master index: [`../README.md`](../README.md)). The 13th–14th-c. **kumbhār** (potter) **sant** of **Ter / Ṭheradhokī** in the Marāṭhwāḍā, the **eldest** of the early **Vārkarī** cohort of Viṭhobā-devotees, abhaṅga-poet, and hero of the workshop vignette where he "tests the saints for ripeness" with his potter's mallet.
> Frame against the lineage roster in [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md), his companions [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md), [Nāmdev](namdev.md), [Janābāī](janabai.md) and [Chokhāmeḷā](chokhamela.md). Two clocks: this is **academic / historical** Kali-Yuga dating, not Purāṇic yuga-time — and the historical clock here is loose, resting almost wholly on hagiography.

## Who He Was

- **Gorā Kumbhār (Goroba, Gorobā Kākā)** — the **kumbhār** (potter) **sant** and abhaṅga-poet of **Ter** (older **Ṭheradhokī / Ṭher**, in present-day Osmanābād / Dhārāśiv district, Marāṭhwāḍā), an ecstatic devotee of **Viṭṭhal / Viṭhobā / Pāṇḍuraṅga** of **Pandharpur**, and a member of the foundational [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md)–[Nāmdev](namdev.md) circle of the **Vārkarī** movement. (Source: Wikipedia, *Gora Kumbhar*; Vārkarī hagiography)
  - *Caste [scholarly]:* born into the **kumbhār** (potter) artisan community, conventionally a low/"Śūdra" rank — like [Nāmdev](namdev.md) the tailor, [Chokhāmeḷā](chokhamela.md) the *mahār*, and Sāvtā the gardener, his trade-caste is integral to the egalitarian, artisan-saint charter of the movement. (Source: Wikipedia, *Gora Kumbhar*)
  - *Honorific [traditional]:* addressed in Vārkarī memory as **Gorobā Kākā** ("Uncle Goroba"), a title of seniority and affection that frames him as the **eldest brother / elder** of the saintly company. (Source: Vārkarī hagiography) [folk]
- **The potter's craft as sādhanā** — his devotion is inseparable from his trade: the wheel, the clay-pit and the **thāpaṭṇe** (the wooden mallet/bat used to beat and shape the pot) recur in his abhaṅgas and legends as instruments of God-remembrance, the workshop itself a place of *kīrtana*. (Source: Vārkarī hagiography; Gora Kumbhar abhaṅgas) [folk]

## Dating & Placement in the Cohort [disputed]

- **Conventional span — 13th–14th c. CE** — Gorā is placed as a **senior contemporary** of [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md) (c. 1275–1296) and [Nāmdev](namdev.md) (c. 1270–1350), often given the longest life of the group; traditional dates of roughly **1267–1317 CE** circulate but are hagiographic, not documentary. (Source: Wikipedia, *Gora Kumbhar*; Vārkarī tradition)
  - *Variant (traditional):* remembered simply as the **oldest** of the assembled sants, his exact years uncertain — the seniority is the load-bearing fact, not the calendar.
- **Two-clocks caveat [scholarly]** — as with all the early Vārkarī saints, biography survives mainly through **Mahīpati's** much later 18th-c. hagiographies (*Bhaktavijaya*, *Bhaktalīlāmṛta*) and oral abhaṅga tradition; the "history" here is **academic-clock** but thin, and the famous vignettes are devotional, not chronicle. (Source: Wikipedia, *Mahipati*; *Gora Kumbhar*)

## The Workshop Vignette — "Testing the Saints for Ripeness" [folk]

- **The gathering at Goroba's workshop** — the signature Gorā Kumbhār legend: a company of sants — [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md), [Nāmdev](namdev.md), **Muktābāī** (Jñāneśvar's sister), and others — gathers at Goroba's potter's yard at Ter. As the **eldest and most spiritually mature**, Goroba is asked to judge **who among them is fully "cooked" / God-realized** and who is still wanting. (Source: Mahīpati, *Bhaktavijaya*; Vārkarī hagiography)
- **The potter's test with the thāpaṭṇe** — drawing on his craft — a potter taps a fired pot to hear whether it rings true or sounds **kacchā** (unbaked, raw) — Goroba **taps each saint's head with his potter's mallet (thāpaṭṇe)** to "test for ripeness," as one tests a pot for proper firing. (Source: Mahīpati, *Bhaktavijaya*) [folk]
  - *The verdict on Nāmdev:* Goroba declares **[Nāmdev](namdev.md) still "unbaked" (kacchā / ardha-kacchā)** — ripe in love but **not yet completed**, because he still lacks a **living guru** and clings to God as confined to the Pandharpur image. The other saints he passes. (Source: Mahīpati, *Bhaktavijaya*; Wikipedia, *Namdev*)
  - *Nāmdev's reaction:* stung, Nāmdev protests to **Viṭhobā** himself; the deity confirms the verdict and **directs him to a living teacher** — **Viṣobā Khecara** (the Nāth-influenced guru) — under whom Nāmdev is finally "fired to completion," learning the all-pervading formless presence. The vignette thus functions as the **narrative hinge** that explains Nāmdev's turn from pure saguṇa Viṭṭhal-worship toward the nirguṇa register (see [`namdev.md`](namdev.md)). (Source: Mahīpati, *Bhaktavijaya*; Vārkarī hagiography)
  - *Variant (telling/cast):* some retellings vary the roster of saints present and whether the "unripe" verdict singles out **Nāmdev** alone or also others; the **constant** is Goroba as senior tester and Nāmdev as the one sent on to complete his realization under a guru. Catalog the variation; do not flatten. (Source: Vārkarī hagiography) [regional]

## The Child in the Clay-Pit [folk]

- **The child trampled while lost in kīrtana** — the most poignant Goroba legend: absorbed in *nāma-saṅkīrtana* while treading clay in his pit (or while singing of Viṭhobā), Gorā **fails to notice his own infant child** crawl into the clay and is said to have **trampled the child underfoot**, killing it — his God-absorption so total that the world fell away. (Source: Mahīpati, *Bhaktalīlāmṛta / Bhaktavijaya*; Vārkarī hagiography)
  - *The restoration / aftermath:* in the devotional telling, the loss is met not with collapse of faith but with **Viṭhobā's grace** — variants have the child **restored to life** by the Lord, or the grief itself confirming Goroba's surrender; the story is told as the extreme **measure of his absorption (anuṣṭhāna / ekāgratā)**, not as carelessness. (Source: Vārkarī hagiography) [folk]
  - *The vow of stillness (variant):* a linked tradition holds that Gorā, or his wife, **vowed never to let his clay-treading hands work again** / that he stopped treading clay after the loss, until Viṭhobā intervened — a motif underlining the cost of total devotion. (Source: Vārkarī hagiography) [regional/disputed]

## His Works & Cult

- **The abhaṅgas of Gorā Kumbhār** — a body of Marathi *abhaṅgas* circulates under his name within the Vārkarī corpus, sung in *kīrtana* alongside those of [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md), [Nāmdev](namdev.md), [Janābāī](janabai.md) and [Chokhāmeḷā](chokhamela.md); they draw imagery from the potter's craft and the name of **Viṭṭhal**. (Source: Vārkarī abhaṅga tradition)
  - *Count [disputed]:* the authentic-core-vs-later-accretion problem common to all early sants applies — the genuine corpus is small and unfixed, an open roster. [scholarly]
- **The samādhi at Ter** — Gorā's **samādhi shrine at Ter** (Ṭheradhokī) is a living pilgrimage point; his image is carried in the Vārkarī processions, and a **pālkhī (palanquin) tradition** associates him with the great **Āṣāḍhī / Kārtikī wārī** to Pandharpur. (Source: Wikipedia, *Gora Kumbhar*; Vārkarī tradition) [traditional]
- **Veneration as "Gorobā Kākā"** — invoked as the **elder** of the saintly family of Pandharpur; the potter-saint stands beside the tailor [Nāmdev](namdev.md), the gardener Sāvtā, the goldsmith Narahari and the *mahār* [Chokhāmeḷā](chokhamela.md) as proof that the Vārkarī path drew its saints **from every trade and caste**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Gora Kumbhar*, *Warkari*)

## Place in the Succession

- **Within the Jñāneśvar–Nāmdev circle** — Gorā belongs squarely to the **founding generation** of the Vārkarī movement (see [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md)), the cohort gathered around [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md) and [Nāmdev](namdev.md) at the close of the 13th c.; he is its **senior figure**, the "uncle" whose workshop hosts the saints. (Source: Wikipedia, *Gora Kumbhar*; Vārkarī tradition)
- **The artisan-saint pattern** — with him the movement's defining shape is clear: a fellowship of **working-caste householder-saints** — potter, tailor, gardener, goldsmith, maidservant ([Janābāī](janabai.md)), *mahār* ([Chokhāmeḷā](chokhamela.md)) — whose sanctity is enacted **in and through their trades**, not apart from them. (Source: Wikipedia, *Warkari*)
