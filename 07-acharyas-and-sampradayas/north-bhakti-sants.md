## The two streams — Nirguṇa & Saguṇa

The north/west bhakti field is conventionally split by the **form of the divine** the poet addresses. This is the doctrinal fault-line of the whole movement, cutting across language and region. Per repo variant policy, both streams and disputed memberships are cataloged.

- **Nirguṇa-bhakti ("of the attributeless")** — devotion to a **formless, imageless, name-beyond-name** absolute (*nirguṇa Brahman*); rejects temple icons, image-worship, caste hierarchy and priestly mediation, often drawing on Nāth-yoga and Sant interiority. The **Sant tradition** (*sant-mat*) proper — Kabīr, Ravidās, Nānak, Dādū. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kabir*, *Sant (religion)*) [scholarly]
- **Saguṇa-bhakti ("of the qualified")** — devotion to **God-with-form-and-attributes** (*saguṇa Brahman*): a named, embodied, image-worshipped *iṣṭa-devatā*, almost always **Rāma** or **Kṛṣṇa** (with Rādhā). Tulsīdās (Rāma), Sūrdās/Mīrābāī/the Aṣṭachāp (Kṛṣṇa), the whole Vārkarī and Vaiṣṇava-sampradāya world. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramcharitmanas*, *Bhakti movement*) [scholarly]
- **The hinge — Rāmānanda** — his theological breadth (he accepted *both* saguṇa and nirguṇa Brahman) is the standard explanation for why his disciples **branched into both currents at once** — Kabīr/Ravidās into nirguṇa, the Rāma-bhaktas into saguṇa. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramananda*) [scholarly]
- *Caveat [scholarly]:* the nirguṇa/saguṇa line is a heuristic, not a wall — Rāmānanda, and arguably Nāmdev, sit on both sides; manuscripts assign the "same" poet nirguṇa verses in one recension and saguṇa in another (esp. Nāmdev, Mīrā).

## Rāmānanda — the root of the north Indian movement

- **Svāmī Rāmānanda (Rāmānand)** — Vaiṣṇava ascetic-poet of the Gangetic plain, the **pioneering organizer of bhakti in north India** and founder of the **Rāmānandī Sampradāya** (Rāmāvat), today the largest ascetic order of north-Indian Vaiṣṇavism. An early **social reformer**: accepted disciples regardless of caste, gender or creed, and preached/sang in vernacular Hindi rather than Sanskrit. Centred on **Rāma–Sītā** devotion; doctrinally a continuation of the [Rāmānuja / Śrī Sampradāya](./four-vaishnava-sampradayas.md) Viśiṣṭādvaita line, which he is traditionally placed several generations after. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramananda*; Britannica, *Ramananda*)
  - *Dating [disputed]:* commonly **c. 1400–1470 CE**; the *Encyclopaedia of Indian Literature* gives **1366–1467**; broadly "14th–mid-15th c." Birth-date traditionally **30 December**, death uncertain. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramananda*; Vajiram & Ravi)
  - *The "twelve disciples" [disputed/traditional]:* the *Bhaktamāl* tradition makes Kabīr, **Ravidās**, **Pīpā**, **Sena**, **Dhanna** and others his pupils — the seedbed of the whole movement. Postmodern scholars note the **near-total absence of any mention of Rāmānanda in the disciples' own verse** and doubt the literal lineage; others defend it on epigraphic grounds. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramananda*) [scholarly]
- **Rāmānandī Sampradāya** — the *bairāgī* (Rāma-devotee) ascetic order he founded; its *nāgā* (militant) and *tyāgī* sub-orders became a major institution of the north, with chief centres at **Ayodhyā** and the *kumbha* fairs. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramanandi Sampradaya*; Grokipedia)

## The Nirguṇa Sants — formless devotion, caste rejection

- **Kabīr (Kabīr Dās)** — the towering **nirguṇa sant**; a low-caste **Julāhā (weaver) of Vārāṇasī**, raised Muslim, traditionally a disciple of [Rāmānanda](#rāmānanda--the-root-of-the-north-indian-movement). Fierce iconoclast who **rejected the ritualism of both Hinduism and Islam** — temple and mosque, idol and namāz, caste and clergy alike — pointing instead to the formless **Rām**/Brahman within (the Upaniṣadic Brahman–Ātman). His blunt, paradoxical couplets are the most quoted verse in north-Indian devotion. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kabir*)
  - *Dating [disputed]:* traditional lifespan **1398–1518 CE** (a famous 120-year span); academic scholarship favours **c. 1440–1518**, some **1398–1448**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kabir*)
  - *Key works (oral, later compiled) [scholarly]:* the **Bījak** (the Kabīr Panth's core anthology — *Sākhī*, *Ramainī*, *Śabad*), the **Sākhī Granth**, *Kabīr Parachaī*; his verses are the **largest non-Sikh contribution to the [Ādi Granth / Guru Granth Sāhib](../90-literature-corpus/README.md)**.
  - *Birth/death legends [folk]:* born of a Brāhmaṇa widow and floated on a pond (found by the weaver Nīru–Nīmā); at death his body **turned to flowers**, divided between Hindus (cremation, Kāśī) and Muslims (burial, Maghar), resolving the sectarian tug-of-war. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kabir*)
  - **Kabīr Panth** — the sect that reveres him as founder (~9.6 million followers), straddling Hindu and Sikh worlds. [scholarly]
- **Ravidās (Raidās, Guru Ravidāss)** — **Camār (leather-worker / Dalit) sant-poet** of Vārāṇasī, traditionally a disciple of Rāmānanda and a contemporary/associate of Kabīr; a **nirguṇa-bhakti** voice of casteless, ritual-free, fearless (*nirbhau*) surrender. His utopia of an egalitarian city, **Begampura** ("land without sorrow / without tax"), is one of bhakti's great social images. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*; Grokipedia, *Guru Ravidass Jayanti*)
  - *Dating [disputed]:* variously **c. 1377–1528** or **c. 1450 onward**; exact dates unrecoverable. [scholarly]
  - *Works [scholarly]:* ~**40–41 hymns in the [Guru Granth Sāhib](../90-literature-corpus/README.md)**; founder-figure of the modern **Ravidāssia** religion. **Mīrābāī** is traditionally remembered as his disciple [folk].
- *Other early Sants (Rāmānanda's circle) [traditional]:* **Pīpā** (a Rājpūt king turned ascetic), **Sena** (a barber), **Dhanna** (a Jāṭ farmer), **Sadhnā** (a butcher) — the deliberately **cross-caste, cross-trade** roster that advertised bhakti's social levelling; several appear in the *Guru Granth Sāhib*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramananda*)

## The Saguṇa Kṛṣṇa-poets of the north & west

- **Sūrdās** — the **blind master-poet of Braj-bhāṣā**, supreme of the **[Aṣṭachāp](./nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md)** (the eight poet-singers of [Vallabha's Puṣṭimārga](./nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md)), whose Kṛṣṇa-songs are the liturgical music of that sect. Object: child-Kṛṣṇa and the Braj *līlā* ([`../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md`](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md)). (Source: Wikipedia, *Surdas*; Fiveable, *Prominent Bhakti Saints*)
  - *Dating [disputed]:* often **c. 1478–1583 CE**; manuscript-critical work puts his floruit earlier/shorter. [scholarly]
  - *Key work [scholarly]:* the **Sūrsāgar ("Ocean of Sūr")** — a vast Braj-bhāṣā song-anthology on Kṛṣṇa's childhood and youth; the historical core is far smaller than the later accreted corpus. Also *Sūr-saravalī*, *Sāhitya-laharī*.
- **Mīrābāī (Mīrā Bāī)** — **Rājpūt princess-saint** turned wandering Kṛṣṇa-devotee; the most beloved woman's voice of bhakti, singing total erotic-spiritual surrender to Kṛṣṇa as her lover-lord **Giridhar Nāgar** ("Lifter of Govardhana"). Defied marital and caste convention; traditionally drank poison sent by her in-laws and survived. Worship-object: [`../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md`](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md). (Source: Wikipedia/Fiveable; ResearchGate, *Mirabai and Tulsidas*)
  - *Dating [disputed]:* commonly **c. 1498–1547 CE** (Mewar/Merta, Rajasthan); a wide hagiographic spread. ~**1300 padas** are attributed to her (the authentic historical core is much smaller). [scholarly]
  - *Lineage legend [folk]:* remembered as a disciple of **Ravidās**, tying the Rājpūt saguṇa-singer to the Dalit nirguṇa-sant — a deliberately boundary-crossing memory. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*)
- **Narsiṃha Mehtā (Narsī Mehtā)** — the **Ādi Kavi ("first poet") of Gujarati**, a Nāgar Brāhmaṇa Kṛṣṇa-devotee of **Junagadh/Saurashtra**; a saguṇa singer of Kṛṣṇa's *rāsa-līlā* who, like Mīrā, scorned caste pride (famously dined with untouchables). (Source: Wikipedia, *Narsinh Mehta*; BAPS)
  - *Dating [disputed]:* **c. 1414–1481 CE** (also given 1414–1488). [scholarly]
  - *Key works [scholarly]:* ~**740 padas/bhajans** — *Suratsaṅgrāma*, *Rāsa-sahasra-padī*, *Sudāmā-carita*, *Govinda-gamana*, the autobiographical *Hāra-māḷā* and *Hūṇḍī* miracle-songs. His **"Vaiṣṇava Jana To"** became **Mahātmā Gāndhī's favourite bhajan** and a near-anthem of the freedom movement. (Source: Wikipedia, *Narsinh Mehta*; BAPS)

## Tulsīdās — the Saguṇa Rāma-poet and the *Rāmcaritmānas*

- **Gosvāmī Tulsīdās** — the supreme **saguṇa Rāma-bhakta**, an **Awadhi (eastern Hindi) poet** of the Vārāṇasī/Ayodhyā region, in the [Rāmānandī](#rāmānandī-sampradāya) Rāma-devotional stream; arguably the single most influential figure in popular north-Indian Hinduism, who put the *Rāmāyaṇa* story into the mouths and hearts of the Hindi-speaking world. Object: Rāma — see [`../04-deep-dives/rama-and-the-ramayana.md`](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/README.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Tulsidas*; Britannica, *Tulsidas*)
  - *Dating [disputed]:* traditionally **1532–1623 CE** (V.S. 1589–1680). [scholarly/traditional]
  - **Rāmcaritmānas ("Holy Lake of the Acts of Rāma")** — his masterwork: a devotional Awadhi retelling of Vālmīki's [Rāmāyaṇa](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/README.md) in **seven *kāṇḍas***, composed **1574–1576/77** (begun at Ayodhyā on Rāma-Navamī, V.S. 1631); the "Bible of north India," basis of the **Rāmlīlā** pageant and recited in countless households. Reframes Rāma explicitly as supreme Brahman-with-form. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramcharitmanas*) [scholarly]
  - *Other works (of ~22 attributed, ~12 major) [scholarly]:* **Vinaya-patrikā** ("Petition" — personal longing/penitence), **Kavitāvalī**, **Gītāvalī**, **Dohāvalī**, **Hanumān Cālīsā** (the 40-verse Hanumān hymn, the most-recited devotional text in India [folk-attributed]), *Janakī-maṅgala*, *Pārvatī-maṅgala*.

## The Vārkarī Sampradāya — Maharashtra's Viṭṭhala stream

> The Marathi pilgrimage-tradition (*vārī*) centred on **Viṭṭhal / Viṭhobā** of **Pandharpur** — Kṛṣṇa standing arms-akimbo on a brick — regarded as a form of Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa. Emerged in Karnataka in the 12th c., crystallized at Pandharpur; its scripture is the saints' own *abhaṅga* poetry. Cross-ref the order-frame in [`four-vaishnava-sampradayas.md`](./four-vaishnava-sampradayas.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Warkari*, *Vithoba*)

- **Jñāneśvar (Dnyāneśvar, Jñānadeva)** — the **founding luminary of the Vārkarī movement** and of Marathi devotional literature; a Nāth-lineage child-prodigy of **Āḷandī** who died at **21 by living *samādhi*** (*sañjīvana samādhi*). (Source: Wikipedia, *Dnyaneshwar*; Prepp)
  - *Dating [scholarly]:* **1275–1296 CE** (samādhi 1296).
  - **Jñāneśvarī (Bhāvārtha-dīpikā)** — his great **Marathi commentary-paraphrase of the [Bhagavad Gītā](../90-literature-corpus/README.md)** in ~9,000 *ovī* verses (1290), the foundational text of Marathi spirituality; plus the philosophical **Amṛtānubhava ("Nectar of Experience")** and the *Cāṅgadeva Pāsaṣṭī*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Dnyaneshwar*) [scholarly]
- **Nāmdev (Nāmadeva)** — **tailor/calico-printer (śimpī) sant** of Pandharpur, Jñāneśvar's contemporary and co-founder of the Vārkarī *kīrtana* tradition; a rare **pan-Indian** bhakta who travelled to the Punjab. (Source: Wikipedia, *Namdev*; sanatanhindudharma.org)
  - *Dating [disputed]:* **c. 1270–1350 CE**.
  - *Works [scholarly]:* Marathi **abhaṅgas** (with Jñāneśvarī and Tukārām's verse, the basis of the Vārkarī canon) **and ~61 Hindi/Marwari hymns in the [Guru Granth Sāhib](../90-literature-corpus/README.md)** — straddling the saguṇa Viṭṭhala-bhakti of the south and the nirguṇa Sant idiom of the north (the clearest case of a poet on **both sides** of the nirguṇa/saguṇa line). [disputed]
- **Eknāth (Ekanātha)** — Brāhmaṇa scholar-saint of **Paithan**, the great **consolidator** between Jñāneśvar's age and Tukārām's; restored a reliable text of the *Jñāneśvarī* and democratized scripture into Marathi. (Source: Wikipedia/Britannica; Prepp)
  - *Dating [disputed]:* **c. 1533–1599 CE**.
  - *Works [scholarly]:* **Eknāthī Bhāgavata** (Marathi commentary on Skandha 11 of the [Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/README.md)), **Bhāvārtha Rāmāyaṇa**, **Rukmiṇī Svayaṃvara**, and the *bhāruḍ* (allegorical folk-dramatic songs).
- **Tukārām (Tukobā)** — **Śūdra grocer/cultivator sant** of **Dehu**, the **emotional apex of the Vārkarī tradition** and its most beloved abhaṅga-poet; sang of Viṭṭhal in plain, intense, self-abasing Marathi. (Source: Wikipedia, *Warkari*; Prepp)
  - *Dating [disputed]:* **c. 1568–1650 CE** (also **1608–1650**). Traditionally taken bodily to *Vaikuṇṭha* at his death (his abhaṅga manuscripts, drowned in the Indrāyaṇī river, are said to have floated back intact [folk]).
  - *Works [scholarly]:* the **Tukārām Gāthā** — several thousand **abhaṅgas**, the culminating scripture of the Vārkarī corpus.

## Śaṅkaradeva — the Assamese Vaiṣṇava reformer

- **Mahāpuruṣa Śaṅkaradeva (Śrīmanta Śaṅkaradeva)** — 15th–16th c. **polymath saint-reformer of Assam**, founder of **Ekasaraṇa Dharma** ("shelter-in-One"), a monotheistic, **single-refuge devotion to Kṛṣṇa/Viṣṇu (as Nārāyaṇa)** that rejected image-multiplicity, sacrifice and caste — Assam's distinctive bhakti synthesis. Ekasaraṇa's institutional frame is cross-referenced in [`four-vaishnava-sampradayas.md`](./four-vaishnava-sampradayas.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Ekasarana Dharma*; Anantam IAS)
  - *Dating [disputed]:* **1449–1568 CE** (a traditional ~120-year lifespan; some give birth **1483**). [traditional]
  - *Key works & creations [scholarly]:* the **Kīrtana-ghoṣā** (devotional verse-anthology), Assamese renderings of the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa**, the **bargīt** (borgeet) sacred song-genre, the **aṅkīyā nāṭ** one-act devotional plays and **bhāonā** theatre; institutionally the **sattra** (monastery) and **nāmghar** (prayer-hall) network that still organizes Assamese Vaiṣṇavism. His chief disciple **Mādhavadeva** (1489–1596) continued the line with the *Nāma-ghoṣā*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ekasarana Dharma*)

## How they relate — a compact map

- **Root:** Rāmānanda (both streams) → splits into the Nirguṇa Sants and the Saguṇa Rāma/Kṛṣṇa poets.
- **Nirguṇa (formless, anti-caste, anti-ritual):** Kabīr, Ravidās, Pīpā/Sena/Dhanna → feed the **Sant-mat** and the **[Guru Granth Sāhib](../90-literature-corpus/README.md)**.
- **Saguṇa-Rāma:** Tulsīdās → *Rāmcaritmānas* → popular Rāma-bhakti, [Rāmlīlā](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/README.md).
- **Saguṇa-Kṛṣṇa (north/west):** Sūrdās (with the [Aṣṭachāp / Puṣṭimārga](./nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md)), Mīrābāī, Narsiṃha Mehtā → Braj/Gujarati Kṛṣṇa-bhakti.
- **Saguṇa-Viṭṭhal (Maharashtra Vārkarī):** Jñāneśvar → Nāmdev → Eknāth → Tukārām, around Pandharpur.
- **Assam:** Śaṅkaradeva's Ekasaraṇa — a regional monotheistic synthesis of its own.
- These vernacular singers are the **devotional mass-base** beneath the Sanskrit-Vedānta founders cataloged in [`four-vaishnava-sampradayas.md`](./four-vaishnava-sampradayas.md), [`nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md`](./nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md) and [`adi-shankara.md`](./adi-shankara.md).

All dates above should be cross-checked against [`recorded-history.md`](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md); the **traditional (sectarian/hagiographic)** spans frequently run a century or more wider than the **scholarly (manuscript-critical)** ones.
