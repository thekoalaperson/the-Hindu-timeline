# Vedānta Deśika, Piḷḷai Lokācārya & Maṇavāḷa Māmunigaḷ — the Later Śrīvaiṣṇava Ācāryas

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](README.md) — the ācārya chain **after [Rāmānuja](ramanuja-and-madhva.md)** (Parāśara Bhaṭṭar → the Śrīraṅgam commentators → Piḷḷai Lokācārya → Vedānta Deśika → Maṇavāḷa Māmunigaḷ) and the **Teṅkalai / Vaḍakalai ("cat / monkey") split** in depth. Up-link: section index [`07-…/README.md`](README.md).
> Sits in the **first ~1.6% of Kali Yuga** — 12th–15th c. CE [scholarly] on the academic clock; on the scriptural clock, tradition frames these teachers as continuing the rescue of Veda-dharma in a deepening Kali age, several of them hailed as descents (aṃśas/rebirths) of divine actors. The split's *summary* lives in [`ramanuja-and-madhva.md`](ramanuja-and-madhva.md) §"The Teṅkalai / Vaḍakalai split"; this file is the canonical deep treatment.

---

## After Rāmānuja — the Śrīraṅgam succession & the commentarial age

- **Parāśara Bhaṭṭar** — son of Rāmānuja's beloved disciple **Kūrattāḻvān (Kūreśa)**, raised (per tradition) at Rāmānuja's own direction and installed as head of the [Śrīraṅgam Raṅganātha](../06-sacred-geography/srirangam-ranganatha.md) establishment after him; traditional dates c. **1122–1174** [traditional]. Works: **Bhagavad-guṇa-darpaṇa** (the classic Śrīvaiṣṇava commentary on the Viṣṇu Sahasranāma), **Śrī Raṅgarāja Stava** and **Śrī Guṇa Ratna Kośa** (hymn to Raṅganāyakī/Śrī). (Source: Wikipedia, *Parasara Bhattar*)
- **Bhaṭṭar converts Nañjīyar** — Bhaṭṭar travels to debate the Advaitin scholar **Mādhava (Vedānti)**, defeats and converts him; the convert becomes his successor **Nañjīyar**, author of the **Onbadināyirappaḍi ("9,000-paḍi")** commentary on Nammāḻvār's Tiruvāymoḻi. (Source: Wikipedia, *Parasara Bhattar*, *Nanjiyar*) [traditional]
- **Nampiḷḷai and the Īḍu** — Nañjīyar's successor **Nampiḷḷai** becomes the great oral expositor of the [Divya Prabandham](../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md) at Śrīraṅgam; his lectures on the Tiruvāymoḻi are written down by his disciple **Vaḍakku Tiruvīdhi Piḷḷai** as the **Īḍu Muppattāṟāyirappaḍi ("36,000-paḍi")** — the most authoritative Tiruvāymoḻi commentary, treasured especially by the later Teṅkalai school. (Source: Wikipedia, *Nampillai*; Raman, *Self-Surrender (prapatti) to God in Shrivaishnavism*) [scholarly]
- **Periyavāccān Piḷḷai** — fellow disciple of Nampiḷḷai, honoured as **vyākhyāna-cakravartī** ("emperor of commentators"); the only ācārya traditionally credited with commentaries on **the entire 4,000-verse Divya Prabandham**, including his own **Irupattunālāyirappaḍi ("24,000-paḍi")** on the Tiruvāymoḻi. (Source: Wikipedia, *Periyavachan Pillai*) [traditional]
- **The maṇipravāḷa medium** — this whole commentarial age writes in **maṇipravāḷa** ("gems and coral"), a fused Tamil–Sanskrit register created precisely because the school held *both* the Tamil Prabandham and the Sanskrit Veda to be revelation (*ubhaya-vedānta*, "the dual Vedānta"). (Source: Raman; Wikipedia, *Sri Vaishnavism*) [scholarly]

## Piḷḷai Lokācārya & the rahasya-granthas

- **Piḷḷai Lokācārya** — elder son of Vaḍakku Tiruvīdhi Piḷḷai (tradition: born by Nampiḷḷai's blessing and named after him, "Lokācārya" being Nampiḷḷai's honorific); traditional dates **1205–1311** [traditional], with other reckonings placing his death at the 1323 sack (see below) [disputed]. He is the doctrinal fountainhead of what became the **Teṅkalai** school. (Source: Wikipedia, *Pillai Lokacharya*)
- **The Aṣṭādaśa Rahasyas ("eighteen secrets")** — his corpus of **eighteen rahasya-granthas** in maṇipravāḷa, esoteric manuals on the three great mantras and on surrender; chief among them **Mumukṣuppaḍi** (on the Tirumantra, Dvaya and Carama-śloka), **Tattva-traya** (on the three realities — cit, acit, Īśvara — nicknamed the *kuṭṭi* ("pocket") *Bhāgavata*), **Artha-pañcaka** (the five things a soul must know), and above all **Śrīvacana Bhūṣaṇa**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Pillai Lokacharya*; Raman) [scholarly]
- **Śrīvacana Bhūṣaṇa — radical grace** — the "Ornament of sacred sayings" teaches that the Lord's grace is **nirhetuka** (causeless), that any self-effort smuggled into surrender insults the Lord's sole proprietorship of salvation, that **Śrī/Lakṣmī acts as puruṣakāra** (mediatrix) rather than as an independent saviour, and that devotion to one's **ācārya (ācārya-abhimāna)** can itself carry the soul — theses the later Teṅkalai treat as definitive and the Vaḍakalai contest point by point. (Source: Wikipedia, *Srivachana Bhushanam*; Raman, *Self-Surrender*) [scholarly]
- **The flight with Namperumāḷ** — when Muslim armies of the Delhi Sultanate struck Śrīraṅgam, the aged Piḷḷai Lokācārya escorted the temple's processional image (**Namperumāḷ**) out of the shrine and southward to safety; he died on the journey at **Jyotiṣkuḍi** near Madurai, entrusting the line to his disciples. (Source: Wikipedia, *Pillai Lokacharya*; Spencer, "Crisis of Authority" literature on the 14th-c. Srirangam raids) [traditional]
  - *Variant (dating):* tradition and scholarship split on **which raid** — Malik Kafur's 1311 campaign or the **1323 Ulugh Khan sack** of Śrīraṅgam; the 1323 raid is the one most accounts attach to the idol's decades-long southern exile. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ranganathaswamy Temple, Srirangam*) [disputed] [scholarly]
- **Aḻagiya Maṇavāḷa Perumāḷ Nāyanār** — Piḷḷai Lokācārya's younger brother, author of **Ācārya Hṛdayam** ("the heart of the ācārya"), an esoteric maṇipravāḷa companion-work reading Nammāḻvār's inner life; a Teṅkalai core text later commented on by Maṇavāḷa Māmunigaḷ. (Source: Wikipedia, *Alagiya Manavala Perumal Nayanar*) [traditional]

## Vedānta Deśika (Veṅkaṭanātha)

- **Vedānta Deśika** — born **Veṅkaṭanātha** at **Tūppul** near [Kāñcīpuram](../06-sacred-geography/kanchipuram-city-of-temples.md); traditional dates **1268–1369** (a 101-year life) [traditional]; revered by the Vaḍakalai as an **aṃśa of the temple-bell of Tirumala Veṅkaṭeśvara** ([Tirupati](../06-sacred-geography/tirumala-tirupati-venkateshwara.md)) [traditional]. Trained by his maternal uncle **Ātreya Rāmānuja (Appuḷḷār)** in the Vātsya Varadācārya line; by tradition a prodigy who completed all śāstras by age twenty. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vedanta Desika*; Hopkins, *Singing the Body of God*)
- **Titles** — honoured as **kavi-tārkika-siṃha** ("lion among poets and logicians") and **sarva-tantra-svatantra** ("master of all arts and sciences"); tradition says the titles *Vedāntācārya* and *sarva-tantra-svatantra* were conferred by **Raṅganātha and Raṅganāyakī** at Śrīraṅgam themselves. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vedanta Desika*) [traditional]
- **A hundred-plus works** — tradition credits him with **over 100 works** (lists run to ~120) across **four languages** — Sanskrit, Tamil, Prakrit and maṇipravāḷa — spanning kāvya, drama, logic, polemics, ritual, and devotional hymns to the deities of many [divya-deśams](../06-sacred-geography/108-divya-desams.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Vedanta Desika*; Hopkins) [scholarly]
  - **Rahasya-traya-sāra** — his maṇipravāḷa magnum opus, "the essence of the three secrets" (Tirumantra, Dvaya, Carama-śloka): the definitive **Vaḍakalai** statement of prapatti as a deliberate, śāstra-governed act with aṅgas (component disciplines). (Source: Wikipedia, *Vedanta Desika*)
  - **Pādukā-sahasram** — **1,008 verses in praise of Raṅganātha's sandals** (the pādukās that ruled Ayodhyā for Bharata), composed — per tradition — **in a single night** to answer a rival poet's challenge, its *citra-paddhati* chapter containing verses arranged in picture-patterns (chariot-bandha, etc.). (Source: Wikipedia, *Paduka Sahasram*) [traditional]
  - **Polemics & philosophy** — **Śatadūṣaṇī** ("the hundred objections") against Advaita (66 survive); **Tattva-muktā-kalāpa** with his own gloss *Sarvārtha-siddhi*; **Nyāya-siddhāñjana**; **Seśvara-mīmāṃsā** (reading Pūrva-mīmāṃsā as theistic); **Yatirāja-saptati**, seventy verses praising Rāmānuja. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vedanta Desika*) [scholarly]
  - **Kāvya & drama** — **Yādavābhyudaya** (epic on Kṛṣṇa's life, later commented on by the Advaitin Appayya Dīkṣita), **Haṃsa-sandeśa** (a swan-messenger poem answering Kālidāsa's cloud), **Saṅkalpa-sūryodaya** (allegorical drama in which the virtues battle the vices for the soul), **Subhāṣita-nīvī**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vedanta Desika*; Hopkins, *Singing the Body of God*) [scholarly]
- **The 1327 massacre and the Śrutaprakāśikā** — during the Sultanate assault on Śrīraṅgam (dated 1323 or 1327 in different accounts [disputed]), tradition holds Deśika **hid among the heaped corpses** of slain devotees to survive, and escaped carrying the sole manuscript of **Sudarśana Sūri's Śrutaprakāśikā** (the great sub-commentary on Rāmānuja's Śrī Bhāṣya) together with the author's two young sons, taking refuge at **Satyamaṅgalam** in the Karnataka country. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vedanta Desika*; Hopkins) [traditional]
- **Return and the Gopaṇa verses** — after the Vijayanagara commander **Gopaṇa (Goppanārya) of Gingee restored the Raṅganātha image to Śrīraṅgam (c. 1371)**, Deśika — by then over a hundred in the traditional count — composed celebratory verses in Gopaṇa's honour, preserved in temple epigraphy; the restoration is among the earliest firmly datable moments in the whole story. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ranganathaswamy Temple, Srirangam*; epigraphic literature) [scholarly]
- **Vairāgya-pañcaka** — invited (per tradition, via his contemporary **Vidyāraṇya**) to the wealth of the Vijayanagara court, Deśika declined with the five verses of the **Vairāgya-pañcaka**, professing that a servant of the Lord of Hastigiri needs no royal gold. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vedanta Desika*) [traditional]

## Maṇavāḷa Māmunigaḷ (Varavaramuni)

- **Maṇavāḷa Māmunigaḷ** — born **Aḻagiya Maṇavāḷa Perumāḷ Nāyanār** at **Āḻvār Tirunagari** (Nammāḻvār's town); traditional dates **1370–1443** [traditional]; disciple of **Tiruvāymoḻi Piḷḷai (Śrīśailēśa)**, himself in Piḷḷai Lokācārya's line; the consolidator of the **Teṅkalai** school. The Teṅkalai revere him as **a rebirth of Rāmānuja**, returned to finish savouring the Tiruvāymoḻi through the Īḍu. (Source: Wikipedia, *Manavala Mamunigal*) [traditional]
- **Restorer of Śrīraṅgam** — settling at [Śrīraṅgam](../06-sacred-geography/srirangam-ranganatha.md) after the post-invasion recovery, he reorganized worship, repaired shrines and re-established the recitation and study of the Divya Prabandham there. (Source: Wikipedia, *Manavala Mamunigal*) [traditional]
- **The Īḍu kālakṣepam and the "Śrīśailēśa" taniyan** — tradition's crown story: at Raṅganātha's own command Māmunigaḷ delivered a **year-long exposition (kālakṣepam) of the Īḍu 36,000-paḍi** in the temple; at its close **the Lord himself appeared as a small boy** and recited the invocatory verse **"Śrīśailēśa-dayā-pātraṃ…"** in his teacher's honour — the taniyan that Teṅkalai (and many other) temples still recite at the beginning and end of every Prabandham recital, making the deity formally his disciple. (Source: Wikipedia, *Manavala Mamunigal*; Raman) [traditional]
- **Works** — Tamil: **Upadeśa Rattina Mālai** (versified history of the Āḻvārs and ācāryas), **Tiruvāymoḻi Nūṟṟandādi**, **Ārti Prabandham**; Sanskrit: **Yatirāja Viṃśati** (twenty verses to Rāmānuja); plus authoritative maṇipravāḷa **commentaries on Śrīvacana Bhūṣaṇa, Tattva-traya, Mumukṣuppaḍi and Ācārya Hṛdayam**, which fixed the Teṅkalai reading of Piḷḷai Lokācārya. (Source: Wikipedia, *Manavala Mamunigal*) [scholarly]
- **The aṣṭa-dig-gajas** — his **eight chief disciples** ("elephants of the eight directions"), including **Ponnaḍikkāl Jīyar** (first pontiff of the Vānamāmalai/Tōtādri Maṭha) and **Kōil Kandāḍai Aṇṇan**, through whom nearly all Teṅkalai lineages descend. (Source: Wikipedia, *Manavala Mamunigal*, *Vanamamalai Mutt*) [traditional]

## The cat and the monkey — the split in depth

Summary lives in [`ramanuja-and-madhva.md`](ramanuja-and-madhva.md); depth here. Both schools accept Rāmānuja's Viśiṣṭādvaita entire — the quarrel is over **how surrender works**, **which canon leads**, and eventually **who controls the temples**.

- **Markaṭa-nyāya (Vaḍakalai, "monkey principle")** — as a baby monkey must **cling** to its moving mother, the soul must actively co-operate: prapatti is a **deliberate act with prescribed limbs** (aṅga-prapatti), performed once with full awareness, per Vedānta Deśika's Rahasya-traya-sāra. (Source: Britannica, *Tenkalai*; Raman) [scholarly]
- **Mārjāra-nyāya (Teṅkalai, "cat principle")** — as a kitten is **carried in its mother's mouth**, contributing nothing, the soul contributes nothing: grace is **nirhetuka** (causeless), and treating surrender as a meritorious *act* re-smuggles self-effort into what is God's work alone, per Śrīvacana Bhūṣaṇa. (Source: Britannica, *Tenkalai*; Raman) [scholarly]
- **The place of Śrī/Lakṣmī** — Vaḍakalai: Lakṣmī is **co-infinite with the Lord and a co-grantor of mokṣa**; Teṅkalai: Lakṣmī is the supreme **jīva** and **puruṣakāra** (mediatrix who recommends the soul) but not herself the means of salvation. (Source: srivaishnavan.com, *Vadakalai and Tenkalai*; Raman) [traditional] [scholarly]
- **The aṣṭādaśa-bheda ("eighteen differences")** — later polemical digests systematize **eighteen points**: whether prapatti has aṅgas; whether sins after surrender need expiation (Teṅkalai: the Lord enjoys even the soul's faults as a mother the dirt on her child — *doṣa-bhogya*; Vaḍakalai: prāyaścitta applies); whether kaivalya (soul-enjoyment) is a permanent lesser goal; the scope of the Lord's mercy vs his justice; and more. (Source: srivaishnavan.com, *Vadakalai and Tenkalai*) [traditional]
- **Canon and language** — Vaḍakalai ("northern culture") gives interpretive primacy to the **Sanskrit Veda-Vedānta**, Teṅkalai ("southern culture") to the **Tamil Divya Prabandham** — the "Sanskrit monkeys / Tamil cats" of modern scholarship — with the Teṅkalai correspondingly more open on caste-access to prapatti's fruits. (Source: Raman, *Self-Surrender (prapatti) to God in Shrivaishnavism*) [scholarly]
- **Visible badge — the ūrdhva-puṇḍra** — the schools even mark foreheads differently: the Vaḍakalai white mark is **U-shaped**, the Teṅkalai **Y-shaped with a stem extending onto the nose-bridge** — the flashpoint of famous temple disputes (including 19th-c. litigation over which nāmam the temple elephant at Kāñcī should wear). (Source: Appadurai, *Worship and Conflict under Colonial Rule*; Wikipedia, *Urdhva Pundra*) [scholarly]
- **Chronology of the split** — the doctrinal positions crystallize with Piḷḷai Lokācārya and Vedānta Deśika (late 13th–14th c.), **but the two men were not sect-founders and never anathematized each other**; scholarship dates the hardening into rival, temple-controlling *kalais* to the 15th–18th centuries, climaxing in colonial-era lawsuits over honours and management at Śrīraṅgam, Kāñcī and Tirupati. (Source: Appadurai, *Worship and Conflict under Colonial Rule*; Raman) [scholarly]

## Seats (maṭhas) and lineages today

- **Vaḍakalai seats** — the **Ahobila Maṭha** (tradition: founded **1398**, when the young ascetic **Ādivaṇ Śaṭhakopa Jīyar** was initiated — per the maṭha's account — by **Narasiṃha of Ahobilam himself** [traditional]); the **Parakāla Maṭha** of Mysore (Deśika's lineage via Brahmatantra Svatantra Jīyar, later rājaguru seat of the Mysore Woḍeyars); and the later **Āṇḍavan āśramas** of Srirangam. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ahobila Mutt*, *Parakala Mutt*) [traditional] [scholarly]
- **Teṅkalai seats** — Śrīraṅgam's **Śrīraṅga Nārāyaṇa Jīyar Maṭha** (associated with Māmunigaḷ's reorganization), the **Vānamāmalai (Tōtādri) Maṭha** under Ponnaḍikkāl Jīyar's succession, and centres at **Sriperumbudur** (Rāmānuja's birthplace) and Āḻvār Tirunagari. (Source: Wikipedia, *Manavala Mamunigal*, *Vanamamalai Mutt*) [traditional]
- **Shared map** — both kalais worship across the same [108 divya-deśams](../06-sacred-geography/108-divya-desams.md) and both claim [Melkote](../06-sacred-geography/melkote-tirunarayanapuram.md) and Śrīraṅgam's Rāmānuja heritage; the geography is common even where the taniyans differ. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sri Vaishnavism*)
