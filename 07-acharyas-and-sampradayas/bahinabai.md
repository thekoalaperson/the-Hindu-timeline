# Bahiṇābāī (Bahiṇā) — The Autobiographical Vārkarī Sant

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](./README.md) (master index: [`../README.md`](../README.md)). The 17th-c. **Brāhmaṇa woman sant** of Maharashtra, disciple of the Śūdra grocer-poet **[Tukārām](tukaram.md)**, and author of the **Bahiṇābāīcī Abhaṅga-gāthā / Ātmanivedana** — the most candid first-person spiritual *autobiography* in the Marathi **Vārkarī** corpus.
> Frame against the lineage roster in [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md), the guru in [`tukaram.md`](tukaram.md), and the founder-philosopher in [`jnaneshwar.md`](jnaneshwar.md). Two clocks: this is **academic / historical** dating (c. 1628–1700 CE), not Purāṇic yuga-time.

## Who She Was

- **Bahiṇābāī (Bahiṇā, Bahinabai Siyodkar)** — a **Deśastha Brāhmaṇa** married woman of mid-17th-c. Maharashtra who became a **Vārkarī sant** and a disciple of **[Tukārām](tukaram.md)**; remembered above all for an **autobiographical cycle of *abhaṅgas*** narrating her own life with unusual frankness — including her conflict between *bhakti* and the duties of a Brahmin wife. (Source: Wikipedia, *Bahina Bai*; Tharu & Lalita, *Women Writing in India*)
  - *Dates [scholarly]:* conventionally **c. 1628–1700 CE**, making her a near-contemporary and outliving disciple of Tukārām (d. 1649/1650). (Source: Wikipedia, *Bahina Bai*; Feldhaus, *Bahina Bai*)
  - *Distinct person [disputed-by-confusion]:* **NOT** the 19th–20th-c. **Khandeshi folk-poet Bahiṇābāī Caudharī (1880–1951)** of Jalgaon, famous for oral Ahirāṇī/Khandeshi verse — a wholly different woman of a later age. This file is the **Vārkarī sant**, not the folk-poet; the shared name routinely causes conflation. (Source: Wikipedia, *Bahinabai Chaudhari* vs *Bahina Bai*)

## Why She Matters — The Self-Narrative

- **A rare double rarity** — both a **woman** *and* a **Brāhmaṇa** within a bhakti stream whose great voices are overwhelmingly **male and low-caste** (Tukārām the Śūdra grocer, Nāmdev the tailor, **Jānābāī** the maidservant — see [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md)); her position **inverts** the usual Vārkarī social profile and makes her testimony singular. (Source: Tharu & Lalita; Feldhaus)
- **Devotional autobiography, not hagiography** — her verse is told **in the first person about herself**, recording inner doubt, marital strife, social shame, and ecstasy; this **Ātmanivedana** ("self-surrender / self-disclosure") is a landmark of pre-modern Indian women's self-writing, distinct from the third-person *caritra* (saint-biography) genre. (Source: Tharu & Lalita, *Women Writing in India*; Feldhaus)
- **A primary first-person source on Tukārām's circle** — because she writes as a living participant, her *abhaṅgas* are also a **documentary witness** to the Dehu Vārkarī milieu around Tukārām, valued by historians alongside the Gāthā itself. (Source: Feldhaus, *Bahina Bai: A Translation*)

## Life Arc — Her Own Account [folk / autobiographical]

- **Child marriage and a hostile husband** — born to a Brahmin family, **married very young** (traditionally at about age three to five) to a much older Brahmin widower, **Gaṅgādhar Paṭhak (Pāṭhak)**; her husband is, by her own narration, **orthodox, learned, irascible, and bitterly opposed** to her devotion. (Source: Wikipedia, *Bahina Bai*; Feldhaus)
- **The wandering household** — economic hardship drove the family to move repeatedly (Devagaon, Kolhapur and other towns), the displacement forming the backdrop to her spiritual turn. (Source: Feldhaus; Tharu & Lalita)
- **The calf** — the signature legend: in her wandering days she became attached to a **calf** that followed her everywhere; at a *kīrtan* the calf, overcome, is said to have **collapsed and died** (in some tellings entering a trance of devotion), and **Bahiṇā fell unconscious / near death** in grief — a turning point dramatizing her own absorption into *bhakti*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Bahina Bai*; Feldhaus) [folk]
  - *Variant (rebirth gloss):* her *abhaṅgas* frame her devotion across **prior births**, the calf-episode read as the karmic fruition of earlier lives of practice. [late text]
- **Vision of Tukārām and *dīkṣā* in a dream** — she had a **vision / dream-encounter of [Tukārām](tukaram.md)**, who gave her the *mantra* and accepted her as disciple; this **dream-initiation** mirrors Tukārām's own dream-*dīkṣā* from Bābājī Caitanya and is a Vārkarī signature — spiritual authority running through grace and the saints rather than a Sanskritic *guru-paramparā*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Bahina Bai*; Feldhaus; cf. [`tukaram.md`](tukaram.md)) [folk]
- **The husband's opposition — the central drama** — her *abhaṅgas* record in raw detail the **conflict between her devotion to a Śūdra guru and her duty as a Brahmin wife**:
  - **Caste scandal** — her open discipleship to **Tukārām, a Śūdra**, brought social outrage and her husband's fury; for an orthodox Brahmin couple, a wife's surrender to a low-caste *guru* was a grave transgression. (Source: Feldhaus; Tharu & Lalita)
  - **Threat of abandonment / violence** — she narrates her husband's **rage, threats to leave or harm her**, and his eventual (in her telling) **change of heart**, reconciling to her path. (Source: Wikipedia, *Bahina Bai*; Feldhaus)
  - **The unresolved tension she refuses to flatten** — uniquely, she does **not** simply renounce the household; she holds **wifely duty (*pativratā* / *strī-dharma*) and bhakti together in tension**, voicing the genuine difficulty rather than a tidy victory of one over the other. This honesty is what scholars prize. (Source: Tharu & Lalita; Feldhaus) [scholarly]
- **Children and end** — she is said to have borne children (a son, **Viṭhobā**, traditionally), continued composing, and **died c. 1700**; a tradition holds she **foretold the circumstances of her own death** in verse. (Source: Wikipedia, *Bahina Bai*; Feldhaus) [folk]

## The Work — Bahiṇābāīcī Abhaṅga-gāthā

- **The Abhaṅga-gāthā / Ātmanivedana** — her collected **Marathi *abhaṅgas***, the **autobiographical core** of which narrates her life and inner struggle in first person; transmitted within the Vārkarī tradition and later **edited and printed** in the modern era. (Source: Feldhaus, *Bahina Bai: A Translation of Her Autobiography and Verses*, 1985)
  - *Form:* the **abhaṅga** — short, plain, end-stopped Marathi devotional verse made for **collective *kīrtan*** — the same form perfected by [Tukārām](tukaram.md) and reaching back to [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md) and Nāmdev. (Source: cf. [`tukaram.md`](tukaram.md); [`jnaneshwar.md`](jnaneshwar.md))
  - *Count [disputed]:* the surviving corpus is numbered in the **several hundred** *abhaṅgas*; the **authentic core vs. later accretion** is an open philological question, as with the Tukārām Gāthā. [scholarly]
- **Themes** — devotion to **Viṭṭhal / Viṭhobā** of Pandharpur; the **dignity and difficulty of a woman's path** to God within marriage; reverence for Tukārām and the Vārkarī saints; and a frank theology of *bhakti* that treats lived domestic conflict as spiritual material. (Source: Tharu & Lalita; Feldhaus)
- **Place in the canon** — she stands in the Vārkarī line **Jñāneśvar → Nāmdev → Eknāth → Tukārām** as a **late-17th-c. disciple-generation voice**, and as the tradition's foremost **woman autobiographer**, complementing the earlier maidservant-sant **Jānābāī** (treated in [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md)). (Source: Wikipedia, *Warkari*; Feldhaus)
