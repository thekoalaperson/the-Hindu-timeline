# Balinese Hinduism — Agama Hindu Dharma

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](README.md) — the living Hindu religion of **Bali** (and its Javanese remnants): a **Śaiva-Siddhānta-rooted, Buddhist-blended, ancestor- and rice-centred dharma** carried from **Majapahit Java** to Bali and reorganized in the 20th century as **Agama Hindu Dharma**. Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md).
> Sits entirely in the historical Kali Yuga — two clocks apply: **[traditional]** dating from *babad* chronicles and lontar texts vs **[scholarly]** dating from inscriptions and modern records; spine file [`recorded-history.md`](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md). Companion regional-tradition file: [`sankaradeva-and-assamese-vaishnavism.md`](sankaradeva-and-assamese-vaishnavism.md); for the Rāma-story's Indonesian career see [`southeast-asian-ramayanas.md`](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/recensions/southeast-asian-ramayanas.md). Everything here is **[regional]** by definition; tags below mark the further folk/oral/disputed layers.

## Transmission — from India to Java to Bali

- **Indianization of the archipelago [scholarly]** — Hindu (and Buddhist) religion, Sanskrit, and the Śaka calendar reached Java and Bali in the first millennium CE via traders, brahmins and court culture; Old Javanese *kakawin* poetry (including the *Kakawin Rāmāyaṇa*, c. 9th c.) carried the epics east — see [`southeast-asian-ramayanas.md`](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/recensions/southeast-asian-ramayanas.md).
- **Belanjong pillar, Sanur (914 CE)** — the earliest dated Balinese inscription: king **Śrī Kesarī Warmadewa** records victories, in Sanskrit and Old Balinese — first hard evidence of an Indic royal cult on Bali. (Source: Belanjong pillar inscription) [scholarly]
- **Mpu Kuturan and the kahyangan tiga (c. 11th c.)** — Balinese tradition credits the Javanese sage-minister **Mpu Kuturan** (era of king **Udayana** and his Javanese queen **Mahendradatta**) with organizing every village around **three temples** (see below) and integrating the island's sects into one village-dharma. (Source: Balinese lontar/babad tradition) [traditional]
- **Airlangga (r. c. 1019–1043)** — Bali-born son of Udayana and Mahendradatta, restorer of the East-Javanese kingdom; his reign is the narrative setting of the **Calon Arang** tale (below). (Source: Old Javanese inscriptions; *Calon Arang*) [scholarly]
- **Majapahit conquest of Bali (1343 CE)** — the Javanese empire's general **Gajah Mada** subdues Bali; Javanese court religion, caste usage (*triwangsa*), literature and temple style are grafted onto the older Balinese base. (Source: *Nāgarakṛtāgama*; Balinese babad) [scholarly]
- **Siwa-Buddha synthesis** — Majapahit religion held Śiva and Buddha as two faces of one truth; the *Kakawin Sutasoma* of **Mpu Tantular** (14th c.) states "**Bhinneka Tunggal Ika**" — "they are different, they are one" (of Buddha and Śiva) — the line that is now Indonesia's national motto. Bali inherits this as parallel **Śaiva and Bauddha priesthoods** within one religion. (Source: *Kakawin Sutasoma* 139.5) [scholarly]
- **Fall of Majapahit and the exodus (late 15th–early 16th c.)** — as Islamic sultanates (Demak) rose in Java, Majapahit's priests, poets, artisans and nobles migrated to Bali (traditional date-cluster c. 1478; final collapse by c. 1527 [scholarly]), making Bali the ark of Javano-Hindu civilization. (Source: babad chronicles) [scholarly]
- **Dang Hyang Nirartha (16th c.)** — the last great Javanese brahmin migrant (*Pedanda Sakti Wawu Rauh*, "the newly-arrived powerful priest"), arriving from Java c. **1537** [traditional dating] to the Gelgel court of **Dalem Baturenggong**. Credited with: reforming the **pedanda** high-priesthood (most *pedanda Siwa* lineages descend from him); instituting the **padmasana** shrine — the empty lotus-throne to the Supreme; and founding or consecrating the chain of **sea temples** (Tanah Lot, Rambut Siwi, Uluwatu, among others). Tradition holds he attained **mokṣa** at **Pura Luhur Uluwatu**, vanishing in light. (Source: *Babad Dwijendra* hagiography) [traditional] — his Buddhist counterpart **Dang Hyang Astapaka** heads the *pedanda Buda* lines. [traditional]

## Theology — Sang Hyang Widhi Wasa and the unseen

- **Sang Hyang Widhi Wasa** — "the Divine Orderer/Ruler": the one Supreme God of Balinese Hinduism, beyond form and conception, also called **Acintya** ("the unthinkable") — iconographically an **empty throne** (the padmasana) or a radiant figure ringed with flames. All gods are His manifestations. (Source: Balinese lontar theology; Parisada doctrine)
  - *Note [scholarly]:* the term's **prominence is modern** — it was foregrounded in the 1950s–60s campaign to have Balinese religion recognized under Indonesia's monotheist state ideology (Pancasila); the theology of a supreme Śiva-beyond-form is old (Śaiva-Siddhānta lontars), the branding is 20th-century.
- **Dewa and bhatara** — the pantheon continues the Indian one: **Brahmā, Wisnu, Siwa** as the Trimūrti (cross-ref [`trimurti-and-tridevi.md`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md)); Siwa also as **Batara Guru**; plus deified ancestors (*bhatara*) and localized gods — all understood as rays of Widhi. (Source: lontar *Bhuwana Kosa*, *Wrhaspati Tattwa*) [regional]
- **Śaiva-Siddhānta core [scholarly]** — the speculative lontars of Bali (*Bhuwana Kosa*, *Wrhaspati Tattwa*, *Ganapati Tattwa*) are Old-Javanese Śaiva-Siddhānta works: Śiva as supreme cause, the soul's bondage and release — the doctrinal cousin of the Indian Āgamic schools cataloged in [`shaiva-schools.md`](shaiva-schools.md) and [`../90-literature-corpus/agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md`](../90-literature-corpus/agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md).
- **Panca Sradha — the five beliefs** — the modern catechism of Agama Hindu Dharma: (1) **Brahman/Widhi** (one God), (2) **Ātma** (the soul), (3) **Karmaphala** (fruit of deeds), (4) **Punarbhawa** (rebirth), (5) **Mokṣa** (liberation). (Source: Parisada Hindu Dharma Indonesia doctrine) [scholarly]
- **Sekala–niskala** — reality is twinned: the **seen** (*sekala*) and the **unseen** (*niskala*); ritual keeps the two in traffic. Paired with **rwa bhineda**, the doctrine of necessary complementary opposites (the black-and-white *poleng* cloth) — the metaphysic behind the Barong–Rangda standoff below. (Source: Balinese ritual idiom) [regional]
- **Tri Hita Karana** — "the three causes of well-being": harmony with **God** (*parhyangan*), with **fellow humans** (*pawongan*), and with **nature** (*palemahan*) — the ethical charter cited for everything from temple layout to the subak system. (Source: Parisada-era formulation of older practice) [scholarly]
- **Desa-kala-patra** — "place, time, circumstance": the explicit Balinese principle that ritual and dharma flex by locality and occasion — why no two villages' rites match and none is "wrong." (Source: Balinese customary law, *adat*) [regional]

## Temples — kahyangan tiga and Besakih

- **Kahyangan tiga — the three village temples** — every *desa adat* maintains: **Pura Puseh** ("navel" temple, of origins, toward the mountain — Wisnu), **Pura Desa / Bale Agung** (village center — Brahmā), and **Pura Dalem** (temple of death, toward the sea, near the cremation ground — Siwa and **Durgā** in her fierce aspect). Institution ascribed to Mpu Kuturan (above). (Source: Balinese temple tradition) [traditional]
- **Kaja–kelod axis** — sacred direction runs mountain-ward (*kaja*, pure) to sea-ward (*kelod*, impure); **Gunung Agung** is the island's Meru, the fixed pole of orientation — compare [`meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md`](../06-sacred-geography/meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md). (Source: Balinese spatial custom) [regional]
- **Pura Besakih — the Mother Temple** — the great complex of some two dozen linked temples high on Gunung Agung's slope, centred on **Pura Penataran Agung**; the paramount sanctuary of all Bali, seat of state rituals. (Source: Balinese tradition; modern description) [regional]
- **Eka Dasa Rudra** — the greatest of all Balinese rites: a purification of the **eleven Rudras** (the whole cosmos) held at Besakih **once a century**. The 1963 performance was catastrophically interrupted when **Gunung Agung erupted during the ceremony** (killing over a thousand); the rite was performed again, completed, in **1979**. Read variously as divine wrath or wrong timing — the eruption sparing Besakih itself is told as a sign. (Source: documented history; Balinese interpretation) [scholarly] / [folk]
- **Sad kahyangan** — the "six great sanctuaries" said to steady the island — Besakih, Uluwatu, Lempuyang Luhur, Goa Lawah, Batukaru, and others; **the lists disagree from source to source** and more than six temples claim membership. (Source: Balinese temple lore) [disputed] [regional]

## Rice and water — Dewi Sri, Dewi Danu, and the subak

- **Dewi Sri** — the **rice goddess**, life of the paddy, honored in field-shrines and in the woven **cili** figure; in Balinese theology assimilated to **Śrī-Lakṣmī** as consort of Wisnu (cross-ref [`lakshmi.md`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/lakshmi.md)). (Source: Balinese-Javanese agrarian religion) [regional] [folk]
  - *Variant (Javanese/Sundanese folk cycle, e.g. Wawacan Sulanjana):* rice first sprouts **from the grave of a divine maiden** (Sri) slain or dying in heaven — a Southeast-Asian "grain-from-the-buried-goddess" myth with no Sanskrit-purāṇic source. [folk] [oral tradition]
- **Dewi Danu** — goddess of the **crater lakes**, above all **Lake Batur**, worshipped at **Pura Ulun Danu Batur** (and at Pura Ulun Danu Bratan): mistress of the irrigation waters, ranked in Batur's own tradition second only to the lord of Gunung Agung. (Source: Pura Ulun Danu Batur tradition) [regional]
- **Subak — the water-temple religion** — rice irrigation is run by **subak** cooperatives whose schedules are coordinated through a **hierarchy of water temples** rising from field-shrine to Ulun Danu Batur; anthropologist **J. Stephen Lansing** showed the temple network functions as a working ecological control system ("priests and programmers"). (Source: Lansing 1991) [scholarly]
- **UNESCO recognition (2012)** — the subak cultural landscape was inscribed as World Heritage explicitly **as a manifestation of Tri Hita Karana**. (Source: UNESCO listing) [scholarly]

## Calon Arang — Rangda vs Barong

- **The Calon Arang tale** — set in **Airlangga's** 11th-century Java: the widow-witch **Calon Arang of Girah (Dirah)**, enraged that none will marry her daughter **Ratna Manggali**, worships **Durgā** in the graveyard and unleashes plague on the kingdom. The sage **Mpu Bharadah** sends his disciple **Mpu Bahula** to wed Ratna Manggali and steal the witch's palm-leaf of power; Bharadah then defeats and (in most tellings) redeems/kills her. Oldest surviving Old-Javanese manuscript dated **1462 Śaka = 1540 CE** [scholarly]. (Source: *Calon Arang*, Old Javanese prose)
  - *Variant [folk] [disputed]:* Balinese lore identifies the widow-witch with the historical queen **Mahendradatta**, exiled for sorcery — an identification history does not support but the stage tradition keeps.
- **Rangda** — "the Widow": the fanged, flame-tongued, pendulous-breasted demon-queen of the **leyak** (witches), the Calon Arang figure fixed into a sacred mask; understood as the terrible face of **Durgā** (cross-ref [`devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md) and the tantric register in [`shakta-and-tantra-lineages.md`](shakta-and-tantra-lineages.md)). (Source: Balinese dance-drama tradition) [regional]
- **Barong** — the shaggy, lion-like protector-beast (**Barong Ket**, chief of several barong animal-forms), "king of the spirits," animated in folk etymology by the forest-lord **Banas Pati Raja**. His mask, like Rangda's, is a consecrated power-object kept in the Pura Dalem. (Source: Balinese dance-drama tradition) [regional] [folk]
- **The battle that never ends** — in the Barong-Rangda drama (and the Calon Arang exorcistic play), Barong and Rangda fight; entranced kris-dancers turn their blades on their own chests and are kept unharmed by Barong's power; **neither side wins** — the performance restores balance (*rwa bhineda*), it does not stage a victory of good over evil. (Source: Balinese performance tradition) [regional]

## The festival clocks — Nyepi, Galungan, and the two calendars

Bali runs **two calendars at once** — its own "two clocks": the Indian-derived **Śaka lunar year** (epoch 78 CE) and the 210-day Javanese-Balinese **pawukon** cycle; major feasts hang on each. (Cross-ref the Indian systems in [`hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md`](../00-time-and-cosmology/hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md); festival frame: [`festivals-and-vratas.md`](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md).)

- **Nyepi — the Day of Silence (Śaka New Year)** — the island stops: the **catur brata penyepian** forbids fire and light (*amati geni*), work (*amati karya*), travel (*amati lelungan*) and amusement (*amati lelanguan*); even the airport closes. (Source: Balinese observance) [regional]
- **Melasti and Tawur Kesanga** — in the days before Nyepi, temple images are carried to the sea for purification (**Melasti**); on New Year's Eve the great **bhūta-yajña** (**Tawur Kesanga**) feeds and expels the **bhuta-kala** demon-forces (cross-ref the demonic taxonomy in [`rakshasas.md`](../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md)). (Source: Balinese observance) [regional]
- **Ogoh-ogoh** — huge papier-mâché demon effigies paraded with noise on the eve (*pengrupukan*) and then burned, so the demons find a silent, "empty" island the next day. The monumental parade form is recent — it spread from the **1980s** — grafted onto the old exorcism. (Source: documented practice) [scholarly] [folk]
- **Galungan** — pawukon feast (every 210 days) of the **victory of dharma over adharma**; the deified **ancestors descend** to visit their family shrines, greeted with offerings and the bowed bamboo **penjor** poles lining every road. (Source: Balinese observance) [regional]
  - *Origin legend (Usana Bali cycle):* Galungan commemorates **Indra's defeat of the shape-shifting tyrant Mayadenawa** of Bedulu, who had banned worship; the spring **Tirta Empul** (Tampaksiring) was struck forth by Indra to revive his poisoned troops, and the river **Petanu** was cursed by the tyrant's blood. (cross-ref [`indra.md`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/indra.md)) [regional] [folk]
- **Kuningan** — ten days after Galungan, the ancestors **return above**; yellow rice (*nasi kuning*) gives the day its name. (Source: Balinese observance) [regional]
- **Odalan** — every temple keeps its consecration-anniversary each 210-day pawukon year — thousands of temple birthdays, the religion's everyday pulse; **Saraswati day** honors the goddess of the lontar books. (Source: Balinese observance) [regional]

## Death, priesthood, and the Javanese remnant

- **Ngaben — cremation as liberation** — the dead are cremated (often after temporary burial): borne in a tiered **bade/wadah** tower, spun at crossroads to confuse the spirit, burned in an animal **sarcophagus** (the bull *lembu* for high castes), freeing the **ātma** toward rebirth; later rites (*nyekah/memukur*) refine the soul into a deified ancestor of the house-temple, ashes going to the sea. (Source: Balinese mortuary tradition; cross-ref Indian ancestor-rites in [`shraddha-and-pitr-rites.md`](../05-concepts-and-dharma/shraddha-and-pitr-rites.md)) [regional]
- **Pedanda — the high priests** — brahmin (*Brahmana*) high priests in two lineages, **pedanda Siwa** (from Nirartha) and **pedanda Buda** (from Astapaka). The pedanda's daily **sūrya-sevana** liturgy — mudrās, mantras, and bell — culminates in the priest **becoming Śiva for the rite** and thereby making **tirtha** (holy water), the indispensable sacrament of every Balinese ceremony ("the religion of holy water," *agama tirtha*). (Source: C. Hooykaas, *Surya Sevana*, documenting the liturgy) [scholarly] [regional]
- **Pemangku** — the far more numerous commoner temple-priests keeping the daily cult of village and clan temples, alongside consecrated *dalang* puppeteers and *balian* healers. (Source: Balinese practice) [regional]
- **The Tengger remnant [regional] [folk]** — in the highlands around **Mount Bromo** (from *Brahmā*), East Java, the **Tengger** people — claiming Majapahit descent — never converted to Islam. At the yearly **Yadnya Kasada** they cast offerings into the live crater, redeeming the legend of their founders **Roro Anteng and Joko Seger** (whence "Tengger"), whose prayed-for twenty-five children were granted on condition the youngest, **Raden Kusuma**, be given back to the volcano — he entered the crater, asking to be honored ever after. Their non-caste priests (*dukun pandita*) were formally folded under Agama Hindu in the 20th century. (Source: Tengger oral tradition; modern ethnography) [oral tradition] [scholarly]
- **Modern reorganization [scholarly]** — under the independent Indonesian state (which recognizes only scripture-based monotheisms), Balinese leaders won recognition of **Agama Hindu Bali (1958)**, founded the **Parisada Hindu Dharma** council (**1959**, later *Parisada Hindu Dharma Indonesia*), codified the Panca Sradha, and re-linked Bali to Indian Hinduism; the umbrella later gathered Tengger, Javanese, and other Indonesian Hindu revivals. (Source: modern historiography, e.g. Michel Picard's studies)
