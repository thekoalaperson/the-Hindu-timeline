# Nāmdev (Nāmadeva) — The Pan-Indian Kīrtana Sant

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](./README.md) (master index: [`../README.md`](../README.md)). The 13th–14th-c. calico-printer / tailor (**śimpī**) **sant** of **Pandharpur**, co-founder of the Marathi **Vārkarī** *kīrtana* tradition, contemporary of [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md), and the rare bhakta venerated **both** in the southern saguṇa Viṭṭhal-cult and the northern nirguṇa Sant world.
> Frame against the lineage roster and the Nirguṇa/Saguṇa divide in [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md). Two clocks: this is **academic / historical** Kali-Yuga dating, not Purāṇic yuga-time — and even the historical clock here is contested ("the two Nāmdevs").

## Who He Was

- **Nāmdev (Nāmadeva, Nāmā, Nāmdeo)** — the **śimpī** (tailor / calico-printer / cloth-dyer) **sant** of **Pandharpur** on the Bhīmā (Candrabhāgā), an ecstatic singer of **Viṭṭhal / Viṭhobā / Pāṇḍuraṅga**, and — with [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md) — a **co-founder of the Vārkarī *kīrtana* tradition**. Unusually for an early Marathi sant, his fame and verse spread far beyond Maharashtra, into the Hindi/Punjabi north. (Source: Wikipedia, *Namdev*; sanatanhindudharma.org)
  - *Caste [scholarly]:* born into the **śimpī** (tailor) artisan community, conventionally a low/"Śūdra" rank — his low birth, like Tukārām's and Ravidās's, is integral to the egalitarian charter of the movement. (Source: Wikipedia, *Namdev*)
  - *Family [folk/traditional]:* son of **Dāmāśeṭī** and **Goṇāī**, with wife **Rājāī** and a household of children; the maidservant **[Janābāī](janabai.md)** lived and sang in his house and became a celebrated abhaṅga-poet in her own right, addressing him as **Nāmā** in her verse. (Source: Wikipedia, *Namdev*, *Janabai*)

## Dating — the "Two Nāmdevs" Problem [disputed]

- **Conventional span — c. 1270–1350 CE** — the most cited modern dates, making him a near-exact contemporary of [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md) (c. 1275–1296) and outliving him by decades. (Source: Wikipedia, *Namdev*)
  - *Variant (traditional hagiography):* **1270–1350**, a long life of ~80 years spanning the Yādava twilight and early Delhi-Sultanate Deccan.
- **The "two Nāmdevs" hypothesis [disputed/scholarly]** — because the **Marathi Viṭṭhal-abhaṅgas** and the **Hindi/Marwari nirguṇa hymns** differ so sharply in idiom and theology, some scholars have proposed **two distinct poets** later conflated under one name (e.g. a 13th–14th-c. Pandharpur Nāmdev and a later northern "Nāmdev"). Others defend a **single, widely-travelled poet** whose register shifted between southern and northern audiences. Catalog both; do not collapse. (Source: Wikipedia, *Namdev*; academic Sant-literature scholarship)
- **Textual-criticism caveat [scholarly]** — as with Kabīr and Tukārām, the **authentic core vs. later accretion** is unresolved; the corpus attributed to "Nāmdev" is an **open roster**, and attribution drove the very "two Nāmdevs" debate.

## Pandharpur & Viṭṭhal-Bhakti — the Saguṇa Side

- **Viṭṭhal-devotion at Pandharpur** — Nāmdev's primary religious world is the **saguṇa** worship of **Viṭhobā** (Kṛṣṇa/Viṣṇu standing arms-akimbo on a brick), the deity, town and Puṇḍalīka-legend cataloged in [`../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md`](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md). His abhaṅgas address Viṭṭhal as intimate friend, mother and lord. (Source: Wikipedia, *Namdev*, *Vithoba*)
- **The kīrtana co-founder** — Nāmdev is traditionally credited as a **founder of the Vārkarī performative *kīrtana*** — communal call-and-response singing of the divine name (*nāma-saṅkīrtana*), the devotional engine of the whole movement and the practice his very name ("he of the Name") came to embody. (Source: Wikipedia, *Warkari*; sanatanhindudharma.org)
- **Partnership with Jñāneśvar [traditional]** — hagiography pairs Nāmdev and [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md) as close companions who toured pilgrimage sites together; Nāmdev is said to have composed verses memorializing Jñāneśvar's *sañjīvana samādhi* at Āḷandī (1296) and his own grief after. (Source: Wikipedia, *Namdev*, *Dnyaneshwar*) [folk]

## The Legends [folk]

- **The temple that turned** — the most famous Nāmdev miracle: at a shrine (often told at **Auṇḍhā Nāgnāth**), priests barred the low-caste tailor from the front; Nāmdev sang from behind, and **the temple itself revolved** to face him. The story is the tradition's charter-myth of bhakti overriding caste-gatekeeping. (Source: Wikipedia, *Namdev*; Vārkarī hagiography)
- **God who ate from his hand / drank the milk** — child-Nāmdev, sent to offer milk/food to the image, is said to have **made Viṭṭhal physically accept it**, taking the literalism of a child's love as true *darśana*. (Source: Vārkarī hagiography) [folk]
- **The guru Viṣobā Khecara** — despite his ecstatic Viṭṭhal-love, Nāmdev is given a **living guru, Viṣobā Khecara** (a Nāth-influenced teacher), who corrects his notion that the divine is confined to the Pandharpur image — teaching the **all-pervading formless** presence, a hinge that helps explain his later nirguṇa register. (Source: Wikipedia, *Namdev*) [traditional]

## The Northern Journey & the Nirguṇa Side

- **Travels to the Punjab/north [traditional]** — Nāmdev is remembered as having journeyed far north, spending years in the **Punjab** (the village **Ghuman**, Gurdaspur district, claims him and holds his shrine); this northern sojourn is the bridge by which a Marathi Viṭṭhal-singer entered the **Sant-mat** world. (Source: Wikipedia, *Namdev*)
- **~61 hymns in the Guru Granth Sāhib [scholarly]** — about **sixty-one compositions** attributed to **"Nāmdev"** in **Hindi/Marwari/old-Hindavī** are enshrined in the Sikh **[Guru Granth Sāhib](../90-literature-corpus/README.md)**, among the *bhagat-bāṇī* of non-Sikh saints; these are predominantly **nirguṇa** in idiom — the formless, name-beyond-name absolute, anti-ritual and anti-caste — rather than Pandharpur saguṇa Viṭṭhal-worship. (Source: Wikipedia, *Namdev*; Guru Granth Sāhib)
- **The clearest straddler of the Nirguṇa/Saguṇa line [disputed]** — Nāmdev is the **textbook case** of a single poetic identity sitting on **both sides** of bhakti's doctrinal fault-line: the **saguṇa Viṭṭhal-bhakta** of the Marathi south and the **nirguṇa Sant** of the Hindi north. This duality is exactly what fuels the "two Nāmdevs" debate and the caveat (in [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md)) that the nirguṇa/saguṇa divide is a heuristic, not a wall. (Source: Wikipedia, *Namdev*, *Sant (religion)*)

## His Works

- **The Marathi abhaṅgas** — Nāmdev's Marathi *abhaṅgas* and *kīrtana*-songs form, **together with [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md)'s *Jñāneśvarī* and the [Tukārām](tukaram.md) Gāthā, the core scriptural canon of the Vārkarī tradition** — the movement's authority resting on the saints' own vernacular poetry rather than a Sanskrit *guru-paramparā*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Warkari*, *Namdev*)
  - *Count [disputed]:* the authentic-core-vs-accretion question applies; "several thousand" abhaṅgas circulate under his name, of which the genuine corpus is smaller and unfixed — open roster. [scholarly]
- **The Hindi/Marwari hymns (Gurū Granth)** — the ~61 northern compositions, in a register continuous with **Kabīr** and **Ravidās**, the **largest single bridge** between Maharashtra's Viṭṭhal-bhakti and the north-Indian Sant corpus. (Source: Guru Granth Sāhib; Wikipedia, *Namdev*)
- *Note [scholarly]:* the **language gap itself** — fluent Marathi abhaṅga on one hand, Hindavī/Marwari nirguṇa hymn on the other — is the primary evidence both for a single widely-travelled poet **and** for the "two Nāmdevs" split; the file keeps both readings live.

## Place in the Succession

- **Jñāneśvar → Nāmdev → Eknāth → [Tukārām](tukaram.md)** — the canonical Vārkarī four-saint sequence (see [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md)). Nāmdev is the **second pillar**: the singer who turned Jñāneśvar's philosophy into communal *kīrtana* and carried the movement's name far beyond Marathi country. (Source: Wikipedia, *Warkari*)
- **The household line** — the maidservant **[Janābāī](janabai.md)**, Nāmdev's contemporary in his own home, is the most celebrated of the early women Vārkarī poets; her abhaṅgas frequently name **Nāmā** and embed her within his family's devotion. (Source: Wikipedia, *Janabai*, *Namdev*)
- **Pan-Indian afterlife** — venerated as **Sant Nāmdev** across Maharashtra and as **Bhagat Nāmdev** in Sikh and Punjabi tradition (with a shrine at **Ghuman**); his cross-regional, cross-tradition reach is unmatched among the early Vārkarī sants. (Source: Wikipedia, *Namdev*)
