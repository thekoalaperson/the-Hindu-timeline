# Āṇḍāḷ — the Only Female Ālvār and the Tiruppāvai

*Sits under [07-acharyas-and-sampradayas](./README.md). See the broader saint waves in [alvars-and-nayanars.md](./alvars-and-nayanars.md); literary corpus context in [../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md](../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md). Master index: [../README.md](../README.md).*

Āṇḍāḷ (Tamil *Āṇḍāḷ*, "she who rules [the Lord]"; birth-name **Kōdai / Gōdā**, "garland"; honorific **Nācciyār**, "the Lady") is the **only woman among the twelve Ālvārs** and the only female author in the *Nālāyira Divya Prabandham* (the "Tamil Veda"). Foster-daughter of the Ālvār **Periyāḻvār (Viṣṇucittar)** of Srivilliputhur, she composed two works — the **Tiruppāvai** (30 verses) and the **Nācciyār Tirumoḻi** (143 verses) — that together fuse *madhura/bridal bhakti* with the Mārgaḻi-month vow tradition. Worshipped as an incarnation of **Bhū-devī** and as the model bride of Viṣṇu, she is the only Ālvār enshrined and ritually married to the deity in living temple cult. **Dating note:** Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition assigns her birth to **785 CE** (Āḍi month, Pūram star) [traditional]; academic consensus places her hymns in the **9th–10th c. CE** [scholarly]; legendary Vaishnava chronology pushing the Ālvārs into the Dvāpara/Kali yugas is [disputed].

---

## Names and identity

- **Kōdai / Gōdā** - birth-name meaning "garland" (Sanskrit *Gōdā* = "she who gives a garland / gives cattle/song"); the foundational name behind her garland-legend epithet. (Source: [Andal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal))
- **Āṇḍāḷ** - the name by which she is universally known, glossed "she who rules" — i.e. she who, by her devotion, "rules over" or wins the Lord. (Source: [Andal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal))
- **Nācciyār** - "the Lady / consort"; honorific marking her status as bride of Viṣṇu, used in her work-title *Nācciyār Tirumoḻi*. (Source: [Nachiyar Tirumoli — Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Nachiyar_Tirumoli))
- **Sūḍikkoḍutta Nācciyār** - "the Lady who gave [garlands] after wearing them," her cult-epithet from the garland legend (see below). (Source: [Andal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal))
- **Bhū-devī incarnation** - revered as an *avatāra* of **Bhū-devī / Bhū-Lakṣmī**, the Earth-goddess consort of Viṣṇu — fitting both her discovery *in the earth/garden* and her status as the Lord's bride. (Source: [Andal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal))
  - *Variant (Bhū vs Lakṣmī):* some sources call her an incarnation of **Bhū-Lakṣmī** specifically, others of **Śrī/Lakṣmī** more broadly, or of both as aspects of the Goddess. [folk]
- **Place in the twelve:** counted among the canonical twelve Ālvārs; some older listings of **ten** Ālvārs treat her (with Madhurakavi) as appended/honorary — Āṇḍāḷ being a goddess rather than a "core" hymnist. [folk] (Source: see [alvars-and-nayanars.md](./alvars-and-nayanars.md))

## Birth, discovery, and the garland legend

- **Foster-father Periyāḻvār (Viṣṇucittar)** - Brahmin garland-maker and temple-servant of Srivilliputhur, famed for *vātsalya bhakti* (loving Viṣṇu as the child Kṛṣṇa) and his benediction *Tiruppallāṇḍu*; childless, he raised the foundling girl as his own. See his entry in [alvars-and-nayanars.md](./alvars-and-nayanars.md). (Source: [Periyalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periyalvar))
- **Discovery beneath the tulasī** - Periyāḻvār found the infant girl under (or in) a **tulasī (holy basil) plant** in the temple flower-garden (*nandavanam*) of the Vaṭapatraśāyī Viṣṇu temple at Srivilliputhur; he named her Kōdai and reared her in deep Kṛṣṇa-devotion. The earth-born discovery underwrites her identification with **Bhū-devī**. (Source: [Andal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal); [Srivilliputhur Andal Temple — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivilliputhur_Andal_Temple))
- **Birth date (traditional)** - Āḍi month, **Pūram** *nakṣatra*, **785 CE** per Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition; celebrated as **Āḍi Pūram**, her birth-festival. [traditional] (Source: [Andal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal))
- **The garland legend — "Sūḍikkoḍutta Nācciyār"** - Kōdai secretly **wore the flower-garlands** her father strung for the temple deity, gazing at her reflection as the Lord's bride, before they were offered. Periyāḻvār, finding a stray hair, was horrified at the "polluted" offering and withheld the garland. That night **Viṣṇu appeared in his dream**, declaring he *preferred* the garlands already worn by Kōdai — that her wearing sanctified rather than defiled them. Thereafter the worn garland was offered daily, earning her the epithet *Sūḍikkoḍutta Nācciyār*, "she who gave after wearing." (Source: [Andal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal); [Andal — Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Andal))
  - *Variant (the discovered hair):* the "flaw" Periyāḻvār noticed is variously a strand of hair, the imprint of her wearing, or simply his knowledge that she had worn it. [folk]

## The Tiruppāvai — the Mārgaḻi / Dhanurmāsa hymn

The **Tiruppāvai** ("the sacred *pāvai* [vow/song]") is Āṇḍāḷ's 30-verse (*pāsuram*) garland of hymns, the most popular and most-recited portion of the *Nālāyira Divya Prabandham*. Composed c. 9th–10th c. CE [scholarly], it is sung throughout the Tamil month of **Mārgaḻi** (mid-Dec to mid-Jan), one verse per day. (Source: [Tiruppavai — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruppavai))

- **The Pāvai nōnbu (vow)** - the poem dramatizes the *pāvai nōṉbu*, an old Tamil rite in which **unmarried girls** rise before dawn in Mārgaḻi, bathe, and undertake a month-long penance/vow to win a good husband — here transposed so the longed-for bridegroom is **Kṛṣṇa/Viṣṇu** himself. Each of the 30 days is named for one *pāsuram*. (Source: [Tiruppavai — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruppavai))
- **Mārgaḻi = Dhanurmāsa** - the month is the **Dhanurmāsa** (sun in Sagittarius/*Dhanus*), considered the most auspicious for Viṣṇu-worship; the Gītā's "of months I am Mārgaśīrṣa" is invoked. Pre-dawn (*Brāhma-muhūrta*) recitation of the Tiruppāvai is the signature ritual of Śrīvaiṣṇava temples in this month. (Source: [Tiruppavai — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruppavai))
- **Persona — the *gopī* / cowherd-girl** - Āṇḍāḷ casts herself and her companions as **gopīs of Āyarpāḍi (Gokula)**, waking one another to go bathe and worship the child-cowherd Kṛṣṇa — fusing the Mārgaḻi vow with Bhāgavata *Kṛṣṇa-līlā*. See Kṛṣṇa material in [../04-deep-dives](../04-deep-dives) and [../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md). (Source: [Tiruppavai — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruppavai))

### Internal structure (30 pāsurams)

- **Verses 1–5 — invocation & resolve** - announce the vow, the auspicious month, and the disciplines of the *nōṉbu* (purity, simple food, renunciation), invoking Nārāyaṇa as the boon-giver. (Source: [Tiruppavai — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruppavai))
- **Verses 6–15 — waking the maidens** - the "awakening" decad: Āṇḍāḷ rouses ten sleeping *gopī* companions one by one (the famous *"Elē iḷam kiḷiyē..."* call-and-answer), an allegory of awakening *jīvas* to devotion. (Source: [Tiruppavai — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruppavai))
- **Verses 16–20 — waking the Lord's household** - the girls reach Nanda's palace and wake the **gatekeepers, Nappinnai (Kṛṣṇa's beloved/Nīḷā-devī), Baladeva, and Kṛṣṇa** himself, pleading entry. (Source: [Tiruppavai — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruppavai))
- **Verses 21–29 — praise & surrender (prapatti)** - extolling Kṛṣṇa's glory and surrendering wholly, asking only for *eternal service* (*kaiṅkarya*) to him. (Source: [Tiruppavai — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruppavai))
- **Verse 30 — *phala-śruti*** - the fruit-declaration and authorial signature: "Kōdai of Viṣṇucittar of Putuvai (Srivilliputhur)" — whoever recites these gains the Lord's grace. (Source: [Tiruppavai — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruppavai))
- **Theological status** - Śrīvaiṣṇava ācāryas call the Tiruppāvai the **"seed of the Vedas"** (*Vedam anaittukkum vittu*) when expounded by a guru; it encodes *artha-pañcaka* and the doctrine of *prapatti* (surrender). [scholarly] (Source: [Tiruppavai — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruppavai))

## The Nācciyār Tirumoḻi — bridal mysticism and the dream-wedding

The **Nācciyār Tirumoḻi** ("the Lady's Sacred Sayings") is Āṇḍāḷ's longer, more emotionally raw work — **143 verses in 14 decads (*tirumoḻi*)** — of intense, sometimes anguished *bridal mysticism* (*nāyikā-bhāva*: the soul as bride pining for the Lord). (Source: [Nachiyar Tirumoli — Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Nachiyar_Tirumoli); [Andal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal))
  - *Variant (verse count):* commonly given as **143 verses** (14 decads); some summaries round it to ~140. [folk]

- **Decad 1 — Kāma / Manmatha vow** - Āṇḍāḷ invokes **Kāma-deva (Manmatha)**, performing a vow and sandcastle-building rite, begging the love-god to unite her with Viṣṇu — a daring inversion of the usual ascetic devotion. (Source: [Nachiyar Tirumoli — Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Nachiyar_Tirumoli))
- **The "Vāraṇam Āyiram" dream-wedding (decad 6)** - the celebrated decad in which Āṇḍāḷ **dreams her marriage to Viṣṇu** in vivid Tamil-Vedic detail: a thousand elephants in procession, the groom's arrival, the tying of the *tāli* (wedding pendant), *agni* circumambulation, and the *grhya* rites — step by ritual step. This is the textual core of her "dream-wedding" identity. (Source: [Andal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal); [Goda Krishna Kalyanam — JKYog](https://www.jkyog.org/blog/story-of-goda-krishna-kalyanam/))
- **Messenger and lament motifs** - other decads send **cuckoos, clouds, and conch (Pāñcajanya)** as love-messengers to Viṣṇu, and voice the pangs of separation (*viraha*) — classical *akam* (love-poetry) conventions turned theological. (Source: [Nachiyar Tirumoli — Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Nachiyar_Tirumoli))
- **Naming the bridegroom** - Āṇḍāḷ fixes her love on specific Viṣṇu shrines/forms — **Veṅkaṭa (Tirumala), Raṅganātha (Srirangam), Vaṭapatraśāyī (Srivilliputhur)** — refusing any earthly groom; this shrine-specific longing sets up the marriage legend. (Source: [Andal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal))

## The marriage to Raṅganātha and the merging legend

- **Refusal of mortal marriage** - grown to marriageable age, Kōdai declares she will wed **only Viṣṇu**, never a mortal; Periyāḻvār despairs of finding such a groom. (Source: [Andal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal))
- **The twin dream-command** - **Raṅganātha** (the reclining Viṣṇu of **Srirangam**) appears in dreams to *both* Periyāḻvār and the Srirangam temple priests, commanding that **Āṇḍāḷ be brought to Srirangam in bridal attire** to be wed to him. (Source: [Where Heaven Meets Earth — Om Spiritual Shop](https://www.omspiritualshop.com/blogs/news/where-heaven-meets-earth-the-goddess-s-divine-wedding-with-lord-ranganathan); [Andal — Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Andal))
- **The merging (Goda-Kalyāṇam)** - borne in palanquin to Srirangam and dressed as a bride, Āṇḍāḷ enters the sanctum, ascends to **Raṅganātha's couch (Ādiśeṣa)**, and **merges bodily into the deity in a burst of light**, vanishing — the literal consummation of her bridal devotion and a paradigm of *jīva–Brahman* union (*sāyujya mokṣa*). [traditional] (Source: [Andal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal); [Goda Krishna Kalyanam — JKYog](https://www.jkyog.org/blog/story-of-goda-krishna-kalyanam/))
  - *Variant (groom's form):* the divine groom is named **Raṅganātha** of Srirangam in the dominant legend; some popular retellings frame him as **Kṛṣṇa** (the *Goda Krishna Kalyāṇam*), consistent with the Tiruppāvai's Kṛṣṇa-persona. [folk]
  - *Variant (Periyāḻvār's recognition):* upon the merging, Raṅganātha is said to address Periyāḻvār as **father-in-law (*māmā*)**, formally making the human Ālvār the Lord's kin — a motif celebrated in temple liturgy. [folk]
- **Living ritual** - the wedding is re-enacted annually as the **Āṇḍāḷ / Goda Tirukkalyāṇam**, and her cult-form *Sūḍikkoḍutta Nācciyār* receives the garland worn each day before it is offered to the deity, recapitulating the garland legend. (Source: [Srivilliputhur Andal Temple — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivilliputhur_Andal_Temple))

## The Srivilliputhur cult and worship

- **Srivilliputhur (Putuvai) Āṇḍāḷ Temple** - her home shrine in Virudhunagar district, Tamil Nadu, twinned around **Vaṭapatraśāyī (Viṣṇu reclining on the banyan-leaf)** and **Āṇḍāḷ**; birthplace of both Periyāḻvār and Āṇḍāḷ. Its towering **gopuram (~59 m / ~192 ft, 11 tiers)** is the emblem on the **Tamil Nadu state government seal**. (Source: [Srivilliputhur Andal Temple — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivilliputhur_Andal_Temple); [Incredible India — Srivilliputtur](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/tamil-nadu/madurai/srivilliputtur-andal-temple))
- **Worshipped as Bhū-devī / consort** - across Śrīvaiṣṇava temples Āṇḍāḷ is enshrined as a *consort-goddess*, often the only Ālvār given a separate sanctum and *utsava* (festival) marriage to the Lord. (Source: [Andal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal))
- **Festivals** - **Āḍi Pūram** (her birth, Tamil month Āḍi); the **Mārgaḻi** month-long Tiruppāvai recitation culminating in pre-dawn worship; and the **Āṇḍāḷ Tirukkalyāṇam** wedding festival. (Source: [Andal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal))
- **Pan-Indian reach** - via the *Āḻvār* canon and Rāmānuja's [Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya](./ramanuja-and-madhva.md), her Tiruppāvai is sung well beyond Tamil country; the Telugu poet-king **Kṛṣṇadevarāya** retold her life in the *Āmuktamālyada* ("she who gives a worn garland"), an emperor's literary tribute. [scholarly] (Source: [Amuktamalyada — Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Amuktamalyada))

## Significance and cross-links

- **Only woman in the Tamil Veda** - Āṇḍāḷ's two works sit inside the *Nālāyira Divya Prabandham* compiled by **Nāthamuni**; she is the sole female voice in the corpus, a rare canonical woman-author in Indian sacred literature. See [alvars-and-nayanars.md](./alvars-and-nayanars.md) and [../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md](../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md). [scholarly]
- **Madhura/bridal bhakti pioneer** - her *nāyikā-bhāva* anticipates later North-Indian women bhaktas (e.g. **Mīrābāī** — see [north-bhakti-sants.md](./north-bhakti-sants.md)) who likewise take Kṛṣṇa as bridegroom. [scholarly]
- **Open-ended roster note:** verse counts, exact decad themes of the *Nācciyār Tirumoḻi*, and the precise wording of the merging legend vary across temple traditions and translators (e.g. Venkatesan's *The Secret Garland*); the rosters above catalog the dominant Śrīvaiṣṇava forms and flagged variants, not an exhaustive set.
