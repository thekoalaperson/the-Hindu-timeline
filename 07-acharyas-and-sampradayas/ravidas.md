# Ravidās (Raidās / Guru Ravidāss) — The Camār Nirguṇa Sant

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](README.md) — a standalone deep-dive on **Ravidās / Raidās / Guru Ravidāss**, the **Camār (leather-worker / Dalit)** nirguṇa sant-poet of Vārāṇasī, his casteless and ritual-free *nirbhau* devotion, his utopian city **Begampura**, his ~40 hymns in the **Gurū Granth Sāhib**, the disciple-legends binding [Mīrābāī](mirabai.md) to him, and his foundational status for the modern **Ravidāssia** religion and Dalit self-assertion. ## Who he was — the cobbler-saint of Kāśī

- **Ravidās (Raidās / Rohidās / Rūīdās / Guru Ravidāss)** — 15th–16th-c. **nirguṇa sant** and poet of **Vārāṇasī (Kāśī/Banāras)**, by birth a **Camār** (the leather-working caste, ranked "untouchable" / Dalit under the varṇa-jāti order). He worked leather and made and mended shoes his whole life — the **cobbler's awl, hide and last** recur in his verse as Kabīr's loom does in his — and never abandoned the trade his caste was despised for, making his open profession of his "low" caste itself an act of devotional defiance. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*; Britannica, *Ravidas*)
- **The name** — *Ravi-dās* / *Rai-dās* ("servant of the Sun/Lord"); his followers honour him as **Guru Ravidāss / Satguru**, not merely a poet. Regional forms include **Rohidās** (Marathi/Gujarati) and **Rūīdās**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*)
- **Theology in one line** — the divine is **nirguṇa** (formless, imageless, beyond caste and rite), found **within**; Ravidās names this absolute **"Rām," "Hari," "Govinda," "Mādho"** — name-beyond-name, not the Daśaratha-son [Rāma](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/README.md). Devotion is *nirbhau* (**fearless**) surrender that dissolves the very distinctions of high and low birth. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*; north-bhakti-sants.md)
- **Language** — composed in **Sant-bhāṣā**: a mixed eastern-Hindi / Braj / Khaṛī-bolī register shared with the Sant-mat stream, sung rather than written by the poet himself. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*) [scholarly]

## Dating — the disputed life [disputed]

The two clocks diverge; no contemporary documentary anchor survives, and the figures rest on hagiography and sectarian *jayantī* reckoning.

- **Traditional / commonly-cited span [traditional]:** **c. 1450–1520 CE** (often given **1377–1540** in some devotional reckonings, an implausibly long span). The **Guru Ravidāss Jayantī** is celebrated on the **full moon of Māgha (Māgh Pūrṇimā)**, the traditional birth-tithi. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*; Grokipedia, *Guru Ravidass Jayanti*)
- *Variant — scholarly window [scholarly]:* a working life roughly **late 15th to early/mid 16th century**, contemporary with [Kabīr](kabir.md) and overlapping the early Sikh Gurūs — fixed less by record than by his presence in the dated **Gurū Granth Sāhib** corpus (compiled 1604). [scholarly]
- **Birthplace** — **Seer Govardhanpur**, a village on the edge of **Vārāṇasī**, today the site of the great **Shri Guru Ravidass Janam Asthan** temple (the principal Ravidāssia pilgrimage centre). (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*; Grokipedia)

## Birth, family and the Brāhmaṇa-challenge legends [folk]

- **Camār parentage [folk]** — tradition names his father **Santokh Dās (Raghu)** and mother **Kalsā (Ghurbinia)**, leather-workers of Seer Govardhanpur; he is said to have married **Lonā (Lonā Devī)** and continued the family cobbler's trade even after his fame. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*; Grokipedia)
- **The "past-life Brāhmaṇa" tale [folk]** — a Bhaktamāl-tradition legend has Ravidās born a Camār as the karmic residue of a former Brāhmaṇa life — a hagiographic device that simultaneously honours and *explains away* his caste, asserting inner purity beneath outward "lowness." [folk] [disputed]
- **The thread / floating-stone miracles [folk]** — popular cycle dramatizing his transcendence of ritual purity: challenged by Brāhmaṇas who scorn an untouchable's worship, Ravidās produces the sacred **janeū (thread)** or **śālagrāma** from within his own chest; in another, his simple offering causes a **stone to float** while the Brāhmaṇas' offerings sink — the heart's devotion outweighing birth and rite. (Source: Grokipedia; popular hagiography) [folk]
- **The Gaṅgā-and-the-bangle tale [folk]** — busy at his cobbler's bench, Ravidās declines a pilgrimage to bathe in the Gaṅgā with the words **"Man changā to kaṭhautī meṅ Gaṅgā"** ("if the heart is pure, the Ganges is in my [cobbler's] water-tub") — his single most-quoted saying, a manifesto that inner purity, not sacred geography, is what counts. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*; popular tradition) [folk]

## The Rāmānanda-discipleship question [disputed]

- **The tradition** — Ravidās is remembered, like [Kabīr](kabir.md#the-rāmānanda-discipleship-question-disputed), as one of the disciples of **[Svāmī Rāmānanda](north-bhakti-sants.md#rāmānanda--the-root-of-the-north-indian-movement)** of Vārāṇasī, whose acceptance of *both* saguṇa and nirguṇa Brahman is the standard explanation for his nirguṇa devotion and for his place in the "twelve disciples" roster of the *Bhaktamāl*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*, *Ramananda*)
- **The scholarly doubt [scholarly]** — the same skepticism that attaches to the Rāmānanda–Kabīr link applies here: the chronological strain, the **near-total absence of Rāmānanda from the disciples' own attested verse**, and the sectarian utility of a single discipular network all suggest the tie is, at least in part, **later hagiographic construction**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramananda*) [disputed]

## Begampura — the city without sorrow

- **Begampura ("the land without sorrow")** — Ravidās's great social image and the most celebrated utopia of north-Indian bhakti: from Persian *beġam* ("without grief"), a city where **there is no second or third rank, no sorrow, no taxation (*khirāj*), no fear, no property loss, no anxiety** — *"That city my friend is called Begampura, a place with neither pain nor fear."* (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*; *Begampura* hymn in the Gurū Granth Sāhib)
- **An egalitarian polity, not just a heaven** — uniquely among the sants, Ravidās frames liberation as a **just city**: all dwell as equals, *"none are second or third, all are one,"* none subordinated by caste or wealth, freedom of movement guaranteed. Scholars (notably **Gail Omvedt**, who titled a study *Seeking Begumpura*) read it as a **social-political vision of casteless equality**, not merely an otherworldly mokṣa. (Source: Gail Omvedt, *Seeking Begumpura*; Wikipedia, *Ravidas*) [scholarly]
- **Why it matters** — Begampura makes Ravidās the bhakti tradition's most direct **anti-caste social visionary**, and is the textual heart of his modern reclamation as a forefather of **Dalit assertion** (below). (Source: Omvedt; Grokipedia) [scholarly]

## The poetry — ~40 hymns in the Gurū Granth Sāhib

- **The Sikh corpus** — **~40–41 hymns (śabads) of Ravidās are enshrined in the [Gurū Granth Sāhib](../90-literature-corpus/README.md)**, compiled by **Gurū Arjan Dev in 1604**; he is one of the **bhagats** (non-Sikh saint-contributors) honoured in the Sikh scripture alongside Kabīr. This is the **earliest dated written corpus** of his verse and the most secure text-critical anchor for both his words and his life. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*; learnreligions.com)
- **The Bhaktamāl tradition** — Ravidās features in **Nābhādās's *Bhaktamāl* (c. 1600)** and is elaborated in **Priyādās's 1712 commentary** — the same hagiographic stream that built the lives of Mīrā and the other sants; his miracle-tales (above) largely descend from this layer. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*, *Bhaktamal*) [scholarly]
- **The Dādū-panthī / Pañc-vāṇī witness** — his verse also survives in the **Rajasthani *Pañc-vāṇī*** anthologies of the **Dādū Panth** (which collect five Sants: Dādū, Kabīr, Nāmdev, Haridās and **Raidās**) — a second independent recension beside the Sikh one. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*; Sant-corpus scholarship) [scholarly]
- *The text-critical caution [scholarly]:* like Kabīr and Mīrā, Ravidās **wrote nothing himself**; his name is an open umbrella over an orally-transmitted corpus written down generations later in disagreeing streams. The **Gurū Granth Sāhib** hymns are the firmest core; later Ravidāssia and folk compositions accreted around it. Every quotation should be tagged by its recension. (Source: cf. [`kabir.md`](kabir.md#the-three-recensions--three-kabīrs-scholarly)) [scholarly]

## The disciple-legends — Mīrābāī and others [folk]

- **Mīrābāī as disciple [folk]** — the most famous legend binds the Rajput princess-saint **[Mīrābāī](mirabai.md#the-rāvidās-discipleship-memory-disputed)** to Ravidās as her **guru** — a deliberately boundary-crossing memory in which a high-born Rajput woman takes an "untouchable" cobbler as spiritual master; some *padas* in Mīrā's name praise him as her teacher. *Scholarly caveat [scholarly]:* there is **no corroborating historical evidence**; chronology is strained and the *guru*-naming verses are likely later accretions. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidas*, *Mirabai*) [disputed]
- **Royal and other devotees [folk]** — hagiography further attaches to him **Queen Jhālī (Jhālāṃ Bāī) of Chittor**, **Rāṇī Pdmāvatī / Mīrā's circle**, and assorted rulers and Brāhmaṇas won over by his sanctity — the standard bhakti motif of the high-born humbled before the saintly "low-born." (Source: Grokipedia; Bhaktamāl tradition) [folk]
- **The network effect [scholarly]** — these ties, like the Rāmānanda discipleship, function to **knit the great bhaktas into one paramparā**; they encode the tradition's claim that devotion overrides caste, more reliably than they record events. (Source: cf. [`mirabai.md`](mirabai.md), [`kabir.md`](kabir.md)) [scholarly]

## Ravidāssia — from sant to founder of a religion

- **The Ravidāssia movement** — Ravidās is the **founder-figure and supreme Guru of the Ravidāssia (Ravidasia) religion**, a distinct faith that grew from the **Ad-Dharm / Dalit assertion movements** of 20th-c. Punjab among the **Camār / Chamar (Ad-Dharmī)** community, with a large diaspora (UK, North America, Italy). It venerates Ravidās as Satguru and centres on his teaching of casteless equality. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidassia*; Grokipedia) [scholarly]
- **The 2009 break [scholarly]** — the movement moved toward a **formally separate identity** after the **2009 murder of Sant Niranjan Dass's deputy, Sant Rāmānand**, in a Vienna *dera* attack; in **2010 at Vārāṇasī (Seer Govardhanpur)** the Ravidāssia community proclaimed a distinct religion with its own scripture and greeting. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidassia*) [scholarly]
- **Amritbāṇī Gurū Ravidāss Jī** — the movement's own scripture, the **Amritbani Guru Ravidass Ji**, a compilation of **~240 hymns of Ravidās** installed in Ravidāssia temples (*Dera Sachkhand Ballan*, Jalandhar, is the principal seat) in place of — or alongside — the Gurū Granth Sāhib; the greeting is **"Jo Bole So Nirbhai, Sri Guru Ravidass Maharaj Ki Jai."** (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidassia*, *Amritbani Guru Ravidass*) [scholarly]
- **Symbols** — the movement's emblem is the **"Har(i)" insignia** and the flag bearing it; **Begampura** names its ideal and its anthem of casteless equality. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ravidassia*) [scholarly]

## Legacy — the Dalit forefather

- **Dalit assertion** — beyond the *dera* movement, Ravidās is a **principal saint-icon of Dalit self-respect across north India**, invoked by anti-caste and Ambedkarite currents as scriptural proof that the "untouchable" reached the heights of the sacred and preached equality from the bottom of the order. (Source: Omvedt, *Seeking Begumpura*; Grokipedia) [scholarly]
- **Living devotion** — **Guru Ravidāss Jayantī (Māgh Pūrṇimā)** is celebrated with large processions, especially in **Punjab, Haryana, UP and the diaspora**; his *bhajans* and "Begampura" are sung across the Sant and folk traditions. (Source: Grokipedia, *Guru Ravidass Jayanti*) [folk]
- **Place in the bhakti network** — counted with [Kabīr](kabir.md), [Mīrābāī](mirabai.md), Sūrdās and Tulsīdās among the great north-Indian sants (see the full roster in [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md)); within it he is the **nirguṇa, anti-caste social visionary** whose verse fed both the [Gurū Granth Sāhib](../90-literature-corpus/README.md) and a religion that bears his name. (Source: north-bhakti-sants.md) [scholarly]
