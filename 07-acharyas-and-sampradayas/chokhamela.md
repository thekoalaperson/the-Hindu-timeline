# Chokhāmeḷā and the Mahār Vārkarī Household

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](./README.md) (master index: [`../README.md`](../README.md)). The first-tier 14th-c. **Vārkarī** poet-saint **Chokhāmeḷā (Cokhāmeḷā)** of **Pandharpur** — born a **Mahār** ("untouchable") and the most celebrated **Dalit** voice of the Marathi *bhakti* movement — together with his whole composing household: wife **Soyarābāī**, sister **Nirmaḷā**, son **Karmameḷā** and brother-in-law **Banka**.
> Frame against the Vārkarī lineage and succession in [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md), the household poetics of [Janābāī](janabai.md), and the shared **dignity-of-the-low** charter he holds with the Camār sant [Ravidās](ravidas.md). Two clocks: this is **academic / historical** Kali-Yuga dating, not Purāṇic yuga-time — and the historical poet must be distinguished from the saint of the miracle-narratives.

---

## Who he was — the Mahār devotee of Viṭhobā

- **Chokhāmeḷā (Cokhāmeḷā, Cokhā, Cokhobā)** — 14th-c. **Vārkarī sant** and *abhaṅga*-poet of **Pandharpur** on the Bhīmā (Candrabhāgā), an ecstatic devotee of **Viṭṭhal / Viṭhobā / Pāṇḍuraṅga**, and by birth a **Mahār** — the Maharashtrian community ranked "untouchable" under the varṇa-jāti order, traditionally bound to village watch-duty, scavenging and corvée labour. (Source: Wikipedia, *Chokhamela*; Vārkarī hagiography)
- **The defining tension** — as a Mahār he was **barred from entering the Viṭhobā temple** he loved; his devotion was conducted from outside the threshold, and this exclusion is the central fact around which both his life-legend and his protest-poetry are organised. (Source: Wikipedia, *Chokhamela*; Zelliot, *An Anthology of Dalit Literature*)
- **Place in the succession [scholarly]** — Chokhāmeḷā belongs to the first generation of named Vārkarī sants around [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md) and [Nāmdev](namdev.md); tradition makes him a contemporary and disciple-associate of Nāmdev's circle, the same milieu that produced the maidservant-poet [Janābāī](janabai.md) and the potter-saint Gorā Kumbhār. (Source: Wikipedia, *Chokhamela*; Ranade, *Mysticism in Maharashtra*) [traditional]
- **Dating [disputed]** — conventionally placed in the **early–mid 14th century**, dying **c. 1338 CE**; like all early Vārkarī figures the dates rest on hagiography rather than record, and the "two clocks" caution of the section applies — this is contested **historical** Kali-Yuga time, not yuga-reckoning. (Source: Wikipedia, *Chokhamela*) [disputed]

## The poetry of caste-exclusion and surrender

- **"Ūs ḍoṅgā parī rasa nohe ḍoṅgā"** — his single most-quoted *abhaṅga*: *"The sugarcane is crooked, but its juice is not crooked; why be deceived by outward form?"* — the body / caste / birth may be deemed "low" and bent, but the inner essence (the juice, the soul, the devotion) is pure and straight. A manifesto that birth-status cannot defile the bhakta's heart. (Source: Chokhamela *abhaṅga* corpus; Zelliot, *From Untouchable to Dalit*)
  - *Companion lines [regional]:* in the same idiom — *"the fire is black, the smoke is black, but the flame is pure"* / *"the Gaṅgā's water is muddy in flood, yet holy" * — a recurring poetics of an impure exterior over an undefiled core. (Source: Vārkarī *abhaṅga* tradition) [regional]
- **Protest within surrender** — Chokhāmeḷā's verse holds two registers at once: anguished **complaint** at his exclusion (*"You made me low-born — why this injustice?"*) and total **self-surrender (śaraṇāgati)** to Viṭṭhal. He does not reject the deity or the path; he turns the pain of untouchability into the very ground of his devotion. (Source: Wikipedia, *Chokhamela*; Zelliot) [scholarly]
- **The threshold as theme** — many *abhaṅgas* address the saint's standing **at the temple gate / step**, outside the sanctum, calling to Viṭṭhal across a boundary he is forbidden to cross — the spatial fact of caste rendered as devotional longing. (Source: Chokhamela *abhaṅga* corpus) [regional]
- **Shared charter with Ravidās [scholarly]** — Chokhāmeḷā is to Marathi Vārkarī devotion what the Camār [Ravidās](ravidas.md) is to north-Indian nirguṇa *bhakti*: the canonical **untouchable saint** whose open avowal of his "low" caste, and whose insistence that devotion overrides birth, became foundational to later Dalit self-assertion. (Source: Zelliot, *From Untouchable to Dalit*) [scholarly]

## The death-legend and the chanting bones [folk]

- **Death in the wall-collapse** — the central legend: Chokhāmeḷā, conscripted with other Mahārs into **corvée labour (*veṭh / beggar*)** repairing a town wall (often told as at **Mangalvedhe / Mangalvedha**, near Pandharpur), is killed when **the wall collapses** upon the workers. His death thus comes in the very degraded labour his caste was bound to. (Source: Wikipedia, *Chokhamela*; Mahīpati, *Bhaktavijaya*) [folk]
- **Nāmdev recovers the bones** — [Nāmdev](namdev.md), seeking his friend among the rubble, is said to have identified Chokhāmeḷā's remains because **his bones still chanted "Viṭṭhal, Viṭṭhal"** — the divine name continuing to sound from the dead saint's body, the miracle that confirms his sanctity past death. (Source: Mahīpati, *Bhaktavijaya / Bhaktalīlāmṛta*) [folk]
- **The samādhi at the temple steps** — Nāmdev is said to have gathered the bones and built Chokhāmeḷā's **samādhi at the foot of the steps of the Viṭhobā temple** at Pandharpur — so that the saint barred in life from entering the temple rests **at its very threshold**, where pilgrims bow before ascending. The placement is read as both the tradition's apology for his exclusion and its enduring memorial of it. (Source: Wikipedia, *Chokhamela*; Pandharpur hagiography) [folk/regional]
  - *Variant (devotional reading):* the step-side *samādhi* is honoured as the **first darśana** of the pilgrimage — the low-born saint becomes the gatekeeper-blessing every devotee touches before the god. (Source: Vārkarī tradition) [regional]

## The composing household — a roster of poet-saints

The Mahār household around Chokhāmeḷā is itself an *abhaṅga*-producing lineage; the tradition preserves verse under five family names, and the corpus must be catalogued by author, not merged.

- **Soyarābāī — wife** — a poet-saint in her own right and the **most important** of the household after Chokhāmeḷā; her *abhaṅgas* (some signed *"Cokhyācī Mahārī"*, "the Mahār woman of Chokhā") confront **bodily impurity and caste-pollution** head-on, arguing that the body's defilement — menstruation, birth, death-pollution — is universal and cannot single out the "untouchable": *"the polluted is born from the polluted... in whose body is purity?"* Her work is among the earliest extant verse by a Dalit woman. (Source: Soyarabai *abhaṅga* corpus; Tharu & Lalita, *Women Writing in India*) [scholarly]
- **Karmameḷā — son** — author of the household's most **bitter and questioning** *abhaṅgas*, which turn on Viṭṭhal with reproach: *why was I born into a Mahār home? why give me hunger and a low birth and then withhold even your temple?* — devotion shading into accusation, the sharpest articulation of the injustice the family lived. (Source: Karmamela *abhaṅga* corpus; Zelliot) [scholarly]
- **Nirmaḷā — sister** — Chokhāmeḷā's sister, also remembered as a composer of *abhaṅgas* within the family's devotional circle; her surviving verse is slighter but secures the household as a **family of poet-saints** rather than a single voice. (Source: Vārkarī tradition; Zelliot) [regional]
- **Banka (Bankā) — brother-in-law** — Nirmaḷā's husband (Chokhāmeḷā's brother-in-law / *meheṇā*), counted among the Mahār Vārkarī saints and credited with his own *abhaṅgas*; he completes the household roster of five composing kin. (Source: Vārkarī tradition; Wikipedia, *Chokhamela*) [regional]
- **A household poetics [scholarly]** — like [Nāmdev](namdev.md)'s home (with the maidservant [Janābāī](janabai.md)), Chokhāmeḷā's household shows early Marathi *bhakti* as a **collective family practice**: men and women, across a single Mahār home, all signing *abhaṅgas* — and uniquely here, all speaking **from below the caste-line**, making this the densest cluster of Dalit devotional voice in the medieval corpus. (Source: Tharu & Lalita; Zelliot) [scholarly]

## Afterlife and legacy

- **Canonised in the Vārkarī corpus** — Chokhāmeḷā's and Soyarābāī's *abhaṅgas* are sung in the living **Vārkarī** *kīrtana* and on the **Pandharpur pilgrimage (*vārī*)** alongside [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md), [Nāmdev](namdev.md) and [Tukārām](tukaram.md); he is a fixed member of the sant-canon, not a marginal figure. (Source: Wikipedia, *Warkari*, *Chokhamela*)
- **Hagiographic memory** — his life and the bone-miracle are transmitted chiefly through **Mahīpati's** 18th-c. *Bhaktavijaya* and *Bhaktalīlāmṛta*, the standard Marathi *bhakta*-hagiographies, layered over the older oral tradition. (Source: Mahīpati, *Bhaktavijaya*; Wikipedia) [scholarly]
- **Modern Dalit icon [scholarly]** — Chokhāmeḷā became a **central symbol of Mahār self-respect** in 19th–20th-c. Maharashtra; early Mahār social organisation and institutions bore his name, before **B. R. Ambedkar's** movement shifted from a devotional, temple-entry framing (which Chokhāmeḷā embodied) toward rejection of caste Hinduism altogether — so that he stands at the hinge between bhakti-era and modern Dalit assertion. (Source: Zelliot, *From Untouchable to Dalit*) [scholarly]
