# Tukārām — the Abhaṅga Apex of the Vārkarī Tradition

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](./README.md) (master index: [`../README.md`](../README.md)). The 17th-c. Śūdra grocer-cultivator **sant** of **Dehu**, emotional summit of the Marathi **Vārkarī** Viṭṭhal-*bhakti* movement, and author of the **Tukārām Gāthā** — several thousand *abhaṅgas* in plain, raw Marathi.
> Frame it against the lineage roster in [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md); the deity and town in [`../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md`](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md) (Pandharpur — Viṭhobā). Two clocks: this is **academic / historical** dating, not Purāṇic yuga-time.

## Who He Was

- **Tukārām (Tukobā, Tukārām Mahārāj)** — **Śūdra grocer / petty trader and cultivator** of **Dehu** on the **Indrāyaṇī** river near Pune; the most beloved and most quoted *abhaṅga*-poet of the Vārkarī tradition, singing of **Viṭṭhal / Viṭhobā / Pāṇḍuraṅga** of Pandharpur in stripped-down, self-abasing, often irreverent Marathi. (Source: Wikipedia, *Tukaram*; Prepp; New World Encyclopedia)
  - *Caste [scholarly]:* family of the **Kuṇbī** (Maratha cultivator) community, conventionally classed **Śūdra**; ancestral trade was **grain-selling (vāṇī / grocer)** alongside small-scale farming and money-lending — the basis of his common epithet "the grocer-saint." (Source: Wikipedia; New World Encyclopedia)
  - *Family:* son of **Bolhobā (Bolhobā More)** and **Kanakāī**; clan deity inherited from a forebear **Viśvambhar**, a Viṭṭhal-devotee — i.e. Tukārām was **born into** Vārkarī devotion, not a convert to it. (Source: New World Encyclopedia; siddhayoga.org)

## Dating — the Two-Clock Problem [disputed]

- **Death year — relatively firm:** **1649/1650 CE** is the date most traditions converge on for his *vaikuṇṭha-gamana* (departure). (Source: Wikipedia; New World Encyclopedia)
- **Birth year — genuinely contested:** scholars and hagiographers assign **1568, 1577, 1598, 1606, or 1608 CE**; **c. 1608–1650** and **c. 1598–1650** are the most cited modern spans. Catalog all, do not collapse. (Source: New World Encyclopedia; Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (popular Maharashtra usage):* **1608–1650** is the figure on most public memorials and the Govt.-era film tradition.
  - *Variant (older hagiography):* **1577–1650**, giving him a ~73-yr life.

## Life Arc

- **Famine and ruin** — an early **famine (c. 1629)** killed his **first wife Rakhumābāī (Rakhmā)** and a son by starvation, wiped out his trade and farm, and broke him into renunciatory grief; this collapse is traditionally the hinge that turned the failing grocer toward total Viṭṭhal-devotion. (Source: New World Encyclopedia; siddhayoga.org)
- **Avalī / Jījābāī, the second wife** — capable but worldly, remembered in hagiography (and in Tukārām's own verse) as **scolding and nagging**, unable to grasp his abandonment of livelihood for *kīrtan*; the household poverty-vs-devotion tension is a recurring, self-deprecating motif in his *abhaṅgas*. (Source: VivekaVani; New World Encyclopedia)
- **Dream-initiation, no living guru** — Tukārām states he had **no guru in the flesh**; in a dream a teacher named **Bābājī Caitanya** laid a hand on his head, declared the lineage, and gave the *mantra* **"Rām Kṛṣṇa Hari."** This dream-*dīkṣā* is itself a Vārkarī signature — the tradition's authority runs through Viṭṭhal and the saints' poetry, not through a Sanskritic *guru-paramparā*. [folk] (Source: VivekaVani; New World Encyclopedia)
- **The Brahmin antagonists** — opposition from orthodox pundits who resented a Śūdra teaching scripture is central to his hagiography:
  - **Rāmeśvar Bhaṭṭ** — a learned Brahmin whose discourse-audiences drained to Tukārām's *kīrtans*; he had Tukārām's manuscripts thrown into the Indrāyaṇī, then later **repented and became a devotee**. (Source: New World Encyclopedia)
  - **Mambājī (Mambājī Gosāvī)** — a local ascetic who scorned and harassed Tukārām and likewise **later relented**. (Source: New World Encyclopedia; VivekaVani)
- **The grocer who gave it away** — stock hagiographic incidents (cancelling debtors' loans by sinking the ledgers in the river; minding sugarcane/grain and giving it to children and the poor) dramatize his refusal of acquisitive *vāṇī* life. [folk] (Source: siddhayoga.org; New World Encyclopedia)

## The Tukārām Gāthā — His Work

- **Tukārām Gāthā (Abhaṅga Gāthā)** — the Marathi compendium of his poems, composed roughly **c. 1632–1650**; the **culminating scripture of the Vārkarī canon**, sitting alongside Jñāneśvar's *Jñāneśvarī* and Nāmdev's hymns as the movement's living text. (Source: Wikipedia, *Tukaram*; Prepp)
  - *Count [disputed]:* tradition counts about **4,500 abhaṅgas**; the figure "several thousand" is the safe scholarly statement, and the **authentic core vs. later accretions** is genuinely unresolved — open roster. (Source: Wikipedia; New World Encyclopedia)
  - *Textual-criticism note [scholarly]:* **no surviving manuscript predates the late 17th c.**, i.e. roughly 50–100 years after his death; the canon as printed rests on copies attributed to descendants (see *recopying* below), so the boundary of the genuine corpus is a real philological problem. (Source: Grokipedia summary; academic consensus)
- **The *abhaṅga* form** — short, end-stopped Marathi devotional verse, metrically simple, made for **collective *kīrtan*** singing; Tukārām is its acknowledged master. The diction is deliberately **plain, colloquial, sometimes coarse or irreverent**, addressing Viṭṭhal as friend, master, and target of complaint — the opposite of Sanskrit court polish. (Source: Wikipedia; siddhayoga.org)
- **Themes** — total surrender (*śaraṇāgati*) to Viṭṭhal; the worthlessness of mere ritual and caste-pride; the dignity of the low-born devotee; ferocious **social criticism** of hypocritical Brahmins and of caste hierarchy delivered from within devotion, not from outside religion. (Source: HinduTone; Ambedkarite Today)

## The Legends [folk]

- **The drowned manuscripts** — ordered by **Rāmeśvar Bhaṭṭ** to destroy his "unauthorized" poems, Tukārām sank his manuscripts in the **Indrāyaṇī** river, then **fasted and prayed on the bank for 13 days**; on the thirteenth the manuscripts **floated up intact and dry**. The miracle is the tradition's charter-myth: divine vindication of a Śūdra's right to scripture over Brahmin gatekeeping. (Source: mkvyoga.com; New World Encyclopedia)
  - *Variant (historicizing gloss):* since no originals survive from his lifetime, the surfaced texts were **re-copied by kin** (his grandson **Santājī** / other relatives) — the legend encoding the real act of transmission. [scholarly]
- **Bodily ascent to Vaikuṇṭha (*sadeha-vaikuṇṭha-gamana*)** — at his end (1649/1650) Tukārām is said to have **told Avalī he was going to Vaikuṇṭha**, gone up the hillock at Dehu, and been **taken bodily**, in a radiant chariot sent by Viṭṭhal, before a gathered crowd — leaving **no corpse**. This deathless departure parallels the saint-ascent motif and is commemorated at Dehu. (Source: mkvyoga.com; New World Encyclopedia)
  - *Variant [disputed]:* sceptical / rationalist readings propose **drowning, murder by enemies, or simple disappearance**, with the chariot-ascent as later hagiographic overlay — flagged but not the tradition's own account.

## Place in the Succession

- **Jñāneśvar → Nāmdev → Eknāth → Tukārām** — the canonical Vārkarī four-saint sequence, in which Tukārām is the **culmination and emotional apex**, the *kalaśa* (finial) on the temple the earlier sants built. (Source: Wikipedia, *Warkari*; and [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md))
  - **[Jñāneśvar (Dnyāneśvar)](north-bhakti-sants.md)** — the **founder-philosopher**, c. 1275–1296, whose *Jñāneśvarī* (Marathi gloss on the [Bhagavad Gītā](../90-literature-corpus/README.md)) is the movement's intellectual base.
  - **[Nāmdev](north-bhakti-sants.md)** — the **tailor-sant** and co-founder of Vārkarī *kīrtan*, the pan-Indian bhakta (some verses in the [Guru Granth Sāhib](../90-literature-corpus/README.md)).
  - **[Eknāth](north-bhakti-sants.md)** — the **Brahmin consolidator** of Paithan, c. 1533–1599, who restored a reliable *Jñāneśvarī* text and bridged the gap to Tukārām's age.
  - *Note:* Tukārām thus represents the **Śūdra grocer** completing a chain begun by a renunciant Brahmin (Jñāneśvar) — the social inclusiveness across caste is itself the lineage's claim.
- **Śivājī connection [disputed/folk]** — tradition links the **Maratha king Śivājī** to Tukārām (sending gifts, attending *kīrtans*, or being counselled by him); historicity is doubtful and the episode is best tagged legend, though it signals Tukārām's standing in 17th-c. Maharashtra. (Source: New World Encyclopedia)
- **Modern afterlife** — the 1936 Marathi film ***Sant Tukaram*** (Prabhat) made him a national icon; his abhaṅgas remain core to Marathi devotional and household culture. (Source: Wikipedia)

## The Pandharpur Vārī — the Living Cult

- **The vārī (vāri) pilgrimage** — the twice-yearly (chiefly **Āṣāḍhī**) Vārkarī walking pilgrimage to **Viṭhobā at Pandharpur** on the Bhīmā/Candrabhāgā; *Vārkarī* literally means "one who performs the vārī." See the deity, Puṇḍalīka legend, and town in [`../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md`](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Pandharpur Wari*; *Vithoba*)
- **The pālkhī–pādukā procession** — a saint's **pādukā (sandals/footprints)** are carried in a **pālkhī (palanquin)** from his shrine to Pandharpur. **Tukārām's pālkhī departs from Dehu**; Jñāneśvar's from **Ālandī** — the two great palanquins converging on Pandharpur with ~a million pilgrims. (Source: Wikipedia, *Pandharpur Wari*)
  - *Founding [scholarly]:* the **pādukā-pālkhī custom was begun in 1685 by Tukārām's youngest son Nārāyaṇ Mahārāj**; the wari in its present organized form was reshaped in the **1820s** (Haibatravbābā Ārphaḷkar for Jñāneśvar's pālkhī, and Tukārām's descendants).
  - *Timing:* the procession (~21 days on foot) culminates at the **Viṭhobā temple on Śayanī / Āṣāḍhī Ekādaśī** (June–July). For 2026 the Tukārām Mahārāj pālkhī is set to leave Dehu in early July. (Source: Wikipedia; mypunepulse.com; StayVista)
