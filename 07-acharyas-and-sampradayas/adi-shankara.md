# Ādi Śaṅkara & Advaita Vedānta

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](./README.md) — the founder-organizer of the **Smārta** stream and **Advaita Vedānta** (radical non-dualism), and the **Daśanāmī** monastic order with its four cardinal *āmnāya pīṭhas*. Up-link: section index [`README.md`](./README.md).
> Sits in the historical Kali-Yuga; dating is the central dispute — **[traditional]** matha-succession placing him in the 6th–5th c. BCE vs **[scholarly]** epigraphy/text-criticism placing him c. 700 CE. Two-clocks method: [`recorded-history.md`](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md). Compare the rival Vedānta founders catalogued in the [section index](./README.md): Rāmānuja (Viśiṣṭādvaita), Madhva (Dvaita).

## Life & person

- **Ādi Śaṅkara (Śaṅkarācārya, Bhagavatpāda)** — Kerala-born Brahmin ascetic, philosopher and reformer; chief systematizer of **Advaita Vedānta**, organizer of the **Daśanāmī** sannyāsī order and the four cardinal *mathas*, and central figure of the **Smārta** *pañcāyatana* tradition. Born at **Kālaḍi** (Kerala, on the Pūrṇā/Periyar river) to an aged, devout, formerly childless Nambūtiri couple — traditionally named **Śivaguru** (father) and **Āryāmbā** (mother) [folk]. Said to have died at age **32**, traditionally at **Kedārnāth** in the Himalayas (some accounts: Kāñcīpuram). (Source: Wikipedia, *Adi Shankara*)
  - *Hagiographic life-arc [folk]:* born after his parents' worship of Śiva at Tṛśśūr (hence "Śaṅkara"); precocious mastery of the Vedas; renounces as a boy after a crocodile seizes his leg in the river, forcing his mother's consent to *sannyāsa*; journeys north to find his guru; composes commentaries and the *digvijaya* debate-tour; installs the four pīṭhas; disappears at Kedārnāth. (Source: Sringeri Math, *Madhavīya Śaṅkara-Digvijaya*; esamskriti)
- **Guru: Govinda Bhagavatpāda** — Śaṅkara's initiating teacher, met on the banks of the Narmadā; himself a disciple of **Gauḍapāda**, author of the *Māṇḍūkya-Kārikā* (the *Āgama-śāstra*) — the proto-Advaita text whose *ajātivāda* (non-origination) Śaṅkara inherits and comments upon. (Source: Wikipedia, *Adi Shankara*)
- **Four principal disciples (*śiṣya*)** — each traditionally linked to one of the four pīṭhas (Source: Sringeri Math, "Primary Disciples"; Hindupost):
  - **Sureśvarācārya** — formerly the householder **Maṇḍana Miśra** (per most hagiographies); author of *Naiṣkarmya-siddhi* and the *vārttikas*; first head of **Śṛṅgeri** (South). [folk]
  - **Padmapādācārya** — author of the *Pañcapādikā* (commentary on Śaṅkara's *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya*); associated with **Govardhana / Purī** (East). [folk]
  - **Hastāmalakācārya** — child-sage of the *Hastāmalakīya* hymn; first head of **Dvārakā** (West). [folk]
  - **Toṭakācārya (Troṭaka)** — composer of the *Toṭakāṣṭaka*; first head of **Jyotirmaṭha** (North). [folk]

## Advaita Vedānta — the doctrine

- **Advaita ("not-two") Vedānta** — the radical non-dualist reading of the *Upaniṣads*, *Brahmasūtra* and *Bhagavad-Gītā* (the *prasthāna-trayī*). Core claim: **Brahman** (pure, attributeless consciousness, *nirguṇa*) is the sole reality; the individual self (*ātman*) is **identical** with it; the plural world is **Māyā** — not unreal but *mithyā* (neither real nor unreal), a superimposition (*adhyāsa*) removed by liberating knowledge (*jñāna*). Encapsulated in the four *mahāvākyas* ("great sayings"). (Source: Britannica, *Brahma-sutra-bhashya*; Wikipedia, *Adi Shankara*)
  - *Two truths [scholarly]:* **vyāvahārika** (conventional/empirical) vs **pāramārthika** (absolute) levels — the world is real at the transactional level, sublated only at realization. Liberation (*mokṣa / jīvanmukti*) is attainable while living.
  - *Place among the Darśanas:* the dominant Vedānta sub-school; doctrinal context in [Upavedas, Vedāṅgas & the Six Darśanas](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md). Differentiated from the bhakti-Vedāntas of [Rāmānuja and Madhva](./README.md) by its denial of ultimate self–God difference.

## The Digvijaya & the debate with Maṇḍana Miśra

- **Digvijaya ("conquest of the directions")** — the hagiographic pan-Indian tour on which Śaṅkara debates and converts rival schools (Mīmāṃsakas, Buddhists, Jains, Śāktas, Kāpālikas, Sāṅkhyas), establishes the four pīṭhas, and reorders monastic life. Best-known account: the **Madhavīya Śaṅkara-Digvijaya** attributed to Mādhava/Vidyāraṇya (Śṛṅgeri tradition). (Source: cultureandheritage.org; Sringeri Math)
- **Debate with Maṇḍana Miśra** — the signature episode: Śaṅkara challenges the eminent Mīmāṃsā ritualist **Maṇḍana Miśra** (a pupil of **Kumārila Bhaṭṭa**) at Māhiṣmatī. Maṇḍana's learned wife **Ubhaya Bhāratī** (regarded as an incarnation of Sarasvatī) serves as **umpire**; the wager is that the loser takes the victor's mode of life. Śaṅkara prevails, and Maṇḍana takes *sannyāsa* — becoming, in most tellings, **Sureśvarācārya**. (Source: esamskriti; Wikipedia, *Maṇḍana Miśra*, *Ubhay Bharati*)
  - *Variant — the kāma-śāstra round (Sringeri/Madhavīya tradition) [folk]:* Ubhaya Bhāratī, unable to defeat Śaṅkara in dialectic, questions him on the art of love (*kāma*), of which the celibate ascetic is ignorant; Śaṅkara requests a recess and, by *parakāya-praveśa* (yogic entry into another's body), enters the corpse of the recently dead **King Amaruka**, learns the subject, and returns to win. (Source: Sringeri Math, *Madhavīya Śaṅkara-Digvijaya*, Part 3)
  - *Variant — identity of Maṇḍana [disputed/scholarly]:* the equation **Maṇḍana Miśra = Sureśvara** is hagiographic; modern scholarship treats Maṇḍana Miśra (author of the *Brahmasiddhi*) and Sureśvara as **distinct authors** with differing positions, so the conversion-story is doubted. (Source: Wikipedia, *Maṇḍana Miśra*)
- **Caution on the hagiographies [scholarly]:** the *Śaṅkara-Vijaya* biographies postdate Śaṅkara by **several centuries**, are mutually contradictory, and are "politically motivated hagiographies" rather than reliable history — even the count, names and routes of the pīṭhas vary by tradition. (Source: Wikipedia, *Adi Shankara*; advaita-vedanta.org)

## Dating — traditional vs academic [disputed]

- **Scholarly consensus** — first half of the **8th century CE**, roughly **c. 700–750 CE**; Indologist John Koller cites "the best recent scholarship" for **born 700, died 750 CE**. An older, now-superseded convention gave **788–820 CE** (still widely repeated). Based on text-critical relation to other authors (e.g. references to Kumārila, Dharmakīrti) and absence of earlier attestation. [scholarly] (Source: Wikipedia, *Adi Shankara*; advaita-vedanta.org)
- **Matha-succession (traditional) datings** — the pīṭhas' own records place him a millennium earlier, in the **6th–5th c. BCE**, but disagree among themselves [traditional]:
  - *Kāñcī tradition:* Kali year 2593 = **509 BCE** (born), d. 477 BCE.
  - *Govardhana (Purī):* **507 BCE**.
  - *Dvārakā:* **491 BCE**.
  - *Jyotirmaṭha:* **485 BCE**.
  - *Jagannātha Purī:* **484 BCE**.
  - (Source: Wikipedia, *Adi Shankara*; advaita-vedanta.org, "Sankara's Date")
- *Note:* the gap between traditional (~6th c. BCE) and academic (~8th c. CE) datings is among the largest dating disputes in Indian intellectual history; this repo catalogs both per the two-clocks policy in [`recorded-history.md`](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md).

## The four Āmnāya Pīṭhas (mathas)

- Four cardinal seats, one per direction, each assigned a **Veda**, a ***mahāvākya***, a *sampradāya* name, and a first head. (Note: hagiographies vary; the standard scheme below follows the mathas' own tradition.) (Source: Hindupost, "Four Mathas of Adi Shankaracharya"; Wikipedia, *Jyotir Math*; Sringeri Math)
  - **Śṛṅgeri Śāradā Pīṭha (South)** — Karnataka, on the Tuṅga river; **Yajur-Veda**; *mahāvākya* **"Aham Brahmāsmi"** ("I am Brahman"); *Bhūri* / **Bhūrivara** *sampradāya*; first head **Sureśvarācārya**. [traditional]
  - **Dvārakā / Śāradā Pīṭha (West)** — Gujarat; **Sāma-Veda**; *mahāvākya* **"Tat tvam asi"** ("That thou art"); *Kiṭavāra* / **Kitavara** *sampradāya*; first head **Hastāmalakācārya**. [traditional]
  - **Govardhana Pīṭha, Jagannātha Purī (East)** — Odisha; **Ṛg-Veda**; *mahāvākya* **"Prajñānam Brahma"** ("Consciousness is Brahman"); *Bhogavāra* *sampradāya*; first head **Padmapādācārya**. [traditional]
  - **Jyotirmaṭha / Jyotiṣpīṭha, Badrīkāśrama (North)** — Uttarakhand; **Atharva-Veda**; *mahāvākya* **"Ayam ātmā Brahma"** ("This self is Brahman"); *Nanda(vara)* *sampradāya*; first head **Toṭakācārya**; headquarters of the Giri, Parvata & Sāgara sub-orders. [traditional]
  - *Variant — Kāñcī Kāmakoṭi Pīṭha [disputed]:* the Kāñcīpuram math claims to be a fifth seat founded by Śaṅkara himself (his "throne of omniscience," *sarvajña-pīṭha*) and his final abode; the four canonical mathas and most scholarship do not recognize it among the original four. (Source: Wikipedia, *Adi Shankara*)

- **Daśanāmī ("ten-name") order** — Śaṅkara's reorganization of *Ekadaṇḍī* renunciants into ten monastic surnames distributed among the four pīṭhas: **Sarasvatī, Bhāratī, Purī** (Śṛṅgeri); **Tīrtha, Āśrama** (Dvārakā); **Vana, Araṇya** (Purī); **Giri, Parvata, Sāgara** (Jyotirmaṭha). The pīṭha heads carry the title **Śaṅkarācārya**. (Source: Hindupost; Wikipedia, *Jyotir Math*; Vajiram & Ravi)

## Pañcāyatana Pūjā & the Smārta synthesis

- **Pañcāyatana pūjā** — the Smārta worship of **five deities** (Viṣṇu, Śiva, Devī/Śakti, Sūrya, Gaṇeśa) arranged as equal faces of one **Brahman**, the worshipper placing their *iṣṭa-devatā* at center — an integrative liturgy traditionally credited to Śaṅkara to harmonize sectarian rivalries. A six-deity form (*ṣaṇmata*, adding Skanda/Kumāra) is also named. (Source: Wikipedia, *Panchayatana puja*; section [`README.md`](./README.md))
  - *Variant — antiquity of the rite [scholarly]:* attribution to Śaṅkara is traditional, but archaeology shows *pañcāyatana* maṇḍalas and temple-plans **predating** him — Gupta-era examples, and a set from the village of **Nand** dated to the **Kuṣāṇa** era (pre-300 CE) — so Śaṅkara at most popularized, not invented, the form. (Source: Wikipedia, *Panchayatana puja*)

## Key works

- **Authentic core (scholarly consensus)** (Source: Wikipedia, *Adi Shankara* & *Adi Shankara bibliography*; advaita-vedanta.in):
  - **Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya** — his master commentary on Bādarāyaṇa's *Brahmasūtra*; the foundational Advaita text, opening with the famous *adhyāsa-bhāṣya* on superimposition. [scholarly]
  - **Bhāṣyas on ten principal Upaniṣads** — Īśa, Kena, Kaṭha, Praśna, Muṇḍaka, Māṇḍūkya (with Gauḍapāda's *Kārikā*), Taittirīya, Aitareya, Chāndogya, Bṛhadāraṇyaka. [scholarly]
  - **Bhagavad-Gītā-bhāṣya** — earliest extant complete commentary on the Gītā, completing the *prasthāna-trayī*. [scholarly]
  - **Upadeśasāhasrī ("A Thousand Teachings")** — the only non-commentarial prose-and-verse work whose authorship is broadly accepted; a direct manual of Advaita instruction. [scholarly]
- **Attributed / doubtful authorship**:
  - **Vivekacūḍāmaṇi ("Crest-Jewel of Discrimination")** — celebrated prakaraṇa on viveka and liberation; **authenticity questioned and mostly rejected** by modern scholarship, yet so woven into Śaṅkara's heritage that it is inseparable from his image. [disputed/late text] (Source: advaita-vedanta.in; Wikipedia, *Adi Shankara*)
  - *Other prakaraṇas & hymns (mostly traditional attribution) [folk]:* **Ātmabodha, Tattvabodha, Aparokṣānubhūti, Śataśloki, Daśaślokī**; devotional **stotras** — *Bhaja Govindam* (*Mohamudgara*), *Saundaryalaharī*, *Śivānandalaharī*, *Kanakadhārā-stotra*, *Nirvāṇaṣaṭka*, *Bhavānī-aṣṭakam*. Over **300 texts** bear his name; most are judged works of later admirers, eponymous scholars, or pretenders. (Source: Wikipedia, *Adi Shankara bibliography*)
