# Nimbārka, Vallabha & Chaitanya — Bhakti Vedānta

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](README.md) — the three Kṛṣṇa-centred Vedānta founders who gave Vaiṣṇava bhakti its dominant medieval shape: **Nimbārka** (Dvaitādvaita; Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa), **Vallabha** (Śuddhādvaita; Puṣṭimārga; Śrīnāthjī) and **Caitanya** (Acintya-bheda-abheda; Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism). Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md).
> Companion to [`adi-shankara.md`](adi-shankara.md) (Advaita) — these are the *bhakti* answer to Śaṅkara's non-dualism, each carrying a rival Vedānta sub-school. Sits in the historical Kali-Yuga; dating split **[traditional]** (matha-succession / hagiography) vs **[scholarly]** (epigraphy / text-criticism) — two-clocks method: [`recorded-history.md`](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md). Their object of worship is mapped in [`../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md`](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md) and [`../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).

## Why these three together

- **A shared object, three Vedāntas** — Nimbārka, Vallabha and Caitanya each install **Kṛṣṇa** (with **Rādhā**) as the supreme reality — not as an *avatāra* of Viṣṇu but, in Vallabha's and Caitanya's readings, as *svayaṃ bhagavān* (God Himself, per *Bhāgavata* 1.3.28) — yet each frames the soul–God relation differently: **Dvaitādvaita** (difference-and-non-difference), **Śuddhādvaita** (pure non-dualism with a real world), **Acintya-bheda-abheda** (inconceivable difference-and-non-difference). All three are *bhedābheda*-family answers to Śaṅkara's pure Advaita. (Source: Wikipedia, *Nimbarka*, *Pushtimarg*, *Gaudiya Vaishnavism*)
- **Two of the four canonical sampradāyas + one later order** — Nimbārka heads the **Kumāra (Nimbārka) Sampradāya**; Vallabha is the best-known ācārya of the **Rudra Sampradāya** (Viṣṇusvāmī's line); Caitanya's **Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism** later claimed affiliation to Madhva's **Brahma Sampradāya**. The four-stream frame is in the [section index](README.md). (Source: Poojn.in, *Four Vaishnava Sampradayas*)

## Nimbārka — Dvaitādvaita & the Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa couple

- **Nimbārkācārya (Nimbāditya, Niyamānanda)** — Telugu Brāhmaṇa ascetic-philosopher, chief proponent of **Dvaitādvaita** ("dualistic non-dualism") and founder of the **Nimbārka Sampradāya**, one of the four canonical Vaiṣṇava lineages. Major figure in spreading worship of the **divine couple Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa** as joint supreme. Born at **Prātiṣṭhāna / Paithan** (Maharashtra) into a Telugu Brāhmaṇa family; settled in the **Braj** region near Mathurā. (Source: Wikipedia, *Nimbarka*; Itihaas.ai)
  - *Name-origin legend [folk]:* to honour a *sannyāsī* guest after sunset (when ascetics may not eat), Nimbārka trapped the setting **sun (arka)** in a **neem (nimba)** tree so its light lingered — hence "Nimba-arka." (Source: Wikipedia, *Nimbarka*; historified.in)
- **Dvaitādvaita doctrine** — the *jīva* (soul) and the world (*jagat*) are **simultaneously distinct and non-distinct** from Brahman (= Kṛṣṇa), a relation Nimbārka calls **svābhāvika bhedābheda** ("natural difference-and-non-difference"): like waves to the sea or rays to the sun, the soul is a real *part* (*aṃśa*) yet wholly dependent on and non-different in essence from God. Brahman is *saviśeṣa* (qualified, with attributes), against Śaṅkara's *nirguṇa* Brahman. Five categories: Brahman, *cit* (souls), *acit* (matter), and the relations of difference/non-difference. (Source: Philosophy Institute, *Nimbarka's Dvaitadvaita*; Wikipedia, *Nimbarka Sampradaya*)
- **Rādhā as supreme consort** — Nimbārka's tradition is among the earliest to elevate **Rādhā** to full divine status, worshipped inseparably with Kṛṣṇa (the **Yugala** / divine pair); the soul's goal is to serve the couple's eternal *līlā* in Goloka/Vṛndāvana. Object of devotion detailed in [`../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md`](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Nimbarka Sampradaya*; historified.in)
- **Guru-paramparā (the Kumāra line)** — the Nimbārka Sampradāya traces itself from **Kṛṣṇa → Haṃsa (Viṣṇu) → Sanaka & the four Kumāras → Nārada → Nimbārka** (hence the alternate name **Kumāra / Sanakādi / Catuḥsana Sampradāya**), founded by the eternal celibate sages who are Brahmā's mind-born sons. (Source: Wikipedia, *Nimbarka Sampradaya*; Grokipedia)
- **Key works** (Source: Wikipedia, *Nimbarka*; en-academic):
  - **Vedānta-pārijāta-saurabha ("Fragrance of the Celestial Pārijāta of Vedānta")** — his concise commentary (*bhāṣya*) on Bādarāyaṇa's *Brahmasūtra*; noted as preserving an **archaic, pre-Śaṅkara style** of sūtra-commentary. Expanded by his disciple **Śrīnivāsa** in the *Vedānta-kaustubha*. [scholarly]
  - **Vedānta-kāmadhenu daśaślokī ("Ten Verses")** — a devotional ten-verse compendium of the doctrine, "suited to a devotee who does not want abstract logic"; also called the *Siddhānta-ratna*. [scholarly]
  - *Devotional [folk]:* the **Kṛṣṇa-stavarāja** and other hymns are traditionally attributed.
- **Dating — the widest variant spread of the three [disputed]:**
  - *Academic [scholarly]:* most often **c. 1130–1200 CE** (11th–12th c.); Vijay Ramnarace's recent study argues for the **7th century CE** (before Śaṅkara), making him potentially the earliest of all the sampradāya founders. (Source: Wikipedia, *Nimbarka*)
  - *Variant — much earlier [traditional/folk]:* sect tradition places him in the **6th–7th c. CE** or, in maximalist hagiography, as far back as **3096 BCE** (early Kali-Yuga, near Kṛṣṇa's own era). (Source: Wikipedia, *Nimbarka*)
- **Seat & later sampradāya** — the order's principal seat is at **Salemabad** (Nimbark Tirth, Rajasthan); it established major centres across **Braj**, anchoring the region as the heartland of Kṛṣṇa-bhakti. The Vṛndāvana sub-tradition flourished under **Harivyāsa Devācārya** and the Rasik saints. (Source: Wikipedia, *Nimbarka Sampradaya*; Grokipedia)

## Vallabha — Śuddhādvaita & the Puṣṭimārga

- **Vallabhācārya (Vallabha, Mahāprabhu Vallabhācārya)** — **1479–1531 CE** [scholarly], Telugu Brāhmaṇa theologian; founder of the **Puṣṭimārga** ("Path of Grace/Nourishment") and systematizer of **Śuddhādvaita** ("pure non-dualism"); the leading ācārya of the **Rudra Sampradāya** (the line of **Viṣṇusvāmī**). Born at **Champāraṇya** (Chhattisgarh) to parents fleeing Vārāṇasī; traditionally a partial incarnation of **Agni / the mouth of Kṛṣṇa**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Pushtimarg*; Vajiram & Ravi, *Vallabhacharya*)
  - *Variant — birth-year [folk/disputed]:* commonly **1479 CE** (V.S. 1535); some Puṣṭimārga accounts give **1473 CE**. Death (*tirobhāva*) at Vārāṇasī in **1531 CE**, traditionally by entering the Gaṅgā at Haṇumān-ghāṭ and vanishing in a flame. (Source: pushtimarg.net; Hindu Info Pedia)
- **Śuddhādvaita doctrine** — "pure" (*śuddha*) non-dualism: Brahman (= **Kṛṣṇa**) is the sole reality and the world is **real, not Māyā/illusion** — it is a genuine, un-transformed (*avikṛta-pariṇāma*) manifestation of Brahman, who is *sat-cit-ānanda* and manifests the world as a real expression of his *līlā*. This is the sharpest break from Śaṅkara's [Advaita](adi-shankara.md), which calls the world *mithyā*; Vallabha keeps the non-dualism but **affirms the world's reality**. Souls are real sparks (*aṃśa*) of Brahman; bondage is the obscuring of *ānanda*. (Source: bhaktisetuhaveli.org, *Shuddhadvaita*; Vajiram & Ravi)
- **Puṣṭimārga — the Path of Grace** — salvation comes by **divine grace (*puṣṭi*, "nourishment")** and loving service (*sevā*), not by knowledge or works alone; the devotee surrenders via the formula of **Brahma-sambandha** (initiatory self-dedication of body, mind and wealth to Kṛṣṇa) and serves Kṛṣṇa intimately as a household deity. Distinguished from the *maryādā-mārga* (path of rule/effort). (Source: Wikipedia, *Pushtimarg*; GKToday, *Pushtimarg Sect*)
- **Śrīnāthjī** — the central icon of the Puṣṭimārga: **Kṛṣṇa as a seven-year-old child lifting Mount Govardhana** with one hand (the *Govardhana-dhāraṇa* episode). The self-manifest image on **Govardhana hill** in Braj was discovered/served by Vallabha; later moved (1672, fleeing Aurangzeb) to **Nāthdwāra** (Rajasthan), still the chief shrine. Govardhana-līlā: [`../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md`](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md). (Source: Grokipedia, *Shrinathji*; Wikipedia, *Pushtimarg*)
- **Key works** (Source: Vajiram & Ravi; bhaktisetuhaveli.org):
  - **Aṇubhāṣya (Brahmasūtra-aṇubhāṣya)** — his Śuddhādvaita commentary on the *Brahmasūtra*; left **incomplete** and finished by his son Viṭṭhalanātha. [scholarly]
  - **Subodhinī** — extensive commentary on the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa**, foregrounding Kṛṣṇa's *līlā*, grace and beauty; incomplete (breaks off in the later skandhas). [scholarly]
  - **Ṣoḍaśa-granthāḥ ("Sixteen Treatises")** — sixteen short devotional manuals of the path, incl. *Siddhānta-rahasya*, *Puṣṭi-pravāha-maryādā*, *Catuḥślokī*, *Yamunāṣṭakam*. [scholarly]
  - **Tattvārthadīpa-nibandha** — a comprehensive doctrinal digest. [scholarly]
- **Lineage & succession** — the *gosvāmī* heads of the sect descend by **blood from Vallabha** (a hereditary, household-deity tradition, unusual among sampradāyas). His younger son **Viṭṭhalanātha (Goswami Viṭṭhaleśa, Gusāīñjī, 1515–1585)** completed his works, organized temple *sevā*, and formed the **Aṣṭachāp**. The seven (later more) *gaddīs* (seats) descend through Vallabha's grandsons. (Source: Wikipedia, *Pushtimarg*; pushtimarg.net)
- **Aṣṭachāp ("Eight Seals")** — the **eight poet-saints** of the Puṣṭimārga whose Braj-bhāṣā *pada*-songs form the sect's liturgical music; named for the poetic **signature (*chāp*)** each sealed his verses with. Four were Vallabha's disciples, four Viṭṭhalanātha's. (Source: Wikipedia, *Aṣṭachāp*; Vraj Dwar):
  - **Sūrdās** — the blind master-poet, *Sūrsāgar*; greatest of the eight. **Kumbhandās**, **Paramānanddās**, **Kṛṣṇadās** (Vallabha's four); **Nanddās**, **Govindsvāmī**, **Chītasvāmī**, **Caturbhujdās** (Viṭṭhalanātha's four). [scholarly]

## Caitanya Mahāprabhu — Acintya-bheda-abheda & Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism

- **Caitanya Mahāprabhu (b. Viśvambhara Miśra; "Nimāi"; Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya)** — **1486–1534 CE** (b. 18 Feb 1486, Phālguna full-moon; d. 14 June 1534), Bengali saint of **Navadvīpa** (Nadia, W. Bengal); ecstatic founder of **Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism** and inaugurator of mass **saṅkīrtana** (congregational chanting) of the **Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra**. Son of Jagannātha Miśra and **Śacīdevī** (daughter of the scholar Nīlāmbara Cakravartī). (Source: Wikipedia, *Chaitanya Mahaprabhu*; ExoticIndiaArt)
  - *Theological status [traditional]:* his own tradition worships him as the **combined avatāra of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa** — Kṛṣṇa taking Rādhā's golden complexion and her mood of love to taste his own sweetness — counted the **yuga-avatāra** of Kali (the "golden avatāra," *Gaura*). Other sampradāyas regard him only as a great **devotee**, not God. [disputed] (Source: Wikipedia, *Gaudiya Vaishnavism*)
- **Life-arc** (Source: Wikipedia; ExoticIndiaArt; vaishnava-heritage.org):
  - Prodigious Sanskrit scholar and *naiyāyika* running a *toḷ* (school) in Navadvīpa; spiritual turn after travelling to **Gayā** (c. 1508–09) for his father's *śrāddha*, where he was initiated by the ascetic **Īśvara Purī** into the ten-syllable **Gopāla mantra** and overtaken by ecstatic Kṛṣṇa-bhāva. [scholarly]
  - Launched nightly **saṅkīrtana** with **Nityānanda** and **Advaita Ācārya** at Śrīvāsa's courtyard; took **sannyāsa in 1510** at **Katwa** from **Keśava Bhāratī** (a Bhāratī of Śaṅkara's [Daśanāmī](adi-shankara.md) order), receiving the name **Kṛṣṇa Caitanya**. [scholarly]
  - Made **Jagannātha Purī** ([Puri](../06-sacred-geography/sapta-puri-and-sacred-cities.md)) his base for his last ~24 years; toured **South India**, then **Vṛndāvana/Braj**, "rediscovering" the lost holy sites of Kṛṣṇa's *līlā*. Died at Purī in 1534 (traditions: merged into the Jagannātha/Ṭoṭā-Gopīnātha deity; or drowned in the sea in ecstasy). [folk/disputed]
- **Acintya-bheda-abheda doctrine** — "**inconceivable** (*acintya*) **difference-and-non-difference**": the soul (and the world) is **simultaneously one with and different from** Kṛṣṇa, a relation beyond logic's grasp — "like a molecule of sunshine to the sun," identical in **quality**, infinitesimally different in **quantity**. Synthesizes the monist and dualist extremes; God's *śaktis* (energies) are non-different from him yet really distinct. Caitanya wrote almost no philosophy himself — the doctrine was systematized by the **Goswāmīs**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Achintya Bheda Abheda*, *Gaudiya Vaishnavism*)
- **Saṅkīrtana & Harināma** — Caitanya's signature practice: public, musical, congregational chanting of the **Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra** ("Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma Hare Hare") and dancing as the *yuga-dharma* of Kali — the holy **Name (*harināma*)** held to be non-different from Kṛṣṇa himself and the sole sufficient means of liberation in this age. (Source: Wikipedia, *Chaitanya Mahaprabhu*; bhaktiparv.com)
- **Pañca-tattva** — the five "truths" who are Caitanya's intimate forms/associates, invoked before the mahā-mantra: **Caitanya** (Bhagavān), **Nityānanda** (*prakāśa*/expansion), **Advaita Ācārya** (avatāra), **Gadādhara** (*śakti*) and **Śrīvāsa** (devotee). (Source: Wikipedia, *Gaudiya Vaishnavism*)
- **The Six Goswāmīs of Vṛndāvana** — the scholar-disciples Caitanya deputed to recover Braj and codify his teaching in Sanskrit (he left almost nothing written) (Source: Wikipedia, *Gaudiya Vaishnavism*):
  - **Rūpa Goswāmī** — chief theologian; *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* (the science of *bhakti-rasa*), *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*, *Vidagdha-mādhava*. [scholarly]
  - **Sanātana Goswāmī** — Rūpa's elder brother; *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* (the ritual/conduct code), *Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta*. [scholarly]
  - **Jīva Goswāmī** — their nephew; the **Ṣaṭ-sandarbha** (six systematic treatises — Tattva, Bhagavat, Paramātma, Kṛṣṇa, Bhakti, Prīti), the philosophical capstone of the school. [scholarly]
  - **Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Goswāmī** — compiled the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* with Sanātana; installed Rādhāramaṇa. **Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa Goswāmī**. **Raghunātha Dāsa Goswāmī** — exemplar of renunciation. [scholarly]
- **The Brahma-Madhva-Gauḍīya claim [disputed/late text]** — the order's formal affiliation to **Madhva's** [Brahma Sampradāya](README.md) (via Mādhavendra Purī → Īśvara Purī) was first argued by **Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa** in the **18th c.** to win a Vṛndāvana ritual dispute; scholars note **no historical mention of Caitanya in Madhva records**, and many treat the link as later-constructed. (Source: Wikipedia, *Gaudiya Vaishnavism*)
- **Key written legacy** — Caitanya himself left only the **Śikṣāṣṭakam ("Eight Verses of Instruction")**, summarizing his whole mission (humility lower than a blade of grass, tolerance like a tree, ceaseless chanting, pure love beyond desire for liberation). His life and teaching are recorded in the great hagiographies **Caitanya-caritāmṛta** (Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja) and **Caitanya-bhāgavata** (Vṛndāvana Dāsa). (Source: Wikipedia, *Chaitanya Mahaprabhu*)
- **Later reach** — the Gauḍīya line runs through **Narottama Dāsa, Śyāmānanda, Śrīnivāsa Ācārya**, the 19th–20th-c. revival of **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** and **Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī**, and the global **ISKCON** (founded 1966 by **A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda**). (Source: Wikipedia, *Gaudiya Vaishnavism*; section [`README.md`](README.md))

## Comparative snapshot

- **Soul–God relation:** Nimbārka = *bhedābheda* (natural, real difference + non-difference) · Vallabha = *śuddhādvaita* (non-dual, world **real**) · Caitanya = *acintya-bhedābheda* (difference + non-difference, **inconceivable**). All reject Śaṅkara's *māyāvāda*. (Source: Wikipedia, comparative)
- **Supreme deity:** all three center **Kṛṣṇa with Rādhā** in Braj/Goloka; Vallabha & Caitanya make Kṛṣṇa *svayaṃ bhagavān* (source of avatāras, not an avatāra). See [`../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md). [traditional]
- **Path:** Nimbārka = service to the Yugala couple · Vallabha = *puṣṭi* grace + *sevā* (Brahma-sambandha) · Caitanya = *saṅkīrtana* / *harināma* + *rāgānugā-bhakti*. (Source: as cited above)
- **Sampradāya slot:** Nimbārka = Kumāra/Nimbārka (canonical 4) · Vallabha = Rudra/Viṣṇusvāmī (canonical 4) · Caitanya = Gauḍīya (later, claims Brahma-Madhva line). See [`README.md`](README.md). [scholarly]
