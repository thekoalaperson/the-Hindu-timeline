# Hinduism of the indenture diaspora (Caribbean, Mauritius, Fiji)

> Parent: [Acharyas & Sampradayas](README.md) — the living Hinduism carried by **girmitiya** (indentured) labour across the British and French sugar colonies, 1834–1920. A "regional-diaspora" node alongside [Balinese Hinduism](balinese-hinduism-agama-hindu-dharma.md); its South-Indian goddess stream continues [Māriyammaṉ](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/mariamman.md), and its Bhojpuri devotional core is the [Rāmcaritmānas of Tulsīdās](tulsidas.md).
> Sits **wholly in the documented Kali Yuga** — every date here is [scholarly]/historical (19th–21st c.); the *content* it carries (the Tretā-yuga Rāma story, the Māri cult) keeps its own clock. ## The girmitiya migration — indenture, 1834–1920

- **Girmit and girmitiya** — after Britain abolished slavery (**1834**), planters replaced enslaved Africans with contracted Indian labour under a system of indenture; the migrants' word for the labour contract was **girmit** (a Bhojpuri rendering of English *agreement*), and one bound by it a **girmitiya**. Most were recruited from the **Bhojpur** belt of eastern Uttar Pradesh and Bihar (Awadhi/Bhojpuri speakers) and, secondarily, from the Tamil/Telugu south. (Source: colonial emigration records) [scholarly]
- **Mauritius — the first and largest depot (1834)** — the first indentured Indians landed at Port Louis on **2 November 1834** aboard the *Atlas*; roughly half a million passed through the **Aapravasi Ghat** immigration depot (UNESCO World Heritage, 2006). Mauritius became the demographic heartland of the diaspora. (Source: Aapravasi Ghat / Mauritius archives) [scholarly]
- **British Guiana (Guyana, 1838)** — the ships *Whitby* and *Hesperus* delivered the first "Gladstone coolies" to John Gladstone's estates on **5 May 1838**; the experiment's early abuses briefly halted, then resumed the trade to the Caribbean. (Source: colonial records) [scholarly]
- **Trinidad — the *Fatel Razack* (1845)** — the first indentured ship, the *Fath Al Razak* ("Victory of Allah the Provider"), reached Port of Spain on **30 May 1845** with ~225 immigrants; **Indian Arrival Day** commemorates it. (Source: Trinidad archives) [scholarly]
- **Fiji — the *Leonidas* (1879)** — indenture to the Pacific began when the *Leonidas* landed at Levuka on **14 May 1879**; ~60,000 Indians came before the system closed. (Source: Fiji National Archives) [scholarly]
- **The end of indenture (1917–1920)** — under nationalist and humanitarian pressure (Gokhale, Gandhi, C. F. Andrews, the reports on abuse) recruitment was suspended in **1917** and the surviving contracts ran out by **1920**, when the girmit system formally ended. (Source: Government of India, indenture-abolition record) [scholarly]
- **Religion in the "jahaji" bond** — on the crossing of the *kālā pānī* ("black water," which orthodoxy held to break caste), shipmates became **jahājī-bhāī** ("ship-brothers"), a fictive kinship that dissolved sub-caste barriers; a flattened, congregational Hinduism — centred on a portable book, the home shrine, and the temple — took shape on the estates. (Source: diaspora ethnography) [scholarly]

## The Rāmcaritmānas as portable scripture

- **One book for a people** — the girmitiyas carried little, but Tulsīdās's **Awadhi *Rāmcaritmānas*** (composed **1574 CE** [scholarly]; see [tulsidas.md](tulsidas.md)) travelled as the single indispensable text: memorizable, sung, and doctrinally self-sufficient, it became the diaspora's scripture, grammar-book, and moral code at once. (Source: diaspora religious history) [scholarly] [regional]
- **Rāmāyaṇ satsaṅg and the mandali** — weekly household and estate gatherings ("**Rāmāyaṇ**" recitals) chant the *Mānas* in the **caupāī–dohā** metre, often to a **nau-din** (nine-day) or full **akhaṇḍ pāṭh** cycle at life-events; the reciter-expositor is the **pandit / kathā-vācak**. This congregational singing, not temple liturgy, is the backbone of Caribbean and Fijian Hindu life. (Source: ethnography of Trinidad/Guyana/Fiji Hinduism) [regional]
- **Two clocks** — the *Mānas* narrates the **Tretā-yuga** deeds of Rāma (the scriptural frame; cf. the Adhyātma-Rāmāyaṇa substrate Tulsīdās drew on, [adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md)), but its career *as diaspora scripture* is a **Kali-historical** fact of the last ~180 years. The corpus keeps the two apart. (Source: this file's convention)
- **The oral-epic stream travelled too** — alongside the *Mānas*, folk performance genres of the Hindi belt (birhā, the Ālhā heroic cycle, chowtāl/phagwā songs) crossed with the migrants; on the wider indigenous-oral layer see [regional-and-oral-epics.md](../90-literature-corpus/regional-and-oral-epics.md). [regional] [oral tradition]

## Trinidad — Rāmleelā, jhaṇḍī, and Hanumān

- **Rāmleelā (Ramleela)** — the Tretā-story is enacted outdoors over some **ten nights** in the Dussehra season on open savannahs, climaxing in the burning of a towering **Rāvaṇa effigy**; the best-known grounds are at **Dow Village (Chase Village), Felicity, St Augustine and Enterprise**. It is community theatre, vrata and public assertion of Hindu presence in one. (Source: Trinidad Ramleela tradition) [regional]
- **Jhaṇḍī — the prayer-flag** — after a household **pūjā**, a triangular cloth **jhaṇḍī** (Bhojpuri *jhaṇḍā*, "flag") is hoisted on a tall bamboo in the yard, one flag per deity honoured; the colour codes the god — **red for Hanumān**, saffron/yellow for Sūrya, Lakṣmī or Viṣṇu, white for Sarasvatī, black in the Kālī rites. The bristling clusters of jhaṇḍī outside a home are the signature visual marker of Indo-Caribbean Hinduism. (Source: Indo-Caribbean domestic ritual) [regional] [folk]
- **Hanumān / the red flag** — Hanumān (the *Mānas*'s hero-devotee) is the most invoked deity for protection and strength; the **Hanumān Chālīsā** is a household staple (see [hanuman.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md), [hanuman-chalisa.md](../04-deep-dives/stotras/hanuman-chalisa.md)). The **85-ft Hanumān Mūrti at the Dattatreya temple, Carapichaima** (Todd's Road; consecrated **2003**) — the tallest Hanumān outside India at its unveiling — anchors the cult. (Source: Dattatreya Yoga Centre / Trinidad) [regional]
- **A largely Sanātanī, Vaiṣṇava mainstream** — the majority tradition is orthodox **Sanātana Dharma**, murti-pūjā and *Mānas*-centred, institutionalized through the **Sanatan Dharma Maha Sabha** (Bhadase Sagan Maraj, from 1952) which built temples and denominational schools; a reformist **Arya Samaj** minority also took root (cf. [modern-teachers-and-reform.md](modern-teachers-and-reform.md)). (Source: Trinidad Hindu institutional history) [scholarly]

## Kālī-Māī and the "Madrasi" rites (Guyana & Trinidad)

- **The Madras stream** — South Indian (Tamil/Telugu, colonially "**Madrasi**") indentured labourers carried the **grāmadevatā** cult — above all the goddess **Māriyammaṉ / Kālī-Māī** — into Guyana and Trinidad; it survives as a distinct, ecstatic tradition set apart from the Bhojpuri *Mānas* mainstream. This is the diaspora continuation of the cult mapped in [mariamman.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/mariamman.md). (Source: Indo-Caribbean Śākta ethnography) [regional]
- **The Kālī-Māī temple ("Coolie church" / Kali temple)** — worship centres on the fierce Mother and a **retinue of Tamil guardian-deities**: **Māriyammaṉ**, **Muṉīśvaraṉ / Munesh Prem**, **Kaṭṭeri**, **Madurai Vīraṉ**, **Sangili Karuppaṉ** and Hanumān among them; the shrines cluster on Guyana's coast (Corentyne, Berbice) and in central Trinidad. (Source: Guyanese Kali-Mai ethnography, e.g. Stephanie Bilby / Keith McNeal) [regional] [folk]
- **Possession, drumming, and healing** — services feature **tappu/drum** music, the descent of a deity into a chosen medium (the "**marlo**"/manifesting devotee), oracular healing and the resolution of affliction; the goddess "works" through the entranced body — the same trance-and-cure logic recorded for Māri in South India. (Source: Kali-Mai ritual ethnography) [regional] [folk]
- **Blood-offering and its contest** — animal sacrifice (fowl, goat) to the Mother persists in some Kālī-Māī yards, exactly the **bali** the Sanskritizing temple-Māri sheds; Indo-Caribbean reformers and the Vaiṣṇava mainstream have long pressed to "cool" or vegetarianize the cult, an ongoing tension rather than a settled reform. (Source: diaspora Śākta studies) [disputed] [regional]
- **Māri continuity — the through-line** — the Caribbean Kālī-Māī is not a new deity but the **transplanted village-goddess complex**: severed-head icons, neem, the cooling/heating physics and firewalking all recur, confirming the *mariamman.md* equation at the far edge of migration. (Source: comparative grāmadevatā studies) [scholarly]

## Mauritius — Gaṅgā Talāo and the Śivarātri pilgrimage

- **The crater-lake** — **Grand Bassin**, a crater lake in the **Savanne** highlands, is the holiest Hindu site of Mauritius, renamed **Gaṅgā Talāo** ("Lake of the Ganges"). (Source: Mauritius Hindu tradition) [regional]
- **The discovery-dream legend** — tradition holds that in **1897 (first pilgrimage 1898)** the priest **Paṇḍit Jhummon Giri Gosāgne (Sanadhoo Giri Gossaye)** was granted a **vision/dream** that the waters of this remote lake were joined to the sacred **Gaṅgā** of India; he led the first pilgrimage, and the lake became a surrogate Ganges for a people cut off from the river by the *kālā pānī*. In **1972** water carried from the Ganga at Hardwar was ceremonially poured into the lake, sealing the identification. (Source: Mauritian oral tradition; temple record) [folk] [oral tradition]
- **Mahā Śivarātri kāvaḍī pilgrimage** — on **Mahā Śivarātri** (Phālguna Kṛṣṇa caturdaśī) several hundred thousand pilgrims walk barefoot, many carrying decorated wooden **kanwar/kāvaḍī** arches, from across the island to Gaṅgā Talāo to collect holy water and offer it to **Śiva** — the largest Śivarātri gathering, and one of the largest Hindu assemblies, outside India. (Source: Mauritius Śivarātri observance) [regional] — for the festival's scriptural charter see [shivaratri-hunter-legend.md](../04-deep-dives/shivaratri-hunter-legend.md) and [festivals-and-vratas.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md).
- **The colossi** — the lakeside now bears the **108-ft Mangal Mahādev** (a standing Śiva modelled on the Sursagar Śiva of Vadodara; unveiled **2007**) and a matching **Durgā/Mā Kālī** statue, monumentalizing the pilgrimage site. (Source: Mauritius, Ganga Talao complex) [regional]

## Fiji — the Sanātan–Ārya Samāj split and South Indian firewalking

- **A community of two rites** — Fiji-Indians divide between a **North Indian (Bhojpuri, "Calcutta") majority** whose Hinduism is *Mānas*-centred, and a **South Indian ("Madras") minority** carrying the Tamil goddess/Murugan tradition — two liturgical worlds within one indentured population. (Source: Fiji Indian social history) [scholarly]
- **Sanātan vs. Ārya Samāj** — early in the 20th c. **Ārya Samāj** missionaries (heirs of **Dayānanda Sarasvatī**; cf. [modern-teachers-and-reform.md](modern-teachers-and-reform.md)) brought Vedic reform — rejecting murti-pūjā, purāṇic ritual, hereditary caste and (in polemic) the authority of the *Rāmcaritmānas* itself — against an orthodox **Sanātana Dharma** majority defending image-worship and the *Mānas*. The rift ran through temples, schools, cremation practice and cultural life. (Source: John D. Kelly; Fiji Hindu history) [scholarly]
- **The great public debates (śāstrārtha)** — the two camps fought set-piece scriptural disputations in the 1920s–30s; the reformists organized as the **Ārya Pratinidhi Sabhā of Fiji** (with missionaries such as **Paṇḍit Ram Sharma**), the orthodox as the **Sanātan Dharm Pratinidhi Sabhā** — a doctrinal contest, deliberately *not* resolved here: both wings are recorded and attributed. (Source: Fiji Hindu institutional history) [scholarly] [disputed]
  - *Variant emphasis:* some historians read the split less as pure theology than as a **status/leadership contest** among Fiji-Indian elites over who spoke for "the Hindu community." (Source: Kelly, *A Politics of Virtue*) [scholarly]
- **South Indian firewalking (Tīmiti)** — the Madras minority keeps the **Māriyammaṉ firewalk**: after ~10 days of fasting and abstinence, yellow-clad devotees (some with **skewer/lance body-piercing**) cross a bed of glowing coals as a vow, the unburnt feet proving the goddess's cooling grace. The rite is centred on temples such as the **Śrī Śiva Subramaniya** temple at **Nadi** (the largest Hindu temple in the southern hemisphere). This is the same **tīmiti** cult exported worldwide from Tamil Nadu — distinct from indigenous Fijian *vilavilairevo* firewalking at Beqa. (Source: Fiji Māri/firewalking ethnography) [regional] [folk]
- **Two clocks** — none of the Fijian material carries a yuga-date: the Sanātan/Ārya dispute and the firewalking cult are **Kali-historical** phenomena of c. 1900–present, even as the goddess and the *Mānas*-Rāma they honour belong to the older cyclic frame. (Source: this file's convention)
