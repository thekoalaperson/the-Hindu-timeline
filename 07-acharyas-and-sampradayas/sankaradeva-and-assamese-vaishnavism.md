## What makes Ekaśaraṇa distinctive

- **"Shelter in one" (eka-śaraṇa)** — a strict, almost austere **monotheism**: total refuge (*śaraṇa*) in a **single** supreme God, **Kṛṣṇa / Viṣṇu / Nārāyaṇa / Hari** (treated as one), to the deliberate exclusion of all other deities, including the Goddess, Śiva, and the wider pantheon. Unlike most north-Indian bhakti, it rejects **Rādhā-worship**, *gopī*-erotic devotion, and the *mādhurya* (sweetheart) mood — devotion here is **dāsya** (servant) and **vātsalya** (the child-Kṛṣṇa loved as a son), not romantic. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ekasarana Dharma*; testbook.com)
- **Bhakti without idol or sacrifice** — Śaṅkaradeva downplayed Vedic ritualism, image-worship and animal sacrifice (a sharp break from the dominant **Śākta/Tantric** religion of medieval Assam), and replaced them with **śravaṇa-kīrtana**: hearing and congregationally singing the name and deeds of God. Open to **all castes and even Muslims**; among his disciples were the Muslim **Cānd Sāī (Cāṇḍ Khān)** and the tribal/low-caste devotees the **Kala Saṃhati** later gathered. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sankardev*; diademy.com)
- **Vernacular over Sanskrit** — its scriptures, songs and plays are in **Assamese** (and the artificial **Brajāvalī**), elevating the spoken tongue to a sacred-literary language — the single largest force in shaping Assamese identity. (Source: ijnrd.org; borthakursiasacademy.com) [scholarly]
- **Doctrinal placement [scholarly]:** broadly **bhedābheda**-family Vaiṣṇavism centred on the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*; not a formal Vedānta *bhāṣya* school like [Rāmānuja/Madhva](ramanuja-and-madhva.md), but a practice-first, *Bhāgavata*-first devotionalism. Often grouped with the eastern saguṇa wave alongside [Caitanya's Gauḍīya line](nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md), though the two arose independently.

## The Cāri Vastu — the four reference-objects

The whole religion is structured around **four "things" (cāri vastu)** the devotee holds to. This is the load-bearing doctrinal frame. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ekasarana Dharma*)

- **Nāma** — the **Name**, chanting/singing God's names (the formula *rāma-kṛṣṇa-nārāyaṇa-hari*); the primary act of devotion (śravaṇa-kīrtana). Established by Śaṅkaradeva.
- **Deva** — the **single God**, Kṛṣṇa alone as supreme reality. Established by Śaṅkaradeva.
- **Guru** — reverence for the **spiritual preceptor**; the line of *gurus* from Śaṅkaradeva on. Notably this fourth-listed-but-third-added principle was **contributed by Madhavadeva**, who introduced it when he accepted Śaṅkaradeva as guru at **Belaguri**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ekasarana Dharma*)
- **Bhakat** — the **community of devotees** (*sat-saṅga*), congregational fellowship; established by Śaṅkaradeva.
- *Note [scholarly]:* the **Saṃhati** schools (below) re-weight these four — Nika emphasizes *bhakat*/congregation, Kala emphasizes *guru*, Purusha emphasizes *nāma* — and the differences in emphasis are essentially the doctrinal content of the schism.

## Śaṅkaradeva — life and the founding

- **Śrīmanta Śaṅkaradeva** — poet, playwright, dancer, musician, painter, social reformer and saint; the founder of Ekaśaraṇa and the architect of classical Assamese culture. Born into a **Bhuyan (Kāyastha-Śiromaṇi) family** at **Bordowā / Batadrava (Alipukhuri, Tembuani)** in present-day **Nagaon district**, Assam. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sankardev*; vajiramandravi.com)
  - *Dating [disputed]:* traditional lifespan **1449–1568 CE** — a celebrated **119-year span**; modern scholars who doubt the longevity propose a later birth, c. **1479–1484**. Death traditionally at **Bheladuar** (Cooch Behar), under the patronage of the Koch king **Naranārāyaṇa**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sankardev*) [scholarly]
- **Pilgrimage & turn to Bhāgavata-bhakti** — orphaned young, schooled under **Mahendra Kandalī**; undertook a long (≈12-year) **pan-Indian pilgrimage** to the tīrthas, exposure to the wider bhakti ferment, before returning to propagate single-refuge devotion grounded in the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*. (Source: vajiramandravi.com; testbook.com) [traditional]
- **First performance — Cihna Yātrā** — tradition credits him with staging the **Cihna Yātrā**, regarded as the **first theatrical/visual-art presentation** of the movement (with his own painted scenery and music), the seed of the later *aṅkīyā nāṭ* drama. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sankardev*) [traditional]
- **Patronage & persecution** — operated first under the **Ahom** kingdom (where orthodoxy and the Brahmin establishment opposed him; a son-in-law **Hari**, and later figures, were persecuted) and later found protection in the **Koch (Kamatā)** kingdom under **Naranārāyaṇa** and his general-brother **Cilārāi**. (Source: borthakursiasacademy.com) [scholarly]
- **Institutions founded** — established the **than** at Bordowā and the network of **sattra** (monasteries) and **nāmghar** (prayer-houses) that became the social spine of the valley (see below).

## The scripture — Kīrtana-ghoṣā and the Assamese Bhāgavata

- **Kīrtana-ghoṣā** — Śaṅkaradeva's **masterpiece** and the central devotional text of Ekaśaraṇa: a large collection of **narrative verse-cantos glorifying Kṛṣṇa**, composed in Assamese for **congregational singing** (kīrtana). It is, with three other works, one of the **four canonical scriptures** of the faith. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sankardev*, *Ekasarana Dharma*)
- **The four sacred texts of Ekaśaraṇa [scholarly]:** (Source: Wikipedia, *Ekasarana Dharma*)
  - **Kīrtana-ghoṣā** — Śaṅkaradeva's devotional songs (above).
  - **Nāma-ghoṣā** — Madhavadeva's "book of the Name" (below).
  - **(Śaṅkaradeva's) Bhāgavata** — his Assamese rendering of the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*.
  - **Ratnāvalī / Bhakti-Ratnāvalī** — Madhavadeva's translation of a devotional anthology.
- **Assamese Bhāgavata renderings** — Śaṅkaradeva rendered **eight books of the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*** into Assamese verse, most importantly **Book X (the *Daśama*)** on Kṛṣṇa's birth and childhood-līlā — the textual fountainhead for his Kṛṣṇa-devotion. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sankardev*; cross-ref [`../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md`](../04-deep-dives/krishna-lila.md))
- **Other doctrinal works** (Source: Wikipedia, *Sankardev*):
  - **Bhakti-Pradīpa** ("lamp of devotion") and **Bhakti-Ratnākara** ("ocean of devotion") — devotional/doctrinal compendia.
  - **Nimi-Navasiddha-Saṃvāda** ("dialogue of King Nimi and the nine *siddhas*") — a doctrinal dialogue expounding the path of bhakti, drawn from the *Bhāgavata*'s 11th book.
  - **Anādi-pātana**, **Gunamālā** (a terse Kṛṣṇa-glory verse-garland written for the Koch king in a single day, per tradition), and **Rukmiṇī-haraṇa kāvya**.

## Borgeet — the "noble songs"

- **Borgeet (Bargīta, "great/noble song")** — a genre of **devotional lyric songs** Śaṅkaradeva originated, set to a **distinct raga-tāla system** unique to Assam (its rāgas and tālas not shared with other Indian classical traditions, and partly shaped by indigenous **Bodo, Mising, Karbi, Rabhā** musical idioms). Sung in **sattra** and **nāmghar** worship, and the musical bedrock of the **Sattriya** dance and *aṅkīyā nāṭ* drama. (Source: Wikipedia, *Borgeet*; assaminfo.com)
- **Brajāvalī, the song-language** — borgeets are composed in **Brajāvalī**, an **artificial literary dialect Śaṅkaradeva fashioned by blending Assamese with Maithili** (and the Braj/Mithilā devotional idiom), prized as ideal for melodious lyric. (Source: assaminfo.com; Wikipedia, *Borgeet*) [scholarly]
- **The fire and the 34 survivors [traditional]:** Śaṅkaradeva is said to have composed **240 borgeets**, but a **fire destroyed the manuscripts**; only about **34** could be recovered from memory. Grieved, he stopped composing borgeets and asked **Madhavadeva** to continue the genre. (Source: assaminfo.com; atributetosankaradeva.org)
- **Madhavadeva's borgeets** — Madhavadeva composed **~157–191** further borgeets (counts vary by source), focused especially on the **child-Kṛṣṇa** (vātsalya). Together the two saints' borgeets form the living song-canon. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhavdev*, *Borgeet*) [variant counts]

## Aṅkīyā Nāṭ & Bhāonā — the theatre

- **Aṅkīyā Nāṭ** — the **one-act devotional drama** Śaṅkaradeva created (a pioneering Indian vernacular theatre form): plays in **Brajāvalī/Assamese** weaving **narration (*sūtradhāra*), dialogue, borgeet music, dance, masks and costume** to dramatize episodes from Kṛṣṇa's life. (Source: drsanjib.net; Wikipedia, *Sankardev*)
- **Bhāonā** — the **performance/enactment style** of the aṅkīyā nāṭ: the staged traditional theatre, still performed in nāmghars and sattras, with its characteristic *gāyan-bāyan* (singers-drummers), masks (*mukhā*) and the *sūtradhāra* compère. (Source: drsanjib.net) [scholarly]
- **Śaṅkaradeva's plays** (Source: Wikipedia, *Sankardev*): **Cihna Yātrā** (the proto-play), **Rukmiṇī-haraṇa** (abduction/marriage of Rukmiṇī), **Patnī-prasāda**, **Kāliya-damana**, **Keli-gopāla**, **Pārijāta-haraṇa**, and **Rāma-vijaya** — together usually counted as **six surviving aṅkīyā nāṭs** (Cihna Yātrā lost).
- **Madhavadeva's plays (jhumurā)** — Madhavadeva wrote a set of short plays / **jhumurā** focused on the child-Kṛṣṇa, including **Arjuna-bhañjana**, **Bhojana-vihāra**, **Pimparā-guchowā**, **Cor-dharā** ("catching the thief"), **Bhūṣaṇa-harā** and others — nine in the standard count. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhavdev*) [variant counts]
- **Bṛndāvanī Vastra** — a celebrated **woven textile/tapestry** (≈40 yards) Śaṅkaradeva had woven c. **1554** (under his supervision, by weavers in the Koch domain) depicting scenes of Kṛṣṇa's **Vṛndāvana līlā**; surviving fragments are held in **museums in London** (British Museum / V&A) and elsewhere — a rare material relic of the movement. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sankardev*) [scholarly] — cross-ref textile/arts in [`../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/README.md`](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/README.md).

## The institutions — Sattra & Nāmghar

- **Nāmghar (kīrtana-ghar)** — the **village prayer-house**, the congregational hall at the heart of every Ekaśaraṇa community; site of *nāma-kīrtana*, bhāonā, and (historically) village assembly and justice. The nāmghar made the religion a **whole-village social institution**, not merely a temple cult. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ekasarana Dharma*; spmiasacademy.com)
- **Maṇikūṭ & the Guru-āsana** — instead of an idol, the nāmghar's sanctum (**maṇikūṭ**) holds the **sacred book** (the *Bhāgavata* / Kīrtana-ghoṣā) on a throne (*guru-āsana / siṃhāsana / thāpanā*) as the focus of worship — concretely expressing the **bookless-of-idol, name-centred** theology. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ekasarana Dharma*) [scholarly]
- **Sattra** — the **monastery**, headed by a **Sattrādhikār** (abbot) who traces apostolic lineage (in most lines from Śaṅkaradeva). Sattras run **xoron-lowā / śaraṇa-lowā** ("taking refuge") initiation, preserve manuscripts, and are the conservatories of **Sattriya dance, borgeet and bhāonā**. The great sattras (Barpetā, Auniāti, Daksinpāt, Garamur, Kamalābāri on **Majuli** island, Bordowā, Patbausi, etc.) remain living centres. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ekasarana Dharma*; gktoday.in)
- **Sattriya dance** — the **classical dance** form nurtured in the sattras (recognized as one of India's classical dances), born of the bhāonā/borgeet complex, performed to Brajāvalī/Assamese/Sanskrit Kṛṣṇa-lyrics. (Source: gktoday.in) [scholarly] — cross-ref [`../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/README.md`](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/README.md).

## Madhavadeva — chief disciple, the Nāma-ghoṣā

- **Madhavadeva (Mādhavadeva)** — Śaṅkaradeva's foremost disciple, spiritual companion (*prāṇa-bandhava*) and **successor**; a poet of the first rank and the man who institutionally consolidated the faith after the founder's death. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhavdev*)
  - *Dating [disputed]:* traditional **1489–1596 CE**; historian **Baniprasanna Misra** argues for a later birth (~**1514**) on chronological grounds. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhavdev*) [scholarly]
- **The Belaguri debate (conversion)** — once a **Śākta** (Goddess-worshipper) committed to animal sacrifice, Madhavadeva was brought to Śaṅkaradeva by his brother-in-law **Gayāpāṇi (renamed Rāmadāsa)**; in a famous **debate at Belaguri (the *maṇi-kāñcana saṃyoga*, "union of jewel and gold")**, Śaṅkaradeva's recitation of a *Bhāgavata* verse on the futility of sacrifice converted him to single-refuge bhakti (c. **1522/1532**). His acceptance of Śaṅkaradeva as **guru** is what added **Guru** as the cāri-vastu principle. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhavdev*, *Ekasarana Dharma*) [traditional]
- **Nāma-ghoṣā** — Madhavadeva's masterwork, the **"book of the Name"**, also called **Hajāri-ghoṣā** ("thousand couplets"): a sublime devotional poem of single-refuge praise, ranking with the Kīrtana-ghoṣā as a core scripture and a peak of Assamese literature. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhavdev*, *Ekasarana Dharma*)
- **Other works** — **Bhakti-Ratnāvalī** (Assamese rendering of a devotion anthology, one of the four sacred texts), **Janma-rahasya** (on creation/dissolution), the **jhumurā** plays (above), ~157–191 borgeets, and an Assamese **Rāmāyaṇa** rendering. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhavdev*) — Rāma-material cross-ref [`../04-deep-dives/rama-and-the-ramayana.md`](../04-deep-dives/ramayana/README.md).
- **Barpetā** — Madhavadeva established and developed the great **Barpetā Sattra (Kīrtanghar)**, a principal seat of the Nika tradition and of the post-founder organization. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhavdev*) [scholarly]

## The Saṃhati schisms — four branches

After the founders' era the movement fractured into **four Saṃhatis** ("orders / congregations"), differing mainly in **which cāri-vastu they stress**, in attitude to Brahminism and image-worship, and in apostolic line. The variant policy is to catalog all four with their leaders. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ekasarana Dharma*)

- **Brahma Saṃhati** — led by **Dāmodaradeva** and **Haridev** (who **did not accept Madhavadeva's leadership**, separating into their own line). The most **Brahminical** branch: **re-admitted Vedic ritual, image-worship and Brahmin sattrādhikārs**, and is the one major line **not** tracing its abbots from Śaṅkaradeva. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ekasarana Dharma*) [scholarly]
- **Puruṣa Saṃhati** — founded by Śaṅkaradeva's **grandsons Puruṣottama Ṭhākur and Caturbhuja Ṭhākur**. Stresses **Nāma**; tolerates **limited Brahminical rites and image-worship**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ekasarana Dharma*)
- **Nika Saṃhati** — founded by **Madhavadeva's** disciples **Padma Atā (Badalā Padma Atā), Mathurādāsa and Keśava Atā**. ("Nika" = clean/pure.) Stresses **bhakat / sat-saṅga (congregation)** and **strict purity codes**; **bans idol-worship** outright — the most austere, "purest" branch, custodian of much of the borgeet/Barpetā tradition. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ekasarana Dharma*) [scholarly]
- **Kala Saṃhati** — founded by **Gopāla Atā (Gopāladeva) of Bhavānīpur**. Stresses the **Guru** (often to the point of guru-as-God). The most **socially expansive** branch — won the largest following among **tribal, low-caste and marginalized communities**; its sattras (notably the Moāmoriā / Mahāpuruṣiā in Upper Assam) became the base of the **Moāmoriā rebellion** against the Ahom monarchy in the 18th century. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ekasarana Dharma*) [scholarly]
- *Open roster note:* sub-orders and individual sattra affiliations multiply beyond these four heads; the four-Saṃhati frame is the standard scholarly skeleton, not an exhaustive census. [scholarly]
