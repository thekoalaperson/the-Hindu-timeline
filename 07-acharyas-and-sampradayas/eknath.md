# Eknāth (Ekanātha) — The Consolidator of the Vārkarī Tradition

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](./README.md) (master index: [`../README.md`](../README.md)). The 16th-c. Brāhmaṇa scholar-*sant* of **Paithan**, the great **consolidator** between [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md)'s founding age and [Tukārām](tukaram.md)'s *abhaṅga* apex — restorer of a reliable **Jñāneśvarī** text, author of the **Eknāthī Bhāgavata**, the **Bhāvārtha Rāmāyaṇa**, the **Rukmiṇī Svayaṃvara**, and the vernacularizing **bhāruḍ** folk-songs.
> Frame it against the lineage roster in [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md); the texts he glossed in [`../90-literature-corpus/README.md`](../90-literature-corpus/README.md). Two clocks: this is **academic / historical** dating (c. 1533–1599 CE), not Purāṇic yuga-time.

## Who He Was

- **Eknāth (Marathi Ekanātha, "Eknāth Mahārāj")** — a learned **Deśastha Brāhmaṇa** householder-saint of **Paithan (Pratiṣṭhāna)** on the **Godāvarī**, the pivotal mid-tradition figure of the Marathi **Vārkarī** Viṭṭhal-*bhakti* movement; scholar, textual editor, commentator, and folk-poet who carried Sanskrit scripture into accessible Marathi. (Source: Wikipedia, *Eknath*; Britannica; Prepp) [scholarly]
- **Dating [scholarly]:** **c. 1533–1599 CE** is the conventional span; some traditions give birth as **1528**. His *samādhi* is placed at **Paithan in 1599** (on Phālgun Vadya Ṣaṣṭhī, the *Eknāth Ṣaṣṭhī* still observed there). He thus sits **roughly two and a half centuries after Jñāneśvar (d. 1296)** and **a generation before Tukārām (d. 1649/1650)** — the chronological bridge of the lineage. (Source: Wikipedia, *Eknath*; Britannica)
- **Family [traditional]:** said to descend from the saint **Bhānudās**, an earlier Vārkarī devotee credited with returning the Viṭṭhal image to Pandharpur; orphaned young and raised by grandparents. He remained a **married householder** (wife **Girijābāī**, children including son **Hari Paṇḍit** and daughter **Goḍāvarī**), modelling sainthood within domestic life rather than renunciation. (Source: Wikipedia, *Eknath*; Vārkarī hagiography) [traditional]

## Guru — Janārdana-svāmī

- **Janārdana-svāmī of Devagiri (Daulatābād)** — Eknāth's guru, a **Datta-upāsaka** (devotee of Dattātreya) who served as a fort administrator under the Deccan Sultanate. Eknāth went to him at Devagiri and underwent rigorous discipline and study; the guru-bond is so central that Eknāth signed his verses with the *bhaṇitā* (signature) **"Eka-Janārdana"** — fusing his own name with his guru's. (Source: Wikipedia, *Eknath*; Britannica) [traditional]
- **Dattātreya darśana [folk]:** tradition holds that through Janārdana-svāmī's grace Eknāth received **vision of Dattātreya** (the three-faced Brahmā-Viṣṇu-Śiva sage-deity), grounding his lineage in the Datta-sampradāya alongside the Vārkarī Viṭṭhal-cult. (Source: Vārkarī / Datta hagiography) [folk]

## Restoring the Jñāneśvarī — the Critical-Text Work

- **The corrupted Jñāneśvarī** — by Eknāth's day the text of Jñāneśvar's foundational **[Jñāneśvarī](jnaneshwar.md)** (the c. 1290 Marathi gloss on the [Bhagavad Gītā](../90-literature-corpus/README.md)) had become **garbled through 250+ years of manuscript copying**, with interpolations and scribal corruption obscuring the original. (Source: Wikipedia, *Eknath*, *Dnyaneshwari*) [scholarly]
- **Eknāth's recension (c. 1584)** — Eknāth undertook a deliberate **collation and purification of the text**, producing the **standard reliable recension** on which the modern printed Jñāneśvarī ultimately rests. This is a rare instance of a self-conscious **critical-text restoration** in pre-modern Indian vernacular literature, and his single most consequential service to the tradition. (Source: Wikipedia, *Dnyaneshwari*; and [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md)) [scholarly]
  - *Legend of finding the samādhi [folk]:* tradition says Eknāth was guided (in a dream/vision of Jñāneśvar) to the **Āḷandī samādhi** where a tree-root had pierced the saint's throat; on removing it he recovered the authentic text — a hagiographic dramatization of the editorial act. (Source: Vārkarī hagiography) [folk]

## Social Inclusiveness — Bhakti Against Caste

- **Caste-transgressing compassion** — though a Brāhmaṇa by birth, Eknāth repeatedly **defied caste boundaries**: he is remembered for feeding **untouchables (Mahār/Mātaṅga)** at *śrāddha* rites, for carrying a thirsty Mahār child, and for **bathing a sick outcaste**. Orthodox Paithan Brāhmaṇas censured him; he held that devotion and the indwelling self override birth-purity. (Source: Wikipedia, *Eknath*; Vārkarī hagiography) [folk/traditional]
- **The Gaṅgā-water episode [folk]** — returning from pilgrimage with sacred Godāvarī/Gaṅgā water meant for **Rāmeśvaram**, Eknāth is said to have **poured it down the throat of a dying donkey** in the desert, teaching that the living God in a suffering creature outranks ritual destination. (Source: Vārkarī hagiography) [folk]
- **A charter for vernacular access** — this ethic is inseparable from his literary project: rendering Sanskrit scripture into Marathi and into **folk-song** was itself the democratization of the sacred beyond Brāhmaṇa monopoly — continuous with Jñāneśvar's buffalo-recites-Veda myth. (Source: Britannica; and [`jnaneshwar.md`](jnaneshwar.md)) [scholarly]

## His Works — Scripture into Marathi

- **Eknāthī Bhāgavata (Ekanāthī Bhāgavata)** — his magnum opus: an extended **Marathi commentary-cum-exposition on Skandha 11 (the Eleventh Book) of the [Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/README.md)** — the *Uddhava-gītā*, Kṛṣṇa's last teaching to Uddhava. In thousands of **ovī** verses Eknāth expands the Sanskrit into a complete vernacular spiritual manual; composed in part at **Vārāṇasī**, where local pandits are said to have first opposed and then honoured it. (Source: Wikipedia, *Eknath*; Primary text: *Eknāthī Bhāgavata*) [scholarly]
- **Bhāvārtha Rāmāyaṇa** — his great **Marathi Rāmāyaṇa** ("Rāmāyaṇa of inner meaning"), a devotional retelling of Rāma's story. Eknāth **left it incomplete at the Yuddha-kāṇḍa** (some accounts: died during the Uttara/war book); it was **completed by his disciple Gāvbā (Gāvbā / Gāvabā)**. It became a foundational Marathi Rāma-text. (Source: Wikipedia, *Eknath*; Primary text: *Bhāvārtha Rāmāyaṇa*) [scholarly]
- **Rukmiṇī Svayaṃvara (Rukmiṇī Svayaṃvar)** — a Marathi narrative poem on **Kṛṣṇa's winning and wedding of Rukmiṇī**, drawn from the Bhāgavata, a beloved devotional ākhyāna in the Vārkarī repertoire. (Source: Wikipedia, *Eknath*; Primary text: *Rukmiṇī Svayaṃvara*) [scholarly]
- **Bhāruḍ (Bhāruḍe) — the folk-dramatic songs** — Eknāth's most original popular form: **allegorical, often comic or riddling Marathi songs**, performable as **folk-drama / street-theatre**, in which everyday characters and trades (the *vāsudeva*, the *gondhaḷī*, the snake-charmer, the bride, the beggar, the Muslim *fakir*) become vehicles for spiritual teaching. The *bhāruḍ* **democratized scripture into the idiom of the marketplace and the village**, reaching the unlettered directly. (Source: Wikipedia, *Eknath*; Primary text: *bhāruḍ* corpus) [scholarly]
  - *Form note:* alongside the *bhāruḍ* he composed numerous **abhaṅgas** and **gauḷaṇī** (Kṛṣṇa-*gopī* songs), feeding the same Vārkarī *kīrtan*-liturgy as Jñāneśvar's *Haripāṭha* and anticipating Tukārām's abhaṅga mastery. (Source: Wikipedia, *Eknath*)
- **Editorial vs. authorial legacy** — uniquely in the lineage, Eknāth's stature rests **both** on **original composition** (Bhāgavata, Rāmāyaṇa, bhāruḍ) **and** on **textual scholarship** (the Jñāneśvarī recension) — hence "consolidator" rather than only poet. (Source: Britannica) [scholarly]

## Place in the Succession — the Bridge

- **Jñāneśvar → Nāmdev → Eknāth → Tukārām** — the canonical Vārkarī four-saint sequence. Eknāth is the **third link, the consolidator**, who **recovered the textual heritage of the first two and prepared the ground for the fourth**: without his restored Jñāneśvarī and his vernacular corpus the tradition's continuity across the long gap to Tukārām is hard to imagine. (Source: Wikipedia, *Warkari*; and [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md))
  - **[Jñāneśvar (Dnyāneśvar)](jnaneshwar.md)** — the **founder-philosopher** (c. 1275–1296), whose Jñāneśvarī Eknāth restored.
  - **[Nāmdev](north-bhakti-sants.md)** — the **tailor-sant**, co-founder of Vārkarī *kīrtan*, contemporary of Jñāneśvar.
  - **[Tukārām](tukaram.md)** — the **Śūdra grocer-sant** of Dehu (c. 1598/1608–1650), the *abhaṅga* apex who follows Eknāth.
  - *Note:* the chain runs from a **Nāth-yogī excommunicate-Brahmin** (Jñāneśvar) through a **Brāhmaṇa scholar** (Eknāth) to a **Śūdra grocer** (Tukārām) — its social breadth is itself the lineage's claim, and Eknāth's caste-defying acts make him the hinge of that breadth.
- **The Paithan samādhi and Eknāth Ṣaṣṭhī** — his shrine on the Godāvarī at Paithan remains a pilgrimage centre; the **Eknāth Ṣaṣṭhī / Paithan yātrā** (Phālgun) commemorates his *samādhi*, and an **Eknāth Mahārāj pālkhī** joins the wider Vārkarī palanquin culture toward Pandharpur. (Source: Wikipedia, *Eknath*; Vārkarī tradition) [traditional]
