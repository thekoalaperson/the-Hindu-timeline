# Gārgī Vācaknavī & Maitreyī — The Brahmavādinīs of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](README.md) — the two iconic **women sages (brahmavādinīs)** of the Upaniṣadic age, both anchored to the **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad** and its central teacher **Yājñavalkya**. Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md).
> "Two clocks" caution: these belong to the **scriptural Vedic age** — placed by tradition in the latter Tretā / Dvāpara span of teacher-genealogies, but text-critically the *Bṛhadāraṇyaka* is among the oldest Upaniṣads (c. 7th–6th c. BCE [scholarly]). They are cataloged here, in the human-institutional layer, because they became the canonical *exemplars* invoked across later centuries — see [Ādi Śaṅkara](adi-shankara.md), whose *Bṛhadāraṇyaka-bhāṣya* comments on both dialogues, and the text-context in [`90-literature-corpus/`](../90-literature-corpus/README.md).

---

## The setting — Janaka's debate-assembly

- **The contest of Videha** — King **Janaka of Videha** holds a great sacrifice and assembles the most learned Brahmins, offering a thousand cattle (gold-horned) to whoever is the greatest knower of Brahman. **Yājñavalkya** boldly claims the prize first, provoking a sequence of challengers to interrogate him. (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 3.1)
- **The roll of challengers** — eight interlocutors question Yājñavalkya in turn; **Gārgī Vācaknavī** is the only woman among them, questioning in the **sixth** brāhmaṇa (BU 3.6) and again in the **eighth** (BU 3.8). (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 3.1–3.9)

## Gārgī Vācaknavī — the warp-and-woof inquirer

- **Gārgī Vācaknavī** — a *brahmavādinī* (woman expounder of Brahman), patronymic "**Vācaknavī**" (descendant of Vacaknu); presented as a peer in the assembly, fully entitled to challenge the master in open Vedic debate. (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 3.6, 3.8)
- **The first questioning (BU 3.6) — the chain of "warp and woof"** — Gārgī asks on what each cosmic level is "woven, warp and woof" (**otaṃ ca protaṃ ca**), driving Yājñavalkya up a regress through the worlds:
  - *The ascent (BU 3.6):* this world is woven on **water** → water on **air (vāyu)** → air on the **sky-worlds (antarikṣa-loka)** → those on the **gandharva-worlds** → on the **sun (āditya)** → on the **moon (candra)** → on the **stars (nakṣatra)** → on the **worlds of the gods (deva-loka)** → on the worlds of **Indra** → on the worlds of **Prajāpati** → on the **worlds of Brahman (brahma-loka)**.
  - **The warning** — when Gārgī presses "**on what, then, are the worlds of Brahman woven?**", Yājñavalkya checks her: *"Gārgī, do not question too much (atipraśna), lest your head fall off."* He warns she is asking about a deity that must not be reasoned past; Gārgī falls silent — for the moment. (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 3.6)
- **The second questioning (BU 3.8) — the two final arrows** — after the other challengers are spent, Gārgī asks the assembled Brahmins' leave to put **two questions**, comparing herself to a warrior of Kāśī or Videha who strings two whetted arrows; if Yājñavalkya answers both, none can defeat him in debate about Brahman. (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 3.8)
  - **Question one** — *"That which is above heaven, below the earth, between heaven and earth, and which is called past, present and future — on what is that woven, warp and woof?"* Yājñavalkya answers: on **space / ether (ākāśa)**. (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 3.8)
  - **Question two** — *"And on what, then, is ākāśa woven?"* Yājñavalkya answers with the celebrated teaching of the **Imperishable (akṣara)**: it is on the *akṣara* that ākāśa is woven, warp and woof. (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 3.8)
  - **The akṣara discourse** — Yājñavalkya describes the *akṣara* by **negation (neti)**: neither gross nor subtle, without shadow, attachment, taste, smell, eyes, ears, speech, mind, or measure; at its command sun and moon, heaven and earth, moments and hours hold their stations; it is the **unseen seer, unheard hearer, unthought thinker, unknown knower** — there is no other seer, hearer, thinker or knower but it. (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 3.8.8–3.8.11)
  - **Gārgī's concession** — satisfied, Gārgī turns to the assembled Brahmins and declares that none of them will defeat Yājñavalkya in debate about Brahman; she withdraws her challenge with honor — a *concession that is itself a vindication of her standing as questioner*. (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 3.8.12)
- **Status [scholarly]** — Gārgī is the paradigm of the female *ṛṣikā* / *brahmavādinī* in the dialogic Upaniṣads; her name recurs in later listings of learned women, and the **akṣara** doctrine she elicits is one of the most cited cosmological passages of the Upaniṣads. (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 3.6, 3.8; Wikipedia, *Gargi Vachaknavi*)

## Maitreyī — the wife who asked after immortality

- **Maitreyī** — one of the **two wives of Yājñavalkya**; the *Bṛhadāraṇyaka* says he had **Maitreyī, a knower of Brahman (brahmavādinī)**, and **Kātyāyanī**, who had only "a woman's understanding" (i.e., worldly knowledge) — the contrast itself marking Maitreyī as the learned partner. (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 2.4.1, 4.5.1)
- **The setting of the dialogue** — appears **twice**, in the **Maitreyī-brāhmaṇa** of BU 2.4 (Mādhyandina recension) and again, with fuller development, in BU 4.5; framed at the moment Yājñavalkya resolves to leave the householder life for **renunciation (sannyāsa / parivrājya)** and wishes to divide his estate between the two wives. (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 2.4, 4.5)
- **The question of wealth and deathlessness** — offered her share, Maitreyī asks: *"If this whole earth filled with wealth were mine, would I become immortal by it?"* Yājñavalkya replies **no** — life would be like that of the wealthy, *"but there is no hope of immortality through wealth"* (**amṛtatvasya tu nāśāsti vittena**). Maitreyī answers: *"What should I do with that by which I do not become immortal? Tell me, sir, what you know."* (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 2.4.2–3, 4.5.3–4)
- **The ātman discourse — "for love of the Self"** — Yājñavalkya then gives his great teaching: it is **not for love of the husband** that the husband is dear, but **for love of the Self (ātman)**; nor for love of the wife, sons, wealth, the gods, the Vedas, or the worlds — *"all this is dear for the sake of the Self."* The **ātman** alone is to be seen, heard, reflected on, and meditated upon (**ātmā vā are draṣṭavyaḥ śrotavyo mantavyo nididhyāsitavyaḥ**); by knowing the Self, all is known. (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 2.4.5, 4.5.6)
  - **The "lump of salt" simile** — Yājñavalkya likens the Self to a lump of salt dissolved in water: invisible yet present in every drop; so the great Being is a mass of pure consciousness (**vijñāna-ghana**) arising from and dissolving back into the elements, *"after death there is no consciousness (na pretya saṃjñāsti)"* — a saying that puzzles Maitreyī and draws further explanation of non-dual knowing. (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 2.4.12–14, 4.5.13–15)
- **Status [scholarly]** — Maitreyī is the canonical model of the woman who chooses **brahmavidyā over wealth**; the dialogue is foundational for Advaita (commented by [Ādi Śaṅkara](adi-shankara.md)) and is among the most quoted Upaniṣadic passages on the priority of the Self. (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 2.4, 4.5)

## The brahmavādinī category & later invocation

- **Brahmavādinī** — the technical term for a woman who studies, expounds, and debates Brahman; the *Bṛhadāraṇyaka* uses it of Maitreyī directly and exemplifies it in Gārgī's debate-conduct. Later tradition distinguishes **brahmavādinī** (lifelong scholar of the Veda) from **sadyovadhū** (one who studies until marriage). (Source: *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 2.4.1; *Harita-smṛti*, as cited in scholarship [late text])
- **Canonical exemplars of women's Vedic learning** — Gārgī and Maitreyī (alongside figures such as Lopāmudrā, Apālā, Ghoṣā, Romaśā in the Ṛgveda) are the stock names cited to evidence women's access to the Veda in the *brahmavādinī* age; both are invoked in later debates over women's right to **brahmavidyā** and Vedic study. (Source: Wikipedia, *Gargi Vachaknavi*, *Maitreyi*)
  - *Variant — Maitreyī the hymn-seer [disputed]:* some traditions credit Maitreyī with composing or "seeing" Ṛgvedic hymns (a handful of verses are attributed to a Maitreyī), conflating the Upaniṣadic wife with a Vedic *ṛṣikā* of the same name; the identification is uncertain. (Source: Wikipedia, *Maitreyi* [disputed])
  - *Reception [scholarly]:* in modern Hindu reform and women's-education discourse both names are standard touchstones for an indigenous precedent of the learned woman; institutions and colleges bear their names (e.g., Gargi College, Maitreyi College, Delhi).
- **Two clocks** — by the **scriptural clock** these sages belong to the Vedic teacher-lineage age (traditionally Tretā/Dvāpara within the genealogies of the *Bṛhadāraṇyaka*'s *vaṃśa* lists); by the **historical clock** the dialogues are fixed as **pre-Buddhist Upaniṣadic prose, c. 7th–6th c. BCE** — see the method in [`90-literature-corpus/`](../90-literature-corpus/README.md). [scholarly]
