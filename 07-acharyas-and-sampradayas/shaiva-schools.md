# Śaiva Schools & Orders

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](./README.md) — the **Śaiva** stream (Śiva as supreme *iṣṭa-devatā*), the most internally diverse of the sectarian fields, mapped onto its philosophical schools, ascetic orders and founding ācāryas. Up-link: section index [`README.md`](./README.md).
> Sits in the historical Kali-Yuga; dating is **[traditional]** (guru-succession / hagiography) vs **[scholarly]** (epigraphy / text-criticism) per the [two-clocks method](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md). Compare the Vaiṣṇava lineages ([Rāmānuja & Madhva](./ramanuja-and-madhva.md)) and the Smārta organizer [Ādi Śaṅkara](./adi-shankara.md), who debated and reordered several Śaiva orders below. Philosophical scaffolding: the six [Darśanas](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md); Tamil sources in the [Tamil canon](../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md).

## The two great divisions — Atimārga & Mantramārga

- **Atimārga ("outer / higher path")** — the older, exclusively ascetic stream, open only to Śaiva *sannyāsins*; named because adherents held it led **straight to liberation** (*sadyo-mukti*) without intermediate planes. Vedic-tinged (sometimes called *Raudra*, from Vedic Rudra). Sub-traditions: **Pāśupata**, the **Lākula** (a Pāśupata offshoot), and the **Kālāmukha** (offshoot of the Lākula). (Source: Wikipedia, *Shaivism*; New World Encyclopedia, *Kapalika and Kalamukha*)
- **Mantramārga ("path of mantras")** — the later, **tantric** stream open to both renunciates (*sannyāsī*) and householders (*gṛhastha*); the Āgama/Tantra-based traditions. Encompasses dualist **Śaiva Siddhānta**, the non-dual **Trika / Kashmir Śaivism**, the **Kaula / Kāpālika** Bhairava cults, and the later **Vīraśaiva** and **Nāth** sampradāyas. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shaivism*; AllSaivism)
  - *Three-fold textual map of the whole tradition [scholarly]:* **Āgama-śāstra** (revealed scripture), **Spanda-śāstra** (vibration doctrine), **Pratyabhijñā-śāstra** (recognition doctrine) — a division used especially within the Kashmir lineage. (Source: AllSaivism, *Kashmir Saivism*)

## Pāśupata — the oldest Śaiva order (Atimārga)

- **Pāśupata Śaivism** — the **earliest** identifiable Śaiva sub-tradition; ascetic, Atimārga. Attested at the start of the Common Era in the *Śānti Parva* of the Mahābhārata; an organized movement from the **1st–2nd c. CE**, dominant in South India **7th–14th c.** Doctrine = the five categories: *kārya* (effect/creation), *kāraṇa* (cause = Paśupati/Śiva), *yoga* (union), *vidhi* (observance), *duḥkhānta* (end of suffering). (Source: Wikipedia, *Pashupata Shaivism*; *Shaivism*)
- **Lakulīśa** — the reforming ācārya (~2nd c. CE), traditionally a **Gujarat** sage and regarded as an **incarnation of Śiva** (depicted *ūrdhva-liṅga*, holding a club, *lakula*); the system was renamed **Lākula / Lakulīśa-Pāśupata** after him. Purported author of the foundational **Pāśupata-sūtra**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Pashupata Shaivism*; Grokipedia, *Pashupata Shaivism*)
  - *Variant (sloap.org, "Transformation of Rudra to Pashupata") [scholarly]:* Lakulīśa as the historicized terminus of a longer evolution of the Vedic Rudra into a sectarian Paśupati cult — the "incarnation" framing is later theological overlay on an existing ascetic order.
- **Kaundinya** — commentator (4th–5th c. CE) whose **Pañcārtha-bhāṣya** ("commentary on the five topics") on the Pāśupata-sūtra is the chief surviving exposition; lays out the triad **pati** (lord), **paśu** (bound soul), **pāśa** (fetter) that all later Śaiva schools inherit. (Source: Wikipedia, *Pashupata Shaivism*; Grokipedia)
  - *Ascetic practice [scholarly]:* deliberate courting of public censure (*nindā*) — feigning madness, snoring, lewd gesture — to transfer one's bad karma to detractors and accept their merit; a graded path through *vyakta* (open) and *avyakta* (hidden) observance toward union in a cremation-ground (*śmaśāna*) final stage. (Source: Wikipedia, *Pashupata Shaivism*)

## Kālāmukha & the Atimārga ascetic orders

- **Kālāmukha ("black-faced ones")** — a prominent ascetic sect within the Pāśupata/Lākula stream, active in **South India / the Deccan** c. **8th–14th c. CE**; institutionally powerful, controlling temple-and-monastery complexes (notably the **Śakti-pariṣad** networks) in Karnataka. Named for a black forehead-mark; sometimes confused in polemics with the skull-bearing Kāpālikas, but were a temple-based monastic order, not purely cremation-ground ascetics. (Source: New World Encyclopedia, *Kapalika and Kalamukha*; Grokipedia, *Kalamukha*; Britannica)
  - *Variant on lineage [scholarly]:* the Kālāmukhas as an **offshoot of the Lākula**, itself an offshoot of the Pāśupata — the standard Atimārga descent Pāśupata → Lākula → Kālāmukha. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shaivism*)
  - *Historical fate [scholarly]:* the Kālāmukha temple-network in Karnataka was largely **absorbed by / displaced by the rising Vīraśaiva (Liṅgāyat) movement** from the 12th c., many of its sites passing to the new tradition. (Source: New World Encyclopedia; Grokipedia, *Kalamukha*)

## Kāpālika & Aghorī — the skull-bearing Bhairava cults

- **Kāpālika ("skull-men")** — a **tantric, non-Purāṇic** Śaiva cult of the Mantramārga/Kaula type, originating in medieval India c. **4th–8th c. CE**; from *kapāla*, "skull." Worshippers of **Bhairava** (terrible Śiva); the ascetic carried a **khaṭvāṅga** (skull-topped staff) and an **empty human skull** as a begging bowl, observed the *mahāvrata* ("great vow") in imitation of Śiva's penance for the beheading of Brahmā, and used cremation-ground, antinomian and transgressive *pañca-makāra* rites for *siddhi* and liberation. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kapalika*; Britannica, *Kapalikas*)
  - *Cultural footprint [folk]:* the Kāpālika is a stock figure in classical Sanskrit drama as a sinister tantric (e.g. in Bhavabhūti's *Mālatī-Mādhava*); [Ādi Śaṅkara's](./adi-shankara.md) *digvijaya* hagiographies feature a Kāpālika antagonist who seeks his head as a sacrifice. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kapalika*)
- **Aghorī (Aghorā)** — the **only surviving** order descended from the Kāpālika tradition; cremation-ground ascetics of Bhairava who pursue radical **non-duality** by transcending all distinction of pure/impure (corpse-meditation *śava-sādhanā*, skull-bowl, ash-smearing). (Source: Wikipedia, *Aghori*)
  - *Founder-figure [traditional]:* trace their order to **Bābā Kīnārām (Keenaram)** — a Vārāṇasī Śaivite ascetic said to have lived **~150 years**, dying in the **latter half of the 18th c.**; his seat is the Krīṃ-Kuṇḍ āśrama in Varanasi. (Source: Wikipedia, *Aghori*)
  - *Variant on antiquity [disputed]:* practitioners claim deep antiquity (back to a primordial Aghorā/Śiva), while academics date the **named order** to Kīnārām's 17th–18th c. reform of older Kāpālika practice. (Source: Wikipedia, *Aghori*)

## Śaiva Siddhānta — the dualist Mantramārga & the Tamil ācāryas

- **Śaiva Siddhānta** — the **earliest organized tantric (Mantramārga) Śaiva sampradāya**, originally a pan-Indian, Sanskrit, **dualist-pluralist** Āgamic school (c. 5th–6th c.), later flourishing as a distinctly **Tamil** theology. Core realism: **pati** (Śiva), **paśu** (souls) and **pāśa** (bonds — *āṇava*, *karma*, *māyā*) are **coexistent and beginningless**; liberation is the soul's union-without-identity with Śiva by grace. Twenty-eight **Śaiva Āgamas** + the Tamil **[Tirumuṟai](../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md)** and **Meykaṇḍa Śāstras** are canonical. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shaiva Siddhanta*; shaivam.org)
- **The Nāyaṉārs** — 63 Tamil Śaiva poet-saints (6th–9th c.; e.g. **Tirujñāna Sambandar, Appar, Sundarar, Māṇikkavācakar**) whose hymns form the *Tirumuṟai*; the devotional substrate the Siddhānta ācāryas later systematized. Hagiography: **Cēkkiḻār's** *Periya Purāṇam*. Cataloged with the [Tamil bhakti canon](../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Shaiva Siddhanta*)
- **The four Santāna Kuravar (Santāna ācāryas)** — the 13th–14th c. Tamil "lineage of gurus" who founded the **Meykaṇḍar Sampradāya** and authored the doctrinal core; revered as the *Santāna Kuravars*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Meykandar*, *Shaiva Siddhanta*; saivaphilosophy.blogspot)
  - **Meykaṇḍar (Meykaṇḍa Tēvar)** — 13th c.; author of the twelve-sūtra **Śivajñāna-bodham** (*Civañāṉa-bōtam*), the foundational systematic text of Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta, propounding pluralistic realism (God, souls, world coexistent and beginningless). (Source: Wikipedia, *Meykandar*)
  - **Aruḷnandi Śivācārya (Aruḷnanti Civam)** — disciple of Meykaṇḍar; author of the **Śivajñāna-siddhiyār** and *Irupaviruparu*, expanding the *Civañāṉa-bōtam*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shaiva Siddhanta*; saivaphilosophy.blogspot)
  - **Maṟaijñāna Sambandar (Umāpati's guru's line)** — disciple of Aruḷnandi; transmitter to Umāpati. (Source: WebSearch summary of *Shaiva Siddhanta*)
  - **Umāpati Śivācārya (Umāpati Civam)** — 14th c. (fl. c. 1313); composed **eight** further treatises explaining the Siddhānta, including the **Tiruvaruṭpayaṉ** and *Civappirakācam*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shaiva Siddhanta*)
  - *The fourteen Meykaṇḍa Śāstras [traditional]:* the works of the Santāna Kuravar plus others total **fourteen** Tamil texts (*Meykaṇḍa Cāttiraṅkaḷ*, "shastras that perceived the truth") held canonical by Tamil Śaivas. (Source: saivaphilosophy.blogspot, *14 Meykanda Sastras*)

## Kashmir Śaivism — Trika / the non-dual Mantramārga

- **Kashmir Śaivism (Trika)** — the **monistic / non-dual** Śaiva system of Kashmir (post-**850 CE**), drawing on the monistic **Bhairava Tantras**; "Trika" ("triad") for its threefold scheme (Śiva–Śakti–Nara, or the three goddesses Parā, Parāparā, Aparā). Holds the world to be a **real self-manifestation** of one absolute consciousness (Parama-Śiva), against Siddhānta dualism. Three textual streams: **Āgama, Spanda, Pratyabhijñā**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kashmir Shaivism*; AllSaivism; IEP, *Kashmiri*)
- **Vasugupta** — 9th c. founder-figure; tradition holds **Śiva revealed the Śiva-Sūtras** (77 aphorisms) to him, inscribed on a rock, to counter dualism — the seed-text of the whole school. (Source: New World Encyclopedia, *Kasmir Saivism*; SUNY, *Aphorisms of Śiva*)
  - **Spanda-śāstra** — the "vibration" doctrine: ultimate reality as **spanda**, the divine creative pulsation of Śiva–Śakti. Central texts the **Śiva-Sūtra** and the **Spanda-Kārikā** (9th c., attributed to Vasugupta or his disciple **Kallaṭa**). (Source: New World Encyclopedia; AllSaivism)
- **Somānanda** — early-10th c.; founder of the **Pratyabhijñā** ("recognition") school with his *Śiva-dṛṣṭi*; teacher of Utpaladeva. (Source: IEP, *Kashmiri*)
- **Utpaladeva** — c. **925–975 CE**; systematized the **Pratyabhijñā** philosophy ("recognition of one's own Self as Śiva") in the **Īśvara-pratyabhijñā-kārikā**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kashmir Shaivism*; New World Encyclopedia)
- **Abhinavagupta** — **c. 950–1015/1016 CE**; the towering polymath who **reconciled** the Āgama, Spanda and Pratyabhijñā streams into a unified non-dualism. Major works: the encyclopaedic **Tantrāloka**, its digest **Tantrasāra**, the **Īśvara-pratyabhijñā-vimarśinī**, and the **Gītārtha-saṃgraha** (a Kashmiri Gītā commentary); also seminal aesthetics (*rasa/dhvani*, *Abhinavabhāratī*). (Source: New World Encyclopedia; Wikipedia, *Kashmir Shaivism*)
  - **Kṣemarāja** — Abhinavagupta's disciple; popularizing commentator (*Pratyabhijñā-hṛdaya*, *Spanda-saṃdoha*, *Śiva-Sūtra-vimarśinī*). (Source: AllSaivism)
  - *Variant — Krama & Kaula sub-streams [scholarly]:* alongside Trika, Kashmir hosted the **Krama** (Kālī-centred, gradational worship) and **Kaula** lineages; later Śākta **Śrī Vidyā** is deeply interwoven, Śakti being internal to Śiva. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kashmir Shaivism*, *Shaivism*)

## Vīraśaiva / Liṅgāyat — the 12th-c. Kannada reform

- **Basava (Basavaṇṇa)** — **c. 1105–1167 CE**; Kannada poet-philosopher-statesman, treasurer and de facto chief minister under the Kalachuri king **Bijjala II** of Karnataka; credited as founder/organizer of the **Liṅgāyat (Vīraśaiva)** tradition — a Śaiva *bhakti* movement rejecting caste hierarchy, Vedic ritual sacrifice, temple and priestly mediation in favour of direct personal devotion to Śiva. (Source: Wikipedia, *Basava*; Grokipedia, *Basava*)
- **Iṣṭaliṅga** — Basava's signature practice: a small personal **Śiva-liṅga** worn on the body (in a pendant/casket), worshipped by every initiate **regardless of birth or gender**, replacing the Brahmanical sacred thread as the constant emblem of *bhakti*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Basava*)
- **Anubhava Maṇṭapa ("experience pavilion")** — the assembly Basava established at Basavakalyāṇ, called the **"first religious parliament in the world"**: an academy of mystics and *śaraṇas* (devotees) of all backgrounds who debated spiritual experience and produced the vachana corpus. (Source: Wikipedia, *Anubhava Mantapa*; Grokipedia)
- **Vachanas** — concise free-verse Kannada devotional/philosophical poems ("sayings") that carried Vīraśaiva thought in the vernacular; emphasized **labour (kāyaka) as worship**, social equality and ethics. Major *vachanakāras* (Source: Wikipedia, *Basava*; PolSci Institute):
  - **Allama Prabhu** — mystic, presiding head (*śūnya-siṃhāsana*) of the Anubhava Maṇṭapa.
  - **Akka Mahādevī** — woman saint-poet; renunciate devotee of *Cennamallikārjuna* (Śiva), foundational to Lingāyat social critique.
  - **Cennabasava, Siddharāma, Maḍivāḷa Mācideva** — among the leading *śaraṇa* poets of the assembly.
- *Variant — Vīraśaiva vs Liṅgāyat identity [disputed]:* one view treats **Vīraśaiva** and **Liṅgāyat** as synonyms (Basava reforming a pre-existing Vīraśaiva order with five *pañcācārya* peeṭhas of antiquity); another (and a modern political-legal claim in Karnataka) holds **Liṅgāyat = Basava's distinct, monotheistic, anti-Vedic religion**, separate from an older, more Vedic Vīraśaivism. (Source: Vajiram & Ravi, *Lingayats and Veerashaivas*; Medium, *From Basava to Karnataka Politics*)
- *Doctrine [scholarly]:* the soul's progress through the **ṣaṭ-sthala** (six stages) to union with Śiva (*aikya*); philosophically often classed as **Śakti-viśiṣṭādvaita** (qualified non-dualism through Śakti). (Source: Wikipedia, *Basava*; Lingayatism, Bharatpedia)

## Nāth Sampradāya — the Siddha-yogī order

- **Nāth (Nātha) Sampradāya** — a **Śaiva Siddha-yogī** order of India and Nepal that crystallized in the **12th–13th c.**, synthesizing Śaivism, Tantra, and Yoga (with Buddhist Siddha influence); its members are *yogīs* of the "Lord" (*nātha*) lineage with **Śiva (Ādinātha)** as first guru, organized as the **Navnāth** (nine masters). Believed to descend from earlier **Pāśupata, Kāpālika, Kaula and Siddha** cults. (Source: Wikipedia, *Natha Sampradaya*; Britannica, *Gorakhnath*)
- **Matsyendranāth (Macchindranāth)** — **9th–10th c.**; the foundational human guru of the Nāths and of the Kaula yoga stream; in Nepal identified with the bodhisattva **Avalokiteśvara/Karuṇāmaya** (Red Matsyendranāth of Patan). (Source: Wikipedia, *Natha Sampradaya*, *Gorakhnath*)
- **Gorakhnāth (Gorakṣanāth)** — **11th–13th c.** (dating disputed); disciple of Matsyendranāth and the chief **organizer** of the order and of **haṭha-yoga**; reputed author/inspiration of the *Gorakṣa-śataka*; founder of the **Kānphaṭa** ("split-ear," from the large initiatory earrings inserted through split ear-cartilage) yogī order and the Gorakhpur seat. (Source: Wikipedia, *Gorakhnath*; Britannica)
  - *Variant on dating [disputed]:* sources place Gorakhnāth anywhere from the 9th–10th to the 12th–13th c.; the connection of Gorakhnāth–Kānphaṭa–haṭha-yoga is, per Guy Beck, "beyond question." (Source: Wikipedia, *Gorakhnath*; *Natha Sampradaya*)
  - *Variant — the Navnāth roster [folk]:* the nine *nāths* vary by tradition but commonly include Ādinātha (Śiva), Matsyendra, Gorakṣa, Jālandhara, Kānipa/Kṛṣṇapāda, Caurangī, and others; regional Maharashtrian Nāth lists differ. (Source: Wikipedia, *Natha Sampradaya*)
- *Doctrinal/practical core [scholarly]:* perfection of the body as the vehicle of liberation — *kuṇḍalinī*, *haṭha-yoga*, *nāda* and the pursuit of *kāya-siddhi* / immortality (*jīvanmukti*); a key bridge from tantric Śaivism to later pan-Indian yoga. (Source: Wikipedia, *Natha Sampradaya*)
