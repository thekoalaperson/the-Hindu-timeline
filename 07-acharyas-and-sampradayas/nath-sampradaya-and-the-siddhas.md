# The Nāth Sampradāya, the Navnāth & the 84 Mahāsiddhas

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](README.md) — the **Siddha/Nāth esoteric world**: the Śaiva yogī order of "Lords" (*nātha*), its **Navnāth** roster of nine masters, and the trans-sectarian **84 Mahāsiddhas** (*Caurāsī Siddha*) shared with Vajrayāna Buddhism. Expands the stub Nāth block in [`shaiva-schools.md`](shaiva-schools.md).

## The order — Nāth Sampradāya, descent and structure

- **Nāth (Nātha) Sampradāya** — a **Śaiva Siddha-yogī** order of India and Nepal that crystallized **12th–13th c. CE**, fusing Śaivism, Tantra and Yoga with Buddhist Siddha influence; members are *yogīs* of the "Lord" (*nātha*) lineage, with **Śiva (Ādinātha)** as the first guru. (Source: Wikipedia, *Natha Sampradaya*; Britannica, *Gorakhnath*)
- **Esoteric descent [scholarly]** — the order is held to descend from the older **Pāśupata → Kāpālika → Kaula → Siddha** cremation-ground and tantric streams, inheriting their *kuṇḍalinī*, alchemical and antinomian sādhanā while disciplining it into a yogic monasticism (see [`shaiva-schools.md`](shaiva-schools.md)). (Source: Wikipedia, *Natha Sampradaya*)
- **Kānphaṭa ("split-ear") yogīs** — the most distinctive Nāth sub-order, named for the large **earrings** (*kuṇḍala* / *mudrā*) inserted through **split ear-cartilage** at initiation; organized by Gorakṣanāth around the **Gorakhpur** seat and a network of *maṭhas*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Gorakhnath*; Britannica)
- **Twelve panths [traditional]** — the Kānphaṭa order is traditionally divided into **twelve subdivisions** (*bārah panth*), each tracing to a particular Nāth master; the *Aughaṛ* (uninitiated/ash-smeared) and the eared *Darśanī* form the two main grades. (Source: Wikipedia, *Natha Sampradaya*)
- **Doctrinal/practical core [scholarly]** — perfection of the body as the vehicle of liberation: *haṭha-yoga*, *kuṇḍalinī*, *nāda* (inner sound), and the pursuit of *kāya-siddhi* / immortality (*jīvanmukti*); the great historical bridge from tantric Śaivism to pan-Indian yoga. Key text: the **Gorakṣa-siddhānta-saṅgraha**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Natha Sampradaya*; Gorakṣa-siddhānta-saṅgraha)
- **Tamil parallel [regional]** — the Nāth Siddhas overlap the southern **Tamil Siddhars** (*Cittar*), with shared figures (Gorakṣa = **Korakkar**, Matsyendra = **Macchamuni**) and a shared alchemy-and-yoga programme; cross-linked in [`tamil-siddhars.md`](tamil-siddhars.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Siddhar*, *Natha Sampradaya*)

## The Navnāth — the nine Nāthas

- **Navnāth ("nine Nāthas")** — the canonical line of nine perfected gurus from whom the order's *panths* descend; rosters **vary by region**, never to be merged. (Source: Wikipedia, *Navnath*, *Natha Sampradaya*)
  - *Pan-Indian / Kānphaṭa roster [traditional]:* **Ādinātha** (Śiva), **Matsyendra/Mīnanāth**, **Gorakṣa**, **Jālandhara/Jvālendra**, **Kāṇipa/Kṛṣṇapāda**, **Caurangī**, with additional masters filling the nine. (Source: Wikipedia, *Natha Sampradaya*; Nāth hagiography)
  - *Maharashtrian roster [regional]:* the Marathi **Navnāth** tradition lists **Matsyendra, Gorakṣa, Jālandhar (Jālindar), Kāṇifnāth, Carpaṭinath, Nāganath, Bhartṛnath (Bhartari), Revaṇnath, Gahininath** — and counts **Adbhangnath** among the masters; this is the line invoked in the [`jnaneshwar.md`](jnaneshwar.md) Vārkarī genealogy (Jñāneśvar's guru **Nivṛttināth** was initiated by Gahininath). (Source: Navnāth Bhaktisār; Wikipedia, *Navnath*)
- **Ādinātha** — Śiva himself as the primordial guru and source of the *nātha* teaching; the lineage's revelatory fountainhead. (Source: Nāth hagiography)
- **Matsyendranāth (Macchindranāth / Mīnanāth)** — **9th–10th c.**; first **human** guru of the Nāths and of the **Kaula** yoga stream; legend says he overheard Śiva's secret yoga while in a fish's belly. In Nepal he is identified with the bodhisattva **Avalokiteśvara / Karuṇāmaya** (Red Matsyendranāth of Patan), worshipped in a great chariot festival. (Source: Wikipedia, *Matsyendranath*, *Gorakhnath*)
  - *Kadali episode [folk]:* Matsyendra falls under enchantment in the women's kingdom of **Kadalī**; his disciple Gorakṣa enters in disguise and rouses him with the cry *"Cala Macchindra, Gorakh āyā!"* — a stock Nāth hagiographic motif. (Source: Nāth hagiography; Navnāth Bhaktisār)
- **Gorakṣanāth (Gorakhnāth)** — **11th–13th c.** (disputed); chief **organizer** of the order and of **haṭha-yoga**, disciple of Matsyendra; reputed author/inspiration of the **Gorakṣa-śataka**; founder of the **Kānphaṭa** order and the Gorakhpur *maṭha*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Gorakhnath*; Britannica)
  - *Variant on dating [disputed]:* placed anywhere 9th–13th c.; the Gorakhnāth–Kānphaṭa–haṭha-yoga link is, per Guy Beck, "beyond question." (Source: Wikipedia, *Gorakhnath*)
- **Jālandharanāth (Jvālendra / Hāḍipā / Jālindar)** — guru of **Kāṇipa** and (in the Maharashtrian cycle) of **Gopīcand**; associated with Jālandhar in the Punjab; appears in both Nāth and Buddhist Mahāsiddha lists. (Source: Wikipedia, *Jalandharnath*; Nāth hagiography)
- **Kāṇipā / Kṛṣṇapāda (Kāṇifnāth / Kāṇhapā)** — disciple of Jālandhara; the Nāth counterpart of the Buddhist Mahāsiddha **Kṛṣṇacārya/Kāṇha**; a major Maharashtrian Nāth and one of the *panth* founders. (Source: Wikipedia, *Natha Sampradaya*; Caturaśīti-siddha-pravṛtti)
- **Caurangīnāth (Pūraṇ Bhagat)** — the prince whose hands and feet are severed on a stepmother's false accusation and who is restored and perfected by Matsyendra/Gorakṣa; identified with the Mahāsiddha **Caurangī**; hero of the Punjabi **Pūraṇ Bhagat** legend. (Source: Nāth hagiography; Caturaśīti-siddha-pravṛtti)
- **Gahininath (Gaibīnāth)** — Maharashtrian Nāth, guru of **Nivṛttināth**, hence the spiritual grandfather of [`jnaneshwar.md`](jnaneshwar.md) and the channel by which the Nāth current feeds the **Vārkarī** movement. (Source: Navnāth Bhaktisār; Wikipedia, *Navnath*)
- **Revaṇnath, Carpaṭinath, Nāganath, Adbhangnath, Bhartṛnath** — the remaining Maharashtrian masters who complete the nine and found regional *panths*; **Carpaṭi** also appears in the Buddhist 84. (Source: Navnāth Bhaktisār)

## The living devotional cycle — Navnāth Bhaktisār & the ballads

- **Navnāth Bhaktisār / Navnāth Kathāsār** — the popular **Marathi prose-and-verse hagiographic cycle** (Mālū / Dhuṇḍisut Mālū, 18th c.) narrating the miracles, initiations and wanderings of the nine Nāths; still recited as a **devotional *grantha*** (*pothī*) in Maharashtrian households for *siddhi* and protection. (Source: Navnāth Bhaktisār / Navnāth Kathāsār)
- **Bhartṛhari (Bhartṛnath) cycle [folk]** — the **king of Ujjain** who renounces his throne (over the deathless-fruit and the unfaithful queen **Piṅgalā**) to become a Nāth yogī under Gorakṣa/Jālandhara; the subject of a vast north-Indian **mendicant ballad** tradition sung by *Jogī*-bards. (Source: Nāth hagiography; Bhartṛhari-Gopīcand ballads)
- **Gopīcand cycle [folk]** — the **Bengal/Rajasthan king** whom his mother **Maināvatī** persuades to renounce, initiated by **Jālandhara/Hāḍipā**; the **Bhartṛhari–Gopīcand** ballads are the most widely sung Nāth epics, dramatizing renunciation against royal and marital attachment. (Source: Nāth hagiography; Bhartṛhari-Gopīcand ballads)

## The 84 Mahāsiddhas — Caurāsī Siddha

- **Caurāsī Siddha ("the 84 perfected ones")** — the canonical roster of **eighty-four** tantric adepts, the legendary fountainhead of **haṭha-yoga, rasāyana alchemy** and **Sahaja Tantra**; **shared between Nāth (Hindu) and Vajrayāna-Buddhist** tradition, with substantial overlapping membership. (Source: Caturaśīti-siddha-pravṛtti; Wikipedia, *Mahasiddha*)
- **Caturaśīti-siddha-pravṛtti** — Abhayadatta Śrī's (12th c.) **"Lives of the Eighty-Four Siddhas,"** the standard Buddhist enumeration of the 84, each with a biographical legend and a doctrinal *dohā*; transmitted in Tibetan as the **Grub-thob brgyad-cu** (*grub thob brgyad cu rtsa bzhi*). (Source: Caturaśīti-siddha-pravṛtti; Grub-thob brgyad-cu)
- **Shared Hindu–Buddhist members [scholarly]** — figures claimed by **both** traditions include **Matsyendra/Mīnapā/Lūipa**, **Gorakṣa**, **Jālandhara (Jālandharipā)**, **Kāṇha/Kṛṣṇacārya (= Kāṇipa)**, **Caurangī**, and **Saraha**; the same names recur in Nāth and Vajrayāna lists, marking a single trans-sectarian Siddha milieu. (Source: Caturaśīti-siddha-pravṛtti; Wikipedia, *Mahasiddha*, *Natha Sampradaya*)
- **Lūipa** — conventionally the **first** of the 84; a royal renunciate who attains realization eating fish-entrails (*lohita*) on the Ganges; foundational guru of the Cakrasaṃvara transmission. (Source: Caturaśīti-siddha-pravṛtti)
- **Saraha** — the **arrow-smith** brahmin Siddha, author of the *Dohākoṣa* ("Treasury of Songs"); fountainhead of **Mahāmudrā / Sahaja** poetry and of the Apabhraṃśa *dohā* form later echoed by [Kabīr](kabir.md). (Source: Caturaśīti-siddha-pravṛtti)
- **Nāgārjuna (the Siddha)** — the **tantric alchemist** Nāgārjuna (distinguished from the Madhyamaka philosopher), master of **rasāyana** and *amṛta*-making; disciple **Caryāpā/Nāgabodhi**. (Source: Caturaśīti-siddha-pravṛtti)
- **Virūpa** — the Siddha who **stops the sun** to keep drinking at a tavern; foundational to the Sakya **Lamdré**; emblem of antinomian power. (Source: Caturaśīti-siddha-pravṛtti)
- **Tilopa & Nāropa** — the **Bengali** Siddha Tilopa and his disciple **Nāropa** (the *ṣaḍ-dharma* / Six Yogas), the human source of the **Kagyü** Mahāmudrā transmission to Marpa and Milarepa. (Source: Caturaśīti-siddha-pravṛtti; Vajrayāna Mahāsiddha tradition)
- *Variant on the number [scholarly]:* the "84" is a **typological** rather than exact count — different lists name overlapping but non-identical sets, and the Nāth and Buddhist rosters diverge in their tails while agreeing on the core; the number 84 itself is a symbolic completeness (cf. the 84 lakh *yonis*). (Source: Wikipedia, *Mahasiddha*; Caturaśīti-siddha-pravṛtti)
