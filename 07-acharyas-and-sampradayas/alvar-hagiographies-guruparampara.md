# Alvar hagiographies — the Guruparampara legends

> Where this sits: the sacred biographies (*caritam*) of the Tamil Vaiṣṇava Āḻvārs, as fixed in the Śrīvaiṣṇava *guruparampara* ("succession of teachers") literature — the miracle-lives that the roster in [alvars-and-nayanars.md](alvars-and-nayanars.md) compresses to one line each.
> Parent: [Ācāryas & Sampradāyas](README.md) · Master index: [../README.md](../README.md).

The twelve **Āḻvārs** left hymns (the *Nālāyira Divya Prabandham*) but almost no biography; their lives are preserved instead in a later **hagiographic layer** composed by the Śrīvaiṣṇava ācāryas to sanctify the poets as *nitya-sūris* (eternal attendants of Viṣṇu descended to earth). The two canonical vehicles are the Sanskrit **Divya Sūri Caritam** of **Garuḍavāhana Paṇḍita** (a Śrīraṅgam physician-devotee, c. 12th c. CE) and the Tamil-Maṇipravāḷa **Guruparampara Prabhāvam** of **Pinbaḻagiya Perumāḷ Jīyar** (the *6000-paḍi* recension, c. 13th–14th c. CE), with the shorter *3000-paḍi* of the Vaḍakku-Tiruvīdhi Piḷḷai line. These texts are **hagiography, not chronicle** — they narrate the Lord physically responding to His devotees. This file catalogs the five most-cited miracle-episodes; each saint's hymnal output is in the [roster file](alvars-and-nayanars.md), and [Nammāḻvār](nammalvar.md), [Āṇḍāḷ](andal.md), and [Tirumaṅgai](tirumangai-alvar.md) have their own deep-dives.

## The Mudal Āḻvārs at Tirukkōvilūr — the fourth in the dark

The **Mudal ("first three") Āḻvārs** — **Poigai**, **Bhūtam**, and **Pey** — are made ayonija (not womb-born): sprung respectively from a golden lotus, a *kurukkatti* creeper-flower, and a red lily on three successive days, each an aṃśa of Viṣṇu's emblems (discus, mace, sword — variously listed). The episode that binds them is the *Idai-kaḻi* ("narrow vestibule") legend at **Tirukkōvilūr** on the Peṇṇai river, seat of Trivikrama (Ulagaḷanda Perumāḷ). (Source: *Divya Sūri Caritam*, Mudal Āḻvār account; *Guruparampara Prabhāvam 6000-paḍi*)

- **The storm-corridor** — wandering separately, the three converge one dark, rain-lashed night on the porch/antechamber of a hermitage at Tirukkōvilūr. Poigai arrives first, finding room for **one to lie down**; Bhūtam comes, and there is room for **two to sit**; Pey comes, and all **three stand pressed together** in the blackness. (Source: *Guruparampara Prabhāvam 6000-paḍi*, life of Poigai Āḻvār)
- **The unseen fourth** — in the crush they feel a **fourth body** jostling among them. Poigai kindles a lamp to see who intrudes; by its light they perceive **Viṣṇu Himself** (with Śrī) wedged into the corridor, drawn by the density of their devotion. The three burst into spontaneous song. (Source: *Divya Sūri Caritam*, Mudal Āḻvār account)
- **The three lamps / the three Tiruvandhādhis** — Poigai lights a cosmic lamp — *"earth as the vessel, the surrounding ocean as ghee, the blazing sun as flame"* — and sings the **Mudal Tiruvandhādhi** (100 verses); Bhūtam lights a lamp of *bhakti* — *"love as the vessel, longing as ghee, the melting mind as wick"* — and sings the **Iraṇḍām Tiruvandhādhi**; Pey, needing no lamp, **sees Lakṣmī first, then Viṣṇu beside her** ("*Tirukkaṇḍēṉ*…") and sings the **Mūnṟām Tiruvandhādhi**. These 300 verses are traditionally the **earliest stratum of the Prabandham**. (Source: *Guruparampara Prabhāvam 6000-paḍi*)
  - *Variant (which emblem each embodies):* the trio are aligned with Viṣṇu's *pāñcāyudha* (discus/Sudarśana, conch/Pāñcajanya, mace/Kaumodakī) in differing assignments across recensions; the hagiographies do not agree on a fixed mapping. [folk]

## Tirumaḻiśai Āḻvār — "Kaṇikaṇṇaṉ pōha" and the rolled-up serpent-bed

**Tirumaḻiśai Āḻvār**, of disputed (reputedly part-sage) birth, settled at **Tiruveḵkā** in Kāñcīpuram, shrine of the reclining Viṣṇu **Yathoktakāri** ("He who did as He was told"). His devoted disciple was **Kaṇikaṇṇaṉ**. (Source: *Guruparampara Prabhāvam 6000-paḍi*, life of Tirumaḻiśai Āḻvār)

- **The king's demand** — the local Pallava king, hearing of Kaṇikaṇṇaṉ, commands the disciple to **sing the king's praises**; Kaṇikaṇṇaṉ refuses, saying he sings only of the Lord. The affronted king **banishes** him from the city. (Source: *Divya Sūri Caritam*, Tirumaḻiśai account)
- **"Kaṇikaṇṇaṉ pōha" — the departure verse** — Tirumaḻiśai will not remain where his disciple is exiled. He addresses the reclining deity in a Tamil verse: *"**Kaṇikaṇṇaṉ pōhinṟāṉ** — Kaṇikaṇṇaṉ is leaving; O Lord on the serpent-bed, **roll up Your snake-couch** (Ādiśeṣa) and come with us, for I too am departing."* The Lord **obeys**, gathering up His serpent-bed and following His devotee out of the shrine. (Source: *Guruparampara Prabhāvam 6000-paḍi*)
- **The city's darkness and the recall** — with Viṣṇu gone, Kāñcīpuram is thrown into gloom and disorder; the alarmed king relents and **begs all three back**. Tirumaḻiśai sings a return-verse — *"Kaṇikaṇṇaṉ is coming back; **spread out Your serpent-bed** again, O Lord"* — and Viṣṇu re-enters and reclines as before. From having "done as He was told," the deity keeps the name **Yathoktakāri / Sonna-vaṇṇam Seida Perumāḷ**, "the Lord who did exactly as spoken." (Source: *Divya Sūri Caritam*, Tirumaḻiśai account)
  - *Variant (the king's name):* the Pallava ruler is left unnamed in the older account and is variously supplied in later retellings; the hagiographies fix the *deity's* epithet, not the king. [folk]

## Tiruppāṇ Āḻvār — carried into Śrīraṅgam on Lōka Sāraṅga's shoulders

**Tiruppāṇ Āḻvār**, of the **Pāṇar** (bard) community, held himself outside the walls of **Śrīraṅgam**, singing to **Raṅganātha** from the far bank of the **Kāverī** and never presuming to cross the caste barrier into the sanctum. His single work is the **Amalanādipirāṉ** (10 verses). (Source: *Guruparampara Prabhāvam 6000-paḍi*, life of Tiruppāṇ Āḻvār; see [../06-sacred-geography/srirangam-ranganatha.md](../06-sacred-geography/srirangam-ranganatha.md))

- **The pebble of the priest** — the temple archaka **Lōka Sāraṅga Muni** goes to the Kāverī at dawn to draw water for the Lord's worship. Tiruppāṇ, lost in song on the path, does not hear the call to move aside; the priest **flings a stone** that strikes the Āḻvār's forehead and draws blood. Ashamed, Tiruppāṇ withdraws. (Source: *Divya Sūri Caritam*, Tiruppāṇ account)
- **The Lord bleeds** — returning to the sanctum, Lōka Sāraṅga finds **Raṅganātha's own forehead bleeding** at the same spot — the Lord bearing His devotee's wound. That night Raṅganātha commands the priest in a dream: bring Tiruppāṇ into My presence **borne on your own shoulders**, since his feet must not touch the ground he thinks unworthy of him. (Source: *Guruparampara Prabhāvam 6000-paḍi*)
- **The Muni-vāhana and the merging** — Lōka Sāraṅga carries Tiruppāṇ **on his shoulders** into the sanctum (hence the Āḻvār's epithet **Muṉivāhaṉa / Muṉivāhaṉar**, "he whose vehicle was a sage"). Set before Raṅganātha, Tiruppāṇ sings the **Amalanādipirāṉ**, a rapt vision moving **from the Lord's lotus-feet up to His face**; at the tenth verse — *"having seen the coral mouth… my eyes will look on nothing else"* — he **merges bodily into Raṅganātha** and vanishes, refusing to profane his Lord-filled eyes with any lesser sight. (Source: *Divya Sūri Caritam*, Tiruppāṇ account)
  - *Variant (the stone):* some tellings soften it to a warning pebble or a splash of water; the older accounts specify a stone drawing blood, the point being the mirrored wound on the deity. [folk]

## Kulaśekhara Āḻvār — the snake-pot ordeal

**Kulaśekhara Āḻvār**, the Cēra king-saint of *Perumāḷ Tirumoḻi* fame, was renowned for a love of **Bhāgavatas** (Śrīvaiṣṇava devotees) so total that it eclipsed his kingship — he seated wandering Vaiṣṇavas above his ministers and would drop affairs of state to hear the *Rāmāyaṇa* recited. (Source: *Guruparampara Prabhāvam 6000-paḍi*, life of Kulaśekhara Āḻvār)

- **The jealous ministers' plot** — resenting the king's favour to the Śrīvaiṣṇavas, courtiers **hide a jewel** from the palace/temple treasury and accuse the visiting devotees of theft, hoping to turn Kulaśekhara against them. (Source: *Divya Sūri Caritam*, Kulaśekhara account)
- **The snake-pot (*pāmbuk-kuḍam*) ordeal** — to vindicate the Bhāgavatas' innocence and shame the accusers, Kulaśekhara has a **pot containing a live venomous cobra** brought in and thrusts his **bare hand inside**, declaring that if the devotees are guiltless the serpent will not strike. He **draws out his hand unharmed**; the snake does not bite, the devotees are cleared, and the plot collapses. (Source: *Guruparampara Prabhāvam 6000-paḍi*)
- **Significance** — the ordeal becomes the emblem of Kulaśekhara's doctrine that **the Lord's servants are inseparable from the Lord**; an insult to a Bhāgavata is an insult to Viṣṇu. His hymns voice the same self-abasing *dāsya* devotion (wishing to be a step, a fish, a bird at Tirupati/Śrīraṅgam). (Source: *Divya Sūri Caritam*, Kulaśekhara account)
  - *Variant (identity):* tradition identifies him with a historical Cēra ruler (often equated with **Kulaśekhara Perumāḷ**); the exact king is [disputed] and the equation is [traditional].

## Periyāḻvār — the Tiruppallāṇḍu and the Lord on Garuḍa

**Periyāḻvār (Viṣṇucittar)**, the Brahmin garland-maker of **Srivilliputhur** and foster-father of [Āṇḍāḷ](andal.md), earned his honorific "the *great* Āḻvār" by an act of *vātsalya* (parental protectiveness) toward God Himself. (Source: *Guruparampara Prabhāvam 6000-paḍi*, life of Periyāḻvār)

- **The debate at Madurai** — the Pāṇḍya king **Vallabhadeva** sets a purse of gold on a pillar and summons scholars to establish the **supreme deity (para-tattva)**. Commanded in a dream by the Lord, the unlettered Viṣṇucittar attends, and **proves from scripture that Nārāyaṇa is supreme**, winning the contest and the purse. (Source: *Divya Sūri Caritam*, Periyāḻvār account)
- **The Lord on Garuḍa** — as Viṣṇucittar is paraded in triumph through Madurai on the royal elephant, **Viṣṇu appears in the sky mounted on Garuḍa** (with Śrī) to bless and witness His victorious devotee. (Source: *Guruparampara Prabhāvam 6000-paḍi*)
- **Tiruppallāṇḍu — the benediction to God** — seeing the Lord thus exposed to the world's dangers and "evil eye," Viṣṇucittar is seized by a **parent's terror for a beloved child's safety**; jingling small bells/cymbals, he spontaneously sings the **Tiruppallāṇḍu** — *"**Pallāṇḍu pallāṇḍu**…" ("many years, many years — may You live long!")* — a *maṅgalāśāsana*, invoking **protection and long life upon Viṣṇu Himself** rather than begging boons of Him. For this inversion he is named **Periyāḻvār**, and the Tiruppallāṇḍu is placed **first in the entire Nālāyira Divya Prabandham**. (Source: *Divya Sūri Caritam*, Periyāḻvār account)
  - *Variant (the vision's place):* the Garuḍa-mounted appearance is set over Madurai in the debate-account; some retellings echo it at Srivilliputhur. The constant is the *maṅgalāśāsana* motive — love that fears for God. [folk]

## The two clocks — dating the Āḻvārs

- **Scriptural / traditional (cyclic) frame** — the *Guruparampara* assigns the Āḻvārs legendary lives spanning the **Dvāpara and early Kali yugas**, with the Mudal Āḻvārs placed as early as **c. 4203 BCE** and the line closing before **~2700 BCE**; the hagiographies treat all twelve as *nitya-sūris* incarnate, not as ordinary historical persons. [traditional] [disputed]
- **Academic / historical frame** — modern scholarship dates the Āḻvārs to **[scholarly] c. 6th–9th c. CE**, in the Pallava–Pāṇḍya Tamil country, and the *hagiographic texts themselves* later still — the **Divya Sūri Caritam c. 12th c. CE** and the **Guruparampara Prabhāvam 6000-paḍi c. 13th–14th c. CE** [late text]. The miracle-episodes are therefore **devotional biography composed centuries after the saints**, encoding Śrīvaiṣṇava theology (*prapatti*, *bhāgavata-śeṣatva*, *maṅgalāśāsana*) in narrative form.

## Sources

**Primary (hagiographic) texts**
- **Divya Sūri Caritam** of Garuḍavāhana Paṇḍita (Sanskrit, c. 12th c. CE) — verse-lives of the Āḻvārs; accounts of the Mudal Āḻvārs (Tirukkōvilūr), Tirumaḻiśai, Tiruppāṇ, Kulaśekhara, and Periyāḻvār.
- **Guruparampara Prabhāvam, 6000-paḍi** of Pinbaḻagiya Perumāḷ Jīyar (Tamil-Maṇipravāḷa, c. 13th–14th c. CE); with the shorter *3000-paḍi* recension — the standard Śrīvaiṣṇava succession-history and Āḻvār hagiography.
- **Nālāyira Divya Prabandham** — the hymns the legends attach to: *Mudal/Iraṇḍām/Mūnṟām Tiruvandhādhi* (Mudal Āḻvārs), *Amalanādipirāṉ* (Tiruppāṇ), *Perumāḷ Tirumoḻi* (Kulaśekhara), *Tiruppallāṇḍu* (Periyāḻvār).
