# Śākta & Tantra Lineages

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](README.md) — the **Goddess-centred (Śākta)** and **Tantric** streams of teacher-lineages (*sampradāya / paramparā*), distinct from the Vedānta *ācāryas* catalogued elsewhere in this section: [Ādi Śaṅkara](adi-shankara.md), [Rāmānuja & Madhva](ramanuja-and-madhva.md). Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md).
> Sits in the historical Kali-Yuga; most datable figures fall **c. 6th–18th c. CE** by [scholarly] dating, while the lineages claim Vedic/Purāṇic ṛṣi-origins (Agastya, Dattātreya, Vasiṣṭha). Two-clocks method: [`recorded-history.md`](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md). The pantheon worshipped here — the great Goddess and the **Daśa Mahāvidyā** — is mapped in [`../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md), [Devī Māhātmya & the Great Goddess](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devi-mahatmya-and-the-great-goddess.md), and [Satī, Dakṣa & Pārvatī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md).

## Orientation: the two great axes of Tantra

- **Śākta vs Śaiva Tantra** — Tantra (the *Āgama* corpus, revealed as Śiva–Śakti dialogue) divides by ruling deity. **Śākta** streams worship the Goddess as *para-śakti* (supreme power); **Śaiva** streams (Trika, Krama, Spanda, Pratyabhijñā of Kashmir) worship Śiva but are heavily Goddess-inflected — the boundary is porous, and Abhinavagupta's synthesis is essentially a Goddess-cored Śaivism. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kashmir Shaivism*, *Shaktism*)
- **The two streams of Śākta Tantra** — classically **Śrīkula** ("family of Śrī/Lalitā Tripurasundarī," benign, southern, Brahmanically assimilated) and **Kālīkula** ("family of Kālī," fierce, originally Kashmiri/eastern, transgressive). Srividya descends from the former; Krama and the Mahāvidyā cults from the latter. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shri Vidya*, *Shaktism*)
- **The two paths (*ācāra*) — Dakṣiṇācāra vs Vāmācāra** — the practical axis cutting across all streams; see [§ Vāmācāra vs Dakṣiṇācāra](#vāmācāra-vs-dakṣiṇācāra--the-two-paths) below. Seven *ācāras* are often listed (Veda, Vaiṣṇava, Śaiva, Dakṣiṇa, Vāma, Siddhānta, Kaula), grouping into the two broad heads. (Source: Indica Today, *Demystifying Tantra IV*)

## Śrīvidyā — the Śrīkula lineage of Lalitā Tripurasundarī

- **Śrīvidyā** — the great south-Indian Śākta-Tantric system centred on **Lalitā Tripurasundarī** ("Beautiful Goddess of the Three Worlds"), worshipped through the **Śrī Cakra / Śrī Yantra** (nine interlocking triangles) and the secret **Śrī Vidyā mantra** (the *pañcadaśākṣarī*, 15 syllables; esoterically extended to the *ṣoḍaśī*, 16). The most Brahmanically respectable and Vedānta-assimilated of the Tantric schools. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shri Vidya*; Hindupedia, *Sri Vidya*)
  - *The Śrī Cakra:* a *yantra* of nine *āvaraṇa* (enclosures) read either outward-to-inward (*saṁhāra-krama*, dissolution) or inward-out (*sṛṣṭi-krama*, creation); Bhāskararāya's commentary fixed the placement of **108 deities** within it. (Source: vedadhara.com; tantrasadhana.app)
- **The Agastya–Hayagrīva transmission [traditional]** — the foundational charter-myth: in the *Lalitopākhyāna* of the **Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa**, the horse-headed Viṣṇu-form **Hayagrīva** narrates the story, the *Lalitā Sahasranāma* (1000 names) and *Lalitā Triśatī* (300 names) to the ṛṣi **Agastya**. Agastya, his wife **Lopāmudrā**, plus **Durvāsa** and **Dattātreya** are named as Purāṇic Śrīvidyā adepts. (Source: Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa via Wikipedia, *Shri Vidya*; redzambala; tantrasadhana.app)
- **The three sampradāyas (*matha-traditions*)** — Śrīvidyā transmits via three named lineages: **Dakṣiṇāmūrti**, **Ānanda-Bhairava**, and **Hayagrīva**. The lineage named in the Agastya charter is the Hayagrīva tradition. (Source: Hindupedia, *Sri Vidya*)
- **Kādi / Hādi / (Sādi / Kahādi) vidyās [traditional]** — the mantra-school distinction by opening syllable of the 15-syllable vidyā: **Kādimata** (begins *ka-e-ī-la-hrīṁ…*; associated with Manmatha/Kāmarāja and the sage **Lopāmudrā**'s rival, the *Kāmarāja-vidyā*) vs **Hādimata** (begins *ha-sa-ka-la-hrīṁ…*; the *Lopāmudrā-vidyā*). Rarer **Sādi** and **Kahādi** recensions are also catalogued. (Source: Hindupedia, *Sri Vidya*; Indica Today)
  - *Variant (legendary lineage-sages):* the *paramparā* of the vidyā is traced through twelve "knowers" (*upāsakas*) — among them **Manu, Candra, Kubera, Lopāmudrā, Manmatha (Kāmarāja), Agastya, Nandīśa, Sūrya, Indra, Skanda, Śiva, Durvāsa** — split between the Kādi (Manmatha-led) and Hādi (Lopāmudrā-led) lines. (Source: Hindupedia, *Sri Vidya*) [traditional]
- **Samaya vs Kaula within Śrīvidyā** — the internal split: **Samayācāra** ("of the convention/accord," the *antaryāga* internal worship in the cakras of the subtle body, aligned with Dakṣiṇācāra and championed as the pure path by Lakṣmīdhara) vs **Kaulācāra** (external *bahiryāga*, ritual worship of the Śrī Cakra and the *kuṇḍalinī*, tending Vāmācāra). (Source: Wikipedia, *Shri Vidya*; Indica Today)

### Śrīvidyā teachers & commentators

- **Gauḍapāda (Śrīvidyā-attributed) [disputed]** — the *Subhagodaya* and *Śrīvidyāratnasūtra* are ascribed to a "Gauḍapāda"; tradition conflates him with the proto-Advaitin author of the *Māṇḍūkya-Kārikā* (Śaṅkara's *parama-guru*), linking Śrīvidyā to early Advaita. The identification is doubted by scholars. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shri Vidya*) [scholarly]
- **Ādi Śaṅkara (Śrīvidyā attribution) [disputed]** — the devotional *Saundaryalaharī* ("Waves of Beauty," 100 verses on Tripurasundarī and the Śrī Cakra) and the *pañcāyatana* installation of Devī are traditionally ascribed to [Śaṅkara](adi-shankara.md), making him a notional Śrīvidyā founder; modern scholarship treats the *Saundaryalaharī* authorship as uncertain/late. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shri Vidya*) [scholarly]
- **Lakṣmīdhara (Lakṣmīdhara-deśika)** — c. 16th c. commentator on the *Saundaryalaharī* whose gloss became the classic statement of the **Samaya (Dakṣiṇa/internal)** path, explicitly contrasting it with and disparaging the Kaula. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shri Vidya*)
- **Bhāskararāya Makhin (Bhāsurānanda-nātha)** — **1690–1785** [scholarly], Maharashtrian Brahmin polymath and the supreme systematizer of Śrīvidyā; author of **40+ works** including the *Saubhāgya-bhāskara* (commentary on the *Lalitā Sahasranāma*), *Varivasyā-rahasya*, *Setubandha* (on the *Nityāṣoḍaśikārṇava*) and the *Guptavatī* (on the *Devī Māhātmya*). Reconciled the diverse vidyās into one coherent philosophy; his **Bhāskararāya-sampradāya** remains an active living lineage. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shri Vidya*; vedadhara.com)

## Kālīkula — Kaula, Kashmiri Krama & the fierce stream

- **Kaula / Kulamārga** — the broad "clan-path" of Tantra emphasizing the *kula* (the embodied power-network of Śiva–Śakti, the *yoginīs*, and the practitioner's own body), Goddess-centred and willing to use the transgressive *pañcamakāra*. Subdivides regionally and by deity; the Goddess-fierce wing is the **Kālīkula**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kashmir Shaivism*; Indica Today)
- **Kālīkula ("Family of Kālī")** — the cluster of early, "quasi-shamanic," cremation-ground Goddess cults of the first millennium whose presiding deity was **Kālī** (in her many forms — Kālasaṅkarṣiṇī, Guhyakālī, etc.). Source-stream for the Krama and, evolving, for the Daśa Mahāvidyā framework. (Source: hareesh.org, *A Path of Pure Radiance*; Indica Today)
- **Krama ("Sequence/Gradation")** — the radical non-dualist Kālīkula school of Kashmir centred on the **sequential worship of Kālī** as the cyclic phases (*krama*) of cognition (emission–maintenance–retraction–the nameless fourth); deeply influential on, and finally fused into, the Trika. Key early texts: *Kālīkulakramasadbhāva*, *Devīpañcaśatikā*; revealed via the Goddess-form **Maṅgalā / Kālī of the Karavīra cremation ground**. (Source: hareesh.org; academia.edu, *Introduction to the Tantric Krama lineage*)
- **Trika** — the "triadic" (Parā–Parāparā–Aparā Goddesses) non-dual Śaiva-Śākta system of Kashmir, scripturally rooted in the *Mālinīvijayottara*, *Siddhayogeśvarīmata* and *Tantrasadbhāva*; the philosophical chassis into which Krama and Kaula were synthesized. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kashmir Shaivism*)

### Kashmiri lineage-teachers

- **Vasugupta** — c. 9th c.; received the *Śiva Sūtras* (per legend revealed on a rock at Mahādeva mountain), fountainhead of the **Spanda** ("vibration") doctrine. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kashmir Shaivism*)
- **Somānanda → Utpaladeva** — 9th–10th c.; founders of the **Pratyabhijñā** ("recognition") philosophy (*Śivadṛṣṭi*; *Īśvara-pratyabhijñā-kārikā*) — the intellectual spine of Kashmiri non-dualism. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kashmir Shaivism*)
- **Abhinavagupta** — **c. 950–1016 CE** [scholarly]; the towering synthesizer, initiated in **Trika, Pratyabhijñā, Kaula, Krama and Śaiva Siddhānta** lineages. His encyclopedic ***Tantrāloka*** (37 chapters), *Tantrasāra* and *Mālinīślokavārttika* fused the Krama's Kālī-core into a Kaula-Trika; received the Kaula transmission from his guru **Śambhunātha**. Also the master aesthetician (*Abhinavabhāratī*, *Dhvanyāloka-locana*). (Source: Wikipedia, *Kashmir Shaivism*; Dupuche, *Abhinavagupta: The Kula Ritual*)
- **Kṣemarāja** — early 11th c.; Abhinavagupta's chief disciple, author of the *Spanda-nirṇaya*, *Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya* and *Śivasūtra-vimarśinī* — the great consolidator/commentator. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kashmir Shaivism*)
  - *Variant — northern vs southern Trika genealogy [scholarly]:* later Trika divides into the abstract "northern" (Pratyabhijñā-heavy) and the ritual "southern" recensions; the Krama is sometimes treated as a fourth, semi-independent *āmnāya* (the *uttarāmnāya*, "northern transmission") rather than a sub-branch. (Source: academia.edu, *Krama lineage*)

## The Daśa Mahāvidyā cults

- **Daśa Mahāvidyā ("Ten Great Wisdom-Goddesses")** — the canonical decad of fierce-to-benign Tantric goddesses (**Kālī, Tārā, Tripurasundarī/Ṣoḍaśī, Bhuvaneśvarī, Bhairavī, Chinnamastā, Dhūmāvatī, Bagalāmukhī, Mātaṅgī, Kamalā**), each with its own mantra, yantra, *avaraṇa* and dedicated cult. Their grouping (post-Purāṇic, **c. 6th c. CE** onward) marks the rise of *bhakti* within Śāktism and the absorption of yoginī, Śaiva, Vaiṣṇava and Vajrayāna-Buddhist deity-streams into one framework. Full catalogue: [`../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md`](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Mahavidya*; Indica Today, *Dasha Mahavidyas*)
- **Origin myth [traditional]** — the Mahāvidyās emanate from **Satī** (or Pārvatī) when she blocks Śiva's exit in a quarrel, surrounding him with ten terrifying forms — tying the cult to the [Satī–Dakṣa cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Mahavidya*)
- **Buddhist–Hindu cross-stream (Tārā) [scholarly]** — **Tārā**, the second Mahāvidyā, is shared with Vajrayāna Buddhism; her *Nīla-Sarasvatī* / *Ugratārā* forms and the *Tārā Tantra* show the Buddhist-Tantric absorption, especially strong at Kāmākhyā and Tārāpīṭha. (Source: Wikipedia, *Mahavidya*; Indica Today)
  - *Variant (list-order & membership):* the decad's membership and sequence vary by text — some lists substitute **Ṣoḍaśī** for Tripurasundarī, or swap **Annapūrṇā / Bālā / Durgā** in regional recensions; sub-counts of "Mahāvidyā + Siddha-vidyā" classes also differ. (Source: Wikipedia, *Mahavidya*; mahavidya.ca) [disputed]

## Kāmākhyā & Assam Śāktism — the eastern yoni-pīṭha

- **Kāmākhyā (Kāmrūp, Assam)** — pre-eminent **yoginī-pīṭha** and one of the foremost of the 51 [Śakti Pīṭhas](../06-sacred-geography/README.md): the spot where Satī's *yoni* (vulva) fell; the cult-image is a **natural yoni-stone** in a cave-sanctum, the "mouth of the yoginīs." Hub of a distinctive **Kulācāra / Yoginī-Kaula** Tantra blending Hindu, tribal and Buddhist (Vajrayāna) elements. (Source: Wikipedia, *Mahavidya*; yoginiashram.com; *Religions of South Asia*, equinoxpub)
- **Foundational texts** — the **Kālikā Purāṇa** (c. 10th–11th c., the locus classicus for Kāmākhyā's myth and blood-sacrifice rites) and the **Yoginī Tantra** (a "precious source of legendary, semi-historical and topographical traditions about the Goddess"), with the **Kāmākhyā Tantra**. (Source: Indica Today; equinoxpub)
- **Daśa Mahāvidyā temple-complex** — Kāmākhyā hill hosts dedicated shrines to all ten Mahāvidyās, making the site the architectural embodiment of the decad-cult; Kāmākhyā herself is read as a form of Kālī/Tripurā. (Source: Wikipedia, *Mahavidya*; yoginiashram.com)
  - *Variant — "two sides of Kamakhya" [scholarly]:* scholarship distinguishes the erotic/fertility (*kāma*, the yoni) and the deathly/sacrificial (cremation, Tārā) registers blended at the site — the "sweetening of sex and death" in Tantra. (Source: equinoxpub, *The Roots of the Two Sides of Kamakhya*)

## Bengal Śāktism — the bhakti turn

- **The 18th-c. Śākta-bhakti movement** — until the eighteenth century Śāktism was largely esoteric, the preserve of Tantric ascetics; in Bengal it was opened to lay devotees through vernacular **Śyāmā-saṅgīt** (Kālī-songs), reframing the terrifying Goddess as the loving **Mother**. (Source: everyculture.com, *Bengali Shakta*; mahavidya.ca)
- **Rāmprasād Sen** — **c. 1718/1723–1775** [traditional]; the founding Śākta poet-saint of Bengal, author of the *Rāmprasādī* corpus of Kālī-songs; **first** to address Kālī with such tender intimacy — as mother, even as a little girl — rather than as the fierce Tantric deity. Court-poet under Rājā Kṛṣṇacandra of Nadia; subject of many miracle-legends (Kālī as a girl repairing his fence, etc.). (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramprasad Sen*; Britannica)
- **Kamalākānta Bhaṭṭācārya** — **c. 1769–1821** [traditional]; the great successor to Rāmprasād in both poetry and Tantric lifestyle, court-poet of Burdwan; his *Sādhaka-rañjana* / *Kamalākānter Gīti* deepened the Śākta-bhakti song tradition. (Source: Wikipedia, *Kamalakanta Bhattacharya (Bengal)*)
- **Bāmākhepā ("the mad one of Bāmā/Tārā")** — **1837–1911**; born **Bāmācaraṇ Caṭṭopādhyāy** (b. Śivarātri midnight, Āṭlā village, Birbhum); the most famous Tantric of **Tārāpīṭha**, the cremation-ground Śakti-pīṭha of **Tārā**. Disciple of **Kailāśpati Bābā**; perfected *yoga* and Tantric *sādhanā* in the *śmaśāna*, became Tārāpīṭha's spiritual head, and is credited with "sweetening" the terrifying Tārā into an intimate, accessible Mother. *Khepā* ("mad / divinely intoxicated") is a title of high praise. (Source: Wikipedia, *Bamakhepa*; academia.edu, *The Sweetening of Death*)
  - *Variant — relation to Ramakrishna [folk]:* Bengali tradition often pairs Bāmākhepā with **Ramakrishna Paramahaṁsa** (1836–1886, Dakṣiṇeśvar Kālī-priest) as the two great 19th-c. Bengali Goddess-mystics; their meeting is recounted in popular hagiography but is hard to date precisely. (Source: kalimandir.org; popular hagiography) [folk]

## Vāmācāra vs Dakṣiṇācāra — the two paths

- **Dakṣiṇācāra ("right-hand conduct")** — the orthodox, *sāttvic*, externally pure path: Tantric ritual conducted within Brahmanical social/ethical norms, with the **pañcamakāra symbolically substituted** (milk for wine, sesame/honey for meat, etc.) and the union internalized as *kuṇḍalinī* meditation. Aligned with the **Samaya** path of Śrīvidyā. (Source: Wikipedia, *Dakṣiṇācāra*; Grokipedia)
- **Vāmācāra ("left-hand conduct")** — the heterodox, transgressive path that deliberately **breaks taboo** to transcend the dualities of pure/impure, using the literal **Pañcamakāra ("Five Ms")**: *madya* (wine), *māṁsa* (meat), *matsya* (fish), *mudrā* (parched grain), *maithuna* (ritual intercourse). Identified with the **Kaula** path; practiced (in controlled, initiatory form) in Kālīkula, Krama, Kāmākhyā and the fiercer Mahāvidyā cults. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vamachara*, *Panchamakara*; yogawiz.com)
  - *Variant — the seven ācāras [traditional]:* a graded ladder is given — **Vedācāra → Vaiṣṇavācāra → Śaivācāra → Dakṣiṇācāra → Vāmācāra → Siddhāntācāra → Kaulācāra** — so Vāma and Kaula are *higher* rungs of a single ascent, not a separate "black" path; the householder begins low and the realized adept ends in Kaula. (Source: Indica Today, *Demystifying Tantra IV*) [traditional]
  - *Variant — "left/right" as Goddess-orientation [scholarly]:* some readings tie *vāma* not to morality but to the **Goddess's left side / the *vāmā* (woman, Śakti) as the active partner**, the Kālīkula's feminine-led worship vs the Śrīkula's more Śiva-balanced rite. (Source: Grokipedia, *Vamachara*) [scholarly]
- **Caution [scholarly]** — the neat "left = evil / right = good" binary is partly a colonial-era and reformist overlay; in the tradition's own terms both are valid *adhikāra*-graded disciplines, and the transgressive elements are hedged by strict initiation (*dīkṣā*) and secrecy. (Source: Wikipedia, *Left-hand path and right-hand path*; Grokipedia)
