# Liṅgāyat / Vīraśaiva Vachana Saints — Basava, Akka Mahādevi, Allama

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](README.md) — the **12th-c. Kannada Śaraṇa movement** of Kalyāṇa (Basavakalyāṇ, Karnataka): the *vachana* poet-saints, the **Anubhava Maṇṭapa** ("hall of experience"), and the radical reform that became **Liṅgāyatism / Vīraśaivism** — a Śiva-bhakti path rejecting caste, Vedic ritual, temple-priesthood and the karmic cycle of rebirth. Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md).
> This node expands the brief Vīraśaiva entry in [`shaiva-schools.md`](shaiva-schools.md) (where the Liṅgāyats are placed in the Mantramārga and shown absorbing the older Kālāmukha temple-network of Karnataka). For the parallel vernacular bhakti world of the north/west — Kabīr, Mīrā, the sant-mat — see [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md); the Tamil Śaiva singers (Nāyaṉārs) who precede this movement are in [`alvars-and-nayanars.md`](alvars-and-nayanars.md).
> Sits in the historical Kali-Yuga. Dating split **[traditional]** (hagiography / sectarian *purāṇa* / *jayantī*) vs **[scholarly]** (epigraphy / Kalachuri political chronology / text-criticism) — [two-clocks method](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md). The corpus's literary place is in the [Kannada/vernacular bhakti stream](../90-literature-corpus/README.md).

## The movement in one frame — the Śaraṇa revolution

- **Śaraṇa ("one who has surrendered" to Śiva)** — the self-designation of the 12th-c. Kannada devotees gathered around Basava at Kalyāṇa; a lay, householder, anti-clerical fellowship of all castes and both sexes who composed *vachanas* and lived by **kāyaka** (sanctified labor) and **dāsoha** (sharing). The whole movement is the **Śaraṇa movement** / *Śaraṇa chaḷuvaḷi*. (Source: Basava Samithi, *Sharana Philosophy*; Grokipedia, *Sharana (Hinduism)*) [scholarly]
- **Vachana ("saying, utterance")** — the movement's literary vehicle: short, free-verse, rhythmic Kannada prose-poems, each closing with the poet's chosen **aṅkita / mudrā** (signature-name of Śiva). Spontaneous, confessional, anti-ritual, in the spoken tongue — not Sanskrit. The **Anubhava Maṇṭapa** was its fountainhead; ~**300+ named vachanakāras** (vachana-composers) are known, ~**30+ of them women** (the *Śaraṇe*). (Source: Grokipedia, *Vachana sahitya*; Wikipedia, *Anubhava Mantapa*) [scholarly]
- **Two-clock note on the term "Vīraśaiva" vs "Liṅgāyat"** — modern usage splits: many follow Basava as **founder of a distinct Liṅgāyat dharma** (anti-Vedic, anti-caste, casteless burial not cremation); others read it as **Vīraśaivism**, an older Āgamic Śaiva sect (with five *pañcācārya* maṭha-seats: Kedār, Śrīśaila, Ujjain, Varanasi/Kāśī, Rambhāpuri) that Basava reformed. The legal/political dispute over whether Liṅgāyat is a separate religion from Hinduism is a live 20th–21st c. controversy in Karnataka. (Source: Wikipedia, *Lingayatism*; Vajiram & Ravi, *Lingayats and Veerashaivas*) [disputed]
  - *Variant (scholarly consensus):* Basava "**revived, refined and energized an already existing tradition**" rather than founding Liṅgāyatism ex nihilo — the founder/reviver question is genuinely open. (Source: Wikipedia, *Basava*) [scholarly]

## Basava (Basavaṇṇa) — the organizer and elder brother

- **Basava / Basavaṇṇa / Basaveśvara** — central figure and organizing genius of the movement; honorific **"aṇṇa" (elder brother)**. **[traditional]** birth often dated **1105 CE** (sectarian *jayantī*); **[scholarly]** Wikipedia gives **1131–1196 CE**. Born at **Basavana Bāgevāḍi** (Vijayapura/Bijapur district, northern Karnataka) to a Śaiva **Brahmin** family. (Source: Wikipedia, *Basava*; Grokipedia, *Basava*)
  - *Parents:* father **Mādarasa / Mādhavarasa (Mādirāja)** and mother **Madalāmbike (Madalāmbā)**. Wife **Nīlāmbike (Gaṅgāmbike)**, daughter of Bijjala's provincial treasurer. (Source: Wikipedia, *Basava*)
  - *Hagiographic origin [traditional/folk]:* said to have rejected the **Brahminical sacred thread (upanayana / yajñopavīta)** as a boy in protest at birth-based hierarchy, then left for **Kūḍalasaṅgama** (confluence of the Kṛṣṇa and Malaprabhā rivers) to study; Kūḍalasaṅgamadeva ("Lord of the meeting rivers") becomes the form of Śiva he addresses. (Source: Grokipedia, *Basava*; Wikipedia, *Basava*)
- **Chief minister to Bijjala II** — Basava rose as treasurer and then **chief minister (mahā-pradhāna / bhaṇḍārī)** to the **Kalachuri king Bijjala II** (r. **1157–1167 CE**, capital **Kalyāṇa**, modern Basavakalyāṇ, Bidar district). His state office let him institutionalize the Śaraṇa gatherings and fund the **dāsoha** kitchens. (Source: Wikipedia, *Basava*, *Anubhava Mantapa*)
- **Aṅkita / mudrā:** Basava signs his vachanas **"Kūḍalasaṅgamadeva."** (Source: Grokipedia, *Basava*)
- **Attributed vachana works:** the **Ṣaṭsthala-vachana** (discourses on the six stages of salvation), **Kāla-jñāna-vachana** (forecasts), **Mantra-gopya**, **Ghaṭachakra-vachana**, **Rāja-yoga-vachana**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Basava*; WebSearch summary)
- **Reform program:** abolished caste distinction in worship and table-fellowship; rejected temple-icon ritual, animal sacrifice, astrology and superstition; gave every devotee — regardless of birth or sex — the **iṣṭaliṅga** to carry; championed manual labor as worship. "**Behavior, not birth**" determines spiritual standing. (Source: Wikipedia, *Basava*; Vajiram & Ravi, *Basavanna*)
- **The Haraḷayya–Madhuvarasa inter-caste marriage crisis [traditional/scholarly]** — the movement's catastrophe: a marriage arranged between the son of **Haraḷayya** (an "untouchable"/cobbler Śaraṇa) and the daughter of **Madhuvarasa** (a Brahmin Śaraṇa) enraged the orthodox; King Bijjala is said to have ordered the fathers **blinded and executed** (dragged by elephant), triggering Śaraṇa unrest, Bijjala's assassination, and the violent dispersal of the Kalyāṇa community c. **1167**. Basava had by then withdrawn to **Kūḍalasaṅgama**, where he died (merged into the liṅga) — **[scholarly]** 1196; **[traditional]** ~1167–1168. (Source: Grokipedia, *Basava*; Medium, *From Basava to Karnataka Politics*) [disputed on exact sequence/dates]

## Allama Prabhu — the guru of the void (Śūnya-pīṭha)

- **Allama Prabhu (Allamaprabhu)** — the presiding mystic of the Anubhava Maṇṭapa; though **Basava founded/energized** the movement, **Allama was the realized guru** who held its "**Śūnya-siṃhāsana**" (throne of the void) and presided over the assembly. One of the **Trinity of Liṅgāyatism** with Basava and Akka Mahādevi. **[scholarly]** early 12th c.; born at **Balligāvi** (Baḷḷigāve, Shivamogga/Shimoga district). Parents **Sujñāni** and **Nirahaṅkāra**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Allama Prabhu*)
- **Aṅkita / mudrā:** signs his vachanas **"Guheśvara / Guhēśvara"** ("Lord of caves"), from his cave-temple awakening. (Source: Wikipedia, *Allama Prabhu*)
- **Hagiography [traditional/folk]:** a temple drummer/musician who married a dancer **Kamalāte (Kāmalatā)**; her early death plunged him into grief and wandering until, in a cave temple, the siddha **Animiṣayya** gave him a liṅga and instruction, igniting his enlightenment — hence "Guheśvara." (Source: Wikipedia, *Allama Prabhu*)
- **Corpus:** ~**1,300 vachanas** attributed; Basavaraju's 1960 *Allamana Vachana Candrike* fixes **1,321** extant. His teaching is radically non-dual — **bayalu / śūnya** (the absolute void/open-space), the dissolution of the self into Śiva beyond all form, ritual and even *bhakti*-as-attachment. (Source: Wikipedia, *Allama Prabhu*; Grokipedia, *Vachana sahitya*)
- **End [traditional]:** said to have "become one with the liṅga" at **Kadaḷīvana** near **Śrīśaila (Srisailam)** — the same Śaiva forest associated with Akka Mahādevi's end. (Source: Wikipedia, *Allama Prabhu*)
- **Śūnyasampādane** — the great anthology (compiled in the **Vijayanagara era**, 15th–16th c.) staging Allama's **dialogues** with the other Śaraṇas — Basava, Akka Mahādevi, Siddharāma, Channabasava — as a dramatized record of the Anubhava Maṇṭapa's debates and the testing of Akka Mahādevi. (Source: Wikipedia, *Allama Prabhu*) [late text]

## Akka Mahādevi — the woman who renounced clothing for Śiva

- **Akka Mahādevi (Mahādēvi-akka, "elder-sister Mahādevi")** — the foremost woman poet of the movement and of early Kannada literature; **[scholarly]** c. **1130–1160 CE**, born at **Uḍutaḍi (Uḍugaṇi)**, Shivamogga district. Member of the Trinity of Liṅgāyatism. (Source: Wikipedia, *Akka Mahadevi*; Itihaas, *Akka Mahadevi*)
- **Aṅkita / mudrā:** **Chennamallikārjuna** — her name for Śiva, classically rendered by A. K. Ramanujan as "**Lord, white as jasmine**" (*channa-mallika-arjuna*). (Source: Wikipedia, *Akka Mahadevi*; Sahapedia)
- **Corpus:** ~**430 vachanas** survive, plus two short works **Mantrogopya** and **Yogāṅga-trividhi**. Her poetry uses the daring metaphor of **illicit / adulterous love** for the divine — Śiva as her true husband against an earthly one — and the themes of *viraha* (love-in-separation), abandonment and carnal longing transmuted into mystic union. (Source: Wikipedia, *Akka Mahadevi*; Sahapedia, *The Vachanas of Akkamahadevi*)
- **Life-story [traditional/folk]:** said to have been pressured into marriage with a (Jain) king **Kauśika** on condition he never violate her devotion; on his breaking the pact she **left him, renounced all possessions and even clothing — covering herself only with her long hair** — and walked to Kalyāṇa. (Source: Wikipedia, *Akka Mahadevi*; Feminism in India)
- **Tested at the Anubhava Maṇṭapa:** her nakedness and non-conformity scandalized the orthodox; even her **guru Allama Prabhu** had to argue her into the assembly. The *Śūnyasampādane* dramatizes Allama interrogating her, and her victory establishing her as a fully realized Śaraṇe. (Source: Wikipedia, *Akka Mahadevi*; PolSci Institute) [late text]
- **End [traditional]:** journeyed on to the **Kadaḷīvana forest at Śrīśaila (Srisailam)** seeking final union (*aikya*) with Chennamallikārjuna, where she is said to have merged into the divine. (Source: PolSci Institute, *Akka Mahadevi's Final Journey*)

## The other pillars — Channabasava, Siddharāma & the founding poets

- **Channabasava (Channabasavaṇṇa)** — Basava's **nephew** (son of his sister Akkanāgamma) and the movement's chief **systematizer/theologian**; credited with articulating the **ṣaṭsthala** doctrine in vachana form and, after the Kalyāṇa dispersal, helping carry and codify the tradition (associated with **Uḷavi/Ulavi**, where Śaraṇas fled). A leading participant at the Anubhava Maṇṭapa. (Source: Grokipedia, *Anubhava Mantapa*; Wikipedia, *Lingayatism*)
- **Siddharāma (Siddharāmeśvara, of Soḷḷāpur/Sholapur)** — great mystic, yogi and prolific Kannada poet; one of the **five revered vachana poets** with Basava, Allama, Akka Mahādevi and Devara Dāsimayya. Famed for building tanks/temples and public works as *kāyaka*; ~**1,379 vachanas** attributed. **[traditional]** very long life. (Source: Wikipedia, *Siddarama*; WebSearch summary)
- **Devara Dāsimayya (Jēdara Dāsimayya)** — often regarded as the **earliest vachanakāra** (possibly **10th–11th c.**, predating Basava), a weaver-saint; signs **"Rāmanātha."** His priority is part of the evidence that vachana-composition and a Śiva-bhakti reform **predate** Basava's Kalyāṇa phase. (Source: Grokipedia, *Vachana sahitya*) [scholarly]
- **Madivāḷa Mācideva, Hāvina Hāḷa Kallayya, Ambigara Chaudayya, etc.** — the broader roster of artisan-saints (washerman, snake-catcher, boatman) whose very occupational names dramatize the **kāyaka** ideal: every honest trade is a path to Śiva. (Source: Grokipedia, *Vachana sahitya*) [roster open-ended — many minor vachanakāras attested only in late anthologies]
- **The women Śaraṇe (besides Akka Mahādevi):** **Akkamma/Akkanāgamma** (Basava's sister), **Muktāyakka** (sister of Allama, debater), **Nīlāmbike/Gaṅgāmbike** (Basava's wife), **Satyakka**, **Kāḷavve**, **Goggavve**, **Āydakki Lakkamma**, and others — one of the largest bodies of named medieval women devotional poets in India. (Source: Grokipedia, *Vachana sahitya*; PolSci Institute) [roster open-ended]

## Theology — śaṭsthala, iṣṭaliṅga & the rejection of the old order

- **Iṣṭaliṅga** — the personal Śiva-liṅga icon (a small ovoid kaḍ-liṅga in a casket) **worn on the body** (on a thread round the neck) by every initiate, given at **dīkṣā** by a *jaṅgama* (wandering guru) regardless of caste or sex. It internalizes worship: the devotee's own body is the temple, removing any need for **temple, priest or pilgrimage**. Daily **liṅga-pūjā** on the palm replaces public icon-ritual. (Source: Wikipedia, *Basava*, *Lingayatism*; Bharatpedia, *Lingayatism*)
- **Ṣaṭsthala ("six stages")** — the core soteriology: the soul's graded ascent to union with Śiva through six *sthalas* — **(1) Bhakta** (devotee), **(2) Maheśvara** (steadfast), **(3) Prasādi** (recipient of grace), **(4) Prāṇaliṅgi** (one whose very life-breath is the liṅga), **(5) Śaraṇa** (surrendered), **(6) Aikya** (union/oneness with Śiva). Realization = **liṅgāṅga-sāmarasya**, the fusion of *aṅga* (soul) with *liṅga* (Śiva). (Source: Wikipedia, *Lingayatism*; Basava Samithi, *Sharana Philosophy*)
- **The three correlates:** **guru** (the initiating teacher), **liṅga** (the iṣṭaliṅga / Śiva), **jaṅgama** (the living, moving Śiva = the wandering ascetic / any Śaraṇa) — and the gifts owed them: **pādodaka** (water of the guru's feet) and **prasāda** (consecrated offering). (Source: Bharatpedia, *Lingayatism*) [scholarly]
- **Pañcācāra & aṣṭāvaraṇa** — the **five codes of conduct** (*pañcācāra*) and the **eight shields/coverings** (*aṣṭāvaraṇa*: guru, liṅga, jaṅgama, pādodaka, prasāda, vibhūti/ash, rudrākṣa, mantra "Oṃ Namaḥ Śivāya") that armor the devotee — standard in later systematized Vīraśaiva theology. (Source: Bharatpedia, *Lingayatism*) [scholarly; later codification]
- **What the Śaraṇas rejected:** the **caste system & untouchability**; **Vedic ritual authority and the supremacy of Sanskrit**; **temple icon-worship and the priesthood**; **animal sacrifice**; **child marriage and the stigma on widow-remarriage**; **astrology/superstition**; and — distinctively — the **theory of rebirth/karmic cycle and cremation**: Liṅgāyats traditionally **bury** their dead seated (the soul, already united with Śiva via the iṣṭaliṅga, needs no further cycle). (Source: Wikipedia, *Lingayatism*, *Basava*; Vajiram & Ravi) [scholarly]
  - *Variant [disputed]:* whether these rejections make Liṅgāyatism a **non-Hindu, independent religion** (the activist/founder reading) or a **reform sect within Śaivism** (the Vīraśaiva/pañcācārya reading) is the central modern controversy. (Source: Wikipedia, *Lingayatism*; Vajiram & Ravi)

## Kāyaka & dāsoha — the work-ethic and the Anubhava Maṇṭapa

- **Kāyaka ("work / bodily labor as worship")** — the doctrine that **honest manual labor in any trade is sacred**, equal to or higher than ritual: "**Kāyakavē Kailāsa**" — *work itself is Kailāsa* (Śiva's heaven). It sanctifies every occupation, dissolving the hierarchy of "pure" vs "polluting" professions and the priest/laborer divide. (Source: Grokipedia, *Anubhava Mantapa*; Basava Samithi) 
- **Dāsoha ("sharing / offering of service")** — the obligation to **give back the surplus** of one's kāyaka to the community (especially feeding the jaṅgamas and the poor); one should not accumulate. Kāyaka earns, dāsoha distributes — together an early religiously-grounded ethic of dignified labor and redistribution. (Source: Grokipedia, *Anubhava Mantapa*; Basava Samithi)
- **Anubhava Maṇṭapa ("hall / pavilion of [spiritual] experience")** — the assembly Basava founded at **Kalyāṇa**, active chiefly in the **1150s–1160s CE**; styled the **"first religious parliament,"** open to all castes and to women, presided over spiritually by **Allama Prabhu** on the **Śūnya-siṃhāsana**. It was the **crucible of the entire vachana corpus** — a forum where Śaraṇas debated theology, ethics and daily life in the vernacular. (Source: Wikipedia, *Anubhava Mantapa*; Grokipedia, *Anubhava Mantapa*)
  - *Modern continuation [contemporary]:* a "**New Anubhava Maṇṭapa**" monument/complex was sanctioned at Basavakalyāṇ in 2021 to commemorate it. (Source: Civilsdaily; Civils360) [contemporary, non-historical]

## Aftermath & legacy

- **The Kalyāṇa dispersal (c. 1167)** — the Haraḷayya marriage crisis, royal repression and Bijjala's assassination scattered the Śaraṇas; many fled to **Uḷavi** and across Karnataka, **carrying the vachana manuscripts** with them. The literary movement survived its political collapse precisely because the poems were portable and memorized. (Source: Grokipedia, *Basava*; Medium, *From Basava to Karnataka Politics*) [scholarly]
- **Anthologization under Vijayanagara (15th–16th c.)** — the scattered vachanas were collected and the **Śūnyasampādane** and other compilations produced, fixing the canon and the dramatized memory of the Anubhava Maṇṭapa. Later **pañcācārya / virakta maṭhas** institutionalized Vīraśaivism across Karnataka. (Source: Wikipedia, *Allama Prabhu*, *Lingayatism*) [late text]
- **Wider significance** — the movement gave Kannada its first great body of **prose-poetic, casteless, woman-inclusive devotional literature**, an indigenous critique of caste and ritual centuries before later reformers, and a living community that today numbers among Karnataka's largest. Compare the contemporaneous northern vernacular bhakti in [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md) and the Śaiva schools that frame it in [`shaiva-schools.md`](shaiva-schools.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Lingayatism*; Bharathiyam, *Kannada Vachanas*)
