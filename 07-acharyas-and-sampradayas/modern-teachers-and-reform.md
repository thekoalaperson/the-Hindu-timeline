# Modern Teachers & Reform Movements

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](./README.md) — the 19th–20th century gurus, saints and reform societies who re-formed Hinduism under colonial modernity, carried Vedānta and bhakti to the West, and founded the global missions (Ramakrishna Mission, Arya Samaj, ISKCON, BAPS, SRF) that define lived Hinduism today. Up-link: section index [`07-…/README.md`](./README.md).
> Sits in the **late, fully-documented stretch of Kali Yuga** (post-3102 BCE traditional; c. 1772–1977 CE academic) — where scripture-time gives way entirely to datable, archived history. These figures have **birth/death certificates, photographs and printed works**, so dating here is overwhelmingly **[scholarly]**; see the documented-history sweep and the two-clocks method at [`recorded-history.md`](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md).

## The Bengal Renaissance & rational reform (early 19th c.)

The first wave responded to British rule, missionary critique and Enlightenment rationalism by purging "corruptions" (sati, idolatry, caste rigidity) and recovering a purified core. **[scholarly]**

- **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** - "Father of the Indian Renaissance"; **22 May 1772 – 27 Sept 1833**, Bengal. Monotheist reformer and polyglot scholar of Sanskrit, Persian, Arabic and English; campaigned successfully for the **1829 abolition of sati**, opposed idolatry and caste, championed modern education. Founded the **Brahmo Sabha** at Calcutta on **20 August 1828** (with Dwarkanath Tagore), the precursor of the **Brahmo Samaj**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Raja Ram Mohan Roy*; Britannica, *Ram Mohan Roy*) [scholarly]
- **The Brahmo Samaj** - rationalist, monotheist, anti-idolatry reform church drawing on Upaniṣadic theism, deism and Unitarian Christianity; engine of the **Bengal Renaissance**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Brahmo Samaj*; Britannica) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (later leadership / schism):* under **Debendranath Tagore** (from 1843) and **Keshub Chandra Sen** the movement split — Sen's universalism vs the older "Adi Dharm" wing — and **rejected the infallibility of the Vedas**, the doctrinal point on which it broke decisively with the later Arya Samaj. (Source: Wikipedia, *Brahmo Samaj*) [scholarly] [disputed]

## "Back to the Vedas" — Arya Samaj

- **Dayananda Saraswati (Swami Dayananda)** - Hindu ascetic and militant reformer; **b. 1824, Tankara, Gujarat – d. 30 Oct 1883, Ajmer**. Disciple of the blind Vedic grammarian **Virajananda**; preached return to the sole authority of the four **Vedas**, rejecting Puranic idol-worship, polytheism, caste-by-birth, child marriage and untouchability while defending *varṇāśrama* by merit. Author of **Satyārth Prakāś** ("Light of Truth"). Founded the **Arya Samaj** at Bombay on **10 April 1875**. (Source: Britannica, *Dayananda Sarasvati*; Wikipedia, *Dayananda Saraswati*) [scholarly]
- **The Arya Samaj** - "Society of Nobles"; reformist, Veda-centred movement that pioneered the **śuddhi** (reconversion/purification) rite, women's and widow education, and a militant Hindu self-assertion against missionary conversion. (Source: Wikipedia, *Arya Samaj*) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (vs Brahmo Samaj):* the two great reform societies clashed precisely over scripture — **Arya Samaj held the Vedas to be infallible divine revelation**, where the Brahmos held them inferior, human works. (Source: Wikipedia, *Brahmo Samaj*) [scholarly]

## Neo-Vedanta & the global Hindu mission — Ramakrishna–Vivekananda

The pivot from reform-as-purge to **reform-as-export**: a universalist Vedānta presented to the West and re-imported as modern "Hinduism." **[scholarly]**

- **Ramakrishna Paramahamsa** - Bengali mystic and priest of the **Dakshineswar Kālī temple**; **18 Feb 1836 – 16 Aug 1886**. Ecstatic devotee of the Mother Goddess who claimed direct, experiential realization through **multiple paths** (Tantra, Vaiṣṇava bhakti, Advaita, even Islam and Christianity), grounding the doctrine that **all religions lead to the same God**. His monastic disciples formed the first **Math at Baranagore** after his death in 1886. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramakrishna Mission*; Britannica) [scholarly]
- **Swami Vivekananda** (Narendranath Datta) - chief disciple of Ramakrishna and the movement's organizer; **12 Jan 1863 – 4 July 1902**. Delivered the celebrated **"Sisters and Brothers of America"** address at the **World's Parliament of Religions, Chicago, September 1893**, introducing Vedānta and Yoga to the West and becoming "the Messenger of Indian wisdom." Founded the **Ramakrishna Mission on 1 May 1897**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramakrishna Mission*; Britannica) [scholarly]
- **Ramakrishna Math & Mission** - the twin monastic order (Math, est. 1886) and philanthropic body (Mission, est. 1897), fusing **renunciation with social service** (*śiva jñāne jīva sevā* — "serve the living being as God"); headquarters at **Belur Math**, Kolkata. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramakrishna Mission*; Britannica) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (philosophical label):* scholars classify the Mission's teaching as **"Neo-Vedanta"** — a modern, universalist re-reading of [Ādi Śaṅkara's](./adi-shankara.md) Advaita; some traditionalists dispute that it faithfully represents classical Advaita. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramakrishna Mission*) [scholarly] [disputed]

## Integral & evolutionary spirituality — Sri Aurobindo

- **Sri Aurobindo (Aurobindo Ghose)** - Cambridge-educated nationalist turned yogi-philosopher; **15 Aug 1872 – 5 Dec 1950**. Withdrew from the freedom struggle to **Pondicherry in 1910** and evolved **Integral Yoga (Pūrṇa Yoga)** — a path aiming not at world-escape but the **descent of the "Supermind"** to divinize earthly life and matter. Magnum opus **The Life Divine**; epic **Savitri**. Co-founded the **Sri Aurobindo Ashram in 1926** with his collaborator **Mirra Alfassa ("the Mother")**, who later founded the universal township **Auroville (1968)**. (Source: Sri Aurobindo Ashram, *Sri Aurobindo*; Wikipedia) [scholarly]

## Advaitic self-enquiry — Ramana Maharshi

- **Ramana Maharshi** (Venkataraman Iyer) - silent Advaitic sage of **Arunachala**; **30 Dec 1879 – 14 Apr 1950**. At sixteen (1896) underwent a spontaneous **"death-experience"** that resolved into permanent Self-realization, then settled for life at **Tiruvannamalai**. Taught **ātma-vichāra** ("Self-enquiry," the persistent question **"Who am I?"**) to trace the "I"-thought to its source, complemented by **bhakti / surrender to the Self**. **Sri Ramanasramam** grew around him. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramana Maharshi*; Britannica, *Ramana Maharshi*) [scholarly]

## Yoga-Vedanta synthesis & mass dissemination — Sivananda

- **Swami Sivananda Saraswati** - physician-turned-monk who popularized a synthetic, practical yoga; **8 Sept 1887 – 14 July 1963**. From his ashram at **Muni Ki Reti, Rishikesh**, propagated the maxim **"Serve, Love, Give, Purify, Meditate, Realize"** through ~300 books. Founded the **Divine Life Society in 1936** and the **Yoga-Vedanta Forest Academy (1948)**; his lineage seeded global teachers (Swami Satchidananda, Swami Vishnudevananda, the Sivananda Yoga schools). (Source: Wikipedia, *Divine Life Society*; *Sivananda Saraswati*) [scholarly]

## Akṣar-Puruṣottam theism — Swaminarayan & BAPS

- **Swaminarayan (Sahajanand Swami)** - Gujarati ascetic and reformer around whom the **Swaminarayan Sampradāya** formed; **3 Apr 1781 – 1 June 1830**. Preached strict ethics, non-violence, women's uplift and devotion to Kṛṣṇa-Nārāyaṇa; codified conduct in the **Shikshapatri**, teachings in the **Vachanamrut**; worshipped by followers as a manifestation of **Puruṣottam (the supreme God)**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Swaminarayan*; New World Encyclopedia) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (status — folk/devotional):* mainstream followers revere him as the **supreme God incarnate**; outside observers and some Vaiṣṇava lineages classify him as a saint/reformer-guru, not God-incarnate. (Source: New World Encyclopedia, *Swaminarayan*) [folk] [disputed]
- **BAPS (Bochasanwasi Akshar Purushottam Swaminarayan Sanstha)** - the largest Swaminarayan branch, built on the **Akṣar-Puruṣottam Darśan** (the living guru as the perfect devotee *Akṣar* through whom *Puruṣottam* is reached). Founded by **Shastriji Maharaj** in **1905/1907** (murti consecration at **Bochasan, 5 June 1907**); famed for monumental stone **mandirs** (Akshardham, Delhi & New Jersey) and global volunteer networks. (Source: Wikipedia, *Bochasanwasi Akshar Purushottam Swaminarayan Sanstha*; BAPS, *History*) [scholarly]

## The Hindu-Muslim saint — Shirdi Sai Baba

- **Sai Baba of Shirdi** - faqir-saint of uncertain origin revered by **both Hindus and Muslims**; **c. 1838 – 15 October 1918** (Mahāsamādhi). Lived austerely in a dilapidated mosque ("Dwarakamai") at **Shirdi, Maharashtra**, teaching a moral code of **love, forgiveness, charity, contentment, inner peace and devotion to God and guru**, summed up in **"Sabkā mālik ek"** ("One God governs all"). His tomb at Buti Wada became the **Samadhi Mandir**, now a major pilgrimage centre. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sai Baba of Shirdi*; Shree Saibaba Sansthan Trust) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (birth/identity — disputed):* his birth date, birthplace, parentage and even religion are **historically unknown**; competing traditions claim Hindu-Brahmin and Muslim origins alike. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sai Baba of Shirdi*) [scholarly] [disputed]

## Kriya Yoga to the West — Paramahansa Yogananda

- **Paramahansa Yogananda** (Mukunda Lal Ghosh) - first major Indian yoga master to settle and teach in the West; **5 Jan 1893 – 7 Mar 1952**. Disciple of **Sri Yukteswar** in the Lahiri Mahasaya–Babaji **Kriya Yoga** lineage. Came to the U.S. in 1920; founded the **Self-Realization Fellowship (SRF, 1920)** in America and the **Yogoda Satsanga Society of India (YSS, 1917)**. His **Autobiography of a Yogi (1946)** became the single most influential introduction of Indian yoga to the global public. (Source: Wikipedia, *Paramahansa Yogananda*) [scholarly]

## Global Gauḍīya bhakti — Prabhupada & ISKCON

- **A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada** (Abhay Charan De) - **1 Sept 1896 – 14 Nov 1977**; ācārya of the **Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava** (Chaitanya) lineage who carried Kṛṣṇa-bhakti worldwide. Arrived in New York in 1965 at age 69 and founded the **International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON) in July 1966**. Prolific translator of the **Bhagavad Gītā As It Is** and the **Śrīmad Bhāgavatam**; spread congregational **Hare Kṛṣṇa saṅkīrtana** (chanting) across the globe. (Source: Wikipedia / Dharmapedia, *A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada*) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (lineage):* ISKCON's roots run through the [Chaitanya / Gauḍīya sampradāya](./nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md) → **Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati** (Prabhupada's guru); it presents itself as the modern, English-medium continuation of the medieval Bengali bhakti movement, not a new sect. (Source: Wikipedia, *A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada*) [scholarly]
