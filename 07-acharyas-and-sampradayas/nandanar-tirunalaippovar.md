# Nandanar (Tirunālaippōvār) — the full legend

> Cosmic address: Kali Yuga · Śveta-Vārāha Kalpa · 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara — a Tamil Śaiva bhakti saint, one of the 63 Nāyaṉārs, in the Cauvery-delta Chola country. Academic frame: an undatable figure fixed in the Śaiva bhakti wave [scholarly] c. 6th–9th c. CE; his full hagiography is Cēkkiḻār's *Periya Purāṇam* (c. 1135 CE).
> Parent: [Ācāryas & Sampradāyas](README.md) (master index [../README.md](../README.md)). Roster: [The Ālvārs & Nāyaṉārs](alvars-and-nayanars.md). Fellow saints: [The Nālvar](the-nalvar-tamil-shaiva-saints.md).

**Nandanar** (Tamil **Nāntaṉār**), better known by his devotional epithet **Tirunāḷaippōvār** — "the holy one who will go tomorrow" (*tiru* + *nāḷai* "tomorrow" + *pōvār* "the one who will go") — is the only one of the 63 Nāyaṉārs drawn from the **Pulaiyar**, an "untouchable" (later *Dalit*) community of bonded agricultural labourers, drum-makers and leather-workers in the Chola country. His legend — barred by birth from the temples he loved, given darśana when Śiva bends the very laws of the sanctum for him, and finally purified in fire to merge into Naṭarāja at Chidambaram — makes him the corpus's sharpest test-case for the claim that **bhakti dissolves caste**. That claim is itself contested: the same story is read by later Dalit critics as a *re-inscription* of caste. He is the parallel in Tamil Śaivism to the Marāṭhā Mahār saint [Chokhāmeḷā](chokhamela.md) and the North-Indian Camār saint [Ravidās](ravidas.md).

## The two clocks — where Nandanar sits

- **Scriptural / hagiographic frame** — the Nāyaṉārs are treated as timeless *aḍiyārs* ("servants") of Śiva whose lives are exemplary rather than dated; Nandanar's name is enrolled in **Sundarar's *Tiruttoṇḍattokai*** (the 8th (–9th) c. roll-call of "the holy servants"), where the poet vows *"I am the servant of Tirunāḷaippōvār"* — the seed-list from which the canonical 63 were later fixed. (Source: Cuntarar, *Tiruttoṇḍattokai*; [Nayanars — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayanars))
- **Academic / historical frame** — Nandanar himself is **undatable** [scholarly]: unlike [Ciṟuttoṇṭar](../04-deep-dives/siruthondar-nayanar.md) or the [Nālvar](the-nalvar-tamil-shaiva-saints.md), no episode ties him to a datable king. He is conventionally placed inside the Śaiva bhakti flowering, **c. 6th–9th c. CE**, but the figure may be substantially legendary. (Source: [Nandanar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandanar)) [scholarly]
- **Textual layering** — the bare name (Sundarar) → an expanded verse-notice (**Nambiyāṇḍār Nambi's *Tiruttoṇḍar Tiruvandhādhi***, c. late 10th–early 11th c.) → the **full narrative** in **Cēkkiḻār's *Periya Purāṇam*** (**Tirunāḷaippōvār Nāyaṉār Purāṇam**, section no. 18), c. 1135 CE → a 19th-century **musical recension** (Gōpālakr̥ṣṇa Bhāratī, 1861) that fixed the popular image. Each layer adds detail; the caste content sharpens with each retelling. (Source: [Periya Puranam — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periya_Puranam))

## Birth and station — the devotee born "outside the gate"

- **Origins at Ādhaṉūr** — born in **Ādhaṉūr** (Āthaṉūr), a village in the fertile Chola Cauvery delta, into the **Pulaiyar** community: labourers bound to the land of a Brahmin/Vēḷāḷa landholder, who by hereditary duty tanned hides and supplied **leather straps and drum-skins (for the *paṟai* and temple drums)** and worked the paddy fields. His caste made him ritually "unapproachable," barred from entering Śiva temples. (Source: *Periya Purāṇam*, Tirunāḷaippōvār Nāyaṉār Purāṇam)
  - *Variant (community identification) [disputed]:* the *Periya Purāṇam* describes him as a **Pulaiya (Pulaiyar)**; a separate tradition instead identifies him with the **Paṟaiyar** (the drummer-labourer Dalit caste tied to the *paṟai*) — two historically distinct Tamil Dalit communities, catalogued here as alternatives rather than equated. (Source: [Nandanar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandanar))
- **Devotion within constraint** — Nandanar's love of Śiva expressed itself in **service from the threshold**: supplying leather and gut for temple drums and instruments, weaving garlands, cleaning temple precincts and tanks from outside, and worshipping at a distance from the *balipīṭha* line he could not cross. He is the emblem of *toṇḍu* (devotional service) offered from social exclusion. (Source: *Periya Purāṇam*, Tirunāḷaippōvār Nāyaṉār Purāṇam) [regional]
- **The name "Tirunāḷaippōvār"** — his heart burned to make the pilgrimage to **Tillai (Chidambaram)** to see **Naṭarāja**, but each time he set out, awe and the sense of his own unworthiness held him back and he would say *"nāḷai-p-pōvēṉ"* — **"I shall go tomorrow"** — never quite daring the journey. From this endlessly deferred "tomorrow" he earned the affectionate title **Tirunāḷaippōvār**, "the sacred one who will go tomorrow." (Source: *Periya Purāṇam*, Tirunāḷaippōvār Nāyaṉār Purāṇam)

## Tiruppuṅkūr — Śiva bends the sanctum for his devotee

- **The obstructing Nandi** — at the Śiva temple of **Tiruppuṅkūr** (Tiruppuṅkūr, near Sīrkāḻi), Nandanar stood at the boundary he could not cross, but the great stone **Nandi** (Śiva's bull-vāhana) seated before the shrine **blocked his line of sight to the *liṅga***. Moved by his longing, **Śiva commanded Nandi to shift aside** so that Nandanar could have unobstructed darśana of the Lord. (Source: *Periya Purāṇam*, Tirunāḷaippōvār Nāyaṉār Purāṇam)
- **The living relic** — the **Nandi at Tiruppuṅkūr is to this day set off the central axis**, leaning aside, and is shown to pilgrims as the physical proof of the miracle — a rare case where the legend is anchored to a still-visible temple feature. (Source: [Nandanar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandanar)) [regional]
- **The temple tank dug overnight** — the same shrine lacked a sacred tank; Nandanar is said to have **excavated the temple *teppakkuḷam* (tank) single-handedly in one night** by Śiva's grace, so that the god might have his ablution water — the tank at Tiruppuṅkūr is attributed to him. (Source: *Periya Purāṇam*, Tirunāḷaippōvār Nāyaṉār Purāṇam) [regional]

## The forty-*veli* field — the master, the ploughing, and release

- **The landlord's condition** — Nandanar's resolve to finally go to Chidambaram collided with his bondage: his **landholding master** would not release a bonded labourer, and (in the developed narrative) taunted him — he could go worship only once the master's vast fields were ploughed and cultivated, work no one could finish in time. In Gōpālakr̥ṣṇa Bhāratī's recension the master is the sceptical Brahmin landlord **Vēdiyar**, whose mockery is a set-piece of the drama. (Source: *Periya Purāṇam*, Tirunāḷaippōvār Nāyaṉār Purāṇam; Gōpālakr̥ṣṇa Bhāratī, *Nandaṉ Carittiram*) [folk]
- **The field ploughed by grace** — a **forty-*veli* field** (*veli*, a large Tamil land-measure) was **ploughed and made ready overnight** — by Śiva's *gaṇas* / by divine agency — while Nandanar prayed. Confronted with the miracle at dawn, the master recognized his labourer's sanctity and **released him** to make the pilgrimage. (Source: *Periya Purāṇam*, Tirunāḷaippōvār Nāyaṉār Purāṇam)
  - *Variant (the master's conversion) [folk]:* in the popular musical version the landlord is not merely amazed but **converted**, becoming himself a devotee and sending Nandanar off with reverence — a softening absent from the terser *Periya Purāṇam* frame. (Source: Gōpālakr̥ṣṇa Bhāratī, *Nandaṉ Carittiram*)

## Chidambaram — the fire-ordeal and merger with Naṭarāja

- **Arrival at Tillai** — Nandanar reaches **Chidambaram (Tillai)**, the cosmic dance-hall where Śiva as **Naṭarāja** performs the *ānanda-tāṇḍava* in the **Cit Sabhā** (the golden hall of consciousness). But as a Pulaiyar he **cannot cross into the sanctum**, and circles the temple from outside in anguished longing. (Source: *Periya Purāṇam*, Tirunāḷaippōvār Nāyaṉār Purāṇam; see [Pañca Sabhā — the Naṭarāja dance-halls](../06-sacred-geography/pancha-sabhai-nataraja-dance-halls.md))
- **Śiva's command to the Dīkṣitars** — **Śiva appears in a dream to the Tillai Brahmins** (the **Tillaivāḻ Andaṇar / Dīkṣitars**, the hereditary priesthood of Chidambaram, traditionally 3,000) and orders them to **prepare a sacred fire** and let Nandanar enter it — that his devotee may be brought into the shrine cleansed of the body that bars him. (Source: *Periya Purāṇam*, Tirunāḷaippōvār Nāyaṉār Purāṇam)
- **The Agni-praveśa (fire-ordeal)** — a fire is kindled before the temple; **Nandanar enters the flames** and **emerges transfigured** — a radiant sage bearing **matted locks (jaṭā) and the sacred thread**, his earthly form burned away — and, thus purified, **walks into the sanctum**. (Source: *Periya Purāṇam*, Tirunāḷaippōvār Nāyaṉār Purāṇam)
- **Merger into the Lord** — entering the presence of Naṭarāja, Nandanar **merges into the effulgence (jyoti)** of the dancing Lord and attains liberation — vanishing into Śiva before the eyes of the assembled priests. The mode of his end mirrors that of [Māṇikkavācakar](the-nalvar-tamil-shaiva-saints.md), who likewise merged into Naṭarāja at the same shrine. (Source: *Periya Purāṇam*, Tirunāḷaippōvār Nāyaṉār Purāṇam)
  - *Reading (bhakti over purity) [scholarly]:* within the hagiography the fire is not punishment but **theophany** — Śiva himself ordains it and receives him — placing Nandanar's story alongside [Kaṇṇappar](alvars-and-nayanars.md) (the hunter who offered his eyes) as a case of **devotion overriding ritual purity and birth**. (Source: [Nayanars — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayanars))

## Afterlife of the legend — Gōpālakr̥ṣṇa Bhāratī and the caste debate

- **The *Nandaṉ Carittiram* (1861)** — the Tamil composer **Gōpālakr̥ṣṇa Bhāratī** turned the Nāyaṉār's *Periya Purāṇam* notice into a full **musical narrative / dance-drama, the *Nandaṉ Carittira Kīrttaṉai*** ("The Story of Nandaṉ," 1861), a *kālakṣepam* cycle of Carnatic songs. It became **far more famous than the scriptural source**, carrying the legend into concert halls, harikathā performance and film, and fixing characters (the landlord **Vēdiyar**) and songs (e.g. *"Varugalāmō?"* — *"May I, low-born, dare to come to you, O Lord?"*) in popular memory. (Source: [Gopalakrishna Bharati — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopalakrishna_Bharati)) [regional]
- **Stage and screen** — the *Nandaṉ Carittiram* was repeatedly staged and filmed in Tamil — including films titled *Nandanar* (1935; and the celebrated **1942** version starring **"Dandapani Desikar"** as the saint) — making Nandanar one of the most widely dramatized of all the Nāyaṉārs. (Source: [Gopalakrishna Bharati — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopalakrishna_Bharati)) [regional]
- **The caste-and-bhakti controversy** — Nandanar is the central figure in modern debate over whether Tamil Śaiva bhakti **subverted or sanctified** caste [disputed]:
  - *Reading A (liberatory):* the legend shows Śiva **overturning caste barriers** for a devotee — moving Nandi, releasing him from bondage, admitting him to the holiest sanctum — a devotional egalitarianism, and Nandanar is honoured as a **saint above all Brahmins**. (Source: [Nandanar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandanar))
  - *Reading B (conservative / Dalit critique) [scholarly] [disputed]:* later Dalit critics and scholars argue that Bhāratī's Brahminized retelling makes Nandanar **timid and self-abasing** ("I will go tomorrow" as internalized subordination), has him **beg permission** of a Brahmin master, and **purifies him by burning away his Pulaiyar body in fire** before he may enter — so that the story **affirms** caste hierarchy (only a *transformed*, no-longer-untouchable Nandanar is admitted) rather than abolishing it. (Source: [Nandanar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandanar)) [disputed]
- **Naming and worship today** — Nandanar's own shrine and image are venerated among the 63, his festival day observed, and he remains an icon claimed by **Dalit assertion movements** in Tamil Nadu. (Source: [Nandanar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandanar)) [regional]
