# Rāmānuja & Madhva — Viśiṣṭādvaita & Dvaita

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](README.md) — the two great theistic Vedānta ācāryas who broke decisively with [Ādi Śaṅkara's](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md) Advaita and founded the first two of the four canonical Vaiṣṇava [sampradāyas](README.md). Up-link: section index [`07-…/README.md`](README.md).
> Sits in the **first ~1.2% of Kali Yuga** (post-3102 BCE traditional; 11th–14th c. CE academic) — the South-Indian Vaiṣṇava reform that turned Āḻvār / Vāyu bhakti into institutionalized, *paramparā*-transmitted Vedānta. Dating given **[traditional]** (matha-hagiographic) vs **[scholarly]** (epigraphic / text-critical) where they diverge; see the [two-clocks method](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md).

## Rāmānuja & the Śrī Sampradāya (Viśiṣṭādvaita)

The **Śrī Sampradāya** is the Vaiṣṇava order tracing supremacy to **Viṣṇu-with-Śrī (Lakṣmī)**, carrying the Tamil [Āḻvār](../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md) bhakti into Sanskrit Vedānta. Its philosophy is **Viśiṣṭādvaita** — "non-dualism of the qualified (whole)": one Brahman, but eternally *qualified* by sentient souls (*cit*) and insentient matter (*acit*) as its **body** (*śarīra*), Brahman being the indwelling **soul** (*śarīrī*). (Source: Wikipedia, *Sri Vaishnavism*; IEP, *Ramanuja*)

### Precursors (the Sri Vaishnava *pūrvācāryas*)

- **The twelve Āḻvārs** - Tamil Vaiṣṇava poet-saints, c. 6th–9th c. [scholarly], whose 4,000 hymns form the **Nālāyira Divya Prabandham** ("Tamil Veda"); the devotional bedrock the ācāryas systematized. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sri Vaishnavism*) — cross-link: [Tamil canon](../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md).
- **Nāthamuni** - first ācārya of the line, c. 10th c. [scholarly], who **recovered and re-compiled the lost Divya Prabandham** and wedded Āḻvār bhakti to Vedānta; author of *Nyāya Tattva*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramanuja*)
- **Yāmuna / Yāmunācārya (Āḷavandār)** - grandson of Nāthamuni, c. 916–1041 [traditional], the immediate theological forerunner; works incl. *Siddhitraya* and *Stotra Ratna*. Rāmānuja was designated his successor but **the two never met in life** — Yāmuna died as Rāmānuja arrived at Śrīraṅgam. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramanuja*; IEP)

### Rāmānuja (Ilaya Perumāḷ / Rāmānujācārya)

- **Rāmānuja** - supreme systematizer of Viśiṣṭādvaita and the most influential Śrī Vaiṣṇava ācārya; born at **Śrīperumbudūr** (Tamil Nadu), revered as an *aṃśa* (partial descent) of Ādiśeṣa. Settled at **Śrīraṅgam**, reorganized its Raṅganātha temple worship, and built a network of centres across the subcontinent. Propounded **prapatti** (loving self-surrender to God as sole refuge) as the supreme means (*upāya*) to mokṣa, alongside *bhakti*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramanuja*; Britannica, *Ramanuja*; IEP)
  - *Dates — variant (traditional / Śrī Vaiṣṇava matha):* **1017–1137 CE**, a 120-year lifespan — the canonical sampradāya reckoning. (Source: ISKCON Educational Services, *The Life of Ramanuja*; Wikipedia) [traditional]
  - *Dates — variant (academic):* **c. 1077–1157 CE**, inferred from 11th–12th-c. temple records and regional literature outside the tradition, shifting the whole life forward ~60 years. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramanuja*) [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Teachers & break with Advaita** - first studied Advaita under **Yādava Prakāśa** at Kāñcī but **broke with him over scriptural interpretation** (the famous *kapyāsam* exegesis dispute), then aligned with the Yāmuna line via disciples **Mahāpūrṇa (Periya Nambi)** and **Goṣṭhīpūrṇa**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramanuja*; IEP)
- **Exile to Melkote** - tradition holds Rāmānuja fled Cōḻa (Śaiva-aligned) persecution to **Melkote / Tirunārāyaṇapuram in the Hoysaḷa Karnataka country**, founding institutions there before returning to Śrīraṅgam. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sri Vaishnavism*; ISKCON Educational Services) [traditional]

### Works — the navaratna ("nine jewels")

- **Śrī Bhāṣya** - magnum opus; the Viśiṣṭādvaita commentary on the **Brahma Sūtras**, refuting Śaṅkara's reading sūtra by sūtra. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramanuja*)
- **Vedārtha Saṃgraha** - independent treatise giving the unified meaning of the Vedānta, his clearest philosophical exposition. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramanuja*; IEP)
- **Gītā Bhāṣya** - commentary on the **Bhagavad Gītā**, foregrounding *bhakti* and *prapatti*. (Source: Wikipedia)
- **Vedānta Dīpa & Vedānta Sāra** - two shorter Brahma-Sūtra glosses (the "lamp" and the "essence"). (Source: Wikipedia)
- **Gadya Trayam** - three rhapsodic prose-poems composed at Śrīraṅgam: **Śaraṇāgati Gadya, Śrīraṅga Gadya, Vaikuṇṭha Gadya** — the liturgical core of formal surrender (*śaraṇāgati*). (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramanuja*; Sanatana Vibes)
- **Nitya Grantha** - manual of daily worship (*ārādhana*). (Source: Wikipedia)
  - *Authenticity — variant (scholarly):* critical scholars accept the three major commentaries + *Gadya Trayam* with confidence but **question the attribution of some of the nine** to Rāmānuja's own hand. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramanuja*) [scholarly] [disputed]

### Critique of Advaita (vs Śaṅkara)

- **Against nirguṇa Brahman** - Rāmānuja rejects an attribute-less (*nirguṇa*) Absolute: Brahman is **saguṇa**, intrinsically endowed with infinite auspicious qualities (*kalyāṇa-guṇas*) and identical with **Viṣṇu-Nārāyaṇa**. (Source: IEP, *Ramanuja*; Britannica)
- **Against māyā / world-illusion** - the world and souls are **real** modes of Brahman's body, not an illusory superimposition; he attacks Advaita's *avidyā* doctrine in the *mahāsiddhānta* / *sapta-vidhā-anupapatti* ("seven untenables") of the **Śrī Bhāṣya**. (Source: IEP; Wikipedia, *Vishishtadvaita*) [scholarly]
- **Body–soul relation (śarīra–śarīrī bhāva)** - liberation is **not** the jīva's merger-into-identity with Brahman but the soul's realization of itself as Brahman's eternally-distinct mode, enjoying God in **Vaikuṇṭha** without becoming God. (Source: IEP; Wikipedia)

### The Teṅkalai / Vaḍakalai split

The Śrī Sampradāya fractured into two sub-sects (*kalai*, "schools") over how *prapatti* and grace operate — crystallizing by the late 14th c., a permanent division thereafter. (Source: Britannica, *Tenkalai*; Wikipedia, *Sri Vaishnavism*) [scholarly]

- **Vaḍakalai** ("Northern school") - emphasizes the **Sanskrit Veda**; follows **Vedānta Deśika** (1268–1369); holds the soul must make some **effort/co-operation** for grace — **markaṭa-nyāya** ("monkey analogy": the baby monkey *clings* to its mother). (Source: Britannica, *Tenkalai*; *Cats and monkeys*, prajnanambrahmablog)
  - *Vedānta Deśika* - polymath ācārya, author *Rahasya-traya-sāra*, *Yatindra-mata-dīpikā*; intellectual head of the Vaḍakalai. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sri Vaishnavism*) [scholarly]
- **Teṅkalai** ("Southern school") - emphasizes the **Tamil Divya Prabandham**; follows **Maṇavāḷa Māmunigaḷ** (1370–1443); holds grace is **wholly causeless**, the soul utterly passive — **mārjāra-nyāya** ("cat analogy": the kitten is *carried* by its mother, doing nothing). (Source: Britannica, *Tenkalai*; *Cats and monkeys*) 
  - *Variant framing (Raman, Routledge):* scholarship glosses the divide as **"Tamil cats vs Sanskrit monkeys"** — a dispute as much over canon-language (Tamil Prabandham vs Sanskrit Veda) and caste-access to mokṣa as over the mechanics of surrender. (Source: Raman, *Self-Surrender (prapatti) to God in Shrivaishnavism*) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (18 doctrinal points, "aṣṭādaśa bheda"):* later polemicists enumerate **eighteen** systematized differences (on prapatti, the role of Śrī/Lakṣmī in salvation, post-prapatti sin, etc.), not just the cat/monkey image. (Source: srivaishnavan.com, *Vadakalai and Tenkalai*) [traditional]

## Madhva & the Brahma Sampradāya (Dvaita / Tattvavāda)

The **Brahma Sampradāya** centres on **Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa** as the one independent reality. Its philosophy is **Dvaita** ("dualism"), which Madhva himself called **Tattvavāda** ("the realist doctrine") — an uncompromising metaphysical pluralism: God, souls, and matter are **eternally and really distinct**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*; rujuyogi.org)

### Madhva / Ānandatīrtha (Pūrṇaprajña)

- **Madhva** - founder of Dvaita Vedānta; born **Vāsudeva** at **Pājaka near Uḍupi** (coastal Karnataka). Initiated young into the *Ekadaṇḍi* order under the Advaitin guru **Acyutaprekṣa**, he **rejected Advaita** and launched Tattvavāda. Established the **Kṛṣṇa Maṭha at Uḍupi** and the **Aṣṭa Maṭha** (eight monasteries) around it, instituting the **Paryāya** rotation for Kṛṣṇa-worship that has run unbroken for ~700 years. Bore three names: **Pūrṇaprajña** (at *sannyāsa*), **Ānandatīrtha** (on becoming maṭha head), and **Madhva/Madhvācārya**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*; vajiramandravi.com)
  - *Dates — variant (most-cited / academic):* **1238–1317 CE**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*) [scholarly]
  - *Dates — variant (alternative):* **1199–1278 CE** — an earlier reckoning still found in the literature. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*) [disputed]
- **The Uḍupi Kṛṣṇa icon** - tradition: Madhva miraculously obtained a Kṛṣṇa *mūrti* encased in **gopīcandana** (sandal-clay ballast) from a ship off the Karnataka coast, the cargo having come from **Dvārakā**, and enshrined it at Uḍupi facing west (the *kanaka-kindi* window legend of the devotee Kanakadāsa attaches to this idol). (Source: madhwabrahmanas.blogspot, *Biography of Madhvacharya*) [traditional] [folk]
- **Avatāra claim** - Madhva proclaimed himself the **third incarnation of Vāyu** (the wind-god), after **Hanumān** (Rāmāyaṇa) and **Bhīma** (Mahābhārata) — sent to restore right doctrine. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*)
  - *Variant (scholarly caution):* scholars debate whether the scriptural "proof-texts" cited for this Vāyu-avatāra status were authentic or **composed/adduced by Madhva himself**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*) [scholarly] [disputed]

### Works — the Sarvamūla Granthas (37 texts)

- **Brahma Sūtra Bhāṣya** + **Anuvyākhyāna** - his Dvaita commentary on the Brahma Sūtras and its verse-supplement, the **Anuvyākhyāna being his philosophical masterwork**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*)
- **Gītā Bhāṣya & Gītā Tātparya** - twin works on the Bhagavad Gītā. (Source: Wikipedia)
- **Daśopaniṣad Bhāṣya** - commentaries on the principal Upaniṣads. (Source: Wikipedia)
- **Mahābhārata Tātparya Nirṇaya** & **Bhāgavata Tātparya Nirṇaya** - "determinations of the true import" of the epic and the Bhāgavata. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*)
- **Total corpus** - tradition counts **37 Sarvamūla ("all-root") granthas**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*)

### Core doctrine — pañca-bheda (the five eternal differences)

- **The five distinctions** - reality is permanently structured by five irreducible *bhedas*: (1) **God ↔ soul**, (2) **God ↔ matter**, (3) **soul ↔ soul**, (4) **soul ↔ matter**, (5) **matter ↔ matter**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*) — the sharpest contrast with both Śaṅkara's identity and Rāmānuja's qualified-unity.
- **Svatantra / asvatantra** - only **Viṣṇu is independent (svatantra)**; all souls and matter are **dependent (asvatantra; paratantra)**. (Source: rujuyogi.org)
- **Taratamya & eternal damnation** - Madhva uniquely teaches a **gradation (taratamya) of souls** and **tamoyogyas / nitya-saṃsārins** — souls eternally bound, even predestined to perpetual hell (*andhatamas*) — a doctrine without parallel in the other Vedāntas. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*; rujuyogi.org) [scholarly]

### Critique of Advaita (vs Śaṅkara)

- **"Crypto-Buddhism"** - Madhva's most famous polemic: he charged Advaita with being **"Buddhism in disguise" (pracchanna-bauddha)** — teaching Mahāyāna voidism "under the cover of Vedānta." (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*)
- **Against māyā and mahāvākya identity** - he rejects illusory non-difference and reads *tat tvam asi* **non-identically**, deploying realist epistemology (the *sākṣī*, witness-self, as the source of valid knowledge). (Source: rujuyogi.org; Wikipedia, *Dvaita Vedanta*) [scholarly]
- **Also against Rāmānuja** - Dvaita parts from Viśiṣṭādvaita too: where Rāmānuja makes souls/matter the *body* of one Brahman (modal non-duality), Madhva insists on **full ontological separateness** and on the *taratamya* and possibility of eternal bondage that Viśiṣṭādvaita denies. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*)

### The Haridāsa movement

- **Haridāsas** - the Kannada-language **bhakti-musical wing of Madhva's school**, "servants of Hari," who carried Dvaita Siddhānta to the common people through **devaranāmas, sulādis and dāsa-sāhitya**, breaking caste/creed barriers in practice. (Source: Wikipedia, *Haridasa*; madhwabrahmanas.blogspot, *History of Haridasas*)
  - *Key figures (scholarly):* **Naraharitīrtha** (early seed, 13th–14th c.), **Śrīpādarāja** (1422–1480), **Vyāsatīrtha / Vyāsarāja** (1460–1539, the great organizer and Vijayanagara court guru), **Purandaradāsa** (1484–1564, the *Karṇāṭaka-Saṃgīta-Pitāmaha*, "grandfather of Carnatic music"), and **Kanakadāsa** (1509–1609). (Source: Wikipedia, *Haridasa*) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (two streams):* the movement is sometimes split into the **Vyāsakūṭa** (Sanskrit-scholar saints) and the **Dāsakūṭa** (vernacular singer-saints) currents. (Source: Wikipedia, *Haridasa*) [scholarly]

## At a glance — the three Vedāntas compared

| | Śaṅkara — **Advaita** | Rāmānuja — **Viśiṣṭādvaita** | Madhva — **Dvaita** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brahman ↔ soul | identical (one) | one whole, soul = its *mode/body* | really separate |
| World | *mithyā* / *māyā* | real, body of Brahman | real, independent of soul |
| Highest means | *jñāna* | *bhakti* + *prapatti* | *bhakti* + grace, *aparokṣa-jñāna* |
| Mokṣa | identity-merger | God-enjoyment, distinct | God-enjoyment, graded (*taratamya*) |
