# The Four Vaiṣṇava Sampradāyas

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](README.md) — the **cāturvaidya**: the four authorized Vaiṣṇava disciplic lines (*sampradāyas*), each rooted in a founding deity and crystallized into a Vedānta school by a human ācārya in the historical Kali-Yuga. Up-link: section index [`07-…/README.md`](README.md).
> Sits in the **first ~1.2% of Kali Yuga** (post-3102 BCE traditional; the deity-origins are timeless / pre-historical, the human ācāryas are 11th–16th c. CE academic). Dating given **[traditional]** (matha-hagiographic) vs **[scholarly]** (epigraphic / text-critical) where they diverge; see the [two-clocks method](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md). For the deep biographies of two founders see [Rāmānuja & Madhva](ramanuja-and-madhva.md).

---

## The doctrine of the four (the *catuḥ-sampradāya*)

Vaiṣṇava tradition holds that genuine *mantra-dīkṣā* (initiation) is valid only inside an unbroken disciplic chain (*guru-paramparā*); to anchor this in the Kali Yuga, four authorized lines are said to have been inaugurated by four founding agents — **Śrī (Lakṣmī), Brahmā, Rudra (Śiva), and the four Kumāras / Sanaka** — each later headed by a great ācārya. (Source: Poojn.in, *Four Vaishnava Sampradayas*; HareKrishnaMandir.org, *Our Sampradaya*)

- **The locus classicus** - the doctrine is grounded in two verses attributed to the **Padma Purāṇa**: *"any mantra which does not come in disciplic succession (sampradāya) is fruitless"* (*sampradāya-vihīnā ye mantrās te niṣphalā matāḥ*), followed by a verse naming the four founders — **Śrī, Brahmā, Rudra, and the Kumāras (Sanaka et al.)** — whose ācāryas would appear in Kali at **Puruṣottama-kṣetra (Jagannātha Purī, Orissa)**. (Source: Gosai.com, *Sampradaya-Pranali*; raganugaprembhakti, *Bonafide sampradayas*)
  - *Variant (textual-critical) [scholarly] [disputed]:* present critical editions of the Padma Purāṇa **do not contain** this verse; it is known only as a quotation in **Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa's** *Prameya-ratnāvalī* (18th c.), which cites the Purāṇa as its source. Defenders note it is paraphrased by earlier writers; critics treat it as a late Gauḍīya proof-text. (Source: Gosai.com, *Sampradaya-Pranali*; raganugaprembhakti)
  - *Variant (founder-name forms):* the fourth line's founding agent appears as **"Sanaka Ṛṣi" / "the four Kumāras" / "Sanakādi"** interchangeably; the third founder appears as **"Rudra" / "Śiva"**. (Source: HareKrishnaMandir.org; Poojn.in)
- **The four mapped to school + ācārya** - the canonical table. (Source: HareKrishnaMandir.org, *Our Sampradaya*; Poojn.in)
  - **Śrī Sampradāya** — founding deity **Lakṣmī/Śrī** → ācārya **Rāmānuja** → school **Viśiṣṭādvaita**.
  - **Brahma Sampradāya** — founding deity **Brahmā** → ācārya **Madhva** → school **Dvaita (Tattvavāda)**.
  - **Rudra Sampradāya** — founding deity **Śiva/Rudra** → ācārya **Viṣṇusvāmī** (best-known exponent **Vallabha**) → school **Śuddhādvaita** (Puṣṭimārga).
  - **Kumāra / Sanakādi (Nimbārka) Sampradāya** — founding agents the **four Kumāras** → ācārya **Nimbārka** → school **Dvaitādvaita / Bhedābheda**.
- **Why four** - each line is held to teach the same *bhakti* to Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa through a distinct Vedānta lens, so that no school can claim a monopoly on the Absolute; together they bracket the spectrum from near-dualism (Madhva) to near-non-dualism (Vallabha). (Source: Vrajvrindavan.com, *The four Sampradaya*; Poojn.in) [traditional]
  - *Variant (Smārta/Advaita exclusion):* the scheme is **internal to Vaiṣṇavism** — it deliberately omits Ādi [Śaṅkara's](adi-shankara.md) Advaita (Smārta), which Vaiṣṇavas classify outside the four authorized Viṣṇu-supremacy lines. (Source: Poojn.in) [traditional]

## 1. Śrī Sampradāya — Lakṣmī → Rāmānuja (Viśiṣṭādvaita)

The line tracing supremacy to **Viṣṇu-with-Śrī (Lakṣmī)**, in which the Goddess is the eternal mediatrix (*puruṣakāra*) of grace. Founding paramparā: **Lakṣmī → Viṣvaksena → the Āḻvārs / Nāthamuni → Yāmuna → Rāmānuja.** Full ācārya biography, the navaratna works, and the Teṅkalai/Vaḍakalai split are cataloged in [Rāmānuja & Madhva](ramanuja-and-madhva.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Sri Vaishnavism*; Poojn.in)

- **Founding deity** - **Lakṣmī/Śrī** receives the doctrine from **Nārāyaṇa** and transmits it through **Viṣvaksena** (Viṣṇu's commander-in-chief) down to the Tamil [Āḻvārs](../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Sri Vaishnavism*) [traditional]
- **Doctrine — Viśiṣṭādvaita** - "qualified non-dualism": one Brahman eternally *qualified* by souls (*cit*) and matter (*acit*) as its body. Supreme means to mokṣa = **prapatti** (self-surrender). (Source: IEP, *Ramanuja*) — see [full treatment](ramanuja-and-madhva.md).
- **Head ācārya** - **Rāmānuja** (trad. **1017–1137**; acad. **c. 1077–1157** [scholarly] [disputed]), appointed by Śrī Devī per the four-sampradāya doctrine. (Source: Wikipedia, *Ramanuja*) — see [Rāmānuja & Madhva](ramanuja-and-madhva.md).

## 2. Brahma Sampradāya — Brahmā → Madhva (Dvaita / Tattvavāda)

The line tracing supremacy to **Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa as the one independent reality** (*svatantra*), all else dependent (*paratantra*). Founding paramparā: **Nārāyaṇa → Brahmā → Nārada → Vyāsa → Madhva.** Full ācārya biography, the Udupi Kṛṣṇa-Maṭha and Aṣṭa-Maṭha, the *Sarvamūla* corpus, the *pañca-bheda* (five eternal differences) and the controversial *tāratamya* / eternal-damnation doctrine are cataloged in [Rāmānuja & Madhva](ramanuja-and-madhva.md). (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*; Poojn.in)

- **Founding deity** - **Brahmā**, who receives the *tattvavāda* from Nārāyaṇa and transmits it via **Nārada** and **Vyāsa**; Madhva is held to be the **third avatāra of Vāyu** (after Hanumān and Bhīma). (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*) [traditional]
- **Doctrine — Dvaita** - uncompromising realist pluralism: God, souls, and matter eternally and really distinct (*pañca-bheda*). (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*).
- **Head ācārya** - **Madhva / Ānandatīrtha / Pūrṇaprajña** (**1238–1317**), founder of the Udupi seat-cluster. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhvacharya*) — see [Rāmānuja & Madhva](ramanuja-and-madhva.md).

### Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism within Brahma-Madhva (the **Brahma-Mādhva-Gauḍīya** line)

- **The affiliation claim** - **Caitanya Mahāprabhu** (1486–1534) and his theology of **Acintya-bheda-abheda** ("inconceivable difference-and-non-difference") are ritually grafted onto the Brahma (Madhva) line, producing the compound name **Brahma-Mādhva-Gauḍīya Sampradāya**; the link is said to run Madhva → … → **Mādhavendra Purī → Īśvara Purī → Caitanya**. (Source: HareKrishnaMandir.org; iskconcommunications.org, *The Sampradaya of Sri Caitanya*) [traditional]
- **ISKCON's position in the chain** - **A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda** (founder of **ISKCON**, 1966) reckons himself the **32nd link** in this paramparā, counted from Kṛṣṇa through Brahmā, Nārada, Vyāsa, Madhva, Caitanya, and the Six Gosvāmīs to the modern Bengali line (Bhaktivinoda → Bhaktisiddhānta → Prabhupāda). (Source: HareKrishnaMandir.org, *Our Sampradaya*; harekrishnasociety.org) [traditional]
- **The scholarly dispute** - academics (and some rival Vaiṣṇavas) question whether Gauḍīya theology genuinely *derives* from Madhva's Dvaita, given the deep doctrinal gulf (Madhva's hard dualism vs Caitanya's bhedābheda, plus the Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa focus absent in Madhva). The historical bridge rests largely on **Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa's** 18th-c. defense (his *Govinda-bhāṣya* on the Brahma Sūtras and *Prameya-ratnāvalī*), composed to win the Gauḍīyas a recognized seat at the Galta (Jaipur) debate. (Source: iskconcommunications.org; raganugaprembhakti) [scholarly] [disputed]
  - *Variant (rival reading):* some hold Caitanya took *sannyāsa* in the Bhāratī (Śaṅkara Daśanāmī) line and that the Madhva affiliation is a **later, formal regularization** rather than a living transmission. (Source: iskconcommunications.org) [scholarly] [disputed]

## 3. Rudra Sampradāya — Śiva → Viṣṇusvāmī / Vallabha (Śuddhādvaita, Puṣṭimārga)

The line whose founding agent is **Rudra (Śiva)** — the supreme Vaiṣṇava (*vaiṣṇavānāṃ yathā śambhuḥ*) — transmitting Kṛṣṇa-bhakti centred on the child-Kṛṣṇa and the grace-path. Its school is **Śuddhādvaita**, "pure non-dualism." (Source: Wikipedia, *Shuddhadvaita*, *Pushtimarg*; Poojn.in)

- **Founding deity** - **Śiva/Rudra**, who is given the doctrine by Viṣṇu and seeds the line; the human fountainhead is **Viṣṇusvāmī**. (Source: HareKrishnaMandir.org) [traditional]
- **Viṣṇusvāmī** - the named founding ācārya appointed by Rudra; credited with first propounding *śuddhādvaita-brahmavāda* (the universe as a real, pure transformation of Brahman). **[disputed]** — almost nothing survives: his dates are uncertain (variously placed 13th–14th c. or earlier), his works are **lost**, and his historicity is debated; what remains of the Rudra line is effectively the **Vallabha (Puṣṭimārga)** order. (Source: raganugaprembhakti, *Śuddhādvaita-vāda*; Wikipedia, *Pushtimarg*) [scholarly] [disputed]
  - *Variant (transmission chain):* tradition links Vallabha to Viṣṇusvāmī through **Bilvamaṅgala (Līlāśuka)** as intermediate guru, citing the *Śāṇḍilya Saṃhitā*. (Source: raganugaprembhakti) [traditional] [disputed]
  - *Variant (two branches):* the Rudra line is sometimes split into **(a) the Viṣṇusvāmīs** and **(b) the Vallabhas / Puṣṭimārga**, the latter eclipsing the former. (Source: Wikipedia, *Pushtimarg*)
- **Vallabha / Vallabhācārya** - **1479–1531 CE**, the great systematizer who revived Śuddhādvaita and founded **Puṣṭimārga** ("the path of grace/nourishment"), Kṛṣṇa-worship as **Śrīnāthjī** (the child-Kṛṣṇa of Govardhana); seat at **Nāthdwārā (Rajasthan)**. Authored *Aṇubhāṣya* (Brahma-Sūtra commentary), *Subodhinī* (on the [Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md)), and *Ṣoḍaśa-grantha*. (Source: Britannica, *Vallabha*; Wikipedia, *Shuddhadvaita*; bharatpedia, *Vallabha*) [scholarly]
- **Doctrine — Śuddhādvaita** - "*pure* non-dualism": Brahman (Kṛṣṇa) is non-dual and real, and the world is a **real, unmodified manifestation** of Brahman — not illusory (*māyā*) as in [Śaṅkara's](adi-shankara.md) "*a*-śuddha" Advaita, which Vallabha holds is corrupted by an unreal māyā. Liberation is by **puṣṭi** (God's spontaneous grace), not works or knowledge. (Source: Wikipedia, *Shuddhadvaita*; bbhatt01.wordpress) [scholarly]

## 4. Kumāra / Sanakādi Sampradāya — the Four Kumāras → Nimbārka (Dvaitādvaita)

The line whose founding agents are the **four Kumāras** (Sanaka, Sananda, Sanātana, Sanatkumāra — the mind-born celibate sages); also called the **Haṃsa, Catuḥ-Sana, or Sanakādi Sampradāya**. Its school is **Dvaitādvaita / Bhedābheda**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Nimbarka Sampradaya*; HandWiki, *Kumara Sampradaya*)

- **Founding paramparā (with the Haṃsa avatāra)** - the doctrine is revealed by **Haṃsa Bhagavān** (Viṣṇu's swan-avatāra) → the **four Kumāras (Sanakādi)** → **Nārada Muni** → **Nimbārka**. The swan-form's instruction to the Kumāras is the line's origin myth. (Source: vedanta.redzambala, *Nimbarka Sampradaya*; Wikipedia, *Nimbarka Sampradaya*) [traditional]
- **Nimbārka / Nimbāditya** - the human head-ācārya, a Telugu Brahmin who settled at **Govardhana / Vṛndāvana**; revered as an *aṃśa* of the **Sudarśana cakra**; pioneered the joint worship of **Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa** as a divine couple. Chief work: *Vedānta-pārijāta-saurabha* (Brahma-Sūtra commentary) + *Daśaśloki*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Nimbarka Sampradaya*; Grokipedia) [scholarly]
  - *Dates — variant (scholarly):* most academics place Nimbārka **after Rāmānuja**, c. **12th–13th c. CE** (or even 13th–14th). (Source: Wikipedia, *Nimbarka Sampradaya*) [scholarly]
  - *Dates — variant (traditional / early):* the sampradāya itself dates Nimbārka much earlier — some claim **c. 7th c. CE** or before, making him the earliest of the four ācāryas. (Source: vedanta.redzambala; Wikipedia, *Nimbarka Sampradaya*) [traditional] [disputed]
- **Doctrine — Dvaitādvaita (Bhedābheda)** - "dualistic non-dualism": the soul and world are **simultaneously different and non-different** (*bheda* and *abheda*) from Brahman — distinct as effects/parts yet inseparable from their cause, like waves and the sea. Means = *prapatti* + *bhakti* to Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa. (Source: Wikipedia, *Nimbarka Sampradaya*; HandWiki, *Nimbarka Sampradaya*) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (relation to Bhāskara/Yādava):* Bhedābheda predates Nimbārka (Bhāskara, c. 9th c.); Nimbārka's version is distinguished as the **theistic, Kṛṣṇa-centred** *svābhāvika-bhedābheda* ("natural" difference-non-difference). (Source: Wikipedia, *Bhedabheda*) [scholarly]

## The four side by side

| Sampradāya | Founding agent | Head ācārya | Vedānta school | Core stance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Śrī** | Lakṣmī/Śrī | Rāmānuja (1017–1137 trad.) | Viśiṣṭādvaita | qualified non-dualism; soul = Brahman's body |
| **Brahma** | Brahmā | Madhva (1238–1317) | Dvaita / Tattvavāda | real, eternal 5-fold difference |
| **Rudra** | Śiva/Rudra | Viṣṇusvāmī → Vallabha (1479–1531) | Śuddhādvaita | pure non-dualism; world a real manifestation; grace (puṣṭi) |
| **Kumāra/Sanakādi** | four Kumāras (via Haṃsa) | Nimbārka (12–13th c. acad. / 7th c. trad.) | Dvaitādvaita / Bhedābheda | simultaneous difference + non-difference |

(Source: Poojn.in; HareKrishnaMandir.org; Wikipedia, *Nimbarka Sampradaya*, *Shuddhadvaita*, *Madhvacharya*, *Sri Vaishnavism*)

- **Later orders mapped onto the four** - the major post-classical Kṛṣṇa-bhakti movements each claim one of the four as parent: **Gauḍīya / ISKCON** → Brahma-Madhva; **Puṣṭimārga (Vallabha)** → Rudra; the **Haridāsī / Rādhāvallabha** and **Nimbārkī** orders of Vraja → Kumāra. The Vārkarī and Ekasaraṇa streams (see [section index](README.md)) sit largely outside the formal four. (Source: Poojn.in; HareKrishnaMandir.org) [scholarly]
