# Janābāī (Sant Janī) — The Maidservant Poet of Pandharpur

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](./README.md) (master index: [`../README.md`](../README.md)). The 13th–14th-c. **Vārkarī** woman poet-saint, the **maidservant (*dāsī*)** in [Nāmdev](namdev.md)'s household at **Pandharpur**, and one of the most beloved women voices of Marathi *bhakti* — her *abhaṅgas* surviving alongside those of [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md), Nāmdev and [Tukārām](tukaram.md).
> Frame against the lineage roster and the Vārkarī succession in [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md). Two clocks: this is **academic / historical** Kali-Yuga dating, not Purāṇic yuga-time — and the historical poet must be distinguished from the legendary Janī of the miracle-narratives.

## Who She Was

- **Janābāī (Janī, Sant Janī, Dāsī Janī)** — a 13th–14th-c. Marathi **poet-saint** of the **Vārkarī** movement, remembered above all as the **maidservant (*dāsī*)** in the Pandharpur household of [Nāmdev](namdev.md), and as one of the earliest and most cherished **women voices** of the Viṭṭhal cult. (Source: Wikipedia, *Janabai*)
- **Birth & origin [traditional]** — born at **Gangākheḍ** (Gangakhed), on the **Godāvarī** in present-day Parbhani district, to parents **Dāmā** and **Karuṇḍ**, who are said to have been low-caste Vārkarī devotees; after her mother's death the young Janī was placed in the service of Nāmdev's family at **Pandharpur**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Janabai*) [folk]
- **Caste & status [scholarly]** — her low birth and her station as a household **servant-woman** are integral to her appeal and to the egalitarian charter of *bhakti*: like Nāmdev (a tailor), Tukārām and Ravidās, she embodies the movement's claim that devotion, not birth or gender, opens the divine. (Source: Wikipedia, *Janabai*)
- **The *dāsī* self-signature** — she famously signs her verse not as a poet but as **"Dāsī Janī" ("Janī the maidservant" / "the servant-girl Janī")**, folding her lowly social role directly into her devotional identity. (Source: Marathi *abhaṅgas*; Wikipedia, *Janabai*)

## Dating — the Vārkarī Generation [disputed]

- **Conventional span — c. 1258–1350 CE** — the most commonly cited modern dates make her a **contemporary of [Nāmdev](namdev.md)** (c. 1270–1350) and of [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md) (c. 1275–1296), placing her squarely in the **founding generation** of the Vārkarī *kīrtana* tradition. (Source: Wikipedia, *Janabai*)
- **Lifelong household tenure [traditional]** — tradition has her serving in Nāmdev's home across decades, from Nāmdev's own lifetime, so that her biography is braided into his family's story rather than recorded independently. (Source: Wikipedia, *Janabai*) [folk]
- **Textual-criticism caveat [scholarly]** — as with all the early Vārkarī sants, the **authentic core vs. later accretion** in her corpus is unresolved; precise dates are unrecoverable, and what survives of "Janī" is an **open roster** of *abhaṅgas* transmitted through the Vārkarī song-tradition.

## The Household-Labor Hagiography [folk]

- **Devotion in the work of the home** — Janābāī's distinctive idiom is **bhakti embedded in domestic drudgery**: grinding grain at the quern (*jāte*), sweeping, plastering with cow-dung, gathering dung-cakes, fetching water, washing — the menial tasks of a *dāsī* become her continuous prayer and the very imagery of her verse. (Source: Marathi *abhaṅgas*; Wikipedia, *Janabai*)
- **Viṭṭhal the helper at the grindstone** — the best-loved cluster of legends has **Viṭhobā / Viṭṭhal Himself descending to help Janī with her chores** — turning the heavy quern with her, sweeping the yard, gathering cow-dung, even combing her hair and bathing her — the Lord of Pandharpur lowering Himself to the servant-girl's labor out of love. (Source: Vārkarī hagiography) [folk]
  - *Variant (the grinding-song *abhaṅgas*):* several of her most famous verses are **quern-songs (*jātyāvarīl ovyā* / grinding-songs)** in which she sings *Viṭhū* by her side at the millstone — the genre of women's work-song turned into devotional poetry. (Source: Marathi *abhaṅgas*)
- **Viṭṭhal as friend and intimate** — her theology addresses Viṭhobā not with distant awe but as **friend, helper, mother and household companion** — she scolds Him, summons Him, claims Him as her own (*"my Viṭhū"*); the intimacy of the servant who treats God as a member of the family is her signature note. (Source: Marathi *abhaṅgas*; Wikipedia, *Janabai*) [scholarly]
- **The stolen-necklace / theft legend** — a recurring miracle-tale has Janī **falsely accused of stealing the temple's necklace (or other temple property)** and condemned to death; Viṭṭhal intervenes to save His devotee, the wrongful charge dissolving by divine grace. (Source: Vārkarī hagiography) [folk]
  - *Variant (regional tellings):* details of the accusation, the accuser and the mode of deliverance vary across regional Vārkarī retellings; the constant is **God defending the lowly servant-woman against the powerful**. [regional]
- **Witness to Jñāneśvar's *samādhi* [traditional]** — some hagiographic strands place Janī among the devotees present in the circle around [Nāmdev](namdev.md) and [Jñāneśvar](jnaneshwar.md), connecting her to the foundational events of the tradition (e.g. Jñāneśvar's *sañjīvana samādhi* at Āḷandī, 1296). (Source: Vārkarī hagiography) [folk/disputed]

## Association with Nāmdev & the Viṭṭhal Cult

- **In Nāmdev's house** — Janī's life and verse are inseparable from **[Nāmdev](namdev.md)'s family**: she lived as the *dāsī* of his household, knew his parents **Dāmāśeṭī** and **Goṇāī** and his domestic world, and her *abhaṅgas* repeatedly name **"Nāmā"** as elder, master and fellow-devotee. (Source: Wikipedia, *Janabai*, *Namdev*)
- **The Pandharpur / Viṭṭhal world** — her entire devotional universe is the **saguṇa** worship of **Viṭhobā** at **Pandharpur** on the Candrabhāgā (Bhīmā) — the deity, town and Puṇḍalīka-legend of the wider Vārkarī cult (see [`../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md`](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md)). (Source: Wikipedia, *Janabai*, *Vithoba*)
- **The *kīrtana* movement** — she belongs to the first flowering of communal **Vārkarī *kīrtana*** (*nāma-saṅkīrtana*) that Nāmdev is credited with founding; her work-songs and *abhaṅgas* fed directly into the singing tradition rather than into Sanskrit scripture. (Source: Wikipedia, *Warkari*)
- **A woman's voice in the roster** — she is the **most celebrated of the early women Vārkarī poets**, standing in any roll of Marathi women saints much as **Āṇḍāḷ** does among the Tamil Ālvārs or **Mīrābāī** among the northern bhaktas. (Source: Wikipedia, *Janabai*) [scholarly]

## Her Works

- **~300+ *abhaṅgas*** — roughly **300 or more *abhaṅgas* are attributed to Janābāī**, preserved in the Vārkarī song-corpus; this makes her, despite her humble station, one of the **more prolific** of the early Marathi sants by surviving count. (Source: Wikipedia, *Janabai*)
  - *Count caveat [scholarly]:* as with Nāmdev and Tukārām, the figure mixes a genuine core with later attributions — the corpus under "Janī"/"Dāsī Janī" is an **open roster**, not a fixed canon.
- **Preserved in the *Abhaṅga-gāthā* / *Sakal Sant Gāthā*** — her verses survive principally within the great **Vārkarī anthologies** — the **Abhaṅga-gāthā** and the collected **Sakal Sant Gāthā** ("Collection of All the Saints") — gathered alongside the *abhaṅgas* of Nāmdev, Eknāth, Tukārām and the wider Vārkarī fellowship. (Source: Marathi *Sakal Sant Gāthā*; Wikipedia, *Janabai*)
- **The work-song genre** — her best-known verses are **grinding-songs and chore-songs**, a women's oral genre elevated into bhakti literature; alongside these are intensely personal *abhaṅgas* of longing for, and quarrel with, Viṭhobā. (Source: Marathi *abhaṅgas*) [scholarly]
- **Verses on the sants** — she is also credited with *abhaṅgas* **about other saints** — including biographical/devotional verses on [Nāmdev](namdev.md) and on the Vārkarī fellowship — making her an early witness to the movement's own self-memory. (Source: Wikipedia, *Janabai*)

## Place in the Tradition

- **Jñāneśvar → Nāmdev → Eknāth → [Tukārām](tukaram.md)** — within this canonical Vārkarī succession (see [`north-bhakti-sants.md`](north-bhakti-sants.md)), Janābāī stands beside the **second pillar, [Nāmdev](namdev.md)**, as his household devotee and the foremost **woman poet** of the early movement. (Source: Wikipedia, *Warkari*)
- **Standard in the women-saints roster** — she is a fixed name in any list of **Marathi women bhakti poets** (with later figures such as Bahiṇābāī and Muktābāī), and in pan-Indian rosters of women saints alongside **Mīrābāī**, **Lallā / Lal Ded**, **Āṇḍāḷ** and **Karaikkāl Ammaiyār**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Janabai*) [scholarly]
- **Living veneration** — venerated as **Sant Janābāī**, she is honored at **Pandharpur** and at **Gangākheḍ** (her traditional birthplace, where a shrine/*samādhi* is maintained); her grinding-songs remain part of the living Vārkarī and Marathi domestic-devotional repertoire. (Source: Wikipedia, *Janabai*) [regional]
