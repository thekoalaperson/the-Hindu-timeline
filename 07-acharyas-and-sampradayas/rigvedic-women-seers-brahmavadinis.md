# The Rigvedic brahmavadinis / women seers

> Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](README.md) — the **women seers (ṛṣikās / brahmavādinīs)** to whom the Ṛgveda itself attributes hymns: the oldest named female voices in the tradition. Up-link: section index [`README.md`](README.md).
> "Two clocks" caution: by the **scriptural clock** these belong to the Vedic *mantra-dṛṣṭā* age (placed by tradition deep in the Tretā / Dvāpara teacher-genealogies); by the **historical clock** their hymns are layers of the [Ṛgveda](../90-literature-corpus/vedas/rigveda.md), the oldest c. 1500–1200 BCE, with the late-Maṇḍala-10 hymns younger — method in [recorded-history.md](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md). [scholarly]

---

## The category — brahmavādinī / ṛṣikā

- **The set** — the Ṛgveda's traditional *anukramaṇī* (index of seers) credits roughly **two dozen hymns to women** (the count varies by reckoning, c. 20–27); these women are the canonical **first-tier ṛṣikās**, the named female *mantra-dṛṣṭās* ("seers of the mantra") of the oldest Indic text. (Source: Bṛhaddevatā 2.82–84; Wikipedia, *Rishika (Vedic)*)
- **Brahmavādinī vs. sadyovadhū** — later tradition splits learned women into the **brahmavādinī** (lifelong student/expounder of the Veda) and the **sadyovadhū** (one who studies only until marriage); the Ṛgvedic seers are read back as the paradigm brahmavādinīs. (Source: *Harīta-smṛti*, as cited in scholarship [late text])
- **The Bṛhaddevatā list** — Śaunaka's *Bṛhaddevatā* (a late-Vedic index of Ṛgvedic deities and seers) gives the stock enumeration of women seers — **Ghoṣā, Godhā, Viśvavārā, Apālā, Upaniṣad (Niṣad), Juhū, Aditi, Indrāṇī, Sāramā, Romaśā, Urvaśī, Lopāmudrā, Yamī, Śaśvatī, Śrī, Lākṣā** and others — the canonical roll repeated in nearly all later catalogs. (Source: Bṛhaddevatā 2.82–84 [late text])
- **Relation to repo's other women** — overlaps with the Upaniṣadic [Gārgī & Maitreyī](gargi-and-maitreyi.md) (a later, *dialogic* age), and with two Ṛgvedic seers already cataloged elsewhere: **Lopāmudrā** (the Agastya dialogue-hymn RV 1.179, see [agastya.md §4](../04-deep-dives/agastya.md)) and **Vāk Āmbhṛṇī** (the Devī Sūkta RV 10.125, see [rigveda.md](../90-literature-corpus/vedas/rigveda.md) and [saraswati.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saraswati.md)). The four canonical members below are the ones with no prior treatment.

## Ghoṣā Kākṣīvatī — the seer healed by the Aśvins

- **Ghoṣā** — daughter of **Kakṣīvat** (himself a Ṛgvedic seer) and granddaughter of **Dīrghatamas**; seer of the twin hymns **RV 10.39–40**, praises addressed to the physician-twins the **Aśvins**. (Source: Ṛgveda 10.39–40; Bṛhaddevatā)
- **The affliction** — tradition holds Ghoṣā was struck by a disfiguring skin disease (read as leprosy / *kuṣṭha* in later glosses), which kept her **unmarried in her father's house** into old age; her hymns plead with the Aśvins, the great divine healers, for a cure and a husband. (Source: Ṛgveda 10.39–40; classical commentary [late text])
- **The cure and marriage** — the Aśvins, moved by her praise, **restore her health and youth**, after which she is wedded; the hymn-cycle is read as her thanksgiving. **RV 10.40** speaks of the Aśvins as bridegroom-bringers and seekers-out of the lonely. (Source: Ṛgveda 10.40)
  - *Variant (the son's hymn):* a tradition holds her son **Suhastya** also became a seer, the Ghoṣā line thus producing a multi-generation chain of *mantra-dṛṣṭās*. (Source: classical commentary [late text])
- **Status [scholarly]** — Ghoṣā is the standard first name in any list of Ṛgvedic ṛṣikās; her hymns are among the rare Vedic texts giving a **woman's first-person plea** for personal healing and union, addressed to the most "human" of the Vedic gods.

## Apālā Ātreyī — the Indra dialogue and the skin-cure

- **Apālā** — daughter of **Atri** (of the Atri-clan seers); seer of **RV 8.91**, a vivid little dialogue-hymn to **Indra**. (Source: Ṛgveda 8.91; Bṛhaddevatā)
- **The Soma offering** — the hymn narrates Apālā going to the water, finding a Soma plant, and **pressing it with her teeth** to offer Indra, calling the god to drink — an intimate, almost folk-ritual scene of a woman performing the Soma pressing herself. (Source: Ṛgveda 8.91)
- **The triple purification** — pleased, Indra **draws Apālā three times through the holes of his chariot, cart, and yoke**, and in the process makes her **skin pure / golden**: tradition reads this as a cure for a disfiguring skin condition that had estranged her father and husband. (Source: Ṛgveda 8.91.7)
- **The threefold boon** — in the dialogue Apālā asks Indra to make grow what is barren: her **father's bald head**, his **fallow field**, and her **own body (lower belly / kakṣa)** — a triad of fertility-and-restoration boons that the god grants. (Source: Ṛgveda 8.91)
  - *Variant (reading the cure):* commentators differ on whether Apālā's affliction is leprosy-type skin disease or a more general "ugliness/barrenness"; the *Ṣaḍviṃśa* and later glosses elaborate the skin-cure reading. (Source: classical commentary [disputed])
- **Status [scholarly]** — RV 8.91 is one of the most-discussed women's hymns of the Ṛgveda for its rare picture of a woman as **independent ritual actor** in direct, bantering exchange with Indra.

## Romaśā — the king's-bride hymn

- **Romaśā** — named seer of a fragment in **RV 1.126** (verse 7, sometimes the closing couplet); presented as the **wife of King Bhāvayavya / Svanaya** (the patron praised in the surrounding *dānastuti*, the "gift-praise" hymn of Kakṣīvat). (Source: Ṛgveda 1.126; Bṛhaddevatā)
- **The bridal dialogue** — the verses are an intimate exchange between Romaśā and her royal husband: she answers his touch, declaring herself **"hairy / fleecy all over (romaśā)" like a ewe of the Gandhāris** — a frank conjugal verse, exceptional in the Ṛgvedic corpus for its plain erotic register in a woman's voice. (Source: Ṛgveda 1.126.7)
  - *Variant (name as epithet):* "Romaśā" may be less a personal name than the descriptive epithet of the speaking queen, the verse being attached to the Svanaya *dānastuti* of Kakṣīvat; the seer-attribution is a traditional reading of the *anukramaṇī*. (Source: classical commentary [disputed])
- **Status [scholarly]** — Romaśā is invariably listed among the ṛṣikās though her contribution is a **single dialogue-verse**, prized as a candid woman's voice on marriage rather than for ritual or cosmology.

## Viśvavārā Ātreyī — the woman at the fire-altar

- **Viśvavārā** — of the **Atri** clan (hence Ātreyī); seer of **RV 5.28**, a short hymn to **Agni**, the fire-god. (Source: Ṛgveda 5.28; Bṛhaddevatā)
- **The dawn fire-offering** — the hymn shows Viśvavārā **herself kindling and tending the sacrificial fire at dawn**, pouring the oblation and praying for concord, long life, and prosperity for the household — a woman acting as the **officiant (hotṛ-like) at the Agni rite**, not merely a wife in attendance. (Source: Ṛgveda 5.28)
- **The prayer for harmony** — a verse asks that **husband and wife be of one mind**, the household united in dharma — read as a model of the wife as co-celebrant of the domestic fire. (Source: Ṛgveda 5.28.3)
- **Status [scholarly]** — Viśvavārā is the standard fourth member of the canonical ṛṣikā quartet (with Ghoṣā, Apālā, Romaśā); her hymn is cited as Vedic evidence for **women performing the fire-sacrifice in their own right**.

## Already cataloged elsewhere (overlap members)

- **Lopāmudrā** — seer of the husband-wife dialogue **RV 1.179**, urging the ascetic Agastya to the householder's procreative dharma; treated in [agastya.md §4](../04-deep-dives/agastya.md). (Source: Ṛgveda 1.179)
- **Vāk Āmbhṛṇī** — daughter of the seer **Ambhṛṇa**, seer of the **Devī Sūkta / Vāgāmbhṛṇī Sūkta RV 10.125**, where seer (*ṛṣikā*) and deity (*devatā*, Speech proclaiming herself the all-pervading power) are identical — a foundation-stone of later Śākta theology; see [rigveda.md](../90-literature-corpus/vedas/rigveda.md) and [saraswati.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saraswati.md). (Source: Ṛgveda 10.125)
- **Other names in the roll** — **Godhā, Indrāṇī (RV 10.86, the Vṛṣākapi dialogue), Sāramā, Juhū Brahmajāyā, Śaśvatī, Yamī (RV 10.10, the Yama–Yamī dialogue), Urvaśī (RV 10.95, the Purūravas dialogue), Aditi** — variously credited as ṛṣikās in the *anukramaṇī* and *Bṛhaddevatā*, several being the female speaker of a famous dialogue-hymn rather than a sole composer. (Source: Bṛhaddevatā 2.82–84 [late text])

## Reception & two clocks

- **Canonical proof-text for women's Vedic learning** — Ghoṣā, Apālā, Romaśā, Viśvavārā, Lopāmudrā and Vāk are the stock names cited (alongside the later [Gārgī and Maitreyī](gargi-and-maitreyi.md)) to evidence women's access to the Veda; they recur in modern reform and women's-education discourse as the indigenous precedent for the learned woman. (Source: Wikipedia, *Rishika (Vedic)*)
- **Two clocks** — by the **scriptural clock** these seers belong to the Vedic *mantra-dṛṣṭā* age within the genealogies of the Ṛṣis; by the **historical clock** their hymns are stratified layers of the Ṛgveda (family books oldest, Maṇḍala 10 hymns like Ghoṣā's and Vāk's among the youngest, c. late 2nd millennium BCE) — see [recorded-history.md](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md). [scholarly]
