# Nammālvār — the Foremost Ālvār and the Tiruvāymoḻi

*Tirukkuṟuhūr / Āḻvārtirunagari, Pāṇḍya country, Tamil Nadu — c. 8th–9th c. CE (academic) / 3059 BCE (traditional).*
*Sits under [07-acharyas-and-sampradayas](./README.md). See the survey of the saints in [alvars-and-nayanars.md](./alvars-and-nayanars.md) and the ācārya succession in [ramanuja-and-madhva.md](./ramanuja-and-madhva.md). Master index: [../README.md](../README.md).*

**Nammāḻvār** ("Our Ālvār"), born **Māṟaṉ** and titled **Caṭakōpaṉ** (Śaṭhakopa / Caṭhakōpa), is reckoned the **foremost and most prolific of the twelve Ālvārs** — the supreme devotee whose Tamil hymns the Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition holds equal to the Veda itself. His four works form roughly a third of the 4,000-verse **Nālāyira Divya Prabandham**, and his **Tiruvāymoḻi** ("utterance of the sacred mouth") is venerated as the **Drāviḍa / Tamil Veda**, specifically the Tamil counterpart of the **Sāma Veda**. He is the **prapanna-jana-kūṭastha** — the foundational ancestor of all who take refuge (*prapatti*) — and the *mukhya* hymnist around whom the entire Śrīvaiṣṇava ācārya tradition (Nāthamuni → Yāmuna → Rāmānuja) crystallised. **Two-clock dating note:** academic consensus places him in the late 8th–mid-9th c. CE [scholarly]; Vaiṣṇava tradition assigns him to **3059 BCE**, near the start of Kali Yuga [traditional] [disputed].

---

## Identity, birth & legend

- **Names** - born **Māṟaṉ**; honorific **Caṭakōpaṉ** ("vanquisher of Caṭha," the wind that afflicts newborns, which he is said to have subdued at birth); affectionately **Nammāḻvār** ("our Ālvār"); Sanskritised as **Śaṭhakopa / Parāṅkuśa**; also styled **Vakuḷābharaṇa** (wearer of the *makiḻ/vakuḷa* flower) (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar)).
- **Birthplace** - **Tirukkuṟuhūr**, on the Tāmraparṇī river in the Pāṇḍya country, the town renamed **Āḻvārtirunagari** ("the sacred city of the Ālvār") in his honour; he is the only Ālvār whose home shrine bears his own name (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar)).
- **Family** - born to a **Veḷḷāḷar** (agrarian, non-Brahmin) family; father **Kāri** (a Pāṇḍya official) and mother **Uḍaiya Naṅkai / Uḍayanaṅkaiyār**; his non-Brahmin birth is theologically significant — the "Tamil Veda" is revealed through a Śūdra saint, dignifying *bhakti* over caste [scholarly] (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar)).
- **The silent-child legend** - tradition holds he never cried, took food, or spoke from birth, but sat in *padmāsana* in a hollow of a **tamarind tree** (held to be a form of Ādiśeṣa / the Lord) at the Ādinātha temple for **sixteen years**, eyes closed in absorption (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar)). [traditional] [folk]
- **The breaking of silence** - the elder saint **Madhurakavi Āḻvār**, on pilgrimage in the north, saw a brilliant light to the south and followed it to the tamarind tree; he posed the riddle *"If the small (soul) is born in the belly of the dead (body), what will it eat and where will it lie?"* The boy's first-ever words answered it — *"That it will eat, and there it will lie"* — declaring the soul's total dependence on God; Madhurakavi became his disciple on the spot (Source: [Tiruvaymoli Introduction — Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/tiruvaymoli-english/d/doc1206616.html)). [traditional]
- **Lifespan & end** - tradition gives him a brief life (commonly **35 years**) ending in *paramapada-prāpti* (ascent to Vaikuṇṭha), commemorated annually as his apotheosis (see festivals below) (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar)). [traditional]

## The four works — Nammālvār's "Tamil Veda"

His disciple **Madhurakavi** is credited with compiling and preserving the four works; together they total **1,296 verses** by the strict sum of their parts (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar)).

- **Tiruvāymoḻi** ("utterance of the sacred mouth") - his magnum opus, **1,102 verses (pāsurams)**, structured as **10 *pattu* (hundreds)**, each divided into **10 decads (tiruvāymoḻi)** of (mostly) 10 verses, composed in the chained **antāti** form (the last word/syllable of one verse opening the next), the whole cycle closing back on its first word. It is the climactic text of the Prabandham, recited last in temple liturgy. Equated with the **Sāma Veda** — the sung Veda — in the Śrīvaiṣṇava scheme (Source: [Tiruvaymoli — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruvaymoli)).
- **Tiruviruttam** ("the sacred *viruttam*") - **100 verses**; an *akam* (love-poetry) cycle of *viraha* (separation-longing), the soul as a heroine pining for the Lord. Equated with the **Ṛg Veda** (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar)).
- **Tiruvāciriyam** (Tiru Aciriyam) - the shortest, **7 verses**, in the *āciriyam* metre; a hymn of God's glory and the soul's surrender. Equated with the **Yajur Veda** (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar)).
- **Periya Tiruvandhādhi** ("the great sacred *andhādhi*") - **87 verses** in *andhādhi* (verse-chaining) form, philosophical and reflective. Equated with the **Atharva Veda** (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar)).
- **Theological reading** - the four are not merely "like" the Vedas but, in Śrīvaiṣṇava self-understanding, are **the Veda in Tamil** ("he poured the cream of the Vedas into his songs"), making *bhakti/prapatti* accessible to all who cannot access Sanskrit śruti [scholarly] (Source: [Tiruvaymoli Introduction — Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/tiruvaymoli-english/d/doc1206616.html)).

### Variants — verse counts & Veda-correspondences
- *Variant (total verse count — 1,296 vs 1,352):* the arithmetic sum of the four works is **1,296** (1,102 + 100 + 7 + 87) per Wikipedia/Grokipedia; some Śrīvaiṣṇava listings and the cross-reference in [alvars-and-nayanars.md](./alvars-and-nayanars.md) give **1,352**, counting additional/variant attributions or different per-work tallies. Both circulate; the discrepancy is in the smaller works' enumeration. (Sources: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar); [Alvars — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvars)) [disputed]
- *Variant (which work = which Veda):* the standard mapping pairs **Tiruvāymoḻi = Sāma Veda**, **Tiruviruttam = Ṛg Veda**, **Tiruvāciriyam = Yajur Veda**, **Periya Tiruvandhādhi = Atharva Veda**; some sources state the four "represent respectively the Sāma, Ṛg, Yajur and Atharva Vedas" in list order (placing Tiruvāymoḻi as Sāma first) — the substance (Tiruvāymoḻi = Sāma) is constant. (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar))
- *Variant (Tiruvāymoḻi verse count — 1,102 vs 1,100):* the decad structure ideally yields 1,000, but several decads carry an 11th "signature" (*phala-śruti*) verse, giving the canonical **1,102**; rounded references to "~1,100" appear. (Source: [Tiruvaymoli — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruvaymoli))

## Status: prapanna-jana-kūṭastha & the head of the surrendered

- **Prapanna-jana-kūṭastha** - the "foremost / ancestral pillar (*kūṭastha*) of the community of the surrendered (*prapanna-jana*)"; Nammāḻvār is venerated as the original *prapanna* whose self-surrender (*prapatti / śaraṇāgati*) is the model for the whole Śrīvaiṣṇava soteriology (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar)). See *prapatti* in [ramanuja-and-madhva.md](./ramanuja-and-madhva.md).
- **Foremost Ālvār** - among the twelve he holds the central liturgical place; in temple recitation the Prabandham culminates in his Tiruvāymoḻi, and his image is carried at the head of festival processions (Source: [Tiruvaymoli — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruvaymoli)).
- **Ranked among the top Hindu mystics** - tradition and some modern surveys place him among the very greatest devotional poets, alongside Māṇikkavācakar and Kabīr [scholarly] (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar)). Cf. [kabir.md](./kabir.md).
- **Madhurakavi's *ācārya-bhakti***  - uniquely, his disciple **Madhurakavi Āḻvār** is himself counted among the twelve Ālvārs though his sole work, *Kaṇṇinuṇ Siṟuttāmbu* (11 verses), praises **not Viṣṇu but Nammāḻvār** — establishing the Śrīvaiṣṇava principle that devotion to the *guru/ācārya* is devotion to God (Source: [alvars-and-nayanars.md](./alvars-and-nayanars.md)).

## Foundation of the Śrīvaiṣṇava ācārya tradition

The transmission from the Ālvārs' Tamil hymns to systematic Viśiṣṭādvaita theology runs through Nammāḻvār's Tiruvāymoḻi as its scriptural anchor.

- **Nāthamuni's recovery (c. 824–924 CE)** - the founding ācārya **Nāthamuni** is said to have heard fragments of Tiruvāymoḻi sung, then recovered the lost full Prabandham; legend holds he recited Madhurakavi's 11 verses 12,000 times at Āḻvārtirunagari until Nammāḻvār appeared in yogic vision and revealed the entire 4,000, plus their music and esoteric meaning. He set them to music and instituted their temple recitation as *Drāviḍa Veda* (Sources: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar); [alvars-and-nayanars.md](./alvars-and-nayanars.md)). [traditional] [folk]
- **Yāmuna (Āḷavandār)** - Nāthamuni's grandson and successor, who began the philosophical defence of the Ālvār corpus and prepared the ground for Rāmānuja; the Tiruvāymoḻi's teaching on the soul's dependence and surrender feeds directly into his theology (Source: [ramanuja-and-madhva.md](./ramanuja-and-madhva.md)).
- **Rāmānuja (1017–1137 CE, trad.)** - systematiser of **Viśiṣṭādvaita**; his bhakti-prapatti synthesis is, in tradition, a Sanskrit-Vedānta articulation of Nammāḻvār's Tamil vision — Rāmānuja is even called the one who "fulfilled" the Ālvār's longing. His commentary on the Brahma-sūtra (Śrī Bhāṣya) and the Tamil hymns are treated as a single *Ubhaya Vedānta* ("dual Vedānta," Sanskrit + Tamil) (Source: [ramanuja-and-madhva.md](./ramanuja-and-madhva.md)).
- **The commentarial tradition on Tiruvāymoḻi** - a celebrated chain of vyākhyānas measured in *paḍi* (units of 32 syllables):
  - **Āṟāyirappaḍi (6,000)** - the earliest written commentary, by **Tirukkurukaippirāṉ Piḷḷāṉ** (a cousin-disciple of Rāmānuja), aligning the text with Viśiṣṭādvaita (Source: [The Tamil Veda — Piḷḷāṉ's Interpretation of Tiruvāymoḻi (acharya.org PDF)](https://acharya.org/bk/pb/jc/ttv-piot.pdf)).
  - **Onpadināyirappaḍi (9,000)** - by **Nañcīyar**.
  - **Paṉṉīrāyirappaḍi (12,000)** - by **Vādikesari Aḻagiya Maṇavāḷa Jīyar**.
  - **Irupattunālāyirappaḍi (24,000)** - by **Periyavāccāṉ Piḷḷai**.
  - **Muppattāṟāyirappaḍi / Īḍu (36,000)** - the **Īḍu**, the most authoritative, by **Vaḍakku Tiruvīdhi Piḷḷai** recording the oral exposition of his teacher **Nampiḷḷai** (13th c.) (Source: [Tiruvaymoli — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruvaymoli)).
  - *Variant (the "five commentaries"):* the set is traditionally enumerated as the **Five Bhāṣyas / *aimpaḍi*** (6k, 9k, 12k, 24k, 36k); later glosses (e.g. by Maṇavāḷa Māmuni) extend the chain. (Source: [Tiruvaymoli — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruvaymoli)) [scholarly]
- **Divya deśams** - Nammāḻvār's hymns praise a large share of the **108 divya deśams** (sacred Viṣṇu abodes); his decads are the liturgical "praise" attached to many shrines, making him the chief witness of the temple network (Source: [Tiruvaymoli — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruvaymoli)).

## The Āḻvārtirunagari cult & festivals

- **Ādinātha temple, Āḻvārtirunagari** - the saint's home shrine (one of the **Nava Tirupati**, the nine Viṣṇu temples of the Tāmraparṇī belt); the **tamarind tree** of the legend is still venerated there as a living relic that "never sheds its leaves at night" (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar)). [folk]
- **Garuḍa Sevai** - the festival in which the *utsava-mūrtis* of the Nava Tirupati (and the Ālvār) are carried on Garuḍa mounts; Nammāḻvār is central to this Tāmraparṇī-region celebration (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar)).
- **Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī / Adhyayana Utsava (Srirangam)** - during the great recitation festival at **Srirangam**, the **Tiruvāymoḻi is recited in full**, culminating in the dramatic enactment of **Nammāḻvār's ascent to Vaikuṇṭha (paramapada)** — the saint's image is taken into the sanctum (the "Vaikuṇṭha doorway," *Paramapada Vāsal*), staging his liberation before the assembled devotees (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar)).
- **Iconography** - depicted as a young yogi in *padmāsana*, eyes inward, often beneath the tamarind, wearing the *vakuḷa/makiḻ* garland; flanked in shrines by Madhurakavi (Source: [Nammalvar — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar)).
- *Variant (apotheosis date):* his *paramapada* is liturgically tied to the day after **Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī** in the Tamil month of **Mārgaḻi**, the closing of the 21-day Adhyayana Utsava; regional temples observe it on their own festival calendars. (Source: [Tiruvaymoli — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruvaymoli)) [traditional]

## Significance

- The **bridge from Tamil bhakti to Sanskritic Vedānta**: Nammāḻvār's Tiruvāymoḻi is the single text that the Śrīvaiṣṇava ācāryas treat as Veda-equivalent, giving the school its distinctive **Ubhaya Vedānta** (Tamil + Sanskrit dual scripture).
- His **prapatti** (self-surrender) becomes the defining soteriology of Śrīvaiṣṇavism, later splitting into the **Vaḍakalai** and **Teṉkalai** sub-schools' debate over grace (cf. [ramanuja-and-madhva.md](./ramanuja-and-madhva.md)).
- As a **non-Brahmin saint** whose hymns are chanted as Veda, he is a standing theological argument that *bhakti* transcends caste — paralleling the Nāyaṉār saints Nandanar and Kaṇṇappar (see [alvars-and-nayanars.md](./alvars-and-nayanars.md)).
- **Open roster flag:** the verse-count (1,296 vs 1,352), exact dating (3059 BCE vs 8th–9th c. CE), and the full commentarial chain beyond the five Bhāṣyas remain variably reported across traditional and academic sources [disputed].
