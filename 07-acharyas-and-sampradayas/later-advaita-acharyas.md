# The later Advaita ācāryas — from Sureśvara to Sadāśiva Brahmendra

> Parent: [Ācāryas & Sampradāyas](README.md). Detail node continuing [Ādi Śaṅkara & Advaita Vedānta](adi-shankara.md) past the four direct disciples: the post-Śaṅkara scholastics who split Advaita into the **Bhāmatī** and **Vivaraṇa** sub-schools (Sureśvara, Padmapāda, Vācaspati Miśra, Prakāśātman), then the great medieval and early-modern masters — **Vidyāraṇya** of Śṛṅgeri, **Madhusūdana Sarasvatī**, **Appayya Dīkṣita**, and the avadhūta **Sadāśiva Brahmendra**.
> Two clocks: all sit in **Kali Yuga** (begun 3102 BCE) of the current 28th Mahāyuga; academically they span **c. 8th–18th c. CE** [scholarly], though the Śṛṅgeri maṭha's own succession-chronology — anchored to a **509–477 BCE** Śaṅkara — pulls the early figures into the pre-Common-Era [disputed]. See the [two-clocks method](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md). The *texts* these ācāryas wrote are profiled at [Vedānta Prakaraṇa Granthas](../90-literature-corpus/darshanas/vedanta-prakarana-granthas.md); this file profiles the *teachers*.

---

## What "later Advaita" means

- **The scholastic turn** — Śaṅkara left a *bhāṣya* (commentary) tradition, not a closed system; the centuries after him are a running argument over questions he left under-determined — above all the **locus (āśraya) and object (viṣaya) of avidyā** (ignorance), the mechanism by which the one Brahman *appears* as many jīvas, and whether liberating knowledge (*jñāna*) suffices by itself or needs sustained meditation (*prasaṃkhyāna / nididhyāsana*). The answers cleave into two lineages. (Source: Wikipedia, *Advaita Vedanta*, "Sub-schools"; IEP, *Advaita Vedanta*)
- **Doctrinal placement** — for where Advaita sits among the six *darśanas*, see [Ātman–Brahman & the Six Darśanas](../05-concepts-and-dharma/atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md) and [Upavedas, Vedāṅgas & the Six Darśanas](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md); for the māyā/avidyā technicalities the sub-schools dispute, [Māyā & Avidyā](../05-concepts-and-dharma/maya-and-avidya.md).

## Sureśvara — the Vārttikakāra

- **Sureśvarācārya** — a direct disciple of Śaṅkara and, by Śṛṅgeri tradition, its first pontiff; hagiography identifies him with the converted Mīmāṃsā ritualist **Maṇḍana Miśra** (the equation is *disputed* by modern scholars, who treat Maṇḍana, author of the *Brahmasiddhi*, as a separate thinker — see [adi-shankara.md](adi-shankara.md), "Debate with Maṇḍana Miśra"). [scholarly] c. 8th c. CE. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sureshvara*; *Maṇḍana Miśra*)
- **The vārttikas** — his monumental verse-glosses (*vārttika*, "critical annotation") on Śaṅkara's own commentaries: the **Bṛhadāraṇyaka-upaniṣad-bhāṣya-vārttika** (the longest, ~11,000 verses) and the **Taittirīya-upaniṣad-bhāṣya-vārttika** — hence his epithet **Vārttikakāra**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sureshvara*)
- **Naiṣkarmya-siddhi** — his independent Advaita manual ("Establishment of Actionlessness"), arguing that liberation is by knowledge alone and that ritual action (*karma*) cannot combine with or contribute to it (*jñāna-karma-samuccaya* rejected). Also the **Mānasollāsa**, a gloss on Śaṅkara's *Dakṣiṇāmūrti-stotra*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sureshvara*; *Naishkarmya Siddhi*)
- **Doctrinal signature** — Sureśvara locates **avidyā in Brahman / pure consciousness itself** (not in the jīva) and holds that the great sayings (*mahāvākya*) produce immediate liberating cognition — positions the later **Vivaraṇa** school would inherit. His stream is sometimes named the **Vārttika-prasthāna**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sureshvara*, "Philosophy") [scholarly]

## Padmapāda and the two sub-schools

The post-Śaṅkara tradition bifurcates into two named lines, after the two seminal sub-commentaries on Śaṅkara's *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya*. Variants are cataloged, not merged — the schools genuinely disagree.

### Vivaraṇa (Padmapāda → Prakāśātman)

- **Padmapādācārya** — direct disciple of Śaṅkara (associated with Purī), author of the **Pañcapādikā**, a commentary that survives only as a gloss on the first four *sūtras* (the *catuḥsūtrī*) of the *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Padmapada*; [adi-shankara.md](adi-shankara.md))
- **Prakāśātman** — [scholarly] c. 10th–13th c. CE (dating uncertain) — wrote the **Pañcapādikā-Vivaraṇa**, the elaboration that *names the school*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Advaita Vedanta*; *Prakāśātman*)
- **Positions** — avidyā's **locus is Brahman itself** (Brahman is both *āśraya* and *viṣaya* of ignorance); there is **one** ignorance; the jīva is a **reflection (pratibimba-vāda)** of Brahman in avidyā; and **verbal knowledge from the mahāvākya alone directly liberates** — no separate meditative discipline is required. (Source: Wikipedia, *Advaita Vedanta*, "Bhamati and Vivarana"; IEP) [scholarly]
- **Later carriers** — Vidyāraṇya's **Vivaraṇa-prameya-saṃgraha** digests this school (aligning Vidyāraṇya himself with the Vivaraṇa line).

### Bhāmatī (Vācaspati Miśra)

- **Vācaspati Miśra** — [scholarly] c. 9th–10th c. CE (a work of his is dated to *saṃvat* 898, read as either **841 or 976 CE** — itself disputed); the great Maithila polymath who commented authoritatively on **all six darśanas** (the *Sāṃkhya-tattva-kaumudī*, the *Nyāya-vārttika-tātparya-ṭīkā*, the Yoga *Tattva-vaiśāradī*, the Mīmāṃsā *Nyāya-kaṇikā*), earning the title *ṣaḍ-darśanī-vallabha*, "master of the six systems." (Source: Wikipedia, *Vachaspati Mishra*; IEP)
- **Bhāmatī** — his sub-commentary on Śaṅkara's *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya*, which names the school. *Variant [folk]:* tradition says he named it after his wife **Bhāmatī**, who served him unnoticed through the years of composition. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vachaspati Mishra*; *Bhamati*)
- **Positions** — avidyā's **locus is the individual jīva** (Brahman is only its *object*); there are as **many** ignorances as jīvas; the jīva is Brahman **delimited (avaccheda-vāda)** by its internal organ, not a reflection; and knowledge needs **prasaṃkhyāna** — repeated meditative contemplation (*nididhyāsana*) as an auxiliary — before it matures into liberation. (Source: Wikipedia, *Advaita Vedanta*; IEP) [scholarly]
- **Later carriers** — **Amalānanda's** *Vedānta-kalpataru* glosses the Bhāmatī, and **Appayya Dīkṣita's** *Kalpataru-parimala* glosses that in turn (§ below).

### The fault-lines at a glance

| | **Vivaraṇa** (Padmapāda/Prakāśātman) | **Bhāmatī** (Vācaspati) |
|---|---|---|
| Locus (āśraya) of avidyā | Brahman itself | the individual jīva |
| Object (viṣaya) of avidyā | Brahman | Brahman |
| Number of avidyās | one | many (one per jīva) |
| Nature of jīva | reflection (*pratibimba*) | delimitation (*avaccheda*) |
| Means to mokṣa | mahāvākya-knowledge alone suffices | knowledge **+** prasaṃkhyāna/meditation |

(Cross-links: the disputed māyā-vs-avidyā mapping is cataloged in [maya-and-avidya.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/maya-and-avidya.md); the manuals in [vedanta-prakarana-granthas.md](../90-literature-corpus/darshanas/vedanta-prakarana-granthas.md).)

## Vidyāraṇya — Śṛṅgeri, the *Pañcadaśī*, and Vijayanagara

- **Vidyāraṇya** — [scholarly] 14th c. CE, the twelfth **Jagadguru of the Śṛṅgeri Śāradā Pīṭha** (pontificate c. **1380–1386**); the towering figure of medieval Advaita, who consolidated the whole tradition into teaching form. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vidyaranya*; Sringeri Math)
- **The Mādhava identification [disputed]** — tradition equates the pre-sannyāsa Vidyāraṇya with **Mādhava(-ācārya)**, the brahmin minister-preceptor said to have guided **Harihara I and Bukka Rāya** in founding the **Vijayanagara Empire** (1336 CE) as a bulwark of dharma against the Delhi Sultanate — making the philosopher also an empire-builder. Scholars debate whether Mādhava the statesman and Vidyāraṇya the pontiff are one person or two. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vidyaranya*; *Vijayanagara Empire*) [disputed]
- **The learned family** — his brother **Sāyaṇa** produced the standard medieval **bhāṣyas on the Vedas** (the *Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya*), traditionally under Vidyāraṇya's patronage and direction; their father was Māyaṇa. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sayana*; *Vidyaranya*)
- **Works** — the **Pañcadaśī** ("of the fifteen [chapters]"), the great medieval Advaita summa in three pentads (viveka / dīpa / ānanda); the **Jīvanmukti-viveka** (liberation-while-living, argued largely through the *Yoga Vāsiṣṭha*); the **Vivaraṇa-prameya-saṃgraha** (digest of the Vivaraṇa school); and the **Anubhūti-prakāśa**. The **Sarvadarśana-saṃgraha** (a doxography of sixteen systems that ranks Advaita highest) and the **Mādhavīya Śaṅkara-Digvijaya** (the best-known Śaṅkara hagiography) are traditionally ascribed to him/Mādhava — attribution debated. (Source: Wikipedia, *Vidyaranya*, *Sarvadarshanasangraha*; texts profiled in [vedanta-prakarana-granthas.md](../90-literature-corpus/darshanas/vedanta-prakarana-granthas.md) §5)
- *Variant — co-authorship of the Pañcadaśī [disputed]:* a persistent tradition assigns some chapters (usually the later ones) to Vidyāraṇya's Śṛṅgeri predecessor and teacher **Bhāratītīrtha**; the seam is unsettled. (Source: Swahananda, trans., *Pañcadaśī*, intro)

## Madhusūdana Sarasvatī — the *Advaitasiddhi* and the bhakti reconciliation

- **Madhusūdana Sarasvatī** — [scholarly] c. **1540–1640 CE** (flourished late 16th c.); Bengal-born (Kotalipada/Faridpur region) Advaitin of the **Sarasvatī** (Daśanāmī) order who settled at **Kāśī** (Varanasi) and became the most formidable Advaita dialectician of the early-modern age. His guru is usually given as **Viśveśvara Sarasvatī**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhusudana Saraswati*)
- **Advaitasiddhi ("Establishment of Non-duality")** — his masterwork: a point-by-point refutation of the **Nyāyāmṛta** of the Dvaita champion **Vyāsatīrtha** (Vyāsarāja), answering the Mādhava school's logical assault on Advaita. It is the high-water mark of the centuries-long **Advaita–Dvaita polemic**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhusudana Saraswati*; *Advaitasiddhi*)
  - *The four-cornered debate [scholarly]:* Vyāsatīrtha's *Nyāyāmṛta* → Madhusūdana's *Advaitasiddhi* → the Dvaitin **Rāmācārya's** *Nyāyāmṛta-taraṅgiṇī* → the Advaitin **Gauḍa Brahmānanda's** *Guru-candrikā / Laghu-candrikā* — a running textual war between the schools. (Source: Wikipedia, *Advaitasiddhi*; see the Dvaita side at [raghavendra-tirtha-and-the-dvaita-line.md](raghavendra-tirtha-and-the-dvaita-line.md) and [haridasa-and-carnatic-bhakti.md](haridasa-and-carnatic-bhakti.md))
- **The Kṛṣṇa-bhakta Advaitin** — despite his uncompromising non-dualism he was a passionate devotee of **Kṛṣṇa**: he wrote the **Bhakti-rasāyana**, arguing that *bhakti* is itself a supreme value (*puruṣārtha*) and an aesthetic *rasa*, not a mere means; the **Gūḍhārtha-dīpikā**, a devotional Advaita commentary on the **Bhagavad-Gītā**; and the **Siddhānta-bindu** on Śaṅkara's *Daśaślokī*. His famous verse professes that even a knower of the attributeless Absolute cannot give up "the something blue-hued that roams the bank of the Yamunā." (Source: Wikipedia, *Madhusudana Saraswati*; *Bhaktirasayana*)
  - *Variant — the Daśanāmī Nāgas legend [folk]:* tradition holds that, alarmed at attacks on Hindu ascetics, Madhusūdana obtained through the Mughal court (Akbar, via Bīrbal) sanction to admit non-Brahmins into the militant **Nāga** (armed) branches of the Daśanāmī order to defend pilgrims and maṭhas. (Source: traditional Daśanāmī accounts; Wikipedia, *Naga sadhu*) [folk] [disputed]

## Appayya Dīkṣita — the Śaiva polymath of the Advaita house

- **Appayya Dīkṣita** — [scholarly] c. **1520–1593 CE**; a Smārta brahmin of **Adayapalam** (near Kāñcīpuram / Arni, Tamil country), patronized by **Cinna Bomma Nāyaka** of Vellore; a polymath tradition credits with **104 works** across Vedānta, Śaiva theology, poetics, and Mīmāṃsā. (Source: Wikipedia, *Appayya Dikshita*) [traditional — the "104" is a round figure]
  - *Name-collision [disputed]:* there were **at least three** Appayya Dīkṣitas in the family over generations; the famous polymath is **Appayya Dīkṣita I** — later namesakes are sometimes conflated with him. (Source: Wikipedia, *Appayya Dikshita*)
- **The Advaita doxographer** — his **Siddhānta-leśa-saṅgraha** ("Compendium of the Positions of the Doctrine") is the indispensable survey of the *internal disagreements* of later Advaita — it lays out, author by author, exactly where the Bhāmatī and Vivaraṇa schools and their offshoots part ways. His **Kalpataru-parimala** is a sub-sub-commentary in the **Bhāmatī** line (on Amalānanda's *Vedānta-kalpataru*). (Source: Wikipedia, *Appayya Dikshita*; *Siddhantalesasangraha*)
- **The Śaiva theologian** — an ardent Śiva-devotee, he championed **Śrīkaṇṭha's Śiva-Viśiṣṭādvaita (Śiva-Advaita)** with the great commentary **Śivārka-maṇi-dīpikā** on the *Śrīkaṇṭha-bhāṣya*, and composed Śaiva hymns — the **Ātmārpaṇa-stuti** (*Unmatta-pañcāśat*) and the **Śivānanda-laharī-candrikā**. Yet he also argued for **Hari–Hara** parity and impartiality between Śiva and Viṣṇu, and engaged the Mādhva dialectician **Vijayīndra Tīrtha**. (Source: Wikipedia, *Appayya Dikshita*; see [shaiva-schools.md](shaiva-schools.md))
- **The literary critic** — his **Kuvalayānanda** (on *alaṃkāra*, poetic figures) and **Citra-mīmāṃsā** became standard textbooks of Sanskrit poetics for centuries; also the **Vṛttivārttika**. His grand-nephew **Nīlakaṇṭha Dīkṣita** carried the family's learning into the 17th c. (Source: Wikipedia, *Appayya Dikshita*)

## Sadāśiva Brahmendra — the silent avadhūta

- **Sadāśiva Brahmendra** — [scholarly] d. c. **1755 CE**; an **avadhūta** (naked, wandering, God-intoxicated renunciant) and celebrated **jīvanmukta** of the Kāverī delta in the Tamil country, whose *samādhi* is at **Nerur** (near Karur). His guru was **Paramaśivendra Sarasvatī** of the Kāñcī lineage. He observed near-total silence (*mauna*). (Source: Wikipedia, *Sadasiva Brahmendra*)
- **The Advaita works** — the autobiographical Advaita poem **Ātma-vidyā-vilāsa**; a **Brahma-sūtra-vṛtti**; the **Yoga-sudhākara** (a commentary on Patañjali's *Yoga-sūtras*); and the **Siddhānta-kalpavallī** — treatises of a realized non-dualist rather than a scholastic debater. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sadasiva Brahmendra*)
- **The Carnatic composer** — he set pure Advaita and Kṛṣṇa-bhakti into simple, luminous **Sanskrit kṛtis** that entered the Carnatic repertoire and are sung to this day — *Manasa sañcara re*, *Pibare rāma-rasam*, *Khelati mama hṛdaye*, *Bhajare gopālam*, *Sarvaṃ Brahma-mayam*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Sadasiva Brahmendra*; Carnatic tradition)
- *Miracle-hagiography [folk]:* stories cluster around him — that a soldier who struck off his hand as he walked, oblivious, through a harem saw it restored at the saint's touch; that he entered *samādhi* buried in sand and re-emerged elsewhere. Recorded as the lore of a jīvanmukta, not history. (Source: traditional accounts; Wikipedia, *Sadasiva Brahmendra*) [folk] [oral tradition]
