# The Tamil Siddhars (Patiṉeṇ Cittar) — The Southern Siddha Tradition

*Detail node under [`07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/`](README.md) (master index [`../README.md`](../README.md)) — the South-Indian **Tamil Siddhar** (*Cittar*) tradition: an esoteric Śaiva yogī-alchemist-physician lineage parallel to and overlapping the [Nāth/84-Siddha world](nath-sampradaya-and-the-siddhas.md), but a **distinct system** with its own canon, scripture, and medicine.*
*Sits in the historical Kali-Yuga; dating is **[traditional]** (guru-succession / hagiography) vs **[scholarly]** (epigraphy / text-criticism) per the [two-clocks method](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md) — the lineage frames its founders as **deathless adepts still alive across yugas**, while academically the poetic corpus runs c. 6th–18th c. CE. Doctrinal frame: [`shaiva-schools.md`](shaiva-schools.md); the bhakti counterpart-saints in [`the-nalvar-tamil-shaiva-saints.md`](the-nalvar-tamil-shaiva-saints.md); literary corpus in [`../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md`](../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md); the subtle-body map in [`../05-concepts-and-dharma/chakras-and-kundalini.md`](../05-concepts-and-dharma/chakras-and-kundalini.md), the rāja/haṭha context in [`../05-concepts-and-dharma/yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md`](../05-concepts-and-dharma/yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md), the medical comparison in [`../05-concepts-and-dharma/ayurveda-and-the-science-of-life.md`](../05-concepts-and-dharma/ayurveda-and-the-science-of-life.md); doctrinal summary node at [`../05-concepts-and-dharma/the-eighteen-tamil-siddhars.md`](../05-concepts-and-dharma/the-eighteen-tamil-siddhars.md).*

## The tradition — who the Siddhars are

- **Cittar / Siddhar ("the perfected ones")** — a Tamil class of **realized yogī-mystics** who pursued bodily perfection (*kāya-siddhi*) and liberation through yoga, *kuṇḍalinī*, alchemy and medicine, rejecting caste, temple ritual and idolatry in favour of inner experience of Śiva-as-light. (Source: Wikipedia, *Siddhar*; T.N. Ganapathy, *The Philosophy of the Tamil Siddhas*)
- **A distinct system, not merely southern Nāths [scholarly]** — though they **overlap** the [Nāth Sampradāya and the 84 Mahāsiddhas](nath-sampradaya-and-the-siddhas.md) (shared figures, shared alchemy-and-yoga programme), the Tamil Siddha stream has its **own scripture** (the *Tirumantiram*), its own medical science (*Siddha-vaidya*), and its own antinomian Tamil song-canon — a parallel lineage to be cataloged separately, never merged. (Source: Wikipedia, *Siddhar*, *Siddha medicine*)
- **Patiṉeṇ Cittar — "the Eighteen Siddhars" [traditional]** — the canonical lineage is conventionally numbered **eighteen**, but **rosters vary by tradition** (medical, alchemical, yogic and regional lists differ); the number is typological completeness rather than a fixed membership. (Source: Wikipedia, *Siddhar*; Siddha-vaidya tradition)
- **Agastya (Agathiyar) as fountainhead [traditional]** — the Vedic sage **Agastya**, who in Tamil tradition brought the language and the *Tamil grammar* (*Akattiyam*) south of the Vindhyas, is held to be the **ādi-guru of the Siddhars** and patron of Siddha medicine; many *Siddha-vaidya* texts are ascribed to him. (Source: Wikipedia, *Agastya*, *Siddhar*)
- **Murugan & Palani [regional]** — the Siddhar cult is bound to **Murugan (Kārttikeya)** and especially the hill-shrine of **Palani (Paḻani)**, where the *mūrti* is said to be a *navapāṣāṇa* alchemical image cast by **Bhogar**; Murugan is the deity of the Siddha-yogic *jñāna*. (Source: Wikipedia, *Bhogar*, *Palani Murugan Temple*)

## The Eighteen Siddhars — Patiṉeṇ Cittar (rosters vary)

- **The Eighteen** — the most-cited roster, never to be merged across traditions; spellings and members differ by source, and "eighteen" functions as a sacred totality (cf. the eighteen Purāṇas, eighteen days of the war) rather than a fixed membership. (Source: Wikipedia, *Siddhar*; Siddha-vaidya lineage lists)
  - *Common roster [traditional]:* **Tirumūlar**, **Agathiyar (Agastya)**, **Bhogar**, **Koṅkaṇar**, **Karuvūrār**, **Iṭaikkāṭar (Iṭaikkāṭṭu-cittar)**, **Civavākkiyar**, **Pāmbāṭṭi-cittar**, **Korakkar**, **Macchamuni**, **Sundarānandar**, **Rāmadevar (Yākōbu)**, **Kuthambai**, **Dhanvantari**, **Pāmbāṭṭi**, **Puṇṇākkīcar**, **Pulippāṇi**, **Kamala-muni** — with regional substitutions. (Source: Wikipedia, *Siddhar*)
  - *Variant (the "standard" temple list) [regional]:* one widely-circulated eighteen runs **Nandi-tēvar, Agattiyar, Tirumūlar, Korakkar, Koṅkaṇar, Sattaimuni, Vāṉmīkar (Vālmīki), Rāmadevar, Bhogar, Pulippāṇi, Iṭaikkāṭar, Kamala-muni, Macchamuni, Karuvūrār, Sundarānandar, Kuthambai, Pāmbāṭṭi, Dhanvantari**. (Source: Tamil temple lineage lists)
  - *Variant (Patañjali as Siddhar) [disputed]:* some rosters claim the yoga-sūtra **Patañjali** (*Pataññcali-cittar*) among the eighteen, linking Tamil breath-yoga to the rāja-yoga canon (cf. [`../05-concepts-and-dharma/yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md`](../05-concepts-and-dharma/yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md)). (Source: manuscript lists) [disputed]
  - *Variant (overlapping totals) [disputed]:* alongside the eighteen, traditions count **9 Nava-Siddhar**, and lists naming *Pulastya, Vyāsa, Vālmīki, Nandi, Kūrmamuni, Cattanāthar* blur the Siddhars with the purāṇic ṛṣis. (Source: manuscript lists)
  - *Variant (jīva-samādhi shrines) [regional]:* each Siddhar is venerated at a **jīva-samādhi** (living-tomb) shrine — e.g. Bhogar and Agattiyar at **Palani**, Karuvūrār at **Karuvai/Tanjore**, Pāmbāṭṭi at **Marutamalai**, Sundarānandar at **Madurai/Kūṭal** — the pilgrimage anchors of the cult. (Source: temple tradition)
  - *Overlap with the Nāths [scholarly]:* **Korakkar = Gorakṣanāth**, **Macchamuni = Matsyendranāth**; these shared masters mark the single trans-sectarian Siddha milieu detailed in [`nath-sampradaya-and-the-siddhas.md`](nath-sampradaya-and-the-siddhas.md) — though the Tamil Eighteen remain a **separate roster** from the Buddhist/tantric 84 Mahāsiddhas. (Source: Wikipedia, *Siddhar*, *Natha Sampradaya*)
- **Tirumūlar (Tirumūlanāyaṉār)** — the foundational Tamil Siddhar, counted among both the **63 Nāyaṉārs** and the Eighteen Siddhars; legend says the Kailāsa-yogī **Sundaranātha** entered the corpse of a dead cowherd *Mūlan* to tend his cattle and could not return, becoming Tirumūlar and singing the *Tirumantiram* — one verse a year for 3,000 years. [folk] (Source: Periya Purāṇam; Wikipedia, *Tirumular*)
- **Agathiyar (Agastya)** — *ādi-cittar*, "foremost of the Siddhars" and head of the eighteen; ascribed innumerable *Siddha-vaidya*, *varma* (pressure-point) and alchemical works (the *Agattiyar* corpus — *Agattiyar 2000 / 1200 / 12000*, *Agattiyar Paripūraṇam*, *Pūrṇa-sūtram*, much of it pseudonymous accretion across centuries [disputed]), the symbolic author behind much of the corpus; said to dwell **deathless in the Potikai (Pothigai) hills**. [traditional] (Source: Wikipedia, *Agastya*, *Siddhar*; Agattiyar corpus)
- **Bhogar (Pōkar)** — the great **alchemist-Siddhar**, traditionally caster of the **Palani navapāṣāṇa** Murugan image from nine poisons/minerals — so that the image's **abhiṣeka-water is held medicinal** [folk]; reputed author of the **Bhogar 7000** (*Pōkar Ēḻāyiram*); a legend identifies him with a Chinese sage **Bo-Yang** (in folk syncretism even Lao-Tzu), framing a folk China-link. [folk] (Source: Wikipedia, *Bhogar*; Palani temple tradition)
- **Koṅkaṇar (Koṅkaṇa-cittar)** — Siddhar of rasavāda alchemy and medicine, named in the medical-alchemical lineage and credited with works on *muppu* and metallurgy. (Source: Wikipedia, *Siddhar*; Siddha-vaidya tradition)
- **Karuvūrār (Karuvūr-tēvar)** — Siddhar-poet associated with the Chola court and the **Tanjore Bṛhadīśvara temple** consecration; his hymns enter the *Tirumuṟai* canon (Book 9, *Tiruvicaippā*). [regional] (Source: Wikipedia, *Siddhar*; Tirumuṟai tradition)
- **Civavākkiyar (Sivavākkiyar)** — the most **iconoclastic** Siddhar-poet; his *Civavākkiyam* mocks caste, image-worship, Brahminical ritual and scriptural authority, insisting on the formless Śiva within — the sharpest voice of Siddhar antinomian *jñāna*. (Source: *Civavākkiyam*; Wikipedia, *Sivavakkiyar*)
- **Pāmbāṭṭi-cittar ("the snake-charmer Siddhar")** — author of the *Pāmbāṭṭi-cittar pāṭalkaḷ*, whose every verse ends in the refrain **"āḍu, pāmbē!"** ("dance, O snake!") — the *snake* being the **kuṇḍalinī** roused up the spine; a classic of Siddhar coded poetry. (Source: *Pāmbāṭṭi-cittar pāṭalkaḷ*; Wikipedia, *Siddhar*)
- **Sundarānandar** — Siddhar-yogin of the Agastya line; verses on yoga and *kāya-kalpa* are attributed to him; venerated at Madurai. [regional] (Source: Siddhar poetry tradition; manuscript lists)
- **Iṭaikkāṭar, Kuthambai, Pulippāṇi, Rāmadevar** — further Siddhar-poets of the song-canon; **Iṭaikkāṭar** is reputed for prophecy, **Pulippāṇi** as Bhogar's disciple-physician, **Rāmadevar (Yākōbu)** linked in legend to West-Asian travel. [folk] (Source: Wikipedia, *Siddhar*; Siddhar poetry tradition)

## The Siddha system — yoga, alchemy, medicine

- **Siddha-vaidya (Siddha medicine)** — one of India's classical medical systems, **distinct from Āyurveda** (though sharing the *tridoṣa* — *vātam–pittam–kapham* —, *pañca-bhūta* and *sapta-dhātu* frame; cf. [`../05-concepts-and-dharma/ayurveda-and-the-science-of-life.md`](../05-concepts-and-dharma/ayurveda-and-the-science-of-life.md)), native to the Tamil country and ascribed to the Siddhars (above all Agastya, its named founder-physician); strongly **mineral- and metal-based** (*rasa*, *paṣāṇa*, *uppu*; the calcined ash-medicines **centūram / pārpam / cuṇṇam**), with *varma* (vital-point) therapy and diagnosis by **nāḍi (pulse-reading)** plus the *eṇ-vakai* **eight examinations** (tongue, complexion, voice, eye, touch, stool, urine, pulse). (Source: Wikipedia, *Siddha medicine*; Siddha-vaidya corpus)
- **Rasavāda (alchemy, *irasavātam*)** — transmutation of base metals to gold and the fixing of mercury (*pārada/irasam*, the "living metal," wedded to sulphur in a Śiva–Śakti chemistry); pursued as both literal chemistry and as an inner metaphor for transmuting the perishable body into the deathless. (Source: Wikipedia, *Siddhar*, *Siddha medicine*; Bhogar 7000)
  - *Two registers at once [scholarly]:* Siddhar alchemy works **externally** (mercury–sulphur–salt processing of metals and body) *and* **internally** — the verses code "mercury" as *bindu*/semen and the alchemical fire as **kuṇḍalinī**, so one text carries both laboratory and yoga. (Source: Bhogar 7000; Tamil alchemical corpus; D. G. White, *The Alchemical Body*)
- **Muppu — the "universal salt" [disputed]** — the Siddhars' secret **three-salt elixir**, the "philosopher's stone" of Tamil alchemy, said to fix mercury, transmute metals and confer the deathless body; its exact composition is deliberately concealed in the verses. (Source: Siddhar alchemical corpus; Koṅkaṇar texts) [disputed]
- **Kāya-kalpa (*kāyakaṟpam*)** — the Siddhar art of **bodily rejuvenation and longevity** (herbo-mineral *kalpa* preparations, breath-control, "sealing the body's gates"), aimed at the immortal, light-bodied frame (*oḷi uṭampu*) fit for liberation-in-the-body (*jīvanmukti*) — deathlessness across yugas. (Source: Wikipedia, *Siddhar*; Siddha-vaidya tradition; Siddhar kāya-kalpa texts)
- **Yoga & kuṇḍalinī** — the Siddhars practise *aṣṭāṅga yoga*, the core breath-discipline **vāci-yoga** (*vāci* = breath/*vāyu*: reversing the breath, stilling the mind, uniting the vital airs), and the raising of **kuṇḍalinī** through the spinal centres to the *sahasrāra*; the *Tirumantiram* is the earliest Tamil text to expound *aṣṭāṅga yoga*, *cakras*, *nāda* and the yogic body (subtle-body map: [`../05-concepts-and-dharma/chakras-and-kundalini.md`](../05-concepts-and-dharma/chakras-and-kundalini.md)). (Source: *Tirumantiram*; Wikipedia, *Tirumular*)
- **Aṣṭamā-siddhi** — the eight occult attainments (*aṇimā, mahimā, laghimā, garimā, prāpti, prākāmya, īśitva, vaśitva*) are byproducts the Siddhars are said to wield but are warned not to crave. (Source: Siddhar/yoga tradition)
- **Jīva-samādhi — not death [folk]** — a perfected Siddhar does not "die" but enters **conscious living entombment** (*jīva-samādhi*), remaining present at the shrine (Palani, Marutamalai, Madurai and others) — the cult's logic of deathlessness. (Source: temple tradition)
- **Coded "twilight" language [scholarly]** — Siddhar songs deliberately use riddling **sandhyā-bhāṣā**-style imagery (snakes, hens, wells, salt, the "house") to veil tantric and alchemical instruction from the uninitiated — hence the deceptive simplicity of the verse. (Source: T.N. Ganapathy, *The Philosophy of the Tamil Siddhas*; Wikipedia, *Siddhar*)

## The scripture & song-canon

- **Tirumantiram — the Tenth Tirumuṟai** — Tirumūlar's ~3,000-verse Tamil poem is canonized as **Book 10 of the twelve Tirumuṟai**, the **only purely doctrinal-yogic** book in that Śaiva canon; it is the **foundational Tamil scripture of Yoga and Tantra**, expounding Śaiva Siddhānta, *aṣṭāṅga yoga*, *kuṇḍalinī*, mantra and the famous teaching *"aṉbē civam"* ("Love is Śiva"). [scholarly] (Source: *Tirumantiram*; Wikipedia, *Tirumantiram*; cf. [`../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md`](../90-literature-corpus/tamil-canon-sangam-and-bhakti.md))
  - *Dating (two clocks):* tradition assigns Tirumūlar to deep antiquity [traditional]; scholarship places the text c. **6th–10th c. CE** (often 7th–8th c.), with later strata. [scholarly] [disputed] (Source: Wikipedia, *Tirumantiram*)
- **Bhogar 7000 (*Pōkar Ēḻāyiram*)** — the great alchemical-yogic compendium ascribed to **Bhogar** in seven thousand verses, a cornerstone of Siddha alchemy, *kāya-kalpa* and the Palani *navapāṣāṇa* lore. [late text] (Source: *Bhogar 7000*; Wikipedia, *Bhogar*)
- **The Siddhar songs (*Cittar pāṭalkaḷ*)** — the loose corpus of antinomian devotional-philosophical lyrics — **Civavākkiyar's *Civavākkiyam***, the **Pāmbāṭṭi-cittar** songs, Paṭṭiṉattār-adjacent verse — sung in plain Tamil, anti-ritual and anti-caste, a counter-stream to temple bhakti. (Source: Siddhar poetry corpus; Wikipedia, *Siddhar*)
- **Siddha-vaidya corpus** — the technical literature of medicine, *varma*, alchemy and *kalpa* ascribed to Agathiyar, Bhogar, Koṅkaṇar, Pulippāṇi, Dhanvantari and Yūki — the textual backbone of the living Siddha medical tradition of Tamil Nadu. (Source: Siddha-vaidya corpus; Wikipedia, *Siddha medicine*)
