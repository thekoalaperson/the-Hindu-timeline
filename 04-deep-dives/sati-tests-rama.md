# Satī Tests Rāma — the Sītā-Guise and the Estrangement of Śiva and Satī

> Cosmic address: a **hinge episode joining two clocks** — narratively set in the [Tretā Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) (Rāma wandering the [Daṇḍaka forest](../06-sacred-geography/dandakaranya-rama-forest.md) bereft of Sītā), yet belonging to the primordial **Satī cycle** of divine biography that ends at the [Dakṣa-yajña](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md). The tradition itself notices the knot (see §F).
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · See also: [Satī, the Dakṣa-Yajña & Pārvatī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md) · [Śiva Myth-Cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) · [Rāmcaritmānas](ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md)

This is the story the Śaiva tradition tells of **why Śiva and Satī were already estranged before the Dakṣa-yajña**: Satī, watching the supreme Śiva bow to a weeping human prince, **doubts Rāma's divinity**, tests him by **assuming Sītā's own form**, is instantly recognized — and Śiva, learning that his wife has worn the body of the Lord's consort, **mentally renounces her as wife**. Told most fully in the *Śiva Purāṇa* (**Rudra-saṃhitā, Satī-khaṇḍa, chapters 24–26** in the J.L. Shastri / AITM numbering; chapter divisions vary by edition), it was taken up by **Tulsīdās** as the opening frame of the [*Rāmcaritmānas*](ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md) — making Satī's doubt, in effect, the question the entire *Mānas* exists to answer. The episode is **absent** from the [Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa](ramayana/README.md) and from the Bhāgavata Purāṇa's Dakṣa-yajña account; variants are catalogued below, never merged.

---

## A. The setting: Śiva's obeisance to a weeping prince

- **Rāma bereft in the Daṇḍaka forest** — In the Tretā age, Rāvaṇa has carried off Sītā; **Rāma**, with **Lakṣmaṇa**, wanders the [Daṇḍaka forest](../06-sacred-geography/dandakaranya-rama-forest.md) searching for her, lamenting like an ordinary man separated from his beloved — the [Araṇya-kāṇḍa](ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md) situation as seen from outside, through Śaiva eyes. (Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra-saṃhitā, Satī-khaṇḍa 24; Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa)
- **Śiva and Satī come upon Rāma** — **Śiva**, roaming the earth with **Satī**, sees Rāma from afar and, thrilled with devotion, **salutes him** — hailing his victory — *without approaching or revealing himself*, so as not to interrupt the human līlā Rāma is enacting. (Śiva Purāṇa, Satī-khaṇḍa 24)
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa):* Tulsīdās supplies a fuller occasion — Śiva has just visited the sage **Kumbhaja (Agastya)** with Satī to hear the story of Rāma, and on the return journey through the forest catches sight of Rāma; he bows with the words hailing him as *saccidānanda*, "purifier of the world," and passes on without disclosing who he is.
- **Satī's doubt is born** — Satī is bewildered: her husband is the **lord of the universe**, to whom gods and sages bow — why does *he* make obeisance to a kṣatriya prince who weeps and searches for his wife like any deluded mortal? Can the unborn, all-pervading Brahman take a human body and grieve in ignorance? (Śiva Purāṇa, Satī-khaṇḍa 24; Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa)
- **Śiva's explanation fails** — Śiva tells her that this is **Viṣṇu himself, incarnate as Rāma** at the prayer of the gods for the destruction of Rāvaṇa (see [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)), and that the grief is līlā, not delusion. **Satī remains unconvinced.** (Śiva Purāṇa, Satī-khaṇḍa 24; Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa)
- **Śiva permits the test** — Perceiving that words will not remove the doubt, Śiva — smiling, in the Purāṇic telling — tells Satī to **go and test Rāma herself** and awaits her return. (Śiva Purāṇa, Satī-khaṇḍa 24)
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas):* Śiva, who knows her heart though she says nothing, seats himself beneath a **banyan (vaṭa) tree** to wait, inwardly troubled that doubt has entered Satī and foreseeing no good from it.

## B. The test: Satī in Sītā's form

- **Satī assumes the form of Sītā** — Reasoning that if this prince is really the omniscient Lord he will see through any disguise — and that if he is a mortal maddened by loss he will mistake her for his wife — **Satī takes on the exact form of Sītā** and places herself in Rāma's path. (Śiva Purāṇa, Satī-khaṇḍa 24; Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa)
- **Rāma recognizes her at once** — Rāma is not deceived for an instant. He **salutes Satī by name** — greeting her as the daughter of Dakṣa, the beloved of Śambhu — and asks where Śiva is and **why she is wandering the forest alone in this guise**. The doubt collapses; Satī is filled with shame and confusion. (Śiva Purāṇa, Satī-khaṇḍa 24; Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa)
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas):* Tulsīdās adds the courteous detail that Rāma smiles, **joins his palms, announces his own name together with his father's** (Daśaratha), and then asks after "Vṛṣaketu" (bull-bannered Śiva) — divine omniscience wearing perfect human manners.
- **How did he know?** — Satī asks how he recognized her; in the Śaiva telling Rāma's knowledge is bound up with his **devotion to Śiva** — the Lord's grace and the mutual love of Hari and Hara make concealment between them impossible, and Rāma speaks to Satī of Śiva's greatness. (Śiva Purāṇa, Satī-khaṇḍa 24–25)
- **The vision of multiplied Rāmas** — As the abashed Satī withdraws, she is granted a staggering vision: **Rāma, Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa appear before her, behind her, on every side** — countless Rāmas with countless attendant gods, Brahmās, Viṣṇus and Śivas doing homage — the one Lord reflected through all creation. Terrified and awed, she closes her eyes; when she looks again the vision is gone. (Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa) [this cosmic-vision expansion is Tulsīdās's; the Śiva Purāṇa account ends the test with the recognition and Rāma's words]

## C. The return and the lie

- **Śiva asks how she tested Rāma** — Satī returns to where Śiva waits; he asks her, knowing already in his heart what has happened, how she made her trial of Rāma. (Śiva Purāṇa, Satī-khaṇḍa 25; Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa)
- **Satī conceals the truth** — In Tulsīdās's telling Satī, unable to confess, **lies**: "I made no test — I bowed to him just as you did." **Śiva sees the whole event in meditation** — the Sītā-guise, the recognition, the shame. (Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa)
  - *Variant (Śiva Purāṇa, Satī-khaṇḍa 25):* the Purāṇic Satī returns dejected and the emphasis falls not on a lie but on Śiva's **coming to know** that she had worn Sītā's form; the dramatized falsehood and its detection by yogic insight are the *Mānas*'s sharpening of the scene. [The exact wording of Satī's reply differs across editions and retellings; the estrangement, not the lie, is the invariant core.]

## D. Śiva's vow: the mental renunciation of Satī

- **The reasoning of the vow** — Śiva reflects: Satī has **assumed the body of Sītā**, the consort of his chosen Lord. Sītā stands to Śiva as the divine Mother stands to the devotee; a woman who has worn that form **can no longer be embraced as a wife** without destroying the path of devotion and transgressing dharma. (Śiva Purāṇa, Satī-khaṇḍa 25; Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa)
- **The renunciation is mental, not spoken** — Śiva **abandons Satī as wife in his heart**, taking a firm inner vow to have no conjugal relation with her in that body, while outwardly continuing to speak with her kindly as before. He does not repudiate, curse or abandon her physically — the estrangement is entirely interior, which is precisely its tragedy. (Śiva Purāṇa, Satī-khaṇḍa 25; Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa)
- **The celestial voice** — As the vow is taken, a **voice from the sky (ākāśavāṇī)** resounds, praising Śiva: *"Well done, Maheśa — who but you, firm of vows, could keep such a resolve?"* **Satī hears the proclamation**, understands what has happened, and is pierced with grief — yet can say nothing. (Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa; the heavenly acclamation of the vow is likewise reported in retellings of the Śiva Purāṇa account)
- **The long silence on Kailāsa** — They return to [Kailāsa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md). In the *Mānas*, Śiva seats himself beneath a banyan and passes into a **prolonged, unbroken samādhi**; when at last he emerges, the sorrowing Satī comes and sits before him, and Śiva, perceiving her grief, tells her sacred tales — a marriage continued as discourse, with its heart withdrawn. (Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa; cf. Śiva Purāṇa, Satī-khaṇḍa 25–26, where Śiva likewise keeps aloof while conversing as before)

## E. From estrangement to the Dakṣa-yajña

- **The stated cause of what follows** — Both texts present this estrangement as the **immediate backdrop of Satī's death**: an already-wounded Satī, no longer held by Śiva as wife, is the Satī who insists on going uninvited to her father's sacrifice and who, insulted there, gives up the body. The full Dakṣa-yajña sequence — the snub, the self-immolation, Vīrabhadra, the [Śakti Pīṭhas](../06-sacred-geography/51-shakti-peethas.md) — is treated in [Satī, the Dakṣa-Yajña & Pārvatī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md). (Śiva Purāṇa, Satī-khaṇḍa; Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa)
- **Dakṣa's own grievance runs in parallel** — Immediately after the vow-chapters, the Satī-khaṇḍa (ch. 26) turns to the **second cause**: at a great sacrificial assembly all rise to honor the arriving **Dakṣa** except Śiva, and the humiliated Prajāpati **curses Śiva to be excluded from sacrificial shares** — the direct motive for the yajña snub. The Purāṇa thus deliberately layers **two estrangements** — husband from wife, father-in-law from son-in-law — before the catastrophe. (Śiva Purāṇa, Satī-khaṇḍa 26)
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa IV.2–4):* the great Vaiṣṇava account of the Dakṣa-yajña **contains no Rāma-test at all**; Dakṣa's assembly-insult and curse are the sole cause, and Śiva and Satī are unestranged to the end. The Sītā-guise episode belongs to the **Śaiva and Rāma-bhakti streams**, not to the Bhāgavata. [Traditions kept separate by design.]
- **Satī's dying wish and rebirth** — In the *Mānas*, Satī, casting off the body in yogic fire, prays that **in birth after birth she may love Śiva's feet** — answered in her rebirth as **Pārvatī**, whose renewed marriage to Śiva leads to the birth of Kārttikeya and the death of [Tārakāsura](tarakasura-vadha.md). (Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa; Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra-saṃhitā, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa)

## F. The chronological knot — two clocks, and the tradition's own answer

- **The apparent impossibility** — Satī's life and death belong to **primordial divine biography** (Dakṣa the Prajāpati, the deva-age before Pārvatī), which the Purāṇic frame associates with the earliest manvantara-era of this creation; yet Rāma's forest exile sits in the **Tretā Yuga of the current 28th Mahāyuga** of the Vaivasvata manvantara. Taken flatly, Satī would have to be alive long after the yajña at which she died — or Rāma incarnate ages before his time. The corpus keeps these two datings distinct rather than forcing them together.
- **The traditional harmonization: līlā recurs every cycle** — The Rāma-bhakti tradition answers through **cyclic recurrence**: Rāma's deeds are enacted **in every Tretā Yuga of every cycle**, so a Satī of an earlier kalpa can meet a Rāma of that kalpa's Tretā. Tulsīdās builds this into the *Mānas* itself through the immortal crow **Kākabhuśuṇḍi**, who reports having witnessed Rāma's childhood līlā again and again across countless ages — "Hari is infinite, and infinite are Hari's stories," told differently in different cycles. (Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl- & Uttara-kāṇḍa framing)
- **The text-historical reading** [scholarly] — Academically, the episode is a **medieval Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava bridge-device**: the extant Śiva Purāṇa is a late compilation (oldest surviving strata placed c. **10th–11th century CE**, much material later — see the [Śiva Purāṇa profile](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/shiva-purana.md)), and the scene writes Rāma-devotion *into* Śiva's own biography, subordinating neither god to the other. Tulsīdās (composing **1574 CE**, Kali-Yuga documented history) inherited the motif from this Śaiva-Purāṇic stream — his principal Vedāntic source, the [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md), shares the Śiva-narrates-to-Pārvatī frame but not the Satī-test itself. The chronological "impossibility" is thus, historically, the seam where two devotional corpora were stitched. [scholarly]

## G. The Tulsīdās frame: why the Mānas begins here

- **Satī's doubt is the engine of the whole poem** — The [*Rāmcaritmānas*](ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md) is narrated, in its outermost frame, by **Śiva to Pārvatī** — and Tulsīdās motivates that narration by *this* episode: Pārvatī, remembering the doubt of her former birth as Satī, asks Śiva to resolve it, and Śiva's answer is the Rāma-story entire. The estrangement narrative is therefore not an episode *in* the *Mānas* so much as the **question to which the Mānas is the reply**. (Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa; cf. the [three frame-dialogues](ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md))
- **Hari–Hara harmony as doctrine** — The episode's theology cuts both ways and is meant to: **Śiva bows to Rāma** (Rāma is supreme Brahman), while **Rāma reveres Śiva** and is knowable through Śiva's grace (in the *Mānas*, Rāma elsewhere declares Śiva's devotee dearer to him than his own). For [Tulsīdās](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/tulsidas.md) the scene is the charter of **Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava non-rivalry** in the North-Indian bhakti synthesis. (Rāmcaritmānas; [scholarly] reading of its sectarian program)
- **The severity of the vow as bhakti-ideal** — Within the bhakti frame, Śiva's renunciation of his own beloved wife — because her borrowed form touched the dignity of the Lord's consort — is presented as the extreme standard of a devotee's integrity: even Satī may not be both the Mother's form and Śiva's bride. The tradition treats the vow as praiseworthy (the ākāśavāṇī) *and* as the seed of tragedy — both readings stand in the texts side by side. (Śiva Purāṇa, Satī-khaṇḍa 25; Rāmcaritmānas, Bāl-kāṇḍa)

Shastri / AITM translation numbering; editions vary) — the principal account: Śiva's obeisance to Rāma in the forest, Satī's doubt, the test in Sītā's form, Rāma's recognition, Śiva's mental vow renouncing Satī as wife (chs. 24–25), and the pivot to Dakṣa's curse and enmity (ch. 26).

- *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* IV.2–4 — cited **for contrast**: the Vaiṣṇava Dakṣa-yajña narrative, which contains no Rāma-test and no prior estrangement.
- *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa* — cited for the shared Śiva-to-Pārvatī narrative frame (without the Satī-test episode).
