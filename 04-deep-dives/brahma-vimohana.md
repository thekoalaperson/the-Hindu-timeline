# Brahmā-vimohana and the Brahma-stuti — Kṛṣṇa Multiplies Himself into All Vraja

> *Where this sits: the **Brahmā-vimohana-līlā** ("the bewildering of Brahmā") of [Kṛṣṇa](./krishna-lila.md) in Vṛndāvana — Bhāgavata Purāṇa **10.13–14** — set in the closing [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga. The touchstone episode of Vaiṣṇava metaphysics on the simultaneous omnipresence of Bhagavān, crowned by the celebrated **Brahma-stuti**.*
>

The **Brahmā-vimohana-līlā** ("the deluding of Brahmā") is the episode in which the creator-god **Brahmā**, having just witnessed Kṛṣṇa's killing of the demon **Aghāsura**, tests the boy-cowherd's divinity by stealing away the entire company of cowherd boys (*gopas*) and their calves and hiding them in a mountain cave; Kṛṣṇa thereupon **expands himself into every missing boy and every missing calf**, so perfectly that for a full year no one in Vraja — not the mothers, not the cows, not even Balarāma at first — perceives any loss; when Brahmā returns and sees all the boys revealed as four-armed Viṣṇu-forms worshipped by every being, he collapses in **vimohana** (utter bewilderment) and offers the great hymn of surrender, the **Brahma-stuti** of 10.14. It is told most fully in the **[Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md) 10.13–14**; the older **[Harivaṃśa](./harivamsha.md)** (Viṣṇu Parva) and **Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Book 5** carry the Vraja boyhood but lack this developed multiplication-and-stuti episode, which is the Bhāgavata's own theological masterpiece. In the [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md) biography it is a single bullet; this file expands it. It follows directly on the [infancy and Vṛndāvana demon-slayings](./krishna-balya-lila.md) and anticipates the same self-multiplication that crowns the [Rāsa-līlā](./rasa-lila.md).

"Two clocks" reminder - *In-narrative* the episode falls in Kṛṣṇa's fifth year, in the closing [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) (traditionally before 3102 BCE). *Text-critically* it is a mature first-millennium-CE Bhāgavata composition; the metaphysical hymn of 10.14, with its precise *advaita*-answering theology of *bhakti* over *jñāna*, reflects the Purāṇa's developed Vaiṣṇava-Vedānta and has no equivalent in the older Harivaṃśa. Keep scriptural time distinct from textual dating. [scholarly]

## The setting — the picnic lunch (Bhāgavata 10.13)

- **The forest meal (Bhāgavata 10.13)** — On the morning after the slaying of **Aghāsura** (the python-demon, brother of Pūtanā and Bakāsura), Kṛṣṇa, Balarāma and the cowherd boys take their lunch-packets into the forest, seat themselves in a flower-like circle around Kṛṣṇa with their calves grazing nearby, and eat together — the much-loved scene of the *bāla-līlā* picnic. The boys' calves stray off, and while Kṛṣṇa goes alone to fetch them, Brahmā makes his move. (Bhāgavata 10.13.8–15)
- **Brahmā's curiosity (Bhāgavata 10.13)** — Astonished by the killing of Aghāsura and the liberation of the demon's soul into Kṛṣṇa's body, **Brahmā** wishes to see more of the cowherd boy's power; the text frames his act as the bewilderment of one who would test the very source of his own creative power. (Bhāgavata 10.13.15) [the narrator marks Brahmā's test as itself a delusion]

## The theft and the year of substitution (Bhāgavata 10.13)

- **The abduction of the calves (Bhāgavata 10.13)** — Brahmā secretly carries off the **calves** and hides them away. Kṛṣṇa, searching the forest and riverbank and not finding them, returns to discover the **boys** also gone — for Brahmā has now hidden the cowherd boys too, stowing both companies in a cave (*nidrayā*, in a state of yogic sleep) on the slopes of a mountain. (Bhāgavata 10.13.16–18)
- **Kṛṣṇa's expansion — ātma-vibhūti (Bhāgavata 10.13)** — Knowing at once that this is Brahmā's doing, Kṛṣṇa, to console the mothers and cows of Vraja *and* to confound the creator, **multiplies himself** (*ātmānaṃ ... bahudhā*), becoming **every cowherd boy and every calf** by his own self-expansion — manifesting each as it had been, in the same number. (Bhāgavata 10.13.19–20)
- **Perfect identity (Bhāgavata 10.13)** — The substitute boys and calves are identical in **bodily form, features, hands, feet, staffs, horns, flutes, dress, ornaments, in voice and habit, in temperament and play** — exact down to the smallest detail; Kṛṣṇa becomes them in their every individuality, *himself the soul of each, sustaining all by himself.* The text's doctrinal point: he who is the *ātman* of all things can be all things without ceasing to be himself. (Bhāgavata 10.13.19–22) [the locus classicus of *ananta-rūpa*, his unlimited simultaneous forms]
- **The deepened affection (Bhāgavata 10.13)** — Re-entering Vraja each evening as all the boys and all the calves at once, Kṛṣṇa is suckled and fondled by the mothers and the cows — and over the following days the **mothers' love for "their" sons and the cows' love for "their" calves grows even more intense than before**, since the object of their *vātsalya* is now secretly the Lord himself. Even the cows, who had never shown such fondness, run to lick and nurse the substitute calves. (Bhāgavata 10.13.23–35)
- **Balarāma's wonder (Bhāgavata 10.13)** — The increase of affection puzzles **Balarāma**; investigating, he perceives by his own divine insight that all the boys and calves are none other than **Kṛṣṇa's self-manifestations** (*viṣṇu-māyā*), and asks Kṛṣṇa, who reveals the truth to him. This is the one perception that pierces the līlā from within. (Bhāgavata 10.13.37–41) [in some readings Balarāma alone shares the secret]
- **The full year (Bhāgavata 10.13)** — Kṛṣṇa sustains the substitution for **one complete year** (lacking five or six nights by the reckoning of some commentators) — Brahmā's "moment" being, on the creator's own time-scale, a fraction of an instant, yet a full earthly year in Vraja. The "two clocks" of divine and human time are built into the narrative itself. (Bhāgavata 10.13.40) [scholarly]

## Brahmā's return and the great bewilderment (Bhāgavata 10.13)

- **Brahmā returns (Bhāgavata 10.13)** — After what is to him a mere moment, Brahmā comes back expecting to find Vraja desolate — and instead sees Kṛṣṇa playing exactly as before with all the boys and calves, while the originals still lie asleep in his cave. He is stupefied: *how can both companies exist at once?* (Bhāgavata 10.13.42–48)
- **The theophany of the four-armed forms — vimohana (Bhāgavata 10.13)** — To dispel Brahmā's confusion Kṛṣṇa reveals the boys and calves as they truly are: each appears as a **four-armed Viṣṇu-form (Nārāyaṇa)**, dark-blue, clad in yellow silk, bearing **conch, discus, mace and lotus**, crowned and garlanded, served by the powers of creation — **Time, Nature (prakṛti), the Vedas, the cosmic functions** and all beings personified worship them; each is encircled by *aṇimā* and the other mystic perfections and adored by the very deities Brahmā commands. Brahmā, the would-be tester, is plunged into total **vimohana** — his senses arrested, struck dumb "like a deity-image before a deity." (Bhāgavata 10.13.45–57) [the episode that names the whole līlā: Brahmā the deluder is himself deluded]

## The Brahma-stuti — Brahmā's hymn of surrender (Bhāgavata 10.14)

The whole of chapter **10.14** is Brahmā's prayer once Kṛṣṇa withdraws the vision and stands again as the simple cowherd boy. The **Brahma-stuti** ("Brahmā's praise") is among the most quoted passages in all Vaiṣṇava theology — the creator's confession of the supremacy of devotion over speculative knowledge.

- **The opening reverence (10.14.1)** — Brahmā dismounts his swan, prostrates, and beholds Kṛṣṇa as the supreme truth he could not grasp; the hymn begins with awe at the Lord appearing as a cowherd child. (Bhāgavata 10.14.1)
- **Bhakti over jñāna — *jñāne prayāsam udapāsya* (10.14.3)** — The most-cited verse: *"Abandoning the labour of knowledge (jñāne prayāsam udapāsya), one who lives submissively (san-mukharitāṃ bhavadīya-vārtām), receiving the talk of you from the saints and offering body, word and mind — he conquers you, the otherwise-unconquerable (ajita), in his very heart."* Devotion accomplishes what *jñāna* cannot: the Lord, *ajita*, is "conquered" only by surrendered love. (Bhāgavata 10.14.3) [the doctrinal heart of the stuti]
- **The futility of mere knowledge (10.14.4)** — Those who reject the path of self-surrender and toil only at speculative knowledge **labour in vain**, reaping only trouble, like one who threshes empty husks (*na ... śramam evopalabhante*). (Bhāgavata 10.14.4)
- **The inconceivability of Bhagavān (10.14.21)** — Brahmā confesses that even he, the creator, cannot fathom the Lord's *acintya* (inconceivable) glory and *māyā*: *"How can I, born of your lotus, comprehend your power, when you display such opulence by your own potency?"* The Lord's self-multiplication just witnessed is the standing proof that he is beyond the measure of any created intellect. (Bhāgavata 10.14.21)
- **The greatness of the cowherds (10.14.31–34)** — Brahmā extols the unique fortune of **Nanda, Yaśodā and the people of Vraja**, whose intimate love wins the Supreme that even yogis and Vedas cannot reach; he prays to be born — even as a blade of grass or a creeper — in Vṛndāvana, that the dust of these devotees' feet might fall on him. (Bhāgavata 10.14.30–34) [the charter of Vraja-bhakti's supremacy]
- **The confession and departure (10.14.32–43, 10.14.58–60)** — Brahmā begs forgiveness for his prideful test, salutes the *acintya-tattva* of Bhagavān, circumambulates Kṛṣṇa, and returns to his abode. Kṛṣṇa then brings back the original boys and calves from the cave; to them, in *māyā*, no time at all has passed, and the day's play resumes seamlessly. The cowherd boys run home telling of "today's" slaying of Aghāsura — though a year has elapsed. (Bhāgavata 10.14.58–60)

## Why this episode is a touchstone of Vaiṣṇava metaphysics

- **Simultaneous omnipresence demonstrated, not merely asserted** — Where the Upaniṣads *declare* the Self to be all, the Brahmā-vimohana **dramatises** it: the same Kṛṣṇa is one boy and a thousand boys, the indweller (*antaryāmin*) of each yet undivided in himself. Commentators (Śrīdhara Svāmī, Jīva Gosvāmī, Viśvanātha Cakravartī, Vallabha) read the chapter as the Purāṇa's proof-text for *ananta-rūpa* and the *acintya-bhedābheda* relation of the Lord to his manifestations. [scholarly]
- **The same logic crowns the Rāsa** — The self-multiplication here (one Kṛṣṇa as every boy) is the structural twin of the [Rāsa-līlā](./rasa-lila.md) (one Kṛṣṇa between every pair of gopīs): in both, *yoga-māyā* lets the indivisible Lord be wholly present to each devotee at once. The two passages are read together as the Bhāgavata's twin statements of loving omnipresence. [scholarly]
- **Bhakti subordinates jñāna at the mouth of the creator himself** — That it is **Brahmā** — patron of the Vedas and of cosmic knowledge — who declares *jñāne prayāsam udapāsya* gives the *bhakti*-over-*jñāna* teaching its maximal authority; the verse is a cornerstone citation for the Gauḍīya, Vallabha and broader bhakti schools. [scholarly]
