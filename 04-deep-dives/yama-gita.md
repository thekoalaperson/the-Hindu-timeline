# Yama Gītā (Viṣṇu Purāṇa version) — Yama's charge to spare Viṣṇu's devotees

> Cosmic address: a short soteriological *gītā* embedded in **[Viṣṇu Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/vishnu-purana.md) Aṃśa III, adhyāya 7** — the god of death **[Yama](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/yama.md)** privately instructs his own noose-bearing *dūta* to steer clear of any mortal who has taken refuge in **Viṣṇu / Madhusūdana**, confessing "I am lord only of the rest." Reached through the Kali-yuga dialogue of **Parāśara and Maitreya**, quoting **Bhīṣma's** answer to **Nakula** at the close of the Mahābhārata. Its twin recensions stand in the **[Agni Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/agni-purana.md)** (ch. 381) and the **Nṛsiṃha (Narasiṃha) Purāṇa** (ch. 8).
> Parent: [Deep Dives](README.md) · Up to [the Hindu Timeline](../README.md)

The **Yama-Gītā** ("the Song of Yama") is not one text but a small family of Purāṇic passages in which the **lord of death and dharma** himself teaches the way past his own jurisdiction. The best-known member — and the one that gave the whole cluster its Vaiṣṇava fame — is **[Viṣṇu Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/vishnu-purana.md) III.7**, a scene of striking economy: Yama, watching his fierce servant set out with the soul-noose (*pāśa*) in hand, leans to his ear and warns him to *avoid* the devotees of Hari, for over them he has no power. It is the mirror-image and near-relative of the [Ajāmila](ajamila.md) episode of the Bhāgavata, where Yama likewise orders the **Yamadūtas** never to approach those who chant the Name. Where Ajāmila dramatizes the doctrine in narrative, the Yama-Gītā states it as **Yama's own standing order**, and so became a favourite proof-text of Vaiṣṇava liturgy that the *bhakta* is *yama-viṣaya-atīta* — beyond the reach of death's officer.

---

## Where it sits — the two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic frame** — The *outer* dialogue (Parāśara instructing his pupil Maitreya, the frame of the whole Viṣṇu Purāṇa) sits at the **dawn of the present [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)**. The passage it quotes — **Nakula's question to the dying [Bhīṣma](../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md)** on the bed of arrows — belongs to the **end of Dvāpara / the Mahābhārata war** (traditionally just before 3102 BCE). Yama's own confidential words to his messenger are *frameless and timeless*: a permanent policy of the death-realm, not a dated event.
- **Academic / historical frame** — [scholarly] The *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* is generally dated **c. 400–500 CE** (H. H. Wilson produced the first English translation in **1840**); the *Nṛsiṃha Purāṇa*, an Upapurāṇa cited already by Rāmānuja's milieu, **c. 5th–10th century CE**; the *Agni Purāṇa*, an encyclopaedic late compilation, **c. 800–1100 CE**. The extraction of these chapters as free-standing recitable *Yama-Gītās* (like the sixty-odd minor Gītās catalogued at [Lesser Gītās](lesser-gitas-catalog.md)) is a **later liturgical move**, part of the pan-sectarian bhakti settlement that also produced the Rāma-, Śiva-, and Devī-Gītās.
- **Genre note** — A "*gītā*" here means a versified doctrinal set-piece put in a divine or authoritative mouth; the Yama-Gītā's distinctive twist is that the *teacher of liberation is Death himself*, echoing the archetype of **Yama teaching Naciketas** in the *Kaṭha Upaniṣad* — a lineage the Agni recension makes explicit.

## I. The frame — how the Song reaches us (Viṣṇu Purāṇa III.7)

- **Maitreya's question** — In the Viṣṇu Purāṇa's third book, the pupil **Maitreya** asks the sage **Parāśara** (Vyāsa's father, the Purāṇa's narrator): by what means may a man become **exempt from the authority of Yama** and escape the tortures of the *narakas*? (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* III.7)
- **The chain of transmission** — Parāśara answers that this same question was once put by **Nakula** — youngest of the sons of Pāṇḍu — to his grandsire **Bhīṣma**, and that Bhīṣma repeated to him a secret **dialogue between Yama and one of his own servants (a *dūta*/minister)**. The teaching thus descends *Yama → his messenger → (overheard, via Bhīṣma) → Nakula → Parāśara → Maitreya* — a deliberately layered pedigree that lends the death-god's private admission maximal authority. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* III.7)
  - *Note:* several manuscript traditions insert a further inner link — that Bhīṣma had it from a learned Brāhmaṇa who received it from an omniscient sage — but the load-bearing frame is the Yama-to-messenger confidence overheard and handed down. [scholarly]

## II. Yama's charge to his messenger — "the Vaiṣṇavas excepted"

- **The whisper in the ear** — The heart of the chapter: seeing his own attendant depart **noose in hand** (*pāśa-hasta*) to fetch the dead, **Yama draws him close and warns him in his ear** to *keep clear of one class of men altogether* — those devoted to Viṣṇu. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* III.7)
- **The famous couplet** — In H. H. Wilson's 1840 rendering Yama says: *"**Keep clear of the worshippers of Madhusūdana. I am the lord of all men, the Vaiṣṇavas excepted.** I was appointed by Brahmā, who is reverenced by all the immortals, to restrain mankind and regulate the consequences of good and evil in the universe. But he who obeys Hari as his spiritual guide is here independent of me; for Viṣṇu is of power to govern and control me."* The half-line **"*prabhur aham anya-nṛṇāṃ na vaiṣṇavānām*"** ("I am lord of other men, not of the Vaiṣṇavas") is the phrase most quoted from the whole Purāṇa in later liturgy. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* III.7; the couplet is cited across Vaiṣṇava anthologies)
- **Yama's own subordination** — The theological point Yama concedes is precise: he governs *by Brahmā's appointment*, over the general run of souls; but the surrendered devotee has taken a **higher spiritual sovereign (Hari)**, and Viṣṇu is *of power to govern and control Yama himself*. Death's officer therefore has no writ over the Lord's own. This exactly parallels **Yama's confession in the [Ajāmila](ajamila.md) coda** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 6.3), where he counts himself among the twelve *mahājanas* and orders his agents to shun the chanters of the Name. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* III.7; cf. *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 6.3.26–29)

## III. The marks of the man the noose cannot bind

- **Who counts as "a Vaiṣṇava"** — Yama does not leave the exempt class vague; his instruction lists the **signs of the true devotee** his servant must recognize and avoid: one whose mind is fixed unwaveringly on Kṛṣṇa/Vāsudeva, who is **even-minded toward all creatures**, harms none, is truthful, pure of heart, free of greed and pride, and constant in **remembrance and worship of Hari**. Such conduct, not sectarian marks alone, is what places a soul beyond the *pāśa*. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* III.7)
- **Devotion over ritual atonement** — Like the Ajāmila teaching, the passage ranks **bhakti / refuge in Viṣṇu above *karma-kāṇḍa* and prāyaścitta** as the means of escaping Yama — tying it to the broader corpus discourse on [pāpa, puṇya and prāyaścitta](../05-concepts-and-dharma/papa-punya-and-prayaschitta.md) and the karmic ledger Yama and [Citragupta](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/narakas-and-afterlife.md) keep. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* III.7)
- **The consolation** — Its practical fruit is the assurance a *bhakta* need not fear the [Narakas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/narakas-and-afterlife.md): the very administrator of hell has ordered his staff to pass him by. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* III.7)

## IV. The Nṛsiṃha Purāṇa recension (chapter 8)

- **A Man-Lion inflection** — The **Nṛsiṃha (Narasiṃha) Purāṇa** carries a closely parallel Yama-Gītā in its **eighth chapter**, narrated in the Purāṇa's Vyāsa-frame. Here Yama again instructs his *dūtas* to **avoid the devotees of Viṣṇu — and specifically the worshippers of [Nṛsiṃha / Narasiṃha](prahlada-narasimha.md)** — retelling the same exemption in the register of the Man-Lion avatāra the Purāṇa is named for. (*Nṛsiṃha Purāṇa* ch. 8)
- **Hell relieved by the Name** — This recension adds a vivid motif absent from the terse Viṣṇu-Purāṇa scene: **souls already in the *narakas*, on hearing or uttering the names of Hari, are eased of their torments** — the punitive realm itself yielding before the Name. (*Nṛsiṃha Purāṇa* ch. 8) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (devotee identified):* some summaries name the ascetic whom Yama's servants **fail to seize** as a great *tapasvin* meditating on Viṣṇu; the identification is not uniform across manuscripts and should be held loosely. [disputed]
- **Relation to the VP** — The Viṣṇu- and Nṛsiṃha-Purāṇa versions form the **"Vaiṣṇava-exemption" branch** of the Yama-Gītā tradition: short, narrative, and doctrinally identical (Yama has no power over Hari's own), differing mainly in the avatāra emphasized. (comparative note) [scholarly]

## V. The Agni Purāṇa recension (chapter 381) — the yogic Yama-Gītā

- **A different kind of Song** — The **Agni Purāṇa**'s Yama-Gītā, in its **381st chapter** (part of the Purāṇa's closing *yoga–mokṣa* section, alongside the *Gītā-sāra* digest noted in the [Agni Purāṇa map](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/agni-purana.md)), is **not** the "avoid the Vaiṣṇavas" scene at all but a compact **philosophical–yogic discourse on liberation** placed in Yama's mouth. (*Agni Purāṇa* ch. 381)
- **Yama and Naciketas** — This recension frames the teaching as **Yama's instruction to Naciketas**, consciously drawing on the *Kaṭha Upaniṣad* archetype of Death teaching the boy the secret of the deathless. It treats **desire and renunciation, the paths of knowledge (*jñāna*) and yoga, and the discrimination of the imperishable Self**. (*Agni Purāṇa* ch. 381) [scholarly]
- **The chariot of the body** — It deploys the classic **[Kaṭha](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md) chariot-metaphor** — the body as chariot, the senses as horses, the mind as reins, the intellect (*buddhi*) as charioteer, the Self as the rider — as the model of the disciplined ascent to self-realization and release. (*Agni Purāṇa* ch. 381)
- **Its branch** — The Agni recension thus forms the **"jñāna–yoga" branch** of the Yama-Gītā tradition, Advaitic and Upaniṣadic in temper rather than devotional; it shares the *speaker* (Yama, master of the passage from death) but not the *doctrine* of the Viṣṇu-Purāṇa scene. The two branches are **cataloged, not merged.** [scholarly]

## VI. Comparison, reception, and liturgical use

- **Three Yama-Gītās, two branches** — Tradition and the *gītā*-anthologies (e.g. the Sanskrit-documents Yama-Gītā collation and Kāñchī's *Pradhāna-Gītā-Sañcaya*) count **three Yama-Gītās** — *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* III.7, *Nṛsiṃha Purāṇa* ch. 8, and *Agni Purāṇa* ch. 381 — falling into a **Vaiṣṇava-refuge pair** (VP + Nṛsiṃha) and a **standalone yogic member** (Agni). (comparative; [Lesser Gītās catalog](lesser-gitas-catalog.md)) [scholarly]
- **Liturgical afterlife** — The Viṣṇu-Purāṇa couplet became a **stock citation in Vaiṣṇava sampradāyas** — Śrī-Vaiṣṇava, Gauḍīya, and others — invoked with the Ajāmila episode to establish that surrender to Viṣṇu lifts the soul out of Yama's ledger. As a short recitable unit the Yama-Gītā entered *stotra* and *nitya-pāṭha* collections. [scholarly]
- **The point of the paradox** — The device's power lies in *who* delivers the exemption: not a partisan of Viṣṇu but the **impartial magistrate of karma**, who administers reward and punishment for the entire cosmos and yet publicly concedes one category of soul lies outside his authority. The confession of the judge is worth more than the plea of the advocate. This is the same theological gesture as **[Śiva singing the Rudra-Gītā to Viṣṇu](pracetas-rudra-gita.md)** and **Yama's testimony in the [Ajāmila](ajamila.md) coda** — a "rival" or superior authority voluntarily exalting Hari. [scholarly]
- **Name-collisions to keep distinct** — Do **not** conflate: (a) the *Vaiṣṇava-exemption* Yama-Gītā (VP III.7 / Nṛsiṃha 8) with (b) the *yogic* Yama-Gītā (Agni 381); nor either with (c) the underlying **Yama–Naciketas dialogue of the *Kaṭha Upaniṣad***, which is a source, not itself a "Yama-Gītā"; nor with (d) a **Yama-Gītā** sometimes cited within the *Śiva Purāṇa* tradition, which recasts the yogic material. [disputed]
