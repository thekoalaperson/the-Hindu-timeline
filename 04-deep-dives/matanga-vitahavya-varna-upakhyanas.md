# Mātaṅga and Vītahavya — the two Anuśāsana varṇa-change upākhyānas

> *Cosmic address: a matched **pair of upākhyānas** from the **[Anuśāsana Parva](mahabharata/13-anushasana-parva.md)** of the **Mahābhārata**, narrated by the dying **Bhīṣma** on his bed of arrows to **Yudhiṣṭhira** at the close of the **[Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)**, about events set in the deep ṛṣi-past. Two adjacent chapters, deliberately opposed on the hardest question of varṇa-dharma — can a man change his caste? **Mātaṅga**, Caṇḍāla-born, is told by **Indra** that Brāhmaṇahood cannot be won in a single life by any austerity; **Vītahavya**, a Kṣatriya Haihaya king, is turned into a Brāhmaṇa in an instant by **Bhṛgu's** single true word.*
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Master index: [README](../README.md)

Two **short didactic tales (upākhyānas)** that the *Mahābhārata* places side by side in the **Anuśāsana Parva** precisely so their morals collide. Bhīṣma tells them to Yudhiṣṭhira as instruction on the **immutability — or mutability — of varṇa**, and the redactor's hand is visible: the **Mātaṅga** story (a Caṇḍāla who fails, through the fiercest tapas, to become a Brāhmaṇa) is followed almost immediately by the **Vītahavya** story (a Kṣatriya who becomes a Brāhmaṇa effortlessly, by a sage's fiat). Read alone, each teaches; read together, they stage the tension inside orthodox varṇa-theory between **birth (janma)** and **grace/word (vāc)** as the ground of Brāhmaṇahood. This node is their canonical home; the **Vītahavya war** that frames the second tale is treated in full on the [Kāśī-line node](../03-lineages-vamsha/kashi-line-divodasa.md), and both names surface only as bare gotra-entries in [Bhṛgu](bhrigu.md) and the [ṛṣi-lineage roster](../08-beings-and-bestiary/rishi-lineages-and-gotras.md).

---

## The pairing — why these two chapters sit together

- **A deliberate diptych on varṇa-change** — Bhīṣma answers Yudhiṣṭhira's questions on caste, duty, and the power of Brāhmaṇas with two case-studies that reach **opposite verdicts**: Brāhmaṇahood is *unattainable by effort* (Mātaṅga) and *conferred by a word* (Vītahavya). The corpus rule applies — **the two are not to be harmonized**; the epic itself preserves the disagreement, and the value is in the tension. [scholarly]
- **Chapter numbering across recensions** — in the **Ganguli/Calcutta line** the Mātaṅga tale runs **Anuśāsana Parva §§ XXVIII–XXX** and the Vītahavya tale **§§ XXX–XXXI** (Ganguli's own section-count for this stretch spans roughly **XXVII–XXXI**); the **critical-edition/vulgate** chapter numbers are commonly given as **13.27–30 (Mātaṅga)** and **13.31 (Vītahavya)**. The [Kāśī-line node](../03-lineages-vamsha/kashi-line-divodasa.md) cites the Vītahavya material as **Anuśāsana Parva 30**; the small offset is a recension artefact, not a different story. [disputed: chapter numbers vary by edition]
- **Both hinge on Indra and on a Bhārgava** — Mātaṅga does tapas *to Indra*, who refuses him; Vītahavya's antagonist **Divodāsa fortifies Vārāṇasī *at Indra's command*** yet still loses, and the tale resolves in the hermitage of **Bhṛgu**. The junctions are structural, not incidental. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva)
- **Two-clock note** — the **narration frame** is fixed in the epic's own chronology (Bhīṣma's instruction after the [Kurukṣetra war](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md), the **Dvāpara–Kali junction**); the **events narrated** float in the **deep antiquity** of the current Mahāyuga (the Kāśī / Haihaya wars of the Lunar line, and Mātaṅga's nameless age). The [Anuśāsana Parva](mahabharata/13-anushasana-parva.md) as a text is, on the **academic clock**, one of the **latest strata** of the epic — a didactic accretion — **[scholarly] c. 200 BCE–400 CE**, well after the war-narrative core. [late text]

## Name-collisions to keep straight

- **This Mātaṅga is *not* the Rāmāyaṇa sage** — the Caṇḍāla-born ascetic of the Anuśāsana Parva must not be confused with **Ṛṣi Mātaṅga of Ṛṣyamūka** (Kiṣkindhā), whose curse on **Vālī** makes the hill a refuge for **Sugrīva** and whose hermitage the Hampi tradition localizes at **Mataṅga hill** — a wholly different figure (see [Sugrīva & Vālī](characters/sugriva-and-vali.md) §§ III). [scholarly]
- **Not Mātaṅgī the Mahāvidyā** — the outcaste **Goddess Mātaṅgī** (the "Tantric Sarasvatī," worshipped through impurity/*ucchiṣṭa*) shares the Caṇḍāla-associated *root* but is a distinct **Śākta** node ([Daśa Mahāvidyā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md) § 9); a boon in the Mātaṅga tale (that he be worshipped by women) is sometimes read as an etiological hook, but the corpus keeps them separate. [scholarly]
- **Not *mātaṅga* the common noun** — Sanskrit *mātaṅga* also simply means "elephant," and appears as such in celestial-animal and dikpāla contexts (the elephant-vāhana / diggaja register — see [celestial animals](../08-beings-and-bestiary/airavata-and-celestial-animals.md), [Dikpālas](../08-beings-and-bestiary/dikpalas-and-lokapalas.md)); those are not this person. **This is the flagged false-positive set** — corpus searches for "Mātaṅga" surface Mātaṅgī / dikpāla-elephant / Hampi hits, none of them the Anuśāsana ascetic.
- **This Vītahavya is the Haihaya king** — not to be diluted into the abstract **"Vītahavya" gotra-name** that appears in the Bhārgava gaṇa-lists ([rishi-lineages](../08-beings-and-bestiary/rishi-lineages-and-gotras.md); [Bhṛgu](bhrigu.md)); those bare entries *are* the downstream trace of this very tale — the gotra exists **because** the king was made a Brāhmaṇa. The tale is the origin the lists presuppose.

## Mātaṅga — Brāhmaṇahood refused (Anuśāsana Parva 27–30)

- **The whipped colt and the she-ass's revelation** — Mātaṅga, raised as a Brāhmaṇa's son, is driving a chariot yoked with a young ass to a sacrifice and **strikes the colt** to hurry it. The mother **she-ass consoles her calf**: do not grieve at this cruelty, for the driver is no true Brāhmaṇa but a **Caṇḍāla** — begotten on a Brāhmaṇa woman by a man of the barber (*nāpita*) / Caṇḍāla caste — and so "knows no better." (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva ~28) [the trope: an animal exposes hidden birth]
- **The truth confirmed** — shaken, Mātaṅga returns and presses his mother, who **confirms the adultery**: his blood is mixed, his supposed Brāhmaṇa lineage a fiction. His whole identity collapses on the disclosure. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva ~28)
- **The vow to win Brāhmaṇahood by tapas** — refusing his fate, Mātaṅga resolves to **become a Brāhmaṇa by austerity** and undertakes **penances of terrifying severity** to propitiate **Indra** — standing on one leg, reducing himself to skin and sinew over immense spans of time. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva ~28–29)
- **Indra's repeated refusal** — Indra appears and tells him plainly: **Brāhmaṇahood cannot be attained in this life** by one Caṇḍāla-born, however fierce the tapas. Mātaṅga renews his penance again and again; each time Indra returns and **refuses again**, offering other boons instead. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva ~29)
- **The doctrine of gradual ascent** — Indra's teaching is the tale's core: varṇa can rise only **across many births**, by stages — a Caṇḍāla might over hundreds of lifetimes climb Śūdra → Vaiśya → Kṣatriya → Brāhmaṇa — but **never in one leap in a single life**. Brāhmaṇahood is the **highest and hardest** station, guarded precisely against the shortcut Mātaṅga attempts. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva ~29) [the birth-supremacist reading of varṇa]
- **The collapse, and the boon he is given instead** — when Mātaṅga, near death after standing a hundred years on his toe, **falls**, Indra catches him and, still refusing Brāhmaṇahood, grants a consolation boon: the power to **move at will and assume any form**, and to be **honoured and worshipped by women** as a deity-like being (a "Chāṇḍāla"/Mātaṅga cult-figure). Mātaṅga accepts, and **dies without the one thing he sought**. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva ~30)
- **The moral** — Bhīṣma draws it explicitly: Brāhmaṇahood is **supremely difficult**, unattainable by austerity alone by the low-born; birth-station is a near-fixed ladder climbed only over lifetimes. It is a **conservative charter** for the immutability of varṇa. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva ~30) [disputed within tradition — see the countervailing tale below]

## Vītahavya — Brāhmaṇahood conferred (Anuśāsana Parva 31)

- **The Haihaya war on Kāśī** — **Vītahavya**, king of the **[Haihayas](../03-lineages-vamsha/ekavira-ekavali-haihaya-origin.md)** (the war-clan of the [Kārtavīrya cycle](jamadagni-parashurama.md)), and his **hundred sons** grind down three generations of the **[Kāśī](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md) kings**: **Haryaśva** is slain, his son **Sudeva** defeated, and Sudeva's son **[Divodāsa](../03-lineages-vamsha/kashi-line-divodasa.md)** — though he **fortifies Vārāṇasī at Indra's command** — is routed, his army destroyed, and flees alone. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva ~30) 
- **Bharadvāja's rite and the born-avenger** — the refugee Divodāsa reaches his *purohita* **[Bharadvāja](bharadvaja.md)**, who performs a **putrakāmeṣṭi** (son-desiring sacrifice). The son born of it, **Pratardana**, matures with supernatural speed, leads Kāśī's army back, and **exterminates the hundred sons of Vītahavya to the last man**. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva ~30)
- **Sanctuary with Bhṛgu** — the defeated **Vītahavya flees to the hermitage of [Bhṛgu](bhrigu.md)** and takes refuge. Pratardana pursues and **demands the king be surrendered** for slaughter. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva ~31)
- **Bhṛgu's true word that makes itself true** — Bhṛgu answers that **"there are no Kṣatriyas here — all in this hermitage are Brāhmaṇas."** Because a great ṛṣi's word cannot be false, the utterance **transmutes Vītahavya into a Brāhmaṇa on the instant**; Pratardana, unable to strike a Brāhmaṇa, **turns home**. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva ~31) [the *satya-vāc* / power-of-the-word mechanism]
- **The Bhārgava line seeded** — Vītahavya, now a **Brahmarṣi**, begets **Gṛtsamada** (so like Indra in form that the Daityas once seized him mistaking him for the god). From Gṛtsamada — remembered as the seer of **Ṛgveda Maṇḍala II** — descends the line to **Śunaka** and his son **Śaunaka**, in whose family, tradition says, members of **all four varṇas** arose. Thus a Kṣatriya's grandson founds a **Brāhmaṇa gotra**, the very [Vītahavya/Gṛtsamada Bhārgava gaṇa](../08-beings-and-bestiary/rishi-lineages-and-gotras.md) of the pravara-lists. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva ~31) 
  - *Variant (gotra-tradition):* Gṛtsamada is elsewhere styled an **Āṅgirasa adopted into the Bhṛgu line**, not a birth-Bhārgava — a competing account of how the gotra fused ([rishi-lineages](../08-beings-and-bestiary/rishi-lineages-and-gotras.md) § 4). [disputed]
- **The moral** — the tale glorifies the **potency of the Brāhmaṇa's word** and, obliquely, the **mutability of varṇa by grace**: what Mātaṅga could not seize in a lifetime of tapas, Vītahavya receives in a sentence, because a Bhṛgu willed it. (*Mahābhārata*, Anuśāsana Parva ~31)

## The opposed morals — reading the diptych

- **Effort vs. word (janma vs. vāc)** — Mātaṅga's fate says varṇa is **fixed by birth** and immovable within a life; Vītahavya's says it is **movable by the fiat of a sage**. The epic sets them **adjacent and unreconciled** — an editorial decision that lets the reader hold both the conservative and the fluid doctrines of caste in view at once. [scholarly]
- **Who holds the power** — in both, the low- or mixed-status seeker is **passive to a superior**: Mātaṅga is *refused* by Indra; Vītahavya is *elevated* by Bhṛgu. The difference is not the aspirant's merit but the **decision of the higher party** — one god withholds, one ṛṣi grants. Varṇa-change, in both, is **not earned but bestowed** (or denied). [scholarly]
- **The Brāhmaṇa-supremacist frame** — read together the pair actually **reinforces Brāhmaṇa primacy** from both ends: Brāhmaṇahood is so high the Caṇḍāla cannot climb to it, *and* so much the ṛṣi's to give that a king receives it only at a Brāhmaṇa's word. The tension is real but the ideology is consistent — **the Brāhmaṇa is the gatekeeper of the highest status either way.** [scholarly]
- **Parallels in the corpus** — the varṇa-crossing by ṛṣi-fiat mirrors the **caru-swap in the Kānyakubja/Kuśika line** (Gādhi–Viśvāmitra: a Kṣatriya destined to Brāhmaṇahood) noted at the [Kāśī-line node](../03-lineages-vamsha/kashi-line-divodasa.md); the "benefactor's verdict on caste" register also runs through the Śānti/Anuśāsana didactic tales (cf. [minor Śānti-Parva upākhyānas](shanti-parva-minor-upakhyanas.md), [Gautama's curse on the ungrateful brāhmaṇas](gautama-curse-on-the-brahmins.md)). [scholarly]

## Sources

- **Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana Parva — the Mātaṅga upākhyāna (vulgate 13.27–30; Ganguli §§ XXVIII–XXX)** — Bhīṣma tells Yudhiṣṭhira of the Caṇḍāla-born Mātaṅga: the whipped ass-colt and the she-ass's disclosure of his mixed birth; the mother's confirmation; the vow to win Brāhmaṇahood by tapas to Indra; **Indra's repeated refusal**; the doctrine that varṇa rises only across many births, never in one life; the collapse after a century on one toe; the consolation boon (shape-shifting; worship by women); death without Brāhmaṇahood.
- **Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana Parva — the Vītahavya upākhyāna (vulgate 13.31; the war-frame at 13.30; Ganguli §§ XXX–XXXI)** — the Haihaya king **Vītahavya** and his hundred sons crush the Kāśī kings Haryaśva, Sudeva and Divodāsa (who fortifies Vārāṇasī at Indra's command); **Bharadvāja's putrakāmeṣṭi** and the born-avenger **Pratardana**, who exterminates Vītahavya's sons; Vītahavya's flight to **Bhṛgu**, and Bhṛgu's true word ("there are only Brāhmaṇas here") that **makes him a Brāhmaṇa instantly**; the descent through **Gṛtsamada → Śunaka → Śaunaka** and the Bhārgava gotra.
