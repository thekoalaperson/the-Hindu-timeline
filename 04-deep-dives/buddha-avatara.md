# Buddha as the Avatar of Vishnu

> Cosmic address: the **Buddha-avatāra** (avatāra-vāda — the *theological* descent, to be held distinct from the historical Gautama), the **ninth** descent in the dominant Kṛṣṇa-as-eighth [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) lists, placed in our present **Kali-yuga**. Primary scripture: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.3.24 and the **Mayāmoha** narrative of *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 3.17–18.
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · See also: [Prahlāda & Narasiṃha](prahlada-narasimha.md) · [Vāmana & Bali](vamana-bali.md) · [Ṛṣabha, Bharata & Jaḍa Bharata](rishabha-bharata-jada.md).

This file treats the **doctrine** of the Buddha-avatāra — *why* Viṣṇu is said to have descended as the Buddha and what that descent is held to accomplish — as distinct from where Buddha sits in the avatar-count (which the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) file catalogs). The remarkable fact is that the canon preserves **two flatly opposed readings** of the same avatāra: an early **anti-Vedic "deluder" (mohana)** doctrine in which Viṣṇu's purpose is to lead the asuras *away* from dharma to their ruin; and a later **compassion / ahiṃsā** reinterpretation in which the same descent is an act of mercy that ends Vedic animal sacrifice. The figure is the dominant religion's theological **incorporation of the Buddha** — a real, c. 8th-century-CE response to Buddhism — not a biography of Siddhārtha Gautama.

---

## I. The avatāra-vāda frame — Buddha as a planned descent

- **The terse Bhāgavata announcement** — Among the avatāra-summaries of Canto 1, Viṣṇu "will become Buddha, the son of **Añjana**, in the **Kīkaṭa** country (Magadha), at the beginning of Kali-yuga, in order to delude those who are envious of the faithful (the *dvija* / theists)." (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.3.24)
  - *Variant (birth-name & mother):* the *Bhāgavata* gives the mother as **Añjana** and the land as **Kīkaṭa**, NOT the historical Śuddhodana / Māyā / Lumbinī of Buddhist tradition — a deliberate sign that the Purāṇa describes a *theological* Buddha, not the recorded Gautama. [scholarly]
- **Placement on the two clocks** — On the **scriptural clock** the descent is fixed at the *opening of Kali-yuga* of the present 28th Mahāyuga; on the **historical clock** the doctrine itself crystallizes only c. 8th c. CE (see §IV). The avatāra-vāda thus back-dates a historical theological move into mythic time. [scholarly]
- **The "innumerable descents" license** — Because the *Bhāgavata* insists the avatāras are *asaṅkhyeya* ("innumerable," 1.3.26), the lists can absorb a new figure like Buddha without rupture; he enters as a *prādhānika* (principal) selection, classed by later theology as an **āveśa** (empowerment) rather than a *pūrṇa* (full) descent. [theological systematization]

## II. Reading A — the Mayāmoha "deluder of the asuras" (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.17–18)

The oldest sustained narrative motive for the descent is **destructive, not compassionate**: Viṣṇu deludes the demons off the Vedic path precisely to make them killable.

- **The demons made invincible by dharma** — The *daityas*, led by an asura host, defeat the *devas* because they have become rigorous in **Vedic observance and austerity (tapas)**; so long as they keep the Veda, the gods cannot overcome them. The gods appeal to Viṣṇu. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 3.17)
- **Birth of Mayāmoha** — From his body Viṣṇu emits **Māyāmoha** ("Delusion-by-Illusion"), a naked or red-robed mendicant *deluder*, and sends him to the asuras on the banks of the Narmadā. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 3.17–18)
- **The seduction away from the Vedas** — Māyāmoha preaches a *non-Vedic* doctrine — variously rendered as the **ārhata (Jain) and bauddha (Buddhist)** heterodoxies, mocking animal sacrifice, denying Vedic authority, and teaching naked asceticism or nihilism — until the asuras abandon the Veda, lose their protective merit, and are destroyed by the devas. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 3.18)
  - *Variant (which heresy):* the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* has Māyāmoha teach a **composite of Jain and Buddhist tenets** (the "*nagna*" naked-ascetic and *bauddha* lines), making the deluder a screen for *all* nāstika (Veda-rejecting) systems, not Buddhism alone. [late text] [disputed — the explicit "Buddha" naming is firmer in the *Bhāgavata* than in the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*'s Māyāmoha episode]
- **Theological sting** — In this reading the avatāra is *polemical*: the Buddha-form exists so that **wrong belief becomes a weapon**, drawing the unworthy to their doom. It is the Purāṇic establishment's way of explaining away a rival religion's success — those who follow it were *meant* to be deluded. [scholarly]
- **Parallel logic to other descents** — Like [Narasiṃha](prahlada-narasimha.md) threading a boon's loopholes and [Vāmana](vamana-bali.md) conquering by *yācñā* (begging) rather than force, the Buddha-deluder wins by **stratagem, not battle** — the demons defeat themselves once their dharma is removed.

## III. Reading B — the compassionate ender of sacrifice (Gītā Govinda)

By the high medieval period the polemic inverts: the very same descent is recast as Viṣṇu's **mercy**.

- **Jayadeva's Daśāvatāra-stotra** — In the **Daśāvatāra hymn** opening the *Gīta Govinda* (12th c.), Jayadeva praises Keśava-as-Buddha: "**nindasi yajña-vidher ahaha śruti-jātam / sadaya-hṛdaya darśita-paśu-ghātam**" — "*Out of the compassion of your heart you censure the whole corpus of Vedic ritual that ordains the slaughter of beasts.*" (Jayadeva, *Gīta Govinda*, Daśāvatāra-stotra, verse on Buddha) [devotional reinterpretation]
- **Ahiṃsā as the descent's purpose** — Here the avatāra's mission is to **stop Vedic animal sacrifice (paśu-ghāta)** out of *dayā* (compassion) for all living beings — a near-total reversal of the Mayāmoha reading, where censuring sacrifice was a *trick* to ruin the asuras. [regional/devotional]
- **A negotiated reconciliation** — The compassion reading let a Veda-centred tradition both **honour** the Buddha's evident moral force and **absorb** him: the avatāra concedes ahiṃsā's appeal while keeping Viṣṇu (not the Buddha) supreme. [scholarly]
- **Why two readings coexist** — The house holds *both*: the same Purāṇic stratum that calls him a deluder of the wicked is later read devotionally as the merciful one. Never collapse them — the texts disagree on *intent* even where they agree on *identity*. [disputed]

## IV. The historical incorporation (the historical clock)

- **Not in the earliest avatar lists** — Buddha is **absent** from the oldest fixed enumerations; the Daśāvatāra crystallizes with figures like Matsya–Kūrma–Varāha first, Buddha entering relatively late. [scholarly]
- **Datable inclusion c. 6th–8th c. CE** — Buddha appears as a Viṣṇu-avatāra across several Purāṇas (*Viṣṇu*, *Bhāgavata*, *Agni* 49, *Garuḍa* 1.86, *Padma*, *Varāha*) by roughly the **8th century CE** — the period when Brahmanical traditions were absorbing and answering Buddhism's prestige. [historical text-dating]
- **A theological annexation** — Mainstream scholarship reads the avatāra as a **strategy of incorporation**: rather than oppose Buddhism head-on, the dominant tradition *subordinated* the Buddha by making him a controlled manifestation of Viṣṇu — neutralizing a rival by enfolding it. [scholarly]
- **Epigraphic / list evidence** — Some early Daśāvatāra schemes (e.g. the 7th-c. Ādivarāha inscription at Mahābalipuram) carry Buddha while *omitting* Kṛṣṇa, showing the slot was contested and the canon still fluid when Buddha was admitted. [historical art-dating] See the list-table in [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Buddhist non-acceptance** — Buddhist tradition itself never accepts this identification; the avatāra-vāda is a **Vaiṣṇava claim about the Buddha**, asymmetrically rejected by Buddhists. [scholarly]

## V. List-placement (cross-reference, not duplicated here)

- **Ninth in the Kṛṣṇa-as-8th lists** — In *Agni Purāṇa* 49, *Garuḍa Purāṇa* 1.86, and allied enumerations, Buddha is the **9th** of ten, between Kṛṣṇa (8th) and Kalki (10th). (*Agni Purāṇa* 49; *Garuḍa Purāṇa* 1.86.10–11)
- **Twenty-first / twenty-third in the long lists** — In the 22-avatar *Bhāgavata* schema (1.3) Buddha is the **21st**, and the **23rd** in the extended 24-avatar list — a Kali-yuga *līlā*-avatāra either way.
  - *Variant (who fills the 9th slot):* Smārta / Śrī-Vaiṣṇava and some lists put **Balarāma** at 9 and drop Buddha; the full side-by-side comparison of traditions lives in [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md). [regional/sectarian]
- **Distinct from this file** — For *which* tradition counts Buddha where, see [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md); this file is concerned only with the *doctrine and narrative* of the descent.

## Sources

**Primary scripture**
- *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* **1.3.24** — the planned descent as "Buddha, son of Añjana, in Kīkaṭa (Magadha), at the start of Kali-yuga, to delude the enemies of the faithful"; frame at 1.3.26 (*avatārā hy asaṅkhyeyāḥ*).
- *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* **3.17–18** — the **Mayāmoha (Māyāmoha)** narrative: Viṣṇu emits the deluder to seduce the Veda-strong asuras into nāstika (Jain/Buddhist) heterodoxy so the devas may destroy them — the anti-Vedic "deluder" reading.
- *Agni Purāṇa* **49** and *Garuḍa Purāṇa* **1.86** — Buddha enumerated as the ninth of the ten avatāras (Kṛṣṇa-as-8th ordering).
- *Padma Purāṇa* and *Varāha Purāṇa* — further Purāṇic attestations of Buddha among Viṣṇu's descents (8th-c.-CE-horizon incorporation).
- Jayadeva, *Gīta Govinda* — **Daśāvatāra-stotra** (12th c.): "*nindasi yajña-vidher… sadaya-hṛdaya darśita-paśu-ghātam*" — the compassion / ahiṃsā reinterpretation, Buddha as ender of Vedic animal sacrifice. [devotional reinterpretation]
