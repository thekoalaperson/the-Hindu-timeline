# Veṇu-Gītā — The Song of Kṛṣṇa's Flute

> *Where this sits: the lyric overture to the gopī song-cycle — at the opening of autumn the cowherd girls of Vraja rapturously describe **Kṛṣṇa playing his flute (veṇu)** as he drives the cattle home through the Vṛndāvana woods. It occupies **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.21**, in the [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga ([Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)), just before the rāsa cycle.*
>
> *Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Master index: [../README.md](../README.md) · Companions: [Rāsa-līlā](./rasa-lila.md) · [Bhramara-gītā](./bhramara-gita-uddhava-sandesha.md) · [Kṛṣṇa-bālya-līlā](./krishna-balya-lila.md) · [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md)*

The **Veṇu-gītā** ("Song of the Flute") is **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.21** — nineteen verses in which the gopīs of Vṛndāvana, watching [Kṛṣṇa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) lead the cows home at dusk with his flute at his lips, pour out a rapturous lyric describing how the flute-song enchants the entire forest. It is the **lyric overture to the rāsa cycle**: sung *by* the gopīs *about* Kṛṣṇa, it kindles the longing that the [rāsa-līlā](./rasa-lila.md) (10.29–33) will consummate. Stationed between the autumn-season opening (10.20) and the garment-theft (*vastra-haraṇa*, 10.22), it is the first of the three great gopī song-insets — Veṇu-gītā (10.21), Gopī-gītā (10.31), [Bhramara-gītā](./bhramara-gita-uddhava-sandesha.md) (10.47) — and stands in parallel stature to them, the repo's own [Bhāgavata catalog](./bhagavata-tales-catalog.md) flagging it a candidate for its own file. This is that file.

## Setting — Bhāgavata 10.20–21 (the autumn frame)

- **Onset of śarad (autumn)** — The rains have ceased; the **autumn (śarad) season** clears the skies, settles the muddy roads and ripens the lotuses and lakes of Vṛndāvana. The Purāṇa devotes 10.20 to this season-description (*ṛtu-varṇana*), setting the stage of clear water and quiet groves. (Bhāgavata 10.20)
- **Kṛṣṇa enters the forest** — In this purified autumn world Kṛṣṇa, with [Balarāma](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) and the cowherd boys (*gopas*), drives the cows into the woods of Vṛndāvana, the trees flowering and fruiting, peacocks calling, the hill (Govardhana) and the Yamunā near at hand. (Bhāgavata 10.21.1–4)
- **The flute sounds** — Kṛṣṇa, garlanded and crowned with a peacock-feather, sets his **veṇu (bamboo flute)** to his lips and fills the forest with music; the gopīs in the village hear it and, struck with love, begin to praise it to one another. The whole chapter is framed as **their speech** — the maidens describing the scene they cannot join. (Bhāgavata 10.21.5–7)

## The Song proper — Bhāgavata 10.21 (what the flute enchants)

- **The gopīs' overture** — The cowherd girls, hidden in their homes, describe Kṛṣṇa's beauty and the sound of the flute to each other, their minds drawn out of their bodies after him. The entire song is *prema* kindled at a distance — desire in its first stirring, the seed of the rāsa. (Bhāgavata 10.21.5)
- **The deer enchanted** — The **does and deer** of the forest, hearing the flute, approach Kṛṣṇa with their mates and gaze on him with offerings of love-laden eyes, motionless — the gopīs envy the animals that may draw near where they cannot. (Bhāgavata 10.21.11)
- **The birds as sages** — The **birds (especially the cuckoos and peacocks)** perched in the flowering trees are likened to **sages (munis)**: eyes shut, all other sounds forgotten, absorbed in the flute as in meditation. (Bhāgavata 10.21.12, 14)
- **The rivers stilled** — The **rivers**, their currents arrested by love, halt their flow and embrace Kṛṣṇa's feet with arms of waves, offering lotuses — the very waters stop to listen. (Bhāgavata 10.21.15)
- **The clouds as canopy** — A **cloud**, out of love and to shield Kṛṣṇa from the sun, spreads itself as a friendly umbrella over his head and showers tiny flowers, behaving like an affectionate companion. (Bhāgavata 10.21.16)
- **The peacocks dance** — The **peacocks** break into ecstatic dance at the flute's note, mistaking it (in the gopīs' conceit) for thunder, the whole forest answering Kṛṣṇa's music with motion. (Bhāgavata 10.21.10–12)
- **The trees and creepers thrill** — Even the **trees, creepers and plants** of Vṛndāvana respond: bowing under fruit and flower, dripping honey-sap, their bodies *bristling* (horripilation, *romāñca*) as a devotee's at the touch of the divine — inert nature shown thrilling with bhakti. (Bhāgavata 10.21.18–19)
- **The hill (Govardhana) blessed** — The gopīs call **Govardhana hill** the most fortunate, for it offers Kṛṣṇa water, soft grass, caves and bulbs, and bears the touch of his and Balarāma's feet. (Bhāgavata 10.21.18)
- **The cows and calves transfixed** — The **cows and calves**, ears cupped like vessels to catch the nectar of the flute, stand with mouthfuls of half-chewed grass, tears in their eyes, frozen mid-graze in love. (Bhāgavata 10.21.13)

## The flute itself — the gopīs' envy of the veṇu

- **Envy of the veṇu** — The emotional climax of the song is the gopīs' **jealousy of the flute** pressed to Kṛṣṇa's lips. They marvel that a mere **hollow bamboo** should drink the nectar (*amṛta*) of Kṛṣṇa's lips — a juice that is by right the gopīs' own — leaving for them, his kinswomen, only tears. (Bhāgavata 10.21.9)
- **"What austerities did the flute perform?"** — They wonder aloud what penance (*tapas*) the bamboo must have done in a former life to earn so intimate a touch; the reed's rivers (its ancestral bamboo-line) are called blessed for having borne it. This *veṇu-asūyā* (envy of the flute) is the most-quoted conceit of the chapter and a fixture of later Vaiṣṇava lyric. (Bhāgavata 10.21.9) [scholarly]
- **The flute as supreme devotee** — Commentators read the flute as the ideal *bhakta*: empty of self (hollow), pliant, surrendered, it lets Kṛṣṇa's breath become music — so that the gopīs' envy is also the soul's longing to be so emptied and so used. [scholarly]

## Place in the gopī song-cycle and the līlā

- **The lyric overture to the rāsa** — The Veṇu-gītā opens the **mādhurya** movement of the Bhāgavata's tenth canto: desire is first *kindled* here (10.21), tested in *separation within the dance* in the Gopī-gītā (10.31), and made *permanent in absence* in the [Bhramara-gītā](./bhramara-gita-uddhava-sandesha.md) (10.47). All three are gathered and contrasted in [Rāsa-līlā](./rasa-lila.md). [scholarly]
- **Placement before the rāsa (two episodes intervene)** — The Veṇu-gītā stands **before** the rāsa proper, separated from it by the **garment-theft** (*vastra-haraṇa* / *cīra-haraṇa*, 10.22) and the **wives of the brāhmaṇas** episode (*yajña-patnī-mokṣaṇa*, 10.23). For that surrounding sequence see [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md). [scholarly]
- **Continuity with the boyhood** — The flute-herding scene caps the **Vṛndāvana boyhood** of demon-slayings and cow-pasturing treated in [Kṛṣṇa-bālya-līlā](./krishna-balya-lila.md): the same cowherd who killed Pūtanā and Dhenuka now stills the forest with song, the *vīra* (heroic) mood giving way to the *mādhurya* (sweet) mood. [scholarly]
- **Parallel in stature to the other gītās** — Like the Gopī- and Bhramara-gītās it is a *prema*-lyric, not a *jñāna*-discourse — distinct in kind from the philosophical "Gītās" of the [Lesser Gītās Catalog](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md), and named alongside its sisters as a song *of* love rather than a teaching *about* it. [scholarly]

## Variants & the wider tradition

- *Variant (the singer):* the Bhāgavata frames the song as **the gopīs collectively** speaking to one another; it names no individual singer. The later Gauḍīya and Braj traditions foreground **Rādhā** (unnamed in the Purāṇa) as the leading voice. The Purāṇa itself does not name Rādhā. [late text] [disputed]
- *Variant (Braj vernacular):* the flute-song generated a rich vernacular afterlife — **Sūrdās** and the *aṣṭachāp* poets of the *Sūrsāgar* expand the *veṇu*-envy and the forest's rapture into independent Braj-bhāṣā lyrics. [regional] [late text]
- *Variant (the flute's names):* devotional tradition multiplies titles for the instrument — *veṇu*, *muralī*, *vaṃśī* — and theologises the *muralīdhara* (flute-bearer) form of Kṛṣṇa around this chapter; iconography of Kṛṣṇa with flute draws directly on the Veṇu-gītā. [regional]
- *Note (two clocks):* by the scripture-internal clock the flute-song falls in the **Dvāpara Yuga** of Kṛṣṇa's Vṛndāvana youth (traditionally well before the 3102 BCE Kali cusp); by the academic clock it belongs to the [Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md)'s composition, dated c. **9th–10th c. CE**, South Indian provenance. [scholarly]
