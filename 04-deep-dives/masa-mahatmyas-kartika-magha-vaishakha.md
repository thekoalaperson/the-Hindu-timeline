# Māsa-māhātmyas — the Glories of Kārttika, Māgha, Vaiśākha, Mārgaśīrṣa & the Puruṣottama Month

> Cosmic address: a **cross-yuga, recurring observance** — whole lunar months consecrated as vratas, their kathās set loosely "in former ages" and their practice recurring every year of **Kali-yuga**; the māhātmya texts themselves are late purāṇic strata carried chiefly by the *Padma* and *Skanda Purāṇas*. [late text]
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Companions: [Ekādaśī-māhātmyas](ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md) · [festivals & vratas](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md) · [Padma Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/padma-purana.md) · [Skanda Purāṇa khaṇḍa-map](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/skanda-purana-khanda-map.md) · [Hindu calendar & pañcāṅga](../00-time-and-cosmology/hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md)

Where the [Ekādaśī cycle](ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md) sanctifies a *day* each fortnight, the **māsa-māhātmyas** sanctify **entire months**: Kārttika (Oct–Nov) for lamps and Tulasī, Māgha (Jan–Feb) for the dawn bath, Vaiśākha (Apr–May) for water-gifts against the heat, Mārgaśīrṣa (Nov–Dec) as Kṛṣṇa's own month, and the intercalary **adhika-māsa** rebranded as **Puruṣottama-māsa**. Each month-charter comes wrapped in kathās whose recurring engine is **merit earned cheaply and transferred generously** — a bath, a lamp, a cup of water outweighing lifetimes of sin, and its fruit assignable to the dead, the damned, and the cursed. This file is the corpus's canonical home for the genre and its framing stories, acknowledged elsewhere only in passing in [padma-purana.md](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/padma-purana.md).

---

## The genre and its frames

- **What a māsa-māhātmya is** — a month-glorification: rules (*vidhi*) for the month-long vrata — daily pre-dawn bathing (*snāna*), specified worship, specified charity (*dāna*), things forbidden — interleaved with reward-stories (*kathā*) proving the fruit. The genre belongs to the **vrata literature** of the later Purāṇas, thickest in the *Padma* and *Skanda*. (*Padma Purāṇa*; *Skanda Purāṇa*, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa) [late text]
- **The Padma Purāṇa's carriers** — Vaiśākha-māhātmya in the **Pātāla-khaṇḍa (85–103)** as a dialogue of **Nārada to King Ambarīṣa**; month-vrata and snāna praise in the **Svarga-khaṇḍa (22–23, 30–31)**; Māgha-snāna māhātmya in the **Uttara-khaṇḍa (125–129)**, alongside the huge Kārttika-māhātmya block of the same khaṇḍa. Chapter numbering varies across editions (Ānandāśrama vs. Veṅkaṭeśvara). (*Padma Purāṇa*) [late text]
- **The Skanda Purāṇa's carriers** — the Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa devotes whole upakhaṇḍas to months: **Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya (~36 ch.)**, **Mārgaśīrṣa-māsa-māhātmya (~17 ch.)**, **Vaiśākha-māsa-māhātmya (~25 ch.)** → mapped in [skanda-purana-khanda-map.md](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/skanda-purana-khanda-map.md). (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa) [late text]
- **Calendar mechanics behind the months** — the luni-solar months, their seasonal lock, and the intercalation that produces the adhika-māsa are laid out in [hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md](../00-time-and-cosmology/hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md) (§4); the living festivals of each month in [festivals-and-vratas.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md).

## Kārttika-māhātmya — the month of lamps

- **The charter** — Kārttika is proclaimed the month **dearest to Viṣṇu** (as Dāmodara): its vrata is the pre-dawn **Kārttika-snāna**, daily **Tulasī-worship**, and above all **dīpa-dāna** — lamps lit for Viṣṇu in temples, at the Tulasī plant, on rivers, and hoisted skyward (*ākāśa-dīpa*) to light the way of the ancestors (cf. [śrāddha & pitṛ rites](../05-concepts-and-dharma/shraddha-and-pitr-rites.md)). No gift in Kārttika is said to equal the gift of a lamp. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa, Kārttika-māhātmya; *Skanda Purāṇa*, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa, Kārttikamāsa-māhātmya) [late text]
- **The mouse that lit the lamp** — in a Viṣṇu shrine a **mouse**, creeping up to eat the ghee of a dying lamp, tugs at the wick — and in dragging it **rekindles and raises the flame**. For that unintended dīpa-sevā in Kārttika the creature is freed from its animal birth and rises to a high estate — the genre's sharpest statement that in this month **even accidental merit counts in full**. (Kārttika-māhātmya as carried in the *Padma*/*Skanda* recitation tradition; chapter placement varies by edition) [late text]
  - *Variant (vrata-kathā booklets):* popular tellings make the animal a **rat or a lizard**, or add a hunter whose arrow-nock accidentally trims the temple wick to the same effect — the motif is stable, the protagonist is not. [folk]
- **Dhaneśvara the sinner saved by proximity** — the vaiśya **Dhaneśvara**, a lifelong cheat, dies of snakebite on the **Narmadā bank** where crowds are keeping the Kārttika vrata. In Yama's court no merit of his own is found — only the **touch and company of Kārttika-vratins** and the lamplight he stood in; that borrowed merit cancels his hells and wins him a celestial (guhyaka) estate. Merit here is almost **contagious**. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Kārttika-māhātmya) [late text]
- **Tulasī, the month's presiding plant** — the Kārttika-māhātmya of the *Padma Purāṇa* embeds the **Jalandhara–Vṛndā** cycle as the aetiology of Tulasī, whose worship anchors the month and culminates in the **Tulasī-vivāha** (Prabodhinī Ekādaśī to Kārttika-pūrṇimā) → full tellings in [jalandhara-vrinda.md](jalandhara-vrinda.md) and, for the *Brahmavaivarta*'s Śaṅkhacūḍa version of the Tulasī origin, [shankhachuda-tulasi.md](shankhachuda-tulasi.md). (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa) [late text]
- **Bhīṣma-pañcaka — the five days of Bhīṣma** — the vrata of Kārttika's **last five days** (śukla Ekādaśī through Pūrṇimā), the month's austerity distilled: tradition charters it on **[Bhīṣma](characters/bhishma.md)** on the bed of arrows, who through these days delivered his dharma-discourse to Yudhiṣṭhira, and holds that **Kṛṣṇa instituted the pañcaka in his honor**, promising its keeper the fruit of the whole month. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa; codified for Gauḍīya practice in the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*) [late text]
- **Kārttika-pūrṇimā, the stacked full moon** — the month closes on **Tripurī-pūrṇimā**, kept as the anniversary of Śiva's burning of the triple cities → [tripura-dahana.md](tripura-dahana.md); at Kāśī it is **Dev-Dīpāvalī**, the gods' own festival of lamps on the ghats → [kashi-varanasi.md](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md) [regional]; and one tradition sets the appearance of **Matsya** on this day [traditional]. (Pan-purāṇic calendar lore; see [festivals-and-vratas.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md))

## Māgha-māhātmya — the dawn bath, and merit on trial

- **The rule of Māgha-snāna** — through Māgha, bathing **before sunrise in cold sacred water** — any tīrtha, but supremely the **Triveṇī at Prayāga** — burns sin accumulated over lifetimes; the month-long riverside residence (*kalpavāsa*) at Prayāga survives as the **Māgha-melā**, swelling into the Kumbha → [prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md](../06-sacred-geography/prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md). (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa 125–129; Prayāga-māhātmya lore in the [*Matsya Purāṇa*](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/matsya-purana.md)) [late text]
- **Hemakuṇḍala's two sons** — the merchant **Hemakuṇḍala**, having piled up wealth (not all of it cleanly), turns in old age to wells, gardens, and austerity and dies well; his sons **Śrīkuṇḍala and Vikuṇḍala** squander the estate on vice and die young. Śrīkuṇḍala falls to Yama's hells — but **Vikuṇḍala is claimed by a shining deva-messenger (devadūta)**, because once, in Māgha, he had **bathed in the Yamunā in the company of a pious friend**. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa, Māgha-māhātmya) [late text]
- **Vikuṇḍala's debate with the Devadūta** — carried heavenward, Vikuṇḍala objects: *how can two accidental baths outweigh a life of sin — and how can my brother burn while I ascend?* The devadūta expounds the māhātmya doctrine: merit is weighed, not counted; Māgha-snāna merit is of a different order; and — the genre's boldest claim — **merit can be made over to another**. Vikuṇḍala **assigns the fruit of his Māgha baths to his brother**, Śrīkuṇḍala is released from hell, and both rise — the karma-transfer debate staged as theology. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa, Māgha-māhātmya) [late text]
- **The five gandharva maidens delivered** — five **gandharva maidens**, cursed for a lapse into a wretched piśāca-state, haunt the wilderness until a passing vratin **gifts them a share of his Māgha-snāna merit**; the curse breaks and their celestial forms return. The tale twins with Vikuṇḍala's: transferred bath-merit reaches even beings outside the human estate. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Māgha-māhātmya) [late text]
  - *Note:* the cursed-gandharva-restored motif recurs across the vrata corpus (cf. Jayā and Kāmadā Ekādaśīs in the [Ekādaśī catalog](ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md)); the māhātmyas reuse a shared stock of deliverance plots. [scholarly]

## Vaiśākha-māhātmya — the month of water in the heat

- **The frame: Nārada to Ambarīṣa** — the *Padma Purāṇa*'s Vaiśākha-māhātmya is narrated by **Nārada to King Ambarīṣa** (the Ekādaśī exemplar-king → [ambarisha-durvasa.md](ambarisha-durvasa.md)): Vaiśākha, the month of **Madhusūdana/Mādhava**, is declared the best of months as the Gaṅgā is best of rivers. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Pātāla-khaṇḍa 85–103) [late text]
- **The month's signature dāna: relief from heat** — Vaiśākha falls in high summer, so its merit-list is **water and shade**: roadside water-booths (*prapā-dāna*), gift of water-pots, fans, sandal-paste, umbrellas, footwear, planting shade-trees, and the pre-dawn bath — small mercies to travelers weighed as supreme sacrifice. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Pātāla-khaṇḍa; *Skanda Purāṇa*, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa, Vaiśākhamāsa-māhātmya) [late text]
- **King Mahīratha, who gave his merit to hell** — King **Mahīratha**, rich in Vaiśākha-vrata merit, passing (in Yama's realm's precincts) the cries of **hell's sufferers**, **makes over his accumulated merit to them**; the tormented are released and refreshed, and the king, his merit spent yet multiplied by the giving, ascends to Viṣṇu's world — the māhātmya's grandest merit-transfer, a king emptying hell by charity of karma. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Pātāla-khaṇḍa, Vaiśākha-māhātmya) [late text]
- **Śrutadeva the expounder** — within the māhātmya the learned brāhmaṇa **Śrutadeva** serves as doctrinal mouthpiece, expounding to a king the greatness of Vaiśākha rites and of Viṣṇu-bhakti — the kathās are hung on his teaching dialogues. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Pātāla-khaṇḍa, Vaiśākha-māhātmya) [late text]
  - *Disambiguation:* not the **Śrutadeva of Mithilā**, Kṛṣṇa's poor brāhmaṇa devotee honored alongside King Bahulāśva (*Bhāgavata* 10.86 → [Bhāgavata tales catalog](bhagavata-tales-catalog.md)); the shared name marks a shared "perfect brāhmaṇa" type, not one figure.
- **Devaśarman's previous birth** — the māhātmya's past-life revelation tale: the case of **Devaśarman**, whose present affliction is traced by a seer to **lapses in a former birth**, and whose remedy is the Vaiśākha observance — merit in this month reaching backward across births as it elsewhere reaches across persons. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Pātāla-khaṇḍa, Vaiśākha-māhātmya; tellings vary in detail across editions) [late text] [disputed]
- **The month's fixed stars** — **Akṣaya-tṛtīyā** (Vaiśākha śukla 3), the "imperishable day" stacked with charters (Paraśurāma's birth, Gaṅgā's descent, one reckoning of Tretā's start), and **Buddha-pūrṇimā** close the month's living calendar → [festivals-and-vratas.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md). [traditional]

## Mārgaśīrṣa — the month Kṛṣṇa calls his own

- **The Gītā's warrant** — "**Of months I am Mārgaśīrṣa** (*māsānāṁ mārga-śīrṣo 'ham*), of seasons the flower-bearing spring" — Kṛṣṇa's vibhūti-list itself consecrates the month, the one māsa-glorification spoken in the epic core rather than a late māhātmya. (*Bhagavad-gītā* 10.35 → [bhagavad-gita.md](bhagavad-gita.md))
- **The gopīs' Kātyāyanī-vrata** — in the **first month of Hemanta** (Mārgaśīrṣa) the maidens of Vraja bathe at dawn in the Yamunā and worship **Kātyāyanī** through a month-long vrata to win Kṛṣṇa as husband — the Bhāgavata's own Mārgaśīrṣa month-vrata, prelude to the [rāsa-līlā](rasa-lila.md). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.22)
- **The Skanda's Mārgaśīrṣa-māhātmya** — a ~17-chapter upakhaṇḍa of the Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa prescribing the month's snāna, Keśava-worship, and dānas → [skanda-purana-khanda-map.md](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/skanda-purana-khanda-map.md). (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa) [late text]
- **The month's Ekādaśī anchors** — Mārgaśīrṣa carries **Utpannā** (the birth of the goddess Ekādaśī — the founding legend of that whole cycle) and **Mokṣadā/Gītā-jayantī** (the traditional day the Gītā was spoken) → [ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md](ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md). [traditional]

## Puruṣottama-māsa — the leap month adopted by God [late text]

- **The mechanics** — roughly every 32–33 months the luni-solar calendar inserts an **adhika-māsa** (a lunar month containing no saṁkrānti) to stay season-locked → [hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md](../00-time-and-cosmology/hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md) §4. Ritually it began as **mala-māsa**, the "impure month," in which marriages, upanayana, and most auspicious rites are suspended. (Calendar śāstra; dharmaśāstra digests)
- **The adoption kathā** — the personified **Mala-māsa**, reviled by all and barred from every rite, comes weeping to **Viṣṇu in Vaikuṇṭha**; Viṣṇu, moved, leads the outcast month to **Kṛṣṇa in Goloka**, who **bestows his own name — Puruṣottama —** upon it, declaring it thereby not the least but the **best of months**, its observances bearing multiplied fruit precisely because no worldly business competes with them. (*Puruṣottama-māsa-māhātmya*, transmitted under the *Padma Purāṇa*'s banner; a Nārada-frame vrata text) [late text]
- **The vrata** — month-long snāna, lamp-dāna, recitation of the *Bhāgavata* or the māhātmya itself, and the signature gift of **thirty-three sweet cakes (apūpa-dāna)**; the leap fortnights also carry the two intercalary Ekādaśīs, **Padminī and Paramā** → [ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md](ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md). (*Puruṣottama-māsa-māhātmya*) [late text]
- **Textual status** — the printed māhātmya circulates as Padma-Purāṇa material but is absent from the older strata; scholars treat it as a **late-medieval vrata compilation**, the theological rescue of an inauspicious calendar unit — the adoption story doing for the month what the month does for its observers. [scholarly] [late text]

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural clock** — the kathās float in an unnumbered mythic past ("there was a king…"), deliberately timeless because the vratas recur every year; the datable anchors are Dvāpara's close — **Bhīṣma's five days of teaching** (whence Bhīṣma-pañcaka), **Kṛṣṇa's Gītā declaration** of Mārgaśīrṣa, and the **gopīs' Kātyāyanī month** — while the practice itself belongs to every Kali-yuga year.
- **Academic clock [scholarly]** — the month-māhātmyas sit in the **late strata of composite Purāṇas**: the *Padma*'s khaṇḍas span roughly the 4th–15th c. CE with the vrata-māhātmya layers judged late-medieval, and the *Skanda*'s Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa upakhaṇḍas are likewise post-core accretions; the Puruṣottama-māhātmya is later still. As a genre they document the **medieval vrata economy** — lay observance, cheap grace, and transferable merit — rather than early purāṇic narrative. (Hazra; Rocher) [scholarly]
