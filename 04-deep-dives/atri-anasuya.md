# Atri and Anasūyā

> *Where this sits: a deep-dive into the saptarṣi household at the root of the timeline — Atri the mind-born Prajāpati of the current ([7th, Vaivasvata](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)) Manvantara, and Anasūyā the first-tier pativratā whose chastity births the three deity-portions Soma, Dattātreya, and Durvāsā. ⬆ Parent index: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Master index: [README](../README.md)*

**Atri** (*Atri*, "devourer" / one of the original Saptarṣi) and his wife **Anasūyā** (*An-asūyā*, "she who is free of envy/jealousy") form the single most generative couple of the present world-age: from their austerity descend the **Moon-god Soma** — and through him the entire **[Chandravaṃśa (Lunar dynasty)](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md)** — the avadhūta **[Dattātreya](dattatreya.md)**, and the wrathful sage **[Durvāsā](ambarisha-durvasa.md)**. Anasūyā is canonized across the Purāṇas as the archetypal chaste wife (*pativratā*), and her counsel to Sītā in the *Rāmāyaṇa* is the tradition's locus classicus for *pātivratya-dharma*.

---

## Atri — Prajāpati and Saptarṣi

- **Mind-born son of Brahmā** — Atri is one of the **mānasaputras** (mind-born sons) and Prajāpatis emitted by Brahmā at the dawn of creation, ranked among the seven primordial ṛṣis. (Bhāgavata 3.12; Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa)
  - *Variant (born from the eyes):* One tradition has Atri spring from Brahmā's **eyes** (*netra*), a folk-etymology playing on his role as a "seer." [folk]
- **Saptarṣi of the current Manvantara** — Atri is counted among the **Saptarṣi** (Seven Sages) presiding over the present **[Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)**, alongside Vasiṣṭha, Kaśyapa, Gautama, Bharadvāja, Viśvāmitra, and Jamadagni. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.1) — see [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md).
  - *Variant (rotating office):* The Saptarṣi roster differs by Manvantara and by Purāṇa; Atri is on most lists for the current age but the membership is not fixed across cosmic cycles. [scholarly]
- **Vedic ṛṣi** — Atri is the seer to whom the **fifth maṇḍala** of the **Ṛgveda** (the "Ātreya maṇḍala") is traditionally ascribed; his line, the **Ātreyas**, is one of the foundational gotras. (Ṛgveda, Maṇḍala V; gotra tradition)
- **Genealogical root via Soma** — Through his son **Soma/Candra**, Atri is the ultimate ancestor of the entire Lunar dynasty: Soma → Budha → Purūravas → the Aila kings → Yadu, Pūru, and the houses of the Yādavas, Kauravas, and Pāṇḍavas. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.6; Bhāgavata 9.14) — see [Soma, Tārā & Budha](soma-tara-budha.md) and the [Lunar lineage](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).

## Anasūyā — the first-tier pativratā

- **Daughter of Kardama and Devahūti** — Anasūyā is one of the nine daughters of the Prajāpati **Kardama** and **Devahūti**, hence sister of **Kapila** (and through her sisters, kin to many ṛṣi-lines). (Bhāgavata 3.24.22–24)
  - *Variant:* Some Purāṇas make her a daughter of **Dakṣa**, married to Atri among Dakṣa's many sage-marriages. [regional]
- **The supreme chaste wife** — Anasūyā's *pātivratya* is so potent it becomes a cosmic force; her very name ("free of envy") marks her as the embodiment of the un-jealous, wholly devoted wife. She is listed among the *pañca-satī* / great chaste women in some enumerations. (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa; later māhātmyas)
- **Tapas-power over nature** — In one celebrated tale she **halts the flow of the river** (or makes a barren tract bloom, or shortens night/day) by the force of her vow during a drought, demonstrating that *satya-saṃkalpa* born of fidelity can override natural law. (Purāṇic Anasūyā cycle) [regional]

## The Trimūrti chastity-test (Mārkaṇḍeya & Bhāgavata)

- **Atri and Anasūyā's austerity** — Seeking a son equal to the Supreme, Atri (in some tellings with Anasūyā) performs severe tapas; the Lord appears and grants that **portions of all three** of Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and Śiva will be born to the couple. (Bhāgavata 4.1.15–33)
- **The provocation** — In the dominant telling, **Nārada** praises Anasūyā to the three goddesses **Sarasvatī, Lakṣmī, and Pārvatī** as the most chaste woman alive; stung, the goddesses send their husbands to break her vow. (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa; later sthala-māhātmyas)
- **The impossible demand** — Arriving disguised as mendicants while Atri is away, the Trimūrti demand that Anasūyā serve them food **naked (nagna)** — a request a chaste wife can neither refuse (a guest must be honored) nor grant (without breaking her vow). (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa)
- **The infants miracle** — By the power of her vow (sprinkling water / *satya-saṃkalpa*), Anasūyā turns the three gods into **crying infants**; in that infant state she serves them naked without sin, then suckles and cradles them. (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa)
  - *Variant:* She declares, "If my chastity is true, let these become my children," and the transformation is instant.
- **The goddesses' plea** — Sarasvatī, Lakṣmī, and Pārvatī, finding their husbands missing, come to Anasūyā and beg their return; she restores them. Pleased, the Trimūrti grant that they will be **born as her sons**. (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa)
- **The three deity-portions born** —
  - **Soma / Candra** — the **Brahmā-portion**, rises as the Moon and becomes the [progenitor of the Lunar dynasty](soma-tara-budha.md). (Bhāgavata 4.1)
  - **Dattātreya** — the **Viṣṇu-portion**, remains as the combined teacher and archetypal avadhūta. (Bhāgavata 4.1) — see [Dattātreya](dattatreya.md).
  - **Durvāsā** — the **Śiva-portion**, departs to wander as the irascible curse-wielding ascetic. (Bhāgavata 4.1) — see [Ambarīṣa–Durvāsā](ambarisha-durvasa.md).
  - *Variant (unified single avatāra):* In Datta-devotional tradition the three fuse into **one child, Dattātreya**, with three heads and six arms — the literal Trimūrti-in-one. (Datta Sampradāya hagiography)
  - *Variant [late text]:* A telling has the three born by direct divine fiat without Atri's biological fatherhood, foregrounding pure divine descent.
- **Apportionment of the births** — The Purāṇas split on which child carries which deity's portion: the **Brahmā↔Soma / Viṣṇu↔Datta / Śiva↔Durvāsā** mapping above is the Bhāgavata's; some retellings swap Brahmā's and Śiva's assignments or treat Soma as a separate lunar emanation rather than a "child." [disputed]

## The Rāmāyaṇa episode — Anasūyā counsels Sītā

- **Rāma's visit to Atri's hermitage** — Leaving Chitrakūṭa after Bharata's visit, Rāma, Sītā, and Lakṣmaṇa come to the **āśrama of Atri and Anasūyā**; Atri honors them and presents his aged, austerity-radiant wife. (Vālmīki, Ayodhyā 116–117)
- **Anasūyā's discourse on pātivratya** — Anasūyā instructs **Sītā** at length in **wifely dharma** — that the husband is the wife's deity and refuge, and that supreme devotion to him is the highest path — the *Rāmāyaṇa*'s definitive sermon on *pātivratya-dharma*. (Vālmīki, Ayodhyā 117–118)
- **The divine gifts** — Pleased, Anasūyā bestows on Sītā **celestial unfading garments, ornaments, cosmetic paste (aṅgarāga), and a garland** that will keep her ever-fresh and radiant; Sītā then narrates her own *svayaṃvara* and marriage at Anasūyā's request. (Vālmīki, Ayodhyā 118)
- **Departure toward Daṇḍaka** — After the night at the hermitage, the trio takes leave and enters the deep **Daṇḍaka forest**, opening the Āraṇya Kāṇḍa. (Vālmīki, Ayodhyā 119)
  - *Variant (recension placement):* Vālmīki's standard text closes the **Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa** with this episode, but some manuscript traditions and retellings open the **Āraṇya Kāṇḍa** with the Atri–Anasūyā visit; the placement varies by recension. [scholarly]

## Tradition-by-tradition notes (variant catalog)

- **Padma Purāṇa** — Elaborates the Anasūyā chastity-test and the river-halting tale within its sthala-māhātmya material, and develops her cult at pilgrimage sites associated with Atri's hermitage. (Padma Purāṇa) [regional]
- **Datta Sampradāya** — Treats Anasūyā as the divine mother of the unified Dattātreya and a focus of devotion in her own right; her *pātivratya* is read as the spiritual engine of the avatāra. (Datta Sampradāya hagiography)
- **Regional cult sites** — Hermitages and shrines of Atri–Anasūyā are localized variously (e.g. in the Chitrakūṭa region and the Sahyādri/Western Ghats), each with its own māhātmya tying the couple to local geography. [regional]
