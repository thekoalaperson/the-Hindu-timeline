# Kapila and the Kapila-gita

> The fifth of the Bhāgavata's twenty-two līlā-avatāras: the sage who *founds Sāṃkhya*, teaches his own mother **Devahūti** the way of liberation through devotion (the **Kapila-gītā**, SB 3.25–33), and — in a separate, distant strand — sits in eternal meditation in the netherworld where the **60,000 sons of Sagara** blunder upon him and are burnt to ash, seeding the whole Gaṅgā-descent cycle.
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Up to master index [../README.md](../README.md). · Companions: [Lesser Gītās catalog](lesser-gitas-catalog.md) · [Bhāgavata Tales catalog](bhagavata-tales-catalog.md).

**Kapila** ("the tawny/reddish one") is the standard name of the founder of the **Sāṃkhya** *darśana*, but Hindu tradition braids at least two figures under it: a primordial *avatāra* of Viṣṇu and a later mortal sage credited with the [*Sāṃkhya-sūtra*](#sources). The **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** makes him **Devahūti-putra Kapila** — the son born to the patriarch **Kardama** and his wife **Devahūti** in the [Svāyambhuva Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-01-svayambhuva.md), and counts him as the **fifth** in its list of twenty-two (or twenty-four) descents (SB 1.3.10). His teaching to his mother, the **Kapila-gītā** or **Devahūti-putra-gītā**, is one of the great Purāṇic *gītās* indexed in the [Lesser Gītās catalog](lesser-gitas-catalog.md#purāṇic--independent-gītās-the-path-to-60--pointers-only). "Two clocks" note: *in-narrative* he belongs to the first (Svāyambhuva) Manvantara of the present Kalpa, deep in [manvantara-past] cosmic prehistory; *text-critically* his Sāṃkhya is a theistic redaction of a school whose sūtra-literature is dated centuries into the common era. [scholarly]

## The two (or three) Kapilas — disambiguation first

- **Avatāra-Kapila (Bhāgavata)** — the Lord descended as Devahūti's son to expound *ātma-vidyā* / theistic Sāṃkhya; the figure of SB 3.24–33. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.24, 1.3.10*)
- **Sāṃkhya-founder Kapila (philosophical tradition)** — the historical/legendary *ācārya* to whom the atheistic, dualistic classical Sāṃkhya (puruṣa/prakṛti, 24/25 *tattvas*, **no Īśvara**) and the *Sāṃkhya-sūtra* are attributed. The Bhāgavata's theistic Kapila is, on the textual view, a Vaiṣṇava *re-reading* meant to reclaim Sāṃkhya for *bhakti*. [scholarly] [disputed — single vs. multiple Kapilas]
- **Vāsudeva-Kapila / netherworld-Kapila** — the sage meditating in *rasātala/pātāla* whose glance incinerates Sagara's sons (Rām. Bāla 38–40; Bhāgavata 9.8). Tradition often *identifies* him with the avatāra; the Rāmāyaṇa simply calls him "Kapila … Vāsudeva," and the Bhāgavata frames the burning as the sons' own karma, not the sage's wrath. (*Rāmāyaṇa Bāla 40; Bhāgavata 9.8.11–13*) [disputed — identity of the two Kapilas]
  - *Variant (Gītā lists):* a separate **Kapila-gītā** is sometimes listed among the "60+" Purāṇic gītās; the Bhāgavata 3.25–33 discourse is the substantial one and the referent here. (see [Lesser Gītās catalog](lesser-gitas-catalog.md)) [folk]

## The birth-frame: Kardama and Devahūti (SB 3.21–24)

- **Devahūti's marriage** — **Devahūti**, daughter of **Svāyambhuva Manu** and Śatarūpā, is given to the ascetic **Kardama Prajāpati**; she serves him through a long householder life that yields **nine daughters** (later wives of the great *ṛṣis* — including **Anasūyā** to Atri) before a son is granted. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.22–23*)
- **The Lord as her son** — Viṣṇu promises Kardama that He Himself will be born as their son to teach the *sāṃkhya-yoga* of self-realization; thus **Kapila** is born, and Kardama, his duty done, retires to renunciation. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.24*) → cf. the same Svāyambhuva-era court in [Dhruva](dhruva.md).
- **Devahūti's plea** — Left alone, aged and longing for liberation, Devahūti approaches her divine son and asks him to dispel the darkness of *saṃsāra*; her request opens the discourse. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.25.7–11*)

## The Kapila-gītā — theistic Sāṃkhya to Devahūti (SB 3.25–33)

The discourse runs across **nine chapters** of the Third Canto and is the Bhāgavata's fullest statement of *Sāṃkhya recast as devotion*. Narrated within the *Maitreya–Vidura* dialogue.

- **Bhakti as the first and last word (3.25)** — Kapila opens by declaring that **devotional service (bhakti-yoga)** is the easiest path to liberation; *saṅga* (association) with the holy loosens the heart's knots, while attachment to the worldly binds. He defines *sat-saṅga* and the marks of a *sādhu*. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.25*)
- **The tattvas — enumeration of prakṛti (3.26)** — He lays out the Sāṃkhya cosmology: **puruṣa and prakṛti**, the three *guṇas*, *mahat-tattva*, *ahaṅkāra*, *manas*, the ten senses, five *tanmātras*, five gross elements — but crucially subordinates *prakṛti* to **the Lord (Īśvara/Kāla)** who sets it in motion. This is the decisive break from atheistic classical Sāṃkhya. [scholarly]
- **Liberation of the puruṣa (3.27)** — The self, though entangled in the modes, is liberated by discriminative knowledge plus devotion; *jñāna* without *bhakti* is incomplete. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.27*)
- **Aṣṭāṅga-yoga and meditation on the form of Viṣṇu (3.28)** — Kapila teaches an eightfold *yoga* of posture, breath, and withdrawal culminating not in formless absorption but in **meditation on the limbs of the Lord's form** (feet to face, in order) — yoga as devotional contemplation. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.28*)
- **The grades of bhakti and the power of Time (3.29)** — He classifies devotion by *guṇa* (*tāmasa*, *rājasa*, *sāttvika*) and praises *nirguṇa* unmotivated bhakti as highest; declares the Lord present in **all beings**, so contempt for any creature is an offence; and unveils **Kāla (Time)** as His terrifying, all-devouring aspect. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.29*)
- **The wheel of saṃsāra and the horrors of rebirth (3.30–31)** — A vivid teaching on the soul's bondage, the **suffering in the womb (garbha-stuti)** where the encased *jīva* prays for release, birth, the forgetfulness that follows, and the karmic descent of the un-devout. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.30–31*)
- **The dead-end of fruitive religion (3.32)** — Even those who reach the lunar/heavenly worlds by ritual and ancestor-worship must return; only surrender to the Lord ends the cycle. He distinguishes *karma*, *jñāna*, and *bhakti* destinations. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.32*)
- **Devahūti's liberation (3.33)** — Devahūti grasps the teaching, takes up *bhakti-yoga*, and attains realization; she retreats to the **Sarasvatī** (the place **Siddhapada**) and is liberated. Kapila takes leave of his mother and departs **north-east to the ocean**, where — tradition holds — he abides in *samādhi* at the river-mouth later called **Gaṅgāsāgara**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.33*)
  - *Variant (place of Kapila's seat):* the Bhāgavata sends Kapila to the sea in the north-east (the **Gaṅgā-Sāgara** confluence, Sagar Island, Bengal); the Rāmāyaṇa locates the sage's meditation in the **netherworld (rasātala)** the digging sons break into. The pilgrim-tradition harmonizes the two at **Kapil Muni's āśrama, Gaṅgāsāgara**. [regional] [folk]

## The burning of Sagara's sons — the netherworld Kapila (SB 9.8–9; Rām. Bāla 38–42)

A separate narrative strand, in the **Ninth Canto's Sūryavaṃśa history** and in Vālmīki's Bāla Kāṇḍa, where Kapila is the immovable sage whose presence — not whose anger — destroys the trespassers. This is the hinge that launches the **Gaṅgāvataraṇa**. → fuller dynastic context in [Sūryavaṃśa & Candravaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md#0-the-common-root--vaivasvata-manu); Rāmāyaṇa telling in [Bāla Kāṇḍa](ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md).

- **Sagara's two queens and 60,000 sons** — King **Sagara** of Ayodhyā (Ikṣvāku line) has by **Sumati** sixty thousand sons, and by **Keśinī** the single son **Asamañjas** (whose own son is **Aṃśumān**). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.8.1–9; Rāmāyaṇa Bāla 38*)
- **The Aśvamedha and the stolen horse** — Sagara performs the **horse-sacrifice**; **Indra**, fearing the king's growing power, steals the sacrificial horse and tethers it beside the meditating Kapila in the netherworld. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.8.7–9; Rāmāyaṇa Bāla 39*)
- **The 60,000 dig up the earth** — Commanded to find the horse, the sons **excavate the whole earth** (in the Rāmāyaṇa, deepening the ocean-bed — a folk etymology for *sāgara*, "ocean," from Sagara). (*Rāmāyaṇa Bāla 39–40*)
- **The fatal accusation and the burning** — Finding the horse by the sage, they **wrongly accuse Kapila of theft** and rush him; he opens his eyes (Rāmāyaṇa) / they are consumed by the **fire of their own offence** (Bhāgavata), and the **60,000 are reduced to ash** on the spot. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.8.11–13; Rāmāyaṇa Bāla 40*)
  - *Variant (cause of death):* the **Rāmāyaṇa** has the sage's wrathful glance (*roṣa*) incinerate them; the **Bhāgavata** is careful to say Kapila, free of the modes, bore no anger — the sons were burnt **by their own bodies' fire**, kindled by aggression against a saint. The Mahābhārata (Vana Parva) has **Kapila** burning them likewise. [disputed — agency of the burning]
- **Aṃśumān recovers the horse** — Sagara sends his grandson **Aṃśumān**, who approaches Kapila with reverence; the sage gives back the horse, blesses him, and reveals that **only the heavenly Gaṅgā**, brought down to wash the ashes, can liberate the dead sons. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.8.20–28; Rāmāyaṇa Bāla 41–42*)
- **The seed of the Gaṅgā-descent** — Neither Aṃśumān nor his son **Dilīpa** can accomplish it; it falls to the great-grandson **Bhagīratha**, whose austerities bring **Gaṅgā** to earth (Śiva breaking her fall in his locks — *Gaṅgādhara*) to redeem the 60,000 — the **Gaṅgāvataraṇa** / *Bhāgīrathī*. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.9; Rāmāyaṇa Bāla 42–44*) → see [Bāla Kāṇḍa](ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md) and the [Solar dynasty line](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).

## Doctrine and reception

- **Theistic vs. atheistic Sāṃkhya** — The Bhāgavata's Kapila is the textual instrument by which Vaiṣṇavism **annexes Sāṃkhya**: the same 24/25-*tattva* scaffold the [Yājñavalkya-gītā](lesser-gitas-catalog.md#śānti-parva-book-12--mokṣa-dharma--rāja-dharma-gītās) and the *Sāṃkhya-kārikā* use, but crowned with *Īśvara* and *bhakti*. Classical Sāṃkhya (Īśvarakṛṣṇa's *Kārikā*, the later *Sāṃkhya-sūtra*) is **non-theistic**. [scholarly]
- **The Sāṃkhya-sūtra** — the aphoristic *Sāṃkhya-pravacana-sūtra* is attributed to Kapila, but is a **[late text]** (commonly dated to the medieval period, well after the *Sāṃkhya-kārikā*) — the attribution is honorific, not chronological; another "two clocks" caution. [scholarly] [disputed authorship]
- **Cult and pilgrimage** — **Kapil Muni's temple at Gaṅgāsāgara (Sagar Island)** is a major *makara-saṅkrānti* (mid-January) pilgrimage, fusing the Devahūti-son and the netherworld sages into one venerated *muni*. [regional] [folk]
