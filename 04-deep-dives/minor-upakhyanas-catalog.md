# Catalog of Minor Upākhyānas & Tales

> Where this sits: an **index of the smaller insets** (upākhyānas, ākhyānas, exemplary tales) of the epics and Purāṇas that do not yet have their own dedicated file in [04-deep-dives](README.md).
> Each entry below is a 1–2 line stub with chronological placement & source, flagged **[stub — needs own file]** unless a link shows it already exists.
> Back to the [deep-dives index](README.md) · [master index](../README.md).

This roster is **deliberately open-ended.** The Mahābhārata alone contains, by Hiltebeitel's count, **67 sub-tales explicitly labelled *upākhyāna*** (plus dozens more *ākhyānas* and unlabelled insets), and the Purāṇas add hundreds more. This file catalogs the high-value tales named in the project's completeness sweep and a handful of immediate neighbours; it does **not** claim to exhaust the corpus. New stubs should be appended here, then promoted to their own files as they are written. [scholarly]

## Stubs awaiting dedicated files — chronologically arranged

### Cosmic dawn / Sun-dynasty deep past

- **Māndhātā (Māndhātṛ)** - Ikṣvāku-line cakravartin, son of Yuvanāśva — born from his **father's** body (the king drank the consecrated fertility-water by mistake), suckled by Indra ("*mām dhāsyati*" → Māndhātā); a model digvijaya emperor cited as the archetype of just rule. Chronological anchor: very early Tretā/Sun-dynasty, ancestor of [Trishaṅku](trishanku.md)/[Hariścandra](hariscandra.md) line. (MBh. Vana-parva & Droṇa-parva; Bhāgavata 9; Viṣṇu P.). → **Promoted:** [mandhata.md](mandhata.md)

- **Galava's horses (Gālava–Mādhavī–Aṣṭaka)** - the disciple Gālava, pressed by Viśvāmitra for an impossible guru-dakṣiṇā of **800 white horses each with one black ear**, is given Yayāti's daughter **Mādhavī**, who through a boon renews her virginity after each birth; she is loaned in turn to kings Haryaśva (Ayodhyā), Divodāsa (Kāśi) and Uśīnara (Bhoja) — 200 horses + one heroic son each — then to Viśvāmitra, bearing **Aṣṭaka**; only 600 horses are ever found and the debt is closed by Garuḍa's counsel. Mādhavī finally rejects all suitors at her svayaṃvara and becomes a forest ascetic. A dark exemplum of the price of guru-dakṣiṇā and of women as exchange-currency. Placement: Yayāti's generation, links to [Yayāti](yayati.md). (MBh. Udyoga-parva 105–121). [disputed — read as proto-feminist critique by modern scholars] → **Promoted:** [galava-madhavi.md](galava-madhavi.md)

### Bhṛgu / Aṅgiras lineage tales (frame: snake-sacrifice & Vana-parva)

- **Ruru & Pramadvarā** - young Bhārgava Ruru loses his betrothed Pramadvarā (daughter of the apsarā Menakā and Viśvāvasu) to a snakebite on the eve of marriage; he gives **half his own remaining lifespan** to revive her, then vows to kill every snake he meets until a *ḍuṇḍubha* (non-venomous serpent, the cursed sage Sahasrapāt) preaches *ahiṃsā* to him. Frames the broader serpent narrative. Placement: early Bhṛgu genealogy, Ādi-parva. (MBh. Ādi-parva, Pauloma sub-parva). → **Promoted:** [ruru-pramadvara.md](ruru-pramadvara.md)

- **Kaca & Devayānī** - the deva-side youth **Kaca** infiltrates the asura-guru **Śukrācārya** to steal the secret of *mṛta-sañjīvanī* (raising the dead); killed thrice by asuras and revived, he finally learns the vidyā from within Śukra's own belly; he rejects the love of Śukra's daughter **Devayānī**, who curses him, beginning the chain that leads to [Yayāti & Devayānī/Śarmiṣṭhā](yayati.md). Aetiology of the sañjīvanī-vidyā and of inter-clan enmity. Placement: pre-Yayāti, Ādi-parva (Sambhava). (MBh. Ādi-parva 76–78). → **Promoted:** [characters/kacha-devayani.md](characters/kacha-devayani.md)

- **Aṣṭāvakra** - born "crooked in eight ways" after cursing his father Kahoḍa from the womb for mispronouncing the Veda; as a boy he travels to King Janaka's court, **defeats the philosopher Bandin (Vandin)** in debate (avenging his drowned father and freeing those Bandin had cast into the sea), and recovers his father; later straightened by bathing in the Samaṅgā. Namesake of the later Advaitic *Aṣṭāvakra Gītā* [late text]. Placement: Vana-parva (told to the Pāṇḍavas). (MBh. Vana-parva 132–134). → **Promoted:** [characters/ashtavakra.md](characters/ashtavakra.md)

- **Yavakrī (Yavakrīta / Yavakrīda)** - son of Bhāradvāja who, jealous that the Veda-learned **Raibhya** is honoured while his ascetic father is slighted, wins Vedic knowledge directly from Indra by extreme tapas — then grows arrogant, molests Raibhya's daughter-in-law, and is slain by a *rākṣasa* (and a *kṛtyā* spirit + a śūdra) conjured from Raibhya's matted lock; revived later by Bhāradvāja's intervention. A parable on tapas-pride versus disciplined learning; basis of Karnad's *The Fire and the Rain* [folk/modern adaptation]. Placement: Vana-parva, Tīrtha-yātrā. (MBh. Vana-parva 135–138). **[stub — needs own file]**

### Śānti / Anuśāsana-parva dharma-exempla (didactic insets)

- **Tulādhāra & Jājali** - the proud forest-ascetic **Jājali** (so still that birds nested in his matted hair) is told by a heavenly voice that he is not equal in dharma to **Tulādhāra**, a humble grocer of Vārāṇasī; Tulādhāra expounds *ahiṃsā*, honest livelihood and the merchant's even-handed scales (*tulā*) as true righteousness over showy penance. A keystone non-violence / householder-dharma sermon. Placement: Śānti-parva, Mokṣadharma. (MBh. Śānti-parva 261–264). **[stub — needs own file]**

- **Vipula-Ruci (Vipula guarding Ruci)** - a self-contained didactic inset of the Anuśāsana didactic-cycle. The sage **Devaśarman**, obliged to leave on a sacrifice and knowing Indra covets his beautiful wife **Ruci**, charges his foremost disciple **Vipula** (a Bhārgava) to guard her chastity during his absence. Distrusting both Indra's wiles and the frailty of ordinary watching, Vipula enters Ruci's body by **yoga** — joining his rays to hers and binding her senses from within — so that when Indra arrives in seductive guise her limbs will not stir to his approach; Indra, finding her thus warded and beholding Vipula's tapas, departs foiled. Vipula then withdraws from her body, restores her unaware, and on Devaśarman's return reports his success and is praised. A peripheral exemplum on **guarding a teacher's trust** (the limits and burdens of a *guru-kārya*), the perils of placing a young ascetic to watch over a woman, and the power of yogic self-mastery; framed as counsel on the protection of women and the safeguarding of fidelity. The **means are contested**: read by some as pious, disinterested devotion (Vipula touches Ruci without lust, purely to honour his guru) and by others as a coercive violation of her autonomy and consent (her body and senses are seized without her knowledge). Fits the roster as a minor Anuśāsana didactic tale rather than a first-tier node. Placement: Anuśāsana-parva. (MBh. Anuśāsana-parva 40–43, "Vipula-Upākhyāna"). [disputed — read variously as devotion or coercion] **[stub — needs own file]**

- **Mudgala (the gleaner)** - the sage **Mudgala** of Kurukṣetra lives by *uñchavṛtti* (gleaning fallen grain), feeds even the testing **Durvāsā** without anger through repeated provocations, and is offered bodily ascent to heaven — which he **declines** on learning svarga too has an end, choosing instead the path beyond rebirth (*mokṣa*). Exemplar of equanimity and renunciation of even heaven. Placement: Vana-parva. (MBh. Vana-parva 246–247 / "Mudgala-Upākhyāna"). → **Promoted:** [characters/mudgala.md](characters/mudgala.md)

- **The Pious Hunter (Dharmavyādha) & Kauśika** - the arrogant brāhmaṇa ascetic **Kauśika**, humbled first by a *paṭivratā* housewife (see below) and then by a **butcher of Mithilā**, receives the **Vyādha-Gītā** — that dharma lies in selfless duty in one's own station, service to parents, and *svadharma* over ritual purity. Often counted as its own *upākhyāna*. Placement: Vana-parva (told by Mārkaṇḍeya). (MBh. Vana-parva 205–215). → **Promoted:** [vyadha-gita.md](vyadha-gita.md)

- **The Pativratā (the devoted wife / Sumanā · Kauśika frame)** - the housewife who, having served her husband, keeps the impatient brāhmaṇa Kauśika waiting and then **rebukes his anger by reading his mind** — demonstrating that wifely devotion (*pati-vratā*) generates spiritual power equal to a sage's, and directing him to the Dharmavyādha. (Named **Sumanā** in some retellings [folk].) Sets up the Vyādha-Gītā frame above. Placement: Vana-parva. (MBh. Vana-parva 205–206). → **Promoted:** treated as Act Two of [vyadha-gita.md](vyadha-gita.md)

- **Sudarśana & Oghavatī (hospitality / atithi-dharma)** - King/sage **Sudarśana**, devotee of Agni who has conquered death by mastering the duty of the guest, instructs his wife **Oghavatī** to honour every *atithi* utterly; when Dharma comes disguised as a brāhmaṇa demanding she serve him with her own body, her obedience and Sudarśana's unwavering hospitality conquer Death itself. A radical *atithi-deva* exemplum. Placement: Anuśāsana-parva. (MBh. Anuśāsana-parva 2). [disputed — extreme reading of hospitality] **[stub — needs own file]**

### Vidura-nīti tales & gnomic insets

- **Vidura-nīti (the parables)** - within Udyoga-parva, the wise half-brother **Vidura** counsels the sleepless Dhṛtarāṣṭra with a chain of pithy fables and maxims on statecraft, self-control and dharma — including the celebrated **"man in the well"** allegory (the traveller hanging by a vine over a pit of serpents, lapping honey-drops while mice gnaw the root = the soul ensnared in saṃsāra, later retold to Dhṛtarāṣṭra in the Strī/Vana frame). A nīti-anthology rather than a single tale. Placement: Udyoga-parva 33–40. (MBh. Udyoga-parva, "Prajāgara-parva"). → **Promoted:** [vidura-niti.md](vidura-niti.md)

### Bhāgavata / Purāṇic detachment-tales

- **Piṅgalā the courtesan** - among the **24 gurus of the avadhūta** (Dattātreya) recited to King Yadu in the Uddhava-Gītā, the Videha courtesan **Piṅgalā**, wearied of waiting all night for a paying lover, suddenly attains detachment — realising that hope/desire is the cause of misery and the inner Lord the only true beloved — and sleeps in peace. Classic *vairāgya* parable. Placement: current Dvāpara/Kali cusp, Kṛṣṇa's teaching to Uddhava. (Bhāgavata 11.8). Cross-link: [Dattātreya](dattatreya.md). → **Promoted:** [pingala-gita.md](pingala-gita.md) (the parallel Mbh XII Mokṣadharma telling, with the Bhāgavata variant catalogued)

- **Śuka (Śukadeva)** - Vyāsa's parrot-headed, ever-renunciate son, born already liberated (*jīvanmukta*), who tarried in the womb fearing māyā and at birth walked straight to the forest; he is the **narrator of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa** to the dying King Parīkṣit over seven days. His brief householder-test by Janaka and instant return to renunciation is itself an upākhyāna. Placement: end of Dvāpara, frames the whole Bhāgavata. (Bhāgavata 1–2 & 12; MBh. Śānti/Mokṣadharma "Śuka's flight"). Cross-link: [Mārkaṇḍeya](markandeya.md) (fellow immortal-renunciate). **[stub — needs own file]**

## Note on scope & promotion

- **Counts vary by recension.** The number of MBh upākhyānas depends on which sub-tales are reckoned (Hiltebeitel: 67 labelled *upākhyāna*; far more if *ākhyānas* and unlabelled insets are added). Treat any total as recension-bound.
  - *Variant (Hiltebeitel, ed. *Argument and Design*, 2016):* **67** explicitly *upākhyāna*-named insets in the Pune Critical Edition. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (popular/devotional retellings):* "108 stories" and similar round numbers appear but are **not** text-critical. [folk]
- **Neighbouring tales not yet stubbed** (candidates for future entries): Cirakārin (the slow-deliberating son), Gautamī & the fowler (the snake, death and time), Kapotopākhyāna (the self-sacrificing pigeon, Śibi-cycle — see [Śibi](shibi.md)), Kṛtaghna/Gautama the ungrateful brāhmaṇa, Suśarmā, Indradyumna, Ṛṣyamūka tales, the Śyena-Kapotīya (hawk-and-dove). This list is **not closed.**
- **Promotion rule:** when a stub is expanded into its own file under `04-deep-dives/`, replace its stub here with a one-line link in the "Already promoted" section above, and add it to [README.md](README.md).
