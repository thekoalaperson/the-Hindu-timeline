# Dilīpa and Nandinī

> *Where this sits: a deep-dive on the opening set-piece of Kālidāsa's **Raghuvaṃśa** (cantos 1–2) — the childless Sūryavaṃśa king **Dilīpa** serving the wish-cow **Nandinī** at Vasiṣṭha's command, his offer of his own body to the lion that seizes her, and the boon that produces **[Raghu](raghu.md)**, eponym of the dynasty. Up to the [Deep Dives index](README.md). The line is the senior Ikṣvāku stem of the [Sūryavaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).*
> *Two clocks: as itihāsa Dilīpa is a Tretā-Yuga Ikṣvāku king of the present 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara — Rāma's great-great-great-grandfather (Dilīpa → Raghu → Aja → Daśaratha → Rāma). As text the whole devotional tale is Kālidāsa's **Raghuvaṃśa** (c. 4th–5th c. CE), elaborating a name the Purāṇic and Rāmāyaṇa king-lists give only in passing. [scholarly]*

---

## Dramatis personae & the two Dilīpas

- **Dilīpa (दिलीप)** — the Ikṣvāku / Sūryavaṃśa king of **Ayodhyā** at the head of Kālidāsa's *Raghuvaṃśa*; ideal sovereign, father of **Raghu**, great-grandfather of Daśaratha (*Raghuvaṃśa* 1–2). Kālidāsa opens the poem with him as the archetype of dharmic kingship before the dynasty even has its eponym.
- **Sudakṣiṇā (सुदक्षिणा)** — Dilīpa's queen, of the **Magadha** royal house; she shares the cow-service and is the mother of Raghu (*Raghuvaṃśa* 1.31–32, 2).
- **Vasiṣṭha (वसिष्ठ)** — the Saptarṣi and hereditary **purohita of the Ikṣvākus**, keeper of the wish-cow, who diagnoses the king's childlessness and prescribes the penance (*Raghuvaṃśa* 1.59 ff.). See [Viśvāmitra & Vasiṣṭha](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md), where the same sage's wish-cow ignites a different epic feud.
- **Nandinī (नन्दिनी, "the delightful")** — the wish-cow (*kāmadhenu*) of Vasiṣṭha's hermitage; in the *Raghuvaṃśa* she is the **daughter of Surabhi / Kāmadhenu**, the celestial cow of plenty (*Raghuvaṃśa* 1.81 ff.).
  - *Variant (which Dilīpa) [disputed]:* the Purāṇas distinguish an earlier **Dilīpa I** — descendant of Aṃśumān, in the **Bhagīratha / Gaṅgā-descent** line of the same Sūryavaṃśa — from the **Dilīpa II** who is Raghu's father. Kālidāsa's Nandinī-service tale belongs to **Raghu's father**, not to Bhagīratha's ancestor; the king-lists keep them as separate reigns. See [Sūryavaṃśa & Candravaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
  - *Variant (which Nandinī) [disputed]:* "Nandinī" is also the name of the wish-cow whose **theft by the Vasus** triggers Vasiṣṭha's curse and the birth of **[Bhīṣma](characters/bhishma.md)** (Mbh Ādi Parva). The two stories share a cow-name and the sage Vasiṣṭha but are wholly distinct episodes; the timeline does **not** merge them.

## 1. The flawless king and the diagnosis of childlessness (Raghuvaṃśa canto 1)

- **The ideal sovereign** — Kālidāsa opens the poem by praising Dilīpa as the model king: broad-shouldered, lion-waisted, devoted to dharma, taxing his subjects only as the sun draws water to return it a thousandfold as rain (*Raghuvaṃśa* 1.12–26). The whole canto establishes him as the dynasty's moral benchmark before its eponym is born.
- **The one lack — no son** — Despite every virtue and a long, just reign, Dilīpa and Sudakṣiṇā are **childless**; the want of an heir is the single shadow on his rule (*Raghuvaṃśa* 1.32–34).
- **The journey to Vasiṣṭha's hermitage** — The royal couple drive in a single chariot, without their army, to the forest āśrama of the family preceptor **Vasiṣṭha** to seek the cause and the remedy (*Raghuvaṃśa* 1.35–58).
- **The sage's diagnosis — the slighted cow** — By his yogic sight Vasiṣṭha discloses the karmic cause: returning once from Indra's heaven, Dilīpa had **passed by the wish-cow Surabhi (Kāmadhenu)** under the wishing-tree without paying her homage; the offended cow **cursed him to remain childless** until he won the favour of her progeny (*Raghuvaṃśa* 1.72–80).
  - *Variant (the offence) [late text]:* the lapse is sometimes read as inadvertent (the king was hurrying, mind on duty) rather than contemptuous — Kālidāsa stresses that even a flawless king's smallest omission of reverence carries karmic weight. The moral, not the malice, is the point.
- **The remedy prescribed** — Vasiṣṭha sets the expiation: Dilīpa and Sudakṣiṇā are to **serve Surabhi's daughter, the cow Nandinī**, then in the hermitage, with total, humble devotion; the cow's grace will lift the curse and grant a son (*Raghuvaṃśa* 1.81–88).

## 2. The service of Nandinī — twenty-one days of devotion (Raghuvaṃśa cantos 1–2)

- **The vow of cow-service (go-sevā)** — Laying aside royal state, Dilīpa undertakes to **tend Nandinī as a herdsman**: following her by day as she grazes the forest, guarding her, garlanding her, standing when she stands and resting when she rests; Sudakṣiṇā worships her at morning and evening (*Raghuvaṃśa* 1.89–95, 2.1–25).
- **The duration — twenty-one days** — The penance runs for **twenty-one days**; the cow tests the king's constancy through the whole term, and on the final day stages the supreme test (*Raghuvaṃśa* 2; the 21-day count is the tradition's standard reckoning of the service).
- **Perfect attendance** — Kālidāsa dwells on the king's flawless attention: he shields Nandinī from heat and insects, follows her into deep forest, and his queen receives the cow's evening return like a daughter-in-law — the canto is a sustained icon of guru- and cow-devotion (*go-bhakti*) (*Raghuvaṃśa* 2.1–25).
  - *Variant (the cow as tester) [scholarly]:* the poem frames Nandinī not as a passive boon-giver but as an active **examiner of the king's sincerity**; the lion-test (below) is her own contrivance to prove that his service is unconditional, not a transaction for a son.

---

## 3. The lion-test — Dilīpa offers his own body (Raghuvaṃśa canto 2)

- **The lion seizes Nandinī** — On the final day, deep in a Himālayan gorge, a great **lion springs upon Nandinī** and pins her; the cow lows in distress (*Raghuvaṃśa* 2.26–36).
- **The king's arm is frozen** — Dilīpa snatches an arrow to kill the lion, but his **hand is fixed motionless on the quiver as if painted** — he cannot draw, held by an unseen power (*Raghuvaṃśa* 2.32–36). The helplessness is itself part of the test.
- **The lion speaks — Śiva's servant** — The lion declares itself no ordinary beast but a creature stationed there by **Śiva** as the keeper of a withered divine **deodar (devadāru) tree** that Pārvatī tends like a son; Nandinī, it says, is its lawful, fated food, and the king cannot save her (*Raghuvaṃśa* 2.37–55).
- **Dilīpa offers his own body** — Rather than return to Vasiṣṭha having failed to protect the cow entrusted to him, Dilīpa **offers his own flesh to the lion in Nandinī's place**, asking only that the cow — a brahmin's ritual wealth — be spared; he kneels and bows his body to be eaten (*Raghuvaṃśa* 2.56–62). This self-offering is the moral climax of the whole episode — kingship valued below the protection of the entrusted and the brahmin's cow.
- **The test dissolves — the boon won** — At his selfless surrender the **lion vanishes**: it was an illusion conjured by **Nandinī herself** to test him. The cow, satisfied, hails his devotion and bids him **drink her milk** as the boon; Dilīpa drinks only with Vasiṣṭha's leave, and Nandinī grants that a son worthy of the line will be born (*Raghuvaṃśa* 2.63–75).
  - *Variant (the lion's nature) [late text]:* the illusion is variously read as Nandinī's own *māyā* or as a glamour sanctioned by Śiva to prove the king; Kālidāsa makes the cow the author of the test, and the dissolving of the lion the sign of the penance fulfilled.

---

## 4. The boon fulfilled — the birth of Raghu (Raghuvaṃśa cantos 2–3)

- **The blessing and the return** — Nandinī blesses Dilīpa and Sudakṣiṇā with a son of unmatched glory; the couple take leave of Vasiṣṭha and return to Ayodhyā, the curse lifted (*Raghuvaṃśa* 2.69–75).
- **The conception and birth of Raghu** — In due course Sudakṣiṇā conceives and bears a son; he is named **Raghu** ("the swift"), and he will so eclipse his forebears that the whole senior Solar line is renamed the **Raghuvaṃśa** after him (*Raghuvaṃśa* 3.1–21; see [Raghu](raghu.md)).
- **The charter-function** — The episode is the *Raghuvaṃśa*'s **charter-story**: it founds the dynasty's name on an act of cow- and guru-devotion and on a king's readiness to give his body for what is entrusted to him — the poem's template of *dharmic* kingship, against which Raghu, Aja, Daśaratha, and Rāma are all later measured (*Raghuvaṃśa* 1–3; [scholarly]).

## 5. Placement, parallels, and the "two clocks"

- **Genealogical placement** — In the Solar king-list: **… → Dilīpa (II) → Raghu → Aja → Daśaratha → Rāma → Kuśa & Lava**. Full tables in [Sūryavaṃśa & Candravaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md). The Purāṇas (Viṣṇu IV, Bhāgavata IX) give Dilīpa only as a name in the descent, without the Nandinī penance.
- **Distinct from the other wish-cow tales** — Kālidāsa's Nandinī is Vasiṣṭha's cow rewarding devotion with a son; she must be kept apart from (a) **Śabalā / Nandinī**, the cow whose seizure sparks the [Viśvāmitra–Vasiṣṭha](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md) feud, and (b) the **Nandinī stolen by the Vasus** that dooms the Vasu Dyaus to be born as [Bhīṣma](characters/bhishma.md). Same sage, same cow-name, three separate episodes — catalogued, never merged. [disputed]
- **Scriptural clock** — On the sacred reckoning Dilīpa is a **Tretā-Yuga** Ikṣvāku king of the present Mahāyuga, four generations above Rāma.
- **Historical clock** — The tale that fixes Dilīpa's fame is **Kālidāsa's *Raghuvaṃśa***, a c. 4th–5th-century-CE Sanskrit *mahākāvya*; the Nandinī penance, the lion-test, and the body-offering are the poet's literary set-piece, absent from the terse Purāṇic king-lists. The two clocks are kept distinct: Dilīpa the Tretā sovereign of sacred history vs. Dilīpa the exemplar of a Gupta-classical court poem. [scholarly]
