# Varāha — the Boar Avatar and the Slaying of Hiraṇyākṣa

> The **third** of the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md): Viṣṇu as the cosmic **boar (varāha)** who dives into the deluge to lift a drowned **Earth (Bhūdevī / Pṛthvī)** on his tusks and slays the daitya **Hiraṇyākṣa** in a mace-duel that Brahmā urges him to finish before dusk.
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Twin-cycle peers: [Prahlāda & Narasiṃha](prahlada-narasimha.md) · [Vāmana & Bali](vamana-bali.md) · the doorkeepers' curse: [Jaya & Vijaya](jaya-vijaya.md) · cosmogonic kin: [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md) · the milked Earth: [Pṛthu & Pṛthvī](prithu-prithvi.md).

The **Varāha** descent is told most fully in the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Skandha 3** — the cosmogony of **Maitreya to Vidura**, with the boar's emergence at **SB 3.13** and the war with Hiraṇyākṣa at **SB 3.17–19** — and in compact form in **Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.4**, the **Liṅga Purāṇa 1.94**, the epics, and the **Varāha Purāṇa**. It is unusual among the avatāras for fusing two distinct registers: an older **cosmogonic** boar who raises the Earth at the dawn of a *kalpa*, and the later **deliverance** narrative of a specific demon-slaying. It is the standalone peer of [Narasiṃha](prahlada-narasimha.md) and [Vāmana](vamana-bali.md), and — uniquely — it lends its name to the present cosmic day, the **Vārāha (Śveta-Varāha) Kalpa**.

## I. Vedic and Brāhmaṇa antecedents — the boar before the avatāra

- **Emūṣa, the earth-raising boar** — In the **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa (14.1.2.11)**, in the Pravargya rite where earth dug up by a boar is collected for the fire-pot, the Earth was at first "only so large … of the size of a span (*prādeśa*)"; "a boar, called **Emūṣa**, raised her up, and he was her lord **Prajāpati**" (Eggeling's translation). The boar is not yet Viṣṇu but the creator-principle itself. (*Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 14.1.2.11*) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Ṛgveda 8.77.10, 1.61.7; Taittirīya Saṃhitā 6.2.4.2–3):* the oldest **Emuṣa** is a different boar — an adversary, not an earth-raiser. In RV 8.77.10, Viṣṇu Urukrama brings Indra "a hundred buffaloes, a rice-mess cooked in milk, and the boar Emuṣa"; in RV 1.61.7 Indra the archer "pierced the boar through the mountain." TS 6.2.4.2–3 tells the story at length: a boar, the "stealer of the good" (*vāma-moṣa*), guards the **wealth of the Asuras beyond seven hills**; Indra pierces the hills with a tuft of darbha grass and kills it, and **Viṣṇu as the sacrifice** carries it off to the gods — told to explain the name of the *vedi* (sacrificial altar). The ŚB 14.1.2.11 passage gives this boar's name to Prajāpati's earth-raising boar. [scholarly]
- **Taittirīya cosmogonic boar** — **Taittirīya Saṃhitā 7.1.5.1** gives the unnamed boar-cosmogony: "This was in the beginning the waters, the ocean. In it Prajāpati becoming the wind moved. He saw her, and becoming a boar he seized her. Her, becoming Viśvakarman, he wiped. She extended, she became the earth (*pṛthivī*)" (Keith's translation). The **Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa** (1.1.3.5–6, locus as given in Muir's *Original Sanskrit Texts* i.52; Sanskrit not checked) likewise has Prajāpati take boar form and dive for the earth. The **Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10.1.8** (Mahānārāyaṇa) addresses the Earth as "raised by the **black** boar with a hundred arms" (*uddhṛtāsi varāheṇa kṛṣṇena śatabāhunā*; wording checked only through Rao's citation, *Elements of Hindu Iconography* I.1 p. 127; Eggeling, SBE 44 p. 451 n. 1, cites the same image as "Taitt. I, 10, 8" with a "**thousand** arms", so the two secondary citations disagree on locus and count; Sanskrit not checked). Neither Taittirīya passage names the boar Emūṣa. (*Taittirīya Saṃhitā 7.1.5.1; Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10.1.8*) [scholarly]
- **Identification shift Prajāpati → Viṣṇu** — Across the epic-Purāṇic transition the Brāhmaṇa boar (Prajāpati/Brahmā) is re-read as a form of **Viṣṇu / Nārāyaṇa**. The *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* keeps both: the boar is "Brahmā who is Nārāyaṇa," the lord of the Prajāpatis (*VP 1.4.1–8*). The *Bhāgavata* has the boar emerge **from Brahmā's nostril** (*BhP 3.13.18*), which keeps the older link to the creator while making the boar Viṣṇu's avatāra. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa 2.102.2–3, critical ed.):* Vasiṣṭha tells Rāma that from the primeval waters "arose **Brahmā**, the Self-existent, with the deities; he then, becoming a boar, raised up the earth" — the epic boar is Brahmā, not Viṣṇu.
  - *Variant (Liṅga Purāṇa 1.4.69ff, as translated in Rao, Elements of Hindu Iconography I.1 p. 130):* in its cosmogony the *Liṅga* also makes **Brahmā** take the boar form and replace the flooded Earth, though at 1.94 it gives the Viṣṇu-boar who kills Hiraṇyākṣa. Sanskrit not checked.
  - *Note (disputed lineage):* whether the classical Viṣṇu-Varāha is a direct development of the Brāhmaṇa Prajāpati-boar or an independent tribal/folk boar-cult absorbed into Vaiṣṇavism is debated in scholarship; the texts themselves simply equate them. [disputed] [scholarly]

## II. The cosmogonic Varāha — the Vārāha / Śveta-Varāha Kalpa

- **The kalpa named for the boar** — The present **kalpa** (Brahmā's day) is the **Vārāha kalpa**: the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* says the past kalpa was the Pādma and "this kalpa is called Vārāha" (*VP 1.3.28*). The *Bhāgavata* says it is "known as Vārāha, in which Hari became a boar" (*BhP 3.11.36*). At its dawn Viṣṇu took the boar form to retrieve the Earth submerged after the night of dissolution (*VP 1.4.1–8*). The fuller name **Śveta-Varāha-Kalpa**, "the White-Boar aeon," is the form used in the ritual *saṅkalpa* formula; neither verse uses the *śveta-* prefix, and its Purāṇic locus is not verified. — cosmic placement underlies [Pṛthu & Pṛthvī](prithu-prithvi.md), set "in the Śveta-Varāha-Kalpa."
- **The Earth sunk in the waters** — At the start of a new creation the **Earth (Bhūmi / Pṛthvī)** lies sunk in the **cosmic waters**. In the *Bhāgavata*, **Svāyambhuva Manu** asks Brahmā for a place for his offspring, since the Earth is "merged in the great water" (*mahāmbhasi*); Brahmā reflects that while he was creating she was flooded and went down to the depths (*rasā*) (*BhP 3.13.14–17*). In the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* the Lord infers that the Earth lies within the one ocean (*VP 1.4.7*). ("Garbhodaka Ocean" is Prabhupāda's gloss, e.g. at BhP 7.1.41, not a word of the verse.)
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata, Vana-parva — Ganguli 3.141):* Lomaśa tells Yudhiṣṭhira that in the **Kṛta age**, when the primeval god took over **Yama's** office, nothing died. Living beings multiplied until the Earth, crushed by the weight, **sank a hundred yojanas** and asked Nārāyaṇa for help. He became a **one-tusked boar** and raised her a hundred yojanas on his single tusk. Here the cause is overpopulation: there is no deluge and no demon. (Critical-edition locus not verified.)
- **The boar from Brahmā's nostril** — As Brahmā ponders, a tiny boar "no bigger than the tip of a thumb" (*aṅguṣṭha-parimāṇa*) comes out of his **nostril**. In a moment it stands in the sky as large as an elephant (*gaja-mātraḥ*, 3.13.19); Brahmā, marvelling, recalls it as thumb-sized and "in a moment like a boulder" (*kṣaṇād gaṇḍa-śilā-samaḥ*, 3.13.22) — a restatement, not a further stage — and the Lord, "like a great mountain" (*agendra-sannibhaḥ*), roars (3.13.23). The sages of Janaloka, Tapoloka and Satyaloka praise him with chants from the three Vedas. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.13.18–26*)
- **The cosmic dive** — The boar plunges into the waters, tracking the Earth **by smell** (3.13.28), splitting the ocean (3.13.29), and lifts her on his **tusk (daṃṣṭra)** (3.13.31). She rests on his white tusk-tip like a lotus held by an elephant (3.13.33, 3.13.40) — the iconic image of Varāha with Bhūdevī on his tusk. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.13.28–40; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.4.25–26, 1.4.36*)
  - *Note (compact Bhāgavata tellings, not variants):* in the same chapter the rising boar kills a mace-wielding daitya in the water "as a lion kills an elephant" (*BhP 3.13.31–32*). Elsewhere he rips the *ādi-daitya* with his tusk (*BhP 2.7.1*), and BhP 1.3.7 lists Varāha as the **second** of its avatāras, raising the Earth from *rasātala*.
- **The Pṛthvī-stuti and the sages' hymn** — In the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* the Earth praises the boar **when she sees him arrive in Pātāla** (*pātālatalam āgatam*), before the lift (*VP 1.4.11–24*). He answers with a Sāman-toned roar, rises "like a great blue mountain" (1.4.25–26), and is then hymned by Sanandana and the yogins of Janaloka (1.4.30–44). The *Bhāgavata* has **no hymn by the Earth**; its parallel is the ṛṣis' hymn (*BhP 3.13.34–45*). The lift ends with the Earth set on the waters "like a great vessel," levelled, given mountains and divided into the seven *dvīpas*, re-founding the world's geography (*VP 1.4.45–49*).

## III. The Yajña-Varāha allegory — sacrifice as the boar's body

- **The boar as the embodied sacrifice** — Both Purāṇas develop a **yajña-varāha** allegory, each with its own set of correspondences.
  - **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** (the Janaloka yogins' hymn): his feet are the Vedas, his tusks the sacrificial post (*yūpa*), the sacrifices (*yajña*) are in his teeth, his mouth is the fire-altar (*citi*), his tongue the fire, his body-hair the darbha grass. Day and night are his eyes, his head is the supreme Brahman, the refuge of all, all the Vedic hymns (*sūkta*) are his mane, and all oblations are his nostrils. The ladle is his snout, the Sāman chant his deep voice, the *prāgvaṃśa* hall his body, all the *satra*-rites his joints. (*VP 1.4.32–34*; English after Wilson / Dutt)
  - **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (the ṛṣis' hymn) gives a different set: the Vedic metres are in his skin, kuśa grass in his bristles, ghee in his eyes, the four priestly offices in his four legs. The *sruk* ladle is his snout, the *sruva* his nostrils, the *iḍā* his belly, the *camasa* cups his ear-holes, the soma-vessels his throat, his chewing the *agnihotra*. The *prāyaṇīya* and *udayanīya* rites are his tusks, the *pravargya* his tongue, and soma his seed. (*BhP 3.13.35–38*; Prabhupāda's English renders several of these terms loosely) [scholarly]
- **Theology of the allegory** — The reading makes the lifting of the Earth a **cosmic sacrifice** that sustains the world: Varāha is *yajña-mūrti*, the personified rite by which order (*ṛta/dharma*) is upheld. The *Bhāgavata* keeps returning to the epithet: "his boar-body made of all sacrifices" (*sakala-yajña-mayī*, 2.7.1), "the sacrifice-boar" (*yajña-sūkara*, 3.19.9). This is the avatāra's distinctive doctrinal signature, paralleling Narasiṃha's *dveṣa-bhakti* and Vāmana's *dāna*.

## IV. The deliverance Varāha — the slaying of Hiraṇyākṣa (SB 3.17–19)

- **The daitya twins** — **Hiraṇyākṣa** ("golden-eyed") and **Hiraṇyakaśipu** are the twin *daitya* sons of **Diti** by **Kaśyapa**, conceived when Diti pressed Kaśyapa at the **evening twilight** (*BhP 3.14.8ff*). Kaśyapa foretells that the Lord himself will kill them (3.14.39–41), and the embryos' power darkens the worlds and alarms the gods (3.15.1–3). They are the first demonic birth of Viṣṇu's doorkeepers **Jaya and Vijaya**, cursed by the Kumāras (3.15.34–36; Viṣṇu ratifies the curse at 3.16.26, and Brahmā says the two have entered Diti's womb at 3.16.35). Hiraṇyakaśipu is the elder and Hiraṇyākṣa the younger (*anuja*) (3.17.19–20); the naming verse 3.17.18 is construed variously by translators. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.14–17*) — see [Jaya & Vijaya](jaya-vijaya.md); the brother's cycle is [Prahlāda & Narasiṃha](prahlada-narasimha.md).
- **Hiraṇyākṣa's rampage** — Proud of his strength and of a **boon** (3.17.22; Brahmā later confirms "he has a boon from me," 3.18.23), **Hiraṇyākṣa** roams with his mace looking for a fight. The gods hide from him (3.17.22–23), and for "many years" he beats the ocean's waves (3.17.24–26). He reaches **Vibhāvarī**, the capital of **Varuṇa**, and mockingly challenges him; Varuṇa says only the Ancient Person, Viṣṇu, is a match for him (3.17.26–31). **Nārada** tells him where Viṣṇu is, and he hurries down to the depths (3.18.1). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.17.20–3.18.1*)
- **Custody of the Earth** — In the *Bhāgavata*, Hiraṇyākṣa does **not** carry the Earth off. She sank in the deluge (3.13.15–17), and when he arrives he finds the boar **already bearing her up on his tusks** (3.18.2). He laughs at the "forest beast" (*vana-gocaro mṛgaḥ*; Prabhupāda's gloss: "amphibious beast") and claims that the Earth was "entrusted to us, the dwellers of the lower regions, by the creator" (3.18.3). Varāha ironically owns to having "stolen the charge of the dwellers of Rasātala" (3.18.11). BhP 7.1.41 says only that Hiraṇyākṣa was slain "at the lifting of the Earth" (*dharoddhāre*). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.18.2–11; 7.1.41*)
  - *Variant (Liṅga Purāṇa 1.94.3–9):* Hiraṇyākṣa, here the father of **Andhaka**, defeats the gods, **binds the Earth and takes her to the nether regions as his prisoner**. At the gods' plea Viṣṇu becomes the **Yajña-Varāha** and kills him "with the tip of his curved fangs," then brings the Earth up out of the ocean seated on his lap.
  - *Variant (Agni Purāṇa 4.1–2; 275.10–15):* Hiraṇyākṣa conquers the gods and sits in heaven; praised by the gods, Viṣṇu becomes the sacrifice-boar and kills him and his daityas — no abduction of the Earth. The "Vārāha" war is the **third of the twelve devāsura wars**, in which "Varāha slew Hiraṇyākṣa … and lifted up the Earth."
  - *Note (cosmogonic vs. demonic cause):* the **Vedic/Brāhmaṇa** boar and **VP 1.4** know only the cosmogonic lift, with **no demon at all**, and the **Mahābhārata** (3.141 Ganguli) blames the Earth's sinking on overpopulation. The *Bhāgavata* welds the demon in as an **obstructor** of the lift. The **abduction** that makes the lift a rescue belongs to the *Liṅga* telling and to popular retellings. [scholarly]
- **The duel** — Seeing the frightened Earth on his tusks, the boar rises out of the water with Hiraṇyākṣa chasing him (3.18.6–7). He **sets the Earth on the surface of the waters**, places his own power in her (*adadhāt sva-sattvam*; Prabhupāda's gloss: "the ability to float"), and is praised by Brahmā and showered with flowers — all **before** the fight (3.18.8). The two then trade blows with their maces "like two bulls over a cow" (3.18.14–19). **Brahmā** arrives to watch (3.18.20–21) and urges Viṣṇu to kill the demon before "the most dreadful twilight" brings the demon's hour of strength, since "the muhūrta called *abhijit* has now gone" (*abhijin nāma yogo mauhūrtiko hy agāt*, 3.18.27; "midday" and "most opportune for victory" are Prabhupāda's gloss) (3.18.22–28). The *Bhāgavata* thus sets the **climax** in the afternoon before dusk and gives no long duration for the fight; it does not say when the duel began. The "thousand-year duel" of popular modern retellings (e.g. Britannica's entry) is not in BhP 3.17–19, VP 1.4, Liṅga 1.94 or Agni 4 — locus not verified. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.18*)
- **Hiraṇyākṣa-vadha** — The demon knocks Varāha's mace from his hand but refuses to strike an unarmed foe, and the Lord calls up his **Sudarśana** discus (3.19.2–5). Varāha kicks away the demon's mace with his left foot and catches it on the next throw (3.19.9–11). The discus **cuts the demon's trident** (3.19.13–14) and later dispels his storm of conjured illusions (3.19.17–22), while Diti, far away, feels her heart shudder (3.19.23). Finally Varāha **slaps him at the root of the ear with his hand** (*kareṇa karṇa-mūle*), "as Indra struck Vṛtra" (3.19.25), and he falls like an uprooted tree (3.19.26). Brahmā says he died "struck by the Lord's foot" (*padāhataḥ*) while gazing at his face (3.19.28). The gods give thanks (3.19.30), and Varāha returns to his own abode (3.19.31). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.19*) In the *Liṅga* telling the kill is made with the tusk (1.94.8), as in BhP 2.7.1.
- **The first of three births** — As with Hiraṇyakaśipu under Narasiṃha, Hiraṇyākṣa, slain by Viṣṇu himself, discharges the **first** of the three Jaya–Vijaya births. He is not yet released: Brahmā says the two "will return to their own positions after a few such births" (3.19.29), and they are freed from the curse only in their third birth, as Śiśupāla and Dantavakra (named at 7.1.33; the three births at 7.1.39–46). His brother's grief and rage at the boar's deed opens the [Narasiṃha cycle](prahlada-narasimha.md) (7.2.1). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.19.29; 7.1.41–46; 7.2.1*)
- **Afterlives of the boar-myth** — Two Purāṇas extend the story past the lift. These are sequels, not competing tellings:
  - *Note (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.29.23):* the Earth tells Kṛṣṇa that **Naraka** was begotten on her "by the touch" of the Lord "when I was upheld by thee in the shape of a boar," making Varāha the father of the demon Kṛṣṇa later kills — see [Narakāsura-vadha](narakasura-vadha.md).
  - *Note (Liṅga Purāṇa 1.94.26–32):* when Viṣṇu leaves the boar form, the tusk, pressed down by the Earth's weight, falls away; **Śiva** picks it up and wears it as an ornament on his head and chest.

## V. Iconography and the two clocks

- **Earliest monumental forms** — Varāha is among the earliest avatāras attested in monumental art. The colossal relief at the **Udayagiri Caves (Cave 5), Madhya Pradesh**, dated c. 401 CE from the Gupta-era-82 inscription of the time of Candragupta II in the neighbouring Cave 6, shows the **boar-headed man** (the Bhū- / Nṛ-Varāha type) lifting Bhūdevī, a state-cosmological tableau of the reign. The fully **zoomorphic** form is the free-standing colossal boar at **Eran**, Madhya Pradesh (c. 510 CE; its neck inscription is dated "in the first year" of the Hūṇa king Toramāṇa), its body covered with rows of tiny sages. [scholarly]
- **Mahābalipuram relief** — The **Varāha Maṇḍapa at Mahābalipuram (Tamil Nadu), c. 7th century CE (Pallava)** carries a celebrated panel of Varāha raising the Earth-goddess — among the finest Pallava treatments of the avatāra. [scholarly]
- **Image types** — Rao distinguishes three types (*Elements of Hindu Iconography* I.1, pp. 131–136). The first is **Bhū-Varāha**, also called **Ādi-Varāha** or **Nṛ-Varāha**: a boar-faced, four-armed man with one foot on Ādiśeṣa and Bhūdevī on his bent leg (*Vaikhānasāgama*), on his tusk or in the crook of his elbow (*Śilparatna*). This is the type dominant in monumental sculpture. The *Śilparatna* and *Viṣṇudharmottara* also allow the image to be made **wholly as a boar**, as at Eran. The second type, **Yajña-Varāha**, is seated on a throne with Lakṣmī and Bhūmidevī; the third, **Pralaya-Varāha**, is seated with Bhūmidevī alone. [scholarly]
- **Scriptural clock** — The *Bhāgavata* frames the lift under **Svāyambhuva Manu** at the dawn of the Vārāha kalpa (*BhP 3.13.14–17*), yet tells the Hiraṇyākṣa war as the same descent. Gauḍīya commentators resolve this by positing **two Varāha descents** in one kalpa: the nostril-boar who lifts the Earth in the Svāyambhuva manvantara, and the boar who kills Hiraṇyākṣa in the sixth, **Cākṣuṣa**, manvantara. Prabhupāda's purport to BhP 3.13.31 gives Jīva Gosvāmī's two descents (Svāyambhuva and Cākṣuṣa) and Viśvanātha Cakravartī's view that Maitreya amalgamated them; his purport to BhP 3.14.2 makes the split explicit — the Svāyambhuva boar lifted the Earth and was **white**, the Cākṣuṣa boar killed Hiraṇyākṣa and was **red** (translator's commentary, not the verse). The doctrine is also traced to Rūpa Gosvāmī's *Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta* (verse locus not verified). [late text] Placing Hiraṇyākṣa in **Satya-yuga** comes from Prabhupāda's summary of BhP 7.1 (a translator's gloss), not from the verses checked. A placement in the **Vaivasvata** manvantara is not supported by any verse checked. See [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Historical clock** — Academically there is **no datable anchor**. The demonstrable kernels are the Ṛgvedic boar **Emuṣa** (RV 8.77.10, 1.61.7) and the Saṃhitā/Brāhmaṇa **Prajāpati-boar** (TS 7.1.5; ŚB 14.1.2.11, c. 8th–6th century BCE strata). The Viṣṇu-Varāha emerges by the epic period and the early centuries CE, while the *Rāmāyaṇa* still makes the boar Brahmā. The dedicated **Varāha Purāṇa** is a later Vaiṣṇava compilation. [scholarly]

- *Mahābhārata*, Vana-parva, Ganguli **3.141** (Lomaśa: the Earth sunk a hundred yojanas by overpopulation; the one-tusked boar).
- *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* **2.102.2–3** (critical ed.; Brahmā as the boar). Sanskrit via GRETIL.
- *Varāha Purāṇa* — one of the eighteen **Mahāpurāṇas** (twelfth in VP 3.6.21–23), framed as Varāha's instruction to the Earth-goddess; expands the avatāra's theology and associated tīrthas.

**Vedic / Brāhmaṇa antecedents**
- *Ṛgveda* **8.77.10**, **1.61.7** — the boar **Emuṣa**, brought by Viṣṇu and shot by Indra (Aufrecht text via GRETIL).
- *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* **14.1.2.11** — the boar **Emūṣa** = **Prajāpati** raising the span-sized Earth (Eggeling, SBE 44, p. 451).
- *Taittirīya Saṃhitā* **7.1.5.1** (Prajāpati as wind, then boar, seizes the Earth) and **6.2.4.2–3** (Indra slays the treasure-guarding boar; Viṣṇu as the sacrifice) — Keith's translation, *The Veda of the Black Yajus School*.
- *Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa* 1.1.3.5–6 (locus per Muir, *OST* i.52) and *Taittirīya Āraṇyaka* **10.1.8** — the boar as Prajāpati; "the black boar with a hundred arms."

**Iconography and epigraphy**
