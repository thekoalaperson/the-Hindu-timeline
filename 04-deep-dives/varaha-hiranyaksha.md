# Varāha — the Boar Avatar and the Slaying of Hiraṇyākṣa

> The **third** of the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md): Viṣṇu as the cosmic **boar (varāha)** who dives into the deluge to lift a drowned **Earth (Bhūdevī / Pṛthvī)** on his tusks and slays the daitya **Hiraṇyākṣa** in a thousand-year underwater duel.
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Twin-cycle peers: [Prahlāda & Narasiṃha](prahlada-narasimha.md) · [Vāmana & Bali](vamana-bali.md) · cosmogonic kin: [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md) · the milked Earth: [Pṛthu & Pṛthvī](prithu-prithvi.md).

The **Varāha** descent is told most fully in the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Skandha 3** — the cosmogony of **Maitreya to Vidura**, with the boar's emergence at **SB 3.13** and the war with Hiraṇyākṣa at **SB 3.17–19** — and in compact form in **Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.4** and across the **Varāha Purāṇa**. It is unusual among the avatāras for fusing two distinct registers: an older **cosmogonic** boar who raises the Earth at the dawn of a *kalpa*, and the later **deliverance** narrative of a specific demon-slaying. It is the standalone peer of [Narasiṃha](prahlada-narasimha.md) and [Vāmana](vamana-bali.md), and — uniquely — it lends its name to the present cosmic day, the **Śveta-Varāha-Kalpa**.

---

## I. Vedic and Brāhmaṇa antecedents — the boar before the avatāra

- **Emūṣa, the cosmic boar** — The earliest clear boar-cosmogony appears in the **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa (14.1.2.11)**: the Earth, at first only "so large as the span of a hand," is raised from the waters by a boar named **Emūṣa**, here identified with **Prajāpati**. The boar is not yet Viṣṇu but the creator-principle itself. (*Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 14.1.2.11*) [scholarly]
- **Taittirīya cosmogonic boar** — The **Taittirīya Saṃhitā (7.1.5)** and **Taittirīya Āraṇyaka (10.1.8)** preserve the same motif: a black boar (*varāha*) called **Emūṣa** or **Emūṣaḥ** lifts the earth out of the primeval waters; the **Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa** likewise frames the boar as **Prajāpati** assuming the form to draw up the soaked earth. (*Taittirīya Saṃhitā 7.1.5; Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10.1.8*) [scholarly]
- **Identification shift Prajāpati → Viṣṇu** — Across the epic-Purāṇic transition the Brāhmaṇa boar (Prajāpati/Brahmā) is re-read as a form of **Viṣṇu / Nārāyaṇa**; the *Bhāgavata* even has the boar emerge **from Brahmā's nostril**, narratively preserving the older creator-boar association while making him Viṣṇu's avatāra. [scholarly]
  - *Note (disputed lineage):* whether the classical Viṣṇu-Varāha is a direct development of the Brāhmaṇa Prajāpati-boar or an independent tribal/folk boar-cult absorbed into Vaiṣṇavism is debated in scholarship; the texts themselves simply equate them. [disputed] [scholarly]

## II. The cosmogonic Varāha — the Śveta-Varāha-Kalpa

- **The kalpa named for the boar** — The present **kalpa** (Brahmā's day, the cosmic age in which we live) is the **Śveta-Varāha-Kalpa**, "the White-Boar aeon," named because at its dawn Viṣṇu took the boar form to retrieve the Earth submerged after the night of dissolution. The avatāra thus inaugurates the cosmic day itself. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.3–4; Purāṇic kalpa-lists*) — cosmic placement underlies [Pṛthu & Pṛthvī](prithu-prithvi.md), set "in the Śveta-Varāha-Kalpa."
- **The Earth sunk in the waters** — At the start of a new creation (or after the deluge) the **Earth (Bhūmi / Pṛthvī)** lies sunk in the **cosmic ocean (the Garbhodaka / the lower waters)**; Brahmā, ready to recreate, cannot proceed while she is submerged. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.4; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.13*)
- **The boar from Brahmā's nostril** — As Brahmā ponders the problem, a tiny boar — "the size of a thumb" — issues from his **nostril**, then grows to the size of a mountain/elephant and at last to a colossal cosmic form filling the sky, roaring so that the *ṛṣis* and the gods praise him with Vedic hymns. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.13.18–34*)
- **The cosmic dive** — The boar plunges into the waters, scenting out the Earth by smell, and bears her up on his **tusks (daṃṣṭra)**, the cosmic ocean parting around him as he rises — the iconic image of Varāha with Bhūdevī clinging to or seated on his tusk. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.13.29–45; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.4*)
- **The Bhū-stuti / Pṛthvī's hymn** — As she is lifted, the Earth-goddess praises Varāha; the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*'s "Pṛthvī-stuti" and the *Bhāgavata*'s parallel are the doctrinal heart of the cosmogonic version, re-establishing her on the waters and re-founding the world's geography. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.4*) [scholarly]

## III. The Yajña-Varāha allegory — sacrifice as the boar's body

- **The boar as the embodied sacrifice** — The *Bhāgavata* and *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* develop a sustained **yajña-varāha** allegory: every part of the boar's body is a component of the Vedic **sacrifice (yajña)**. His feet are the Vedas, his tusk the sacrificial post (*yūpa*), his teeth the offerings, his mouth the altar, his tongue the fire, his bristles the *kuśa* grass, his head the seat of the priest, his breath the *Sāman* chant. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.4; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.13.34–44*) [scholarly]
- **Theology of the allegory** — The reading makes the lifting of the Earth a **cosmic sacrifice** that sustains the world: Varāha is *yajña-mūrti*, the personified rite by which order (*ṛta/dharma*) is upheld. This is the avatāra's distinctive doctrinal signature, paralleling Narasiṃha's *dveṣa-bhakti* and Vāmana's *dāna*.

## IV. The deliverance Varāha — the slaying of Hiraṇyākṣa (SB 3.17–19)

- **The daitya twins** — **Hiraṇyākṣa** ("golden-eyed") and **Hiraṇyakaśipu** are the twin *daitya* sons of **Diti** by **Kaśyapa**, born of the twilight conception the gods feared; they are also the first demonic birth of Viṣṇu's cursed doorkeepers **Jaya and Vijaya**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.16–17*) — the brother's cycle is [Prahlāda & Narasiṃha](prahlada-narasimha.md).
- **Hiraṇyākṣa's rampage** — Boon-emboldened, **Hiraṇyākṣa** terrorises the three worlds, challenges **Varuṇa** in the ocean for battle, and is directed by Varuṇa to seek out Viṣṇu; mace in hand, he ranges the cosmic deep looking for a fight. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.17*)
- **The Earth dragged into the deep** — Hiraṇyākṣa seizes the **Earth (Bhūmi)** and drags her down into the **lower waters / Rasātala**, the act that prompts (or merges with) Viṣṇu's boar-descent. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.17–18*)
  - *Variant (cosmogonic vs. demonic cause):* the **cosmogonic** strand has the Earth simply *sunk* after dissolution and Varāha raising her as a creative act; the **deliverance** strand has Hiraṇyākṣa *abduct* her, making the lift a rescue and the duel its climax. The *Bhāgavata* welds the two; the **Vedic/Brāhmaṇa** boar knows only the cosmogonic lift, with **no demon at all**. [scholarly]
- **The thousand-year duel** — Varāha, carrying the Earth upward, is confronted by Hiraṇyākṣa, who mocks the boar and hurls his mace; the two fight a colossal **mace-and-grapple battle** said to last a **thousand years**, with Brahmā arriving to warn Viṣṇu to finish before the demon's strength peaks at dusk. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.18*)
- **Hiraṇyākṣa-vadha** — Varāha sets the Earth safely on the waters, then strikes **Hiraṇyākṣa** dead with a blow of his foreleg/paw (or his discus) at the root of the ear, ending the demon as the gods rain flowers; the **lifting of the Earth on the tusks** is completed and she is re-established. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.19*)
- **Liberation through the Lord's hand** — As with Hiraṇyakaśipu under Narasiṃha, Hiraṇyākṣa — slain by Viṣṇu himself — discharges the **first** of the three Jaya–Vijaya births and attains release; his brother's vengeance opens the [Narasiṃha cycle](prahlada-narasimha.md). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.17–19, 7.1*)

## V. Iconography and the two clocks

- **Earliest epigraphic Daśāvatāra form** — Varāha is among the earliest avatāras attested in art and inscription: the colossal **Udayagiri Caves (Cave 5), Madhya Pradesh, c. 401–402 CE (Gupta period)** monumental relief shows the boar-headed god (Nṛ-Varāha, the **theri_anthropomorphic** standing form) lifting Bhūdevī, a state-cosmological tableau of Candragupta II's reign. [scholarly]
- **Mahābalipuram relief** — The **Varāha Maṇḍapa at Mahābalipuram (Tamil Nadu), c. 7th century CE (Pallava)** carries a celebrated panel of Varāha raising the Earth-goddess — among the finest Pallava treatments of the avatāra. [scholarly]
- **Two forms** — Iconography distinguishes the **Yajña-/Ādi-Varāha** (fully zoomorphic boar) from the **Nṛ-Varāha / Pralaya-Varāha** (boar-headed man), the latter dominant in monumental sculpture; Bhūdevī appears small on the tusk or seated in the crook of the elbow. [scholarly]
- **Scriptural clock** — Cyclically the demon-slaying sits in the **Satya-yuga** demon-cycle of the present (Vaivasvata) Manvantara — the same age as Hiraṇyakaśipu/Narasiṃha — while the **cosmogonic** boar belongs to the *dawn* of the whole **Śveta-Varāha-Kalpa**, before the manvantaras unfold. See [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Historical clock** — Academically there is **no datable anchor**; the Brāhmaṇa boar (Emūṣa/Prajāpati) is the demonstrable kernel (c. 8th–6th century BCE strata), the Viṣṇu-Varāha emerges by the early centuries CE, and the dedicated **Varāha Purāṇa** is a later Vaiṣṇava compilation. [scholarly]

## Sources

**Primary scripture**
- *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (Śrīmad Bhāgavatam), **Skandha 3** — the boar's emergence from Brahmā's nostril and the cosmic lift (**3.13**), the daitya twins' birth (3.16–17), Hiraṇyākṣa's rampage and the abduction of the Earth (3.17–18), and the thousand-year duel and **Hiraṇyākṣa-vadha** (3.19). Narrated by Maitreya to Vidura.
- *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* **1.4** — the compact cosmogonic Varāha: the sunken Earth, the boar-lift, the Pṛthvī-stuti, and the **yajña-varāha** allegory of the boar's body as the sacrifice.
- *Varāha Purāṇa* — the Vaiṣṇava Upapurāṇa framed as Varāha's instruction to the Earth-goddess; expands the avatāra's theology and associated tīrthas.
