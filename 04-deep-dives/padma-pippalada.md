# Padmā and Pippalāda — Dharma tests the sage's young wife

> Cosmic address: a *pativratā-māhātmya* embedded in the **Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra-saṃhitā, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa** (chs. 34–35), told during the appeasement of **Himavān** to persuade him to give young **Pārvatī** to the ascetic **Śiva**. The exemplum sits inside the timeless Śaiva divine-biography of the present **[7th — Vaivasvata — Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)**, though the tale it tells reaches across all four yugas via the curse on Dharma.
> ⬆ Parent index: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Master index: [README](../README.md) · Text map: [Śiva Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/shiva-purana.md) · Frame: [Satī, Dakṣa & Pārvatī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)

When **Himavān** hesitates to marry his daughter **Pārvatī** to Śiva — an ascetic who appeared before him aged, naked-limbed, smeared with ash and terrifying — the **[Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md)**, with **Vasiṣṭha** as spokesman, argue that a wife's fidelity, not a husband's outward form, is what governs a marriage's fortune. Their proof-text is the story of **Padmā**, a king's radiant daughter forced upon a repellent, aged brahmin, whose unbroken chastity turns the seeming misfortune into blessing — including, at its climax, restoring the old sage's youth. It is a two-chapter unit: ch. 34 tells how Padmā comes to be married to **Pippalāda**; ch. 35 tells how **Dharma himself**, in disguise, tests her and is cursed.

---

## Where it sits — the two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic frame** — The *narration* is a set-piece of the Pārvatī-marriage cycle, treated by the corpus as **timeless Śaiva divine-biography** (the same stratum as the [Kāma-dahana and the wedding of Śiva and Pārvatī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)). The *tale itself* is deliberately trans-yugic: its hinge is Padmā's curse that fixes how **Dharma loses a leg each yuga**, so it belongs to the recurring, cross-yuga machinery of cosmic decline rather than to a single dated reign. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Rudra-saṃhitā, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa 34–35)
- **Academic / historical frame** — The *Śiva Purāṇa* in its surviving seven-*saṃhitā* form is a **[scholarly] c. 10th–14th century CE** compilation of older Śaiva material (the Rudra-saṃhitā being its narrative heart); the E. tr. used here is the Śāstrī / Motilal Banarsidass *Ancient Indian Tradition and Mythology* edition. The dharma-as-four-legged-bull etiology it deploys is far older, canonical already in **Manusmṛti 1.81–86** and the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (1.16–17). See the text map at [Śiva Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/shiva-purana.md).

## Chapter 34 — how Padmā is married to Pippalāda

- **King Anaraṇya and his daughter Padmā** — A king **Anaraṇya**, in the line descended from the Manu **Indra-Sāvarṇi**, a devoted worshipper of Śiva who ruled the seven continents, has a hundred sons and one daughter, **Padmā**, "as charming as the goddess Lakṣmī." He loves the daughter above all his sons and sends out **letters of invitation seeking a worthy bridegroom** for her. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa 34)
- **Pippalāda inflamed** — The aged sage **Pippalāda**, returning to his hermitage, meets a **lustful Gandharva** and is himself overcome by a sudden longing for marriage; catching sight of the young Padmā, he learns from bystanders that she is Anaraṇya's daughter and resolves to have her despite his years. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa 34)
- **The threat of ash** — Pippalāda goes to the court and demands Padmā's hand. When the king recoils at giving his lovely daughter to so old and severe a brahmin, the sage threatens to **"reduce everything to ashes"** — king, kin, and kingdom. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa 34)
- **The preceptor's counsel** — Anaraṇya's family-priest and preceptor advise surrender, on the maxim that **"the wise man saves everything by abandoning that one object or person"** when refusal means annihilation. The terrified king gives Padmā, **bedecked in ornaments**, to Pippalāda in marriage "according to the holy rites." (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa 34)
- **Anaraṇya's renunciation** — Grief-stricken at parting with his favourite child, Anaraṇya abandons his throne, retires to the forest for penance, and in the end **attains Śivaloka, free of all ailments**; his eldest son **Kīrtimān** takes the throne and rules righteously. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa 34)

## Chapter 35 — Dharma's test and the curse

- **Padmā the model wife** — At the hermitage, the young Padmā **serves the aged Pippalāda devoutly in body, mind, and speech, "as Lakṣmī serves Viṣṇu,"** while he continues his penances and holy rites in forest and mountain; the couple pass their days content. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa 35)
- **Dharma comes disguised as a lustful king** — The deity **Dharma** (Virtue personified), to *test* her fidelity, takes the **guise of a handsome, lustful king** and accosts Padmā as she goes to bathe in the **celestial river**. He praises her beauty and urges her to **"cast off that ruthless old brahmin forever engaged in penances"** and come to him. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa 35)
  - *Variant (the river's name):* a parallel Śaiva telling names the stream **Puṣpabhadrā**; the Pārvatī-khaṇḍa gives only "the celestial river." [disputed] — do not fix the name from the epitome alone.
- **Padmā's refusal** — She rejects him utterly: **"How can I resort to you — lecherous and mad after women — after forsaking the excellent sage Pippalāda, whose body is sanctified by austerities?"** Fidelity to the ascetic outweighs the disguised king's youth and beauty. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa 35)
- **The curse on Dharma — the yuga-legs etiology** — Enraged at the seducer, Padmā **curses him to gradual decline through the ages**. Learning only afterward that she has cursed **Dharma himself**, she does not revoke it but **refines** it: Dharma shall stand on **four legs in Satya (Kṛta) Yuga, lose one leg in each successive age** (three in Tretā, two in Dvāpara), be reduced to **a single leg and near-total weakness in Kali Yuga**, and then **regain his full four-footed perfection in the next Satya Yuga**. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa 35)
  - *Cross-tradition note:* this is one Purāṇic charter for the pan-Hindu image of **dharma as a bull losing a foot per yuga** — canonically stated at **Manusmṛti 1.81–86** and dramatized in the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (Parīkṣit and the one-legged bull, 1.16–17). The Śiva Purāṇa uniquely traces the *mechanism* to a faithful wife's curse. See [yugas & the Mahāyuga](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md). [scholarly]
- **Dharma's boons — youth restored** — Pleased rather than offended, delighted by her proven chastity, Dharma reveals himself and grants blessings: **Pippalāda is made young, vigorous, and handsome**; **Padmā is made eternally youthful**; the couple are given **ten exceptionally virtuous sons** and **boundless prosperity** — "all kinds of riches were granted to the couple." (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa 35)
- **The frame's moral** — Vasiṣṭha and the seven sages close by drawing the lesson for Himavān: a wife's unwavering devotion **transcends a husband's outward age or fearsomeness and compels divine favour** — therefore he should not fear to give Pārvatī to the ascetic Śiva. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa 35–36)

## Disambiguation — two collisions the corpus flags

**Which Anaraṇya?** — There are (at least) two distinct kings of this name; do not merge them.
- *This Anaraṇya (Śiva Purāṇa):* father of Padmā, of the line of the Manu **Indra-Sāvarṇi**, a Śiva-devotee ruling the seven continents, who yields his daughter to Pippalāda and retires to Śivaloka. A minor figure known only from this episode. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa 34)
- *The other Anaraṇya (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa):* an **Ikṣvāku / Sūryavaṃśa** king of Ayodhyā, descendant of Māndhātṛ, who dies fighting **Rāvaṇa** and curses him that a future scion of his line (Rāma) will slay him — an entirely separate legend, cataloged at [Anaraṇya — the Ikṣvāku king whose curse dooms Rāvaṇa](../03-lineages-vamsha/anaranya-ravana-curse.md). The two share only a name. [scholarly]

**Which Pippalāda?** — The name attaches to three strands; the Śaiva tradition fuses the first two but keeps the third separate.
- *Padmā's husband (this file):* the aged sage tested through his wife by Dharma. Within the Śiva Purāṇa he is treated as the same **orphaned son of [Dadhīci](dadhichi.md)** (reared by pippala trees, hence the name) who appears in the text's Dadhīci/Suvarcā material. (*Śiva Purāṇa*)
- *The Śani-curser (same Śaiva figure, different episode):* Pippalāda, avenging the deaths caused by the gods' demands on his father Dadhīci, **curses Śani (Saturn)** — winning the boon that Saturn shall **not afflict children under sixteen**. Told at [Śiva-avatāras and forms](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-avatars-and-forms.md), with a parallel revenge-cycle (the mare-demon of Śiva's third eye) in the **Brahma Purāṇa's Gautamī-māhātmya**, cataloged at [Gautamī-māhātmya tales](gautami-mahatmya-tales-catalog.md). Kindred Śani-appeasement lore sits at [Daśaratha's Śani-stotra legend](dasharatha-shani-stotra-legend.md) and [Navagraha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md).
- *The Upaniṣadic teacher (separate, [scholarly]):* **Pippalāda** is also the sage who answers six pupils' questions in the **[Praśna Upaniṣad](../90-literature-corpus/upanishads/prashna-upanishad.md)**, and the eponym of the **Paippalāda** recension of the Atharvaveda. The traditional identification of this teacher with Dadhīci's avenger-son is **devotional, not demonstrable** — a name shared across a Vedic teacher and a Purāṇic hero.

## Motif parallels

- **The tested pativratā whose word bends cosmic law** — Padmā's fidelity, like [Anasūyā's](anasuya-halted-sunrise.md) arrest of the sunrise or [Sāvitrī's](savitri-satyavan.md) recovery of Satyavān from Yama, is a force able to curse a *god* (Dharma) and rewrite the order of the yugas — the standard *satya-saṃkalpa* of the faithful wife. (comparative Purāṇic)
- **The god who tests and is cursed** — Dharma-in-disguise testing a chaste woman and being cursed for it echoes the wider "deity tries the devotee's virtue and is rebuked" pattern; here the curse is not undone but *institutionalized* as the yuga-decline of dharma. (comparative)
- **Youth restored to the aged husband** — The reversal of Pippalāda's old age by the wife's proven chastity is the Śaiva counterpart to [Cyavana rejuvenated by the Aśvins](cyavana-sukanya.md) through Sukanyā's fidelity — old sage + young wife + restored youth as reward for constancy. (comparative)
- **A brahmin who seizes a bride by threat** — Pippalāda's ash-threat to win Padmā belongs to the Purāṇic type of the fearsome ascetic whose tapas-power coerces a king, resolved not by the sage's virtue but by the *wife's*. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa 34)
