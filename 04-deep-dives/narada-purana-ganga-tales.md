# Gaṅgā-Māhātmya Tales of the Nārada Purāṇa — Godhā, the Chaste Lady, Kāṣṭhīlā, Kāmodā, and Vasu

> Cosmic address: a cycle of **salvation-tales embedded in the Uttarabhāga of the Nārada Purāṇa**, narrated by the sage **Vasiṣṭha to King Māndhātā** of the Solar line — so framed in the deep dynastic past of the **28th Mahāyuga** (Vaivasvata Manvantara), while the tales themselves float in legendary, un-pinned antiquity; one heroine (Kāmodā) is born at the primordial churning of the milk-ocean. As literature the whole māhātmya stratum is late-medieval [late text] [scholarly].
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Text-map of the whole Purāṇa: [Nārada Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/narada-purana.md) · The frame-legend's canonical treatment: [Ekādaśī-māhātmyas — the Rukmāṅgada–Mohinī frame](ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md)

The **Uttarabhāga** (82 chapters) of the *Nārada Purāṇa* is built as one long dialogue — **Māndhātā asks, Vasiṣṭha answers** — whose two great set-pieces are the **Rukmāṅgada-carita** (the Ekādaśī legend, chs. ~4–37) and a **tīrtha-māhātmya sequence** (chs. 38–81) headed by the **greatness of the Gaṅgā**. Threaded through both are small, vivid **salvation-tales**: creatures and women fallen into wretched births who are raised to Viṣṇu's world by **transferred merit** — the merit of a single Gaṅgā-confluence bath, a Māgha observance, an Ekādaśī fast. This file is the corpus's canonical home for that inner tale-cycle: **Godhā the lizard, the chaste lady, Kāṣṭhīlā the wood-worm, Kāmodā of the Gaṅgā, and Vasu** the pilgrim-guide. The frame-story of Rukmāṅgada himself is treated in [narada-purana.md](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/narada-purana.md) and [ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md](ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md); this file assumes it and goes inward.

## The nested frames — who is telling whom

- **Outer frame: Vasiṣṭha → Māndhātā** — The Uttarabhāga opens with King **Māndhātā** asking Vasiṣṭha "what fire burns both the dry and damp fuel of terrible sins"; Vasiṣṭha answers with the greatness of the **Ekādaśī** fast, and everything that follows — Yama's complaint, the Rukmāṅgada story, the tīrtha chapters — is his reply. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 1) → the royal listener's own deep-dive: [Māndhātṛ](mandhata.md); the narrator's: [Vasiṣṭha](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md).
- **Inner frame I: the Rukmāṅgada-carita (chs. ~4–37)** — Yama's empty hells, Brahmā's creation of the temptress **Mohinī**, Rukmāṅgada's marriage and the terrible Ekādaśī dilemma over prince **Dharmāṅgada** — inside this story the Godhā, chaste-lady, and Kāṣṭhīlā tales occur as episodes and exempla. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga) [late text]
- **Inner frame II: Vasu → Mohinī (chs. 38–81)** — After the carita's climax Mohinī incurs a curse, **Brahmā intercedes**, and she **regains her physical body** (chs. 35–37); thereafter the **brāhmaṇa Vasu**, her priest-guide, expounds to her the greatness of the tīrthas — Gaṅgā first (chs. 38–43), then [Gayā](../06-sacred-geography/gaya-pitru-tirtha.md) (44–47), [Kāśī](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md) (48–51), [Puruṣottama/Purī](../06-sacred-geography/jagannath-puri-purushottama-kshetra.md) (52–61), [Prayāga](../06-sacred-geography/prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md) (62–63), Kurukṣetra, [Gaṅgādvāra/Haridvāra](../06-sacred-geography/haridwar-rishikesh-ganga-dwara.md) (66), [Badarikāśrama](../06-sacred-geography/badrinath-badarikashrama-kshetra.md) (67), **Kāmodā** (68), and onward to [Mathurā–Vṛndāvana](../06-sacred-geography/mathura-vrindavan-braj.md) (79–80), closing with Vasu's own story (81). (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 38–81, Motilal/AITM vol. 5 chapter titles) [late text]

## Godhā, the lizard crushed by the king's horse (Uttarabhāga 14)

- **The accident at the mountain's foot** — As Rukmāṅgada and his new bride Mohinī descend from **Mount Mandara** toward the plain, the king's **horse crushes a she-lizard (godhā)** while pawing the earth; the compassionate king revives the broken creature with water. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 13–14)
- **Her confession: sorcery against a husband** — The lizard, given speech, tells her past: she was a **brāhmaṇa's wife in the city of Śākala** who, neglected by her husband, obtained powders and charms from an ascetic woman and worked a **vaśīkaraṇa (subjugation) rite** on him; the drugged husband wasted with disease. Though she later procured a cure, the sorcery damned her. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 14)
- **The tariff of the sin** — For bewitching her own husband she was cooked in the hell **Tāmrabhrāṣṭra** ("the copper frying-pan") for **fifteen yugas**, then sentenced by Yama to a lizard's body — in which she has crawled for **ten thousand years**. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 14)
- **Mohinī argues against mercy** — Mohinī, true to her Brahmā-made office of vow-breaker, urges the king to abandon "this sinful creature who had been wicked to her own husband," warning that aiding sinners drags down giver and receiver alike — the cycle's recurring foil-voice against grace. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 14)
- **Salvation by one bath's merit** — Rukmāṅgada overrules her and formally gives away his merit of the **Vijayā (Dvādaśī) rite** — a fast-and-bath observance under the **Śravaṇa asterism at the confluence of Gaṅgā and Sarayū**, reckoned equal to a twelve-year fast. The instant the gift is spoken, the lizard becomes **a celestial lady in divine ornaments** and ascends to Viṣṇu's realm. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 14) → the rivers: [sacred rivers & descent myths](../06-sacred-geography/sacred-rivers-and-descent-myths.md), [Sarayū](../06-sacred-geography/sarayu-ayodhya-river.md).

## The chaste lady — the courtesan-keeper's wife (Uttarabhāga 16)

- **Setting: Dharmāṅgada honours Mohinī** — Prince **Dharmāṅgada** receives his father's dangerous new queen with total deference and instructs his own mother that a wife must serve her husband's beloved **without jealousy**, on pain of hell; the tale of the chaste lady is told as the exemplum. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 15–16)
- **The faithful wife of a dissolute man** — A chaste **śūdra woman's** husband squanders himself on a **prostitute**; when he is stricken with a loathsome **fistula/wasting disease**, the courtesan abandons him. The wife borrows money, nurses him through the illness, and dresses his wounds herself. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 16)
- **Her fire, their heaven** — At his death she **follows him onto the funeral pyre**, and by the power of her devotion **both husband and wife ascend to heaven** — the sinner saved by the spouse's virtue, the tale's inverted mirror of Godhā (who destroyed a husband and fell). (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 16)
  - *Caution:* this is a **pativratā/sati exemplum of the late-medieval vrata literature**, prescriptive for its own age; the corpus records it as the text's teaching, not as pan-Hindu norm. [late text]

## Kāṣṭhīlā, the worm in the wood (Uttarabhāga 27–31)

- **Sandhyāvalī and the crow** — Queen **Sandhyāvalī** (Rukmāṅgada's first wife) rescues a **wood-worm (kāṣṭhīlā)** from a crow's beak; the grateful creature speaks and unwinds her long past-life story across five chapters. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 27)
- **The wife who refused her wealth** — In her former birth she was a proud, affluent **brāhmaṇa's wife** who, when her husband fell into vice and poverty, **refused him her ornaments and wealth**; the text's maxim: the woman who withholds life or wealth from her husband is degraded through a hundred births. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 27, 31)
- **The husband's wonder-tale (embedded)** — Her wandering husband's adventures fill the inner chapters: a **rākṣasī** — formerly **Kandalī**, a woman cursed by **Durvāsas** into ogress form — helps him slay the demon **Gobhila** with a divine **śakti** weapon, wins back her benign shape by that righteous act, and becomes his wife; a maiden **Ratnāvalī**, brought from **Vārāṇasī** ("the abode of Tripurāri, where those who die are not reborn"), becomes his second wife; riding the shape-shifted ogress he reaches the city of **Mahodaya (Kanauj)**. A full **praise of Kāśī** interrupts as its own chapter. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 28–30) → [Kāśī-Vārāṇasī](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md).
- **Death, hell, and the worm's body** — Reunited with her transformed husband on the **banks of the Gaṅgā**, the first wife dies; for the old refusal she suffers hell and is reborn **kāṣṭhīlā**, a worm doomed to animal wombs thousands of times. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 30)
- **A quarter of Māgha merit sets her free** — The worm teaches Sandhyāvalī that observance in the month of **Māgha** (with its Ekādaśī fast) burns accumulated sin; moved, the queen **formally transfers one-fourth of her own Māgha merit** — and the worm rises in a **divine aerial chariot to Viṣṇu's region**. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 31, "The Glory of the Month of Māgha") → the month-māhātmya genre: [Kārtika–Māgha–Vaiśākha māhātmyas](masa-mahatmyas-kartika-magha-vaishakha.md).

## The Gaṅgā-māhātmya proper — the stage for the last two tales (Uttarabhāga 38–43)

- **Vasu proclaims the river supreme** — To the restored Mohinī, Vasu declares the **Gaṅgā the most excellent of all sacred places**: even the murderer of a brāhmaṇa is sanctified by her waters; **residence on her banks outweighs all other practices**; **a single mouthful of Gaṅgā water equals a horse-sacrifice**. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 38–39)
- **The images of release** — Sins are pictured crying "Alas!" and fleeing as the bather enters; the devotee **sheds sin as a serpent sheds its slough**; the gods' nectar and Gaṅgā water are set side by side. Subsequent chapters give the fruits of bathing at particular reaches, the rules of **charitable gifts** (including the *guḍa-dhenu*, the "molasses-cow"), and the **procedure of Gaṅgā-worship**. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 38–43) → the river's descent legend and pan-textual cult: [sacred rivers & descent myths](../06-sacred-geography/sacred-rivers-and-descent-myths.md).

## Kāmodā, the churning-born maiden of the upper Gaṅgā (Uttarabhāga 68)

- **Born of the milk-ocean** — Vasu tells Mohinī of **Kāmodā**, third of **four divine maidens** who rose at the **churning of the milk-ocean — Ramā (Lakṣmī), Vāruṇī, Kāmodā, Varā**; she is "the beloved of Hari," wedded to Viṣṇu in unbroken meditation rather than in body. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 68) → the churning itself: [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md).
- **Her seat above Gaṅgādvāra** — Kāmodā abides **ten yojanas above Gaṅgādvāra (Haridvāra)** on the Gaṅgā's bank, in a mountain spot reachable only when the snows melt — a rare Purāṇic mapping of a Himalayan Gaṅgā station. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 68) → [Haridvāra / Gaṅgā-dvāra](../06-sacred-geography/haridwar-rishikesh-ganga-dwara.md).
- **Tears that become lotuses** — Each spring (the text names the **twelfth of Caitra**) Kāmodā, meditating on Viṣṇu, **laughs with delight, and her tears of joy fall into the Gaṅgā as fragrant yellow lotuses**; whoever gathers them from the stream and **worships Lord Śaṅkara (Śiva) with those flowers** attains his cherished desires — a quietly non-sectarian twist in a Vaiṣṇava text. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 68)
- **The twelve-syllable discipline** — Bathing there and repeating the **twelve-syllable (dvādaśākṣara) mantra** for a year wins Viṣṇu's favour; sustained for **twelve years**, the practice yields the direct **vision of Kāmodā** herself. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 68)

## Vasu, the guide who earned Vṛndāvana (Uttarabhāga 81)

- **The narrator's own salvation** — The cycle closes on the teller: **Vasu**, the brāhmaṇa priest who has guided Mohinī through the whole tīrtha-map, counsels her to go and *live* what has been glorified — to make the pilgrimages herself. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 81)
  - *Note on his rank:* the Motilal/AITM translation presents Vasu as Mohinī's **brāhmaṇa priest-guide**; retellings that style him "King Vasu" are conflating him with other Vasus of epic-purāṇic record. [disputed]
- **The boon of Vraja** — Reporting Mohinī's course to **Brahmā**, Vasu receives as boon **residence in Vṛndāvana**; he performs austerities there **more than five thousand years**, until the Lord (**Kṛṣṇa**) appears and grants his wish — to remain in Vṛndāvana forever. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 81) → [Mathurā–Vṛndāvana / Braj](../06-sacred-geography/mathura-vrindavan-braj.md).
- **Nārada seals the cycle** — The sage **Nārada** visits Vasu in Vṛndāvana and recounts to him the deeds Kṛṣṇa will work on earth; delighted, Vasu settles permanently in the cowherds' **Vraja** — the tīrtha-teacher become tīrtha-dweller, awaiting the avatāra. The final chapter (82) then praises the fruit of hearing the *Nārada Purāṇa* itself. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga 81–82) → [Nārada](narada.md).

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural/cyclic frame** — The dialogue is set at the court of **Māndhātā**, deep in the Ikṣvāku past of the present (Vaivasvata) manvantara; **Kāmodā's birth** belongs to the primordial **churning of the milk-ocean**; **Vasu's five-thousand-year vigil** is written as ending on the eve of **Kṛṣṇa's Dvāpara-close descent**. The Godhā, chaste-lady, and Kāṣṭhīlā tales are exempla in legendary antiquity, deliberately un-pinned to any yuga.
- **Academic/historical frame [scholarly]** — The *Nārada Purāṇa* is late and stratified: R. C. Hazra argued a **9th-century core** with accretions before the 11th century, while H. H. Wilson placed the extant text as late as the **16th–17th century**; the Uttarabhāga's tīrtha- and vrata-māhātmyas, with their developed **merit-transfer economy** (a bath's merit gifted to a lizard, a quarter-share of Māgha merit deeded to a worm), are characteristic **medieval Vaiṣṇava pilgrimage-and-vrata literature**. [scholarly] [late text]
