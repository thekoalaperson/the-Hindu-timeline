# Nārada-moha — the Monkey-Face Svayaṃvara & Nārada's Curse on Viṣṇu

> **Where this sits:** A deep-dive into the **Nārada-moha** ("the deluding of Nārada") frame-story that opens the *Śiva Purāṇa*'s **Rudra-saṃhitā, Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa (chs. 2–4)** — the Śaiva etiology of the **Rāma avatāra**. On the cosmic clock the episode sits **before Rāma's descent in [Tretā Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current 28th Mahāyuga; the *text* recording it is a late-Kali compilation (c. 10th–14th c. CE [scholarly]).
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./) · Up to [master index](../README.md)

The story in one line: **[Nārada](narada.md)**, swollen with pride at having "defeated Kāma," is humbled by Viṣṇu's māyā — given a **monkey's face** at the illusory svayaṃvara of princess **Śrīmatī** in King **Śīlanidhi's** phantom city — and in his fury **curses Viṣṇu to a mortal birth, to separation from his wife, and to dependence on monkeys**: the three signatures of the Rāma story. The *Śiva Purāṇa* uses the tale to launch its own teaching frame (the chastened Nārada goes to Brahmā to hear Śiva-tattva, and that dialogue *is* the Rudra-saṃhitā), while **Tulsīdās** retells it in the *Rāmcaritmānas* Bāl-Kāṇḍ as a cause of Rāma's descent — making this one of the most widely known "why did Viṣṇu become Rāma?" stories in North India.

---

## I. The penance and the false conquest of Kāma (Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa ch. 2)

- **Nārada's tapas in the Himalayan cave** — Nārada undertakes a great penance in a Himālayan cave beside the flowing Gaṅgā, entering deep samādhi. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Rudra-saṃhitā, Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa 2)
- **Indra's alarm; Kāma dispatched** — **Indra**, fearing as ever for his throne, sends **Kāmadeva** with Spring (Vasanta) to break the sage's austerity with the arts of desire. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 2) — on Kāma's standard role and arsenal see [Kāmadeva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/kamadeva-and-minor-deities.md).
- **Kāma fails — by Śiva's grace, not Nārada's power** — every art of Kāma is inert at that spot: it is hallowed ground associated with **Śiva's own tapas and the burning of Kāma** (see the Kāma-dahana within [Tārakāsura-vadha](tarakasura-vadha.md)), and by Śiva's boon desire has no purchase there. Kāma retreats defeated. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 2)
- **The seed of the story: pride (garva)** — Nārada, ignorant of the place's secret, concludes that **he has conquered lust by his own merit**. The text is explicit that this delusion is itself the working of **Śiva's māyā**, set in motion to teach the sage. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 2)

## II. The boast — Śiva's warning ignored

- **Nārada boasts to Śiva** — the sage goes straight to Śiva and narrates his "victory" over Kāma. Śiva, who holds Nārada dear, counsels him gently but firmly: **"Do not repeat this tale to anyone — above all, never tell it to Viṣṇu"** (in some renderings: not to Viṣṇu or Brahmā, even if asked). (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 2)
- **The advice disregarded** — deluded, Nārada finds the counsel unpalatable. He goes to **Brahmā**, his father, and boasts; then to **Vaikuṇṭha**, where he tells the whole story to **Viṣṇu** with evident self-satisfaction. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 2)
- **Viṣṇu resolves to humble him** — Viṣṇu, meditating on Śiva, perceives the sage's disease of pride and — like a physician who hurts to heal — resolves to cure it by māyā. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 2–3)

## III. The phantom city — Śīlanidhi's court and Śrīmatī's svayaṃvara (Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa ch. 3)

- **The illusory city on the road** — as Nārada travels on, Viṣṇu's māyā conjures across his path a **vast and splendid city**, rivalling the celestial capitals, ruled by King **Śīlanidhi**. A **svayaṃvara** (bride's-choice assembly) is being prepared for the king's daughter, and princes fill the city. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 3)
- **Śrīmatī** — the princess, **Śrīmatī**, is of such beauty that Nārada — the sage who just "conquered Kāma" — is smitten at first sight. Received with honor by Śīlanidhi and asked to read the girl's fortune, Nārada silently determines that **he himself must win her**: whoever weds her, he perceives, will enjoy incomparable fortune. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 3)
- **The fatal request: "give me your form, Hari"** — unable to win her as a gaunt sage, Nārada hurries to Viṣṇu and begs him for **his own beautiful form** ("give me the form/face of Hari") so that Śrīmatī will garland him. Viṣṇu answers, deliberately double-tongued, that he will do **"what is best for you, as a physician for a patient."** (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 3)
- **The pun that drives the plot** — Viṣṇu grants the request literally on the *other* meaning of ***hari***, which in Sanskrit denotes both **Viṣṇu** and a **monkey**: Nārada receives a splendid body topped with a **monkey's face**, and — the māyā holding — cannot perceive it. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 3)
- **The humiliation at the svayaṃvara** — Nārada seats himself confidently among the assembled kings. Śrīmatī, garland in hand, passes down the rows and **recoils from the monkey-faced suitor**; she garlands instead a resplendent **kingly figure who is Viṣṇu himself**, present within his own māyā, and bride and groom vanish from the assembly. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 3)
- **The two Rudra-gaṇas' taunt** — two attendants (**gaṇas**) of Śiva, present in Brahmin guise, watch the sage strut and laugh at him: *"Go look at your face in a mirror!"* Nārada bends over the water, sees the **monkey face**, and his humiliation flashes into wrath. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 3–4)

## IV. The curses (Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa ch. 4)

- **The curse on the gaṇas** — Nārada first curses the two mocking gaṇas: **"Be born as demons, from a rākṣasa womb!"** They accept the sentence humbly, and the curse stands with the mitigation that it will end and they will return to Śiva's side. The Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa leaves the reborn demons **unnamed**. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 4)
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas; commentarial tradition):* popular tradition, following the Mānas retelling, identifies the two reborn gaṇas with **Rāvaṇa and Kumbhakarṇa**, to be slain and released by Rāma — though the Mānas itself *separately* derives Rāvaṇa's birth from the **Pratāpabhānu** story, so the identification is a harmonization rather than a plain statement of either text. [disputed] (cf. the distinct doorkeeper etiology in [Jaya & Vijaya](jaya-vijaya.md))
- **The curse on Viṣṇu — the three-fold seed of the Rāma avatāra** — Nārada then storms after Viṣṇu, finds him (with Lakṣmī and the maiden at his side), reviles him as a deceiver who torments his own devotees, and pronounces the curse:
  1. **"You shall take birth as a mortal man"** — the human descent;
  2. **"You shall suffer the agony of separation from your wife, grieving for a woman as you have made me grieve"** — Sītā's abduction and Rāma's viraha;
  3. **"Monkeys — creatures of the very form you fastened on me — shall be your companions and helpers"** — the vānara alliance. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 4)
- **Viṣṇu accepts the curse** — Viṣṇu bows to the sage's word and **accepts the curse gladly**, the text presenting his acceptance as providence: the curse becomes the *mechanism* by which the already-intended Rāma descent (mortal birth, wife-separation, monkey allies — see the [Rāmāyaṇa cycle](ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md) and [Hanumān](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md)) is set in scriptural motion. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 4)

## V. The māyā withdrawn — repentance and the Purāṇa's own frame

- **The illusion dissolved** — his purpose served, Viṣṇu (remembering Śiva) withdraws the māyā: princess, bride-price and grievance evaporate; Nārada, restored to himself, is horror-struck at having cursed the Lord, and falls at Viṣṇu's feet begging pardon. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 4)
- **"It was Śiva's māyā"** — Viṣṇu comforts him and re-frames the whole episode in the text's Śaiva theology: the delusion was the working of **Śiva's māyā**, under which Viṣṇu himself also operates, deployed for Nārada's ultimate good — the surgical removal of pride. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 4)
- **The expiation prescribed: Śiva-bhakti** — Nārada asks how to burn away his fault; Viṣṇu directs him to the **worship of Śiva** — singing Śiva's names and glories (the Śatarudriya recitation), devotion to the liṅga, and pilgrimage to Śiva's shrines. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 4)
- **The vanished city; the gaṇas consoled** — journeying back, Nārada finds **no trace of the phantom city**. Meeting the two cursed gaṇas again, he consoles them: the curse will run its course — they will be born as mighty demons, meet their end at the Lord's hands, and **return to Śiva's service**. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 4)
- **The frame-function: launching the Rudra-saṃhitā** — the chastened Nārada then goes to **Brahmā** and asks to be taught the greatness of Śiva; Brahmā's answers **become the narration of the Rudra-saṃhitā itself** (creation, Satī, Pārvatī, Kumāra, the demon-wars). The monkey-face story is thus the *Śiva Purāṇa*'s own on-ramp: pride broken → Śiva-bhakti sought → the Purāṇa taught. (Śiva P., Rudra-s. Sṛṣṭi 4 ff.; see the text-profile [Śiva Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/shiva-purana.md))

## VI. The Rāmcaritmānas retelling (Bāl-Kāṇḍ) [regional]

Tulsīdās retells the whole episode in Awadhi in the **Bāl-Kāṇḍ** of the [*Rāmcaritmānas*](ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md) (composed from 1574 CE — see [Tulsīdās](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/tulsidas.md)), where it is narrated **by Śiva to Pārvatī** as one of the **causes of Rāma's descent** — the channel through which the story became a household tale of the Hindi belt.

- **Same skeleton, Vaiṣṇava-bhakti key** — the Mānas keeps the sequence (Himalayan penance → Kāma's failure → pride → Śiva's warning not to tell Hari → the boast → the illusory city → the monkey face → the mocking gaṇas → the curse on Viṣṇu → repentance), but tunes the theology: the Lord humbles his devotee **out of love**, to destroy the pride that would separate them, and gladly binds himself by the curse for the world's good. (*Rāmcaritmānas*, Bāl-Kāṇḍ)
- *Variant (names):* in the Mānas the king is **Śīlanidhi** but the princess is **Viśvamohinī** ("she who deludes the whole world" — the māyā named outright), not Śrīmatī as in the Śiva Purāṇa. (*Rāmcaritmānas*, Bāl-Kāṇḍ)
- *Variant (whose māyā?):* the Śiva Purāṇa insists the master-illusion is **Śiva's māyā**, with Viṣṇu its instrument; the Mānas — Vaiṣṇava in devotion though narrated by Śiva — treats it as **Hari's own māyā/līlā**. The two framings are cataloged, not merged. [disputed between traditions]
- **The softened curse-ending** — in the Mānas, when Nārada comes to his senses he is aghast; the Lord reassures him, and the curse on the gaṇas is tempered with the promise of **liberation through the Lord** (their demon-birth ending at Rāma's hands). (*Rāmcaritmānas*, Bāl-Kāṇḍ)

## VII. Significance — the avatāra as curse-effect

- **A Śaiva etiology of the Rāma avatāra** — where Vaiṣṇava texts derive Rāma's descent from the Lord's own saṃkalpa (answering the gods' plea against Rāvaṇa; *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, [Bāla Kāṇḍa](ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md)), the Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa derives its three defining features — **mortality, viraha, vānara allies** — from a sage's curse operating inside **Śiva's māyā**. The effect is to fold the greatest Vaiṣṇava story into Śaiva providence [scholarly].
- **One of a family of curse-etiologies** — the corpus preserves several independent "curse explains the avatāra" devices, each attributed and kept distinct: the **[Jaya–Vijaya](jaya-vijaya.md)** doorkeeper curse (Bhāgavata; three demonic births including Rāvaṇa–Kumbhakarṇa), **[Vṛndā's curse](jalandhara-vrinda.md)** on Viṣṇu in the Jālandhara cycle (that his own wife be stolen and he suffer as she did), and this Nārada curse. Purāṇic tradition happily lets all of them stand as concurrent causes.
- **The pride-of-the-devotee motif** — the tale belongs to Nārada's larger characterization (see [Nārada](narada.md)): even the arch-bhakta is not immune to garva, and the Lord's cruelest-seeming trick is diagnosed by the texts as physician's surgery. The instant Kāma is "defeated," Kāma's subtler form — pride, then infatuation — enters by the back door.
- **The hari pun as theology** — the monkey face is not an arbitrary prank: the pun on *hari* (Viṣṇu / monkey) prefigures the curse's third clause and, through it, **Hanumān and the vānaras** — the humiliating mask becomes the emblem of the Lord's future helpers, and (in devotional reading) a hidden honor [folk].
- **Two clocks** — *Scriptural:* the episode precedes and causes the Rāma avatāra, hence sits at the threshold of **Tretā Yuga** of the current 28th Mahāyuga (with Nārada himself a cross-yuga figure). *Academic [scholarly]:* the *Śiva Purāṇa*'s surviving text is dated c. **10th–14th century CE** (oldest layers c. 1000–1100 CE) — i.e., the Śaiva etiology is a late, sectarian re-framing of a first-millennium-BCE-rooted epic; Tulsīdās's Awadhi retelling is precisely datable to **1574 CE onward**. The two frames are kept deliberately distinct.
