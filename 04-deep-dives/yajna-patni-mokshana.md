# Yajña-patnī-mokṣaṇa — the wives of the Vedic brāhmaṇas feed Krishna

> *Where this sits: a short Vṛndāvana līlā of [Kṛṣṇa](./krishna-lila.md) told in **[Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md) 10.23**, set in the closing [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga. It stands between the garment-theft ([vastra-haraṇa](./krishna-lila.md), 10.22) and the [Govardhana-dhāraṇa](./govardhana-dharana.md) (10.24–27) — the Bhāgavata's sharpest set-piece on **bhakti above karma-kāṇḍa**: ritualists refuse the hungry, their wives defy them and are blessed.*
>
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Up to: [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md) · Companions: [Veṇu-gītā](./venu-gita.md) · [Bhāgavata Tales Catalog](./bhagavata-tales-catalog.md) · [Bhakti-rasa & the Bhāvas](../05-concepts-and-dharma/bhakti-rasa-and-the-bhavas.md)

The **Yajña-patnī-mokṣaṇa** — "the deliverance of the sacrificers' wives" — is the twenty-third chapter of the Bhāgavata's tenth canto (SB 10.23, ~48–52 verses), titled in Prabhupāda's rendering *"The Brāhmaṇas' Wives Blessed."* It is a deliberately plain story with a maximal doctrinal payload. Krishna, herding cattle far from home with his cowherd friends, sends the hungry boys to beg food from a company of **brāhmaṇas performing a Vedic sacrifice** nearby; the ritualists, engrossed in the technical correctness of their rite, refuse even to answer. Krishna then sends the boys to the **wives of those same brāhmaṇas** — the *yajña-patnīs* — who, at the mere sound of his name, abandon the sacrifice, seize the offering-food, and rush to him past the forbidding of husbands, fathers, brothers and kin. Krishna praises their love as the highest attainment, feeds on their gift, and sends them home; the humiliated brāhmaṇas then repent, condemning their own birth, learning and ritual expertise as worthless beside the pure devotion of their wives. The chapter is the Bhāgavata's compact charter for the claim that **loving surrender outranks Vedic ritualism**, and it became a favourite proof-text of the [Gauḍīya](../05-concepts-and-dharma/bhakti-rasa-and-the-bhavas.md) and other bhakti schools.

Do not confuse the *yajña-patnīs* with the **gopīs** of Vraja: they are married brāhmaṇa women of a settlement near Vṛndāvana, not the cowherd-girls of the [Rāsa-līlā](./rasa-lila.md). The point of the episode is precisely that devotion crosses the boundary of **varṇa** and of ritual qualification — women barred from the sacrificial fire outstrip the twice-born men who tend it.

---

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic frame** — The episode belongs to Krishna's boyhood at **Vṛndāvana**, in the last years of the [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of the **28th Mahāyuga** of the present **[Vaivasvata (7th) Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)** — traditionally just before the onset of **Kali Yuga in 3102 BCE** (marked by Krishna's departure).
- **Academic / historical frame** — The **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** in its extant form is generally dated **[scholarly] c. 9th–10th century CE** (probably South India). The yajña-patnī chapter belongs to this mature devotional stratum, in which the archaic prestige of Vedic *śrauta* sacrifice is deliberately subordinated to *bhakti*; the tension it dramatizes is a theological programme of the text's own era, not a report of a historical dispute. Keep the frames distinct: the *event* is Dvāpara, the *composition* is early-medieval.

## The narrative (SB 10.23)

- **The hungry cowherd boys** — While Krishna and [Balarāma](./characters/balarama.md) graze the cattle at a distance from Vraja, the cowherd boys grow hungry and come to the two brothers asking for food (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.1). The request is childlike and ordinary — the whole grand argument of the chapter is hung on a boys' empty stomach.
- **Krishna directs them to the sacrifice** — Krishna tells the boys that **brāhmaṇas nearby are performing a Vedic sacrifice desiring elevation to heaven (svarga)**; the boys should go to the sacrificial arena and ask for food, invoking the names of **Balarāma and Krishna** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.2–3).
  - *On the rite:* the text presents it as an ordinary *śrauta*/soma-type oblation for heavenly reward; several translations name it an **Āṅgirasa sacrifice** (a *sattra* in the line of Aṅgiras). The doctrinal point is only that it is orthodox, *phala*-seeking Vedic ritual — *karma-kāṇḍa* at its most respectable. [scholarly]
- **The boys' humble petition** — The boys go, bow to the ground, and ask for a little food "in the name of Krishna and Balarāma," standing before the *brahma-vādins* (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.4–5). Their approach is correct and reverent; nothing in their conduct warrants a refusal.
- **The ritualists refuse** — Though they hear the request, the brāhmaṇas **say nothing and give nothing**. Bound by ritual scruple — the food is consecrated to the sacrifice and, in their reckoning, not to be given out before the rite is complete — and inwardly contemptuous of a request made in the name of mere cowherd boys, they ignore the petition (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.6–7).
  - *The Bhāgavata's verdict:* the text condemns them directly — those puffed up by **birth, learning, austerity, ritual and wealth** but **ignorant of the Supreme** gain nothing by all their observances; their sacrifice is fruitless because it lacks devotion (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.8–11). The refusal is framed not as prudence but as spiritual blindness.
- **The boys return empty-handed** — Rebuffed, the cowherd boys go back and report the failure to Krishna and Balarāma (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.12).
- **Krishna sends them to the wives** — Krishna, unruffled and smiling, tells the boys to go instead to the **wives of those brāhmaṇas**, who are full of love for him; they should announce that Krishna and Balarāma have come and are hungry (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.13–14). The redirection is the hinge of the story: rejected by the fire-tenders, he turns to the household.
- **The wives abandon the sacrifice** — Hearing that **Krishna is near**, the wives — long having heard of his qualities and cherishing him in their hearts — **immediately take the four kinds of food** (*chatur-vidha anna*: chewable, swallowable, lickable, suckable) in vessels and set out to him, **like rivers rushing to the sea** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.18–20).
- **They defy their menfolk** — Their **husbands, fathers, brothers and kinsmen forbid them**, but the wives, their minds seized by Krishna, **do not turn back**; they leave the sacrificial ground and go into the forest to find him (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.20–21). The defiance of male and ritual authority is stated plainly and endorsed by the narrative.
- **The darśana in the grove** — They find Krishna amid the boys and Balarāma — dark-complexioned, in yellow silk, garlanded, a peacock feather and forest flowers in his hair — and, unable to embrace him openly, **drink him in through their eyes and clasp him within their hearts** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.22–23).
- **They offer the feast** — The wives present the abundant food; Krishna, who is *ātmārāma* (self-satisfied), nonetheless receives their offering with pleasure, honouring the love behind it (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.24–25).

## Krishna's teaching to the wives (SB 10.23.28–32)

- **Praise of their devotion** — Krishna welcomes them and declares their love the very fruit of embodied life; such single-minded attachment to him is the highest gain, difficult even for sages (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.26–28).
- **Sends them back to their husbands** — Yet he instructs them to **return home and assist their husbands in completing the sacrifice**: a householder's fire-rites require the wife; a woman of good family should not stand apart from her husband. Their duty (*strī-dharma*) and their devotion are not to be set against each other (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.28–31).
  - *The core doctrine:* nearness of body is **not** required for love of him — by **hearing, seeing in the mind, meditating on, and glorifying** him, a devotee attains him even from a distance; therefore, he says, **go home** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.32). The line is the chapter's theological summit: *bhakti* is interior and needs no ritual precinct, no physical proximity, no varṇa-qualification.
- **The wives return** — Reassured, and their love already accepted, the wives go back to the sacrificial arena; **their husbands do not reproach them**, and the men complete the rite together with their wives (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.33–34).

## The wife who could not go (SB 10.23.35 ff.)

- **Deliverance in absentia** — One wife, **physically restrained by her husband** and unable to join the others, hears from them of Krishna's glories, closes her eyes, and **absorbs herself wholly in the thought of him**. Her bondage to fruitive action (*karma*) dissolves; she **gives up her body made of the three guṇas and attains him directly** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.35 region). [scholarly]
  - *Significance:* a liberation without darśana, without the offering, without leaving the house — the extreme statement of the chapter's thesis that pure remembrance alone carries the soul to the Lord. Commentators pair it with the deaths-in-meditation of yogīs and with [Ajāmila](./ajamila.md)'s rescue by the mere name. [scholarly]

## The brāhmaṇas' repentance (SB 10.23.40–52)

- **Sudden recognition** — With their wives' example before them, the brāhmaṇas **grasp what they have done** and are struck with remorse, realizing they have slighted the very Supreme Lord — Viṣṇu in human form — appearing as a cowherd (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.40–43).
- **The self-condemnation** — In one of the Bhāgavata's most-quoted passages they curse their own credentials: *damn our threefold birth, our vows, our vast learning, our lineage, our expertise in ritual* — for all of it left them **averse to Krishna**, while their unlettered wives, barred from the fire, crossed the ocean of birth-and-death on pure love alone (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.43–48). Birth, education and ritual mastery are named and dismissed as worthless without devotion.
- **Deluded by his Māyā** — They confess that Krishna's **Māyā deludes even the masters of yoga**, and that they were kept from him not by circumstance but by his own bewildering power; they offer him mental obeisance (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.48–50). [scholarly]
- **They dare not go** — Longing to see him, the brāhmaṇas still **do not go — for fear of Kaṃsa**, Krishna's mortal enemy at Mathurā; they salute him in their hearts and turn back to their fire, their repentance interior and incomplete (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.23.51–52). The chapter ends on this pointed irony: the men who possess every external qualification remain paralysed, while their wives have already reached him.

## Theology and reception

- **Bhakti over karma-kāṇḍa** — The episode is the Bhāgavata's clearest single dramatization of its governing thesis: **loving devotion (bhakti) surpasses Vedic sacrificial ritual (karma-kāṇḍa)**. The learned, twice-born, fire-tending men fail; the disqualified women succeed. It is regularly read alongside the Gītā's movement from ritual action to loving surrender. [scholarly] See [Pañca-mahā-yajñas & the Three Debts](../05-concepts-and-dharma/pancha-maha-yajnas-and-the-three-debts.md) for the ritual world the chapter relativizes.
- **Devotion crosses varṇa and gender** — The *yajña-patnīs* embody the Bhāgavata's insistence that access to the Lord is not gated by **varṇa, gender or ritual eligibility**; the wives' ineligibility for the *śrauta* fire is exactly what throws their qualification-by-love into relief (cf. [Varṇa-āśrama & Saṃskāras](../05-concepts-and-dharma/varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md)). [scholarly]
- **Anna offered in love** — That the medium is **food** is not incidental: the chapter is a locus classicus for the idea that food given to the Lord out of devotion outranks food consecrated by mantra alone — the theology later formalized around *naivedya* and *prasāda*. [regional / liturgical]
- **Gauḍīya and Vaiṣṇava esteem** — Gauḍīya tradition ranks the yajña-patnīs among the exemplars of pure devotion; Rūpa Gosvāmī and later commentators (Śrīdhara Svāmī, Viśvanātha Cakravartī) cite the chapter for the doctrine that hearing and remembering the Lord suffice, and that his own words (10.23.32) authorize *bhakti* independent of physical or ritual access. [scholarly]
- **The wife-in-meditation motif** — The wife who dies absorbed in Krishna without ever seeing him is invoked in the tradition as proof of *smaraṇa* (remembrance) as a complete path, kin to [Ajāmila](./ajamila.md) saved by the Name and to [Gajendra](./gajendra-moksha.md) lifted at the point of death. [scholarly]
- **Placement in the tenth canto** — Standing between the *vastra-haraṇa* (10.22) and the [Govardhana](./govardhana-dharana.md) cycle (10.24–27), the chapter forms with them a trio of escalating proofs — the gopīs' surrender of ego, the brāhmaṇa-wives' surrender of ritual duty, and Indra's humbling — all pressing the same claim of Krishna's supremacy through progressively higher stakes (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.22–27; Gauḍīya commentarial reading). [scholarly]

## Name & disambiguation

- **Yajña-patnī-mokṣaṇa** — *yajña-patnī* = "wife of a sacrificer"; *mokṣaṇa* = "deliverance / release." The compound names the chapter by its outcome: the wives are **delivered** (spiritually) even as the sacrifice their husbands guard yields nothing.
- **Not the gopīs** — the *yajña-patnīs* are brāhmaṇa householders, distinct from the cowherd gopīs of the [Veṇu-gītā](./venu-gita.md) and [Rāsa-līlā](./rasa-lila.md); their story makes the varṇa-crossing point precisely by being about women *outside* the pastoral community.
- **Not the "brahmāstra-repentance" and other brāhmaṇa episodes** — do not conflate this with unrelated Bhāgavata brāhmaṇa-tales; the shared thread of "learned men humbled" recurs across the corpus (see [Brāhmaṇa Legends Catalog](./brahmana-legends-catalog.md)), but this chapter is specifically the yajña-patnī story of SB 10.23.
