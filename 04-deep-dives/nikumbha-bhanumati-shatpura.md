# Nikumbha & the Abduction of Bhānumatī — the Ṣaṭpura War

> Where this sits: **Dvāpara Yuga**, 28th Mahāyuga of the [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md) — in the late-Dvārakā period of Kṛṣṇa's life, after the founding of [Dvārakā](../06-sacred-geography/dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md) and before the Kurukṣetra war (Kali Yuga begins **3102 BCE** at Kṛṣṇa's departure, by tradition).
>
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Text home: [Harivaṃśa](./harivamsha.md) (Viṣṇu Parva) · Related war-cycles: [Andhaka](./andhakasura-vadha.md) · [Bāṇāsura–Uṣā–Aniruddha](./banasura-usha-aniruddha.md) · [Śālva & the Saubha](./shalva-saubha-vadha.md)

One of the **late Yādava campaigns** of the Harivaṃśa's Viṣṇu Parva: during a grand Yādava pleasure-excursion from Dvārakā, the dānava **Nikumbha** — a master of illusion — snatches the Bhaima princess **Bhānumatī**, and Kṛṣṇa, **Arjuna**, and **Pradyumna** storm his stronghold **Ṣaṭpura** ("Six-Cities") to recover her. The episode is little known because it survives mainly in the *vulgate* Harivaṃśa (Dutt's translation, Viṣṇu Parva chs. 89–91) and was largely relegated to the appendices by the Critical Edition. ## Which Nikumbha? (disambiguation)

The name Nikumbha belongs to several unrelated figures; this file's subject is the first.

- **Nikumbha of Ṣaṭpura (this file)** — a **dānava**, "enemy of the gods" and master of māyā, abductor of Bhānumatī, slain by Kṛṣṇa's discus. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva ch. 91, Dutt)
- **Nikumbha son of Kumbhakarṇa** — a **rākṣasa** of Laṅkā who fights and dies in the Rāma–Rāvaṇa war; see [Kumbhakarṇa](./characters/kumbhakarna.md). (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa) — a Tretā-Yuga figure, no connection to the dānava.
- **Nikumbha the gaṇa of Śiva** — whose curse empties Vārāṇasī in the Divodāsa cycle, told in the *same* Harivaṃśa but in its first book (Harivaṃśa Parva ~29–32); see [the Kāśī line of Divodāsa](../03-lineages-vamsha/kashi-line-divodasa.md).
- **Nikumbha the piśāca king** — whose hordes hold Kashmir for six months of each year in the [Nīlamata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/nilamata-purana.md). [regional]

Likewise **Bhānumatī** here is *not* [Duryodhana's queen](./characters/duryodhana.md) of Mahābhārata tradition, nor the Bhānumatī listed among Bṛhaspati's daughters in some Purāṇic rosters ([the gurus Bṛhaspati & Śukra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md)); she is a Yādava girl, daughter of the Bhaima chief **Bhānu**.

## Where It Sits in the Harivaṃśa

- **Textual position** — The episode belongs to the string of romance-and-war cycles that close the [Harivaṃśa's](./harivamsha.md) Viṣṇu Parva: it follows the **Andhaka** material (Dutt ch. 88 ends with Śiva reducing [Andhaka](./andhakasura-vadha.md) to ashes at Mandara) and immediately precedes the **Vajranābha–Prabhāvatī** cycle (Dutt chs. 92–98), in which Pradyumna woos Prabhāvatī and the Yādavas destroy the dānava Vajranābha. The two campaigns are companion pieces — successive Yādava expeditions against dānava strongholds. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva, Dutt chs. 88–98)
- **Chapter span** — In Dutt's vulgate numbering: **ch. 89** ("The Yādavas sport in the ocean"), **ch. 90** (the sport continued; the Chālikya music), **ch. 91** ("Nikumbha carries away Bhānumatī") — the abduction, the Ṣaṭpura battle, and the slaying all fall within the long ch. 91. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva, Dutt)
- **Critical-edition status** — P. L. Vaidya's Critical Edition (BORI 1969–71) treats most of this late Viṣṇu-Parva war material as star-passage/appendix accretion rather than core text; the episode is thus best read as a comparatively **late stratum** of the Harivaṃśa. [scholarly] [late text]

## The Setting — the Yādava Pleasure-Excursion (Dutt chs. 89–90)

- **Departure from Dvārakā** — Kṛṣṇa leads the Yādava princes, with thousands of dancing-girls and visiting apsarases, on a sea excursion from Dvārakā toward the shrine of **Piṇḍāraka**, leaving the city under the care of the elders (Ugrasena and Vasudeva); Mount **Raivataka** — the Yādavas' festival hill near Dvārakā (see [Girnar/Raivataka](../06-sacred-geography/girnar-raivataka-datta-tirtha.md)) — frames the excursion country. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva ch. 89, Dutt)
- **The ocean made sweet** — At Kṛṣṇa's word the sea turns fragrant and fresh, filled with gems and lotuses; the clan divides into two parties for water-sports, one led by Kṛṣṇa, one by **Balarāma**, who revels with **Revatī** on kādambarī wine. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva chs. 89–90, Dutt)
- **The Chālikya song** — Apsarases (Urvaśī, Rambhā, Menakā and others) dance and sing Kṛṣṇa's deeds — Pūtanā, Kaṃsa, Govardhana — and perform the celestial **Chālikya** music; house-boats built by Viśvakarman host the night's feasting. The chapters are a set-piece of Yādava court luxury at its height, the calm before the outrage. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva ch. 90, Dutt)

## The Curse of Durvāsas [back-story]

- **Bhānumatī offends the sage** — As a girl, Bhānumatī, daughter of **Bhānu** (a chief of the **Bhaimas**, i.e. the Yādava clan — see [Yādava vaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/yadava-vamsha.md)), unwittingly offended the irascible sage **Durvāsas** in the **garden of Raivata**; he cursed her to be **carried off by an enemy**. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva ch. 91, Dutt; the curse is narrated retrospectively by Nārada.) [Durvāsas' hair-trigger curses are a fixed motif — cf. [Ambarīṣa & Durvāsas](./ambarisha-durvasa.md).]
- **The mitigation** — Entreated afterward, Durvāsas softened the curse: though seized, she would remain **uncontaminated**, would win a handsome and virtuous husband, and would become the mother of many sons and mistress of great riches. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva ch. 91, Dutt)

## The Abduction (Dutt ch. 91)

- **Nikumbha strikes while the clan sports** — With the Yādava fighting men away at the sea-festival, the dānava **Nikumbha**, "enemy of the gods" and a wielder of many illusions, slips into the **pleasure-garden attached to the inner (women's) apartments of Bhānu** and carries the maiden off through the sky toward his stronghold. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva ch. 91, Dutt)
  - *Variant (retellings/summaries):* later digests set the seizure **on Raivataka itself, during the festival** — a natural compression, since the curse was incurred in Raivata's garden and the clan was feasting nearby; Dutt's vulgate text places the snatch in Bhānu's palace garden. [disputed detail]
- **The alarm** — Lamentation breaks out in the women's quarters; the elders (Vasudeva and **Āhuka** in Dutt's rendering) come in wrath to Kṛṣṇa with word of "that great insult" to the house. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva ch. 91, Dutt)
- **The pursuers** — **Kṛṣṇa**, [Arjuna](./characters/arjuna.md) (Pārtha/Dhanañjaya — present among the Yādavas as kinsman and guest), and [Pradyumna](./characters/pradyumna.md) (whom the text styles **Kāma**, the love-god reborn) give chase to Ṣaṭpura. Balarāma takes no part in this fight. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva ch. 91, Dutt)

## The Ṣaṭpura War

- **Ṣaṭpura, the dānava hold** — Nikumbha flees to **Ṣaṭpura** (literally "**Six-Cities**"), the cave-riddled "abode of his kinsmen," thronged with dānavas; Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna take up position at its entrance while the battle unfolds. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva ch. 91, Dutt) [The vulgate gives no precise geography for Ṣaṭpura; the name's resemblance to the modern **Satpura** range of central India is suggestive but nowhere asserted in the text — treat any identification as conjecture. [disputed]]
- **Nikumbha's māyā** — The dānava fights as a shape-shifter and multiplier of forms: he assumes the guise of a **yellow vulture**; from one Nikumbha "divided into twain" he becomes two, then **thousands of Nikumbhas** swarm over earth and sky; when struck down he abandons the stricken body and reappears elsewhere, so the heroes repeatedly think him slain. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva ch. 91, Dutt)
- **The heroes hard-pressed** — Nikumbha fights with a **thorn-studded club** (one club described as hung with **eight bells**); he batters **Pradyumna unconscious**; asuras seize the heroic **Dhanañjaya (Arjuna)** and bear him up into the sky, and Pradyumna catches him as he falls. Pradyumna and Arjuna answer with storms of cane-shaft arrows; Kṛṣṇa plies his club **Kaumodakī**. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva ch. 91, Dutt)
- **The slaying** — Kṛṣṇa at last, by his celestial insight, discerns the **true Nikumbha** amid the phantom doubles and **cuts off his head with the discus**; the dānava crashes down "like an uprooted tree." (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva ch. 91, Dutt) [For the discus in Kṛṣṇa's other campaigns, cf. [Śālva](./shalva-saubha-vadha.md), [Narakāsura](./narakasura-vadha.md); for the asura-war context at large, see the [asura wars & roster](../08-beings-and-bestiary/asura-wars-and-roster.md).]

## Aftermath — Nārada's Disclosure & Bhānumatī's Marriage

- **The curse fulfilled and discharged** — **Nārada** reveals Durvāsas' old curse and its mitigation: Bhānumatī, though carried off, is **untouched and unstained**, exactly as the sage had promised. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva ch. 91, Dutt)
- **Given to Sahadeva** — At Nārada's instance the rescued maiden is bestowed on **Sahadeva, son of Mādrī** — the youngest [Pāṇḍava](./characters/nakula-sahadeva.md) — praised as virtuous, respectful, and heroic; Kṛṣṇa returns with her to Dvārakā, and Sahadeva in time departs with his bride. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva ch. 91, Dutt) [A quiet Harivaṃśa contribution to the Pāṇḍava marriage-rosters: Sahadeva's Yādava wife Bhānumatī appears only in this cycle, not in the epic's own lists.]
- **Sequel: the Vajranābha campaign** — The narrative moves directly to the next dānava war: Pradyumna's romance with **Prabhāvatī**, daughter of the dānava **Vajranābha**, and the destruction of Vajranābha's sky-fortress by the Yādavas (Dutt chs. 92–98) — a companion "late Yādava campaign" of the same textual layer. (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva chs. 92–98, Dutt) [This Vajranābha the dānava is distinct from **Vajranābha the Yādava**, Kṛṣṇa's great-grandson who re-founds the Dvārakā shrine — see [Dvārakā](../06-sacred-geography/dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md).]

## The Two Clocks

- **Scriptural/cyclic frame** — The episode sits in the last lap of **Dvāpara Yuga**: after the flight from Mathurā and founding of Dvārakā, within the mature Dvārakā court period of [Kṛṣṇa's life](./krishna-lila.md) (alongside the [Syamantaka](./syamantaka-jewel.md), [Bāṇāsura](./banasura-usha-aniruddha.md), and Vajranābha cycles), while Arjuna is a grown warrior and guest of the Yādavas — i.e. within living reach of the Mahābhārata war and the Kali threshold of **3102 BCE** (traditional).
- **Academic/text-critical frame** — [scholarly] The Harivaṃśa is a layered text: core strata c. 1st–2nd century BCE to the early centuries CE, with the late Viṣṇu-Parva war cycles (this episode among them) judged by the Critical Edition to be **later accretions**, plausibly mid-first-millennium CE in their vulgate form. The episode's absence from the Viṣṇu and Bhāgavata Purāṇas' Kṛṣṇa biographies is consistent with its being a vulgate-Harivaṃśa specialty. [late text]
