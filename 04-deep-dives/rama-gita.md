# Rāma-Gītā: The Song of Rāma

> *Up to [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · master [index](../README.md). The Vaiṣṇava–Advaita Gītā in which **Rāma instructs Lakṣmaṇa** in pure non-dualism — embedded as the [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](./adhyatma-ramayana.md)'s **Uttara Kāṇḍa, sarga 5** (book 7, ch. 5), counted with the [Śiva-gītā](./shiva-gita.md), [Devī-gītā](./devi-gita.md), and Gaṇeśa-gītā among the first-tier Purāṇic Gītās of the [lesser-Gītās catalog](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md), and modelled self-consciously on the [Bhagavad Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md).*

The **Rāma-Gītā** ("the Song of Rāma") is a compact, uncompromising **Advaita-Vedānta** discourse — usually counted as **62 verses** in a single chapter — in which **Rāma**, after his return to Ayodhyā and coronation, answers his brother **Lakṣmaṇa**'s plea for the knowledge that liberates. It inverts the [Śiva-gītā](./shiva-gita.md): there Rāma is the despairing *pupil* in the forest; here Rāma the crowned king is the *teacher*, and Lakṣmaṇa the seeker. It is the flagship Vedāntic set-piece of the [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](./adhyatma-ramayana.md) — the Rāma-story retold as a manual of non-dualism — and, like its Śaiva and Śākta siblings, it circulates **detached from its parent text** as a recited and commented Vedāntic scripture in its own right. [scholarly]

## The two clocks — scriptural vs. historical

- **Scriptural time** — *In-narrative* the discourse falls in the **[Tretā Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of our 28th Mahāyuga, *after* the war and Rāma's return — set in the **Uttara Kāṇḍa** at Ayodhyā, with Rāma reigning as king. This places it later in Rāma's life than the [Śiva-gītā](./shiva-gita.md) (forest, pre-war) and the [Bhagavad Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md)'s Dvāpara battlefield. [scriptural]
- **Historical / text-critical time** — The Rāma-Gītā is a **late-medieval Advaitic composition**. The Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa itself is generally dated to roughly the **14th–15th century CE** (a Rāmānanda / Smārta-Vaiṣṇava milieu, transmitted as part of the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa); the Gītā within it draws on mature post-Śaṅkara Vedānta. Keep the Tretā-Yuga setting distinct from this manuscript dating. [scholarly]

## Textual placement & frame

- **Location** — The Rāma-Gītā occupies the **Uttara Kāṇḍa (book 7), sarga 5** of the [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](./adhyatma-ramayana.md), a single chapter conventionally counted at **62 ślokas**. (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa 7.5)
  - *Variant (count):* most editions give **62 verses**; a few recensions and translations count **60–64** depending on the inclusion of the framing and concluding ślokas. [disputed — recension]
- **The narrative occasion** — Rāma is **enthroned at Ayodhyā** after the slaying of Rāvaṇa and the recovery of Sītā. **Lakṣmaṇa**, ever the attendant brother, asks Rāma to teach him the *bhakti* and the *jñāna* by which a man crosses beyond *saṃsāra* — supplying the "despondent-seeker" frame that every Gītā needs. (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa 7.5)
- **Reported frame** — Like the whole Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, the dialogue is a nested recitation: **Śiva narrating the secret Rāma-story to Pārvatī**, who reports Rāma's words to Lakṣmaṇa. The Gītā is thus framed as Śiva's own disclosure of Rāma's inner teaching. (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, frame)
- **Self-conscious modelling on the Bhagavad Gītā** — The text deliberately echoes the [Bhagavad Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md): a divine avatāra (Viṣṇu as Rāma rather than as Kṛṣṇa) instructs a beloved kinsman who has asked for liberating knowledge, closing with the avatāra's self-disclosure as the supreme *Brahman*. It is read as the **Rāma-bhakti tradition's answer to the Kṛṣṇa-Gītā**, deliberately occupying the same genre slot. [scholarly]

## The teaching — verse movement

- **Lakṣmaṇa's question** — Lakṣmaṇa asks for the means of release; Rāma answers that **devotion (bhakti) to him as the supreme Lord** is the gateway, and that *jñāna* (knowledge) is the direct cause of liberation — the same *bhakti*-then-*jñāna* sequence as the [Śiva-gītā](./shiva-gita.md). (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa 7.5)
- **The qualified seeker** — Rāma describes the *adhikārin*: one endowed with the **four-fold discipline (sādhana-catuṣṭaya)** — discrimination (*viveka*), dispassion (*vairāgya*), the six virtues, and the longing for release (*mumukṣutva*) — who, having served his guru, is fit for *brahma-vidyā*. Standard Advaita *prakaraṇa* framing. (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa 7.5)
- **Mahāvākya — "tat tvam asi"** — The doctrinal core: the **great sentence "That thou art"** is expounded by analysing the *jīva* (the individual self) and *Īśvara* (the Lord) and resolving both into the one undivided **Brahman**; bondage is *avidyā* (ignorance) and superimposition (*adhyāsa*), liberation is direct knowledge of identity. (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa 7.5)
- **Discrimination of self from sheaths** — Rāma teaches **dṛg-dṛśya-viveka** (the seer vs. the seen) and the negation of the **five sheaths (pañca-kośa)** and three bodies (gross/subtle/causal): the Self is the unchanging witness (*sākṣin*), never the body, senses, mind, or vital airs. (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa 7.5)
- **Māyā and superimposition** — The world is the play of **māyā**, an apparent superimposition on Brahman like silver mistaken on nacre or a snake on a rope; it has no independent reality apart from the witnessing consciousness. Classic *vivarta*-vāda imagery. (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa 7.5)
- **The jīvanmukta** — Rāma describes the **liberated-in-life**: the knower who, having realised "I am Brahman" (*ahaṃ brahmāsmi*), lives free of egoism, desire, and the sense of doership, untouched while the body's *prārabdha* karma plays out. (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa 7.5)
- **The fruit and the seal** — The Gītā closes with the **phala-śruti**: whoever studies or recites this teaching crosses *saṃsāra* and attains Rāma's own supreme abode — a self-referential blessing on the text itself, exactly as Gītās conventionally end. (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa 7.5)

## Doctrine & affiliations

- **Pure Advaita** — The metaphysics is **uncompromisingly non-dualist Vedānta**: one *Brahman*, the world as appearance, *jīva*–*Brahman* identity via *tat tvam asi*, liberation by knowledge alone. It reads as a **Vedāntic *prakaraṇa* (introductory manual)** in the lineage of Śaṅkara's school, with Rāma as the *Īśvara* who is also the *nirguṇa* Absolute. [scholarly]
- **Bhakti–jñāna synthesis** — Like the [Śiva-gītā](./shiva-gita.md), the Rāma-Gītā insists devotion and knowledge form a **single graded path** — *bhakti* to Rāma purifies and qualifies, *jñāna* releases — which lets it serve a **Smārta-Vaiṣṇava / Rāma-bhakti** readership that wants both a personal Lord and *advaita* liberation. [scholarly]
- **Genre kinship** — Its closest structural twins are the **Bhagavad Gītā** (avatāra teaching a kinsman) and the **Śiva-gītā** (Rāma as the shared protagonist, roles reversed). The corpus treats Rāma-Gītā and Śiva-gītā as a **deliberately paired set**. [scholarly]

## An originally independent text, stitched in [scholarly] [disputed]

- **The scholarly view** — A recurring text-critical position holds that the Rāma-Gītā was **originally an independent Advaitic composition** — a free-standing Vedānta *prakaraṇa* — that was **later interpolated into the Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa**'s Uttara Kāṇḍa, rather than composed for that slot. Its self-contained doctrinal arc, its disproportionately technical Advaita vocabulary relative to the surrounding narrative, and its easy detachability are cited as evidence. [scholarly] [disputed]
  - *Variant (against):* others read it as integral to the Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa's overall design — that whole text being a Vedāntic re-reading of the Rāma-story, of which the Gītā is the natural climax — so that "independent then inserted" overstates the seam. [disputed]
- **Stand-alone life** — Whatever its origin, the Rāma-Gītā **circulates on its own**: printed, translated, commented, and **recited as a Vedāntic text** independent of the full Rāmāyaṇa, in the same manner as the Devī-, Śiva-, and Gaṇeśa-Gītās. It is a fixture of the "great Gītās" recitation rosters. [scholarly]

## The other Rāma-Gītā — the South-Indian Dakṣiṇāmūrti text [regional] [late text]

- **A separate, longer work** — Distinct from the Adhyātma-Rāmāyaṇa chapter is a **longer South-Indian Rāma-Gītā** belonging to the **Dakṣiṇāmūrti / Guru-Jñāna-Vāsiṣṭha-Tantra** tradition — a Tantric-Vedāntic dialogue of far greater length, not the 62-verse Lakṣmaṇa discourse. The two share a name but are **wholly different compositions**; rosters that list "Rāma-Gītā" usually mean the Adhyātma one. [regional] [late text]
  - *Variant (frame):* this South-Indian text is transmitted in the orbit of the **Dakṣiṇāmūrti** (Śiva as the silent guru) and **Vāsiṣṭha-tantra** literature, blending *advaita* with Tantric guru-disciple framing — a southern Śaiva-Vedānta milieu rather than the northern Rāma-bhakti one of the Adhyātma version. [regional] [disputed — attribution]
