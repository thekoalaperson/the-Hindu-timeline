# Nara–Nārāyaṇa — the Twin Sages of Badarī

> Where this sits: a deep-dive on the eternal **twin sage-avatāra** of Viṣṇu — Nara and Nārāyaṇa — perpetually performing *tapas* at Badarīkāśrama, sons of [Dharma](../08-beings-and-bestiary/the-prajapatis.md), and the prior births of [Arjuna](./characters/arjuna.md) and Kṛṣṇa.
>

**Nara–Nārāyaṇa** (नर-नारायण) are a paired *ṛṣi-avatāra* of [Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md): two ascetics who are in fact one supreme Being split into a *jīva*-like companion (Nara, "Man") and the supreme Lord (Nārāyaṇa, "Refuge of man"). They embody the eternal model of the soul (Nara) attending upon God (Nārāyaṇa), and of *tapas* and *dharma* upheld through endless ages at the northern hermitage of **Badarī** in the Himālaya. They are simultaneously a primordial creation-event (Satya Yuga, as Dharma's twin sons) and a recurring presence whose final earthly descent is as Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna in the Dvāpara–Kali junction. [scholarly]

## Birth and parentage

- **Sons of Dharma and Mūrti** — Nara and Nārāyaṇa are born to **Dharma** (the *prajāpati* / personification of righteousness) and his wife **Mūrti**, daughter of Dakṣa, as part of the early proliferation of beings. They are reckoned among Dharma's holy offspring and are eternally devoted to austerity. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.1)
  - *Variant (mother as Ahiṃsā/Dakṣa's daughters):* some Purāṇic genealogies make the mother **Ahiṃsā** ("Non-violence") or list Dharma's wives among the thirteen daughters of Dakṣa (Śraddhā, Mūrti, Dayā, etc.), with Nara-Nārāyaṇa specifically the issue of **Mūrti** or **Ahiṃsā**. Catalog both; the Bhāgavata names Mūrti. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.1; Purāṇic genealogy summaries) [late text]
- **Four sons of Dharma reading** — a related tradition counts **four** ascetic sons of Dharma — **Hari, Kṛṣṇa, Nara and Nārāyaṇa** — of whom Nara and Nārāyaṇa are the avatāric pair who take up residence at Badarī; Hari and Kṛṣṇa are sometimes folded into the same supreme identity. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.1) [disputed]
- **Identity as Viṣṇu's portion** — theologically the pair is a partial (*aṃśa*) descent of Viṣṇu-Nārāyaṇa for the protection of *dharma* and the demonstration of *tapas*; Nārāyaṇa is the supreme, Nara the eternal companion-soul. See [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md). (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva)

## Eternal tapas at Badarīkāśrama

- **The hermitage of Badarī** — the twins retire to **Badarikāśrama** (Badrīnāth, in the Himālaya near the source of the Gaṅgā / Alakanandā) and there perform unbroken austerity for the welfare of the worlds, age after age. Badarī becomes the archetypal Vaiṣṇava tapas-sthāna. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva)
- **Two clocks — cyclic recurrence vs. epic episode** — on the *scriptural/cyclic* clock the twin sages' *tapas* is **beginningless and perpetual**, renewed every cosmic cycle; on the *epic/narrative* clock their teaching of **Nārada at Badarī** is a datable frame-event of the [Nārāyaṇīya](./narayaniya.md), and their final embodiment as Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa is fixed to the Dvāpara–Kali junction. The two registers must be kept distinct. [scholarly]
- **Teachers of Nārada** — at Badarī the twin sages impart the **ekāntika (one-pointed) dharma** to the wandering sage **Nārada**, who then journeys north to **Śvetadvīpa**; this is the inner frame of the [Nārāyaṇīya](./narayaniya.md). (Source: Mahābhārata, Nārāyaṇīya / Śānti Parva)

## The Urvaśī episode — humbling Indra's apsarās

- **Indra's fear of the twins' tapas** — alarmed that the austerities of Nara and Nārāyaṇa might win them Indra's own sovereignty, **Indra** dispatches **Kāmadeva** with a host of celestial nymphs (*apsarās*) — accompanied by spring (Vasanta) — to break the sages' penance. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Devī Bhāgavata)
- **Nārāyaṇa produces Urvaśī from his thigh** — wholly unmoved, **Nārāyaṇa** smiles and, to expose the apsarās' inferiority and to humble Indra, strikes (or places a flower upon) his **thigh** (*ūru*), from which springs a nymph of such surpassing beauty that all of Indra's nymphs are shamed. Because she is born of the thigh (*ūru*), she is named **Urvaśī**. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Devī Bhāgavata)
  - *Variant (born of will / from the thigh):* texts differ on whether Urvaśī emerges from the **thigh** (the dominant etymological account, *ūru* = thigh) or is otherwise produced by Nārāyaṇa's yogic power; the thigh-birth giving the name **Urvaśī** is the standard reading. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Devī Bhāgavata)
  - *Variant (Devī Bhāgavata emphasis):* the [Śākta] Devī Bhāgavata frames the episode to magnify the sages' detachment and the supremacy of the divine power working through them. (Source: Devī Bhāgavata) [regional]
- **Urvaśī gifted to the gods** — the sages, untroubled by lust, send **Urvaśī** as a gift to **Indra's** court, where she becomes the foremost of *apsarās*. The episode demonstrates Nārāyaṇa's mastery over desire (he who can *create* the most beautiful of beings has nothing to fear from seduction) and supplies Urvaśī's origin-myth. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Devī Bhāgavata)
  - *Note:* this Urvaśī is the same nymph whose later earthly love-story unfolds with King **Purūravas**; see the dedicated treatment in [Purūravas–Urvaśī](./pururavas-urvashi.md). (Source: cross-tradition)

## Prior births of Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa

- **Nara reborn as Arjuna, Nārāyaṇa as Kṛṣṇa** — the Mahābhārata repeatedly identifies the twin sages as the **previous incarnations of [Arjuna](./characters/arjuna.md) (= Nara) and Kṛṣṇa (= Nārāyaṇa)**; their friendship across yugas explains the inseparable bond of the Pāṇḍava and the Yādava on the Kurukṣetra field. (Source: Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva / Nārāyaṇīya)
- **Recognition on the battlefield** — sages and seers hail Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna as **"the ancient gods Nara and Nārāyaṇa"** descended to relieve Earth's burden; the [Bhagavad Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md)'s dialogue is thereby read as Nārāyaṇa once more instructing Nara. (Source: Mahābhārata; Bhāgavata Purāṇa)
- **Theological force** — because Nara (the soul) eternally serves Nārāyaṇa (God), the Arjuna–Kṛṣṇa relationship becomes the paradigm of *bhakti*: the devotee perpetually accompanying, learning from, and acting for the Lord. (Source: Mahābhārata, Nārāyaṇīya) [scholarly]

## In the avatāra lists and the Naranārāyaṇīya frame

- **A standing member of the avatāra rosters** — the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa's** enumeration of Viṣṇu's *līlā-avatāras* (SB 1.3 and the summary at **SB 11.4**) lists **Nara-Nārāyaṇa** among the descents — the sage-pair who practise penance for the world's good. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.4; SB 1.3)
- **SB 11.4 — the avatāra hymn** — in the eleventh canto's catalogue of incarnations, Nara-Nārāyaṇa appear as the ascetic avatāra exemplifying *tapas* and *dharma*, distinct from the dramatic world-rescuing forms. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.4)
- **Relation to the Daśāvatāra** — Nara-Nārāyaṇa are **not** among the fixed canonical ten ([Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)) but belong to the wider open roster of *līlā*/*vibhava* avatāras (alongside Kapila, Dattātreya, Ṛṣabha, Vyāsa, etc.); do not retroject them into the standardized ten. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa; daśāvatāra tradition) [scholarly]
- **The Naranārāyaṇīya frame** — the [Nārāyaṇīya](./narayaniya.md) of the Śānti Parva places the twin sages at its narrative core: the supreme teaching descends through **Nārāyaṇa → Nara/Nārada → Bhīṣma → Yudhiṣṭhira**, making Badarī the fountainhead of the epic's Pāñcarātra-Vaiṣṇava revelation. (Source: Mahābhārata, Nārāyaṇīya / Śānti Parva)

## Iconography & cult

- **Paired ascetic icon** — depicted as two *muni*s in bark/deer-skin garb with matted locks (*jaṭā*); Nārāyaṇa often four-armed bearing Viṣṇu's emblems, Nara two-armed as the human companion, sometimes with bow (anticipating Arjuna). (Source: iconographic tradition) [scholarly]
- **Badrīnāth association** — the Badrīnāth temple and pilgrimage are tied to the twins' eternal *tapas*; the *badarī* (jujube) tree under which they meditate gives the site its name in the tradition. (Source: regional temple lore) [regional] [folk]
