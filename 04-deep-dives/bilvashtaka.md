# Bilvashtaka — the Eight-Verse Hymn for Offering Bilva Leaves to Shiva

> *Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · The short liturgical octet recited while laying the trifoliate **bilva** leaf on the liṅga; a ritual companion to the [Śiva Mahimna Stotra](stotras/shiva-mahimna-stotra.md) and catalogued among the independent devotional song-hymns of the [Lesser Gītās catalog](lesser-gitas-catalog.md).*

The **Bilvāṣṭaka** (Bilvāṣṭakam, "the eight [verses] of the bilva"), opening *tridalaṃ triguṇākāraṃ trinetraṃ ca triyāyudham...* ("Three-leafed, of the form of the three guṇas, three-eyed and three-weaponed..."), is a short Śaiva liturgical hymn of **eight verses** (an *aṣṭaka*) recited while offering the **trifoliate bilva (bael, *Aegle marmelos*) leaf** — the *bilva-patra* — to Śiva or the Śiva-liṅga. Unlike the grand praise-poems, it is functionally a **mantra of offering (samarpaṇa)**: each verse hands over the leaf and recites the merit thereby gained, the whole closing with a confession of unworthiness and a plea for forgiveness. It is the standard accompaniment to bilva-offering in daily *śiva-pūjā* and especially on **Mahā-śivarātri** and the fortnightly **Pradoṣa**. [scholarly]

For the bilva tree itself as a sacred plant — its botany, its link to Lakṣmī, its place in the Pañcavaṭī — see [sacred-flora-and-fauna](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/sacred-flora-and-fauna.md); this file treats only the **stotra**.

## The trifoliate leaf and its symbolic readings

- **The three leaflets as a theological cipher** — The hymn's governing image is that the single bilva leaf is **three leaflets on one stalk**, and each verse re-reads this trinity. The opening verse glosses it at once as *triguṇākāra* (the **three guṇas** — sattva, rajas, tamas), *trinetra* (Śiva's **three eyes**), and *triyāyudha* (the **three-pointed weapon, the triśūla**). (*Bilvāṣṭaka* v. 1)
  - *Variant (Brahmā–Viṣṇu–Maheśa):* a parallel and very common reading — strong in the wider plant-lore — makes the three leaflets the **trimūrti** (Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Śiva). The repo's botanical entry records all three glosses (three guṇas / three eyes-and-triśūla / trimūrti) side by side. (sacred-flora tradition) [folk]
  - *Variant (the three syllables / three times):* devotional commentary also reads the triad as the three *kālas* (past-present-future) or the three sacred utterances, so that offering one leaf is held to encompass all threefold realities at once. [folk]
- **The leaf as a self-sufficient offering** — The hymn repeatedly stresses that the bilva is *akhaṇḍa* (whole/unbroken) and supremely pleasing to Śiva, so that even a single leaf, rightly offered, suffices where elaborate worship is impossible — the same "devotion over capacity" logic the [Śiva Mahimna Stotra](stotras/shiva-mahimna-stotra.md) makes its theme. (*Bilvāṣṭaka*, refrain) [scholarly]

## Content of the hymn, verse by verse

- **The refrain (the structural spine)** — Most verses close on the line ***eka-bilvaṃ śivārpaṇam*** ("a single bilva [leaf], offered to Śiva") — the act the hymn performs as it is spoken. The aṣṭaka is built as eight variations on this one offering-formula. (*Bilvāṣṭaka*, recurring pāda)
- **v. 1 — the threefold leaf** — *Tridalaṃ triguṇākāraṃ trinetraṃ ca triyāyudham, trijanma-pāpa-saṃhāram...* — the leaf as three guṇas / three eyes / triśūla, and its offering as **destroyer of the sins of three births**. (*Bilvāṣṭaka* v. 1)
- **vv. 2–3 — bilva and the goddess; the merit of leaves** — Verses praise the bilva as a tree **dear to / dwelt in by the goddess (Lakṣmī/Mahālakṣmī)** and tally the **immense merit (puṇya)** of offering its leaves — a single leaf weighed against great sacrifices, the offering said to bring prosperity and to erase accumulated sin. (*Bilvāṣṭaka* vv. 2–3) [folk]
- **vv. 4–6 — fruit of offering on sacred days and at sacred sites** — The verses enumerate the **vastly multiplied fruit** of offering bilva at *kṣetras* and on holy days, and name the offering as a means to **mokṣa / Śiva-loka**, the leaf's three lobes again invoked as the trinity. (*Bilvāṣṭaka* vv. 4–6) [folk]
- **vv. 7–8 — the phala-śruti and the prayer for forgiveness** — The closing verses give the **phala-śruti** (one who offers bilva, or recites this aṣṭaka, is freed of sin and attains Śiva) and end on a **kṣamā-prārthanā** — a plea for forgiveness of all faults in the worship, the offering of "one bilva" standing in for whatever the devotee has done wrongly or left undone. (*Bilvāṣṭaka* vv. 7–8) [scholarly]
  - *Note (forgiveness-formula parallel):* this closing *kṣamā* function — a hymn appended to a pūjā to absolve ritual error (*aparādha*) — is exactly the role tradition assigns the [Śiva Mahimna Stotra](stotras/shiva-mahimna-stotra.md), itself born of a bilva-trampling offense (the gandharva Puṣpadanta's trodden Śiva-offering). The two hymns sit at the same liturgical seam. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (verse-count and refrain):* printed texts vary between counting **eight verses plus a phala-śruti** and folding the phala-śruti into the eighth; the refrain *eka-bilvaṃ śivārpaṇam* appears in most but not every verse across recensions. [disputed]

## Ritual use

- **The act it accompanies — bilva-arpaṇa** — The hymn is recited at the **upacāra of leaf-offering** in *śiva-pūjā*: the worshipper places bilva leaves on the liṅga (often three-leaflet sprigs laid **smooth side / stalk** per ritual prescription), each verse marking an offering. (living liturgical tradition)
- **Mahā-śivarātri and Pradoṣa** — Bilva-offering with the aṣṭaka is held especially efficacious on **Mahā-śivarātri** (the "great night of Śiva") and on each **Pradoṣa** (the dusk of the thirteenth lunar day), the dusk-window when Śiva is said to dance — the same festival calendar on which the Mahimna and Tāṇḍava stotras are sung. (devotional tradition)
- **Substitution and emergency-worship logic** — Because a single leaf is declared sufficient, the hymn underwrites the popular conviction that bilva-worship is the **poor person's and the busy person's full pūjā** — a complete offering reducible to one leaf and eight verses. [folk]
- **Prohibitions and lore (regional, ritual-manual)** — Surrounding practice (not always in the hymn itself) holds that bilva must **not be plucked on certain tithis** (Aṣṭamī, Caturdaśī, Amāvāsyā, Saṅkrānti in various manuals), that **leaves once offered may be washed and reused**, and that the leaf should be offered **inverted / three-lobed and unbroken**. These are *paddhati* (manual) rules layered around the stotra. [regional] [folk]

## Textual strands: Skanda and Padma Purāṇa bilva-māhātmya

- **The aṣṭaka as a stotra without a single fixed home** — The Bilvāṣṭaka circulates as an **independent recitation-text** in *stotra* anthologies and pūjā-paddhatis; like many short liturgical aṣṭakas its verses are **attributed to the Purāṇas** in devotional usage rather than pinned to one critically-edited chapter. Treat the Purāṇic ascription as **traditional, not text-critical**. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Bilva-māhātmya in the Skanda Purāṇa** — The vast, multi-khaṇḍa **[Skanda Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/skanda-purana-khanda-map.md)** carries extensive **bilva-māhātmya** material — praise of the bilva tree and the merit of offering its leaves to Śiva — which the offering-verses echo; the Skanda is the principal Śaiva-tīrtha-and-vrata Purāṇa and a natural reservoir for such liturgy. (Skanda Purāṇa, bilva-māhātmya strands) [scholarly]
- **Bilva-māhātmya in the Padma Purāṇa** — The **Padma Purāṇa** likewise preserves bilva-māhātmya passages glorifying the leaf-offering and the tree's link to the goddess; the Padma is also the home of the independent **Śiva-gītā**, marking it as a Purāṇa with strong Śaiva strata. (Padma Purāṇa, bilva-māhātmya strands) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (which Purāṇa "owns" it):* popular sources variously credit the aṣṭaka or its source-verses to the **Skanda**, the **Padma**, or simply "the Purāṇas"; no single attribution is securely established, and the verses likely **crystallized from shared māhātmya material** common to the Śaiva Purāṇas. [disputed]
- **Relation to the broader bilva-offering scripture** — The merit-claims of the hymn (a single leaf outweighing great sacrifice; offering on Śivarātri washing away sin) are **stock māhātmya topoi** found across the Śiva-, Skanda-, and Padma-Purāṇa Śaiva layers; the aṣṭaka is best read as a **liturgical distillation** of that diffuse praise-literature rather than an excerpt of one passage. [scholarly]

## Cosmic placement — the "two clocks"

- **In-narrative time** — The hymn is **not a narrative episode** anchored to a yuga; its merit-claims (offering on Śivarātri, the leaf's threefold symbolism) belong to the **timeless liturgical/ritual register** of the Purāṇic world. Its myth-allusions (Śiva's three eyes, the triśūla, the bilva as Lakṣmī's tree) are cross-yuga Śaiva commonplaces. Best read as **cross-yuga**. [scholarly]
- **Text-critical time** — As a polished offering-aṣṭaka in stotra anthologies, the hymn as we have it belongs to the **classical/medieval Śaiva devotional layer**; its attribution to the Skanda/Padma Purāṇas is a traditional sourcing, not a manuscript date. Keep the Purāṇic ascription distinct from the literary dating. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Note on Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava balance** — The trimūrti reading of the leaf (Brahmā–Viṣṇu–Maheśa) and the bilva's tie to Lakṣmī give the hymn a quietly **inclusivist** edge; for the parallel Vaiṣṇava emphasis on Viṣṇu's descents the leaf's middle lobe is read to honour, cf. the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md). [folk]
