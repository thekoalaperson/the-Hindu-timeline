# Puṇḍarīka of Puṣkara — the Devotee Absorbed into Viṣṇu

> Cosmic address: an undated human-world devotee-tale ("the greatness of Puṣkara") narrated by **Nārada to King Śibi** in the **[Padma Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/padma-purana.md)**, Uttara-khaṇḍa 218–219 — the Padma's flagship proof-story that **Puṣkara** saves even the worst sinner and grants its true devotee **sāyujya** (absorption into Viṣṇu). Like the rest of the Uttara-khaṇḍa's māhātmya library it is pitched at hearers of the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** [late text].
> Parent: [Deep Dives](README.md) · Site file: [Puṣkara — the Brahmā Tīrtha](../06-sacred-geography/pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md) · Text map: [Padma Purāṇa khaṇḍa-map](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/padma-purana-khanda-map.md)

**⚠ Disambiguation first.** This Puṇḍarīka ("white lotus") is **not** [Puṇḍalīka of Pandharpur](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vithoba-vitthal.md) — the Marathi parent-serving devotee who threw Viṭhobā a brick (canonical home: [Vithoba / Viṭṭhal](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vithoba-vitthal.md); site file: [Pandharpur & the Vārī](../06-sacred-geography/pandharpur-vitthala-wari.md)). The confusion is easy and old: both are Viṣṇu-devotees named Puṇḍarīka/Puṇḍalīka, and the Pandharpur *Pāṇḍuraṅga-māhātmya* also circulates in a Padma-Purāṇa recension. They are different men, at different tīrthas, in different strata — this file's Puṇḍarīka lives, worships and dissolves at **Puṣkara** (Ajmer, Rajasthan), and his story ends not with a waiting image but with **no image at all**: the devotee merges into the Lord's own body. A fuller catalog of the *other* Puṇḍarīkas is at the foot of this file.

## I. The text, the frame, and the two clocks

- **Where it sits in the Padma Purāṇa** — chapters **218–219 of the Uttara-khaṇḍa** (printed/AITM recension), titled "The Greatness of Puṣkara: Puṇḍarīka's Story" and "The Greatness of Puṣkara: Puṇḍarīka attains Absorption into Viṣṇu." They belong to a run of tīrtha-glorifications: Badarikāśrama (ch. 216 — see [Badrināth](../06-sacred-geography/badrinath-badarikashrama-kshetra.md)), Haridvāra (ch. 217 — see [Haridwār](../06-sacred-geography/haridwar-rishikesh-ganga-dwara.md)), **Puṣkara (chs. 218–219)**, Prayāga (chs. 220–221 — see [Prayāgrāj Triveṇī](../06-sacred-geography/prayagraj-triveni-sangam.md)), then Kāśī, Gokarṇa and others (ch. 222 — see [Kāśī](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md)). (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 216–222, AITM/Wisdomlib chapter titles*)
- **The frame dialogue — Nārada and Śibi** — the narrator is **Nārada**; the listener is a king addressed "O lord of kings," named **Śibi** in the text. Śibi's question sets the double agenda (Uttara 218.3–5): *Who is that righteous Puṇḍarīka? What act did he do, due to which the lord, being pleased, lived in his house?* — and *how did his sinful younger brother reach Śrī Hari's place by the grace of this holy place?* (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 218.3–5*)
  - *Note (identity of the king):* whether this frame-Śibi is the famous hawk-and-dove emperor **[Śibi Auśīnara](shibi.md)** is not spelled out in the chapter itself; the name alone is shared. [disputed]
- **A second, Vaiṣṇava Puṣkara-māhātmya** — the Padma's *main* Puṣkara charter is in the **Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa** (Brahmā's yajña, the substitute bride Gāyatrī, **Sāvitrī's curse** — canonical home: [Puṣkara — Brahmā Tīrtha](../06-sacred-geography/pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md)). Uttara 218–219 glorifies the same lake on a wholly different register: not Brahmā's rite but **Viṣṇu-bhakti**, sinner-salvation, and sāyujya. The two māhātmyas coexist in one Purāṇa and are kept distinct here. (*Padma P., Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa Puṣkara chs.; Uttara-khaṇḍa 218–219*)
- **Scriptural clock** — the tale is undated on the yuga-wheel: a human-world story of brāhmaṇa householders, told by Nārada inside the Purāṇic frame. Its genre (tīrtha-māhātmya with phala-śruti) addresses the ordinary pilgrim of the Kali age, and this corpus files its events with the Kali-facing scriptural material.
- **Academic clock [scholarly]** — the Uttara-khaṇḍa's māhātmya chapters are widely treated as among the Padma Purāṇa's **latest strata** (the Purāṇa as a whole compiled roughly 4th–15th c. CE, core layers c. 750–1000 CE, revised into the 14th–16th c.); this tale is part of that late devotional library. [late text] [scholarly]

## II. Chapter 218 — Mālava's gift; Puṇḍarīka the worthy recipient

- **Nārada opens the māhātmya** — "O illustrious one, listen to the very wonderful greatness, causing propitiousness, of the holy place [Puṣkara], situated here." (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 218, opening*)
- **Mālava of Vidarbha** — a learned brāhmaṇa of **Vidarbha-city**, devoted to Viṣṇu, travels to bathe in the **[Godāvarī](../06-sacred-geography/godavari-gautami-dakshina-ganga.md)**, carrying **ten thousand palas of gold** to distribute to brāhmaṇas. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 218*)
- **Half to the nephew** — judging his **sister's son Puṇḍarīka** the "crest-jewel of worthy recipients" (*supātra* — the fit vessel that makes a gift bear fruit), Mālava gives **half the gold to Puṇḍarīka** and distributes the rest to learned brāhmaṇas. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 218*) — for the dāna-and-pātra logic, see [tīrtha & tīrtha-yātrā](../05-concepts-and-dharma/tirtha-and-tirtha-yatra.md).
- **Puṇḍarīka gives it onward** — having bathed in the Godāvarī, Puṇḍarīka gladly gives a **fourth part of his wealth** to learned brāhmaṇas — the worthy recipient is himself a giver, the māhātmya's first proof of his righteousness. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 218*)

## III. Chapter 218 — Bharata, the sinful brother, dies at Puṣkara

- **Bharata's catalogue of sins** — Puṇḍarīka's **younger brother Bharata** is everything Puṇḍarīka is not: he dallies with the prostitute **Prabhāvatī**, ruins himself **gambling**, and turns to **theft**; in a violent entanglement with merchants pursuing a thief, a **brāhmaṇa is killed**. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 218*)
- **Found dying at the lake** — arriving at **Puṣkara**, Puṇḍarīka discovers Bharata **mortally wounded, covered in blood**, and hears from him the full confession of his ruined life; the chapter closes its flashback with the merchants departing in the morning. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 218*)
- **The sinner rises anyway** — dying at the tīrtha, Bharata attains a celestial state and addresses his brother from **a wonderful aerial vehicle with attendants**, declaring: "O very intelligent Puṇḍarīka, due to the grace of this holy place, Puṣkara, I, though a sinner, secured a position in heaven." (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 218–219*)
  - *Comparanda (this corpus):* the fallen-sinner-saved-at-the-brink pattern is the same engine as **[Ajāmila](ajamila.md)** (saved by the accidental Name, *Bhāgavata P.* 6.1–3) and the ghost **Dhundhukārī** (saved by Bhāgavata-recitation, [Bhāgavata-māhātmya](bhagavata-mahatmya-gokarna-dhundhukari.md)) — here the saving instrument is the **place itself**. Traditions kept distinct, not merged.

## IV. Chapter 219 — the hidden merit: a stranger's dead child

- **Puṇḍarīka's question** — "Hearing these words of him, the noble-minded Puṇḍarīka spoke to his brother, when his companions were listening": by what merit does a man of such sins reach heaven? (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 219, opening*)
- **The one good act** — Bharata recalls a single deed: "Once, after having snatched wealth, I, while coming back to my house, saw a dead, helpless child of someone in the market. Putting it on my head, taking it to the auspicious bank of **[Gaṅgā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganga.md)** and adorning it with a garment etc., I performed his funeral rites like burning his body." (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 219*)
- **Stolen money, saving merit** — the rite was financed by his ill-gotten (gambling/robbery) wealth, yet the compassionate funeral of an unclaimed child — a duty no one owed — is the merit that steered him to die at Puṣkara and thence to heaven; the tīrtha's grace completes what the single act began. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 219*) — for śrāddha-logic see [śrāddha & pitṛ rites](../05-concepts-and-dharma/shraddha-and-pitr-rites.md).
- **Bharata ascends** — his account given, Bharata goes to heaven; the māhātmya's first movement (the sinner saved by Puṣkara) is complete, answering the first half of Śibi's question. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 218–219*)

## V. Chapter 219 — the Māgha month: Viṣṇu lives in Puṇḍarīka's house

- **The bath and the wish** — witnessing his brother's ascent, Puṇḍarīka bathes at the holy place with one desire: **"May Viṣṇu in his original form live in my house during the month of Māgha."** (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 219*) — for Māgha's special sanctity (and the Padma's own Māgha-māhātmya) see [māsa-māhātmyas: Kārtika, Māgha, Vaiśākha](masa-mahatmyas-kartika-magha-vaishakha.md).
- **Preparing the house** — Puṇḍarīka adorns his home, **feeds two hundred brāhmaṇas**, and celebrates a festival in expectation of the guest. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 219*)
- **The Lord as house-guest** — **Viṣṇu comes and resides in Puṇḍarīka's house for the entire month of Māgha**, worshipped daily with **tulasī leaves, flowers, incense** and manifold offerings — the boldest form of the bhakti ideal: not a visit, not a vision, but God as month-long resident. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 219*) — on Viṣṇu's forms see [Viṣṇu — forms & vyūhas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md).

## VI. The sāyujya — absorption into Viṣṇu

- **The promise on the full moon** — as Māgha ends at its **full moon (Māghī pūrṇimā)** — the very day the [Puṣkara site tradition](../06-sacred-geography/pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md) holds most sacred for bathing — "the lotus-eyed" Viṣṇu promises Puṇḍarīka absorption into himself and bids him mount **[Garuḍa](../08-beings-and-bestiary/garuda.md)**, "the lord of birds," to return with him to Puṣkara. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 219*)
  - *Interpretive note:* the translation's epithet here — **the lotus-eyed** (*puṇḍarīkākṣa*) receiving the man named **White Lotus** (*puṇḍarīka*) at the lotus-tīrtha (*puṣkara* = "lotus") of the Lotus-Purāṇa (*padma*) — is the māhātmya's quiet four-fold pun. [interpretive]
- **The merging** — at Puṣkara, "from Puṇḍarīka's body a flash went out along with his vital breath and reached Viṣṇu's position": the devotee's prāṇa enters the Lord's own form. This is **sāyujya-mukti** — union/absorption, the most radical of the graded liberations (*sālokya* same-world, *sāmīpya* nearness, *sārūpya* same-form, *sāyujya* merging; see [karma–saṃsāra–mokṣa](../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md)). (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 219*)
- **Phala-śruti** — Nārada closes to the king: "O king, by devoutly listening to or by reciting the greatness, a man obtains the fruit of a **horse-sacrifice**." (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 219, closing*) — the same aśvamedha-equivalence the epic already grants a Puṣkara full-moon bath (*Mahābhārata*, Vana Parva, Tīrthayātrā section; see the [site file](../06-sacred-geography/pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md)).

## VII. What the tale is arguing

- **A two-panel proof of Puṣkara's greatness** — panel one: the tīrtha saves **Bharata**, whose one compassionate act it multiplies past a life of sin (grace to the undeserving). Panel two: it crowns **Puṇḍarīka**, whose sustained bhakti it answers with the *highest* liberation (grace to the deserving). Between them the māhātmya covers the whole moral range of its audience. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 218–219*)
- **Puṣkara made Vaiṣṇava** — the Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa's Puṣkara is **Brahmā's** (yajña, Sāvitrī, Gāyatrī); Uttara 218–219 re-centers the same lake on **Viṣṇu** without mentioning the curse-cycle at all — a window on how late Vaiṣṇava editors annexed a Brahmā-tīrtha into the bhakti economy. [scholarly] (*Padma P., Sṛṣṭi- vs Uttara-khaṇḍa*)
- **Sāyujya as the prize** — unlike Ajāmila (borne bodily to Vaikuṇṭha) or Puṇḍalīka of Pandharpur (who makes the Lord *stay*, fixed on a brick, for all future devotees), this Puṇḍarīka's story ends in **dissolution of the devotee into the deity** — the māhātmya genre's most absolute possible endorsement of its tīrtha. (*Padma P., Uttara-khaṇḍa 219; contrast files linked*)

## VIII. The other Puṇḍarīkas — full disambiguation

- **Puṇḍalīka (Puṇḍarīka) of Pandharpur** [regional] — the Marathi/Vārkarī devotee whose service to his parents made Kṛṣṇa wait on a **brick** (*viṭ*), origin of **Viṭhobā**; told in the *Pāṇḍuraṅga-māhātmya* (Skanda P.; also a Padma P. recension) [late text]. Canonical home: [Vithoba / Viṭṭhal](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vithoba-vitthal.md); site: [Pandharpur & the Vārī](../06-sacred-geography/pandharpur-vitthala-wari.md). **Not this Puṇḍarīka.**
- **King Puṇḍarīka of the Kāmadā Ekādaśī kathā** — the gandharva-king before whom the singer Lalita falters, in the Ekādaśī cycle likewise carried by the Padma's late strata — see [Ekādaśī-māhātmyas catalog](ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md). A third Padma-adjacent Puṇḍarīka, again distinct.
- **Puṇḍarīka the diggaja** — the **south-east quarter-elephant** (Agni's direction; mate **Kapilā**) among the eight diggajas — see [Dikpālas & Lokapālas](../08-beings-and-bestiary/dikpalas-and-lokapalas.md) and [Airāvata & celestial animals](../08-beings-and-bestiary/airavata-and-celestial-animals.md).
- **Puṇḍarīkākṣa / Puṇḍarīkavallī** — "lotus-eyed" as an epithet of Viṣṇu appears throughout iconography and the [108 Divya Deśams](../06-sacred-geography/108-divya-desams.md) (e.g. **Puṇḍarīkākṣan** of Tiruvellarai; **Puṇḍarīkavallī** as a Thāyār name at several deśams) — names, not this person.
- **Puṇḍarīka Viṭṭhala** [scholarly] — the 16th-century **musicologist** (author of the *Nartana-nirṇaya*) — see [Nāṭya-śāstra & the classical arts](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/natya-shastra-and-the-classical-arts.md). A historical author who, confusingly, bears *both* names.
