# Jaya & Vijaya — the Three-Birth Curse

> The connective thread of the avatāra-cycle: Viṣṇu's two Vaikuṇṭha gatekeepers, cursed by the Sanaka-kumāras to be born as asuras **three times**, slain by the Lord in each yuga — so that every great demon-slaying of the Daśāvatāra is the *same two souls* working off one curse.
> Sits across the current [Mahāyuga 28](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md) (Satya → Tretā → Dvāpara). Theological frame from the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (skandhas 3 & 7).
> Up one level: [Deep Dives README](../04-deep-dives/README.md).

**Jaya** ("Victory") and **Vijaya** ("Triumph") are the two **dvāra-pālas** (doorkeepers) of **Vaikuṇṭha**, Viṣṇu's transcendent abode. A single act of pride at the gate earns them a curse that ejects them from the Lord's presence and threads them, as enemies, through three world-ages — Hiraṇyākṣa & Hiraṇyakaśipu in Satya, Rāvaṇa & Kumbhakarṇa in Tretā, Śiśupāla & Dantavakra in Dvāpara. The doctrine that frames it (**dveṣa-bhakti** — that constant, total hatred fixes the mind on God as surely as love) makes the curse a disguised mercy: each death by Viṣṇu's own hand is a return home.

## The gate of Vaikuṇṭha — the offence

- **The Sanaka-kumāras arrive** - The **four Kumāras** — *Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanātana, Sanatkumāra*, the mind-born (mānasa-putra) child-sages of Brahmā — wander to Vaikuṇṭha to behold Viṣṇu. Though immensely old and pure, their *tapas* keeps them in the form of naked five-year-olds (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.15). See [Saptarṣi & the Kumāras context](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md).
- **The gatekeepers bar the way** - **Jaya and Vijaya**, taking the child-sages for mere children and finding their nakedness unfit for the inner court, **block the seventh gate**, saying the Lord rests and may not be disturbed (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.15). This is the seed-act of the whole arc.
- **The Kumāras' curse** - Enraged at being denied sight of the Lord — who, they declare, denies *no one* — the Kumāras pronounce that these two, having a doorkeeper's discriminating mind unfit for Vaikuṇṭha, shall **fall to the mortal world and be born among the impious**, subject to **kāma, krodha, lobha** (lust, anger, greed) (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.15–16).
- **Viṣṇu appears & accepts the curse** - Viṣṇu himself comes to the gate, bows to the Kumāras, and accepts the verdict on his servants' behalf; the curse of a brāhmaṇa-sage **cannot be revoked**, only its terms softened (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.16). The Kumāras, mollified by the Lord's grace, repent their haste but stand by the sentence.

## Vishnu's two options — seven births or three

- **The choice offered** - Viṣṇu gives Jaya and Vijaya two roads back: be born **seven times as his devotees** (and return slowly), or **three times as his enemies** (and return swiftly, each life ended by his own hand) (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.16; the doctrinal exposition is repeated in 7.1, *Yudhiṣṭhira's question to Nārada*).
- **They choose enmity** - Unable to bear seven lifetimes of separation, the two choose the **three demonic births**, preferring to die quickly at the Lord's hand and so be reunited sooner — the paradox that lifelong hatred becomes the fastest path home (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 7.1).
  - *Variant (who chooses):* most retellings have **the gatekeepers** choose; some popular versions frame Viṣṇu as *imposing* the three-enemy term as the kinder option he himself selects for them. [folk]
- **Dveṣa-bhakti — liberation through hatred** - Nārada's teaching to Yudhiṣṭhira (the frame of *Bhāgavata* 7.1): one absorbed in **fear, hatred, or love** of the Lord, if the absorption is total, attains him — Śiśupāla and the rest, hating Kṛṣṇa with every breath, were merged into him at death. This is the **locus classicus** of *dveṣa-bhakti* / "antagonistic devotion." [scholarly]

## The three births — one curse across three yugas

- **Birth #1 — Satya-yuga: Hiraṇyākṣa & Hiraṇyakaśipu** - The brother-asuras, sons of **Diti** and **Kaśyapa**. **Hiraṇyākṣa** drags the Earth (Bhūmi) into the cosmic ocean and is slain by **Varāha** (the boar avatāra); **Hiraṇyakaśipu**, after tormenting his Viṣṇu-devotee son **Prahlāda**, is torn open by **Narasiṃha** (the man-lion). Two demons, **two avatāras** required (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.17–19; 7.2–8). See [Satya-yuga](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/01-satya-yuga.md) and [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
  - *Pairing note:* sources differ on the Jaya↔Vijaya assignment — most map **Jaya → Hiraṇyakaśipu, Vijaya → Hiraṇyākṣa**; the assignment is rarely fixed and some lists invert it. [disputed: which twin is which]
- **Birth #2 — Tretā-yuga: Rāvaṇa & Kumbhakarṇa** - Reborn as the **rākṣasa** brothers, sons of the sage **Viśravas** and **Kaikasī** of Laṅkā. **Rāvaṇa**, the ten-headed abductor of **Sītā**, and his giant brother **Kumbhakarṇa** are both slain by **Rāma** — now a **single avatāra** suffices for the pair, a sign of their declining strength (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 7.1, 7.10; narrated at length in the [Rāmāyaṇa](ramayana/README.md), esp. [Yuddha-kāṇḍa](ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md) and the lineage in [Uttara-kāṇḍa](ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md)). See [Tretā-yuga](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md).
  - *Pairing note:* usually **Jaya → Rāvaṇa, Vijaya → Kumbhakarṇa**. The Rāmāyaṇa itself does **not** frame these two as cursed gatekeepers — that overlay is purely the *Bhāgavata*'s; the Rāmāyaṇa gives them their own ancestry (Pulastya → Viśravas) and a separate Nandīśvara/Nalakūbara curse-cycle. [late text: the gatekeeper-frame is retrofitted onto older epic material]
- **Birth #3 — Dvāpara-yuga: Śiśupāla & Dantavakra** - Reborn as **human** kings and **cousins** of Kṛṣṇa: **Śiśupāla** of Cedi, beheaded by the **Sudarśana Cakra** at Yudhiṣṭhira's Rājasūya, and **Dantavakra** of Karūṣa, killed by Kṛṣṇa shortly after in revenge-attack. The curse's final discharge — both souls' radiance merging visibly into Kṛṣṇa (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 7.1, 10.74, 10.78). Full treatment: [Śiśupāla — the Hundred Offenses](shishupala.md). See [Dvāpara-yuga](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md).
  - *Pairing note:* usually **Jaya → Śiśupāla, Vijaya → Dantavakra**.

## The diminishing-power pattern

- **Devolution down the yugas** - The *Bhāgavata* tradition reads a deliberate **decline** in the demons across births, mirroring the decay of the yugas themselves: **asuras** ruling the whole cosmos (Satya) → **rākṣasas** ruling one island-kingdom (Tretā) → **human** kings ruling provinces (Dvāpara) (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 7.1, interpretive). See [The Four Yugas](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md).
- **Fewer avatāras needed** - A matching sign: Satya needs **two** avatāras (Varāha + Narasiṃha) for the pair, while Tretā and Dvāpara need only **one** (Rāma; Kṛṣṇa) — the demons grow weaker as the world ages. [scholarly gloss on the pattern]
- **Brothers, then cousins** - In births #1 and #2 the two are **full brothers**; in #3 they are **cousins** (Śiśupāla and Dantavakra) — the bond loosens as the curse nears its end. [folk/interpretive observation]

## Variants & textual notes

- *Variant (number of gates / which gate):* the offence occurs at Vaikuṇṭha's **seventh gate** in the common telling; the count is incidental and not consistent across retellings.
- *Variant (seven vs three):* the **3 enemy-births / 7 devotee-births** framework is the standard *Bhāgavata* form. Other Purāṇas and oral traditions sometimes omit the "seven" alternative entirely and present only the three demonic lives. [disputed across texts]
- *Variant (source spread):* the **curse-origin** (gate scene, Kumāras, two options) is told in *Bhāgavata* **skandha 3** (Kapila/Maitreya narration to Vidura); the **three-births recap and the dveṣa-bhakti doctrine** are in **skandha 7** (Nārada to Yudhiṣṭhira after Śiśupāla's death). The Mahābhārata's *Sabhā-parva*, which actually **stages** Śiśupāla's death, names neither the gatekeepers nor the Kumāra-curse — the whole scaffolding is the *Bhāgavata*'s synthesis. See [Sabhā-parva](mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md).
- *Variant (Jaya–Vijaya as other figures):* the names "Jaya" and "Vijaya" attach to several unrelated pairs in the corpus (attendants of various deities, a Gaṇa pair, etc.); only the **Vaikuṇṭha dvāra-pāla** Jaya–Vijaya carry the three-birth curse. [naming caution]
- *Variant (devotee-births reading):* a minority devotional reading holds the two **did** also fulfil devotee-lives elsewhere — but the canonical *Bhāgavata* logic is that choosing the enemy-path **replaced** the seven-life term. [folk/sectarian]

## Why it matters to the timeline

- **The single thread of the Daśāvatāra** - This curse is the narrative glue that makes Varāha, Narasiṃha, Rāma, and Kṛṣṇa **one continuous story**: the same two adversaries reappearing to be liberated. It explains *why* the avatāras keep facing structurally identical demon-pairs across the ages. See [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) and [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
- **Cross-links to all three pairs:** Satya — [Hiraṇyakaśipu/Hiraṇyākṣa in 01-Satya-yuga](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/01-satya-yuga.md) · Tretā — [Rāvaṇa/Kumbhakarṇa in the Rāmāyaṇa](ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md) · Dvāpara — [Śiśupāla/Dantavakra](shishupala.md).

Narrated by Maitreya to Vidura. Cross-ref [Bhāgavata skandha-map](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).

- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.74, 10.78** — Śiśupāla's death/merger at the Rājasūya and Dantavakra's slaying (third birth's discharge).
