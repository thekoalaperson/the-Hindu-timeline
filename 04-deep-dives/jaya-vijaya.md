# Jaya & Vijaya — the Three-Birth Curse

> The connective thread of the avatāra-cycle: Viṣṇu's two Vaikuṇṭha gatekeepers, cursed by the Sanaka-kumāras to be born as asuras **three times**, slain by the Lord in each life — so that every great demon-slaying of the Daśāvatāra is the *same two souls* working off one curse.
> Sits across the current [Mahāyuga 28](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md) (Satya → Tretā → Dvāpara), by convention (see the chronology note under *Variants*). Theological frame from the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (skandhas 3 & 7, with 9.24 and 10.74–78).
> Up one level: [Deep Dives README](README.md).

**Jaya** ("Victory") and **Vijaya** ("Triumph") are the two **dvāra-pālas** (doorkeepers) of **Vaikuṇṭha**, Viṣṇu's transcendent abode (named at *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.16.2). A single act of pride at the gate earns them a curse that ejects them from the Lord's presence and threads them, as enemies, through three births — Hiraṇyākṣa & Hiraṇyakaśipu, Rāvaṇa & Kumbhakarṇa, Śiśupāla & Dantavakra. The doctrine that frames it (the *Bhāgavata*'s **vairānubandha**, "bound by enmity", often labelled **dveṣa-bhakti** — that constant, total hatred fixes the mind on God as surely as love) makes the curse a disguised mercy: each death by Viṣṇu's own hand is a step home.

## The gate of Vaikuṇṭha — the offence

- **The Sanaka-kumāras arrive** - The **four Kumāras** — *Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanātana, Sanatkumāra*, the mind-born (mānasa-putra) child-sages of Brahmā — come to Vaikuṇṭha to behold Viṣṇu (by chance, *yadṛcchayā*, in Nārada's retelling, 7.1.36). Though the eldest of all beings, they appear as **naked boys of five** (*vāta-raśanān … daśārdha-vayasaḥ*, *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.15.30; "five or six", 7.1.37). The whole scene is told by **Brahmā to the devas**, who are alarmed by Diti's pregnancy, inside Maitreya's narration to Vidura (*Bhāgavata* 3.15.1–11, 3.16.1). See [Saptarṣi & the Kumāras context](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md).
- **The gatekeepers bar the way** - Having passed six gates unchecked, the sages are stopped at the **seventh** by two mace-bearing, four-armed doorkeepers (3.15.27–29), who **block them with their staffs, mocking their glory** (*vetreṇa cāskhalayatām … tejo vihasya*, 3.15.30). Nārada's version gives the reason: they **took the sages for mere children** (*śiśūn matvā*, 7.1.37). This is the seed-act of the whole arc.
  - *Note (the "Lord is resting" excuse):* the popular line that the doorkeepers said Viṣṇu was asleep and must not be disturbed is **not** in the Kumāra scene (3.15.27–31). The Bhāgavata attaches sleep to a different, earlier offence: Viṣṇu later reminds the pair that they once **stopped Lakṣmī herself at the gate "while I was resting"** (*mayy upārate*), and that the angry goddess **foretold** this fall (3.16.30). [folk; that the popular line borrows the 3.16.30 detail is an inference, not stated anywhere]
- **The Kumāras' curse** - Enraged at being denied sight of the Lord (3.15.31), the Kumāras declare that two who see **duality** (*antara-bhāva-dṛṣṭyā*) in Vaikuṇṭha must go down to the worlds "where these **three enemies**" (*traya ime ripavaḥ*) hold sway (3.15.34); in Nārada's retelling, they are unfit to dwell at the Lord's feet, "free of *rajas* and *tamas*", and must go to "the most sinful **asura** womb" (7.1.38). The text names only "three enemies". Identifying them is a commentators' gloss: Prabhupāda's purport to 3.15.34 gives "desire, anger and lust", and the familiar triad **kāma, krodha, lobha** (lust, anger, greed) is the *Bhagavad-gītā*'s "threefold gate of hell" (16.21), not this verse's. [translator gloss]
- **The doorkeepers' plea** - Seeing the brāhmaṇa's curse cannot be warded off by any weapon, the two fall at the sages' feet and ask only that, as they sink lower, they **never lose remembrance of the Lord** (3.15.35–36).
- **Viṣṇu appears & accepts the curse** - Viṣṇu comes to the gate with Lakṣmī (3.15.37), takes his servants' offence as his own and **asks the sages' forgiveness** (3.16.2–4), even saying he would cut off his own arm if it turned against brāhmaṇas (3.16.6). He asks that the exile be **short** (3.16.12). The Kumāras admit they have **cursed two faultless persons** and offer to accept any punishment themselves (3.16.25). Viṣṇu replies that the curse was **ordained by him** (*śāpo mayaiva nimitaḥ*, 3.16.26), and tells Jaya and Vijaya that **though able to nullify** it, he will not — it has his approval (*samartho 'pi hantuṁ necche mataṁ tu me*, 3.16.29). They fall, lustre dimmed, and enter **Diti's womb** through Kaśyapa's seed (3.16.33–35).

## The term of the curse — three births

- **The three-birth term (Bhāgavata)** - In the *Bhāgavata* there is **no choice offered**. The limit comes from the **Kumāras themselves**: as the two fall, the compassionate sages tell them they will return "**after three births**" (*janmabhir vāṁ tribhiḥ*, 7.1.39). Viṣṇu's own promise is that, absorbed in him through **anger-intensified concentration** (*saṁrambha-sambhṛta-samādhi*), they will come back **soon** (3.16.26, 3.16.31).
  - *Variant (popular retellings — "seven or three"):* the widely told version in which Viṣṇu offers **seven births as his devotees or three as his enemies**, and the pair choose enmity to return sooner, is **not in** *Bhāgavata* 3.15–16, 7.1 or 7.10. No Purāṇic locus was found; its usual modern citations are devotional websites and retellings, and some versions raise the devotee-births to a hundred. [folk; locus not verified]
  - *Variant (Rāmacaritamānasa):* Tulsīdās also gives the three-birth term as the **brāhmaṇa's word**. Of Jay and Bijay, reborn as Kumbhakarṇa and Rāvaṇa, Śiva tells Pārvatī: "Though killed by a god they did not attain to salvation, for the Brahman had doomed them to **three births**" (*Rāmacaritamānasa* Bāla-kāṇḍa; Growse tr., chaupāī after dohā 129; Gita Press numbering differs). [late text: 16th c. Avadhī]
- **Vairānubandha — liberation through hatred** - Nārada's teaching to Yudhiṣṭhira at the Rājasūya, prompted by Śiśupāla merging into Kṛṣṇa (7.1.13–21): whether by **enmity, fear, affection, lust or devotion**, one who fixes the mind wholly on the Lord reaches him (7.1.26); Nārada even holds that enmity absorbs one more intensely than bhakti-yoga (7.1.27), with the image of the worm that becomes the wasp it fears (7.1.28–29). The list: the **gopīs by kāma, Kaṁsa by fear, Śiśupāla and the kings by hatred (*dveṣa*), the Vṛṣṇis by kinship, the Pāṇḍavas by affection, Nārada's kind by bhakti** (7.1.30–31); only one like Vena, who thinks of him in none of these ways, is lost (7.1.32). The two gatekeepers, "by meditation intensified by enmity" (*vairānubandha-tīvreṇa dhyānena*), returned to Viṣṇu (7.1.47). This is the **locus classicus** of what modern writers call *dveṣa-bhakti* / "antagonistic devotion" — the Bhāgavata's own terms are *vairānubandha* and *vaira-bhāva*. [scholarly: the label]
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa — why only the third death liberates):* Parāśara explains that as **Hiraṇyakaśipu** the demon never knew the man-lion was Viṣṇu, so he gained only power and pleasure as Rāvaṇa; as **Rāvaṇa** he took Rāma for a mortal, so he gained only a royal birth in Cedi; only as **Śiśupāla**, ceaselessly uttering Kṛṣṇa's names in abuse and picturing his form, was he **united with him** at death (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 4.15; Wilson tr. pp. 438–439). Here the chain is **one soul**, with **no gatekeepers, no Kumāras and no curse** — the rebirths are a consequence of how he died.
  - *Variant (curse as pretext):* Gauḍīya theology reads the curse as a mere **pretext** (*vyāja*): "thus Jaya and Vijaya, on the pretext of the curse of Sanaka and the others, [descended] into the world solely for the Lord's pastimes" (*sanakādi-śāpa-vyājena kevalaṁ bhagavato līlārthaṁ*), so that he could have battle-pastimes (*yuddhādi-krīḍā*) to delight his devotees (Jīva Gosvāmī, *Kṛṣṇa-sandarbha* 146.10 in wisdomlib's numbering, which says it follows "the prose of the *Padma Purāṇa*'s Uttara-khaṇḍa", *padmottara-khaṇḍa-gadyānusāreṇa*; Padma locus not verified, and the Uttara-khaṇḍa 110 story below has no Kumāras). It builds on the Lord's own "ordained by me" (3.16.26). [late text; sectarian]

## The three births — one curse across three yugas

- **Birth #1 — Satya-yuga (conventional): Hiraṇyakaśipu & Hiraṇyākṣa** - The brother-asuras, twin sons of **Diti** and **Kaśyapa**, conceived when Diti pressed Kaśyapa for union at the **inauspicious twilight** — Kaśyapa foretells two cruel sons for it (*Bhāgavata* 3.14.8, 3.14.38–39): the curse in Vaikuṇṭha and the twilight conception are **two causes told side by side** for the same birth. **Hiraṇyakaśipu** is the **elder**, Hiraṇyākṣa the younger (7.1.40); but at their naming, Kaśyapa calls Hiraṇyakaśipu the twin who came first "from the body" and Hiraṇyākṣa the one Diti "delivered first" (*yaḥ prāk sva-dehād yamayor ajāyata … yaṁ taṁ hiraṇyākṣam asūta sāgrataḥ*, 3.17.18). The BBT translation reads this as Hiraṇyakaśipu *conceived* first and Hiraṇyākṣa *born* first [translator gloss], so which twin is the elder depends on how the verse is read. Hiraṇyākṣa finds **Varāha** (the boar avatāra) in the ocean depths already **raising the sunken Earth** on his tusks, claims the Earth as belonging to "us of the lower regions", and is slain (3.13.30–31, 3.18.1–6, 3.19); **Hiraṇyakaśipu**, after tormenting his Viṣṇu-devotee son **Prahlāda**, is torn open by **Narasiṃha** (the man-lion) (7.1.41–43; 7.8). Two demons, **two avatāras** required. See [Satya-yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/01-satya-yuga.md) and [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
  - *Note (Earth's drowning):* the *Bhāgavata* has the Earth **already submerged** at the start of creation: it "was flooded by the waters and sank to Rasā" while Brahmā was creating (3.13.17, 3.13.30). It does not say Hiraṇyākṣa dragged it there.
  - *Variant (Liṅga Purāṇa):* here Hiraṇyākṣa, after defeating the devas, **bound the Earth and carried her to the nether regions**, holding her prisoner, until Viṣṇu as Yajña-varāha killed him with his tusk and brought her up (*Liṅga Purāṇa* 1.94.4–14; Shastri ed., AITM vol. 5). No gatekeepers or curse appear in this chapter. This is the Purāṇic source for the popular "Hiraṇyākṣa stole the Earth" image.
- **Birth #2 — Tretā-yuga: Rāvaṇa & Kumbhakarṇa** - Reborn as the **rākṣasa** brothers, sons of the sage **Viśravas** — by **Keśinī** in the *Bhāgavata* (7.1.44), and by **Kaikasī**, daughter of the rākṣasa Sumālī, in the Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa* (Uttara-kāṇḍa 9, Shastri tr.). **Rāvaṇa**, the ten-headed abductor of **Sītā**, and his giant brother **Kumbhakarṇa** are both slain by **Rāma**, who came "to free them from the curse" (*śāpa-muktaye*, 7.1.45); pierced by his arrows, they died absorbed in him as before (7.10.36–37). A **single avatāra** now suffices for the pair. The *Bhāgavata*'s own Rāma narrative is 9.10–11; the story at length is the [Rāmāyaṇa](ramayana/README.md), esp. [Yuddha-kāṇḍa](ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md) and the lineage in [Uttara-kāṇḍa](ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md). See [Tretā-yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md).
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata, Rāmopākhyāna):* Kubera gives Viśravas three rākṣasīs, **Puṣpotkaṭā, Rākā and Mālinī**. Puṣpotkaṭā bears **Kumbhakarṇa and Rāvaṇa**, Mālinī bears Vibhīṣaṇa, and Rākā bears the twins Khara and Śūrpaṇakhā (*Mahābhārata*, Vana-parva, Ganguli tr. §CCLXXIII). There is no gatekeeper frame here either.
  - *Note (Vālmīki):* the Rāmāyaṇa itself does **not** frame these two as cursed gatekeepers — that overlay is the Purāṇas'; the Rāmāyaṇa gives them their own ancestry (Pulastya → Viśravas) and its own curses on Rāvaṇa, e.g. **Nandīśvara**'s (monkeys will destroy him, Uttara-kāṇḍa 16) and **Nalakūbara**'s (his head will split if he forces an unwilling woman, Uttara-kāṇḍa 26). [late text: the gatekeeper-frame is retrofitted onto older epic material]
  - *Variant (Rāmacaritamānasa — one cause among several):* in Tulsīdās's poem, Śiva tells Pārvatī that Rāma's births have **many causes, differing kalpa by kalpa**. In one, Hari's doorkeepers **Jay and Bijay**, cursed by a brāhmaṇa, became Hiraṇyakaśipu & Hiraṇyākṣa and then **Kumbhakarṇa & Rāvaṇa**, and Kaśyapa–Aditi became Daśaratha–Kausalyā. In **another kalpa** the demon **Jalandhara** was protected by his wife's virtue; Hari broke her vow by a stratagem, she cursed Hari, and Hari "did according to her curse", while Jalandhara was born as Rāvaṇa and was slain by Rāma. In another, **Nārada's curse** drove the incarnation (*Rāmacaritamānasa* Bāla-kāṇḍa; Growse tr. dohās 128–131; Gita Press numbering differs). The Mānas goes on to the Manu–Śatarūpā boon and to King **Pratāpabhānu**, cursed by brāhmaṇas to be reborn as Rāvaṇa (Bāla-kāṇḍa, c. dohās 153–175 in the Gita Press numbering used by ramcharit.in). [late text; regional]
- **Birth #3 — Dvāpara-yuga: Śiśupāla & Dantavakra** - Reborn as **kṣatriya** kings (*kṣatriyau*, 7.1.46) and **cousins** of Kṛṣṇa and the Pāṇḍavas — sons of Vasudeva's and Kuntī's sisters (7.1.33; 9.24.28–31): **Śiśupāla** of Cedi, son of Damaghoṣa and **Śrutaśravā** (9.24.39–40), beheaded by Kṛṣṇa's **discus** at Yudhiṣṭhira's Rājasūya (10.74.43); **Dantavakra**, king of **Karūṣa**, son of Vṛddhaśarman and **Śrutadevā** (9.24.37), who later attacked Kṛṣṇa alone to avenge his friends Śiśupāla, Śālva and Pauṇḍraka and was killed by Kṛṣṇa's **Kaumodakī mace** (10.78.1–9). From each body a **light rose and entered Kṛṣṇa** before everyone (10.74.45; 10.78.10); Śiśupāla, "obsessed with hatred through **three lifetimes**", attained him (10.74.46). The curse's final discharge (7.1.46–47; 7.10.38). Full treatment: [Śiśupāla — the Hundred Offenses](shishupala.md). See [Dvāpara-yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md).
  - *Textual note (how Dantavakra dies):* Nārada's summary says both had "their sin destroyed by Kṛṣṇa's **discus**" (*kṛṣṇa-cakra-hatāṁhasau*, 7.1.46; BBT renders it "Kṛṣṇa has struck them with His disc" [translator gloss]), and 7.10.38 has both merging "while you [Yudhiṣṭhira] watched" (*paśyatas te*). The Tenth Skandha instead has Dantavakra killed **by the mace** after Śiśupāla's death (10.78.1–9), in the fighting after the Śālva war, after which Kṛṣṇa re-enters his own city (10.78.13–15). The disc in 10.78 takes Dantavakra's younger brother **Vidūratha** (10.78.11–13). [disputed: internal tension in the Bhāgavata]
- **Which twin is which** - The *Bhāgavata* **never says** which soul is Jaya and which Vijaya: it speaks of "the two" (*tau*) throughout (7.1.40–47; 7.10.35–38). The texts do trace **soul-lines**: the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* runs **Hiraṇyakaśipu → Rāvaṇa → Śiśupāla** as one being (4.14; 4.15), and *Bhāgavata* 9.24.37 calls Dantavakra "the **ṛṣi-cursed son of Diti**" (*ṛṣi-śapto diteḥ sutaḥ*) — the BBT translation glosses this as Hiraṇyākṣa [translator gloss] — giving **Hiraṇyākṣa → Kumbhakarṇa → Dantavakra** for the other. The *names* are assigned only in secondary works, and they disagree: Wikipedia ("Jaya-Vijaya") gives **Jaya** = Hiraṇyakaśipu, Rāvaṇa, Śiśupāla and **Vijaya** = Hiraṇyākṣa, Kumbhakarṇa, Dantavakra; Vettam Mani's *Puranic Encyclopaedia* (s.v. Kumbhakarṇa, citing "Bhāgavata, 7th Skandha") gives **Jaya** = Hiraṇyākṣa, Rāvaṇa, Śiśupāla and **Vijaya** = Hiraṇyakaśipu, Kumbhakarṇa, Dantavaktra. That is internally consistent but **crosses** both textual soul-lines above; its s.v. Jaya entry names them only as a pair. [disputed: which twin is which]

## The diminishing-power pattern

- **Devolution down the ages** - Later readers see a deliberate **decline** in the demons across births, mirroring the decay of the yugas themselves: **asuras** ruling the whole cosmos → **rākṣasas** ruling one island-kingdom → **kṣatriya** kings ruling provinces. The texts give only the bare sequence (7.1.40–46; 7.10.35–38); the reading is not stated there. [folk/interpretive] See [The Four Yugas](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md).
- **Fewer avatāras needed** - A matching sign: the first pair needs **two** avatāras (Varāha + Narasiṃha), while the second and third need only **one** each (Rāma; Kṛṣṇa) — the demons grow weaker as the world ages. [folk/interpretive gloss on the pattern] The *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* reads the sequence differently. Each rebirth is the **fruit of the previous death** at Viṣṇu's hands: Hiraṇyakaśipu's death earned "unlimited power and enjoyment" as Daśānana, "sovereign of the three spheres", and Rāvaṇa's earned the royal Cedi birth. Union comes only when the demon finally sees Kṛṣṇa as the supreme at the moment of death (4.15; Wilson tr. pp. 438–439). On this reading Rāvaṇa is a reward, not a decline.
- **Brothers, then cousins** - In births #1 and #2 the two are **full brothers**; in #3 they are **cousins**, sons of two sisters (*Bhāgavata* 9.24.37, 9.24.40; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 4.14) — the bond loosens as the curse nears its end. [folk/interpretive observation]

## Variants & textual notes

- *Variant (who the gatekeepers were before — Padma Purāṇa):* the *Padma Purāṇa* gives the gatekeepers a **prehistory**: Jaya (elder) and Vijaya (younger) were the two sons of **Kardama and Devahūti**, devotees of Viṣṇu who served as priests at King **Marutta**'s sacrifice, **quarrelled over the fee**, and cursed each other — Vijaya to become a **crocodile**, Jaya an **elephant**. Born on the bank of the Gaṇḍakī, the elephant was seized by the crocodile while bathing, called on Viṣṇu, and the Lord's **discus** freed both and took them to Vaikuṇṭha as his doorkeepers; the place became **Harikṣetra** (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa 110.2–29, Deshpande tr.). The Kumāras and the three demonic births do not appear. The same story, nearly word for word and in the same frame (the attendants instructing Dharmadatta), is in the *Skanda Purāṇa*, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa, Kārttikamāsa-māhātmya 28.1–23 (Motilal Banarsidass tr., via wisdomlib.org). The elephant-and-crocodile rescue is a Gajendra-like story, but this chapter does not name Gajendra. [late text]
- *Textual note (number of gates):* the offence is at Vaikuṇṭha's **seventh gate**, after six passed unchecked (*Bhāgavata* 3.15.27, 3.15.29); Nārada's retelling in 7.1.36–38 gives no gate count.
- *Textual note (chronology of birth #1):* the *Bhāgavata* names no yuga for the first pair. It places Varāha's raising of the Earth at the **start of Svāyambhuva Manu's era** (3.13.1–31), within the same Maitreya narration that then tells of Diti's twins (3.14–19). The Satya-yuga / Mahāyuga-28 placement used across this corpus is the conventional one. [disputed]
- *Variant (seven vs three):* the **3 enemy-births / 7 devotee-births** choice is a **popular retelling**, not the *Bhāgavata*'s form — there the three-birth term is the Kumāras' mitigation (7.1.39) and the swift return Viṣṇu's promise (3.16.26, 31). See the variant under *The term of the curse*. [folk; locus not verified]
- *Variant (devotee-births reading):* in the popular "seven or three" telling, some devotional readings hold that the two **did** also live devotee-lives elsewhere; in that telling's own logic, choosing the enemy-path **replaced** the seven-life term. Neither belongs to the *Bhāgavata*'s text. [folk/sectarian]
- *Variant (source spread):* the **curse-origin** (gate scene, Kumāras, Viṣṇu's acceptance, the fall into Diti's womb) is told in *Bhāgavata* **skandha 3** (3.15–16, Brahmā to the devas, within Maitreya's narration to Vidura); the **three-births recap and the vairānubandha doctrine** are in **skandha 7** (7.1 and 7.10.35–40, Nārada to Yudhiṣṭhira after Śiśupāla's death); the third birth's parentage and deaths are at 9.24.37–40, 10.74 and 10.78. The Mahābhārata's *Sabhā-parva*, which actually **stages** Śiśupāla's death — and already has his radiance (*tejas*) rise from the body and **enter Kṛṣṇa** before the kings (Ganguli tr. §XLIV) — names neither the gatekeepers nor the Kumāra-curse. The rebirth chain **Hiraṇyakaśipu → Rāvaṇa → Śiśupāla** is already in the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* (4.14–15), with no gatekeepers. So the **gatekeeper-and-Kumāra frame** is the *Bhāgavata*'s synthesis; the merger and the rebirth chain are older. [scholarly: relative dating of the Viṣṇu and Bhāgavata Purāṇas] See [Sabhā-parva](mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md).
- *Variant (Jaya–Vijaya as other figures):* the names "Jaya" and "Vijaya" attach to several unrelated pairs (attendants of various deities, a Gaṇa pair, etc.); only the **Vaikuṇṭha dvāra-pāla** Jaya–Vijaya carry the three-birth curse. [naming caution]

## Why it matters to the timeline

- **The single thread of the Daśāvatāra** - This curse is the narrative glue that makes Varāha, Narasiṃha, Rāma, and Kṛṣṇa **one continuous story**: the same two adversaries reappearing to be liberated. It explains *why* the avatāras keep facing structurally identical demon-pairs across the ages. See [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) and [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).

- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Skandha 3** (chs. 13–19) — Varāha raises the sunken Earth (3.13); Diti's twilight conception (3.14); the Vaikuṇṭha gate, the Kumāras' curse, Viṣṇu's acceptance, Lakṣmī's earlier warning, and the fall into Diti's womb (3.15–16, told by Brahmā to the devas); the **first** birth (Hiraṇyākṣa & Varāha, 3.17–19). - **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.24.37–40** — parentage of Dantavakra (Śrutadevā, Vṛddhaśarman of Karūṣa) and Śiśupāla (Śrutaśravā).
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.74.43–50, 10.78.1–12** — Śiśupāla's death and merger at the Rājasūya; Dantavakra's slaying by the mace (third birth's discharge). Text and translation consulted at vedabase.io (BBT).
