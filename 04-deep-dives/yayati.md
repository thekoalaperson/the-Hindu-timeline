# Yayāti — the Curse of Old Age & the Exchange of Youth

> Where this sits: a Chandravamsha (Lunar dynasty) episode of the deep past, narrated as ancestral history in the great epics. Parent: [04-deep-dives](./) · Lineage context: [../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) · Cosmic slot: long before the [Kali Yuga](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/), in the 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara, 28th Mahayuga.

Yayāti, son of Nahuṣa, fifth king of the Chandravamsha, is the pivot on which the entire Lunar dynasty turns: his curse of premature old age, the sons' refusal or gift of their youth, and the resulting disinheritance of the eldest split the line into the **Yādava** (via Yadu → Kṛṣṇa) and **Paurava/Kaurava** (via Pūru → Bharata → Kuru → Pāṇḍavas) branches. His closing speech — that desire is never quenched by indulgence but inflamed by it — is one of the most quoted renunciation passages in the Mahābhārata. Principal sources: **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva 71–93** (the Sambhava and Yayāti upākhyānas) and **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.18–19**; with material in the **Matsya** and **Vishnu Purāṇas** and the supplementary fall-from-heaven and Mādhavī episodes in the **Udyoga Parva**.

---

## I. Backstory — Kacha, Devayānī, and the seeds of the curse

- **Kacha enters Śukra's hermitage** - Bṛhaspati's son Kacha is sent by the devas to serve Śukrācārya (guru of the asuras) for a thousand years to steal the **Mṛtasañjīvanī vidyā** (mantra to revive the dead). (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~71–72)
- **Devayānī falls in love with Kacha** - Śukra's daughter Devayānī is infatuated with the youth; the asuras, fearing the secret will leak, kill Kacha three times — he is revived each time at Devayānī's pleading, once ground to paste and mixed in Śukra's wine so the guru himself drinks him, forcing Śukra to teach Kacha the mantra from within his own belly. (Ādi Parva 72)
- **Mutual curses** - Kacha completes his training and refuses to marry Devayānī (declaring her his "sister," since he was reborn from her father's body). Devayānī curses his learning to fail him; Kacha counter-curses that **no sage's son will ever marry her** — sealing her future marriage to a kṣatriya, Yayāti. (Ādi Parva 72–73)

---

## II. The quarrel by the well — Devayānī and Śarmiṣṭhā

- **The bathing dispute** - Devayānī and **Śarmiṣṭhā** (daughter of the asura king Vṛṣaparvan) bathe together; Indra as a gust of wind mixes up their garments. The two quarrel over precedence — sage's-daughter vs. king's-daughter. (Ādi Parva 75–76)
- **Śarmiṣṭhā throws Devayānī into a dry well** - In fury Śarmiṣṭhā casts the naked Devayānī into a waterless well and leaves her for dead. (Ādi Parva 76)
- **Yayāti rescues her** - King Yayāti, hunting, finds Devayānī in the well, takes her right hand to pull her out; she treats the hand-clasp as a betrothal. He departs to his kingdom. (Ādi Parva 76; Bhāgavata 9.18)
- **Śukra's leverage** - Devayānī refuses to return until avenged. Vṛṣaparvan, needing Śukra (his dynasty's only source of the revival-mantra), capitulates: **Śarmiṣṭhā with a thousand maidens is given to Devayānī as a serving-maid**. (Ādi Parva 78–81)
- **Marriage of Yayāti and Devayānī** - Śukra weds Devayānī to Yayāti, with a strict condition: **Yayāti must never summon Śarmiṣṭhā to his bed.** (Ādi Parva 82; Bhāgavata 9.18)

---

## III. The two queens and the five sons

- **Śarmiṣṭhā's appeal** - Now serving in Yayāti's palace and herself of age, Śarmiṣṭhā privately asks Yayāti to father her child, arguing it is the king's dharma to grant a maiden in season a son. Yayāti yields, secretly. (Ādi Parva 83; Bhāgavata 9.18)
- **Devayānī's sons** - Devayānī bears **Yadu** (eldest) and **Turvaśu**. (Ādi Parva 84; Bhāgavata 9.18)
- **Śarmiṣṭhā's sons** - Śarmiṣṭhā bears **Druhyu**, **Anu** (Anudruhyu), and **Pūru** (youngest of all five). (Ādi Parva 84; Bhāgavata 9.18)
  - *Variant (lists/order):* Mahābhārata and Bhāgavata agree on the five names and that Yadu is eldest and Pūru youngest; the middle ordering (Turvaśu / Druhyu / Anu) is given consistently as Yadu–Turvaśu–Druhyu–Anu–Pūru in the Bhāgavata's enumeration.
- **A daughter, Mādhavī** - In the extended Mahābhārata tradition Yayāti also has a daughter, **Mādhavī**, central to the later Gālava/Udyoga episode (see §VII). (Mahābhārata, Udyoga Parva)

---

## IV. Śukra's curse of premature old age

- **The secret exposed** - Devayānī discovers Śarmiṣṭhā's children resemble the king; confronting Śarmiṣṭhā she learns the truth, and reports the broken oath to her father. (Ādi Parva 85; Bhāgavata 9.18)
- **The curse** - Enraged that his daughter has been wronged, Śukra curses Yayāti to be **instantly overtaken by jarā (decrepit old age)**, despite his being in the prime of life. (Ādi Parva 85; Bhāgavata 9.18)
- **The single concession** - Yayāti pleads that desire is still unspent in him. Śukra's curse cannot be unsaid, but he grants a loophole: **Yayāti may transfer his old age to any willing person and take that person's youth in exchange.** (Ādi Parva 85; Bhāgavata 9.18)
  - *Variant (motive, Bhāgavata):* the Bhāgavata frames Śukra's concession and Yayāti's long indulgence as a moral exemplum on the futility of kāma rather than a tale of dynastic intrigue, with Yayāti's renunciation as the climax.

---

## V. The sons asked — refusal and the gift

- **Yayāti summons his sons** - The aged king asks each son in turn to take on his old age and lend his youth for a fixed term. (Ādi Parva 85–88; Bhāgavata 9.18)
- **Yadu refuses** - The eldest, Yadu, declines, unwilling to surrender his youth.
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata):* Yadu refuses saying youth spent in old age would obstruct his future renunciation/spiritual aim; he is unwilling to bear the misery of decrepitude. (Bhāgavata 9.18)
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata):* Yadu's refusal is plain reluctance; Yayāti curses him that **his descendants will never sit on the throne / never be anointed king** — the scriptural charter for why the senior Yādava line does not hold the imperial seat. (Ādi Parva 85)
- **Turvaśu, Druhyu, Anu refuse** - The next three sons likewise decline; Yayāti pronounces curses fixing each line's lesser fate. (Ādi Parva 85; Bhāgavata 9.18)
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata curses):* Turvaśu's line is cursed to rule among mleccha/mixed peoples and to lose proper dharma; Druhyu's to dwell without a kingdom of horses/chariots in a region without proper roads; Anu's to suffer untimely deaths and bear the fire-priest's duties — each curse retrofitted to that branch's later, marginal history.
- **Pūru gives his youth** - The youngest, **Pūru** (Śarmiṣṭhā's son), gladly accepts his father's old age and gives his own youth, honoring filial duty. (Ādi Parva 88; Bhāgavata 9.18–19)
  - *Variant (term):* the Mahābhārata specifies a fixed exchange-period of **one thousand years**, after which the youth is to be returned. (Ādi Parva 88)

---

## VI. A thousand years of indulgence — and the great renunciation

- **Yayāti enjoys all pleasures** - Restored to youth via Pūru, Yayāti rules and revels for a thousand years, even sporting with the celestial nymph **Viśvācī** in Nandana / Caitraratha gardens, exhausting every sensual delight. (Ādi Parva 88; Bhāgavata 9.19)
- **The realization** - He finds desire only grows with feeding. His celebrated verse: *desire is never appeased by enjoying objects of desire; it only flares the more, as fire fed with ghee (clarified butter).* (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva 88; Bhāgavata 9.19.14)
- **Return of the youth** - Recognizing indulgence as bottomless, Yayāti **takes back his old age and returns Pūru's youth**, fulfilling the exchange ahead of compulsion. (Ādi Parva 88; Bhāgavata 9.19)
- **Pūru crowned heir** - In gratitude Yayāti **passes over his four elder sons and installs Pūru as universal sovereign**, placing the elder brothers under him. (Ādi Parva 88; Bhāgavata 9.19)
- **Yayāti retires to the forest** - The king departs to the forest as an ascetic and ultimately attains svarga (heaven) by his merits. (Ādi Parva 88; Bhāgavata 9.19)

---

## VII. The fall-from-heaven coda & Mādhavī (Udyoga Parva)

- **Pride in heaven** - Enjoying svarga, Yayāti boasts that none equals his merits; Indra, displeased by the conceit, casts him out — but his merit halts his fall in the firmament. (Mahābhārata, Udyoga Parva ~120)
- **Saved by grandsons** - He lands among four royal sages — **Aṣṭaka, Pratardana, Vasumanas, and Śibi** — grandsons through his daughter **Mādhavī**, who share their accumulated merit with him and restore him to heaven; the episode establishes the spiritual standing of daughters' sons. (Udyoga Parva ~120–122)
- **Mādhavī's vow** - Mādhavī, repeatedly "lent" by the sage Gālava to kings to gather dowry-horses (each time regaining virginity), finally chooses the ascetic forest life over remarriage — a striking sub-tale of agency. (Udyoga Parva; [folk/feminist-reading emphasis])

## VIII. Why this episode matters — the Chandravamsha bifurcation

- **The dynastic split** - Yayāti's disinheritance of the elder four in favor of Pūru is the **root fork of the Lunar dynasty**:
  - **Yadu → Yādava line** - ancestors of the Vṛṣṇis, Andhakas, and ultimately **Kṛṣṇa** (see [../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md), [krishna-lila.md](./krishna-lila.md)) — kings by blood but barred from the imperial throne by Yayāti's curse.
  - **Pūru → Paurava line** - through **Bharata** (son of [Duṣyanta and Śakuntalā](./shakuntala-bharata.md), a later Paurava), then **Kuru**, branching into the **Kauravas and Pāṇḍavas** of the [Mahābhārata](./mahabharata/).
  - **Turvaśu, Druhyu, Anu** - cursed to peripheral/foreign rule; later texts identify their lines with Yavana, Bhoja, and assorted border peoples. [scholarly]
- **Doctrinal payload** - the tale is the canonical kāma-vairāgya parable: the futility of sense-indulgence and the turn to renunciation. See [../05-concepts-and-dharma/](../05-concepts-and-dharma/) (purushārthas; karma/saṃsāra/mokṣa) for the doctrinal frame.
- **Genealogical placement** - full vamsha tables in [../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md): Soma → Budha → Purūravas → Āyu → Nahuṣa → **Yayāti** → (Yadu | Turvaśu | Druhyu | Anu | **Pūru**).
