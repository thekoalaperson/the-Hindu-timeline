# Yajña Avatāra

> Where this sits: a deep-dive on Viṣṇu's descent as **Yajña** (यज्ञ, "Sacrifice personified"; also **Yajñeśvara / Yajñeśa**) — the avatāra who is *born into* the founding family of mankind and who, uniquely, **holds the throne of [Indra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/indra.md)** through the first Manvantara. It catalogs the Bhāgavata's avatāra-list placement, the genealogy, and the narrative battle the [Svāyambhuva Manvantara file](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-01-svayambhuva.md) only names in passing.
>

The **Yajña-avatāra** is the form in which [Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) descends as the **personification of the Vedic sacrifice (*yajña*)** at the very dawn of the present Kalpa, in the first ([Svāyambhuva](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-01-svayambhuva.md)) Manvantara. He is doubly distinctive: he is one of the few avatāras with an ordinary human **birth and parentage** (son of Ākūti and Ruci), and he is the rare case where the office of **Indra — king of the devas — is filled by an aṃśa of Viṣṇu himself**, rather than by a separate deity who earned the post through sacrifice. [scholarly]

## Place in the avatāra lists

- **Avatāra #7 in the Bhāgavata's first enumeration** — In the canonical list of *līlā*/*vibhava* descents at **SB 1.3**, Yajña appears as the **seventh** incarnation: after the Kumāras (1), Varāha (2), Nārada (3), Nara-Nārāyaṇa (4), Kapila (5), and Dattātreya (6), the text says the Lord "took birth from Ākūti, the wife of Prajāpati Ruci, as **Yajña**, and ruled together with his sons the demigods, called the **Yāmas**, during the change of the Svāyambhuva Manvantara." (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.3.10)
  - *Note:* this is the wider open roster of *innumerable* descents (SB 1.3.26, *avatārā hy asaṅkhyeyāḥ*), **not** the fixed [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) of the ten — Yajña does not appear among the standard ten, but is a standing member of the "22 / 24" extended lists. [scholarly]
- **A *manvantara*-avatāra** — In the Vaiṣṇava taxonomy of descents (*puruṣa*, *guṇa*, *līlā*, *yuga*, *manvantara*, *āveśa*), Yajña is the textbook example of the **manvantara-avatāra**: the form Viṣṇu assumes to preside over and protect a given Manvantara — here, the **first**. (Source: Pāñcarātra / Bhāgavata commentarial classification) See the taxonomy in [Viṣṇu's Forms & Vyūhas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md). [scholarly]
- **Identified with Sacrifice itself** — The name is not incidental: Yajña *is* the *yajña-puruṣa*, the Lord as the embodiment and the recipient-enjoyer of all sacrifice (*yajña-bhuk*), a theme that runs from the *Puruṣa-sūkta* through the Gītā's "I am the sacrifice" (*ahaṃ kratuḥ*, BG 9.16). The Varāha is likewise allegorized as **Yajña-Varāha** (boar-as-sacrifice) — a related but distinct usage; keep the avatāra-Yajña of Svāyambhuva separate from the boar-allegory. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.13; Bhagavad Gītā 9.16) [scholarly]

## Genealogy — the avatāra born into the first family

- **Parents** — Yajña is born to **Ākūti**, eldest daughter of [Svāyambhuva Manu](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-01-svayambhuva.md) and Śatarūpā, and the Prajāpati **Ruci** (Ruchi). He is thus a **grandson of the first Manu** and a member of the founding family of mankind. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.1.4–7; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.7)
- **The twin Dakṣiṇā** — Ākūti bears **twins**: the boy **Yajña** (a portion of Viṣṇu) and the girl **Dakṣiṇā** ("sacrificial fee / offering"). Dakṣiṇā later becomes **Yajña's own consort** — Sacrifice wedded to its Fee — a transparently allegorical pairing. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.1.4–6)
  - *Variant (adoption motif):* in some tellings Ruci's children are reckoned to **two grandfathers** — Svāyambhuva Manu had promised Ruci that one child would be returned to him; the twins' division between the lines is handled differently across recensions. [late-text drift]
- **The twelve sons — the Yāmas** — Yajña and Dakṣiṇā produce **twelve sons**, the **Yāma** gods (Yāmas), who serve as the **deva-class (gaṇa-devatā)** of the Svāyambhuva Manvantara. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.1.7; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.7)
  - *Variant (names/count of the Yāmas):* the twelve are variously named across Purāṇas; some lists also seat the **Tuṣitas** in this era alongside or instead of the Yāmas, though most assign the Tuṣitas to the 2nd ([Svārociṣa](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-02-svarochisha.md)) Manvantara. [disputed across recensions]

## Yajña as Indra — the Viṣṇu-aṃśa on the deva-throne

- **He holds the post of Indra** — Across the Manvantara-officer lists, the **Indra of the Svāyambhuva era is named "Yajña."** Because [Indra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/indra.md) is an **office** (a rotating title held by a different being each Manvantara), and because Yajña is an aṃśa of Viṣṇu, this is **one of the only times the deva-kingship is occupied by an incarnation of Viṣṇu himself** rather than by a merit-elevated god. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.1; 8.1.20; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.7) [scholarly]
- **Why it is theologically striking** — Normally Viṣṇu *grants* Indra-ship (e.g. to Purandara by the hundred horse-sacrifices) and stands *above* the deva-king as the one Indra worships. Here the Preserver **descends into the office** to steward the first age personally — the manvantara-avatāra and the Indra-post coincide in a single person. (Source: Bhāgavata commentarial tradition) [scholarly]
- **Title** — In this role he is styled **Yajñeśvara / Yajñeśa** ("Lord of Sacrifice") and **Hari** acting as the *Indra* Yajña. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.1)

## The omitted episode — defending Manu's sacrifice against the Rākṣasas (SB 8.1)

The narrative the [Svāyambhuva Manvantara file](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-01-svayambhuva.md) names but does not unpack is told by Śuka in **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.1**, the chapter that catalogs the Manus and their avatāras.

- **The crisis** — During the reign of **Svāyambhuva Manu**, fierce **Rākṣasas (and Asuras / Yātudhānas)**, oppressed by hunger and malevolence, **attack to devour Manu himself** (or, in the sacrificial reading, to consume and despoil his ongoing cosmic *yajña*). (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.1.18–20)
- **Viṣṇu descends as Yajña and takes up arms** — The Lord, present in the world as **Yajña** — born of Ākūti — **assumes the leadership of the devas (the Yāmas, his own sons)** and personally **drives off and slays the Rākṣasas**, protecting Manu and the sacrifice. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.1.19–20)
  - *Reading (allegorical):* because Yajña *is* Sacrifice, the demons "eating the sacrifice" and "eating Manu" are the same threat; the avatāra defends the very rite he embodies. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (who is attacked):* recensions and translators differ on whether the Rākṣasas come to eat **Manu** or to wreck the **sacrificial performance**; the Bhāgavata's wording supports the Manu-protection reading, with the sacrifice-protection sense layered on by commentators. [disputed]
- **The reign that follows** — Having secured the era, **Yajña rules as Indra of the Svāyambhuva Manvantara, surrounded by his Yāma sons**, for the duration of that Manu's period — the avatāra and the deva-king being one. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.1.20; 4.1)
- **Why the manvantara file omits it** — The era-file catalogs Yajña among the *officers* (Manu, Indra, devas, sages, seed-kings) and lists him as a Viṣṇu-aṃśa, but its narrative spine follows the **dynastic line** (Dhruva → Pṛthu → the Prachetas); the Rākṣasa-battle is an avatāra-episode rather than a dynastic one, hence its placement here. [editorial note]

## Distinguish from look-alikes

- **Not the Yajña-Varāha** — The "sacrifice"-allegory of the **Boar** (Varāha's limbs as the parts of the *yajña*, SB 3.13.35ff) is a separate, second avatāra; the Yajña of Svāyambhuva is a man born of Ākūti, not the boar. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.13) [scholarly]
- **Not Dakṣa's sacrifice** — The personified **Yajña** who is **beheaded at Dakṣa's broken sacrifice** (and given a goat's/antelope's head, or who flees as a deer) is the *ritual personified as victim*, a Śaiva-cycle motif — **not** the Svāyambhuva Indra-avatāra, even though both bear the name Yajña. Keep them separate. (Source: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.5–7; cf. [sati-daksha-and-parvati](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)) [scholarly] [disputed — namesake confusion]
- **Not Indra-Purandara** — The Indra of *our* (7th, Vaivasvata) era is **Purandara**, a separate deity; Yajña's Indra-ship belongs strictly to the first Manvantara. See [Indra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/indra.md) on the office vs. the person. [scholarly]

## Two clocks — scriptural vs. historical

- **Scripture-internal placement** — On the tradition's cyclic clock the Yajña-avatāra belongs to the **earliest dawn of the present Kalpa (Śveta-Varāha)**, the first of the 14 Manvantaras — i.e. **~1.9 billion sidereal years before the present** Vaivasvata era on the Purāṇic reckoning. It is among the very first events of "human" dynastic time, immediately after the [Varāha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) rescue of the Earth. [traditional]
- **Academic dating** — As material of the [Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md) (and the older [Viṣṇu Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md)), the Yajña-avatāra and the manvantara-officer scheme are text-critically products of roughly the **mid-1st-millennium CE onward** (Viṣṇu P. earlier, Bhāgavata c. 9th–10th c. CE) — far later than the cosmic age they narrate. The two registers must be kept distinct. [scholarly]
