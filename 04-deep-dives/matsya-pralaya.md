# Matsya avatāra (the Fish and the Pralaya-Flood)

> Cosmic address: the **first** of the classical [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) and the foundational deluge that closes the prior age and opens the present **[7th — Vaivasvata — Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)**. The earliest layer is the Manu-flood of the **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 1.8.1**; the epic layer is the **Mahābhārata's Matsyopākhyāna** (Vana Parva, CE 3.185); the developed avatāra is told in the **Matsya Purāṇa 1–2**, the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.24** and the **Agni Purāṇa 2**.
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · See also: [Samudra Manthana](./samudra-manthana.md) · [Kūrma / Vāmana–Bali](./vamana-bali.md) · [Prahlāda & Narasiṃha](./prahlada-narasimha.md) · [Hayagrīva](./hayagriva.md)

The **Matsya** ("fish") avatāra tells how Viṣṇu, as a great horned fish, warns the righteous Manu — **Vaivasvata Manu** in the epic and in the Matsya and Agni Purāṇas, called **Satyavrata** before his Manu-hood in the Bhāgavata — of a world-ending **pralaya** flood, instructs him to board a boat with the seeds of all life and the **seven sages (saptarṣi)**, and tether it to the fish's horn to ride out the deluge. It is at once a flood-survival myth (the Indian counterpart of the Near-Eastern deluge), the etiological bridge that seats Vaivasvata Manu as progenitor of the present Manvantara, and — in the Bhāgavata/Agni overlay — a Veda-rescue myth in which Matsya slays the Veda-thieving demon. The oldest version (*Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* 1.8.1) has Manu and the fish but **no demon and no Viṣṇu**; the demon and the Viṣṇu-identity are later accretions.

---

## I. The earliest layer — Manu and the fish (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa)

- **Manu finds the fish** — as water for washing is brought to **Manu** in the morning, a fish comes into his hands while he washes; it asks him to rear it in exchange for saving him from a coming flood (*augha*) that "will carry away all these creatures." (*Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* 1.8.1.1–2)
- **The fish's growth** — "fish devours fish" while they are small, so Manu keeps it first in a **jar (kumbhī)**, then in a **dug pit (karṣū)**, then takes it down to the **sea**, where it is beyond harm; it soon becomes a *jhaṣa* (a great fish) — the **prodigious growth** motif that becomes the signature of every Matsya telling. (*Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* 1.8.1.3–5)
- **The warning and the boat** — the fish tells Manu the year of the flood and bids him **prepare a ship** and enter it when the flood rises. (*Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* 1.8.1.4–5)
- **The tethered boat and the northern mountain** — when the flood rises, Manu ties the ship's rope (*nāvaḥ pāśa*) to the fish's **horn (śṛṅga)** and is carried to "yonder **northern mountain**" (*uttaraṃ girim*); the fish tells him to **fasten the ship to a tree** and to follow the water down as it subsides — hence that slope of the northern mountain is called ***manor avasarpaṇam*, "Manu's descent."** (*Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* 1.8.1.5–6) The name *Naubandhana* ("ship-binding") is the Mahābhārata's, not the Brāhmaṇa's (see §II); Eggeling's footnote (after Weber) also compares *nāvaprabhraṃśana*, "the ship's gliding-down," on the Himavat's summit in *Atharvaveda* 19.39.8. [scholarly]
- **Manu as sole survivor and re-progenitor** — the flood sweeps away all creatures and **Manu alone remains**; desiring offspring, he worships and performs austerities and makes a *pāka-yajña*, offering ghee, sour milk, whey and curds **into the waters**; from it after a year a woman arises, whom Mitra and Varuṇa meet; she declares herself **Manu's daughter**, is identified with the ritual **Iḍā**, and through her Manu generates "this race of Manu." (*Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* 1.8.1.6–11)
  - *Text-historical note (Śatapatha layer):* here the fish is **unnamed and not Viṣṇu**; its identification as **Brahmā/Prajāpati** first appears in the Mahābhārata (3.185.48), its identification as Viṣṇu in the Purāṇas. There is **no demon and no Veda-theft**, and the saptarṣi and the "seeds of all" are not yet present — they first appear in the epic (3.185.29–30). [scholarly]

## II. The epic telling — the Matsyopākhyāna (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva)

- **Manu Vaivasvata's penance** — Mārkaṇḍeya tells Yudhiṣṭhira of **Manu, son of Vivasvat (the Sun)**, a great ascetic who, standing on one foot with arms raised at **Badarī in Viśālā**, performs tapas for ten thousand years; the fish comes to him on the bank of the **Vīriṇī** river (CE; Ganguli's text reads **Cīriṇī**). (*Mahābhārata* 3.185.1–6, critical edition; Ganguli, Vana Parva section CLXXXVI)
- **The fish seeks refuge** — a tiny fish asks Manu to save it from the bigger fish, since the strong devouring the weak is "our ordained, eternal way of life"; Manu takes it in his hand and keeps it in an earthen jar (*aliñjara*), rearing it like a son. (*Mahābhārata* 3.185.7–12) The later term *matsya-nyāya*, the "law of the fishes" (might-makes-right), names this idea but is not used in the passage.
- **The growth and the prophecy** — the fish outgrows jar, a **pond (vāpī)** two yojanas long, the **Gaṅgā**, and is taken to the ocean; there it foretells that "all this mobile and immobile" world will go to **pralaya** — "the time of the purging (*saṃprakṣālana*) of the worlds" — and instructs Manu to have a strong ship built with a rope, and to embark with the **seven ṛṣis (saptarṣi)** and **all the seeds**, promising to come **horned (śṛṅgī)** so that he will know it. (*Mahābhārata* 3.185.13–32)
  - *Reading note:* the critical edition has the seeds "as described **by me** before" (*yathoktāni mayā purā*, 3.185.30); the vulgate behind Ganguli's translation has them "enumerated by regenerate **Brahmanas** in days of yore."
- **The tow to safety** — Manu himself sets the rope (*vaṭākaramaya pāśa*) on the fish's horn; the fish drags the ship for "many years" across the one ocean to the **highest peak of the Himavat**, where it tells the ṛṣis to bind the vessel — a peak "known even today" as **Naubandhana**. (*Mahābhārata* 3.185.37–47)
- **The fish reveals itself as Brahmā / Prajāpati** — the saving fish declares, "I am **Brahmā, Prajāpati**; none higher than me is found," and charges Manu to create all beings — gods, asuras and men; Manu, at first bewildered, performs great tapas and creates. The episode names itself "the Purāṇa called **Mātsyaka**." (*Mahābhārata* 3.185.48–53)
  - *Variant (epic vs. Purāṇic identity):* the Mahābhārata's fish is **Brahmā/Prajāpati** (3.185.48); the Purāṇas re-attribute the form to **Viṣṇu** — Manu recognises it as Vāsudeva/Keśava/Janārdana (*Matsya Purāṇa* 1.25–28), Satyavrata as Hari Nārāyaṇa (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.24.27), Manu as Viṣṇu Nārāyaṇa (*Agni Purāṇa* 2.10) — and fold it into the Daśāvatāra. Keep the two attributions distinct. [scholarly]

## III. The developed avatāra — Satyavrata and the Bhāgavata (SB 8.24)

- **King Satyavrata of Draviḍa** — in the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* the hero is **Satyavrata**, a *rājarṣi* devoted to Nārāyaṇa who does tapas living on water, called **lord of Draviḍa** (*draviḍeśvara*); he is the one who "in this mahākalpa" is the son of Vivasvat, **Śrāddhadeva**, placed in Manu-hood by Hari. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.24.10–11, 13; 9.1.2–3) The BBT translation's "During the Cākṣuṣa-manvantara" at 8.24.10 renders only *tatra* ("there/then") and is a translator's gloss.
  - *Variant (the hero's name):* the *Matsya Purāṇa* (1.11, a king who gave his kingdom to his son and did tapas on the **Malaya** mountain for a hundred *ayutas* of years, receiving from Brahmā the boon of protecting all beings at the pralaya, 1.11–16) and the *Agni Purāṇa* (2.4, by the **Kṛtamālā**) call him simply **Manu / Vaivasvata Manu**; the name Satyavrata is the Bhāgavata's.
- **The fish in the palmful of water** — offering *tarpaṇa* water in the river **Kṛtamālā**, Satyavrata finds a tiny **śapharī** fish in his cupped hands and releases it into the river; it pleads not to be left among aquatics that kill their own kind, and he carries it to his hermitage in a water-pot. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.24.12–16)
- **The unstoppable growth** — overnight it outgrows the **kamaṇḍalu**; then a larger water-vessel (*udañcana*; BBT "large well"), where it grows three cubits in a moment; then a **pond**, then ever-larger **lakes**, and finally the **ocean**, where it protests that the mighty *makaras* and other sea-beasts will eat it (BBT "sharks"); bewildered, Satyavrata asks who it is that spread over a hundred yojanas in a day and recognises it as **Hari Nārāyaṇa**, and the Lord answers him as **Matsya**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.24.17–31)
  - *Variant (the vessels):* in the *Matsya Purāṇa* the śapharī falls into Manu's hands during *pitṛ-tarpaṇa* at his hermitage and outgrows a water-pot (*karaka*), a jar (*maṇika*), a **well (kūpa)**, a lake, the **Gaṅgā** and the ocean (1.17–24); the *Agni Purāṇa* gives pot, *udañcana*, lake and ocean (2.6–8).
- **The warning of the pralaya** — Matsya announces that on the **seventh day** the three worlds will sink in the ocean of dissolution; a great boat sent by him will come, and Satyavrata must board it with **all herbs and seeds**, surrounded by the **saptarṣi** and all kinds of **living beings**, lit only by the ṛṣis' radiance, while the fish tows it "as long as **Brahmā's night**" lasts and answers his questions. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.24.32–38; seventh day also *Agni Purāṇa* 2.12)
  - *Variant (the boat):* in the *Matsya Purāṇa* the boat is **built by the host of the gods** (1.30), and Matsya first foretells a hundred-year drought, the seven scorching rays of the Sun, the fires of Aurva, Saṃkarṣaṇa and Śiva's third eye, and seven named dissolution-clouds before the one ocean (2.3–10).
- **The boat tethered by the serpent-rope** — the sea overflows under great rain-clouds; the foretold boat arrives and Satyavrata boards it with the brāhmaṇas, herbs and plants; a **golden, one-horned fish** a *niyuta* of yojanas long appears, and he binds the boat to its horn **using a great serpent as the rope** (*mahāhinā*, 8.24.36; *varatreṇāhinā*, 8.24.45). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.24.39–45) The Sanskrit does not name the serpent; "**Vāsuki**" is the BBT translator's gloss (8.24.36, 45).
  - *Variant (the cord):* the *Śatapatha* and epic use a **plain rope** (*nāvaḥ pāśa*, ŚB 1.8.1.5; *vaṭākaramaya pāśa*, MBh 3.185.37); the Purāṇas use an **unnamed serpent** — the *Matsya Purāṇa*'s serpent comes of itself "in the form of a rope" (*bhujaṃgo rajjurūpeṇa*, 2.18–19), the *Agni Purāṇa* repeats *mahāhinā* (2.13). Retellings that name it **Vāsuki** or **Śeṣa / Ananta** follow later glosses; no Sanskrit locus found in BhP 8.24, MatsP 1–2 or AgP 2 (locus not verified).
- **The Matsya-instruction (the in-flood teaching)** — after Satyavrata's hymn to the fish as supreme guru (8.24.46–53), Matsya, sporting in the ocean, expounds the truth — "a divine **Purāṇa-saṃhitā** endowed with Sāṅkhya, Yoga and ritual" — and the king, seated in the boat with the ṛṣis, hears the eternal **brahman** free of doubt. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.24.54–56) The Bhāgavata does not name this teaching; the *Agni Purāṇa* calls it "the Purāṇa named **Matsya**" (2.15–16), and the *Matsya Purāṇa* presents itself as that discourse, given in answer to Manu's questions on the one ocean (1.10; 2.20–24).
- **Rebirth as Vaivasvata Manu** — Satyavrata, endowed with knowledge by Viṣṇu's grace, becomes **Vaivasvata Manu** "in this kalpa" — the **seventh Manu** and progenitor of the present (7th) Manvantara — the cosmological hinge the whole episode exists to set. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.24.11, 58; seventh Manu 8.13.1; *Matsya Purāṇa* 1.33–34) See [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md).

## IV. The Veda-theft strand — Hayagrīva / Śaṅkhāsura (Bhāgavata, Agni, Skanda & Padma Purāṇas)

- **The Vedas stolen from a sleeping Brahmā** — in the Bhāgavata overlay, at the dissolution ending the past kalpa, as **Brahmā** grows sleepy the Vedas issue from his mouth and the mighty **dānava Hayagrīva**, close by, carries them off; seeing this, Hari takes the śapharī form. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.24.7–9)
- **Matsya slays the thief and restores the Vedas** — when the dissolution is over, Hari kills the asura Hayagrīva and returns the Vedas to Brahmā as he rises from sleep — making Matsya a **Veda-rescuer** like the horse-headed [Hayagrīva](./hayagriva.md). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.24.57; *Agni Purāṇa* 2.16–17)
  - *Variant (who the thief is):* the demon is **Hayagrīva** in the *Bhāgavata* (8.24.8–9, 57) and the *Agni Purāṇa* (2.16); in the Kārttika-month tellings he is **Śaṅkha / Śaṅkhāsura, son of Sāgara (the Ocean)**, who takes the Vedas from Brahmā while Viṣṇu sleeps; the Vedas flee into the waters, the gods wake Viṣṇu on the eleventh of the bright half of Kārttika (the translator's footnote names it Prabodhinī Ekādaśī), he takes the śapharī form and kills Śaṅkha, and the sages he summons at Badarī recover the Vedas from the waters and bring them to him and Brahmā at **Prayāga**. (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa, Kārttikamāsa-māhātmya 13.24–43) This strand has **no Manu and no flood**. [late text]
  - *Variant (Padma Purāṇa):* the theft by the Ocean's son while Viṣṇu sleeps and the waking on the Ekādaśī (*Udbodhinī*) are told in *Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa 90.5–31; in 91.1–11 the small glittering fish falls into the hands of the sage **Kaśyapa** at the Vindhya and outgrows a water-pot, a well, a lake and the sea before killing Śaṅkha — the Manu-role taken by Kaśyapa, again without a flood — and the recovered Vedas found Prayāga as *Tīrtharāja*. (English translations on wisdomlib only; Sanskrit verse numbers not checked) [late text]
  - *Disambiguation — two Hayagrīvas:* this **demon Hayagrīva** slain by Matsya must never be merged with the **avatāra Hayagrīva / Hayaśiras**, the horse-headed Viṣṇu who himself recovers the Vedas from **Madhu and Kaiṭabha** (*Mahābhārata* 12.335; cf. 12.326.56) and who, in the *Matsya Purāṇa*, preserves the Vedas "in horse-form" when the worlds burn (53.5–6). In one telling a horse-necked being **steals** scripture; in the other a horse-headed being **restores** it. See [Hayagrīva](./hayagriva.md). [scholarly]
  - *Note (absent in the earliest layer and in the Matsya Purāṇa):* neither the *Śatapatha* nor the Mahābhārata flood-frame contains the Veda-theft, and the *Matsya Purāṇa*'s own flood narrative has none either — there the Vedas and Purāṇas survive the flood with Manu, and Matsya promises to set the Vedas going again at the beginning of Manu's creation (*Matsya Purāṇa* 2.12–15; adhyāyas 1–176 searched). The theft is a **later Purāṇic accretion** that aligns Matsya with the *jñāna*-avatāra type. [scholarly]

## V. Place in the avatāra-sequence and the two clocks

- **First of the Daśāvatāra** — Matsya heads the standard ten (before Kūrma, Varāha, Narasiṃha, Vāmana), the **fish** that opens the descent-series; in the *Bhāgavata's* extended 22/24-avatāra roster (SB 1.3) Matsya is counted the **tenth** *līlā*-avatāra, after Pṛthu (the "ninth," 1.3.14) and before Kūrma (the "eleventh," 1.3.16). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.3.6–16; standard *daśāvatāra* lists) See [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Why the fish comes first** — the sequence is read as a cosmic-evolutionary ascent — **fish → tortoise → boar → man-lion → dwarf → man** — Matsya the aquatic being fitting the world's watery, pre-formed beginning. [scholarly]
- **Scriptural clock — which pralaya?** — the texts do not agree. The *Bhāgavata* (8.24.7) and *Agni* (2.3) open the story with "the **brāhma naimittika** dissolution at the end of the past kalpa," and the fish tows the boat "as long as Brahmā's night" lasts (SB 8.24.37) — and *naimittika* is precisely **Brahmā's night** at the close of his day (SB 12.4.2–4; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 1.3.22). Yet the *Bhāgavata*'s own avatāra-list places the fish at "the flood of the **Cākṣuṣa** (Manvantara)," saving Vaivasvata Manu on a boat "made of earth" (*mahīmayī*) (SB 1.3.15), and the *Matsya Purāṇa* likewise sets the one ocean "at the end of the Cākṣuṣa interval" (2.14), with Manu to become lord of the Manvantara at the start of the Kṛta age (1.34) — even while describing kalpa-end fires and clouds (2.3–10). (cf. [Kalpa and Pralaya](../00-time-and-cosmology/kalpa-and-pralaya.md))
  - *Variant (commentarial harmonisation):* Gauḍīya commentators read the episode as a sudden, Lord-willed **pralaya at the end of the Cākṣuṣa Manvantara** — a short "night" of a drowsy Brahmā within his day — and distinguish **two fish-descents**: one at the start of the Svāyambhuva Manvantara that kills Hayagrīva, and a second that saves Satyavrata at the end of the Cākṣuṣa. (Rūpa Gosvāmī, *Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta*, and Viśvanātha Cakravartī, as summarised in the BBT purports to SB 8.24.10, 8.24.37 and 8.24.58; the purport says Śrīdhara Svāmī agrees) [late text] [disputed]
- **Historical clock** — academically the narrative has **no datable anchor**; the *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* flood-frame (c. 8th–6th century BCE) is among the **oldest Indian deluge myths**, widely compared with the Mesopotamian (Ziusudra/Utnapishtim) and Biblical (Noah) floods, though direct dependence is unproven; the Viṣṇu-avatāra and Veda-theft form crystallise only in the **Purāṇic period** (the *Matsya Purāṇa* and *Bhāgavata* reaching form in the mid-to-late first millennium CE). [scholarly]
- **Naubandhana and cult** — the "ship-binding" peak (a Mahābhārata name, 3.185.47) is variously localised in the **Himālaya**; Matsya is venerated as the first descent in Vaiṣṇava Daśāvatāra worship and iconography (a horned fish, or a fish-tailed Viṣṇu rising from the deep with conch and discus). [regional]

## Sources

**Primary scripture**
- **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 1.8.1.1–11** — the earliest Indian flood-and-fish myth: Manu, the growing fish (jar, pit, sea), the ship tied to the fish's horn, the northern mountain, the ship fastened to a tree, the slope called *manor avasarpaṇam* ("Manu's descent"), and Manu as sole survivor and re-progenitor through Iḍā — **with no demon and no Viṣṇu-identity**. Sanskrit: Sanskrit Wikisource (Mādhyandina); English: J. Eggeling, SBE 12 (1882).
- **Mahābhārata, Vana Parva 3.185 (critical edition; Ganguli section CLXXXVI) — the Matsyopākhyāna ("Fish-episode")** — Manu Vaivasvata's penance at Badarī, the small fish's plea, the growth and pralaya-prophecy, the saptarṣi and seeds, the tow to the Himavat and **Naubandhana**; the fish reveals itself as **Brahmā / Prajāpati**; the episode calls itself the *Mātsyaka Purāṇa* (3.185.53). Sanskrit: GRETIL (BORI text); English: K. M. Ganguli.
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.24 (with 1.3.15, 8.13.1, 9.1.2–3, 12.4.2–4)** — the developed avatāra: **King Satyavrata** of Draviḍa, the śapharī in the palm, the seven-day warning, the boat bound to Matsya's horn by an **unnamed great serpent** ("Vāsuki" in the BBT translation), the in-flood *Purāṇa-saṃhitā* teaching, Satyavrata's installation as **Vaivasvata Manu**, the "brāhma naimittika" dissolution (8.24.7) vs. the "Cākṣuṣa flood" (1.3.15), and the Veda-theft and slaying of the demon **Hayagrīva** (8.24.8–9, 57). Sanskrit: GRETIL; English and purports: Vedabase (BBT).
- **Matsya Purāṇa 1–2** (Calcutta 1954 ed., GRETIL) — the Purāṇa "spoken by Matsya" to Manu: Manu's tapas on the Malaya and Brahmā's boon, the śapharī at the *pitṛ-tarpaṇa*, the god-built boat, the drought, seven suns and dissolution-clouds, the serpent-rope, the flood "at the end of the Cākṣuṣa interval" (2.14), and the Purāṇa taught in answer to Manu's questions — **no Veda-theft** (also 53.5–6 for Viṣṇu's horse-form).
- **Agni Purāṇa 2** (GRETIL) — a compact Bhāgavata-type telling: Vaivasvata Manu at the Kṛtamālā, the seventh-day warning, the serpent-rope, the "Matsya Purāṇa" heard on the boat, and the slaying of the Veda-stealer Hayagrīva.
- **Skanda Purāṇa, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa, Kārttikamāsa-māhātmya 13** (G. V. Tagare's translation) and **Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa 90–91** (N. A. Deshpande's translation), both read in English on wisdomlib — the **Śaṅkha / Śaṅkhāsura** Veda-theft, its Kārttika setting, the recovery of the Vedas at Prayāga, and (Padma) Kaśyapa as the fish's rescuer.
- **Mahābhārata 12.335** — Hayaśiras recovers the Vedas from Madhu and Kaiṭabha (the avatāra Hayagrīva, for the disambiguation).
