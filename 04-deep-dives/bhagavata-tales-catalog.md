# Bhāgavata Purāṇa — Catalog of Tales

> Where this sits: a **skandha-keyed index** of the narrative episodes of the *Śrīmad Bhāgavata Mahāpurāṇa* (Bhāgavata Purāṇa), the great Vaiṣṇava Purāṇa of 12 skandhas (cantos) and 335 chapters. Its own count is **18,000** verses (*aṣṭādaśa-sāhasram*, Bhāg. 12.13.9); the extant vulgate is closer to **14,000** (the GRETIL e-text carries 14,067 verse-numbers, some of them whole prose paragraphs in Skandha 5). [scholarly] The frame: Sūta (Ugraśravas) tells the Naimiṣa sages what Śuka told King Parīkṣit in his last seven days (Bhāg. 1.1.4–13; 1.19).
> This is an **honest index, not a re-telling**: many of its biggest tales already have dedicated files in [04-deep-dives](README.md); this node enumerates the major episodes, marks which are **[has file]** vs **[index-only]**, and routes the reader. Back to the [deep-dives index](README.md) · [master index](../README.md).

This file is **deliberately an index.** The Bhāgavata is a vast narrative anthology; it would be redundant to re-prose episodes that are already full nodes. Where a tale **has a dedicated file**, the entry is a one-line pointer. Where it does **not**, the entry is a fuller stub flagged **[index-only]**. Chapter (adhyāya) and verse numbers follow the GRETIL e-text, which uses the common vulgate chapter division (as in the Gītā Press edition); other editions can differ by a verse or so. Where the Bhāgavata and a later retelling (or the Mahābhārata) disagree, the difference is given as a nested, attributed variant. [scholarly]

## Skandha map (the 12 cantos at a glance)

The Bhāgavata describes itself twice with ten topics, and the two lists differ. **2.10.1** gives *sarga, visarga, sthāna, poṣaṇa, ūti, manvantara, īśānukathā, nirodha, mukti, āśraya*, the tenth (*āśraya*, the Lord as refuge) being what the other nine serve (2.10.2). **12.7.9–10** gives the ten marks of a *mahāpurāṇa*: *sarga, visarga, vṛtti, rakṣā, antara* (manvantaras), *vaṃśa, vaṃśānucarita, saṃsthā* (dissolution), *hetu, apāśraya*, and notes that "some" count five, by a distinction of great and small Purāṇas (*mahad-alpa-vyavasthayā*, 12.7.10). Narratively the cantos run (chapter counts from the GRETIL text):

- **1 (19 chs.) — Prologue & frame:** Naimiṣa assembly, Vyāsa's despondency & Nārada's counsel, the Pāṇḍava aftermath, Kṛṣṇa's departure, Parīkṣit and Kali, Parīkṣit cursed, Śuka arrives. (Bhāg. 1)
- **2 (10 chs.) — Cosmology:** Śuka on dhāraṇā, the Virāṭ-puruṣa, the avatāra-list, Brahmā's instruction. (Bhāg. 2)
- **3 (33 chs.) — Vidura's pilgrimage; Varāha; Kapila→Devahūti.** (Bhāg. 3)
- **4 (31 chs.) — Dakṣa, Dhruva, Pṛthu, the Pracetas.** (Bhāg. 4)
- **5 (26 chs.) — Priyavrata, Ṛṣabha, Bharata/Jaḍa Bharata, the bhuvana-kośa (geography) & the hells.** (Bhāg. 5)
- **6 (19 chs.) — Ajāmila, Dakṣa's curse on Nārada, Vṛtra, Citraketu, Diti and the Maruts.** (Bhāg. 6)
- **7 (15 chs.) — Prahlāda & Nṛsiṃha; varṇāśrama-dharma.** (Bhāg. 7)
- **8 (24 chs.) — Manvantaras, Gajendra, Samudra-manthana, Vāmana, Matsya.** (Bhāg. 8)
- **9 (24 chs.) — The solar & lunar dynasties (the great genealogical canto).** (Bhāg. 9)
- **10 (90 chs.) — Kṛṣṇa-līlā (the longest canto): Vraja, Mathurā & Dvārakā.** (Bhāg. 10)
- **11 (31 chs.) — Yadu-destruction; the Uddhava-gītā & the 24 gurus; Kṛṣṇa's departure.** (Bhāg. 11)
- **12 (13 chs.) — Kali-yuga prophecy, Parīkṣit's death, pralaya, Mārkaṇḍeya, the Purāṇa-catalogue, colophon.** (Bhāg. 12)

## Skandha 1–2 — Frame & cosmology

- **Vyāsa's despondency & the Bhāgavata's birth** - Nārada finds Vyāsa unsatisfied even after the Veda-division & Mahābhārata, tells of his own previous birth as a maidservant's son, and prescribes the singing of Hari's *līlā*; Vyāsa composes the Bhāgavata and teaches it to his son Śuka (1.7.8). Aetiology of the text itself. (Bhāg. 1.4–7). **[index-only]**
- **The Pāṇḍava aftermath: Aśvatthāman, Kuntī, Bhīṣma** - Aśvatthāman beheads Draupadī's sleeping sons and is captured; Draupadī asks that he be spared, and he is shorn of his head-jewel and expelled (1.7); Kṛṣṇa shields Uttarā's unborn child, the future Parīkṣit, from his *brahmāstra*; Kuntī's hymn asks for calamities, since in calamity she sees Kṛṣṇa (1.8.18–43, esp. 1.8.25); Bhīṣma's hymn and death on the arrow-bed (1.9.32–43). Cross-link: [Aśvatthāman](characters/ashvatthama.md), [Kuntī](characters/kunti.md), [Bhīṣma](characters/bhishma.md). (Bhāg. 1.7–9). **[partial elsewhere]**
- **Kṛṣṇa's departure & the onset of Kali** - Arjuna returns from Dvārakā with news of the Yadu-end and Kṛṣṇa's passing; the Pāṇḍavas crown Parīkṣit and leave for the north (1.14–15). On his conquest Parīkṣit finds Kali, in a king's dress, beating a bull (Dharma) and a cow (Earth); Kali surrenders and is granted gambling, drink, women and slaughter as his places, then gold as a fifth (1.16.4; 1.17.28–40). → Yadu-end fuller in [Mausala-parva](mahabharata/16-mausala-parva.md); king in [Parīkṣit](../03-lineages-vamsha/parikshit.md). (Bhāg. 1.14–17). **[partial elsewhere]**
- **Parīkṣit cursed** - Parīkṣit (Abhimanyu's son, last of the Kuru line), hungry and thirsty on a hunt, gets no welcome from a sage in deep meditation and drapes a dead snake on his shoulder; the sage's boy-son curses the king to die of Takṣaka's bite on the seventh day (1.18.24–37). The king fasts on the Gaṅgā's bank and Śuka arrives (1.19). The frame that occasions the whole recitation. → **[has file]** [Parīkṣit](../03-lineages-vamsha/parikshit.md); cross-link: [Uttaṅka](uttanka.md), [Mahābhārata frame](mahabharata/README.md). (Bhāg. 1.18–19).
  - *Variant (names):* the Bhāgavata leaves both unnamed: the father is only "the Āṅgirasa" (1.18.39) and the son "the sage's boy" (1.18.36). The names **Śamīka** (father) and **Śṛṅgin** (son) come from the Mahābhārata (MBh 1.36.24; 1.38.17, Critical Edition).
- **Śuka's dhāraṇā, the Virāṭ-puruṣa & the second avatāra-list** - the cosmic-body meditation (2.1), the Puruṣa-sūkta-like hymn of the Puruṣa's body (2.6), and Brahmā's longer list of *līlā*-avatāras (2.7). Cross-link: [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md). (Bhāg. 2.1, 2.6, 2.7). **[index-only]**
  - *Variant (which list is "the 22"):* the numbered list of **22 avatāras** is **1.3.6–25** (Kumāras first, Kalki twenty-second), which then says the avatāras are countless (1.3.26) and that "Kṛṣṇa is Bhagavān himself" (1.3.28). The 2.7 list is a separate, longer enumeration with a different order (it includes Hayaśīrṣa, i.e. Hayagrīva, at 2.7.11, absent from 1.3).
- **The catuḥ-ślokī ("four verses")** - Brahmā's vision of Nārāyaṇa, who gives him the gist of the Bhāgavata in four verses: "I alone was before creation… what remains after is I" (2.9.32–35). Gauḍīya and other commentators treat these as the seed of the whole Purāṇa. [scholarly] (Bhāg. 2.9.32–35). **[index-only]**

## Skandha 3 — Varāha & the teaching of Kapila

- **Vidura & Maitreya; Varāha-avatāra** - Vidura meets Uddhava and then Maitreya, the framing dialogue for Skandhas 3–4 (3.1–5); Varāha lifts the Earth; Diti's untimely conception (3.14); the Four Kumāras curse Jaya and Vijaya (3.15–16); Varāha slays Hiraṇyākṣa (3.17–19). → **[has file]** [varaha-hiranyaksha.md](varaha-hiranyaksha.md); cross-link: [Jaya-Vijaya](jaya-vijaya.md), [the Four Kumāras](the-four-kumaras.md), [Vidura](characters/vidura.md). (Bhāg. 3.1–5, 3.13–19).
- **Kardama & Devahūti** - the prajāpati Kardama's tapas, his marriage to Svāyambhuva Manu's daughter Devahūti, the aerial palace, and the birth of nine daughters + the son who is Viṣṇu. → covered in [kapila-devahuti.md](kapila-devahuti.md). (Bhāg. 3.21–24).
- **Kapila's teaching to Devahūti (Kapila-upadeśa / "Kapila-gītā")** - Viṣṇu incarnate as the sage **Kapila** (founder-figure of Sāṅkhya) instructs his mother in the analysis of *prakṛti/puruṣa*, the *tattvas*, devotional *bhakti-yoga* as supreme, *aṣṭāṅga*, embryology and the embryo's prayer in the womb (3.31), and liberation; Devahūti attains mokṣa (3.33). With the Uddhava-gītā, one of the two great philosophical dialogues of the Bhāgavata. → **[has file]** [kapila-devahuti.md](kapila-devahuti.md); cross-link: [Sāṅkhya](../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md). (Bhāg. 3.25–33). [scholarly]
  - *Variant (chapter-count):* the *upadeśa* proper is most often cited as **3.25–32**, with 3.33 narrating Devahūti's liberation; some indices give **3.25–33** inclusive.
  - *Variant (tattvas):* Kapila counts **24** (5 elements, 5 subtle elements, 10 senses, and the fourfold inner organ: *manas, buddhi, ahaṅkāra, citta*; 3.26.11–14) and calls **time (*kāla*) the twenty-fifth** (3.26.15); "some" call time the Puruṣa's power (3.26.16). Classical Sāṅkhya (Īśvarakṛṣṇa) has 25 with *puruṣa* as the 25th and no *citta* or *kāla*. The Bhāgavata itself records the dispute: Uddhava asks why sages count 26, 25, 7, 9, 6, 4, 11, 17, 16 or 13 tattvas (11.22.1–3), and Kṛṣṇa answers that all the counts are right from their own standpoint (11.22). [scholarly]

## Skandha 4 — Dakṣa, Dhruva, Pṛthu, the Pracetas

- **Dakṣa's sacrifice & Satī** - Dakṣa insults Śiva, Satī gives up her body in a fire of her own yogic concentration (4.4.25–27), Vīrabhadra wrecks the yajña, Dakṣa is restored with a goat's head. → **[has file]** [Sati, the Daksha-Yajna & Parvati](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md). (Bhāg. 4.2–7).
- **Dhruva** - the slighted boy-prince who wins the pole-star by tapas. → **[has file]** [dhruva.md](dhruva.md). (Bhāg. 4.8–12).
- **Pṛthu & the milking of the Earth** - Vena's wickedness, the churning of his dead body that produces Pṛthu (first consecrated king), and the milking of Pṛthivī. → **[has file]** [prithu-prithvi.md](prithu-prithvi.md). (Bhāg. 4.13–23).
- **Purañjana (allegory of the city)** - Nārada's allegory to King Prācīnabarhis: **Purañjana** the soul, his "city of nine gates" (the body), the queen (intelligence), his entanglement in saṃsāra, and his rebirth as a woman, the princess of Vidarbha. A rare sustained Purāṇic allegory. → **[has file]** [puranjana-allegory.md](puranjana-allegory.md). (Bhāg. 4.25–29).
- **The Pracetas & the Rudra-gītā** - the ten **Pracetas** (sons of Prācīnabarhis, 4.24.13), sent to multiply the creatures, meet **Śiva**, who teaches them the **Rudra-gītā**, a hymn to Viṣṇu as Vāsudeva, Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna and Aniruddha (the four *vyūhas*, 4.24.33–36); they sing it in the ocean for 10,000 years (4.24.14; 4.30.4), marry Māriṣā, the daughter raised by the trees (4.30.48), and father the reborn **Dakṣa**. A notable Śaiva→Vaiṣṇava bridge passage. → **[has file]** [pracetas-rudra-gita.md](pracetas-rudra-gita.md); cross-link: [lineages](../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md). (Bhāg. 4.24, 4.30–31).

## Skandha 5 — Ṛṣabha, Bharata, geography & hells

- **Priyavrata** - Svāyambhuva Manu's renunciate son who, recalled to kingship, is unhappy that the sun lights only half the earth as it circles Meru, and follows it seven times round in a radiant chariot "to make night day"; the ruts of his chariot-wheels become the seven oceans, dividing the seven *dvīpas* (5.1.30–31). A *bhuvana-kośa* aetiology. (Bhāg. 5.1). **[index-only]**
- **Ṛṣabha, Bharata & Jaḍa Bharata** - the Tīrthaṅkara-like avatāra-king Ṛṣabha, his son Bharata (eponym of Bhārata-varṣa), and Bharata's three lives (king → deer → the realised "dull" brāhmaṇa who instructs King Rahūgaṇa). → **[has file]** [rishabha-bharata-jada.md](rishabha-bharata-jada.md). (Bhāg. 5.2–14).
- **The bhuvana-kośa & the hells (narakas)** - the Purāṇa's systematic cosmic geography (Jambūdvīpa, the dvīpas, Bhū-maṇḍala, the sun and planets, the *śiśumāra* figure, the lower worlds) and the catalogue of the **28 narakas** with their punishments. → **[has file]** [Brahmāṇḍa & bhuvana-kośa](../00-time-and-cosmology/brahmanda-and-bhuvana-kosha.md), [Śiśumāra-cakra](../00-time-and-cosmology/shishumara-chakra.md), [Bila-svarga & the Pātālas](../00-time-and-cosmology/bila-svarga-and-the-patalas.md), [Narakas & the afterlife](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/narakas-and-afterlife.md); cross-link: [sacred geography](../06-sacred-geography/README.md). (Bhāg. 5.16–26).
  - *Variant (number of hells):* 5.26.6 notes that some reckon **21** hells, names those, then adds seven more (*kiñca*: Kṣārakardama and the rest) and totals them itself as **28** (*aṣṭā-viṃśatir narakāḥ*); 5.26.36 then says there are hundreds and thousands of such hells in Yama's realm.

## Skandha 6 — Ajāmila, Vṛtra, Dakṣa-curse cycle

- **Ajāmila** - the fallen brāhmaṇa saved at death by calling his son **"Nārāyaṇa"** — the great *nāma-mahimā* (glory-of-the-Name) parable; frames the Viṣṇudūta–Yamadūta debate. → **[has file]** [ajamila.md](ajamila.md). (Bhāg. 6.1–3).
- **Dakṣa's Haṃsaguhya & the curse of Nārada** - Dakṣa's hymn (6.4), and Nārada's turning of Dakṣa's sons (the Haryaśvas, then the Savalāśvas, also spelt Śabalāśvas, 6.5.1–35) toward renunciation, earning Nārada Dakṣa's curse never to stay in one place (6.5.36–43). (Bhāg. 6.4–5). **[index-only]**
- **Vṛtra-vadha & the Nārāyaṇa-kavaca** - Indra offends Bṛhaspati and takes Tvaṣṭṛ's son Viśvarūpa as priest, who teaches him the **Nārāyaṇa-kavaca** (armour-mantra, 6.8); Indra kills Viśvarūpa, Tvaṣṭṛ raises **Vṛtra** from the fire, and Indra slays him with the thunderbolt made from Dadhīci's bones; here Vṛtra is paradoxically a great devotee (6.11). Indra's sin of brahmin-killing follows (6.13). → **[has file]** [dadhichi.md](dadhichi.md); cross-link: [Indra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/indra.md). (Bhāg. 6.7–13).
- **Citraketu** - the grieving king shown the soul's continuity, who becomes a vidyādhara, laughs aloud at Śiva for holding Pārvatī on his lap in an assembly of sages (6.17.5–8), and is cursed by Pārvatī to be born the demon **Vṛtra**. → **[has file]** [citraketu.md](citraketu.md). (Bhāg. 6.14–17).
- **Diti's vow & the birth of the Maruts** - Diti's penance (the *puṃsavana* vow) to bear an Indra-killer; Indra enters her womb and cuts the embryo into seven, and each of those into seven, the **49 Maruts** (6.18.62–64); 6.19 gives the vow's rite. → [The Maruts](../08-beings-and-bestiary/the-maruts.md). (Bhāg. 6.18–19). **[partial elsewhere]**

## Skandha 7 — Prahlāda & Nṛsiṃha

- **Prahlāda & Hiraṇyakaśipu / Nṛsiṃha-avatāra** - told by Nārada to Yudhiṣṭhira after Śiśupāla's death (7.1): the boy-devotee, his demon-father's boon and tyranny, and the man-lion who bursts from the pillar and kills him at twilight. → **[has file]** [prahlada-narasimha.md](prahlada-narasimha.md); the asura-birth frame in [jaya-vijaya.md](jaya-vijaya.md). (Bhāg. 7.1–10).
- **Śiva and the three cities of Maya** - Nārada's coda: Maya builds three cities of gold, silver and iron for the defeated asuras; Śiva burns them, helped by Viṣṇu (7.10.53–71). → **[has file]** [tripura-dahana.md](tripura-dahana.md). (Bhāg. 7.10.53–71).
- **The dharma of the varṇāśramas & the householder (Nārada to Yudhiṣṭhira)** - the long didactic appendix on social and renunciate duty, including Prahlāda's meeting with a sage who lives by the "python's vow" (*ājagara*, taking only what comes; 7.13). (Bhāg. 7.11–15). **[index-only]**

## Skandha 8 — Manvantaras & the great avatāra-episodes

- **The manvantaras** - the fourteen Manus with their gods, sages and avatāras, past (8.1, 8.5), present and future (8.13–14). → [Manvantara system](../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md). (Bhāg. 8.1, 8.5, 8.13–14). **[partial elsewhere]**
- **Gajendra-mokṣa** - the elephant-king seized by a crocodile, saved by Viṣṇu when he surrenders. → **[has file]** [gajendra-moksha.md](gajendra-moksha.md). (Bhāg. 8.2–4).
- **Samudra-manthana** - the churning of the milk-ocean for amṛta; Kūrma bears Mandara (8.7), Śiva drinks the poison (8.7), the treasures rise (8.8), Mohinī deceives the asuras (8.9), the war after (8.10–11), and Śiva's infatuation with Mohinī (8.12). → **[has file]** [samudra-manthana.md](samudra-manthana.md), [Kūrma](kurma-avatara.md), [Mohinī](mohini-avatara.md). (Bhāg. 8.5–12).
  - *Note (the "14 ratnas"):* 8.8 names the emerging treasures one by one (the sacrificial cow, Uccaiḥśravas, Airāvata, Kaustubha, Pārijāta, the apsarases, Śrī, Vāruṇī, Dhanvantari with the amṛta) but does not total them as fourteen; the fixed count of fourteen *ratnas* comes from other lists.
- **Vāmana & Bali** - Bali's conquest of heaven, Aditi's *payovrata*, the dwarf-avatāra's three steps, and Bali's surrender and rule in Sutala. → **[has file]** [vamana-bali.md](vamana-bali.md). (Bhāg. 8.15–23).
- **Matsya** - the fish-avatāra saves Satyavrata (the future Vaivasvata Manu) and the seeds of life in a boat at the pralaya and recovers the Vedas stolen by Hayagrīva. → **[has file]** [matsya-pralaya.md](matsya-pralaya.md); cross-link: [the 14 Manus](../01-manvantaras/README.md). (Bhāg. 8.24).

## Skandha 9 — The Solar & Lunar dynasties (the genealogical canto)

The great *vaṃśa* canto (24 chapters). Most of its kings are catalogued in [03-lineages-vamsha](../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md); the **named exemplary episodes** are indexed here. Many already have files.

### Solar dynasty (Sūrya-vaṃśa / Ikṣvāku line)

- **Sudyumna / Ilā** - at Vaivasvata Manu's sacrifice the priest's slip (Manu's wife wanted a daughter) produces a girl, **Ilā**, whom Vasiṣṭha has Viṣṇu turn into the boy **Sudyumna** (9.1.14–22); entering Śiva's forbidden grove, Sudyumna becomes a woman again, bears Budha's son Purūravas, and is finally granted to alternate month by month between man and woman — origin-point branching the solar & lunar lines. → **[has file]** [Ilā / Sudyumna](../03-lineages-vamsha/ila-sudyumna.md); cross-link: [Pururavas-Urvaśī](pururavas-urvashi.md). (Bhāg. 9.1).
- **Cyavana & Sukanyā; Kakudmī & Revatī** - Śaryāti's daughter Sukanyā pierces the eyes of the ant-hill-covered sage Cyavana and is given to him in marriage; the Aśvins restore his youth (9.3.1–27). Kakudmī takes his daughter Revatī to Brahmā to ask for a husband, waits out a song, and learns that ages have passed on earth; she marries Balarāma (9.3.29–36). → **[has file]** [cyavana-sukanya.md](cyavana-sukanya.md). (Bhāg. 9.3).
- **Ambarīṣa & Durvāsā** - the Ekādaśī-keeping king, the sage's curse, and the Sudarśana-cakra that chases Durvāsā through the worlds until he begs the king's pardon. → **[has file]** [ambarisha-durvasa.md](ambarisha-durvasa.md). (Bhāg. 9.4–5).
- **Ikṣvāku & the eating of the śrāddha-hare; Vikukṣi** - sent to fetch meat for an *aṣṭakā-śrāddha*, Ikṣvāku's son **Vikukṣi** eats a hare on the way, is banished by his father, and later reigns as **Śaśāda** ("hare-eater") (9.6.6–11). Dynastic seed-episodes. (Bhāg. 9.6). **[index-only]**
- **Saubhari** - the sage who, disturbed in his underwater tapas by mating fish, weds **all fifty** daughters of King Māndhātā by multiplying his body, then renounces it all — a *vairāgya* parable on the snares of desire. (Bhāg. 9.6.38–55). **[index-only — candidate for own file]**
- **Māndhātā** - the cakravartin born from his father Yuvanāśva, who drank consecrated water by mistake; Indra gave the infant his finger to suck. → **[has file]** [mandhata.md](mandhata.md). (Bhāg. 9.6.25–37).
- **Trishaṅku** - the king who sought heaven in his body, hung between worlds. → **[has file]** [trishanku.md](trishanku.md). (Bhāg. 9.7).
- **Hariścandra** - in the Bhāgavata, Hariścandra is the childless king who promises his son Rohita to Varuṇa; Rohita buys **Śunaḥśepa** as a substitute victim (9.7.8–23), and Viśvāmitra, pleased with the king's truthfulness, teaches him (9.7.24–25). It also mentions, in one verse, the years-long fight of Viśvāmitra and Vasiṣṭha as two birds over him (9.7.7). → **[has file]** [hariscandra.md](hariscandra.md), [sunahshepa.md](sunahshepa.md). (Bhāg. 9.7).
  - *Variant (the truth-ordeal):* the famous story of Hariścandra giving up his kingdom, wife and son to keep his word to Viśvāmitra is not in the Bhāgavata; its classic Purāṇic telling is the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa's (see [hariscandra.md](hariscandra.md)).
- **Sagara, the 60,000 sons & the descent of Gaṅgā (Bhagīratha)** - the aśvamedha-horse, the sons burnt to ash before Kapila (9.8.11–12), and **Bhagīratha's** tapas bringing Gaṅgā down to liberate their ashes. → **[has file]** [Sagara](../03-lineages-vamsha/sagara.md), [Gaṅgā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganga.md); cross-link: [Gaṅgāsāgar & Kapila's āśrama](../06-sacred-geography/gangasagar-sagara-dvipa-kapila-ashram.md). (Bhāg. 9.8–9).
  - *Variant (who burnt them):* the Bhāgavata says the princes were burnt "by the fire of their own bodies", struck down by their offence against a great soul, and expressly rejects the saying that the sage's anger burnt them (9.8.12–14). The Rāmāyaṇa (Kapila's angry *huṃkāra*, 1.39.27–28, Baroda Critical Edition) and the Mahābhārata (*tataḥ kruddho mahārāja kapilo*, 3.106.2, Critical Edition) make Kapila's wrath the agent; see [Sagara](../03-lineages-vamsha/sagara.md).
- **Khaṭvāṅga** - the warrior-king who, having helped the devas, learns he has one *muhūrta* left to live, hurries home, and attains liberation in that span by total surrender (9.9.41–49) — a famed *kṣaṇa-mukti* exemplum. → **[has file]** [khatvanga-one-muhurta.md](khatvanga-one-muhurta.md). (Bhāg. 9.9).
- **Rāma (Raghu-vaṃśa précis)** - the Bhāgavata's condensed Rāmāyaṇa (Rāma as avatāra, Rāvaṇa's death, the return). → fuller treatment in [Rāmāyaṇa kāṇḍas](ramayana/README.md). (Bhāg. 9.10–11). **[partial — see ramayana/]**
- **Nimi & the Videha (Janaka) line** - Nimi and Vasiṣṭha curse each other to lose their bodies; Nimi refuses a new body and is given a home in the blinking (*nimeṣa*) of creatures' eyes (9.13.8–11); the sages churn his body and produce Janaka, called Vaideha and Mithila (9.13.12–13). → **[has file]** [Nimi and Vasiṣṭha's mutual curse](../03-lineages-vamsha/nimi-vasishtha-mutual-curse.md), [the Videha line](../03-lineages-vamsha/videha-mithila-line-janaka.md). (Bhāg. 9.13).

### Lunar dynasty (Candra-vaṃśa / Aila line)

- **Soma–Tārā–Budha & the birth of the Moon-line** - the abduction of Bṛhaspati's wife Tārā by Soma; Budha born; lunar dynasty begins. → **[has file]** [soma-tara-budha.md](soma-tara-budha.md). (Bhāg. 9.14).
- **Purūravas & Urvaśī** - the mortal king and the apsarā. → **[has file]** [pururavas-urvashi.md](pururavas-urvashi.md). (Bhāg. 9.14).
- **Paraśurāma** - the Bhṛgu avatāra who clears the earth of kṣatriyas "three times seven" (9.16.19); the Kārtavīrya / Jamadagni cycle. → **[has file]** [parashurama.md](parashurama.md), [jamadagni-parashurama.md](jamadagni-parashurama.md). (Bhāg. 9.15–16).
- **Nahuṣa & Yayāti** - Nahuṣa's fall (cross-link [nahusha.md](nahusha.md)); Yayāti's exchange of old age, Devayānī & Śarmiṣṭhā. → **[has file]** [yayati.md](yayati.md); [Soma-Tārā](soma-tara-budha.md). (Bhāg. 9.18–19).
- **Bharata (son of Duṣyanta & Śakuntalā)** - eponym of the line. → **[has file]** [shakuntala-bharata.md](shakuntala-bharata.md). (Bhāg. 9.20).
- **Rantideva** - the charitable Lunar-line king, son of Saṅkṛti (9.21.2), who after **48 days** without food or water (9.21.4) gives away his share in succession to a brāhmaṇa, a śūdra, and a guest with his dogs, and finally his last water to a *pulkasa* (an outcaste) who begs for it (9.21.5–14). His words: he wants neither the highest state with the eight powers nor freedom from rebirth, only to take on the pain of all embodied beings and live within them so that they are free of suffering (9.21.12). The "lords of the three worlds" then show themselves to him, but the text calls them Viṣṇu's *māyā*; he bows, asks nothing, and fixes his mind on Vāsudeva, and the *māyā* vanishes like a dream (9.21.15–17). A keystone *dāna* / compassion exemplum. → **[has file]** [rantideva.md](rantideva.md); cross-link: [Śibi](shibi.md). (Bhāg. 9.21.1–18).
  - *Variant (the testers):* retellings commonly name the revealed gods as the Trimūrti; the Bhāgavata says only *tribhuvanādhīśāḥ*, "the lords of the three worlds" (9.21.15).
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata):* the epic remembers Rantideva Sāṅkṛti for his vast wealth and hospitality in the "sixteen kings" list (MBh 12.29.113–120, Critical Edition; the Droṇa-parva version stands only in an appendix to 7.49), not for the water-giving.
- **The Pūru / Kuru / Yadu branches → toward Kṛṣṇa** - the canto closes by running the Yadu line down to **Vasudeva** and the imminent birth of Kṛṣṇa, hinging the genealogical canto into Skandha 10. → [Yādava Vaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/yadava-vamsha.md). (Bhāg. 9.22–24). **[partial elsewhere]**

## Skandha 10 — Kṛṣṇa-līlā (the longest canto, 90 chapters)

The narrative heart. The overall arc has a **[has file]** in [krishna-lila.md](krishna-lila.md); most named episodes now also have their own file, listed below, and the **named song-insets** (the *gītā*s) are indexed individually, since each is a discrete devotional set-piece often anthologised on its own.

### Vraja (Vṛndāvana) — the boyhood

- **Birth & the Kaṃsa-demons** - Devakī's eighth child, the river-crossing, Yaśodā's fosterage; Pūtanā (10.6), Śakaṭa and Tṛṇāvarta (10.7), the universe in Kṛṣṇa's mouth (10.8), the binding to the mortar (Dāmodara-līlā, 10.9), the twin Arjuna-trees (10.10), and the calf-demon Vatsa and the crane Baka (10.11). → **[has file]** [krishna-balya-lila.md](krishna-balya-lila.md), [krishna-lila.md](krishna-lila.md). (Bhāg. 10.1–11).
- **Brahmā-vimohana** - after Aghāsura's death (10.12), Brahmā hides the calves and cowherd boys for a year; Kṛṣṇa becomes all of them, and Brahmā, humbled, sings his hymn (10.13–14). → **[has file]** [brahma-vimohana.md](brahma-vimohana.md). (Bhāg. 10.12–14).
- **Forest demons & Govardhana** - Dhenukāsura (10.15); the **Kāliya-damana** (10.16–17); the swallowing of the forest-fire (10.17, 10.19); the **lifting of Govardhana** and the humbling of Indra (10.24–27). → **[has file]** [kaliya-mardana.md](kaliya-mardana.md), [govardhana-dharana.md](govardhana-dharana.md), [krishna-lila.md](krishna-lila.md). (Bhāg. 10.15–27).
- **Veṇu-gītā (the Song of the Flute)** - the gopīs' rapturous description of Kṛṣṇa playing his flute and herding cattle home — Vṛndāvana's creatures, rivers and hills enraptured; sung *by the gopīs about* Kṛṣṇa (from 10.21.7, in a chapter of 20 verses). The lyrical overture to the rāsa cycle. → **[has file]** [venu-gita.md](venu-gita.md). (Bhāg. **10.21**).
- **The rāsa cycle, Gopī-gītā, Yugala-gītā, Bhramara-gītā** - the cluster of gopī-songs:
  - *Rāsa-pañcādhyāya frame:* the **rāsa-līlā** proper (the circle-dance, the gopīs' pride and Kṛṣṇa's disappearance & return). → **[has file]** [rasa-lila.md](rasa-lila.md). (Bhāg. **10.29–33**).
  - **Gopī-gītā** - the 19-verse lament the gopīs sing when Kṛṣṇa vanishes mid-rāsa ("*jayati te 'dhikaṃ janmanā vrajaḥ…*", 10.31.1) — among the most beloved bhakti hymns. → in [rasa-lila.md](rasa-lila.md). (Bhāg. **10.31**).
  - **Yugala-gītā** - the gopīs' daytime song, in pairs of verses, of Kṛṣṇa away in the forest with the herd (10.35.2–25, in a chapter of 26 verses). (Bhāg. **10.35**). **[index-only]**
  - **Bhramara-gītā (the Song to the Bee)** - a gopī's distracted address to a bumblebee, which she takes for the absent Kṛṣṇa's messenger, reproaching him, when Uddhava later visits Vraja. → **[has file]** [bhramara-gita-uddhava-sandesha.md](bhramara-gita-uddhava-sandesha.md). (Bhāg. **10.47.11–21**). [the locus of *vipralambha-bhakti*]
    - *Variant (Rādhā):* the Bhāgavata says only "a certain one" (*kācit*, 10.47.11) and never names Rādhā anywhere. The identification of this gopī, and of the favoured gopī of the rāsa (10.30.28, read as a pun on *ārādhitaḥ*), with **Rādhā** is commentarial and later, especially Gauḍīya. See [Rādhā](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/radha.md). [scholarly]
- **Keśī, Ariṣṭa & Akrūra's summons; the departure for Mathurā** - the bull Ariṣṭa (10.36) and the horse Keśī (10.37) killed; Akrūra's arrival and his vision in the Yamunā (10.38–41), the breaking of the bow, the elephant Kuvalayāpīḍa, the wrestlers and Kaṃsa's death (10.42–44). → **[has file]** [keshi-arishta-keshava.md](keshi-arishta-keshava.md), [kamsa-vadha.md](kamsa-vadha.md). (Bhāg. 10.36–44).

### Mathurā & Dvārakā — the kingly career

- **Uddhava's mission to Vraja** - Kṛṣṇa sends Uddhava to console the gopīs; occasion of the Bhramara-gītā above. → in [bhramara-gita-uddhava-sandesha.md](bhramara-gita-uddhava-sandesha.md). (Bhāg. 10.46–47).
- **The Dvārakā cycle** - each now has its own file:
  - Jarāsandha's sieges, Kālayavana and Mucukunda, the founding of Dvārakā (10.50–52) → [muchukunda-and-kalayavana.md](muchukunda-and-kalayavana.md).
  - Rukmiṇī-haraṇa (10.52–54) → [rukmini-harana.md](rukmini-harana.md).
  - The Syamantaka jewel; Jāmbavatī and Satyabhāmā (10.56–57) → [syamantaka-jewel.md](syamantaka-jewel.md).
  - Naraka-vadha and the captive princesses (10.59) → [narakasura-vadha.md](narakasura-vadha.md).
    - *Variant (number of wives, all within the Bhāgavata):* **16,000** princesses at 10.59.33 and 10.69.2; **16,100** queens at 10.90.29, of whom the eight chief queens are named apart (10.90.30). The popular figure **16,108** adds the eight to 16,100.
  - Uṣā, Aniruddha and the war with Bāṇa, in which Śiva fights for Bāṇa (10.62–63) → [banasura-usha-aniruddha.md](banasura-usha-aniruddha.md).
  - Pauṇḍraka and the burning of Kāśī (10.66) → [paundraka-the-false-vasudeva.md](paundraka-the-false-vasudeva.md).
  - Śālva's flying city Saubha (10.76–77) → [shalva-saubha-vadha.md](shalva-saubha-vadha.md).
  - The poor brāhmaṇa friend, "Sudāmā" (10.80–81) → [sudama-kuchela.md](sudama-kuchela.md).
    - *Variant (name):* the Bhāgavata never names him; *ku-caila*, "poorly clad" (10.80.7), became the southern name Kucela. **Sudāmā** is a later name (see the file); the only Sudāmā in Skandha 10 is the Mathurā garland-maker (10.41.43).
  - The solar-eclipse reunion at Kurukṣetra with Nanda and the gopīs (10.82–84) → [kurukshetra-reunion-gopis.md](kurukshetra-reunion-gopis.md).
  - Devakī's six dead sons brought back (10.85) → [devaki-six-sons-shadgarbha.md](devaki-six-sons-shadgarbha.md).
  - Bhṛgu tests Brahmā, Śiva and Viṣṇu (10.89.1–19) → [bhrigu.md](bhrigu.md).
  - Overview of the whole cycle → [krishna-lila.md](krishna-lila.md). (Bhāg. 10.50–90).
- **Veda-stuti** - the personified Vedas' hymn to the attributeless Absolute, answering Parīkṣit's question how the Vedas can speak of what is beyond qualities (10.87.1); told by the sage Nārāyaṇa to Nārada at his hermitage (10.87.4–5), as once recited by Sanandana; the hymn proper is 10.87.14–41, in a chapter of 50 verses. (Bhāg. **10.87**). **[index-only]**
- **Mahiṣī-gītā** - the queens of Dvārakā, lost in love, address the osprey, the cakravāka bird, the ocean, the moon, the Malaya breeze, the cloud and others as fellow sufferers (10.90.15–24). (Bhāg. **10.90**). **[index-only]**
- **Śiśupāla** - the rival cousin beheaded by Kṛṣṇa's discus at Yudhiṣṭhira's Rājasūya, whose light then enters Kṛṣṇa, enmity over three births having fixed his mind on him (10.74.43–46). → **[has file]** [shishupala.md](shishupala.md). (Bhāg. 10.74).
  - *Variant (the hundred offences):* Kṛṣṇa's promise to Śiśupāla's mother to forgive a hundred offences is the Mahābhārata's (MBh 2.40.22, Critical Edition), not the Bhāgavata's.

## Skandha 11 — The Uddhava-gītā, the 24 gurus & Kṛṣṇa's departure

- **The curse on the Yadus & the Mausala destruction** - Yādava boys dress Sāmba as a pregnant woman and mock the sages gathered at Piṇḍāraka by asking what "she" will bear; the sages curse them to bear an iron club that will destroy the clan (11.1.11–16); later the drunken fratricide at Prabhāsa and Kṛṣṇa's withdrawal (11.30–31). Cross-link: [Mausala-parva](mahabharata/16-mausala-parva.md). (Bhāg. 11.1, 11.30–31). **[partial elsewhere]**
- **The Nine Yogendras (Nava-yogendra) to Nimi** - Nārada tells Vasudeva how the nine adept sons of Ṛṣabha taught King Nimi of Videha the *bhāgavata-dharma* — a compact theology of devotion. → **[has file]** [nimi-nava-yogendra-samvada.md](nimi-nava-yogendra-samvada.md). (Bhāg. 11.2–5).
- **The Uddhava-gītā ("the last message of Kṛṣṇa")** - Kṛṣṇa's final, longest discourse, to his friend-minister **Uddhava** on the eve of his departure: jñāna, bhakti, yoga, *varṇāśrama*, renunciation, the nature of the guru, and detachment; often published as a "sequel" to the Bhagavad-gītā. The second great philosophical dialogue of the Purāṇa (with Kapila's, Skandha 3). → **[has file]** [uddhava-gita.md](uddhava-gita.md); cross-link: [Bhagavad-gītā](bhagavad-gita.md). (Bhāg. **11.6–29**). [scholarly]
  - *Variant (extent and size):* commonly cited as **11.7–29** (1,029 verses in the GRETIL text); counting Uddhava's request in 11.6 gives **11.6–29** (1,079 verses). Published figures near 1,077 are close to the longer count.
  - *Note (inset gītās):* it contains named songs of its own: the **Haṃsa-gītā**, the Lord as a swan answering the Four Kumāras (11.13; → [hamsa-avatar.md](hamsa-avatar.md); not to be confused with the Mahābhārata's Haṃsa-gītā in [hamsa-gita.md](hamsa-gita.md)); the **Bhikṣu-gītā**, the song of the miserly Avantī brāhmaṇa turned mendicant (11.23); and the **Aila-gītā**, Purūravas's song of disgust after losing Urvaśī (11.26). The whole Uddhava-gītā is sometimes also called the "Haṃsa-gītā", though the swan's own teaching is only 11.13. [scholarly]
- **The 24 gurus of the avadhūta** - embedded *within* the Uddhava-gītā: Kṛṣṇa tells of King **Yadu**'s meeting with a young wandering **avadhūta** brāhmaṇa, who lists the **24 gurus** he learned from (11.7.33–35) — earth, air, sky, water, fire, moon, sun, pigeon, python, sea, moth, honey-bee, elephant, honey-thief, deer, fish, the courtesan **Piṅgalā**, the osprey (*kurara*), child, maiden (her bangles), arrow-maker, serpent, spider, and the wasp (*supeśakṛt*). → **[has file]** [dattatreya.md](dattatreya.md), [pingala-gita.md](pingala-gita.md); Piṅgalā is also stubbed in [minor-upakhyanas-catalog.md](minor-upakhyanas-catalog.md). (Bhāg. **11.7–9**).
  - *Variant (who the avadhūta is):* 11.7.25 calls him only "a certain avadhūta brāhmaṇa". His identification with **Dattātreya** rests on the Bhāgavata's own avatāra-lists, which say Yadu, the Haihayas and others won yogic powers through Dattātreya (2.7.4; at 1.3.11 he teaches Alarka and Prahlāda), and on the commentators. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (count):* the list is **24** (11.7.35), but the avadhūta closes by naming his own **body** as a further teacher of detachment (11.9.25). Translations differ on some identifications (e.g. the honey-bee vs. "honey-gatherer", the *kurara* as osprey or hawk). [scholarly]
- **Kṛṣṇa's departure (the hunter Jarā)** - the hunter Jarā, with an arrow made from the last fragment of the iron club, mistakes the resting Kṛṣṇa's foot for a deer (11.30.32–33); Kṛṣṇa ascends; the sea floods Dvārakā, sparing only Kṛṣṇa's own palace (11.31.23). Cross-link: [Mausala-parva](mahabharata/16-mausala-parva.md). (Bhāg. 11.30–31). **[partial elsewhere]**

## Skandha 12 — Kali-yuga, the end of Parīkṣit, & the colophon

- **The course of Kali-yuga & future kings** - the prophetic catalogue of post-Mahābhārata dynasties (Nandas, Mauryas, etc.), the symptoms of Kali, and Kalki's coming (12.2). Cross-link: [yuga-time](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md), [Kalki](kalki.md), [Kali-yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md). (Bhāg. 12.1–3). **[index-only]**
- **The four pralayas** - the systematic catalogue of dissolution (nitya, naimittika, prākṛtika, ātyantika). Cross-link: [Kalpa & pralaya](../00-time-and-cosmology/kalpa-and-pralaya.md). (Bhāg. 12.4). **[index-only]**
- **Parīkṣit & Takṣaka** - Śuka's last instruction (12.5); the king's fearless death by the serpent-bite, closing the frame; Janamejaya's snake-sacrifice (12.6.16–28). Cross-link: [Parīkṣit](../03-lineages-vamsha/parikshit.md), [Uttaṅka](uttanka.md), [Āstīka / snake-sacrifice](mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md). (Bhāg. 12.5–6). **[partial elsewhere]**
  - *Variant (who stops the sacrifice):* in the Bhāgavata, **Bṛhaspati** persuades Janamejaya to end it, since every creature reaps its own karma (12.6.23–28); in the Mahābhārata it is the boy-sage **Āstīka** (MBh Ādi-parva, Āstīka-parvan; see [Ādi-parva](mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md)).
- **Mārkaṇḍeya** - the deathless sage who beholds the pralaya and the child on the banyan-leaf, and resists Kāmadeva. → **[has file]** [markandeya.md](markandeya.md). (Bhāg. 12.8–10).
- **The Purāṇa-catalogue & colophon** - the ten marks of a Purāṇa (12.7.9–10), the list of the 18 Mahāpurāṇas (12.7.23–24), their verse-counts adding up to 400,000, with 18,000 for the Bhāgavata (12.13.4–9), the glory of the Bhāgavata, and the closing benediction. Cross-link: [literature corpus](../90-literature-corpus/README.md). (Bhāg. 12.7, 12.13). **[index-only]**
