# Viṣṇu's eye-offering and the gift of the Sudarśana chakra

> *Cosmic address: a **Cross-Yuga Śaiva charter-myth** — a timeless deva–daitya war in which the preserver-god **[Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md)** worships **[Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** to earn his signature discus. Its charter-text is the **Śiva Purāṇa, Koṭirudra-saṃhitā ch. 34** ("Acquisition of Sudarśana by Viṣṇu").*
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Master index: [README](../README.md)

The story-nucleus in one breath: pressed by invincible **daityas**, Viṣṇu propitiates Śiva at Kailāsa with a **thousand lotuses**, one flower laid down for each of Śiva's **thousand names**; Śiva secretly **withdraws one flower** to test him; finding the count one short, Viṣṇu — whose own eyes the tradition calls **lotuses** — **plucks out one of his eyes** and offers it as the thousandth "lotus"; the delighted Śiva restores him and grants the **Sudarśana Cakra**, with which Viṣṇu instantly routs the demons. The full origin-catalog of the discus lives at [Divyāstras & Divine Weapons](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md); this node is the deep treatment of the eye-offering charter and of the "lotus-eyed" epithets it is made to explain.

---

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic frame** — a **recurring deva–daitya war** in the timeless Śaiva cosmos, not pinned to a single yuga; the same devotional pattern is retold "again and again" wherever the gods are overmatched. In the Purāṇic geography the worship happens at **Kailāsa**, with flowers drawn from the **Mānasa lake** (Mānasa-sarovara). (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Koṭirudra-saṃhitā 34)
- **Academic / historical frame** — the *Śiva Purāṇa* is a **late, composite Śaiva Mahāpurāṇa**; its received recension (the Vidyeśvara-, Rudra-, Koṭirudra-, and other saṃhitās) is dated **[scholarly] c. 10th–14th century CE**, with the Koṭirudra-saṃhitā belonging to the strongly sectarian layer that also frames the twelve **jyotirliṅga** legends. The tale therefore reads a **pre-existing, Vedic-old epithet** ("lotus-eyed") back onto a Śaiva miracle — see the disambiguation below. [scholarly]

## The charter-narrative — Koṭirudra-saṃhitā 34

- **The frame** — Vyāsa's disciple **Sūta** narrates to the assembled sages, who ask *how Viṣṇu obtained the discus Sudarśana by the worship of Harīśvara (Śiva)*. The episode sits among the samhitā's Śiva-worship exempla. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Koṭirudra 34.1–4)
- **The crisis** — **powerful daityas** torment the three worlds and disrupt sacred rites; the afflicted gods take refuge with Viṣṇu: *"We are sorely oppressed by the Daityas; we have resorted to you."* Viṣṇu vows to propitiate Śiva for a weapon equal to the task. (Koṭirudra 34.5–7)
  - *Note (unnamed demons):* the charter-text leaves the daityas **generic and unnamed** — the point is the mode of worship, not a specific asura-slaying. Named uses of the finished discus (Śiśupāla, Rāhu, Jarāsandha's ally, etc.) belong to other cycles and other texts. Do **not** import a name the chapter does not give.
- **The austerity at Kailāsa** — Viṣṇu goes to **Kailāsa**, digs a fire-pit, kindles the sacred fire, and sits in yogic posture, resolved: *"By all means I shall stay here until the Lord is pleased."* (Koṭirudra 34.8–15)
- **The thousand lotuses, one per name** — Viṣṇu recites **Śiva's thousand names** (a Śiva-sahasranāma), and with **each name lays down one lotus** gathered from the **Mānasa lake** — a thousand names, a thousand flowers, a perfect ritual arithmetic. (Koṭirudra 34.18–19)
- **The hidden flower** — to test the depth of Viṣṇu's devotion, **Śiva secretly removes one lotus** from the heap. Reaching the final name, Viṣṇu finds himself **one flower short**; he searches but cannot make up the count. (Koṭirudra 34.20–22a)
- **The offered eye** — rather than break the vow or leave the last name unhonoured, Viṣṇu — reflecting that *his own eye is itself a lotus* — **plucks out one of his eyes and offers it in place of the missing flower**, completing the thousand. (Koṭirudra 34.22)
- **Śiva's boon** — instantly pleased, **Śiva manifests** and, praising the sacrifice, grants Viṣṇu the **Sudarśana Cakra** — a discus *"shining with a mass of splendour,"* radiant as many suns and *destructive of all foes*. (Koṭirudra 34.23–30)
- **The rout** — armed with the discus, Viṣṇu **slays the daityas at once, without strain**, and normalcy returns to the worlds; the gods rejoice. (Koṭirudra 34.31)

## The epithet-etiology — "lotus-eyed" (and why it must be handled carefully)

- **The names the tale claims to explain** — Vaiṣṇava usage calls Viṣṇu **Kamala-nayana / Kamalākṣa** ("lotus-eyed"), **Rājīva-locana** ("blue-lotus-eyed"), **Puṇḍarīkākṣa** ("white-lotus-eyed"), and **Padmākṣa / Padma-locana** ("lotus-eyed"). The Śaiva reading of this episode treats the eye-offering as the **charter-myth** of these epithets: because Viṣṇu once gave an eye *as* a lotus, his eyes are forever "lotuses." (Traditional exegesis on Koṭirudra 34)
- **The chronological honesty** — these epithets are **far older than this Purāṇa**. *Puṇḍarīkākṣa* and *Padmanābha* stand in the **Viṣṇu-sahasranāma** (Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana-parva) and the lotus-eye is a stock Vedic-through-epic simile for Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa long predating the *Śiva Purāṇa*. The Koṭirudra tale is therefore best read as a **later Śaiva etiology retrofitted onto an already-current epithet**, not the historical origin of the name. [scholarly]
- **What the chapter itself says** — the wisdomlib translation grants the discus and praises the eye-sacrifice **without spelling out the epithet-derivation**; the explicit "hence he is called lotus-eyed" gloss is carried by the **surrounding oral/commentarial tradition** and by digests such as the divine-weapons catalog. Flagging this keeps the attribution clean. [disputed]

## The sectarian point — a Śaiva supremacy charter

- **Who worships whom** — the theological force of the tale is that the **great preserver-god himself becomes a devotee of Śiva**, undertakes tapas, recites Śiva's names, and receives his own **most famous weapon as a gift from Śiva**. In the Śaiva view the Sudarśana is not innate to Viṣṇu but **Śiva-derived**, a claim of Śiva's precedence over Viṣṇu. (Koṭirudra 34; cf. [Śiva myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md))
- **A recurring Śaiva move** — the same logic runs through the Koṭirudra-saṃhitā's neighbouring legends, where kings, gods, and even demons win boons and weapons through liṅga-worship; the eye-offering is the Viṣṇu-instance of that pattern. See [Koṭirudra minor liṅga legends](../06-sacred-geography/kotirudra-minor-linga-legends.md) and the [Śiva Purāṇa māhātmya tales](../90-literature-corpus/shiva-purana-mahatmya-tales.md).
- **The competing (non-Śaiva) origin-accounts** — other traditions give the discus a **different provenance**, and the corpus catalogs them side by side rather than merging them:
  - *Variant (solar-forge origin):* **Viśvakarmā** grinds down **Sūrya's** excess radiance on his lathe and forges the **Sudarśana, Śiva's Triśūla, and the Puṣpaka** from the shavings — the discus is *solar*, not Śaiva. (Purāṇic; see [Navagraha / Sūrya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md))
  - *Variant (Rāmāyaṇa seizure):* Viṣṇu **wrests the disc** from the dānava **Hayagrīva** atop Mt. Cakravāna, taking it as spoil. (Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa* tradition)
  - *Variant (churning / already-borne):* in the great mass of Vaiṣṇava narrative the discus is simply Viṣṇu's **eternal attribute**, wielded at the [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md) to sever **Rāhu** and throughout his avatāra-deeds. (Bhāgavata / Viṣṇu Purāṇa usage)
- **Reading the disagreement** — these are **not reconcilable into one history**; they are rival sectarian and regional claims about a single icon. The Śaiva eye-offering asserts Śiva's gift; the solar-forge asserts a cosmic-material origin; the Vaiṣṇava default assumes no origin is needed at all. [disputed]

## The Rāma parallel — the same test, a different god

- **The structural twin (Bengali Rāmāyaṇa)** — the eye-offering has a famous mirror in the **akāl-bodhan**: worshipping **Durgā** before the war on Laṅkā, **Rāma** offers **108 blue lotuses** (nīl-padma); the **Goddess hides one** to test him; Rāma — called **Padma-locana** ("lotus-eyed") from childhood — prepares to **pluck out his own eye** to complete the 108, and Durgā stays his hand and grants victory. **Canonical home: [Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇ](ramayana/recensions/krittivasi-ramayan.md)** (and [Durgā Pūjā in Bengal](../05-concepts-and-dharma/durga-puja-bengal.md)). (Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇ, 15th c. CE) [regional]
- **Same motif, opposite polarity** — in the Śaiva tale the male god is **Viṣṇu** and the tested-toward deity is **Śiva** (a Śaiva claim); in the Śākta tale the devotee is **Viṣṇu-as-Rāma** and the tested-toward deity is the **Goddess** (a Śākta claim). The **hidden flower + offered lotus-eye** is a portable devotional template that each sect turns to its own god's glory. [scholarly]
- **Shared symbol-logic** — both hinge on the pun that the devotee's **eye *is* a lotus** (*kamala/padma* = both flower and eye), so that the missing flower and the plucked eye are, ritually, the *same offering* — the body itself completing the pūjā when the world's flowers run out.
