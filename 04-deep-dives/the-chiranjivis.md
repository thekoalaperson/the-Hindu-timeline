# The Cirañjīvis — the Eight Immortals across the Yugas

> Cosmic address: Śveta-Varāha Kalpa › 51st year of Brahmā › 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara › 28th Mahāyuga › threading **all four yugas** of this Mahāyuga and onward to its end.
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./) · Up to the master index: [README](../README.md)

The **cirañjīvi** ("long-lived ones," from *cira* = long + *jīvin* = living) are the beings boon- or curse-bound to remain on Earth through the **entire current Kalpa** — not absolute immortals (*amara*), but *dīrgha-āyuṣ* whose span equals an age or epoch. They are the connective tissue of this timeline: each appears in multiple yuga-files, often meeting incarnations of the *same* deities across thousands of divine years. Tradition holds several will survive to the close of [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) and assist the [Kalki avatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) in inaugurating the next Satya Yuga.

---

## The Smaraṇa-Śloka (mnemonic verse)

The standard *cirañjīvi-smaraṇa* couplet, recited at dawn for health and longevity (traditional *stotra*; quoted in numerous Purāṇic and devotional compilations):

> अश्वत्थामा बलिर्व्यासो हनुमांश्च विभीषणः।
> कृपः परशुरामश्च सप्तैते चिरञ्जीविनः॥
> सप्तैतान् संस्मरेन्नित्यं मार्कण्डेयमथाष्टमम्।
> जीवेद्वर्षशतं सोऽपि सर्वव्याधिविवर्जितः॥
>
> *Aśvatthāmā Balir Vyāso Hanumāṁś ca Vibhīṣaṇaḥ / Kṛpaḥ Paraśurāmaś ca saptaite cirañjīvinaḥ //*
> *Saptaitān saṁsmaren nityaṁ Mārkaṇḍeyam athāṣṭamam / jīved varṣa-śataṁ so 'pi sarva-vyādhi-vivarjitaḥ //*
>
> "Aśvatthāmā, Bali, Vyāsa, Hanumān, Vibhīṣaṇa, Kṛpa, and Paraśurāma — these **seven** are the immortals. Remembering these seven daily, with **Mārkaṇḍeya** as the eighth, one lives a hundred years free of all ailments."

- **The "7 vs 8" issue** - The first couplet names *seven* (*saptaite*); the second adds Mārkaṇḍeya as *aṣṭama* ("eighth") to round the daily *smaraṇa*. Lists therefore appear as both **Sapta-Cirañjīvi** (7) and **Aṣṭa-Cirañjīvi** (8).
  - *Variant (extended/folk lists):* Some traditions append **Jāmbavān**, and occasionally **Kākabhuśuṇḍi, Agastya, Nārada**, yielding nine or more [folk tradition].

## The Eight (with curse/boon basis, attribute, and cross-yuga thread)

### 1. Aśvatthāmā — the cursed warrior (Dvāpara → Kali)
- **Birth & nature** - Son of guru [Droṇa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) and Kṛpī (Kṛpa's sister); born with a *maṇi* (gem) in his forehead granting freedom from fear, hunger, fatigue (Mahābhārata). Embodies **valor turned to wrath**.
- **The basis of his curse** - After the war, in grief over Droṇa's death, he launches the night-massacre of the Pāṇḍava camp (*Sauptika Parva*) and later aims the **Brahmaśiras / Brahmāstra** at the womb of Uttarā to extinguish the Pāṇḍava line ([Mahābhārata](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md)).
- **The curse itself** - **Kṛṣṇa** revives the stillborn **Parīkṣit** and curses Aśvatthāmā to wander **3,000 years** in jungles, alone, reeking of pus and blood from incurable festering sores, his forehead-gem ripped out — deathless but suffering, until the end of Kali Yuga (Mahābhārata, *Sauptika Parva*).
- **Cross-yuga thread** - The only cirañjīvi whose immortality is a *punishment*; appears in [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and is reputed (folk) to still roam into Kali.
  - *Variant (some Purāṇic readings):* he is to be the **next Vyāsa / a Saptarṣi** in a future manvantara — recasting the curse as eventual elevation [disputed].

### 2. Mahābali (Bali) — the dharmic Daitya king (Satya/Treta → Pātāla, to Kali's end)
- **Lineage & nature** - Daitya emperor, grandson of [Prahlāda](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md), great-grandson of Hiraṇyakaśipu; conqueror of the three worlds, renowned for **generosity and dharmic rule**.
- **The boon-basis** - In the **[Vāmana](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** episode (5th avatāra), Viṣṇu as a dwarf-brahmin begs "three paces" of land; growing cosmic, he covers earth and heaven in two strides and sets the third on Bali's head, pressing him to Pātāla — but, moved by Bali's truthfulness, grants him **rulership of Sutala/Pātāla**, a boon of immortality, and the promise that **Vāmana will guard his door** (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.15–23; Vāmana Purāṇa).
- **Future role** - Destined to become **Indra of the next (8th, Sāvarṇi) manvantara** (Purāṇas) — see [manvantara system](../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md).
- **Cross-yuga thread** - Annual return celebrated as **Onam** (Kerala) [folk tradition]; embodies the persistence of **dāna (charity)** through the ages.

### 3. Veda-Vyāsa (Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana) — the compiler-sage (Dvāpara, spanning ages)
- **Nature** - Son of sage [Parāśara](../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md) and Satyavatī; the **Vyāsa of this 28th Mahāyuga**. Embodies **erudition / preservation of dharma**. Note: "Vyāsa" is an *office* — a new arranger of the Veda each Dvāpara (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.3), so this cirañjīvi-Vyāsa is one specific holder.
- **The works** - Divided the single Veda into four, composed the **[Mahābhārata](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md)** (dictated to Gaṇeśa), the **eighteen [Mahāpurāṇas](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md)** and the **[Brahma-Sūtra](../90-literature-corpus/upavedas-vedangas-darshanas.md)** — the literary spine of this entire timeline.
- **Immortality basis** - Counted immortal by inherent yogic attainment and his ongoing task of safeguarding scripture across yugas (Mahābhārata / Purāṇas); a *jñāna-avatāra* (partial descent of Viṣṇu) in some lists.
- **Cross-yuga thread** - Father (by niyoga) of [Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Pāṇḍu, Vidura](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md); recurring counsellor through the [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) war narrative.

### 4. Hanumān — the deathless devotee (Treta → Dvāpara → Kali)
- **Nature** - Vānara son of Vāyu and Añjanā, *rudrāvatāra* in Śaiva tradition; embodies **selfless bhakti, strength, and service**. The cirañjīvi most actively venerated.
- **Boon-basis (multiple)** - As a child he is struck down by Indra's vajra for leaping at the sun; the assembled devas each grant boons making him invulnerable (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa / Purāṇas). In the **[Rāmāyaṇa](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)**, **Sītā** blesses him with deathlessness and eternal youth, and **Rāma** decrees he shall live as long as Rāma's story is told on Earth.
  - *Variant (Śaiva):* his immortality flows from being an aṁśa of **Śiva/Rudra**.
- **Cross-yuga thread** - Central in [Treta Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) (Sundara/Yuddha Kāṇḍa); reappears in [Dvāpara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) — humbling **Bhīma** in the forest (*Vana Parva*) and riding as the **emblem on Arjuna's banner** (*kapidhvaja*) at Kurukṣetra (Mahābhārata). Believed present wherever the Rāma-kathā is recited [folk tradition].

### 5. Vibhīṣaṇa — the righteous defector (Treta → end of Kalpa)
- **Nature** - Rākṣasa, younger brother of [Rāvaṇa](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md); embodies **dharma over kinship**. Born of devout penance, he counsels Rāvaṇa to return Sītā; rejected, he crosses to Rāma's side (*śaraṇāgati*).
- **Boon-basis** - After Rāvaṇa's fall, **Rāma** crowns him king of **Laṅkā** and grants him a long life lasting until the end of this Kalpa, charging him to uphold dharma in a rākṣasa realm (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, *Yuddha Kāṇḍa*).
- **Cross-yuga thread** - Survives the [Treta](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) epic; the standing king of Laṅkā ever after; a model of *śaraṇāgati* invoked into Kali.

### 6. Kṛpa (Kṛpācārya) — the perennial preceptor (Dvāpara → Kali)
- **Nature** - Born (with twin sister Kṛpī) of sage Śaradvān from seed fallen on śara-grass; raised by King Śantanu — hence *Kṛpa* ("pity/grace"). Royal teacher of the Kuru princes alongside Droṇa. Embodies **continuity of teaching**.
- **Immortality basis** - One of three survivors of the Kurukṣetra war on the Kaurava side (with Aśvatthāmā and Kṛtavarmā); declared a cirañjīvi and named a **Saptarṣi of the coming (8th) Sāvarṇi manvantara** (Mahābhārata; Purāṇas) — linking him to the future [manvantara](../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md).
- **Cross-yuga thread** - Spans the whole [Dvāpara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) war epic; the sage-counselor archetype carried into Kali.

### 7. Paraśurāma — the axe-wielding avatāra (Treta → Dvāpara → Kali, → Kalki's guru)
- **Nature** - **6th [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** of Viṣṇu; Bhārgava brahmin-warrior, son of Jamadagni and Reṇukā. Embodies **martial dharma / the warrior-priest**.
- **Deeds & basis** - Beheads his mother at his father's word (then restores her); annihilates the **Kṣatriyas twenty-one times** to avenge Jamadagni's killing by the Haihaya king Kārtavīrya Arjuna; fills the **five lakes of Samanta-pañcaka** (Mahābhārata; Bhāgavata Purāṇa). As a chiranjivi-avatāra his deathlessness is inherent to his divine descent.
- **Cross-yuga thread (uniquely wide)** - Teacher in **[Treta](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** (gifts Viṣṇu's bow to Rāma after the Sītā-svayaṁvara, then yields his power to him) **and** in **[Dvāpara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** (martial guru of Bhīṣma, Droṇa, and Karṇa — whom he curses to forget his astras). Prophesied to **reappear at Kali's end as preceptor of [Kalki](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** (Kalki Purāṇa) — closing the cosmic loop into the next Satya Yuga.

### 8. Mārkaṇḍeya — the death-conquering sage (Satya origin, all yugas)
- **Nature** - Son of Mṛkaṇḍu; embodies **devotion that conquers death**. Granted by Śiva at birth the choice of a short brilliant life or long dull one; chose the former.
- **Boon-basis** - At sixteen, the destined hour of death, he clasps the Śiva-liṅga chanting the **Mahā-mṛtyuñjaya mantra**; when **Yama** casts his noose it falls on the liṅga, and **Śiva** bursts forth, defeats Yama, and grants him **eternal youth at sixteen forever** (Skanda / Liṅga / Padma Purāṇas; *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* frame). See the dedicated deep-dive: [markandeya.md](markandeya.md).
- **Cross-yuga thread** - The **deathless witness**: in the Mahābhārata's *Vana Parva* and the Purāṇas he survives a **pralaya**, floating on the cosmic waters and seeing the infant Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa on the banyan leaf — a direct eyewitness to dissolution. Interlocutor of the **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa** (which contains the [Devī Māhātmya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md)).

## Frequently-appended ninth: Jāmbavān
- **Jāmbavān (Jāmbavanta)** - The bear-king (*ṛkṣa-rāja*), said to be born of Brahmā and already **many manvantaras old**. In the [Rāmāyaṇa](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) he reminds **Hanumān** of his powers before the ocean-leap and aids the bridge-building. In the [Mahābhārata/Bhāgavata](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) he fights **Kṛṣṇa 21 days** over the **Syamantaka jewel**, recognizes Him as Rāma returned, surrenders the gem and gives his daughter **Jāmbavatī** in marriage (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.56). Spans Treta *and* Dvāpara directly — hence often listed as the **ninth cirañjīvi** [folk tradition].

## Synthesis — what threads them together
- **Three witness *both* Rāma and Kṛṣṇa in the flesh:** Hanumān, Paraśurāma, and Jāmbavān — making them living links between [Treta](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) and [Dvāpara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md).
- **Boon vs. curse:** seven hold immortality as grace (Bali, Vyāsa, Hanumān, Vibhīṣaṇa, Kṛpa, Paraśurāma, Mārkaṇḍeya); **Aśvatthāmā alone is cursed** with it.
- **Three look forward to the next world-cycle:** **Bali** (future Indra), **Kṛpa** (future Saptarṣi), and **Paraśurāma** (Kalki's guru) — each bridging this manvantara/Mahāyuga to the next via the [manvantara system](../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md) and [Kalki](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Each embodies one persisting human attribute** (Aśvatthāmā→valor/wrath, Bali→charity, Vyāsa→wisdom, Hanumān→devotion, Vibhīṣaṇa→righteousness, Kṛpa→guidance, Paraśurāma→martial dharma, Mārkaṇḍeya→faith) — which, tradition says, endures in humanity only while that immortal lives.
