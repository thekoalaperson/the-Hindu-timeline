# Ribhu and Nidāgha — the Viṣṇu Purāṇa's Advaita Parable

> *Up to [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · master [index](../README.md). A two-chapter teaching story from [Viṣṇu Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/vishnu-purana.md) Book II (aṃśas 15–16), set in the timeless milieu of the primordial ṛṣis and narrated within the Svāyambhuva-era [Jaḍa Bharata](./rishabha-bharata-jada.md) frame — companion piece to the much later Śaiva [Ribhu-Gītā](./ribhu-gita.md), which reuses the same guru–disciple pair.*

The story of **Ṛbhu and Nidāgha** is the Viṣṇu Purāṇa's most celebrated statement of *advaita* (non-duality): a guru who returns to his disciple at thousand-year intervals, tests him with the most ordinary questions — about dinner, about a king riding past on an elephant — and dissolves, through them, the disciple's residual sense that "I" and "you," "above" and "below," are finally real. It is the classic case of Advaita taught **not by treatise but by parable**, and its climactic exchange — *"which of us is you, and which is I?"* — became a touchstone quoted across the later Vedāntic tradition. Notably, this signature non-dual teaching sits inside a firmly **Vaiṣṇava** Purāṇa, embedded in a Book otherwise devoted to cosmic geography. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.15–16)

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural time** — The parable belongs to **primordial ṛṣi-time**: Ṛbhu is introduced as a son of **Brahmā** himself, innately possessed of true wisdom, and Nidāgha is the son of the Prajāpati **Pulastya** — placing the events in the deep antiquity of the first generations after creation, before any datable yuga. Its *telling*, however, is doubly framed: **Jaḍa Bharata** (of Priyavrata's line, hence the [Svāyambhuva Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-01-svayambhuva.md) era) recounts it to the king of Sauvīra, and the whole is re-narrated by **Parāśara to Maitreya** in the Purāṇa's outer frame. Two thousand divine years elapse *inside* the story between Ṛbhu's instruction and his second visit. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.15–16)
- **Manuscript / academic time** — The Viṣṇu Purāṇa is among the **oldest extant Purāṇas in present form**; scholarly estimates for its composition range from **c. 400–300 BCE (V. Smith) through c. 275–325 CE (Hazra) to c. 450 CE (Doniger)**, with the Kali king-list giving a *terminus* around the 3rd–4th century CE — see the [Viṣṇu Purāṇa profile](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/vishnu-purana.md) for the full dating debate. The parable thus predates by centuries the systematized Advaita Vedānta of [Ādi Śaṅkara](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/adi-shankara.md) (c. 8th c. CE), and is often cited as evidence of well-developed non-dual teaching in the pre-Śaṅkara Purāṇic layer. [scholarly]

## The frame within the frame

- **Parāśara → Maitreya → Jaḍa Bharata → the Sauvīra king** — The parable is the capstone of the **Bharata cycle** (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.13–16): the royal ascetic reborn as the seemingly inert brāhmaṇa **Jaḍa Bharata** is pressed into service as a palanquin-bearer by the **king of Sauvīra**, astonishes him with a discourse on the non-difference of bearer and borne, and then, asked to teach further, relates the story of Ṛbhu and Nidāgha as his clinching illustration. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.13–16) — full cycle at [Ṛṣabha–Bharata–Jaḍa Bharata](./rishabha-bharata-jada.md).
- **The frame's own resolution** — Having heard the parable, the Sauvīra king abandons his notion of difference, honours the brāhmaṇa, and turns toward liberating knowledge; Bharata, his teaching done, goes his way, himself bound for final release. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.16)

## The protagonists

- **Ṛbhu, son of Brahmā** — Introduced as a son of the supreme **Brahmā**, endowed from birth with innate knowledge of truth (*tattva-jñāna*) — an ever-realized sage who needs no instruction and whose teaching therefore flows from fullness, not learning. Tradition groups him with the primordial [mind-born seers](./the-mind-born-seers.md) of Brahmā. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.15)
  - *Note (distinct figures):* this Advaita teacher Ṛbhu must not be confused with the **Ṛbhus**, the deified artisan-craftsmen of the Ṛgveda who split Tvaṣṭṛ's soma-cup. [scholarly]
- **Nidāgha, son of Pulastya** — Ṛbhu's disciple, son of the Prajāpati **Pulastya** (one of Brahmā's [mind-born Prajāpatis](../08-beings-and-bestiary/the-prajapatis.md)). Ṛbhu imparts the whole of divine knowledge to him, yet perceives that Nidāgha **remains attached to duality** — outwardly learned and ritually impeccable, inwardly still convinced that the world of distinct persons and things is real. Curing this residue is the engine of the whole story. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.15)
- **Vīranagara on the Devikā** — Nidāgha settles at **Vīranagara**, a great and prosperous city on the banks of the **Devikā** river, living as a householder in a grove on its outskirts — the picture of the correct but unliberated brāhmaṇa. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.15)

## First visit — the meal at Vīranagara (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.15)

- **Ṛbhu arrives after a thousand divine years** — When **a thousand divine years** have passed since the original instruction, Ṛbhu resolves to visit his disciple and see the state of his mind. He comes to Nidāgha's door **unannounced and unrecognized**, as an ordinary guest at mealtime. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.15)
- **The rites of hospitality** — Nidāgha, not knowing his guru, receives the stranger with the full courtesies owed a guest — water for the feet, a seat, respectful welcome — and invites him to eat, asking what food would please him and setting before him the best his house affords. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.15)
- **The catechism of dinner** — After the meal Nidāgha plies his guest with the polite questions of a host: *Are you satisfied? Where do you dwell? Whence have you come, and whither are you going?* Ṛbhu turns every question inside out: **hunger and thirst are affections of the vital breath, gratification a passing mode of the mind — none of them belong to the Self**; and for the **one omnipresent Ātman**, "dwelling," "coming," and "going" are words without meaning — he who is everywhere neither arrives nor departs, and the satisfaction the host inquires after belongs to no one. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.15)
- **First recognition** — Hearing speech possible only for one whose mind holds no second, Nidāgha realizes the stranger must be his master. Ṛbhu confirms it: *"I am Ṛbhu, your preceptor, come to you out of regard for the service you once rendered me, to impart true wisdom."* He declares to Nidāgha the **essential unity of all things** — that the whole universe, and Nidāgha himself, are undivided from the supreme Self — and departs. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.15)

## Second visit — the king and the elephant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.16)

- **Another thousand years** — After **another thousand years**, Ṛbhu returns to Vīranagara to test his disciple again. He finds Nidāgha outside the city walls, keeping well apart from a jostling crowd, having come in from the forest bearing fuel and *kuśa* grass. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.16)
- **"The king is entering the city"** — Asked why he stands aloof, Nidāgha explains: the **king**, in great pomp, is entering the city mounted on a **mighty elephant**, and the crowd presses thick, so he waits. Ṛbhu, playing the ignorant rustic, asks: *"Which one is the king, and which is the elephant?"* (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.16)
- **"Above" and "below"** — Nidāgha, amused, answers that **the king is the one above and the elephant the one beneath**. Ṛbhu presses on: *"But what is meant by 'above,' and what by 'below'?"* Exasperated, Nidāgha **leaps onto his questioner's shoulders**, saying: *"See — I am above, like the king; you are below, like the elephant."* (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.16)
- **"Which of us is you, and which is I?"** — Ṛbhu, bearing him, delivers the thrust: *"If you are as the king and I as the elephant, then tell me — **which of us is 'you,' and which is 'I'?**"* The indexical words on which all dualism hangs suddenly find no purchase; Nidāgha's world of separate selves collapses in a single question. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.16)
- **Recognition and prostration** — Nidāgha throws himself at the stranger's feet: *"Surely you are my master Ṛbhu — the mind of no other is so perfected in non-duality."* Ṛbhu assents: he has come, for the sake of his pupil's former devotion, to instruct him once more; and having taught him that **all this is the one undivided Self**, he departs. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.16)
- **Nidāgha's liberation** — By that instruction Nidāgha thereafter **beholds all beings as non-different from himself** and attains final liberation (*mokṣa*) — the parable's terminus, and the model Jaḍa Bharata commends to the Sauvīra king: look upon self and other, friend and foe, as the one Self, for the Self is one in all. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa II.16)

## Teaching and afterlife of the parable

- **Advaita by ordinary language** — The parable's method is distinctive: no cosmology, no scriptural exegesis, only the failure of everyday deixis — *you/I, above/below, here/there* — under non-dual scrutiny. It dramatizes the core claims later systematized as **Ātman = Brahman** (see [Ātman, Brahman & the six darśanas](../05-concepts-and-dharma/atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md)) and the superimposition of difference through ignorance (see [māyā and avidyā](../05-concepts-and-dharma/maya-and-avidya.md)). [scholarly]
- **A Vaiṣṇava home for a non-dual jewel** — The Viṣṇu Purāṇa frames ultimate reality as Viṣṇu/Vāsudeva, and the parable serves its Book-II teaching that the one Lord pervades all beings; later **Advaita Vedāntins** nevertheless claimed the story as a proof-text for unqualified non-dualism, while Śrī-Vaiṣṇavas read the same Purāṇa through Rāmānuja's qualified non-dualism — the two readings coexist, attached to the same chapters. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **The pair migrate across the corpus** — Ṛbhu and Nidāgha became the stock guru–disciple pair of later non-dual literature:
  - *Variant (Śivarahasya Purāṇa, Ṛbhu-Gītā):* the same pair anchor the vast ~2,200-verse **Śaiva** [Ribhu-Gītā](./ribhu-gita.md), where the Viṣṇu Purāṇa's terse parable expands into 44 chapters of sustained *ajāta-vāda* affirmation — a **late South-Indian** reuse of the figures, with no city, king, or elephant. [late text] [regional]
  - *Variant (Tejobindu Upaniṣad):* Ṛbhu instructs Nidāgha in the Advaita-leaning **Tejobindu Upaniṣad** of the Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda (see the [Yoga Upaniṣads overview](../90-literature-corpus/upanishads/yoga-upanishads-overview.md)). [late text]
  - *Variant (Varāha Upaniṣad):* Ṛbhu appears there as the *recipient* — Viṣṇu as the Boar teaches **Ṛbhu** the tattvas and jīvan-mukti (same [overview](../90-literature-corpus/upanishads/yoga-upanishads-overview.md)); the **Annapūrṇā Upaniṣad** likewise carries a "Ribhu/Nidāgha-style" jīvan-mukti teaching (see the [sectarian Upaniṣads overview](../90-literature-corpus/upanishads/vaishnava-shaiva-shakta-upanishads-overview.md)). [late text]
- **Kindred parables** — Within this corpus the story sits beside other "enlightenment by staged encounter" tales: [Cūḍālā and Śikhidhvaja](./chudala-shikhidhvaja.md) in the [Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha](../90-literature-corpus/yoga-vasishtha.md), the [Aṣṭāvakra](./characters/ashtavakra.md)–Janaka dialogue, and the didactic [lesser Gītās](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md). [scholarly]
