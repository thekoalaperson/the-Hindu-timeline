# Paraśurāma avatāra

> Cosmic address: the **sixth** of the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md), born in the **[Tretā Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the present **[Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)** and — as a **cirañjīvi** — surviving across **[Dvāpara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** and **[Kali](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** to the very end of the Mahāyuga.
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · See also: [The Cirañjīvis](./the-chiranjivis.md) · [Kalki avatāra](./kalki.md) · [Karṇa](./karna.md) · [Bhīṣma](./characters/bhishma.md)

**Paraśurāma** (परशुराम, "Rāma-with-the-axe"; also *Bhārgava Rāma*, *Jāmadagnya*) is the brahmin-warrior avatāra: a *cirañjīvi* (deathless one) born to the Bhṛgu sage **Jamadagni** and **Reṇukā**, who avenges his father's murder by exterminating the warring kṣatriya order **twenty-one times** and by reclaiming a strip of western coastland from the sea. He is the avatāra who **does not die** — he bridges Tretā into Dvāpara as guru to [Bhīṣma](./characters/bhishma.md), Droṇa and [Karṇa](./karna.md), breaks Śiva's bow on the threshold of the [Rāmāyaṇa](./ramayana/), and survives to Kali's end as preceptor of [Kalki](./kalki.md). The fullest narrative is the **Mahābhārata** (*Vana Parva* 3.115–117; *Śānti/Anuśāsana* references) and the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (9.15–16); the *Viṣṇu*, *Brahmāṇḍa* and *Padma Purāṇas* supply the Reṇukā material; the Konkan/Kerala land-reclamation legends are largely regional/Purāṇic.

**Two clocks.** Scripturally Paraśurāma is a single deathless figure threading one Mahāyuga from Tretā to Kali; his "appearances" in the Rāmāyaṇa, Mahābhārata, and Kalki Purāṇa are the *same* immortal at different points of that cycle — see [The Cirañjīvis](./the-chiranjivis.md). Claims that map his Konkan/Kerala land-grants onto historical Brahmin migrations or particular dynasties belong to a separate, regional-historical clock and are flagged below.

## I. Lineage and the wish-cow

- **The Bhārgava house** — Paraśurāma is born to **Jamadagni** (son of Ṛcīka and Satyavatī, of the Bhṛgu line) and **Reṇukā** (daughter of King Prasenajit/Reṇu), the youngest of five sons; his fierce temper marks him from boyhood as the *aṃśa* of Viṣṇu come to humble an over-mighty kṣatriya class. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.15; *Mahābhārata* 3.115)
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata 13.4 / Aurva legend):* the militancy of the Bhārgavas is traced to the earlier near-extermination of the Bhṛgus by kṣatriyas, the wrath of **Aurva**, and the *kṣatra*-tinged blood that Ṛcīka's sacrificial *caru* (oblation-porridge) accidentally introduces — explaining why a brahmin should take up the axe. [scholarly]
- **Kāmadhenu / Surabhi at the hermitage** — Jamadagni's āśrama holds the wish-cow **Kāmadhenu** (here often **Suśīlā / Nandinī / Surabhi**), who furnishes endless food and hospitality at a word; she is the engine of the whole tragedy. (*Mahābhārata* 3.116; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.15)
- **The axe (paraśu)** — Rāma Jāmadagnya worships **Śiva**, who grants him the celestial battle-**axe (paraśu)** from which he takes his name, along with weapons and martial mastery — arming a brahmin with kṣatriya power. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.15; Purāṇic) [late text]

## II. The beheading and restoration of Reṇukā

- **Reṇukā's lapse** — Sent to fetch water, **Reṇukā** sees the Gandharva king **Citraratha** (in some texts **Citrasena**) sporting with celestial women, and her mind strays for a moment with desire; she returns having lost her *yogic* purity, and Jamadagni perceives it. (*Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*; *Padma Purāṇa*; cf. *Mahābhārata* 3.116)
- **The father's command** — Enraged, Jamadagni orders each son in turn to **slay their mother**; the four elder sons refuse and are cursed to idiocy/loss of reason. **Paraśurāma alone obeys** and beheads Reṇukā with the axe — the supreme test of obedience to the guru-father. (*Mahābhārata* 3.116; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.16)
- **The boon and restoration** — Pleased by his obedience, Jamadagni grants Paraśurāma boons; he asks that his **mother be restored to life** (with no memory of the death), that his brothers regain their wits, and for invincibility and long life. Jamadagni revives Reṇukā. (*Mahābhārata* 3.116; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.16)
  - *Variant (South Indian / folk — Reṇukā–Yellammā):* in Deccan village cult, the axe falls on both Reṇukā and an outcaste woman **Mātaṅgī** who sheltered her; in the hasty restoration their heads are **swapped onto the wrong bodies**, generating the twin goddesses **Yellammā / Reṇukā** and **Mātaṅgī** — a major folk aetiology absent from the Sanskrit epic. [folk][regional]

## III. Kārtavīrya Arjuna and the murder of Jamadagni

- **Sahasrārjuna the thousand-armed** — **Kārtavīrya Arjuna** (Sahasrārjuna), Haihaya/Cedi king of **Māhiṣmatī**, a devotee of **Dattātreya** granted a thousand arms and near-invincibility, is the over-mighty kṣatriya whose pride the avatāra exists to break. (*Mahābhārata* 3.115–116; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.15)
- **The theft of Kāmadhenu** — Hunting near the hermitage, Kārtavīrya is feasted by the wish-cow; coveting her, he **seizes Kāmadhenu (or her calf) by force** and drags her off, wrecking the āśrama. (*Mahābhārata* 3.116; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.15)
- **First slaying of Kārtavīrya** — Paraśurāma pursues, and in battle **severs the thousand arms** and beheads Kārtavīrya, recovering the cow — the opening blow of the kṣatriya war. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.15–16)
- **Murder of Jamadagni** — In revenge, the **sons of Kārtavīrya** fall on the hermitage in Paraśurāma's absence and **behead the meditating Jamadagni**, carrying off his head; the grieving Reṇukā strikes her breast **twenty-one times**, fixing the number of the coming vengeance. (*Mahābhārata* 3.116–117; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.16)

## IV. The twenty-one-fold extermination of the kṣatriyas

- **The vow of annihilation** — Paraśurāma vows to **rid the earth of kṣatriyas twenty-one times** (*triḥsapta-kṛtvaḥ*), and circuits the world slaughtering the warrior kings and their lines who have grown tyrannical and adharmic. (*Mahābhārata* 3.117; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.16)
- **The five blood-lakes at Samanta-pañcaka** — At **Samanta-pañcaka** (Kurukṣetra) he fills **five pools with the blood** of the slain kṣatriyas and offers *tarpaṇa* (libation) to his ancestors; the pacified Bhṛgu forefathers appear and bless him to relent. (*Mahābhārata* 3.117; cf. 9.53 Tīrtha-yātrā) — this is why the same field is later the site of the Kurukṣetra war.
- **The great gift (mahā-dāna)** — Having cleared the earth, Paraśurāma performs a vast **Aśvamedha** and **gifts the whole earth to the priest Kaśyapa** as *dakṣiṇā*; Kaśyapa then bids him quit the land he has given away, and he withdraws to the **Mahendra** mountain. (*Mahābhārata* 3.117; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.16)
  - *Variant (survival of the line):* tradition explains the continuance of kings by holding that **kṣatriya women** bore sons to brahmins, or that boy-children were hidden (e.g., with the Earth, the Sun, or in mountains), so the *varṇa* was repeatedly regenerated between the twenty-one purges. [scholarly]
- **Retirement to Mahendra** — Paraśurāma settles on **Mahendragiri** (Mahendra mountain, often placed in the Eastern Ghats / Odisha) as a deathless ascetic, emerging only when summoned by dharma. (*Mahābhārata*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.16)

## V. Reclaiming the western coast (Konkan / Kerala)

- **Land from the sea** — Forbidden by Kaśyapa to dwell on the earth he gave away, Paraśurāma **hurls his axe (or shoots an arrow) into the ocean** from the Western Ghats; the sea-god **Varuṇa / Samudra** recedes, and the strip of reclaimed coast — the **Konkan** and **Kerala** (*Paraśurāma-kṣetra* / *Śūrpāraka*) — becomes his new abode and a refuge for resettled brahmins. (*Padma*, *Skanda*, *Brahmāṇḍa* Purāṇas; Kerala/Konkan creation legends) [regional]
  - *Variant (Kerala — Keralolpatti):* the **Keralolpatti** chronicle has Paraśurāma reclaim Kerala, install the **64 brahmin gramas (settlements)** and the social order of Malabar, and consecrate its temples — the regional charter-myth of Kerala's creation and its Nambudiri brahmins. [regional][late text]
  - *Variant (Tuluva / Konkan):* parallel legends make him founder of **Tuḷu-nāḍu** and the western *Saptakoṅkaṇa*, the patron of coastal Paraśurāma-kṣetra shrines (Gokarṇa, Śūrpāraka/Sopara, Udupi region). [regional][folk]
  - *Two-clocks note:* attempts to read these grants as memory of historical Brahmin migration into the Konkan/Kerala littoral are a **historical-clock** interpretation laid over the scriptural figure; the texts present it as a single avatāra's deed, not a dated event. [disputed]

## VI. Across the two epics — the deathless preceptor

- **Breaking Śiva's bow (Rāmāyaṇa)** — In the **Rāmāyaṇa** (*Bāla Kāṇḍa* 74–76), after Rāma Dāśarathi snaps Śiva's bow at Sītā's *svayaṃvara*, the wrathful Paraśurāma confronts him, challenges him to string the **bow of Viṣṇu (Śārṅga)**; Rāma effortlessly bends and draws it, **absorbing Paraśurāma's tejas (martial energy)** — the Tretā moment where one Viṣṇu-avatāra yields to the next. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* 1.74–76) — see [Rāmāyaṇa deep-dives](./ramayana/).
- **Guru of Bhīṣma** — In the Mahābhārata, Paraśurāma is preceptor-in-arms to **[Bhīṣma](./characters/bhishma.md)**; over the cause of **Ambā** the two fight an indecisive multi-day duel (the **Amba/Ambopākhyāna**), neither able to defeat the other, until the gods intervene. (*Mahābhārata* 5.176–186, Udyoga Parva) [scholarly]
- **Guru of Droṇa** — Paraśurāma, distributing his wealth to brahmins before withdrawal, gives **Droṇa** his entire stock of celestial weapons (*astras*) and the science of arms — the source of Droṇa's military supremacy. (*Mahābhārata* 1.121–129, Ādi Parva)
- **Guru and curser of Karṇa** — **[Karṇa](./karna.md)**, posing as a brahmin to learn the **Brahmāstra** from him, lets Paraśurāma sleep on his lap and endures a boring worm without flinching; detecting the kṣatriya endurance, Paraśurāma **curses him** to forget the weapon's *mantra* at the hour of his greatest need — a curse that helps doom Karṇa at Kurukṣetra. (*Mahābhārata* 8.29, Karṇa Parva; *Śānti* refs)
- **Preceptor of Kalki** — As a *cirañjīvi* surviving into Kali Yuga, Paraśurāma will **initiate and arm [Kalki](./kalki.md)**, the tenth avatāra — uniquely, one avatāra serving as *guru* to another and closing the Daśāvatāra loop. (*Kalki Purāṇa*) — see [The Cirañjīvis](./the-chiranjivis.md).

## VII. Why he matters

- **The warrior-priest paradox** — Paraśurāma embodies *kṣātra* power in a *brāhmaṇa* body: he is the one avatāra whose dharma is **violent correction of the warrior class**, a counter-weight myth to the kingly Rāma and Kṛṣṇa avatāras. (*Mahābhārata*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.16) [scholarly]
- **The avatāra who does not die** — Alone among the Daśāvatāra, his incarnation **never ends in death**; he persists as a *cirañjīvi*, which is why he can witness and tutor figures across three yugas. — see [The Cirañjīvis](./the-chiranjivis.md).

## Sources

- **Mahābhārata, Vana Parva 3.115–117** — the core Jāmadagnya cycle: Kārtavīrya Arjuna and the wish-cow, the murder of Jamadagni, the twenty-one-fold extermination, the blood-lakes of Samanta-pañcaka, and the gift of the earth to Kaśyapa; also **3.116** for the Reṇukā beheading-and-restoration.
- **Mahābhārata** — *Ādi Parva* 1.121–129 (Droṇa receiving his weapons); *Udyoga Parva* 5.176–186 (the Bhīṣma–Paraśurāma duel over Ambā); *Karṇa Parva* 8.29 (Karṇa's curse); *Śānti/Anuśāsana* references to Paraśurāma as guru and *cirañjīvi*.
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.15–16** — the avatāra's birth in the Bhṛgu line, the axe of Śiva, the slaying of Kārtavīrya, the twenty-one purges, the Aśvamedha and earth-gift, and retirement to Mahendra.
- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** — Bhārgava genealogy and the Jāmadagnya–Haihaya conflict.
- **Brahmāṇḍa & Padma Purāṇas** — the Reṇukā material (her lapse, the beheading, the restoration) and the western-coast land-reclamation.
- **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa 1.74–76** — Paraśurāma's confrontation with Rāma Dāśarathi and the stringing of Viṣṇu's bow.
- **Kalki Purāṇa** — Paraśurāma as the deathless preceptor who arms Kalki at the close of Kali Yuga. [late text]
- **Keralolpatti & Konkan/Tuluva creation legends** — the regional *Paraśurāma-kṣetra* charter-myths of Kerala (64 brahmin grāmas) and the Konkan littoral. [regional][folk]
