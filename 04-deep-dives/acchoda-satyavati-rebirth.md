# Acchodā → Satyavatī: the Pitṛ-Born River-Maiden and Her Rebirth

> Where this sits: a Pitṛ-kalpa (ancestor-lore) episode that welds the primordial genealogy of the [Pitṛs](../08-beings-and-bestiary/pitrs-and-ancestors.md) to the **Mahābhārata's founding matriarch** — the mind-born maiden **Acchodā** falls by a single misplaced desire and is decreed rebirth as the fisher-girl **Kālī/[Satyavatī](characters/satyavati.md)**, mother of [Vyāsa](vyasa.md). Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md).
> Cosmic slot, two clocks: the fall belongs to the **primordial Pitṛ-genealogy stratum** (framework/cosmogonic, "before" the ordered king-lists); the decreed rebirth lands, by the texts' own dating, at the end of the **Dvāpara Yuga of the current [28th Mahāyuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md)**. On the academic clock the carrying texts — Matsya Purāṇa 13–14 and Harivaṃśa — are dated roughly c. 200–500 CE for their early cores. [scholarly]

This is the tradition's own **prequel to Satyavatī**. The Mahābhārata explains the fisher-queen's strange birth mechanically (a king's seed, a cursed fish); the Purāṇic Pitṛ-kalpa explains it *morally*: the girl in the fish was already someone — a celestial river-maiden, daughter of the ancestor-hosts themselves, working off one moment of misdirected longing. The story survives in the **śrāddha chapters** of the **Matsya Purāṇa (adhyāya 14)** and the **Harivaṃśa (Harivaṃśa-parvan, Pitṛ-kalpa, ch. ~18)**, with parallel Pitṛ-genealogy matter in the Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa, and Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇas — and it is told there precisely to glorify the ancestors and the new-moon śrāddha.

---

## I. Who Acchodā is — the mind-born daughter of the ancestors

- **Acchodā (अच्छोदा, "of clear water")** — a celestial maiden and river, the **mind-born daughter (mānasī kanyā)** of a class of Pitṛs; simultaneously a **Himalayan river and lake** bearing her name (the lake Acchoda, from which the river flows). (Matsya Purāṇa 14; Harivaṃśa, Pitṛ-kalpa; cf. Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇas)
- **Her fathers, the Agniṣvāttas** — the Purāṇic accounts most often assign her to the **Agniṣvātta** class of Pitṛs — in this corpus's Pitṛ-roster, the ancestors associated with the devas; see [Pitṛs & the Ancestors](../08-beings-and-bestiary/pitrs-and-ancestors.md). (Matsya Purāṇa 14; cf. Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa, Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇas)
  - *Variant (class-assignment):* the sevenfold Pitṛ rosters — names, number, and which daughter belongs to which class — **vary text by text** across the Matsya, Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa, and Harivaṃśa accounts; treat any one class-assignment as text-dependent rather than pan-Purāṇic. [disputed]
- **One of a set of world-seeding daughters** — Acchodā belongs to the same series as the other mind-born daughters of the Pitṛ-classes: **Menā** (wife of Himavat, mother of Gaṅgā and Umā/[Pārvatī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)), **Pīvarī** (in the Harivaṃśa's Pitṛ-kalpa, destined wife of [Śuka](shuka-shukadeva.md), Vyāsa's son — so that *both* Vyāsa's mother and his daughter-in-law descend from Pitṛ-daughters), and **Narmadā** (the sacred river). (Harivaṃśa, Pitṛ-kalpa; Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇas)
- **Why the story is told at all** — both the Matsya and Harivaṃśa versions sit inside **śrāddha instruction** (the Pitṛ-kalpa): the tale is the narrative jewel of a doctrinal block on why the ancestors must be fed and honoured; see [Śrāddha & the Pitṛ Rites](../05-concepts-and-dharma/shraddha-and-pitr-rites.md). (Matsya Purāṇa 13–14; Harivaṃśa, Pitṛ-kalpa)

## II. The tapas and the lapse — desiring Amāvasu

- **A thousand years of austerity** — Acchodā, the maiden of the clear waters, performs prolonged tapas (the accounts give a round figure of a **thousand years**), and her fathers the Pitṛs, gratified, descend in celestial glory to grant her a boon. (Matsya Purāṇa 14)
- **The sight of Amāvasu** — among the resplendent descending Pitṛs is the handsome **Amāvasu**, travelling in the company of the apsarā **Adrikā**. Gazing on him, Acchodā is seized by desire and **chooses him — a "father" — as her lover/husband**. (Matsya Purāṇa 14; Harivaṃśa, Pitṛ-kalpa)
- **The steadfast Pitṛ** — Amāvasu **refuses**; he does not swerve toward the maiden's improper wish. The texts hold up his restraint as the model of ancestral steadfastness. (Matsya Purāṇa 14)
- **The fall** — by that one lapse of intent — desire directed at one of the father-generation — Acchodā **loses her yogic/celestial standing and falls from the sky**, stripped of the power that had upheld her; she who was to move among the Pitṛs finds herself sinking below their world. (Matsya Purāṇa 14; Harivaṃśa, Pitṛ-kalpa)
- **The amāvāsyā aitiology** — because the day of this event belongs to the steadfast **Amāvasu**, the **new-moon day is called amāvāsyā** after him, and śrāddha offered to the ancestors on that day is declared to bear **inexhaustible** merit — the story's ritual payload, and the reason it lives inside the śrāddha chapters. (Matsya Purāṇa 14; Harivaṃśa, Pitṛ-kalpa)
  - *Variant (grammatical etymology):* the ordinary derivation of *amāvāsyā* is simply "dwelling together" (*amā + √vas*) — the night the sun and moon stand together — as in the Vedāṅga/Nirukta tradition; the Amāvasu story is a Purāṇic **folk-etymology retro-fitted** onto the Pitṛ-day. [scholarly]

## III. The decree — weeping maiden, consoling fathers, a scripted rebirth

- **Her plea** — fallen and ashamed, Acchodā **begs the Pitṛs for rescue**; seeing her repentance, the compassionate ancestors console her: the lapse must be paid for by a mortal birth, but the birth itself is turned into a destiny. (Matsya Purāṇa 14; Harivaṃśa, Pitṛ-kalpa)
- **The prophecy of the fish-birth** — the Pitṛs decree that she will be born **in the twenty-eighth Dvāpara Yuga** (the present one) as the daughter of the royal seed of **King Vasu** (Uparicara Vasu of Cedi), carried and delivered **from the body of a fish** — and so, though raised among fisherfolk, she will be by blood a **king's daughter**. (Matsya Purāṇa 14; Harivaṃśa, Pitṛ-kalpa)
  - *Detail (the apsarā-fish):* the fish-mother is the apsarā **Adrikā**, cursed into fish-form in the Yamunā — in the Matsya account the very apsarā seen in Amāvasu's company at the fatal moment, so that the two women of that scene meet again as fish-mother and fish-born daughter; see [Apsaras](../08-beings-and-bestiary/apsaras.md). (Matsya Purāṇa 14; cf. Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~57)
- **Named in advance: Kālī / Satyavatī** — the decree names her: she will be the dark-hued **Kālī**, called **Satyavatī**, the fisher-girl of the ferry. (Matsya Purāṇa 14; Harivaṃśa, Pitṛ-kalpa)
- **Mother of the Veda-divider** — the Pitṛs promise that by the sage **Parāśara** she will bear, while yet a maiden, the island-born **Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana [Vyāsa](vyasa.md)**, who will **divide the one Veda into four** — her redemption is to be the mother of the arranger of scripture. (Matsya Purāṇa 14; Harivaṃśa, Pitṛ-kalpa)
- **Queen of the Kurus** — the decree continues: she will wed King **Śāntanu** and bear him two sons, **Vicitravīrya and Citrāṅgada** — the very succession-line whose collapse will force the niyoga births of Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Pāṇḍu, and Vidura. (Matsya Purāṇa 14; Harivaṃśa, Pitṛ-kalpa)
- **Restoration** — the sentence is finite: having discharged this earthly motherhood, Acchodā will **regain the celestial worlds** and her standing among the daughters of the Pitṛs — the fall fully amortized by dynastic and scriptural service. (Matsya Purāṇa 14; Harivaṃśa, Pitṛ-kalpa)

## IV. The fulfilment — the Mahābhārata's Satyavatī, read back

- **The epic's own account** — the Mahābhārata (Ādi Parva ~57) tells the birth "from below": Uparicara Vasu's seed, the hawk, the Yamunā, the fish Adrikā, the twin infants, the fisher-chief's fostering, the fish-smell (**Matsyagandhā**), Parāśara's boon, the island-birth of Vyāsa — all without any mention of a prior Acchodā. The full arc is in [Satyavatī](characters/satyavati.md) and [Ādi Parva](mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md). (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~57)
  - *Note (silence of the epic):* the **Acchodā frame is Purāṇic, not epic** — the Mahābhārata itself knows nothing of Satyavatī's previous birth; the Matsya/Harivaṃśa material is a later devotional-doctrinal overlay that supplies a karmic "why" for the epic's strange "how." [scholarly]
- **The names agree** — the epic's **Kālī** ("the dark one") for Satyavatī matches the name given in the Pitṛs' decree, one of the hooks by which the two traditions are stitched together. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~57; Matsya Purāṇa 14)
- **What the rebirth explains** — read through the Pitṛ-kalpa, every anomaly of Satyavatī's career becomes design: the virgin motherhood of Vyāsa (a decreed redemption, not a scandal), her royal-blood-in-fisher-rank status (a king's seed by decree), and her role as the hinge whose bride-price triggers [Bhīṣma](characters/bhishma.md)'s vow. (Matsya Purāṇa 14; Harivaṃśa, Pitṛ-kalpa)
- **A Pitṛ-daughter serving the pitṛ-line** — the closing irony the Pitṛ-kalpa trades on: the daughter of the ancestors is sent down to **rescue an ancestral line from extinction** — through her the Kuru pitṛs continue to receive descendants and śrāddha. (Harivaṃśa, Pitṛ-kalpa)

## V. The river, the lake, and the literary afterlife

- **Acchodā the river, Acchoda the lake** — the Purāṇic geography places the **Acchoda lake** in the Himalayan north, with the river **Acchodā** flowing from it; the maiden, the river, and the lake are one being in the genealogies, exactly as Narmadā is at once Pitṛ-daughter and river. (Matsya Purāṇa 14; Vāyu, Brahmāṇḍa, Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇas)
- **The fifth-lake variant** — in the sacred-lake rosters of this corpus, Acchoda appears only as an occasional **regional substitute for the fifth slot** of the [Pañca-Sarovar](../06-sacred-geography/panch-sarovar-sacred-lakes.md) (standardly Pampā). [disputed] [regional]
- **Bāṇa's Kādambarī** — the **Acchoda lake** enjoys a famous literary afterlife as the luminous setting of Mahāśvetā's hermitage in **Bāṇabhaṭṭa's Kādambarī** (7th c. CE) — evidence that the "clear-water" lake remained a live piece of imaginative geography a millennium after the Purāṇic core. [scholarly]

## VI. Disambiguations and the two clocks

- **Amāvasu the Pitṛ ≠ Amāvasu the king** — the steadfast ancestor of this tale must not be confused with **Amāvasu son of Purūravas and Urvaśī**, the Lunar-dynasty king whose branch (Kānyakubja → Gādhi → Viśvāmitra) runs through the genealogies at [Gādhi & the caru-swap](../03-lineages-vamsha/gadhi-and-the-caru-swap.md) and [Jahnu](../03-lineages-vamsha/jahnu.md); the shared name is coincidence of nomenclature, not identity. (Matsya Purāṇa 14 vs. Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.7)
- **Two Satyavatī-origins, never merged** — this corpus keeps the two accounts distinct by design: the **epic-mechanical** origin (Ādi Parva: seed, hawk, fish) and the **Purāṇic-karmic** origin (Matsya/Harivaṃśa: Acchodā's fall and decree). They are complementary layers from different textual strata, not a contradiction to resolve.
- **Scriptural clock** — Acchodā's generation as a mind-born daughter belongs to the primordial Pitṛ-genealogies (framework, pre-dynastic); her fall carries a delayed sentence that detonates at the **close of Dvāpara Yuga** of the current 28th Mahāyuga (war traditionally ≈ 3138/3102 BCE), when Satyavatī's marriages set the Kuru succession crisis in motion — see the [Lunar-dynasty tables](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
- **Academic clock** — the Matsya Purāṇa's early core is commonly dated **c. 200–500 CE** and the Harivaṃśa's **c. 1st–3rd c. CE onward**, both later than the epic Ādi Parva material they retro-frame; the Acchodā episode is thus a Purāṇic-era back-formation that dignifies an already-canonical heroine with a celestial prehistory. [scholarly] (See [Matsya Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/matsya-purana.md) and [Harivaṃśa](harivamsha.md) for full dating discussions.)
