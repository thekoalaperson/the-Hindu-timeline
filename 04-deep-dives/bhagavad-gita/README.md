The **Bhagavad Gītā** ("Song of the Lord") is a ~700-verse philosophical dialogue between the warrior-prince **Arjuna** and his charioteer **Kṛṣṇa**, delivered in the no-man's-land between two armies at Kurukṣetra. Though embedded in the [Mahābhārata](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md), it became the de facto summation of Hindu soteriology and one of the three canonical "starting-points" (*prasthāna-trayī*) of Vedānta. This hub gives its textual placement, frame, recensions, dating, the three-*ṣaṭka* / *tat-tvam-asi* architecture, the cross-school commentary matrix, and links to all **18 per-chapter deep-dive files**.

> Note on the two clocks: the Gītā's *in-narrative* time is the close of Dvāpara Yuga (scriptural); its *composition* is dated by academics to c. 2nd c. BCE – 2nd c. CE (text-critical). Keep these distinct throughout. [scholarly]

---

## Textual placement

- **Location in the Mahābhārata** - Occupies the [Bhīṣma Parva](../mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md) (6th of the 18 *parvas*), chapters 23–40 of that parva (critical-edition continuous numbering ≈ 6.25–6.42). The 18 *adhyāyas* of the Gītā echo the 18 *parvas* of the epic and the 18 days of the war. (Mahābhārata, Bhīṣma Parva)
- **A self-contained Upaniṣad** - Each chapter colophon styles the text a *upaniṣad* ("…iti śrīmad-bhagavadgītāsu upaniṣatsu brahma-vidyāyāṃ yoga-śāstre…") — i.e. it presents itself as Brahma-knowledge (*brahma-vidyā*) and yoga-teaching (*yoga-śāstra*), not mere narrative. (Gītā chapter colophons)
- **Gītā-dhyānam** - A 9-verse invocatory hymn (Madhusūdana Sarasvatī, attrib.) prefixes most printed editions, saluting Vyāsa and figuring the Upaniṣads as cows, Kṛṣṇa as milker, Arjuna as calf. [late text] (Gītā-dhyānam)

## The narrative frame (Sañjaya / Dhṛtarāṣṭra)

- **Outer frame** - The whole battlefield scene is narrated *retrospectively* by **Sañjaya**, charioteer-bard of the blind king **Dhṛtarāṣṭra**, granted divine sight (*divya-cakṣus*) by [Vyāsa](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md) so he can relay the war from afar. (Mahābhārata, Bhīṣma Parva)
- **The single opening question** - The Gītā opens with Dhṛtarāṣṭra's one and only verse (1.1): "On the field of dharma (*dharma-kṣetre kuru-kṣetre*), assembled eager to fight, what did my sons and the Pāṇḍavas do?" — every other verse is downstream of this question. (Gītā 1.1)
- **The closing epilogue** - It closes with Sañjaya's awe-struck words (18.74–78): wherever Kṛṣṇa the lord of yoga and Arjuna the archer are, there will be fortune, victory, and morality. (Gītā 18.74–78)
- **Speakers tally** - Kṛṣṇa speaks the vast majority of the ~700 verses; Arjuna ~85; Sañjaya ~40; Dhṛtarāṣṭra exactly 1. The dialogue is thus a *frame within a frame* (Sañjaya reporting Kṛṣṇa-Arjuna to Dhṛtarāṣṭra, all within Vaiśampāyana's recitation to Janamejaya, all within Ugraśravas' to the Naimiṣa sages).

## Recensions & verse count

- **Vulgate (700 verses)** - The standard accepted text, the basis of Śaṅkara's *bhāṣya* and nearly all popular editions; the round number 700 (*sapta-śatī*) is the historic norm and underlies the title *Gītā = "the 700."* [scholarly]
- **Kashmiri / Abhinavagupta recension (~745 verses)** - The text read by **Abhinavagupta** in his *Gītārtha-saṅgraha* (c. 950–1050 CE) contains roughly **fifteen extra verses** and many partial variant readings versus the Vulgate; later Kashmiri reckonings (*Gītā-māna*) cite **751** verses, so even the longer tradition is internally unsettled. The first critical edition reconstructs the Kashmirian Gītā from one Devanāgarī and seven Śāradā manuscripts. [scholarly] (Abhinavagupta, *Gītārtha-saṅgraha*; W. Slaje / academic critical-edition project)
  - *Variant (Rajanaka Lakṣmaṇa Joo, 20th c.):* a short commentary specifically on the extra Kashmiri verses, holding they encode mystical truths cherished by Kashmir Śaivas. [scholarly]
- **Critical Edition (BORI)** - The Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute's critical Mahābhārata reconstructs a Gītā close to the Vulgate's 700, treating the Kashmiri pluses as regional expansion. [scholarly]
- **Why 700 became canonical** - The number is fixed by the *Gītā-māhātmya* and by Śaṅkara's authoritative commentary; divergent counts survive mainly in the northwest manuscript tradition. (tradition + [scholarly])

## Composition dating (the text-critical clock)

- **Mainstream window** - Most academics place the Gītā's composition between the **2nd century BCE and 2nd century CE** (outer estimates c. 5th c. BCE – 1st c. CE), i.e. centuries after its narrative Dvāpara-yuga setting. [scholarly]
- **Stratification debate** - Some scholars (e.g. the "two-layer" hypotheses) argue the Gītā was inserted into or expanded within the epic's Bhīṣma Parva; others defend its compositional unity. The theistic *bhakti* of chs. 7–12 and the explicit Kṛṣṇa-as-Brahman identification are often read as marking the text's mature theistic layer. [disputed] [scholarly]
- **Scriptural vs. academic time** - Tradition dates the dialogue itself to the eve of Kali Yuga (3102 BCE); the *two-clocks* distinction is essential and is kept separate in every per-chapter file. (tradition vs. [scholarly])

## Architecture: the three ṣaṭkas & the tat-tvam-asi map

- **Three sextets (*ṣaṭkas*)** - The 18 chapters are traditionally grouped into three sixes: **Karma-ṣaṭka** (1–6), **Bhakti-ṣaṭka** (7–12), **Jñāna-ṣaṭka** (13–18). Each sextet foregrounds one of the three *yogas* — action, devotion, knowledge — while presupposing the others. (tradition; Vaiṣṇava commentarial scheme)
- **Mapping to *tat tvam asi*** ("That thou art," the [Chāndogya Upaniṣad](../../90-literature-corpus/shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) *mahāvākya*) - Vaiṣṇava ācāryas (esp. in the Rāmānuja lineage) read the three ṣaṭkas as unpacking the great sentence:
  - *tvam* ("thou") — **chs. 1–6**: the nature of the individual self (*jīva* / *ātman*) and its disciplined action.
  - *tat* ("That") — **chs. 7–12**: the nature of the Supreme (*tat* = Brahman/Bhagavān) and devotion to Him.
  - *asi* ("art") — **chs. 13–18**: the *relation/union* of jīva and Brahman — metaphysics and liberation.
  - *Variant (Advaita reading):* the same three sentences are read as collapsing *tvam* and *tat* into non-difference, *asi* asserting identity rather than dependent relation. [scholarly]
- **Numerical symbolism** - 18 chapters ↔ 18 *parvas* ↔ 18 days of war ↔ the 18 *akṣauhiṇī* armies — a deliberate Mahābhārata-wide "18." (tradition)

## The prasthāna-trayī status

- **One of the three "starting-points"** - With the [Upaniṣads](../../90-literature-corpus/shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md) and the **Brahma-sūtras**, the Gītā is one of the three foundational texts every Vedānta school must comment on to *establish* its system. (tradition)
  - **Upaniṣads** = *Śruti-prasthāna* (revealed/axiomatic).
  - **Bhagavad Gītā** = *Smṛti-prasthāna* / *Sādhana-prasthāna* (remembered tradition; the practical-discipline text).
  - **Brahma-sūtras** = *Nyāya-prasthāna* / *Sūtra-prasthāna* (the logical/formulative text).
- **Why a smṛti ranks with śruti** - Its colophons claim Upaniṣadic status and it is "spoken by the Lord," giving it quasi-revelatory authority despite technically being smṛti — hence its inclusion in the trayī. (tradition + [scholarly])

## The 18 chapters — per-chapter deep-dive files

> Per-chapter files are the exhaustive layer; the one-line gloss here is only a finding-aid. *(Roster of 18 files — created as the deep-dive is built out; flagged open-ended.)*

### Karma-ṣaṭka — chapters 1–6 (*tvam*: the self, action, discipline)

- **[Ch. 1 — Arjuna-viṣāda Yoga](01-arjuna-vishada-yoga.md)** ("Arjuna's Despair") - Survey of armies; the conches; Arjuna's collapse and refusal to fight; the problem posed (*kula-dharma* vs. *svadharma*). (Gītā 1)
- **[Ch. 2 — Sāṅkhya Yoga](02-sankhya-yoga.md)** ("Yoga of Knowledge") - Seed-chapter: the imperishable *ātman* (2.20); *niṣkāma-karma* (2.47); *yoga = samatva* (2.48) and *karmasu kauśalam* (2.50); the *sthitaprajña* (2.54–72). (Gītā 2)
- **[Ch. 3 — Karma Yoga](03-to-06-karma-shatka.md)** ("Yoga of Action") - Action inescapable; action as *yajña*; *loka-saṅgraha*; *svadharma* over *para-dharma* (3.35). (Gītā 3)
- **[Ch. 4 — Jñāna-Karma-Sannyāsa Yoga](03-to-06-karma-shatka.md)** ("Renunciation of Action in Knowledge") - Eternal doctrine first taught to Vivasvān; the *avatāra* verse (4.7–8); action burned in the fire of knowledge. (Gītā 4)
- **[Ch. 5 — Karma-Sannyāsa Yoga](03-to-06-karma-shatka.md)** ("Renunciation of Action") - Renunciation and selfless action reach the same goal; the sage actionless while acting. (Gītā 5)
- **[Ch. 6 — Dhyāna / Ātma-saṃyama Yoga](03-to-06-karma-shatka.md)** ("Yoga of Meditation") - Posture, moderation, steadying the restless mind; the *yoga-bhraṣṭa* reborn favorably. (Gītā 6)

### Bhakti-ṣaṭka — chapters 7–12 (*tat*: the Lord & devotion)

- **[Ch. 7 — Jñāna-Vijñāna Yoga](07-to-12-bhakti-shatka.md)** ("Knowledge & Realization") - Kṛṣṇa's lower (*aparā*) and higher (*parā*) *prakṛti*; four kinds of devotees; *vāsudevaḥ sarvam iti* (7.19). (Gītā 7)
- **[Ch. 8 — Akṣara-Brahma Yoga](07-to-12-bhakti-shatka.md)** ("The Imperishable Absolute") - Last thought at death (8.6); *devayāna* vs. *pitṛyāna*; Brahmā's day/night reckoned in yugas (8.17), harmonizing with the [kalpa cosmology](../../00-time-and-cosmology/brahma-lifetime.md). (Gītā 8)
- **[Ch. 9 — Rāja-Vidyā Rāja-Guhya Yoga](07-to-12-bhakti-shatka.md)** ("Royal Knowledge, Royal Secret") - The Lord pervades all yet is not contained; the lowly-born uplifted by devotion; "a leaf, a flower, a fruit, water" (9.26). (Gītā 9)
- **[Ch. 10 — Vibhūti Yoga](07-to-12-bhakti-shatka.md)** ("Divine Glories") - "Among Ādityas I am Viṣṇu… of rivers the Ganges… of letters I am 'A'" (10.21–42) — God as the essence of the best of each kind. (Gītā 10)
- **[Ch. 11 — Viśvarūpa-Darśana Yoga](07-to-12-bhakti-shatka.md)** ("Vision of the Cosmic Form") - The theophany; the *divya-cakṣus*; "if a thousand suns…" (11.12); *kālo'smi* — "I am Time, destroyer of worlds" (11.32). (Gītā 11)
- **[Ch. 12 — Bhakti Yoga](07-to-12-bhakti-shatka.md)** ("Yoga of Devotion") - Devotion to the personal Lord easier than meditation on the formless; the marks of the beloved *bhakta*. (Gītā 12)

### Jñāna-ṣaṭka — chapters 13–18 (*asi*: metaphysics & liberation)

- **[Ch. 13 — Kṣetra-Kṣetrajña-Vibhāga Yoga](13-to-18-jnana-shatka.md)** ("The Field & Its Knower") - Body as *kṣetra*, conscious self as *kṣetrajña*; Sāṅkhya *prakṛti*/*puruṣa*. (Gītā 13)
- **[Ch. 14 — Guṇatraya-Vibhāga Yoga](13-to-18-jnana-shatka.md)** ("The Three Guṇas") - *Sattva*, *rajas*, *tamas* bind the embodied self; the *guṇātīta*. (Gītā 14)
- **[Ch. 15 — Puruṣottama Yoga](13-to-18-jnana-shatka.md)** ("The Supreme Person") - The inverted cosmic *aśvattha* tree; the three *puruṣas* — perishable, imperishable, and Puruṣottama. (Gītā 15)
- **[Ch. 16 — Daivāsura-Sampad-Vibhāga Yoga](13-to-18-jnana-shatka.md)** ("Divine & Demonic Natures") - *Daivī* vs. *āsurī* traits; the three gates to hell — lust, anger, greed (16.21). (Gītā 16)
- **[Ch. 17 — Śraddhā-traya-Vibhāga Yoga](13-to-18-jnana-shatka.md)** ("Three Kinds of Faith") - Faith, food, sacrifice, austerity, charity classed by guṇa; *oṃ tat sat*. (Gītā 17)
- **[Ch. 18 — Mokṣa-Sannyāsa Yoga](13-to-18-jnana-shatka.md)** ("Liberation through Renunciation") - The longest, synthesizing chapter; *tyāga* vs. *sannyāsa*; the *carama-śloka* "abandon all dharmas and take refuge in Me alone" (18.66); Arjuna's "I will do your word" (18.73). (Gītā 18)

## Cross-school commentary matrix

The Gītā's terse verses support radically different systematic readings; each ācārya read it as confirming his own school. Listed roughly chronologically.

- **Śaṅkara — Advaita (non-dualism)** [c. 8th c. CE] - Earliest surviving complete *bhāṣya*. Liberation comes through **jñāna** (identity of *ātman* and *Brahman*); *karma-yoga* is preparatory (mind-purifying) but not the final means; the world is ultimately *māyā*; 18.66 read as surrender to one's own true Self. (Śaṅkara, *Gītā-bhāṣya*)
  - *Variant (Madhusūdana Sarasvatī, c. 16th c., *Gūḍhārtha-dīpikā*):* later Advaitin who harmonizes *jñāna* and *bhakti* within Advaita. [scholarly]
- **Rāmānuja — Viśiṣṭādvaita (qualified non-dualism)** [c. 11th–12th c.] - Climax is **bhakti** to the personal Lord Nārāyaṇa; selves are real, distinct, dependent *modes* of God; *prapatti/śaraṇāgati* (18.66) is the supreme means; rejects *māyā*-monism. Reads the *ṣaṭkas* via the *tat-tvam-asi* map above. (Rāmānuja, *Gītā-bhāṣya*)
- **Madhva — Dvaita (dualism)** [c. 13th c.] - Absolute, eternal distinction between Viṣṇu, souls, and matter; devotion through a *tāratamya* (hierarchy) of souls; liberation is eternal *service*, never identity. (Madhva, *Gītā-bhāṣya* & *Gītā-tātparya*)
- **Nimbārka school — Dvaitādvaita (dual-non-dual / *bhedābheda*)** [Nimbārka c. 12th–13th c.; the standing Gītā commentary by **Keśava Kāśmīrī Bhaṭṭa**, *Tattva-prakāśikā*, 16th c.] - Souls and the world are both different and non-different from Brahman (Kṛṣṇa/Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa); devotional surrender as the path; *Tattva-prakāśikā* is based on a 700-verse recension. (Keśava Kāśmīrī, *Tattva-prakāśikā*)
- **Vallabha — Śuddhādvaita (pure non-dualism)** [1479–1531] - Commentary *Tattva-dīpikā* (continued by descendants); the world is a real manifestation of Kṛṣṇa (no *māyā*-illusion); grace (*puṣṭi*) and loving devotion to Kṛṣṇa as the goal. (Vallabha, *Tattva-dīpikā* / Śuddhādvaita tradition)
- **Jñāneśvar — *Jñāneśvarī / Bhāvārtha-dīpikā*** [1290 CE] - Oldest surviving literary work in Marathi; an Advaita-flavored verse commentary (9,000+ *ovīs*) foundational to the **Vārkarī** *bhakti* tradition of Maharashtra; emphasizes *akṛtrima bhakti* and selfless action as worship. [regional tradition] (Jñāneśvar, *Jñāneśvarī*)
- **Abhinavagupta — Kashmir Śaiva reading** [c. 10th–11th c., *Gītārtha-saṅgraha*] - Reads the Gītā through nondual Kashmir Śaivism (Pratyabhijñā / recognition); preserves the longer **Kashmiri recension** (see above). Kashmiri Śaiva lineage also includes Rājanaka Rāmakaṇṭha and Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara. [scholarly]
- **B. G. Tilak — *Gītā-Rahasya* (karma-yoga / activist)** [written in Mandalay prison 1910–11, pub. 1915] - The Gītā's *primary* message is **karma-yoga** (energetic, selfless action), not renunciation; reads Kurukṣetra as a *dharma-kṣetra* legitimizing just struggle — a charter for nationalist activism. [modern]
- **M. K. Gandhi — *Anāsaktiyoga* (allegorical ahiṃsā)** [Yeravda jail 1929; Eng. tr. *The Gospel of Selfless Action*, Mahadev Desai 1946] - His "spiritual dictionary"; reads the battle *allegorically* as the inner war between good and evil, extracting **anāsakti** (non-attachment) in support of non-violence — explicitly *not* an endorsement of literal war. [modern]
  - *Variant (Vinoba Bhave, *Talks on the Gita*):* a Sarvodaya/spiritual-activist reading in the Gandhian lineage. [modern]
- **Sri Aurobindo — *Essays on the Gita*** [1916–20] - Synthesizes karma, bhakti, jñāna into an *integral yoga* of self-transcendence and divine action in the world; the Gītā as a manual for spiritualizing life rather than fleeing it. [modern]
- **ISKCON / Gauḍīya — *Bhagavad-gītā As It Is*** [A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda, 1968] - Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Personality of Godhead; goal is pure *bhakti* (Kṛṣṇa-prema) in the *acintya-bhedābheda* lineage of [Caitanya](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md); among the most widely circulated modern editions. [modern]

> Open-ended roster note: dozens of further commentaries exist (Yāmunācārya's *Gītārtha-saṅgraha* precursor, Ānandagiri, Śrīdhara Svāmī's *Subodhinī*, Keśava-Kāśmīrī, Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa's Gauḍīya *Gītā-bhūṣaṇa*, Rādhākṛṣṇan, etc.). Listed names are the load-bearing school-founders plus major modern readings. [flagged: not exhaustive]

## The Viśvarūpa (Ch. 11) — quick orientation

- **The cosmic theophany** - Arjuna, granted a *divya-cakṣus*, beholds the **Viśvarūpa** (Universal Form) — infinite faces, arms, bellies; all gods and beings within; "a thousand suns" of splendor (11.12); Kṛṣṇa as **Time** devouring the warriors (11.32, *kālo'smi loka-kṣaya-kṛt*). Full treatment in **[Ch. 11](07-to-12-bhakti-shatka.md)**. (Gītā 11)
