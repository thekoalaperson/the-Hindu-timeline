# Soma, Tārā & the Tārakāmaya War

> Where this sits: a primordial Chandravamsha (Lunar dynasty) origin-episode — the scandal and war that beget **Budha**, founder of the Lunar line. Parent: [04-deep-dives](./) · Lineage context: [../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) · Cosmic slot: early in the current (7th) Vaivasvata Manvantara, long before the [yugas of the 28th Mahayuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md); Soma himself is born of the sage [Atri](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) at the dawn of creation.

This episode is the **mythic seed of the Lunar dynasty (Chandravamsha / Somavamsha)** and the charter for the planet **Budha (Mercury)**. **Chandra/Soma**, the moon-god, abducts **Tārā**, wife of **Bṛhaspati** (Jupiter, preceptor of the devas); the refusal to return her ignites the **Tārakāmaya War** ("the war on account of Tārā"), counted as the **fifth Devāsura Saṅgrāma**. The devas (with Bṛhaspati, Indra, Rudra/Śiva, Viṣṇu) fight Soma's faction (Śukra and the asuras) to the brink of cosmic ruin until **Brahmā** mediates and restores Tārā — pregnant. Her child, claimed by both husband and lover, is born as **Budha**, and Tārā's confession of his true father seals his lunar paternity. Principal sources: **Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.6**, **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.14**, **Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.11**, with the war's military detail in the **Brahmāṇḍa**, **Padma** (Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa 12), **Matsya**, **Vāyu**, and **Harivaṃśa (1.25)**.

## I. Dramatis personae & the seduction

- **Soma / Chandra** - the moon-god, son of the sage Atri (and Anasūyā) in the Purāṇic genealogies, lord of plants, nectar and the brāhmaṇas, performer of the Rājasūya sacrifice. His pride after that yajña is one stated trigger of the abduction. (Viṣṇu P. 4.6; Bhāgavata 9.14)
- **Tārā** ("star") - chaste wife of Bṛhaspati, here the object of Soma's desire; not to be confused with the vānara-queen Tārā of the [Rāmāyaṇa](ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md) nor the Mahāvidyā goddess Tārā. (Viṣṇu P. 4.6)
- **Bṛhaspati** - Āṅgirasa, son of Aṅgiras, guru and purohita of the devas, Jupiter among the navagraha; the wronged husband. (Viṣṇu P. 4.6)
- **The seizure** - Soma carries Tārā off to his abode. The Purāṇas split on her consent:
  - *Variant (abduction by force):* the **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** and **Tārakāmaya War** tradition present a kidnapping — Soma seizes Tārā and refuses every demand to give her back. (Viṣṇu P. 4.6; cf. Brahmāṇḍa P.)
  - *Variant (mutual passion):* the **Devī-Bhāgavata 1.11** and the folk-retellings frame it as an affair — Soma is smitten, Tārā reciprocates, and the two dwell together willingly while Bṛhaspati's messengers are rebuffed. (Devī-Bhāgavata 1.11) [late text]
  - *Variant (some folk versions):* Soma "seizes her by the hair," yet over the days of captivity Tārā develops feelings for him — a hybrid of the two readings. [folk]

---

## II. Motives — why Soma defied the gods

- **Pride after the Rājasūya** - having completed the great Rājasūya consecration, an arrogant Soma takes Tārā as if entitled. (Viṣṇu P. 4.6; Bhāgavata 9.14)
- **Retaliation against Bṛhaspati** - one tradition makes it karmic payback: Bṛhaspati had earlier violated **Mamatā** (pregnant wife of his brother Utathya), so the seduction of his own wife is poetic justice. [scholarly] (cited in the Tārakāmaya War tradition)
- **Soma's antinomian argument (Devī-Bhāgavata)** - confronted on the sin of "enjoying the guru's wife," Soma answers with sophistry — ritual purification erases fault, and "might is right" — explicitly dismissing Bṛhaspati's dharmic claim. (Devī-Bhāgavata 1.11) [late text]
- **Refusal of all envoys** - Soma rebuffs in turn the Maruts, Sādhyas, Dikpālas, Indra's messengers, and even an initial appeal from **Brahmā** himself. (Devī-Bhāgavata 1.11; Viṣṇu P. 4.6)

## III. The Tārakāmaya War — alliances & battle

- **The fifth Devāsura Saṅgrāma** - the deadlock erupts into open war, enumerated as the **fifth** of the great deva-vs-asura wars. (Tārakāmaya War tradition; Padma P. Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa 12)
- **The devas' side (for Bṛhaspati)** - **Bṛhaspati**, **Indra** and the host of devas, the **Yakṣas**, **Rudra/Śiva** leading the forces, and **Viṣṇu**. Śiva's partisanship is motivated: he had studied under Bṛhaspati's father **Aṅgiras**, making Bṛhaspati a teacher's-house ally. (Brahmāṇḍa P.; Devī-Bhāgavata 1.11)
- **Soma's side (the asuras)** - **Śukrācārya** — guru of the asuras and Bṛhaspati's standing rival — brings the **Daityas** and **Dānavas**, with **Virocana** (son of Prahlāda) among the asura champions. (Brahmāṇḍa P.; Tārakāmaya War tradition)
- **The battle** - both armies loose divine astras; the devas assault the asuras with celestial missiles and are answered in kind. The carnage is so great "it seemed the whole world might be destroyed." (Viṣṇu P. 4.6; Brahmāṇḍa P.)
  - *Variant (doomsday weapons, Devī-Bhāgavata):* the combatants ready planet-shattering astras, including Śiva's **Brahmaśiras**, threatening universal annihilation. (Devī-Bhāgavata 1.11) [late text]
  - *Variant (named kills, Brahmāṇḍa):* **Viṣṇu slays the asura Kālanemi**; **Indra slays Virocana** in the fighting. (Brahmāṇḍa P.)

---

## IV. Brahmā's intervention & Tārā restored

- **Brahmā halts the war** - faced with cosmic ruin, **Brahmā** (the Pitāmaha) intervenes at the world's plea, commanding Soma to surrender Tārā. (Viṣṇu P. 4.6; Bhāgavata 9.14)
  - *Variant (threat of Viṣṇu, Devī-Bhāgavata):* Brahmā brokers the truce by warning that **Viṣṇu** will enter the field and end them all if they persist; both sides stand down. (Devī-Bhāgavata 1.11) [late text]
- **Tārā returned, pregnant** - Soma yields and Tārā is given back to Bṛhaspati — but she is already with child. (Viṣṇu P. 4.6; Bhāgavata 9.14; Devī-Bhāgavata 1.11)

---

## V. The paternity dispute & Tārā's confession

- **Both claim the child** - at (or shortly after) the birth a luminous son appears; **both Soma and Bṛhaspati claim him**, reigniting the quarrel. (Viṣṇu P. 4.6; Bhāgavata 9.14)
- **Brahmā questions Tārā** - the gods refer the matter to Brahmā, who demands the truth from Tārā. (Bhāgavata 9.14)
- **The confession** - ashamed, Tārā lowers her head and admits the child is **Soma's**. (Viṣṇu P. 4.6; Bhāgavata 9.14.13–16; Devī-Bhāgavata 1.11)
  - *Variant (the child's threat, folk/Padma):* Tārā at first stays silent out of shame at having lain with two men; the unborn/newborn child himself threatens to curse her unless she names the father, and only then does she murmur, "He is Soma's." [folk] (Padma P. Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa 12)
  - *Variant (no curse, Devī-Bhāgavata):* this version notes no curse on Tārā or the child and no special role for the Goddess beyond the frame — she simply confesses to Brahmā. (Devī-Bhāgavata 1.11) [late text]
- **Bṛhaspati's anger** - in versions stressing the husband's wrath, Bṛhaspati is enraged at the deception; some traditions append a curse motif, but the mainline Purāṇic texts close with reconciliation. [disputed]

---

## VI. Budha — the founder of the Lunar dynasty

- **Naming of Budha** - **Soma names the boy Budha** ("the wise / awakened," the planet Mercury) and takes him to his own realm; he is reckoned a graha of great intelligence. (Viṣṇu P. 4.6; Bhāgavata 9.14)
- **Budha weds Ilā** - Budha marries **Ilā** (Iḍā), the gender-shifting child of Vaivasvata Manu, born through the Mitra-Varuṇa / Maitrāvaruṇa boon; their union is the dynastic hinge. (Viṣṇu P. 4.1, 4.6; Bhāgavata 9.14)
- **Birth of Purūravas** - from Budha and Ilā is born **Purūravas**, first sovereign of the **Chandravamsha (Lunar dynasty)** — the line later running through Nahuṣa → [Yayāti](yayati.md) → Yadu/Pūru to the Yādavas and Pāṇḍavas. (Viṣṇu P. 4.6–7; Bhāgavata 9.14)
- **Genealogical chain** - **Atri → Soma (Candra) → Budha → Purūravas → Āyu → Nahuṣa → [Yayāti](yayati.md) → (Yadu | Turvaśu | Druhyu | Anu | Pūru)** — see the full tables in [../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).

## VII. Echoes, parallels & significance

- **Why it matters** - the scandal is the **etiology of an entire royal lineage**: every Lunar-dynasty king (the Yādavas of [Kṛṣṇa](krishna-lila.md), the Kuru-Pāṇḍava house of the [Mahābhārata](./mahabharata/)) descends from this adulterous union via Budha. The "irregular" origin is openly owned by the tradition.
- **Astronomical allegory** - the navagraha cast (Candra = Moon, Bṛhaspati = Jupiter, Śukra = Venus, Budha = Mercury, born of Moon and "Star") makes the tale a planetary myth: Mercury's never-far-from-the-Sun/Moon proximity read into Budha's lunar birth. [scholarly]
- **Comparative reading** - some popular writers liken the abduction-and-war pattern to the **Trojan War** (a stolen wife igniting a great war); this is a modern comparative gloss, not a textual link. [folk/scholarly]
- **Recurrence of the rivalry** - the Soma–Bṛhaspati and Śukra–Bṛhaspati rivalries here prefigure the broader guru-of-devas vs guru-of-asuras tension threaded through the [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md) and the larger Devāsura cycle.
