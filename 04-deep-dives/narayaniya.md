# The Nārāyaṇīya — Pāñcarātra Theology of the Śānti Parva

> Where this sits: a self-contained ~1,006-verse theological treatise embedded in the **Mokṣadharma** sub-parva of the [Śānti Parva (Book 12)](./mahabharata/12-shanti-parva.md) of the [Mahābhārata](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md), spoken by the dying [Bhīṣma](./mahabharata/12-shanti-parva.md) to **Yudhiṣṭhira** in the immediate aftermath of the Kurukṣetra war (Dvāpara–[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) junction).

The **Nārāyaṇīya** ("the section concerning Nārāyaṇa") is the single most important monotheistic-Vaiṣṇava document inside the Mahābhārata and the **textual seed of classical Pāñcarātra theology**. Where the [Bhagavad Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md) (Book 6) proclaims Kṛṣṇa's divinity on the battlefield, the Nārāyaṇīya in Book 12 systematizes it: it identifies Kṛṣṇa-Vāsudeva with **Hari-Nārāyaṇa, the supreme Brahman**, lays out the **caturvyūha** (four-emanation) doctrine, narrates Nārada's pilgrimage to the **Śvetadvīpa** ("White Island") of the white *ekāntin* devotees, gives one of the **earliest avatāra lists**, and elevates **bhakti / ekāntika devotion** above ritual (*pravṛtti*) and knowledge alike. Together with the Gītā and the Anugītā it forms the Mahābhārata's "three-fold revelation" of Kṛṣṇa, and it became foundational scripture for Śrī-Vaiṣṇavism and the Pāñcarātra Āgamas. [scholarly]

## Textual placement, extent & the two numbering systems

- **Chapter range — two clocks** - the Nārāyaṇīya occupies a continuous block of ~18–19 adhyāyas near the end of the Mokṣadharma, but the chapter numbers differ by recension.
  - *Variant (Pune Critical Edition):* chapters **321–339** of the Śānti Parva. (Source: Hindupedia paraphrase via wisdomlib; Schreiner, *Nārāyaṇīya-Studien*)
  - *Variant (Northern / vulgate, Kumbakonam & Ganguli):* chapters **334–351** (Hindupedia article gives "334–351"); Sacred-Texts/Ganguli runs the block under its own section numbering ending at Section CCCXL+. (Source: Hindupedia, *Nārāyaṇīya*; sacred-texts.com Ganguli)
  - *Note:* the displacement of ~13 chapters reflects additional Mokṣadharma material in the vulgate before the Nārāyaṇīya begins; both traditions agree it is one of the **last major blocks** of the parva. See the [Śānti Parva chapter-count variants](./mahabharata/12-shanti-parva.md). [scholarly]
- **Verse count** - traditionally **~1,006 ślokas** (the figure most often cited); the vulgate's expanded recension is sometimes counted at **~1,310 verses** across its 18 chapters. Catalog both; the discrepancy is recensional. (Source: wisdomlib *Introduction to the Nārāyaṇīya*; Hindupedia)
- **Frame narrative** - nested dialogues, characteristic of the epic. The outermost is **Vaiśampāyana → King Janamejaya** (the snake-sacrifice frame of the whole Mahābhārata); within that, **Bhīṣma → Yudhiṣṭhira** on the bed of arrows; and at the core, the ancient dialogue of **Nārada and Nārāyaṇa** (and the teaching at Badarī of the twin sages **Nara and Nārāyaṇa**). (Source: wisdomlib *Introduction*; Hindupedia)
- **Dating [scholarly]** - widely regarded as among the **latest doctrinal strata** of the Mahābhārata, post-dating the Gītā's core; commonly placed roughly **c. 1st–3rd century CE** (estimates range wider). It is the bridge text between the epic's emergent Kṛṣṇa-bhakti and the formalized Pāñcarātra/Sātvata schools. (Source: Schreiner et al.; Hindupedia paraphrase)

## The supremacy of Hari-Nārāyaṇa (Para Vāsudeva)

- **Nārāyaṇa as supreme Brahman** - the controlling thesis: Nārāyaṇa is the **"God of gods" (devadeva)** and the **supreme Brahman**, the source, support and indwelling Self (antaryāmin) of all beings, transcending even the heavens — a "double transcendence" (beyond the world *and* beyond the unmanifest). (Source: wisdomlib *Introduction*; Hindupedia ch. 1, 8)
- **Identity of Kṛṣṇa, Vāsudeva and Nārāyaṇa** - the text fuses the [Bhāgavata/Sātvata cult's Kṛṣṇa-Vāsudeva](./krishna-lila.md) with the Vedic-cosmic [Nārāyaṇa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md); the historical Kṛṣṇa of the war is declared the very supreme being. (Source: wisdomlib; Hindupedia)
- **Subordination of Brahmā and Rudra** - a striking sectarian claim: even **Brahmā and Rudra (Śiva)** are identified with — and dependent upon — Nārāyaṇa/Kṛṣṇa, who is their ultimate ground. (Source: Hindupedia ch. 8) See [Trimūrti & Tridevī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).
  - *Variant (synthetic / non-sectarian reading):* a later chapter affirms that **five paths — Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Pāñcarātra, Veda and Pāśupata** — all "lead to the same goal, viz. Nārāyaṇa," softening the polemic into an inclusivist hierarchy. (Source: Hindupedia ch. 16)

## The caturvyūha — the Pāñcarātra four-emanation doctrine

The Nārāyaṇīya contains the **earliest scriptural reference to the caturvyūha** ("four-array") doctrine that defines classical Pāñcarātra. (Source: wisdomlib *Introduction*; Hindupedia ch. 6) See the fuller treatment in [Viṣṇu's Forms & Vyūhas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md).

- **Vāsudeva** - the supreme, beginningless emanation (Para form); the highest *vyūha*, identified with the supreme Self / kṣetrajña. (Source: Hindupedia; ISKCON/Pāñcarātra summaries)
- **Saṅkarṣaṇa** - emanates from Vāsudeva; cosmologically associated with the **jīva** (individual soul) and with Śeṣa/Balarāma. (Source: Hindupedia; Pāñcarātra Āgama overviews)
- **Pradyumna** - emanates from Saṅkarṣaṇa; associated with **manas** (mind) and with the cosmic creative principle. (Source: Hindupedia; Pāñcarātra)
- **Aniruddha** - emanates from Pradyumna; associated with **ahaṅkāra** (ego/I-principle) and the active governance of the manifest world. (Source: Hindupedia; Pāñcarātra)
  - *Note (sequence):* "Above all, without beginning, is Vāsudeva; from Vāsudeva is Saṅkarṣaṇa; from Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna; from Pradyumna, Aniruddha" — the canonical descending emanation order. (Source: Tattvavāda paraphrase of Mokṣadharma)
  - *Variant (later Pāñcarātra elaboration):* the four *vyūhas* are correlated with the six divine attributes (*ṣaḍguṇa* — jñāna, śakti, bala, aiśvarya, vīrya, tejas), distributed in pairs across the emanations, and further expanded into *vyūhāntara* and *vibhava* forms in the Āgamas. [late text] (Source: Pāñcarātra Āgama overviews via Scribd; Schrader, *Introduction to the Pāñcarātra*)
- **The fivefold manifestation (Para–Vyūha–Vibhava–Antaryāmin–Arcā)** - the Nārāyaṇīya's *vyūha* core is later schematized by the Āgamas into the classic five modes of Viṣṇu: **Para** (supreme/transcendent), **Vyūha** (the four emanations), **Vibhava** (avatāras), **Antaryāmin** (the inner controller), and **Arcā** (the consecrated temple image). [late text] (Source: Pāñcarātra Āgama summaries; Śrī-Vaiṣṇava theology)
- **Self-attribution to Nārāyaṇa** - the text declares the Pāñcarātra to be **authored by Nārāyaṇa himself** and "perfectly consonant with the four Vedas, nourished by Sāṃkhya and Yoga" (Mokṣadharma ~12.326.100–101 / vulgate ~349.68), a verse repeatedly cited to defend Pāñcarātra orthodoxy. (Source: Tattvavāda; wisdomlib; Hindupedia ch. 16)

## Nārada's journey to Śvetadvīpa and the *ekāntin* worshippers

- **The pilgrimage** - the sage **Nārada**, after receiving teaching from **Nara-Nārāyaṇa at Badarī**, journeys north past **Mount Meru** to the **Śvetadvīpa ("White Island")**, a transcendent realm of pure devotion, to behold the universal form (*viśvarūpa*) of the Lord. (Source: wisdomlib; DharmaRenaissance; Hindupedia ch. 2)
  - *Variant (two visits):* the Hindupedia chapter-summary records a **second visit** of Nārada (ch. 10), structuring the narrative around a return journey. (Source: Hindupedia)
- **The white *ekāntins*** - the island's inhabitants are devotees **"absolutely white in colour," of lunar complexion**, without sense-organs, taking no food, sense-less yet supremely energetic, their minds **wholly concentrated on the Supreme Being** — the paradigm of the perfected single-minded worshipper. (Source: Ganguli/sacred-texts; wisdomlib; Vedanta Kesari 1938, *Cult of Bhakti in the Mahābhārata*)
- **Ekānta / ekāntika dharma** - the central praxis: **exclusive, one-pointed devotion** to Nārāyaṇa (*ekāntitva*), the "eternal and immutable course" practised by the *yatis* and the denizens of the White Island. Nārāyaṇa imparts it to Nārada, who transmits it onward; it is presented as the highest religion, attainable only by **God's grace** and superior to the path of *jñāna*. (Source: search summaries; Hindupedia ch. 15)
- **Śvetadvīpa cross-readings [scholarly] [disputed]** - some modern scholars (and the contrarian chronology writer N. Oak) have speculatively linked the white devotees to foreign/Zoroastrian or Christian influence; the tradition reads Śvetadvīpa as a purely transcendent symbol of inner purity beyond Meru. Catalog as contested external-influence hypotheses. (Source: Nilesh Oak blog; DharmaRenaissance — flagged as speculative)

## The avatāra list and the *vibhava* descents

- **One of the earliest avatāra rosters** - the Nārāyaṇīya supplies an early list of the Lord's descents, grounding them in Vedic precedent and naming forms not always in the later standard ten. The set characteristically includes **Haṃsa (swan/divine teacher), Hayaśiras / Hayagrīva (horse-headed, recoverer of the Vedas), Varāha (boar), Narasiṃha (man-lion), Vāmana (dwarf)** alongside the epic Kṛṣṇa. (Source: wisdomlib *Introduction*; Hindupedia)
  - *Variant (count & members):* wisdomlib calls it "the earliest list of the ten incarnations" (a proto-[Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)), while other summaries count **six principal manifestations**; the membership (notably the prominence of **Haṃsa** and **Hayaśiras**, rare in the later canonical ten) differs from the classical Purāṇic list. Catalog both counts. [scholarly] (Source: wisdomlib; Hindupedia)
  - *Note:* the relation of these *vibhava* avatāras to the *vyūha* emanations is theologically distinct — the *vyūhas* are eternal divine "arrays," the avatāras are descents into the world for dharma; the Pāñcarātra fivefold schema keeps them as separate modes (Vibhava vs Vyūha). See [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).

## Nivṛtti-dharma — the path of withdrawal

- **Pravṛtti vs nivṛtti** - the Nārāyaṇīya frames two great religious orientations: **pravṛtti-dharma** (the path of ritual *action*, worldly engagement, sacrifice and prosperity, associated with Brahmā's outward creation) and **nivṛtti-dharma** (the path of *withdrawal*, renunciation, and liberating devotion that returns the soul to Nārāyaṇa). (Source: Hindupedia ch. 7; Mokṣadharma context)
- **Nivṛtti as supreme** - consistent with the Mokṣadharma's liberation-focus, the text exalts **nivṛtti** — exclusive devotion and renunciation of fruits — over mere *pravṛtti* ritualism, while affirming that ritual duty has its proper subordinate place. (Source: Hindupedia; wisdomlib)
- **Sātvata-dharma** - the devotional religion is named the **Sātvata-dharma** (the religion of the Sātvatas/Yādavas), said to be revealed through both the **Upaniṣads and the Pāñcarātra Āgamas** — the self-identification that ties the epic passage to the historical Bhāgavata/Pāñcarātra community. (Source: Hindupedia ch. 15)
- **Bhakti above jñāna and karma** - the soteriological capstone: **bhakti / ekāntika devotion is the supreme means**, surpassing both knowledge (*jñāna*) and works (*karma*), and reachable only through grace. (Source: wisdomlib; Hindupedia ch. 15)

## Foundational role for later Vaiṣṇavism

- **Charter of the Pāñcarātra Āgamas** - the Nārāyaṇīya's self-description as Nārāyaṇa-spoken, Veda-consonant scripture became the **smārta/itihāsa anchor legitimizing the Pāñcarātra Āgama corpus** (Sāttvata, Pauṣkara, Jayākhya Saṃhitās et al.) against orthodox critics who deemed the Āgamas non-Vedic. [late text] (Source: Schrader, *Introduction to the Pāñcarātra*; Pāñcarātra overviews)
- **Śrī-Vaiṣṇava theology** - the **Para–Vyūha–Vibhava–Antaryāmin–Arcā** schema rooted here is structural to **Rāmānuja's Viśiṣṭādvaita** and the Śrī-Vaiṣṇava temple cult (Arcā-worship). See [Rāmānuja & Madhva](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/ramanuja-and-madhva.md) and the [Four Vaiṣṇava Sampradāyas](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/four-vaishnava-sampradayas.md).
- **Gauḍīya & Dvaita reception** - the **caturvyūha** doctrine is taken up across later schools — Madhva's Dvaita and the Gauḍīya tradition both inherit Vāsudeva-Saṅkarṣaṇa-Pradyumna-Aniruddha as transcendental plenary expansions (with the Bhāgavata's own restatement at SB 11.5.29–30 and the cult of the [Sātvata-tantra forms](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md)). (Source: Vaniquotes; gitabase SB 11.5.29–30)
- **Place in the epic's revelation** - read canonically as the **third panel** of the Mahābhārata's Kṛṣṇa-theology: the [Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md) (pre-war, bhakti + self-revelation), the [Anugītā](./anugita.md) (post-war, jñāna/Sāṃkhya), and the Nārāyaṇīya (post-war, systematic Vaiṣṇava monotheism + Pāñcarātra). (Source: scholarly consensus; wisdomlib)

## Open rosters & cautions

- **Chapter/verse numbers are recension-dependent** - cite both the Critical-Edition (321–339) and vulgate (334–351) ranges; the ~1,006 vs ~1,310 verse counts likewise diverge. [scholarly]
- **The avatāra list is not yet the fixed Daśāvatāra** - membership, count (six vs ten), and ordering vary from the later canonical list; do not retroject the standardized ten. [scholarly]
- **External-influence theories on Śvetadvīpa are speculative** - flag Christian/Zoroastrian-origin claims as contested, not established. [disputed]
