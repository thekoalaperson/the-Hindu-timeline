# The Mokṣadharma Dialogues — Sāṃkhya, Yoga & the Teaching-Conversations of the Śānti Parva

> Where this sits: a dedicated companion to the **Mokṣadharma-parvan** — the vast liberation-doctrine block (≈ Mbh XII.168–353) that closes the **[Śānti Parva](./mahabharata/12-shanti-parva.md)** (Book 12) of the [Mahābhārata](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md). Spoken by the dying **Bhīṣma** from his bed of arrows to **Yudhiṣṭhira** in the closing [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga, on the eve of [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).
>
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Master index: [../README.md](../README.md) · Companions: [Śānti Parva](./mahabharata/12-shanti-parva.md) · [The Anugītā](./anugita.md) · [Bhagavad Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md) · [Yoga Sūtras of Patañjali](./yoga-sutras-of-patanjali.md)

The **Mokṣadharma** ("the dharma of liberation") is the largest single philosophical reservoir in the epic — a loosely-stitched anthology of *saṃvādas* (teaching-conversations), *gīṭās*, *itihāsas* and *upākhyānas* that Bhīṣma recites as the answer to Yudhiṣṭhira's question: *what is the highest good, and how is release (mokṣa) won?* It is the principal pre-classical source for **epic Sāṃkhya-Yoga** — recording the doctrine *before* Īśvarakṛṣṇa's [Sāṃkhya-Kārikā](./yoga-sutras-of-patanjali.md) fixed the "classical" 25-tattva form — and it preserves, side by side and unreconciled, a **theistic 26-principle Sāṃkhya** and an **atheistic 24/25-principle Sāṃkhya**. Chapter numbers below follow the **Kumbhakonam / Ganguli (Calcutta)** vulgate; the **Critical Edition (BORI)** renumbers and excises many sections. [scholarly]

## Frame & placement

- **Speaker, listener, occasion** - Bhīṣma, mortally wounded but awaiting the *uttarāyaṇa* (northern solstice) to die at will, instructs Yudhiṣṭhira on *rājadharma*, *āpaddharma*, and finally *mokṣadharma*. The Mokṣadharma is the third and largest of the three sub-blocks. (Mbh XII; Bhīṣma's discourse)
- **Span** - Conventionally **chs. 168/174–353** of the Ganguli vulgate (the boundary with Āpaddharma is editorial); the Critical Edition's Mokṣadharma is shorter. (Mbh XII)
  - *Variant (boundary):* sources place the start at **ch. 168** (Medhāvin teaches his father) or at **ch. 174** (the formal *mokṣadharma* opening); the start is not crisp. [scholarly]
- **Genre** - Not a single argument but a *florilegium*: most teaching is delivered as **reported ancient dialogues** (*purātana itihāsa*) — a story Bhīṣma recalls, inside which a sage instructs a king, inside which an even older authority is quoted. This nesting is a structural signature shared with the [Anugītā](./anugita.md). (Mbh XII, Mokṣadharma)
- **Two clocks** - The events are *narrated* as Dvāpara-Yuga teaching; the **composition** of the Mokṣadharma is dated by scholars to ~200 BCE–400 CE, the latest doctrinal stratum being among the youngest in the epic. [scholarly]

## The great saṃvādas (teaching-conversations)

- **Sulabhā–Janaka** - The yoginī **Sulabhā**, a wandering mendicant of the **Pradhāna** lineage, hears that King **Janaka** of Mithilā claims to be *liberated-while-ruling* (*jīvanmukta* on the throne). Testing him, she enters his body/mind by yogic power (*yoga-bala*) and debates him from within. Janaka rebukes her — as a woman, a mendicant who mingled with a king, who entered him uninvited — and she replies with the parva's most radical statement: **the self (ātman) is not gendered, not embodied, not a king or a woman; "who is joined to whom?"** Embodiment, varṇa, and sex are accidents of prakṛti, irrelevant to the puruṣa. She exposes that his very rebuke proves he is still **identified with body, gender, office, and kingdom** — hence not free. (Mbh XII.308 [Ganguli]; the *Sulabhā-Janaka-saṃvāda*) [foundational on gender & liberation]
  - *Variant (chapter):* numbered **321** in some summaries, **308** in Ganguli — recension-dependent. [scholarly]
  - *Note:* Sulabhā's speech is also a treatise on **language & meaning** — she analyses the components of valid speech (clarity, non-redundancy, etc.) to show Janaka's accusation is itself defective discourse. (Mbh XII.308) [scholarly]
- **Yājñavalkya–Janaka** - The sage **[Yājñavalkya](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md)** expounds to Janaka a developed **Sāṃkhya enumeration of the tattvas**: the **eight prakṛtis** (avyakta/pradhāna, mahat/buddhi, ahaṃkāra, and the five subtle elements) and the **sixteen modifications** (*vikāras* — five organs of action, five of sense, manas, and the five gross elements), totalling **24**; with **puruṣa** as the **25th**, and — crucially — a **26th** principle: the supreme, all-knowing *kṣetrajña*/Īśvara that the liberated soul realizes as distinct from yet the ground of the 25. This is the parva's clearest articulation of the **24/25/26 ladder**. (Mbh XII.310–311 [Ganguli]; the *Yājñavalkya-Janaka-saṃvāda*)
  - *Variant (count):* the dialogue oscillates — some passages stop at **24 tattvas** (collapsing avyakta and puruṣa), some at **25** (classical Sāṃkhya), some ascend to **26** (theistic). All three are recorded without harmonization. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Pañcaśikha–Janaka** - **Pañcaśikha**, pupil of **Āsuri** (himself pupil of **[Kapila](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md)**, founder of Sāṃkhya), instructs Janaka in the *Pañcaśikha-vākya* — an austere, arguably **atheistic** Sāṃkhya: the three guṇas, buddhi and mahat, the perishability of the body, and freedom as discriminative knowledge — notably **without dwelling on a personal Puruṣa/Īśvara**, and teaching that at death the elements simply disperse. A key witness to the **proto-classical** Sāṃkhya lineage Kapila → Āsuri → Pañcaśikha. (Mbh XII.211–212, 218–219 [Ganguli]; the *Pañcaśikha-saṃvāda*) [scholarly]
- **Vyāsa–Śuka** - **[Vyāsa](../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md)** teaches his renunciate son **Śuka** the highest good: the duties above all duties, the path to Brahman, and the futility of householder attachments. Śuka, perfected, ascends and **merges into the elements / the all-pervading Brahman** — the parva's archetype of the born-liberated soul. A central renunciation-cycle, cross-referenced with the broader Śuka legend. (Mbh XII.224–250 [Ganguli]; the *Śuka-anupraśna*)
  - *Variant (folk/Bhāgavata):* Purāṇic tradition makes Śuka the reciter of the [Bhāgavata Purāṇa](./bhagavata-tales-catalog.md) to Parīkṣit; the Mokṣadharma knows only the liberation-narrative. [folk]
- **Bhṛgu–Bharadvāja** - **Bharadvāja** questions **Bhṛgu** on **cosmogony and varṇa**. Bhṛgu teaches a primeval Being (**Mānasa** — "the Mind-born"), beginningless, unmanifest, immortal, from whom space, wind, fire, water, earth and all creatures arise. Then the famous exchange on caste: Bhṛgu offers a **color-coded varṇa** scheme (Brāhmaṇa white, Kṣatriya red, Vaiśya yellow, Śūdra black); **Bharadvāja objects** that all colors appear in all classes, and that desire, anger, fear, grief, hunger and toil are common to every human — so **what really distinguishes a Brāhmaṇa?** The resolution: **varṇa is by conduct, not birth** — a Brāhmaṇa who lapses into base conduct becomes a Śūdra, and one of any class who lives by righteous *karma/ācāra* rises to Brāhmaṇa-hood. (Mbh XII.182–192 [Ganguli]; the *Bhṛgu-Bharadvāja-saṃvāda*) [foundational on varṇa-by-conduct]
- **Tulādhāra–Jājali** - The penance-proud Brahmin **Jājali**, standing motionless in the forest so long that **birds nested in his matted hair** (he would not move, lest it be *hiṃsā* to the chicks) and raised their young, proclaims aloud that none equals him in merit. A voice answers: *"You are not equal, O Jājali, to **Tulādhāra** in righteousness."* He seeks out Tulādhāra, a **merchant (vaiśya) of Vārāṇasī**, who teaches that the highest dharma is **universal harmlessness (ahiṃsā)** and honest, non-injurious livelihood — that the **butcher's knife and the sacrificial post both shed blood**, and that ritual and austerity are worthless beside friendliness to all beings. The merchant outranks the ascetic. (Mbh XII.253–256 / 261–264 [Ganguli]; the *Tulādhāra-Jājali-saṃvāda*) [foundational on ahiṃsā over ritual]
  - *Cross-link:* pairs thematically with the **Vyādha-gītā** (the righteous butcher of Mithilā teaching the proud Brahmin Kauśika), also reprised in this parva. (Mbh XII.261–264)
- **Manu–Bṛhaspati** - **Bṛhaspati**, preceptor of the gods, questions **Manu** on self-knowledge. Manu teaches the relation of the **field (kṣetra, the body) and the knower of the field (kṣetrajña)**, the supremacy of *jñāna* over works, the unreality of the perceived world for the liberated, and the ascent through the tattvas to the supreme Self — a Sāṃkhya-Vedānta synthesis placed in the mouth of the first lawgiver. (Mbh XII.194–199 [Ganguli]; the *Manu-Bṛhaspati-saṃvāda*)
- **Pitā–Putra saṃvāda (Medhāvin & his father)** - A precocious son, **Medhāvin** (a Brahmin's boy "versed in liberation and worldly order"), urges his complacent father to **renounce now** — for *death does not wait*: *"Each day a man lives, that much of his life is already spent by Death; renounce while you still can."* The son reverses the expected hierarchy (the young teaching the old, *nivṛtti* over *pravṛtti*) — one of the parva's most quoted calls to urgent renunciation. (Mbh XII.169/175 [Ganguli]; the *Pitā-Putra-saṃvāda*)
- **The worm and the sage (Kīṭa-upākhyāna)** - A sage tells a lowly **worm/insect** crossing a road, terrified of an oncoming cart, that even its humble life is precious and that it can rise: through the long ladder of rebirths and accumulated merit, even a worm may climb the chain of being toward higher births and ultimately liberation. A parable of **karmic continuity and the dignity of all life** — and an *ahiṃsā* teaching: no being's fear of death is negligible. (Mbh XII, Mokṣadharma; the *Kīṭa-saṃvāda*) [flag: chapter number varies by recension; rosters of the parva's minor upākhyānas are open-ended]

## The Sāṃkhya enumeration — 24 / 25 / 26

- **The 24 tattvas (the "field," prakṛti and its products)** - **8 prakṛtis** (avyakta/pradhāna; mahat/buddhi; ahaṃkāra; + 5 *tanmātras*) and **16 vikāras** (5 *jñānendriyas* + 5 *karmendriyas* + manas + 5 *mahābhūtas*). This is the world-side, the *kṣetra*. (Mbh XII.310–311; Yājñavalkya-Janaka)
- **The 25th — Puruṣa** - The conscious witness, *kṣetrajña*, distinct from all 24 prakṛtic principles; classical (Īśvarakṛṣṇa) Sāṃkhya stops here, plural puruṣas confronting one prakṛti. (Mbh XII; classical comparandum [Sāṃkhya-Kārikā](./yoga-sutras-of-patanjali.md))
- **The 26th — the supreme Self / Īśvara** - The epic's **theistic** move: above the individual puruṣa stands a **26th**, the all-knowing, all-pervading Lord; liberation is the puruṣa's realization of its identity-in-distinction with this supreme. (Mbh XII.310–311, 308; Yājñavalkya-Janaka, Sulabhā contexts)
- **The standing tension** - The parva never resolves whether Sāṃkhya is **atheistic (25)** or **theistic (26)**. Pañcaśikha leans atheistic; Yājñavalkya supplies the 26th; some passages collapse to 24. The redactors **catalogued rather than harmonized** — a primary reason the Mokṣadharma is invaluable for tracing Sāṃkhya's evolution. (Mbh XII.211–212 vs 310–311) [disputed] [scholarly]
- **Sāṃkhya and Yoga as complementary** - Repeatedly the parva states the two reach the same goal: **Sāṃkhya by discriminative knowledge (jñāna)**, **Yoga by disciplined practice (abhyāsa)** — "the wise see Sāṃkhya and Yoga as one." (Mbh XII.293, 304, 316–317) [cross-link: [Yoga Sūtras](./yoga-sutras-of-patanjali.md), [Bhagavad Gītā 5.4–5](./bhagavad-gita.md)]

## The body as the nine-gated city (navadvāra-pura)

- **The allegory** - The body is a **city of nine gates** (*navadvāra-pura*) in which the embodied self (*dehin*/puruṣa) dwells as sovereign; the senses are its servants, the buddhi its minister, manas the city-officer. Liberation is the indweller's detachment from the city it merely inhabits. (Mbh XII, Mokṣadharma; epic Sāṃkhya)
- **The nine gates** - Two eyes, two ears, two nostrils, the mouth, and the two lower apertures (anus, genitals) — the nine apertures through which the unliberated self engages the world. (epic Sāṃkhya; cf. [Bhagavad Gītā 5.13](./bhagavad-gita.md))
- **Scriptural lineage** - The figure is older than the epic: **Atharva Veda 10.2.31** ("the eight-wheeled, nine-gated citadel of the gods"), **Kaṭha** and **Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣads**, then **Gītā 5.13**; the Mokṣadharma develops it into a full liberation-allegory. (Atharva Veda; [Upaniṣads](../90-literature-corpus/shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md)) [cross-link: [Nachiketā / Kaṭha Upaniṣad](./nachiketa-katha-upanishad.md)]
  - *Variant (elaboration):* the [Bhāgavata Purāṇa](./bhagavata-tales-catalog.md) expands the same metaphor into the full **Purañjana** allegory (the king of the nine-gated city); the Mokṣadharma version is compact. [late text]

## Why the Mokṣadharma matters

- **Pre-classical Sāṃkhya archive** - It freezes Sāṃkhya mid-evolution — the teacher-lineage Kapila → Āsuri → Pañcaśikha, the unsettled 24/25/26 count, and the not-yet-divorced Sāṃkhya-Yoga — making it the single richest source for the system before its [classical codification](./yoga-sutras-of-patanjali.md). [scholarly]
- **Twin counter-orthodox ethics** - Two of its dialogues anchor reformist strands in the tradition: **varṇa-by-conduct** (Bhṛgu-Bharadvāja) and **ahiṃsā over ritual** (Tulādhāra-Jājali), echoed across later [dharma](../05-concepts-and-dharma/README.md) discourse.
- **Gender and the genderless self** - The **Sulabhā-Janaka** debate is the tradition's strongest single statement that **mokṣa is open to all regardless of sex or station**, since the ātman bears no such marks.
- **Sibling texts** - Read alongside its post-war twin the **[Anugītā](./anugita.md)** (which internalizes the fire-sacrifice into the body) and the battlefield **[Bhagavad Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md)**; together the three form the Mahābhārata's philosophical core.
