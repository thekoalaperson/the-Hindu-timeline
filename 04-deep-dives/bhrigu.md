# Bhṛgu and the Bhṛgu-Test of the Trimūrti

> *Where this sits: a deep-dive into **Bhṛgu** (Bhṛgu Ṛṣi) — mind-born Prajāpati, apical patriarch of the **Bhārgava** fire-priest clan, namesake of the **Bhṛgu Saṃhitā**, and the sage who kicked Viṣṇu's chest to adjudicate Trimūrti supremacy. ⬆ Parent index: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Master index: [README](../README.md)*

**Bhṛgu** (भृगु, from √*bhrāj* "to blaze/shine" — the "burning one," a fire-seer like his rival **[Aṅgiras](angiras.md)**) is one of the primordial mind-born Prajāpatis and a frequent member of the **Saptarṣi**. He fathers a line that dominates both the asura priesthood and the warrior-brahmin tradition: his son **Śukra/Uśanas** becomes guru of the asuras (see [Kaca & Devayānī](characters/kacha-devayani.md)), his son **[Cyavana](cyavana-sukanya.md)** roots the rejuvenation lore, and his great-grandson-line through Ṛcīka → Jamadagni produces **Paraśurāma** ([Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)). He is best remembered for the audacious **Bhṛgu-test** — kicking Viṣṇu in the chest to decide which of the Trimūrti is supreme — the act that gives Viṣṇu the **Śrīvatsa** mark and triggers **Lakṣmī's** estrangement. He is a **[creation]**-period figure recurring across manvantaras (the "two clocks": his *name* names a recurring office, distinct from the historical Bhārgava hymn-poets downstream of him).

---

## Origin and identity

- **Mānasaputra of Brahmā** — Bhṛgu is emitted from Brahmā at the dawn of creation as one of the **mind-born sons** (*mānasaputras*) and Prajāpatis, ranked among the primordial ṛṣis and listed in the canonical Prajāpati roster. (*Manu Smṛti* 1.35; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 1.7)
  - *Variant (born from fire / Brahmā's skin):* several Purāṇas derive him from the sacrificial **fire** or from Brahmā's **skin** (*tvac*) at the Varuṇa-sacrifice, a play on the "blazing" etymology. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.1) [folk]
- **Alternate Saptarṣi** — Bhṛgu is counted among the Seven Sages in several manvantara-lists (replacing one of the astronomical seven), heading the gotra-roots alongside Aṅgiras; membership shifts by Purāṇa and world-age. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 3.1) — see [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md). [disputed]
- **Married to Khyāti and Pulomā** — He weds **Khyāti** (a daughter of Dakṣa), who bears **Lakṣmī's foster-line / Śrī** and the twins **Dhātṛ & Vidhātṛ**; and **Pulomā**, who bears **Cyavana**. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 1.10; *Mahābhārata*, Ādi Parva 5–6)

## The Bhārgava line

- **Patriarch of the Bhārgavas** — Bhṛgu heads the **Bhārgava** clan, mythologically the **purohitas of the Daityas/Dānavas**, masters of revival-magic and (in tradition) the **Sāmaveda** — a brahmin line that shades into kṣatriya martial fury. (*Mahābhārata*, Ādi Parva; *Anuśāsana Parva*)
- **Father of Śukra (Uśanas Kāvya)** — Bhṛgu's son **Śukra**, the **Daitya-guru** and planet **Venus**, holds the **Mṛtasañjīvanī vidyā** to revive the slain — the secret at the heart of the [Kaca & Devayānī](characters/kacha-devayani.md) cycle. (*Mahābhārata*, Ādi Parva)
- **Father of Cyavana** — by Pulomā; the sage rejuvenated by the **Aśvins** (origin of *cyavanaprāśa*) and husband of **Sukanyā** — the full cycle at [Cyavana, Sukanyā & the Aśvins](cyavana-sukanya.md). (*Mahābhārata*, Vana Parva; *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* 4.1.5)
- **Ancestor of Paraśurāma** — through Cyavana → **Ṛcīka** → **Jamadagni**, the line produces **Paraśurāma**, the axe-wielding 6th avatāra who exterminates the kṣatriyas twenty-one times. (*Mahābhārata*, Vana/Śānti Parva; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.15–16) — see [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Aurva and the submarine fire** — the Bhārgava **Aurva** (Cyavana's grandson-line) births the world-burning **Vāḍava/Aurva-agni**, restrained into the ocean-mare's-mouth — the line's recurring motif of priestly wrath turned to fire. (*Mahābhārata*, Ādi Parva)

## The Bhṛgu-test of the Trimūrti

The central episode: at a great sacrifice on the **Sarasvatī** banks, the assembled sages dispute **which of the Trimūrti — Brahmā, Viṣṇu or Śiva — is supreme** and most fit to receive the chief sacrificial offerings. Bhṛgu is deputed to test all three.

- **The dispute and Bhṛgu's commission** — The ṛṣis at the Sarasvatī sacrifice cannot agree which deity is *sāttvika* and worthiest of the *yajña-bhāga* (sacrificial share); they send **Bhṛgu** to provoke each god and judge his response. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.89.1–10; *Padma Purāṇa*)
- **Brahmā fails the test** — Bhṛgu enters Brahmā's court and pointedly **withholds obeisance**. Brahmā flares with anger at his own son but, recognizing him, suppresses it — the *rajas*-stained god is disqualified. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.89; *Padma Purāṇa*)
- **Śiva fails the test** — Going to Kailāsa, Bhṛgu **refuses Śiva's embrace** and rebukes him as impure (*piśāca*-natured). The enraged Śiva seizes his trident to kill Bhṛgu and is restrained only by **Pārvatī's** intercession — the *tamas*-stained god is disqualified. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.89; *Padma Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (curse exchanged):* in some Purāṇic tellings Bhṛgu and Śiva exchange curses here — Bhṛgu cursing that Śiva be worshipped only in the aniconic *liṅga* form. [late text] [disputed]
- **The kick to Viṣṇu's chest** — Bhṛgu finds **Viṣṇu** asleep in Vaikuṇṭha with his head on Lakṣmī's lap and, to provoke the supreme test, **kicks the god squarely on the chest** with his foot. Far from anger, Viṣṇu rises, **takes Bhṛgu's foot and gently massages it**, anxious that the sage's tender foot was hurt by his hard chest — the *sāttvika* response that wins him the rishis' verdict. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.89.11–14; *Padma Purāṇa*)
- **Origin of the Śrīvatsa mark** — The print of Bhṛgu's foot remains on Viṣṇu's chest as the auspicious whorl-mark **Śrīvatsa** ("beloved of Śrī"), thereafter a defining iconographic emblem of Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.89; *Padma Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (the foot's curse-protection):* some recensions add that Bhṛgu, abashed at his own act, is consoled — his foot bears a "third eye" Brahmā destroys, or the foot-mark itself becomes a blessing. [folk]
- **The verdict** — Reporting back, Bhṛgu declares **Viṣṇu supreme** in *sattva* and self-control; the ṛṣis award the chief sacrificial offerings to Viṣṇu. The tale is a **Vaiṣṇava charter-myth** for Viṣṇu's primacy among the Trimūrti. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.89) [scholarly]

## Lakṣmī's pique and the regional sequels

- **Lakṣmī affronted** — In the widely-told southern recension, **Lakṣmī** — seated on Viṣṇu's chest as **Śrī** — is enraged that her own seat was **struck by a foot** and that Viṣṇu *tolerated* the insult; affronted, she **leaves Vaikuṇṭha** and descends to earth. (*Veṅkaṭācala-Māhātmya*; *Karavīra-Māhātmya*) [regional]
- **The Tirumala sequel** — Lakṣmī's departure sends Viṣṇu down as **Śrīnivāsa / Veṅkaṭeśvara** to **Tirumala (Tirupati)** in search of her — the founding frame of that *kṣetra*. (*Veṅkaṭācala-Māhātmya*) [regional]
- **The Kolhapur sequel** — In the paired southern reading, Lakṣmī settles at **Karavīra (Kolhapur)** as **Mahālakṣmī** while Viṣṇu settles at Tirupati, so the two shrines are read as the **separated divine couple**; pilgrims to Tirupati are enjoined to also visit Kolhapur. (*Karavīra-Māhātmya*, Padma Purāṇa) [regional] [folk]

## The Bhṛgu Saṃhitā and other authorship

- **Bhṛgu Saṃhitā (astrology)** — Bhṛgu is the reputed compiler of the **Bhṛgu Saṃhitā**, a foundational text of **predictive (jyotiṣa) astrology** cataloguing horoscope-templates for all possible birth-charts — the basis of the South-Indian "Nāḍī" prognostic tradition that claims pre-recorded individual fortunes. (tradition; *Bhṛgu Saṃhitā*) [late text] [disputed]
- **Contributor to the Manu Smṛti** — In the **Manu Smṛti**'s frame, the sages approach **Manu**, who delegates the exposition of the law to **Bhṛgu**; the surviving text is presented as *Bhṛgu's recitation* of Manu's dharma. (*Manu Smṛti* 1.58–60)
- **Vedic seer** — Bhṛgu and the **Bhārgavas** are credited as bringers of **fire** to humankind and as a principal hymn-poet family, paired with the Aṅgirasas as the two great fire-priest lines. (*Ṛgveda*; *Atharvaveda*) [scholarly]

## Manvantara recurrence — the "two clocks"

- **A recurring office** — On the **scriptural clock**, Bhṛgu re-manifests across world-ages as a standing Prajāpati / Saptarṣi, his name naming an office that recurs each cycle. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 3.1–2) [scholarly]
- **Distinct from the later Bhārgavas** — On the **historical / text-transmission clock**, "Bhārgava" labels a vast *family* of later seers and gotra-founders (Jamadagni, Vītahavya, Gṛtsamada, Vadhryaśva, Vainya) downstream of the eponymous Bhṛgu; the corpus rarely separates the primordial ancestor from his namesake descendants. [scholarly]
