# Triśaṅku — Heaven in Limbo

> **Where this sits:** A deep-dive into the Ikṣvāku / [Sūryavaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) king **Satyavrata "Triśaṅku"**, who wished to ascend bodily and alive to Indra's heaven — rejected by his purohita **Vasiṣṭha**, aided by **[Viśvāmitra](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md)**, who in retaliation built a parallel southern heaven (*Triśaṅku-svarga*) and left the king hanging head-downward among new-made stars. The episode is the charter-myth of being stuck between two worlds.
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Cosmic address: Śveta-Varāha Kalpa › 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara › 28th Mahāyuga, **[Tretā Yuga](../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** (Triśaṅku is great-grandfather-deep in the Ikṣvāku line above Rāma; his son is Hariścandra).

The Triśaṅku story is told in two strata that do not fully agree: the **[Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa](ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md)** (sargas 57–60), which makes him a *reformed* righteous king destroyed by hubris and saved-yet-suspended by Viśvāmitra; and the **Harivaṃśa** (and Purāṇic strands), which dwell on his early-life *sins* and his charity to Viśvāmitra's starving family during a twelve-year famine. It is also the episode that costs Viśvāmitra accumulated tapas in his own [ascent to brahmarṣi](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md). Catalog all variants below.

---

## Dramatis personae & the name

- **Satyavrata / Triśaṅku** - Ikṣvāku king of the Sūryavaṃśa, son of **Trayyāruṇa** (Traiyyāruṇa), father of **[Hariścandra](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md)** (the truth-tested king). Original name **Satyavrata** ("true to his vow"); renamed **Triśaṅku** by Vasiṣṭha as a curse-epithet. ([Wikipedia: Trishanku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishanku))
- **Vasiṣṭha** - one of the Saptarṣi, hereditary purohita of the Ikṣvākus, who *refuses* Triśaṅku's wish as adharma and is the source of the curse-name. (See [Viśvāmitra & Vasiṣṭha](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md).)
- **Viśvāmitra (Kauśika)** - the kṣatriya-turned-sage rival of Vasiṣṭha; champions Triśaṅku out of rivalry, pity, and (in the Harivaṃśa) gratitude. The episode is one of the temptations-to-wrath that resets his own tapas.
- **The name "Triśaṅku"** - Sanskrit *tri* ("three") + *śaṅku* ("stake / stump / peg"), i.e. "three-staked" or "three-stumped." Two readings are preserved:
  - *Etymological-moral variant (Harivaṃśa / Purāṇic):* the three "stakes" = his **three great sins** (see §1), for which Vasiṣṭha applied the name in anger. ([Wikipedia: Trishanku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishanku))
  - *Astronomical variant (scholarly):* "three stakes/stumps" denotes the star-pattern of the **Southern Cross (Crux)** — three (or four) bright stars near Alpha/Beta Centauri — which the myth etiologizes as Triśaṅku fixed head-down in the southern sky. [scholarly]

## 1. The three sins (Harivaṃśa / Purāṇic prelude)

The pre-Rāmāyaṇa backstory explaining the curse-name. Satyavrata as a wild young prince commits three transgressions, for which Vasiṣṭha brands him *Triśaṅku*:

- **Sin one — angering his father** - As a reckless prince Satyavrata provokes the fury of his father **Trayyāruṇa**, who banishes him from Ayodhyā. (Harivaṃśa; Purāṇic) ([Wikipedia: Trishanku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishanku))
- **Sin two — abducting a bride** - He carries off another man's bride (a brahmin's bride in some tellings) on her wedding day; this seals his exile. (Harivaṃśa; Purāṇic) ([Wikipedia: Trishanku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishanku))
- **Sin three — killing & eating Vasiṣṭha's cow** - In the wilderness, starving, he slays **Vasiṣṭha's sacred cow (Kāmadhenu / Nandinī)**, eats of her, and feeds the flesh to others — an unforgivable offence to his guru. For these three Vasiṣṭha curses him and names him **Triśaṅku** ("the thrice-sinning / three-staked one"). (Harivaṃśa; Purāṇic) ([Wikipedia: Trishanku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishanku))
  - *Variant on the third sin:* the cow he kills is feeding-fodder turned charity — see §2; the Harivaṃśa frames the cow-slaughter not as wanton but as feeding Viśvāmitra's famished family. The two strands (sin vs. charity) are layered onto the same act.

---

## 2. The twelve-year famine & charity to Viśvāmitra's family (Harivaṃśa)

A distinctively Harivaṃśa episode that recasts the exiled, "fallen" Satyavrata sympathetically:

- **The drought** - A terrible **twelve-year famine** grips the land; no rain falls. Meanwhile sage **Viśvāmitra is away** in distant tapas (on the shore of the great ocean), leaving his wife and sons destitute. (Harivaṃśa) ([Glorious India: 12-Year Famine in Puranic Literature](http://shimlasuresh.blogspot.com/2012/09/12-years-famine-in-puranic-literature.html))
- **The selling of the son** - Viśvāmitra's wife, unable to feed her children, prepares to **sell one of her sons** (named **Gālava** in the tradition, also "Madhyama" / the middle son) for a hundred cattle to survive. (Harivaṃśa)
- **Satyavrata's hunting-charity** - The exiled Satyavrata sustains the family throughout: he hunts and brings **deer, boar, cow, and buffalo flesh**, hangs the meat at the hermitage, and **liberates the boy Gālava** from being sold. He even kills Vasiṣṭha's cow for this purpose. (Harivaṃśa) ([Glorious India](http://shimlasuresh.blogspot.com/2012/09/12-years-famine-in-puranic-literature.html))
- **Viśvāmitra's gratitude** - On returning, Viśvāmitra is moved by Satyavrata's aid to his family and **rewards him** — installing him on his father's throne and later championing his heaven-quest. This gratitude (not mere rivalry) motivates Viśvāmitra's later defiance of the gods on Triśaṅku's behalf. (Harivaṃśa) ([Glorious India](http://shimlasuresh.blogspot.com/2012/09/12-years-famine-in-puranic-literature.html))
  - *Tone variant:* where the [Rāmāyaṇa](ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md) makes Viśvāmitra act from rivalry-and-pity toward a reformed king, the **Harivaṃśa** makes it a debt of gratitude repaid — the same alliance, different motive. Both are preserved here, not harmonized.

## 3. The impossible wish & Vasiṣṭha's refusal (Rāmāyaṇa Bāla Kāṇḍa)

The core episode, narrated by Viśvāmitra himself within the Bāla Kāṇḍa frame:

- **The bodily-ascent wish** - King Triśaṅku, now righteous, conceives the desire to **ascend to Indra's heaven (svarga) in his own living mortal body** — without dying — and asks his purohita **Vasiṣṭha** to perform the enabling sacrifice. ([Rāmāyaṇa Bāla Kāṇḍa](ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md), sargas 57–60) ([Wikipedia: Trishanku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishanku))
- **Vasiṣṭha refuses** - Vasiṣṭha declares the wish **impossible and against dharma** — the body cannot enter heaven; he will not do it. (Bāla Kāṇḍa 57)
- **The sons' curse → cāṇḍāla** - Rebuffed, Triśaṅku turns to **Vasiṣṭha's hundred sons** to perform the rite; insulted that he spurned their father, they **curse him to become a cāṇḍāla** (outcaste). His royal form is instantly disfigured — black skin, coarse iron ornaments, rags — and his court abandons him. (Bāla Kāṇḍa 58–59) ([Wikipedia: Trishanku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishanku))

---

## 4. Viśvāmitra takes the case (Rāmāyaṇa Bāla Kāṇḍa 59–60)

- **The plea to Viśvāmitra** - The cāṇḍāla-king Triśaṅku, who once aided Viśvāmitra (in Harivaṃśa logic), now appeals to him; Viśvāmitra, out of **rivalry with Vasiṣṭha, pity, and gratitude**, agrees to perform the great sacrifice to send Triśaṅku bodily to heaven. (Bāla Kāṇḍa 59)
- **The boycotting sages cursed** - Viśvāmitra summons all ṛṣis; **Vasiṣṭha's sons and the sage Mahodaya refuse to attend** a rite for a cursed cāṇḍāla. Enraged, Viśvāmitra **curses Vasiṣṭha's hundred sons to be reduced to ash / reborn as cāṇḍālas (Niṣādas)** for seven hundred births, and curses Mahodaya likewise. (Bāla Kāṇḍa 59–60) — the wrath that drains his tapas.
- **The lone yajña** - The remaining sages, fearing Viśvāmitra's power, perform the sacrifice. Viśvāmitra invokes the gods to come accept their portions and receive Triśaṅku; **the devas do not come.** (Bāla Kāṇḍa 60)

---

## 5. The ascent, Indra's kick, and the fall (Rāmāyaṇa Bāla Kāṇḍa 60)

- **Raised by tapas** - When the gods spurn the rite, Viśvāmitra channels his **own accumulated tapas**, commanding Triśaṅku to rise; by sheer ascetic power the king **ascends bodily into the sky**, reaching the gates of svarga. (Bāla Kāṇḍa 60) ([Wikipedia: Trishanku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishanku))
- **Indra's rejection** - **Indra and the devas kick him back down**, declaring a cursed cāṇḍāla unfit for heaven; Triśaṅku plummets **head-downward**, screaming "Save me!" to Viśvāmitra as he falls. (Bāla Kāṇḍa 60) ([Wikipedia: Trishanku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishanku))
- **Arrested mid-fall** - Viśvāmitra **halts Triśaṅku's plunge in mid-air** by his power and freezes him there, suspended between earth and heaven. (Bāla Kāṇḍa 60) ([Vedadhara: The Tale of Trishanku](https://www.vedadhara.com/suspended-in-the-skiesthe-tale-of-trishanku))

---

## 6. The counter-heaven (Triśaṅku-svarga) and the upside-down king

- **Viśvāmitra builds a parallel cosmos** - Furious at Indra, Viśvāmitra resolves to **create a second, southern heaven** for Triśaṅku. He fashions, in the southern sky, **new constellations and stars, new oceans/rivers/mountains, new devas, and a new (second) Indra** to rule them — threatening to remake the entire cosmic order. (Bāla Kāṇḍa 60) ([Wikipedia: Trishanku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishanku))
  - *Variant on the compromise:* in one telling, **Indra himself proposes** creating a new svarga below his own just for Triśaṅku to forestall Viśvāmitra's rival creation; Viśvāmitra answers by installing a **new Indra and devas** to occupy it. The end-state (a southern parallel heaven) is the same. [variant]
- **The gods relent** - Alarmed at a second creation and a second Indra, **Brahmā / the devas intervene** and beg Viśvāmitra to stop. A compromise is struck: the **new southern stars and constellations Viśvāmitra has made will remain**, and **Triśaṅku will hang there permanently, fixed head-downward**, shining like an immortal among Viśvāmitra's stars — neither truly in svarga nor on earth. (Bāla Kāṇḍa 60) ([Wikipedia: Trishanku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishanku))
- **The cost to Viśvāmitra** - The whole rage-fueled episode **burns up the tapas Viśvāmitra had accumulated**, resetting his long climb to brahmarṣi — see the [Viśvāmitra–Vasiṣṭha cycle §3–4](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md). [cross-link]

## 7. Astronomical & idiomatic afterlife

- **Triśaṅku = the Southern Cross / southern stars** - The "three stakes" (tri-śaṅku) and the head-downward fixing are widely read as an origin-myth for the **Southern Cross (Crux)** and the bright stars near **Alpha/Beta Centauri**, visible low in southern India's sky. ([Wikipedia: Trishanku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishanku)) [scholarly]
  - *Variant identifications:* some folk/astronomical traditions tie Triśaṅku instead to **Alpha Centauri** specifically, or generically to the "new southern constellations" Viśvāmitra is said to have created. [folk/scholarly]
- **"Triśaṅku-svarga" as idiom** - "Triśaṅku's heaven" / *triśaṅku-svarga* enters Sanskrit and modern Indian languages as a proverb for being **suspended between two states** — neither here nor there, in limbo, unable to advance or retreat. [popular]

## 8. A note on the Taittirīya Upaniṣad Triśaṅku [disputed identity]

- **The "Triśaṅku" of the Śikṣā-vallī** - The **Taittirīya Upaniṣad** (Śikṣā-vallī 1.10) contains a famous self-realization utterance ascribed to a seer **Triśaṅku** ("I am the mover of the tree... my glory is like the peak of a mountain..."). (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.10)
  - *Disputed:* tradition sometimes identifies this Vedic seer-Triśaṅku with the Ikṣvāku king, but most scholars treat them as **distinct figures** sharing a name; the Upaniṣadic Triśaṅku is a realized ṛṣi, not a suspended king. [disputed] ([Wikipedia: Trishanku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishanku))
