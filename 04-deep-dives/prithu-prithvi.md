# Pṛthu — the First King & the Milking of the Earth

> Cosmic address: Shveta-Varaha Kalpa → 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara → early in the present human age, within the [primordial Svāyambhuva-Manu / Dhruva line (early dynastic prehistory)](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md). Counted as the **Pṛthu-avatāra** in the Bhāgavata's 22-avatara list ([dashavatara & avatara-lists](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)).
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./) · Up to [README](../README.md)

Pṛthu (Pṛthu Vainya, "Pṛthu son of Vena") is the archetypal **rajan** — the first consecrated king, the inventor of kingship, agriculture, settlement and trade. The Earth is **Pṛthvī**, "she who belongs to Pṛthu," because he subdued and "milked" her into yielding food. He is an aṃśa (partial descent) of Viṣṇu born expressly to rescue the cosmic order from the misrule of his father Vena.

---

## The lineage: from Dhruva to Vena

- **Descent line** - Svāyambhuva Manu → Uttānapāda → **Dhruva** (the pole-star devotee) → ... → **Aṅga**, a righteous king (Bhāgavata 4.13; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13). Pṛthu thus stands very early in human dynastic prehistory.
- **Aṅga's marriage to Sunīthā** - Aṅga weds **Sunīthā** (Sunītha), daughter of **Mṛtyu** (Death) / in some texts of Kāla-Mṛtyu, embedding a flaw of death in the line (Bhāgavata 4.13).
  - *Variant (Padma Purāṇa):* the dynasty is traced from the sage **Atri**, Aṅga descending through Atri's line.
- **Birth of Vena** - Their son Vena inherits the wickedness of his maternal grandfather Mṛtyu; Aṅga, despairing of his evil son, abandons the throne and disappears into the forest (Bhāgavata 4.13).

## Vena's misrule and execution by the sages

- **Vena crowned** - The kingless realm installs Vena. He forbids all Vedic sacrifice and worship, proclaiming himself the sole object of worship — "I alone am the enjoyer of yajña, I am Hari" (Bhāgavata 4.14; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13).
- **Collapse of dharma** - Under his decree ritual, charity and study cease; rains fail and famine and lawlessness spread (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13).
- **Sages' appeal** - The ṛṣis approach Vena, plead for the restoration of dharma, and warn him; he refuses contemptuously (Bhāgavata 4.14).
- **Death of Vena** - The enraged brāhmaṇas slay the tyrant — with **mantra-killing** (humkāra/blades of kuśa-grass charged by mantra) (Bhāgavata 4.14; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13).
  - *Variant:* some retellings have them strike him down physically after the curse takes hold.

## Churning the corpse: birth of the Niṣāda, then of Pṛthu

The dead body is "churned" (mathana) by the sages — the same ritual-magical technique by which a new being can be drawn out of a corpse.

- **The thigh churned → Niṣāda** - From Vena's **thigh** is rubbed forth a dark, dwarfish hunter, repository of all of Vena's accumulated sin. He is **Niṣāda**, ancestor of the Niṣāda peoples (forest hunters/fishers, the Naiṣāda) (Bhāgavata 4.14; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13). His emergence purges the corpse of evil, leaving it fit to yield a virtuous successor.
- **The arms churned → Pṛthu** - From Vena's **right arm** (some say both arms) is then drawn forth a radiant being: **Pṛthu**, blazing like fire (Bhāgavata 4.15; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata 4.15):* the churning of the arms produces a **couple** — Pṛthu and his queen **Arci** (Arcis), regarded as a partial descent of Lakṣmī, born simultaneously.
- **Marks of Viṣṇu** - Pṛthu bears the **cakra (discus) mark** on his right palm (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13) and, per the Bhāgavata, a **lotus mark** on the soles of his feet; by these Brahmā recognizes him as an aṃśa-avatāra of Viṣṇu (Bhāgavata 4.15). Hence Pṛthu's place in the Bhāgavata's 22-avatara enumeration.

## The first coronation — origin of kingship

- **Consecration as rājan** - The sages and devas crown Pṛthu the first formally **anointed king**; the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa (5.3.5.4) names him "the first consecrated king" — a Vedic anchor older than the Purāṇic elaboration ([scholarly] on dating).
- **Birth of the sūta and māgadha** - At the inaugural sacrifice the bardic castes appear to praise him: the **Sūta** (charioteer-bard/genealogist) and the **Māgadha** (panegyrist). Lacking deeds yet to praise, they are told instead to glorify the king's *future* greatness — the etiology of court eulogy (Bhāgavata 4.15; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13).
- **Royal gifts of the gods** - The devas endow him with divine emblems: the celestial bow **Ājagava**, arrows, armor; Viṣṇu gives the discus, Indra a crown, the seas and mountains bring tribute (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13). He is hailed the first **cakravartin** (Vāyu Purāṇa).

## Subduing and milking the Earth — origin of agriculture

- **The barren, fugitive Earth** - The subjects, starving, beg for food. Pṛthu finds the Earth has withdrawn all herbs and seed into herself (in some versions, swallowed by her, hoarded in famine). He takes up his bow against her (Bhāgavata 4.17; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13).
- **Earth flees as a cow** - To escape, the Earth assumes the form of a **cow (gau)** and flees through the three worlds; Pṛthu pursues her relentlessly, arrow nocked (Bhāgavata 4.17–18).
- **Earth's plea and pact** - Cornered, the Earth (**Pṛthvī/Vasundharā**) pleads that killing her destroys the very ground of all creatures, and offers a covenant: if Pṛthu will **level her surface** and provide a **calf, a milker and a vessel**, she will yield all that they desire as milk (Bhāgavata 4.18; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13).
- **Leveling the Earth** - Pṛthu shatters and flattens the mountains with his bow, clearing the broken, chaotic terrain into plains fit for **ploughing, pasture, villages, towns and trade-roads** — none of which had existed before his reign (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13; the Atharvaveda credits Pṛthu Vainya with first **ploughing/agriculture**).
- **The milking** - Using **Svāyambhuva Manu** as the **calf**, and his own cupped hands (or a vessel), Pṛthu **milks the Earth-cow** of all grains, vegetables and herbs for the welfare of mankind (Bhāgavata 4.18; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13).
- **Earth named Pṛthvī** - As her conqueror, life-giver and protector, Pṛthu becomes the Earth's "father"; she takes his patronymic and is forever called **Pṛthvī** ("Pṛthu's [daughter]") (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13; Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva, names her Pṛthvī on this account).
  - *Variant (Manu Smṛti):* Pṛthvī is reckoned Pṛthu's **wife** rather than his daughter.

### Catalogue of the manifold milkings (the "all beings milk the Earth" motif)

After Pṛthu, every class of being milks the Earth-cow in turn, each with its own calf, milker and vessel, drawing out the boon proper to its nature (Matsya Purāṇa, with variant lists in Bhāgavata 4.18, Padma & Brahma Purāṇas):

- **Ṛṣis (sages)** - milk **tapas / the Vedas (devotion, sacred learning)**, calf Bṛhaspati (or Soma), Veda(s) as the vessel (Bhāgavata 4.18; Matsya Purāṇa).
- **Devas (gods)** - milk **ojas / energy and amṛta-vigor**, calf **Indra**, into a **golden** vessel (Matsya Purāṇa).
- **Pitṛs (ancestors)** - milk **svadhā** (the funerary oblation), calf **Yama** (Antaka), into a **silver** vessel (Matsya Purāṇa).
- **Nāgas (serpents)** - milk **poison (viṣa)**, calf Takṣaka (Dhṛtarāṣṭra in some lists), into a **gourd/clay** vessel (Matsya Purāṇa).
- **Asuras / Daityas** - milk **māyā (illusory powers)**, calf Prahlāda (or Virocana), into an **iron** vessel (Matsya Purāṇa).
- **Yakṣas** - milk **the power to vanish / wealth**, calf Vaiśravaṇa (Kubera), into an **unbaked-clay** vessel (Matsya Purāṇa).
- **Gandharvas & Apsaras** - milk **fragrances and the beauteous arts** (Matsya Purāṇa).
- **Mountains** - milk **herbs, gems and minerals**, calf **Himavān/Meru** (Matsya Purāṇa).
- **Trees / vanaspati** - milk the power **to sprout again when felled** (Matsya Purāṇa).
- *Note [scholarly]:* the exact calf, milker, vessel and boon differ from text to text; the lists above follow the Matsya and Bhāgavata recensions and are not identical across Purāṇas.

## The aśvamedhas and Indra's theft of the horse

- **The hundred horse-sacrifices** - Pṛthu undertakes a series of **aśvamedha (horse-sacrifice)** rites at the Sarasvatī; success in a hundred would unseat Indra (Bhāgavata 4.19).
- **Indra steals the horse** - To prevent it, **Indra** repeatedly steals the sacrificial horse in disguise (as a heretic ascetic), founding by his stratagem various **pāṣaṇḍa / heterodox garbs** (Bhāgavata 4.19).
- **Confrontation and reconciliation** - Pṛthu's son (born of the sacrifice) recovers the horse; Pṛthu is about to slay Indra when **Brahmā intervenes**, halting the hundredth rite. **Viṣṇu appears**, blesses Pṛthu, and Pṛthu **forgives Indra** (Bhāgavata 4.19–20).

## Teaching, reign, and final departure

- **The Four Kumāras' instruction** - The eternal boy-sages **Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanātana, Sanatkumāra** appear and teach Pṛthu **bhakti** and self-knowledge — among the Bhāgavata's notable discourses on devotion (Bhāgavata 4.22).
- **Pṛthu's address to his subjects** - Pṛthu delivers a celebrated speech on the **duties of a king** — protection of dharma, of the brāhmaṇas, and of the people as the king's sacred charge (Bhāgavata 4.20–21).
- **Sons and succession** - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13):* two sons, **Antardhāna (Antardhi)** and **Pālin**, the line continuing through Antardhāna to the **Pracetas**. *Variant (Bhāgavata 4.22):* five sons — **Vijitāśva** (heir, the center), **Haryakṣa** (east), **Dhūmrakeśa** (south), **Vṛka** (west), **Draviṇa** (north).
- **Renunciation and death** - In old age Pṛthu retires with Arci to the forest, performs austerities, enters **samādhi** and voluntarily yields up his body; **Arci** follows him onto the pyre (an early sati narrative) (Bhāgavata 4.23).

## Significance & cross-links

- **Origin of kingship** - The paradigm of the consecrated, dharma-protecting monarch; later kings are styled *like Pṛthu*. See [devas, asuras & the lokas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) for the Indra rivalry, and [lineages/vamsha](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
- **Origin of agriculture & civilization** - Leveling the land, ploughing, milk and grain replacing the chaotic, foodless earth.
- **As avatāra** - One of the 22 līlā-avatāras of the Bhāgavata; see [dashavatara & avatara-lists](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- *[scholarly]* O'Flaherty reads the myth as marking the shift from beef-eating to **dairy + cultivated grain**; Shulman likens Pṛthu's hunter→herdsman duality to the Rudra-Śiva ideal-king archetype.
