# Muchukunda, Kalayavana and the Founding of Dwaraka

> Where this sits: a deep-dive into the **Mathurā-to-Dwārakā exodus** of [Kṛṣṇa](krishna-lila.md) — the pivot at which Jarāsandha's sieges and the foreign king **Kālayavana** drive the Yādavas west, and the long-sleeping Ikṣvāku king **Mucukunda** burns Kālayavana to ash. A hinge between the [Sūryavaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) (Mucukunda's Solar line) and the Candravaṃśa Krishna-cycle. Parent: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Cosmic slot: 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara › 28th Mahāyuga › [Dvāpara-yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md).

This episode (**Bhāgavata Purāṇa X.50–52**; **Viṣṇu Purāṇa V.23**; **Harivaṃśa**, Viṣṇu Parva) braids two clocks: the **Dvāpara** career of Kṛṣṇa, and the far-deeper **Solar-dynasty** past of **Mucukunda**, a son of the Ikṣvāku emperor **Māndhātṛ** who fought a *deva-asura* war ages earlier and slept across whole yugas. Their collision founds the sea-city **Dwārakā** and gives Kṛṣṇa his most-debated epithet, **Raṇacchoḍa** — "the one who quit the battlefield."

---

## I. Jarāsandha's seventeen sieges of Mathurā (Bhāgavata X.50; Harivaṃśa)

- **Why Jarāsandha attacks** — **Jarāsandha**, king of Magadha, was father-in-law of the slain tyrant **Kaṃsa** (whose queens **Asti** and **Prāpti** were his daughters); enraged at Kṛṣṇa's killing of Kaṃsa (see [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md)), he vows to exterminate the Yādavas (*Bhāgavata X.50.1–8; Viṣṇu Purāṇa V.22*).
- **The seventeen invasions** — Jarāsandha besieges **Mathurā** with vast akṣauhiṇī armies **seventeen times**; each time Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma rout his host, yet deliberately let Jarāsandha himself escape so he will return and keep draining the world of asuric kṣatriyas (*Bhāgavata X.50.9–41; Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva*). [scripture]
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa / Viṣṇu Purāṇa):* the count of sieges and the body-count emphasis vary; the Harivaṃśa dwells more on the destruction of Mathurā and the burning of the city than the Bhāgavata's tidy "seventeen."
- **The eighteenth threat: Kālayavana enters** — Knowing he cannot break Mathurā alone, the **Yavana** king **Kālayavana** marches on Mathurā with a colossal *mleccha* (barbarian) host — three crore Yavanas in the epic hyperbole (*Bhāgavata X.50.42–43; Viṣṇu Purāṇa V.23*).

## II. Who was Kālayavana? (Bhāgavata X.50–51; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa V.23)

- **A brahmin-fathered barbarian king** — Kālayavana ("Black Yavana / Yavana of Death") is, in the Purāṇic genealogy, born of the sage **Gārgya** (Garga), raised among the kingless **Yavanas**; by a boon he is invincible — none in open combat, not even Kṛṣṇa or Balarāma, can slay him, which is why Kṛṣṇa resorts to stratagem (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa V.23; Harivaṃśa; Bhāgavata X.51*).
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa / Harivaṃśa):* Gārgya, mocked as impotent by the Yādavas, performs austerities to **Śiva** for a son who will terrorize the clan; Kālayavana is that vengeance-son, reared by the childless Yavana ruler. The Bhāgavata abbreviates this. [textual]

## III. The strategic exodus and the building of Dwārakā (Bhāgavata X.50.49–57; Viṣṇu Purāṇa V.23)

- **The decision to abandon Mathurā** — Pressed between Jarāsandha and Kālayavana, Kṛṣṇa judges that holding Mathurā would cost too many Yādava lives; he resolves to move the entire clan to an unassailable refuge in the western sea (*Bhāgavata X.50.49–50*). [scripture]
- **Vishvakarma builds the sea-fortress** — Kṛṣṇa has the divine architect **Viśvakarman** (see [Viśvakarmā & Tvaṣṭṛ](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishvakarma-and-tvashtar.md)) raise the island-city **Dwārakā** ("the many-gated"), twelve yojanas wide, in a tract of land reclaimed from **Samudra** (the ocean-god) at Kṛṣṇa's request (*Bhāgavata X.50.50–53; Viṣṇu Purāṇa V.23; Harivaṃśa*).
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa):* the city is named **Dvāravatī**; the ocean yields twelve yojanas of land, and Viśvakarman lays it out with gold ramparts, gardens, and reservoirs.
- **The miraculous transfer of the citizens** — By Kṛṣṇa's yogic power the sleeping people of Mathurā are conveyed to the new city, so they wake within Dwārakā's walls (*Bhāgavata X.50.56–57*). [scripture]
- **Raṇacchoḍa — "the deserter of battle"** — Kṛṣṇa then walks **out of Mathurā unarmed and alone** before the advancing Kālayavana, openly fleeing on foot — earning the epithet **Raṇacchoḍa / Raṇchoḍ** ("he who quit the field"). Far from cowardice, it is the bait of a trap; the **Raṇchoḍrāī** temple at Ḍākor (Gujarat) enshrines this name (*Bhāgavata X.51.1–6; Viṣṇu Purāṇa V.23*). [scripture; the Ḍākor cult is regional]

## IV. The cave of Mucukunda — luring Kālayavana (Bhāgavata X.51)

- **The foot-chase** — Kālayavana, recognizing Kṛṣṇa by his beauty and śrīvatsa mark, pursues the fleeing Kṛṣṇa on foot, always a step behind, drawn ever onward but never able to seize or strike him (*Bhāgavata X.51.6–9*).
- **Into the mountain cave** — Kṛṣṇa leads him into a dark cavern in a mountain, where a man lies asleep; Kṛṣṇa slips aside, leaving Kālayavana to find the sleeper (*Bhāgavata X.51.10–12*).
- **The fatal mistake** — Mistaking the sleeping figure for Kṛṣṇa hiding, the arrogant Kālayavana **kicks** (or, in some tellings, kicks awake) the sleeper to rouse and mock him (*Bhāgavata X.51.12–13*).
- **The incinerating glance** — The wakened sleeper — the ancient king **Mucukunda** — opens his eyes; by an old boon, **his first glance upon waking burns to ashes** whatever it falls on, and Kālayavana is instantly reduced to a heap of cinders (*Bhāgavata X.51.13–14; Viṣṇu Purāṇa V.23; Harivaṃśa*). [scripture]

## V. Mucukunda's own arc — the long-sleeping Ikṣvāku king (Bhāgavata X.51.14–63; Harivaṃśa)

- **A Solar-dynasty prince** — **Mucukunda** is a son of the great Ikṣvāku emperor **Māndhātṛ** (see [Māndhātṛ](mandhata.md)), of the [Sūryavaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) — thus he belongs to a far earlier age than Kṛṣṇa's Dvāpara, a deliberate clash of two cosmic clocks (*Bhāgavata X.51.14–18*).
- **Service to the devas** — In a long *deva-asura* war the gods, hard-pressed and without a general, begged Mucukunda to lead their armies; he guarded the devas for a vast stretch of time, before **Kārttikeya (Skanda)** was born to take command (*Bhāgavata X.51.15–17; Harivaṃśa*).
- **The boon of sleep** — When Skanda became the devas' general and Mucukunda was released, he found his kingdom, kin, and whole era long vanished on earth (where ages had passed). Declining other rewards, he asked **Indra** only for **undisturbed sleep**, with the boon that **anyone who woke him would be burnt to ashes** by his glance (*Bhāgavata X.51.18–22*). [scripture]
  - *Variant (folk):* some retellings make the burning-glance an unintended side-effect of accumulated tapas rather than a granted boon. [folk]
- **Awakening in the Dvāpara** — He slept in the mountain cave across yugas until Kṛṣṇa, in the Dvāpara, used him as the instrument to destroy the unkillable Kālayavana — so the boon served a cosmic purpose (*Bhāgavata X.51.22–23*).
- **Recognition of Kṛṣṇa** — Mucukunda beholds the dark, radiant Kṛṣṇa, asks who he is; Kṛṣṇa reveals his identity and the æons that have passed, and Mucukunda offers a celebrated hymn of surrender (the "Mucukunda-stuti," X.51.46–57) (*Bhāgavata X.51.24–57*).
- **Departure to penance** — Kṛṣṇa grants him liberation in a future birth and bids him do tapas; Mucukunda, lamenting the *kali*-tainted world he wakes into, departs north to **Gandhamādana / Badarikāśrama** for austerities (*Bhāgavata X.51.58–63; Harivaṃśa*). [scripture]
  - *Variant (regional tīrtha lore):* several sites — caves near Dwārakā and in the Himālaya — claim to be Mucukunda's resting place; local aetiologies, not in the core Purāṇa. [regional]

## VI. Aftermath and the Dwārakā tīrtha (Bhāgavata X.52.1–13; Viṣṇu Purāṇa V.23)

- **Spoils and the return** — Kṛṣṇa takes the wealth of Kālayavana's leaderless Yavana host and starts back toward Mathurā; Jarāsandha, returning a final time, pursues the brothers (*Bhāgavata X.52.1–6*).
- **The leap from Pravarṣaṇa hill** — To shake Jarāsandha and confirm the Raṇacchoḍa stratagem, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma climb the **Pravarṣaṇa** mountain; Jarāsandha sets it ablaze, and the brothers leap from the burning peak and slip away to Dwārakā (*Bhāgavata X.52.6–11; Viṣṇu Purāṇa V.23*).
- **Dwārakā established as the Yādava capital** — The Yādavas settle the sea-city, which becomes Kṛṣṇa's seat for the rest of his earthly career — the stage for the **Rukmiṇī-haraṇa** and the eight queens (see [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md)) — until its submersion at his departure (*Bhāgavata X.52.12–13*).
- **Aetiology of the Dwārakā tīrtha** — The episode is the charter-myth of **Dwārakā** as one of the four *dhāmas* and seven sacred *purīs* (Sapta-purī): a city given by the ocean and built by Viśvakarman, sanctified as Kṛṣṇa's abode, and (per the Mausala Parva) reclaimed by the sea when he leaves the world — its drowning a recurrent motif in pilgrimage and, latterly, archaeology around modern Dwārakā/Bet Dwārakā. [scripture; the submersion's historicity is disputed]
- **Two clocks** — The episode's whole force is the **collision of timescales**: Mucukunda belongs to the deep Solar-dynasty past of his father **Māndhātṛ** (the "scriptural clock"), yet is woven into Kṛṣṇa's life at the Dvāpara–Kali junction. Tradition harmonizes this by his yuga-spanning sleep — a narrative device linking the two great dynasties at a single cave-mouth, not a historical synchronism. [scholarly]
