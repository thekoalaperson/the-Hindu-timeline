# Kārtika-māhātmya devotee tales — King Cola vs Viṣṇudāsa, Dharmadatta–Kalahā, Dhaneśvara, Satyabhāmā's former birth

> Cosmic address: the cluster of **exemplary devotee tales (upākhyānas)** embedded in the *glorification of the month of Kārtika* — the lunar month sacred to Viṣṇu as **Dāmodara**, recited each Kārtika to charter the vows of pre-dawn bathing (*kārtika-snāna*), lamp-offering (*dīpa-dāna*), and Tulasī worship. Their narrative frame is **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** (the age whose "easy means" they advertise), with the Satyabhāmā tale set in **[Dvāpara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)**. Carried in parallel recensions: **[Skanda Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/skanda-purana.md)**, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa, *Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya* (chs. ~24–29), and **[Padma Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/padma-purana.md)**, Uttara-khaṇḍa (chs. ~88–117). [late text]
> Parent: [Deep Dives](README.md)

The **Kārttika-māhātmya** is the charter of Vaiṣṇava month-piety: it declares Kārtika (Oct–Nov) the dearest month of Viṣṇu, and prescribes *kārtika-snāna* at dawn, the *dīpa-dāna* of lamps in Viṣṇu temples and the "sky-lamp" (*ākāśa-dīpa*) hoisted on a pole, Tulasī-worship and Tulasī-vivāha, night-vigil recitation, gift-giving, and dietary restraint. To prove the disproportion between the smallness of these acts and the vastness of their fruit, the māhātmya threads a set of **devotee tales** through the ritual instruction — the same tale-cluster recited each Kārtika, transmitted in **two parallel recensions (Skanda and Padma) that share plots and diverge in wording; they are catalogued here separately, never merged.** This file is the corpus's canonical home for these five: **King Cola and Viṣṇudāsa**, **Dharmadatta and the piśācī Kalahā**, the sinner **Dhaneśvara**, **Kṛṣṇa telling Satyabhāmā her former birth as Guṇavatī**, and the **Alakṣmī** episode. The Tulasī-origin side of Kārtika lore (Jālandhara–Vṛndā) is treated separately in [jalandhara-vrinda.md](jalandhara-vrinda.md).

## The frame — the greatness of the month of Kārtika

- **Dāmodara-māsa, the month of the lamp** — The māhātmya frames Kārtika as the month in which Viṣṇu (as **Dāmodara**) is most easily pleased; even a poor devotee's dawn bath, a single ghee-lamp, and one Tulasī-leaf are said to outweigh great sacrifices. The vow runs the full month, its climax at **Kārtika-pūrṇimā**, and it opens on **[Prabodhinī Ekādaśī](ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md)** when Viṣṇu wakes from his four-month sleep and **Tulasī-vivāha** begins. (*Skanda*, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa, *Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya*; *Padma*, Uttara-khaṇḍa) [late text]
- **The device of the tales** — Each exemplum answers one objection to the vow's extravagant promises — *can so slight an act free a great sinner? a ghost? win Viṣṇu himself as husband?* — and the tales are the affirmative proof, structurally kin to the "easy-means" logic of [Ajāmila](ajamila.md) (name) and the [Bhāgavata-Māhātmya](bhagavata-mahatmya-gokarna-dhundhukari.md) (kathā). The frame-narrators differ by recension and are left unspecified where the editions disagree. [disputed]

## King Cola and Viṣṇudāsa — wealth against bhakti, and the freed brahma-rākṣasa

- **The two devotees** — **Viṣṇudāsa**, a poor but supremely devout Vaiṣṇava, and the wealthy **Cola** king each keep the Kārtika-vrata with total scruple — the one with almost nothing, the other with a kingdom's resources — so that the tale sets **poverty-with-bhakti against wealth-with-bhakti** as the māhātmya's central question. (*Skanda*, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa, *Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya*, chs. ~24–27) [late text]
- **Viṣṇudāsa cooks his own flesh** — The poor bhakta, bound by a Kārtika vow of hospitality (never to eat without first feeding a guest) and with no food to offer, **cuts flesh from his own body and cooks it** to honour the arriving guest rather than break his vow — the māhātmya's proof that his devotion asks nothing for itself. (*Skanda*, *Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya*) [late text]
  - *Interpretive gloss:* the self-flesh offering places Viṣṇudāsa in the lineage of the "gift of the body" exempla (Śibi, Jīmūtavāhana); tradition reads it as the ground on which his later claim to superior merit rests. [oral tradition]
- **The brahma-rākṣasa on the road** — Travelling, king and bhakta encounter a **brahma-rākṣasa** — a brahmin dead in sin, now a tree-haunting demon — who begs release. Each **donates the merit of a single Kārtika observance**; by that transferred merit the demon is **freed and rises in divine form** to a higher world — the tale's demonstration that Kārtika-merit is transferable and can liberate even the damned. (*Skanda*, *Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya*, chs. ~26–28) [late text]
- **The dispute over whose merit freed him** — King and bhakta then **quarrel over which one's donated Kārtika-merit actually accomplished the liberation** — a contest of devotional honour, not greed — and the quarrel hardens until **armies gather and war threatens**, each unwilling to yield the other the greater bhakti. (*Skanda*, *Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya*, chs. ~27–29) [late text]
- **Viṣṇu's judgment** — Rather than let blood be shed over his name, **Viṣṇu intervenes**: the god declares the vow's power such that even a fraction of one Kārtika sufficed, and — the tale's point — that **the poor Viṣṇudāsa's bhakti was not inferior to the rich king's**; both are honoured and taken to Viṣṇu's abode. (*Skanda*, *Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya*, ch. ~29) [late text]
  - *Variant (Padma recension):* the Padma Uttara-khaṇḍa carries a closely parallel Cola/Viṣṇudāsa episode with variant names and emphasis; the two are recorded side by side, not conflated. [disputed]

## Dharmadatta and the piśācī Kalahā — liberation by Tulasī-water

- **Dharmadatta the observer** — **Dharmadatta**, a pious brāhmaṇa, keeps the Kārtika-vrata with daily Tulasī-worship and the dawn bath, the merit of the Tulasī steadily accruing to him. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa; *Skanda*, *Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya*) [late text]
- **Kalahā the quarrel-ghost** — **Kalahā** ("Strife") was in life a quarrelsome, sinful woman who died unpurified and became a **piśācī** (she-ghost), racked in the ghost-state — her name naming her fault. (*Padma*, Uttara-khaṇḍa) [late text]
- **The drop of Tulasī-water** — By the mere contact of **water sanctified by Tulasī** (dripping from Dharmadatta's worship, or his transferred Kārtika-Tulasī merit), the piśācī Kalahā is **cleansed of her sin and released from ghost-hood**, rising to a good rebirth or heaven — the tale that most sharply asserts the salvific power of Tulasī-tīrtha in Kārtika. (*Padma*, Uttara-khaṇḍa; *Skanda*, *Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya*) [late text]
  - *Thematic note:* the episode is the Kārtika counterpart to the preta-liberations of [Gokarṇa–Dhundhukārī](bhagavata-mahatmya-gokarna-dhundhukari.md) and the ancestor-Ekādaśīs — merit and sacred water reaching even the disembodied dead → cf. [śrāddha & pitṛ-rites](../05-concepts-and-dharma/shraddha-and-pitr-rites.md), [pitṛs & ancestors](../08-beings-and-bestiary/pitrs-and-ancestors.md). The Tulasī cult itself: [sacred flora & fauna](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/sacred-flora-and-fauna.md), [Śaṅkhacūḍa–Tulasī](shankhachuda-tulasi.md).

## Dhaneśvara — the sinner saved by a single Kārtika lamp

- **A life without dharma** — **Dhaneśvara**, a brāhmaṇa by birth, lives as a great sinner — abandoning his rites, given to theft, drink, and the company of the fallen — accumulating sin enough to condemn him to Yama's hells. (*Skanda*, *Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya*; *Padma*, Uttara-khaṇḍa) [late text]
- **The accidental lamp** — Once, in the month of Kārtika and with no pious intent, he is present when a **lamp is lit before Viṣṇu** — he lights, tends, or is the incidental cause of the **kārtika-dīpa** in a Viṣṇu shrine — and this single unwilled meritorious act attaches to him. (*Skanda*, *Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya*) [late text]
- **Sins burned by the flame** — At death, when Yama's messengers come, **the merit of the one Kārtika lamp outweighs a lifetime of sin**: his transgressions are burned away and he is saved, given a good rebirth or Viṣṇu's abode — the māhātmya's strongest claim for *dīpa-dāna*, that even an unintended Kārtika lamp destroys the sin of a lost life. (*Skanda*, *Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya*; *Padma*, Uttara-khaṇḍa) [late text]
  - *Śaiva parallel:* the structure exactly mirrors [Guṇanidhi → Kubera](gunanidhi-kubera.md), the wastrel who accidentally kept a lamp burning before Śiva and won kingship of wealth — the same "accidental-lamp salvation" motif carried on the Vaiṣṇava side by Dhaneśvara. [scholarly]
  - *Doctrinal note:* like [Ajāmila](ajamila.md), the tale presses the disproportion of a trivial act's infinite fruit — the theology of grace over desert examined under [pāpa, puṇya & prāyaścitta](../05-concepts-and-dharma/papa-punya-and-prayaschitta.md).

## Kṛṣṇa tells Satyabhāmā her former birth as Guṇavatī

- **The queen's question** — In **Dvāpara**, at Dvārakā, **Satyabhāmā** asks **Kṛṣṇa** by what merit she became his beloved queen, sharing his side above the other wives. Kṛṣṇa answers by revealing her **past life**. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa; *Skanda*, *Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya*) [late text]
- **Guṇavatī the poor devotee** — In that earlier birth she was **Guṇavatī**, a poor brāhmaṇa's daughter (in some tellings a young widow) with almost nothing, who nonetheless kept the **Kārtika-vrata** flawlessly — the dawn bath, the ghee-lamp before Viṣṇu, Tulasī-worship, and a small gift given in faith. (*Padma*, Uttara-khaṇḍa) [late text]
- **The fruit: Kṛṣṇa as husband** — By that Kārtika-merit alone Guṇavatī was reborn as **Satyabhāmā**, daughter of **Satrājit**, and won **Kṛṣṇa himself as husband** and a share of Lakṣmī-hood — the charter tale that the Kārtika-vrata can grant the Lord as one's own spouse. (*Padma*, Uttara-khaṇḍa; *Skanda*, *Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya*) [late text]
  - *Connection:* the tale is often narrated in the orbit of the **Pārijāta** episode, Satyabhāmā's demand for the celestial tree → [Pārijāta-haraṇa](parijata-harana.md); it ranks Kārtika-devotion above birth and wealth, the female-devotee counterpart to Viṣṇudāsa.

## The Alakṣmī episode — why the lamps and the clean house drive out misfortune

- **Alakṣmī, the elder sister of Śrī** — **Alakṣmī** (also **Jyeṣṭhā**, "the Elder") is the goddess of poverty, hunger, filth, sloth, and strife — risen from the **[churning of the ocean](samudra-manthana.md)** *before* Lakṣmī (hence "elder"), the inauspicious antithesis whom the Śrī-Sūkta already begs to be banished. → full profile in [Lakṣmī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/lakshmi.md). (*Padma Purāṇa*; Ṛgveda Khila, Śrī-Sūkta) [scholarly]
- **Assigned to dwell in the inauspicious** — In the Padma tradition Alakṣmī is wedded to a sage of ill-omen (**Duḥsaha**) and **assigned to dwell wherever there is dirt, quarrelling, oversleeping, idleness, and neglect of worship** — the negative image of exactly what the Kārtika-vrata forbids. (*Padma Purāṇa*) [late text]
- **The Kārtika-vrata as her expulsion** — The Kārtika/Dīpāvalī observances — the pre-dawn rising and bath, the swept and lit house, the rows of lamps, the clatter of festivity — are aetiologized as **driving Alakṣmī out and inviting Śrī-Lakṣmī in**: the vow's rules of cleanliness and wakefulness are precisely those that leave misfortune no dwelling. (*Padma Purāṇa*; living Dīpāvalī tradition) [late text] [regional]
  - *Note:* this episode is the doctrinal hinge between the Kārtika-vrata and the **Dīpāvalī** festival of lights that falls within the month — the lamp that saves Dhaneśvara and the lamp that expels Alakṣmī are the same *kārtika-dīpa*. → festival frame in [festivals & vratas](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md).

## Text, recensions, and the two clocks

- **Two parallel recensions** — The Kārttika-māhātmya circulates in a **Skanda** form (Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa, *Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya*) and a **Padma** form (Uttara-khaṇḍa); they share this tale-cluster with variant wording, sequence, and character-detail, and are **catalogued separately, not merged** — chapter numbers vary by edition and the ranges given here (Skanda ~24–29; Padma ~88–117) follow the printed recensions and should be treated as approximate. [disputed]
- **Scriptural clock** — The devotee tales are set in **Kali Yuga** as vrata-exempla (their whole rhetorical point is Kali's "easy means"); the **Satyabhāmā–Guṇavatī** frame sits in **Dvāpara** at Dvārakā, with Guṇavatī's Kārtika observance a still-earlier lifetime; the **Alakṣmī** origin reaches back to the **churning of the ocean** (cross-yuga). On the scriptural clock Kārtika itself recurs every year of the age (Kali began 3102 BCE).
- **Academic clock [scholarly]** — Both the *Skanda* Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa and the *Padma* Uttara-khaṇḍa carrying these māhātmyas belong to **late, medieval strata (broadly post-1000 CE)** of composite Purāṇas — documents of the **medieval Vaiṣṇava vrata economy** (merit-transfer, lay lamp-piety, the Tulasī and Dīpāvalī cults) rather than early narrative. The tales' "trivial act, infinite fruit" theology is the same late-bhakti current that produced the Ekādaśī-māhātmyas and the Bhāgavata-Māhātmya. [scholarly] [late text]
- **Function** — Across all five the message is uniform and deliberately extreme: Kārtika-merit **liberates a brahma-rākṣasa and a piśācī**, **saves an unintending sinner**, and **wins Viṣṇu himself as spouse** — the vow ranked above wealth (Cola), above birth (Dhaneśvara), and above desert (all), with the poor devotee (Viṣṇudāsa, Guṇavatī) exalted over the rich.
