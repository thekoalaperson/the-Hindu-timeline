# The Bhakti Sūtras — Nārada & Śāṇḍilya

> Deep dive within [04-deep-dives](README.md) of *The Hindu Timeline* — the two aphoristic charters of **bhakti-mārga**, the path of devotion. Their *attributed* authors are timeless: [Nārada](narada.md) is the cross-yuga wandering devarṣi, and Śāṇḍilya a Vedic seer of the Chāndogya Upaniṣad; the *texts* as we have them are late compositions of the Kali era [late text] [scholarly]. For bhakti as one path among three, see [../05-concepts-and-dharma/the-three-margas.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/the-three-margas.md); for the aphorism-genre sibling, [yoga-sutras-of-patanjali.md](yoga-sutras-of-patanjali.md).

Where Patañjali gave Yoga its sūtra-manual, two short texts do the same for devotion: the **Nārada Bhakti Sūtra** (84 aphorisms) and the **Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra** (~100 aphorisms). Both define bhakti, defend its supremacy over karma, jñāna and yoga, and codify its varieties — but in different registers: Nārada's is lyrical and practical, a devotee's handbook whose paradigm is the *prema* of the Vraja gopīs; Śāṇḍilya's is scholastic and polemical, opening in deliberate imitation of the Brahma Sūtras to claim for bhakti the status of a full *śāstra* against the jñāna-schools. Academic dating places both in the post-Gītā, likely medieval period — well after the sages they are ascribed to. [scholarly]

---

## 1. Two Clocks: the Attributed Sages vs the Datable Texts

- **Nārada, the traditional author** — the mind-born son of Brahmā, the vīṇā-carrying messenger-sage who teaches bhakti to [Prahlāda](prahlada-narasimha.md), [Dhruva](dhruva.md) and Vyāsa across every yuga (full profile in [narada.md](narada.md)); within tradition the teaching is therefore timeless/cross-yuga. (traditional ascription)
- **Śāṇḍilya, the traditional author** — a ṛṣi of the Vedic gotra lists, most famous for the **Śāṇḍilya-vidyā** of the Chāndogya Upaniṣad (3.14): *"sarvaṁ khalv idaṁ brahma"* — "all this, verily, is Brahman." Tradition identifies the sūtra-author with this Upaniṣadic seer. (Chāndogya Upaniṣad 3.14; see [../90-literature-corpus/principal-upanishads-detail.md](../90-literature-corpus/principal-upanishads-detail.md))
- **Academic dating — Nārada Bhakti Sūtra** — modern scholarship treats it as a **medieval** composition, generally placed *after* the Bhāgavata Purāṇa, with common estimates around the **10th–12th centuries CE** (some argue earlier, some later); the attribution to the devarṣi is honorific. [late text] [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Academic dating — Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra** — unsettled; proposals span from the early centuries CE to the early second millennium. It predates its principal commentary (Svapneśvara's *bhāṣya*, medieval), and many scholars take it to be **older than the Nārada sūtra**, which cites "Śāṇḍilya's" definition of bhakti (NBS 18) — though that citation may name the sage's *tradition* rather than this exact text. [late text] [scholarly] [disputed]
- **The two-clocks rule applied** — traditionally these are eternal teachings voiced by primordial sages; historically they are late crystallizations of a bhakti doctrine already matured in the [Bhagavad Gītā](bhagavad-gita.md), the [Nārāyaṇīya](narayaniya.md) and the Bhāgavata Purāṇa ([skandha map](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md)). Both frames are kept distinct here. [scholarly]

## 2. Nārada Bhakti Sūtra — Architecture (84 sūtras, 5 chapters)

The received text divides into five short chapters. (Nārada Bhakti Sūtra; chapter divisions vary slightly between editions [disputed])

- **I. The nature of bhakti (NBS 1–24)** — parā bhakti defined as supreme love; its fruits; the rival definitions; the gopī exemplar.
- **II. The supremacy of bhakti (NBS 25–33)** — bhakti ranked above karma, jñāna and yoga; the debate over whether jñāna is its means.
- **III. The means (sādhana) of bhakti (NBS 34–50)** — renunciation, constant worship, hearing and singing the Lord's qualities, and above all the company and grace of the great (*mahat-saṅga*, *mahat-kṛpā*); the shunning of evil company; who "crosses over māyā."
- **IV. Prema defined; gauṇī vs parā (NBS 51–66)** — the ineffability of love; the threefold *secondary* bhakti versus the one supreme love; conduct rules for the aspirant.
- **V. The glory of the devotees (NBS 67–84)** — the marks of the *ekāntins* (one-pointed devotees), the abolition of caste distinctions among them, the **eleven āsaktis**, and the closing roster of bhakti-ācāryas.

## 3. Nārada's Core Doctrines

### 3a. The parā-prema definition
- **"Sā tv asmin parama-prema-rūpā" (NBS 2)** — bhakti *is* "of the nature of supreme love (*parama-prema*) directed to Him," and **"amṛta-svarūpā ca"** (NBS 3) — "of the very nature of immortality/nectar." Gaining it, a person "becomes perfected, immortal, fully satisfied" (*siddho bhavati, amṛto bhavati, tṛpto bhavati*, NBS 4), desires nothing further, grieves at nothing, hates nothing, does not delight in sense objects (NBS 5). (Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 2–5)
- **The catalog of rival definitions (NBS 15–19)** — the sūtra preserves a mini-doxography of earlier teachers: **Vyāsa** (Pārāśarya) — bhakti is loving attachment to *pūjā* and acts of worship; **Garga** — attachment to hearing the Lord's *kathā*; **Śāṇḍilya** — delight in the Self, not opposed to it (*ātma-raty-avirodhena*); and **Nārada's own verdict** — "offering all one's acts to Him, and feeling extreme anguish in forgetting Him" (NBS 19) — a citation that ties the two sūtra-traditions of this file together. (Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 15–19)
- **The gopī exemplars (NBS 21–24)** — "such indeed [was the love] of the gopīs of Vraja" (*yathā vraja-gopikānām*, NBS 21): the milkmaids of the [rāsa-līlā](rasa-lila.md) are the living paradigm of parā bhakti. The text immediately defends them — even there, no forgetting of the Lord's divine glory (*māhātmya-jñāna*, NBS 22); love *without* that knowledge would be mere paramour's passion (*jārāṇām iva*, NBS 23–24). (Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 21–24; cf. [krishna-lila.md](krishna-lila.md))

### 3b. Supremacy over the other mārgas
- **"Sā tu karma-jñāna-yogebhyo 'py adhikatarā" (NBS 25)** — bhakti is *greater* than the paths of action, knowledge and meditation (the mārga triad treated systematically in [../05-concepts-and-dharma/the-three-margas.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/the-three-margas.md)) — **because it is of the nature of the fruit itself** (*phala-rūpatvāt*, NBS 26): the other paths are means to an end, bhakti is the end enjoying itself. God, moreover, "dislikes pride and loves humility" (NBS 27). (Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 25–27)
- **The jñāna debate, staged inside the text (NBS 28–30)** — "some say knowledge alone is its means" (*ity eke*); "others, that the two are mutually dependent" (*anyonyāśrayatvam ity anye*); but "the son of Brahmā [Nārada] says bhakti is its own fruit" (*svayaṁ phala-rūpateti brahma-kumāraḥ*). The clinching analogies: a king's palace does not open to one who merely *knows about* it, and knowledge of food never stilled hunger — only eating does; therefore seekers of liberation should embrace bhakti alone (NBS 31–33). (Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 28–33)

### 3c. The sādhana chain (NBS 34–50)
- **Means to bhakti** — renunciation of sense objects (NBS 35); unbroken loving worship (NBS 36); hearing and singing the Lord's qualities even amid ordinary life (NBS 37); but **chiefly the grace of great souls** (*mahat-kṛpā*, NBS 38) — their company "hard to obtain, unfailing in effect" (NBS 39–40), itself granted only by God's grace (NBS 41). Evil company, breeding lust, anger and delusion, is to be abandoned utterly (NBS 43–45). (Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 34–45)
- **"Who crosses over māyā?" (NBS 46–50)** — the one who abandons attachment, serves the great, resorts to solitude, transcends the three guṇas, gives up acquisition-and-keeping (*yoga-kṣema*); who renounces the fruits of action, then actions themselves; **"who renounces even the Vedas and obtains uninterrupted love-longing"** (NBS 49) — "he crosses, he crosses over māyā — and carries the worlds across with him" (*sa tarati sa tarati sa lokāṁs tārayati*, NBS 50). (Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 46–50)

### 3d. Gauṇī vs parā bhakti (NBS 51–66)
- **The ineffability of prema (NBS 51–55)** — love's true nature "cannot be put into words — like taste for a mute" (*mūkāsvādanavat*, NBS 52); it is "quality-less, desire-free, growing each moment, unbroken, subtlest, sheer experience" (NBS 54); gaining it, the devotee "sees only that, hears only that, thinks only that" (NBS 55). (Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 51–55)
- **Gauṇī (secondary) bhakti is threefold (NBS 56–57)** — divided either by the guṇas (sāttvikī / rājasī / tāmasī) or by the motive of the devotee — the **ārta** (distressed), the **arthārthī** (seeker of gain), the **jijñāsu** (seeker of knowledge) — each earlier grade lower than the next; the scheme consciously echoes the Gītā's four classes of worshipper (Bhagavad Gītā 7.16). Parā bhakti stands beyond all these: motiveless, one-pointed, its own reward. (Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 56–57; Bhagavad Gītā 7.16)
- **Conduct of the aspirant (NBS 61–66)** — no anxiety over worldly loss (NBS 61); duties not dropped before bhakti is firm, but their *fruits* relinquished (NBS 62); talk of women, wealth and unbelievers shunned (NBS 63); pride and hypocrisy abandoned (NBS 64); every act — even lust, anger, pride — redirected toward Him alone (NBS 65); the love to be cultivated is that of "the eternal servant and the eternal bride" (*nitya-dāsya-nitya-kāntā-bhajana*, NBS 66). (Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 61–66)

### 3e. The eleven āsaktis (NBS 82)
- **"Though one, bhakti becomes elevenfold" (ekadhāpy ekādaśadhā bhavati)** — the celebrated list of eleven forms of loving attachment (*āsakti*): **(1) guṇa-māhātmya-āsakti** — attachment to glorifying His qualities; **(2) rūpa-āsakti** — to His beauty; **(3) pūjā-āsakti** — to His worship; **(4) smaraṇa-āsakti** — to remembering Him; **(5) dāsya-āsakti** — to His service; **(6) sakhya-āsakti** — to His friendship; **(7) vātsalya-āsakti** — parental tenderness toward Him; **(8) kāntā-āsakti** — the beloved's love, as wife to husband; **(9) ātma-nivedana-āsakti** — total self-offering; **(10) tan-mayatā-āsakti** — absorption into Him; **(11) parama-viraha-āsakti** — the supreme anguish of separation. (Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 82)
  - *Note (parallel scheme):* the list overlaps with, but is distinct from, Prahlāda's **navadhā bhakti** (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.5.23) — Nārada adds the relational modes and crowns the list with *viraha*, the separation-love dramatized in the [Bhramara-gītā](bhramara-gita-uddhava-sandesha.md). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.5.23; Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 82)
- **The devotee community (NBS 67–81)** — the one-pointed devotees (*ekāntins*), conversing "with choked voice, horripilation and tears," purify their families and the very earth (NBS 68); they make holy places holy, works good, scriptures scriptural (NBS 69); **among them no distinction of birth, learning, beauty, family, wealth or occupation — "because they are His"** (NBS 72–73); disputation avoided (NBS 74–75); non-violence, truth, purity, compassion, faith guarded (NBS 78); the Lord "quickly reveals Himself" to such devotees (NBS 80). (Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 67–81)
- **The closing roster of bhakti-ācāryas (NBS 83)** — the sūtra names its own lineage of devotion-teachers: the **Kumāras, Vyāsa, Śuka, Śāṇḍilya, Garga, Viṣṇu, Kauṇḍinya, Śeṣa, Uddhava, Āruṇi, Bali, [Hanumat](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md), Vibhīṣaṇa** "and others" — again naming Śāṇḍilya, and Uddhava, recipient of the [Uddhava Gītā](uddhava-gita.md). Whoever trusts this teaching of Nārada "attains bhakti, attains the Beloved" (NBS 84). (Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 83–84)

## 4. Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra — ~100 sūtras, the Scholastic Charter

- **"Athāto bhakti-jijñāsā" (ŚBS 1.1.1)** — "Now, therefore, the inquiry into bhakti" — a deliberate echo of the Brahma Sūtra's *athāto brahma-jijñāsā*, announcing that devotion deserves its own systematic *śāstra* with the same dignity as Vedānta. The text runs to roughly **100 sūtras in three chapters (adhyāyas)**, each subdivided into sections (āhnikas); exact numbering varies between editions. (Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra 1.1.1) [disputed: sūtra count/division]
- **The definition: "sā parānuraktir īśvare" (ŚBS 1.1.2)** — bhakti is "supreme attachment (*parānurakti*) to Īśvara," with the next sūtra grounding its fruit in scripture: for one established in it, **immortality** is taught (*tat-saṁsthasyāmṛtatvopadeśāt*) — Svapneśvara's commentary hearing here the Chāndogya's Śāṇḍilya-vidyā. (Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra 1.1.2–3, with Svapneśvara)
- **Broad plan of the three chapters** — Chapter 1: the nature of bhakti and its distinction from (and superiority to) jñāna and yoga; Chapter 2: the subsidiary means — the *gauṇī* (secondary) devotions such as recitation (*japa*), worship and remembrance — together with discussion of Īśvara, the self and the world; Chapter 3: the varieties and fruits of devotion, closing on liberation through the Lord's grace. (Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra, per Svapneśvara's bhāṣya) [scholarly: architecture as commonly summarized; editions differ]
- **Gauṇī vs parā again** — like Nārada, Śāṇḍilya distinguishes the one **parā bhakti** (supreme attachment, the direct cause of liberation) from **secondary** devotions — worship, remembrance, praise — which are means that ripen into it, not identical with it. (Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra, with Svapneśvara)
- **Universal eligibility (adhikāra)** — the Śāṇḍilya tradition, like NBS 72, extends the right to bhakti beyond Vedic ritual qualification — women, śūdras and those outside the varṇa pale are eligible, since love of the Lord presupposes no ritual competence. (Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra with Svapneśvara's bhāṣya) [disputed: exact sūtra placement varies between editions]
- **The Gītā as proof-text** — the argument leans throughout on the [Bhagavad Gītā](bhagavad-gita.md), which Svapneśvara cites constantly; the ŚBS is in effect an aphoristic systematization of the Gītā's devotional theology. (Svapneśvara's bhāṣya) [scholarly]

## 5. The Jñāna-vs-Bhakti Polemic

The Śāṇḍilya sūtra's distinctive edge is its running argument **against the jñāna-schools' claim that knowledge, not devotion, liberates** — the scholastic counterpart of Nārada's palace-and-food analogies. (Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra ch. 1)

- **Bhakti is not a species of jñāna** — knowledge of the Lord can coexist with hatred of Him: the argument (developed by Svapneśvara with the stock examples of Kaṁsa and Śiśupāla, who *knew* Kṛṣṇa full well) shows that cognition alone does not save; what saves is *attachment*, which the haters lack. Knowledge is at most an auxiliary that terminates once devotion arises. (Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra 1.1, with Svapneśvara) [scholarly: argument as standardly summarized]
- **Nor a kriyā (ritual act)** — unlike a Vedic rite, devotion is not *enjoined and performed* by effort of will; like knowledge it arises from its object, yet unlike knowledge it is affective clinging (*anurakti*) — a third thing, irreducible to the Mīmāṃsaka's *karma* and the Vedāntin's *jñāna* alike. (Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra ch. 1, with Svapneśvara) [scholarly]
- **Against jñāna-niṣṭhā as the summit** — where Advaita makes steadfastness-in-knowledge final and bhakti a preparatory purifier, Śāṇḍilya inverts the order: jñāna is the subordinate limb, bhakti the direct cause of mukti by the Lord's grace. (Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra; cf. the school-by-school verdicts in [../05-concepts-and-dharma/the-three-margas.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/the-three-margas.md))
- **Nārada's parallel move** — NBS 28–30 stages the same dispute in three aphorisms and settles it by fiat of the *brahma-kumāra*; the two texts thus record a live medieval controversy from the devotional side. (Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 28–30) [scholarly]

## 6. The Two Sūtras Compared; Commentaries & Reception

- **Register** — Nārada: experiential, aimed at practice (*sādhana*), crowned by the gopī-paradigm and the eleven āsaktis; Śāṇḍilya: dialectical, aimed at establishing bhakti-śāstra's *authority*, crowned by the anti-jñāna polemic. They agree on the gauṇī/parā hierarchy, the supremacy of grace, and universal eligibility. [scholarly]
- **Svapneśvara's bhāṣya** — the principal surviving commentary on the Śāṇḍilya sūtra, medieval in date [disputed: variously placed within the 13th–15th c. CE], reading the sūtra through the Gītā and the Purāṇas; brought to Western scholarship by **E. B. Cowell's** English translation of sūtra-plus-commentary (Calcutta, 1878). [scholarly]
- **Modern currency of the Nārada sūtra** — **Swami Vivekananda** drew on it for the *parā-bhakti* sections of his *Bhakti-Yoga* lectures (1890s); **Swami Tyagisananda's** Ramakrishna Math edition became the standard study text; the Gauḍīya line (see [../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md)) reads the āsaktis as anticipations of Rūpa Gosvāmī's rasa-taxonomy. [late text] [scholarly]
- **Place in the larger bhakti arc** — historically both sūtras sit *downstream* of the Gītā, the [Nārāyaṇīya](narayaniya.md) and the Bhāgavata, and *upstream of or parallel to* the vernacular waves — the Tamil [Āḻvārs and Nāyaṉārs](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/alvars-and-nayanars.md) and the [north-Indian sants](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/north-bhakti-sants.md) — as Sanskrit systematizations of what the singers lived. [scholarly]
