# Gaṇeśa-Gītā: The Song of Gaṇeśa

> Where this sits: an independent **Purāṇic Gītā** of **11 chapters** embedded in the **Krīḍā-khaṇḍa** (chs. 138–148) of the [Gaṇeśa Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/ganesha-and-mudgala-upapuranas.md), spoken by **Gajānana (Gaṇeśa)** to **King Vareṇya** — the principal **Gāṇapatya** counterpart to the [Bhagavad Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md), and the key proof-text for **Gaṇeśa = Para-brahman**.
>
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Master index: [../README.md](../README.md) · Companions: [The lesser Gītās catalog](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md) · [Śiva-Gītā](./shiva-gita.md) · [Bhagavad Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md) · [Devī-Gītā](./devi-gita.md)

The **Gaṇeśa-Gītā** ("the Song of Gaṇeśa") is a self-contained Vedāntic discourse in which **Gajānana**, the [Dvāpara-Yuga incarnation of Gaṇeśa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md), instructs **King Vareṇya** in *karma-*, *jñāna-*, and *bhakti-yoga* and reveals his own **cosmic form (viśvarūpa)** — the entire structure modeled point-for-point on Kṛṣṇa instructing Arjuna. Its single most distinctive feature is that **roughly 90% of its verses are lightly adapted from the Bhagavad Gītā itself**, with "Kṛṣṇa" replaced by "Gaṇeśa," so that the elephant-faced deity steps into the role of the supreme teacher and **Absolute (Para-brahman)**. It stands on the standard *gītā-saṅgraha* rosters beside the [Śiva-Gītā, Devī-Gītā, and Rāma-Gītā](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md#purāṇic--independent-gītās-the-path-to-60--pointers-only) as the **Gāṇapatya** entry in the first tier of Purāṇic Gītās. [scholarly]

## Textual placement & frame

- **Location** — The Gaṇeśa-Gītā sits in the **Krīḍā-khaṇḍa** (the "section of [divine] play," also called Uttara-khaṇḍa) of the [Gaṇeśa Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/ganesha-and-mudgala-upapuranas.md), conventionally counted as **11 adhyāyas** spanning **chapters 138–148**, totalling roughly **414 verses**. (Gaṇeśa Purāṇa, Krīḍā-khaṇḍa 138–148)
  - *Variant (placement):* some catalogues and editions instead associate the Gītā with the **Upāsanā-khaṇḍa**; the **Krīḍā-khaṇḍa 138–148** placement is the one followed in the standard editions and Yeats/Gulyani-style translations. [disputed — khaṇḍa]
  - *Variant (verse count):* totals are cited around **~414 verses** across the 11 chapters; figures vary slightly by recension. [disputed — recension]
- **The narrative occasion** — The setting is the reign of **King Vareṇya**, father (in the Purāṇa's frame) of the demon-slaying avatāra. Having witnessed Gajānana's divinity, the king, troubled by worldly sorrow and the problem of right action, asks the god for the highest knowledge — the Gāṇapatya mirror of Arjuna's despondency on the battlefield. (Gaṇeśa Purāṇa, Krīḍā-khaṇḍa 138)
- **The teacher** — The instructor is specifically the **Gajānana** form of Gaṇeśa (the Dvāpara incarnation, mouse-mounted, slayer of the demon of egotism), not Gaṇeśa in the abstract — anchoring the discourse to the avatāra cycle of the Krīḍā-khaṇḍa. (Gaṇeśa Purāṇa, Krīḍā-khaṇḍa)
- **Reported frame** — Like the rest of the Purāṇa, the dialogue reaches the reader through the standard **sage-to-sage recitation frame** (Vyāsa/Sūta tradition), reporting Gajānana's words to Vareṇya. (Gaṇeśa Purāṇa)

## The eleven chapters

- **Ch. 1 (138) — Sāṃkhya-sārārtha-yoga** — Vareṇya's questions and Gajānana's opening teaching on the imperishable self, the unreality of grief over the body, and the distinction of *kṣetra* from *kṣetrajña* — adapting Bhagavad Gītā 2. (Krīḍā-khaṇḍa 138)
- **Ch. 2 (139) — Karma-yoga** — disinterested action (*niṣkāma-karma*), action without attachment to fruit, as superior to inaction — adapting BhG 3. (Krīḍā-khaṇḍa 139)
- **Ch. 3 (140) — Vijñāna / the avatāra doctrine** — Gaṇeśa as the unborn Lord who takes form age after age to protect dharma; the path of knowledge purifying action — adapting BhG 4. (Krīḍā-khaṇḍa 140)
- **Ch. 4 (141) — Yoga of renunciation of action** — the reconciliation of *saṃnyāsa* and *karma-yoga*, the equanimity of the self-realized — adapting BhG 5. (Krīḍā-khaṇḍa 141)
- **Ch. 5 (142) — Dhyāna / Yoga of meditation** — the disciplines of posture, breath, and mind-control; the steady sage (*sthita-prajña*) seated in Gaṇeśa — adapting BhG 6. (Krīḍā-khaṇḍa 142)
- **Ch. 6 (143) — Yoga of the cosmos / divine glories** — Gaṇeśa as the substratum of all manifestation, the *vibhūti* litany of his pervading powers — condensing BhG 7–10. (Krīḍā-khaṇḍa 143)
- **Ch. 7 (144) — The viśvarūpa (cosmic vision)** — the climax: Gaṇeśa grants Vareṇya **divine sight** and reveals his **all-pervading universal form**, worlds and beings contained within him — the Gāṇapatya answer to **Bhagavad Gītā 11**. (Krīḍā-khaṇḍa 144)
- **Ch. 8 (145) — Kṣetra-kṣetrajña-viveka** — the field and its knower, *prakṛti* and *puruṣa*, the discrimination that liberates — adapting BhG 13. (Krīḍā-khaṇḍa 145)
- **Ch. 9 (146) — Yoga of the *guṇas* / *puruṣottama*** — the three strands of nature (*sattva*, *rajas*, *tamas*) and the supreme Person beyond them — condensing BhG 14–15. (Krīḍā-khaṇḍa 146)
- **Ch. 10 (147) — *Upāsanā* / the divine and demonic** — modes of worship, faith graded by *guṇa*, and the contrast of divine vs. demoniac natures — adapting BhG 16–17. (Krīḍā-khaṇḍa 147)
- **Ch. 11 (148) — Yoga of liberation** — the summation: total surrender (*prapatti*/*śaraṇāgati*) to Gaṇeśa as the highest secret, and the promise of release — adapting **BhG 18**. (Krīḍā-khaṇḍa 148)
  - *Variant (chapter naming):* chapter titles vary across editions and translations; the **11-chapter / 138–148** span and the broad BhG-to-Gaṇeśa-Gītā mapping above are stable, but exact *adhyāya* names differ. [disputed — recension]

## Relation to & divergences from the Bhagavad Gītā

- **The ~90% borrowing** — The defining fact: scholars (e.g. **Yuvraj Krishan**, **Anita Thapan**, **Greg Bailey**) note that the large majority of the Gaṇeśa-Gītā's verses are **directly lifted or minimally reworked from the Bhagavad Gītā**, with the name and pronouns of the deity changed so that **Gaṇeśa occupies Kṛṣṇa's seat** as speaker and Para-brahman. It is the clearest case among the sectarian Gītās of a **template-and-substitution** composition. [scholarly]
- **Compression of 18 into 11** — Where the Bhagavad Gītā has **18 chapters**, the Gaṇeśa-Gītā **condenses them into 11**, fusing several BhG chapters per *adhyāya* (notably collapsing the *vibhūti* chapters 7–10 and the guṇa-chapters 14–15). The narrative spine — despondent questioner → graded yogas → *viśvarūpa* theophany → surrender — is preserved. [scholarly]
- **Theological substitution** — The doctrinal payload shifts from **Vaiṣṇava** to **Gāṇapatya**: the *avatāra*-protecting-dharma verse, the *vibhūti* glories, and the universal-form vision are all reassigned to Gaṇeśa, making him the source from whom Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and Śiva derive their powers — the [inversion of the Trimūrti hierarchy](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/ganesha-and-mudgala-upapuranas.md) characteristic of these upapurāṇas. [scholarly]
- **OṂ / bindu identification** — Layered onto the borrowed Vedānta is the distinctively Gāṇapatya equation of **Gaṇeśa with the *praṇava* (OṂ)** and the **bindu** (pure consciousness) — the theological move that turns the "first-invoked" obstacle-remover into the **first principle of reality**, undergirding the Gītā's claim that Gaṇeśa is the Absolute. [scholarly]
- **What is *not* borrowed** — The frame (Gajānana–Vareṇya, set in the avatāra cycle rather than the Kurukṣetra war) and the Gāṇapatya cosmology are original; the philosophy is the common **Advaita-inflected Vedānta** of the late medieval Gītā corpus. [scholarly]

## Doctrine & affiliations

- **Gāṇapatya proof-text** — The Gaṇeśa-Gītā is the **principal scriptural basis** for the **Gāṇapatya** sect's claim that Gaṇeśa is Para-brahman, the supreme reality — exactly as the Bhagavad Gītā grounds Kṛṣṇa's supremacy and the [Śiva-Gītā](./shiva-gita.md) grounds Śiva's. Each major deity-stream answers the Bhagavad Gītā with a *viśvarūpa* of its own. [scholarly]
- **Advaita-Vedānta core** — Its metaphysics is **non-dualist**: one Brahman (= Gaṇeśa), the world as appearance, *jīva*–*Brahman* identity, liberation by knowledge crowned by devotion — read in Smārta circles as a Gāṇapatya parallel to Śaṅkara's Vedānta. (A traditional Marāṭhī commentary, the *Nāth*-school **Gaṇeśa-Gītā-bhāṣya**, circulates with it.) [scholarly]
- **Bhakti–jñāna synthesis** — Like its model and unlike the purely *jñāna*-leaning [Avadhūta-Gītā](./avadhuta-gita.md) or [Ṛbhu-gītā](./ribhu-gita.md), it presents devotion and knowledge as a **single graded path**, closing (BhG 18-style) on surrender to the Lord as the supreme secret. [scholarly]

## Place among the "great Gītās"

- **First-tier Purāṇic Gītā** — On the standard rosters the Gaṇeśa-Gītā stands with the **Śiva-Gītā** (Padma Purāṇa), **Devī-Gītā** (Devī-Bhāgavata), and **Rāma-Gītā** (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa) as the principal **Purāṇic** Gītās — one per major deity-stream. (see [lesser Gītās catalog → Purāṇic Gītās](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md#purāṇic--independent-gītās-the-path-to-60--pointers-only)) [folk] [scholarly]
- **The most derivative of the set** — Among the great Gītās it is the **closest verbal copy** of the Bhagavad Gītā; the [Śiva-Gītā](./shiva-gita.md) and [Devī-Gītā](./devi-gita.md) mirror the *structure* and *theophany* but compose largely fresh verses, whereas the Gaṇeśa-Gītā **reuses the Bhagavad Gītā's own text**. [scholarly]
- **Independent circulation** — Like the Devī- and Śiva-Gītās, it is printed, commented, and recited **detached from its parent Purāṇa** as one of the "great Gītās." [late text]

## Two clocks — scriptural vs. historical

- **Scriptural time** — *In-narrative* the discourse is uttered in the **[Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara, in the lifetime of the **Gajānana** incarnation — narratively the era of the Mahābhārata and the Bhagavad Gītā itself, which the text deliberately echoes. [scriptural]
- **Historical time** — *Text-critically* the Gaṇeśa-Gītā is a **late-medieval Gāṇapatya composition** within the Gaṇeśa Purāṇa, dated by scholars to roughly **c. 1100–1600 CE** (Hazra, Preston, Thapan, Bailey, Stevenson differ within this window), built **on the finished Bhagavad Gītā as its template**. Keep manuscript dating distinct from the Dvāpara-Yuga setting. [scholarly] [late text]
