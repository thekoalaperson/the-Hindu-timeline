# Vrata-Kathās — A Catalog of Observance-Charter Tales

> Cosmic address: *recurring Kali-Yuga rites with mythic-time charters* — each kathā anchors a vow on the [ritual calendar](../00-time-and-cosmology/hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md) (a tithi, a weekday, a month) to a story set in god-time or in an unplaced "once there was" past; only the Ananta kathā's frame (Kṛṣṇa to Yudhiṣṭhira) and the Śani Māhātmya's hero (Vikramāditya) touch the datable spine. Historically [scholarly] the printed kathās are late-Purāṇic to early-modern pothī literature.
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Festival calendar: [Festivals & Vratas](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md) · Siblings: [Satyanārāyaṇa Vrata Kathā](satyanarayana-vrata-katha.md) · [Ekādaśī-māhātmyas](ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md) · [Śivarātri hunter legend](shivaratri-hunter-legend.md)

A **vrata-kathā** is the story that *is part of the rite*: hearing it completes the vow, and the plot is a ledger proving the observance's objective power — kept, it prospers anyone; slighted, it strikes anyone. This file catalogs the major household kathās not treated elsewhere. **Vaṭa-Sāvitrī** (the banyan-thread vow of wives) is *not* duplicated here — its charter is the Mahābhārata tale itself, treated in full at [Sāvitrī & Satyavān](savitri-satyavan.md). Most texts below are **[late text]** attributions (chiefly to the [Bhaviṣyottara](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/bhavishya-purana.md) and [Skanda](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/skanda-purana.md) Purāṇas) or frankly **[folk]** pothī/oral tellings.

## Ananta Chaturdaśī — Kauṇḍinya and the ananta-thread

*Bhādrapada Śukla 14: worship of Viṣṇu as **Ananta** (the endless one, resting on [Śeṣa-Ananta](../08-beings-and-bestiary/shesha-ananta.md)); a 14-knot silk/cotton cord (the **ananta-dora**) is tied on the arm.*

- **The frame: Kṛṣṇa prescribes it to Yudhiṣṭhira** — the kathā opens with the Pāṇḍavas dispossessed after the dice-game; **Yudhiṣṭhira asks Kṛṣṇa** for a rite to recover fortune, and Kṛṣṇa teaches the **Ananta vrata** — fourteen years of the 14-knot vow — as the means by which the kingdom will return. (Bhaviṣyottara Purāṇa [attrib.], Ananta-vrata-kathā) [late text]
- **Suśīlā ties the thread** — within Kṛṣṇa's telling: **Suśīlā**, daughter of the brahmin **Sumantu** (ill-used by her stepmother in most pothīs), marries the sage **Kauṇḍinya**; on the journey to his home she sees women worshipping Ananta on a riverbank, joins them, and ties the thread — and the couple's poverty turns to prosperity. (Bhaviṣyottara Purāṇa [attrib.]) [late text]
- **Kauṇḍinya tears the thread** — seeing the cord on his wife's arm, Kauṇḍinya scorns it — crediting their rise to his own merit — **snaps the ananta-dora and throws it into the fire**. Their fortune collapses into destitution. (Bhaviṣyottara Purāṇa [attrib.])
- **The riddle-quest through the forest** — remorseful, Kauṇḍinya wanders vowing not to rest until he sees Ananta, asking his way of a **mango tree**, a **cow with calf**, a **bull**, **two ponds**, a **donkey**, and an **elephant** — none knows the god. An **old brahmin** (Ananta veiled) leads him to a cave opening into Viṣṇu's presence; the god explains the beings met as emblems of faults (in common tellings: the tree a brahmin who hoarded learning, the ponds two sisters who gave alms only to each other, the donkey wrath, the elephant pride — details vary by pothī [folk]), and Kauṇḍinya, performing the vrata **fourteen years**, regains all and attains Viṣṇu's world. (Bhaviṣyottara Purāṇa [attrib.]) [late text]
- **Calendar overlays** — the same tithi closes the ten-day Gaṇeśotsava (**Gaṇeśa-visarjana** day) in Maharashtra, and in the South the rite is kept as **Ananta-Padmanābha vrata**; the two observances share the day, not the kathā. ([Gaṇeśa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md); [festival calendar](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md)) [regional]

## Ṛṣi Pañcamī — the Seven Seers and the rajasvalā-doṣa

*Bhādrapada Śukla 5 (day after Gaṇeśa Chaturthī): women bathe with **apāmārga** twigs and worship the [Saptarṣis](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md) with Arundhatī, expiating lapses of the menstrual-seclusion rules (rajasvalā-doṣa).*

- **The frame** — a king (the pothīs commonly name **Sitāśva**) asks **Brahmā** for the vrata that destroys sin best; Brahmā answers with Ṛṣi Pañcamī and its proof-tale. (Bhaviṣyottara Purāṇa [attrib.], Ṛṣi-pañcamī-kathā) [late text]
- **Uttaṅka's daughter** — the brahmin **Uttaṅka** and his wife **Suśīlā** (names vary by pothī) of Vidarbha have a widowed daughter whose body, as she sleeps in their āśrama, is seen **swarming with worms/ants**; questioned inwardly, the cause is a former birth's breach — she entered the kitchen and touched vessels while rajasvalā and never performed Ṛṣi Pañcamī. Performing the vrata frees her body and her future. (Bhaviṣyottara Purāṇa [attrib.]) [late text]
  - *Variant (ox-and-bitch strand, printed pothīs):* a brahmin couple are **reborn as a bullock and a bitch in their own son's household** for the same breach; at the son's śrāddha the bitch's share is defiled (a serpent poisons the milk she then dares not conceal), the son overhears the animals lament their births, learns the cause, and releases his parents by the vrata's merit. [folk] [oral tradition]
- **What it charters** — uniquely among these kathās the beneficiary class is explicit: the vrata is prescribed **to women, against ritual faults held to be women's own**, honoring the seven seers as the arbiters of purity; modern reform readings contest the premise, and Nepal (where **Rishi Panchami** is a major Teej-cycle day) keeps the observance at state scale. [regional] [scholarly]

## Maṅgalā Gaurī — the Tuesdays of Śrāvaṇa

*Śrāvaṇa Tuesdays, kept especially by newly married women (classically for the first five years) for the husband's life — [Gaurī/Pārvatī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md) worshipped as **Maṅgalā**, "the auspicious."*

- **The short-lived son** — a rich, childless merchant (pothīs commonly name **Dharmapāla**) at last gains a son — but fated to **die of snakebite at sixteen**. (printed Maṅgalā-Gaurī pothīs) [folk] [oral tradition]
- **The bride whose mother kept the vrata** — the boy is married (in most tellings while on pilgrimage, before the fated year) to a girl whose **mother had performed the Maṅgalā Gaurī vrata** — earning her daughter **akhaṇḍa saubhāgya**, unwidowable good fortune. When the appointed serpent comes by night, the vrata-merit disarms it (the bride sets out milk and the snake, in the widespread telling, leaves harmless — some pothīs turn it into a necklace of gold by morning [folk]); the sixteenth-year death-writ lapses, and Yama's officers withdraw. The kathā ends with the couple, the merchant's house, and the vrata all confirmed. (printed pothīs; attribution to the Bhaviṣyottara claimed on title pages, untraceable in critical texts [disputed]) [folk] [oral tradition]
- **The Gayā seat** — the **Maṅgalā Gaurī temple at [Gayā](../06-sacred-geography/gaya-pitru-tirtha.md)** is counted in [Śakti-pīṭha lists](../06-sacred-geography/51-shakti-peethas.md) (the breast of Satī in the eighteen-pīṭha rosters); the vrata and the pīṭha share the name and the goddess, not a single charter text. [regional]

## Solah Somvār — the Sixteen Mondays and the dice-game frame

*Sixteen consecutive Mondays (Somvārs) of fasting to Śiva, most often begun in Śrāvaṇa — the Monday-Śiva complex treated on the ground at [Śrāvaṇa observances & Kanwar Yatra](../06-sacred-geography/kanwar-yatra-and-shravan-shiva-observances.md).*

- **The dice game and the priest's lie** — **Śiva and Pārvatī**, sojourning at a mortal temple (pothīs name Amarāvatī or Mandhātā), sit to **dice** (the recurring divine game of the [Śiva myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)); Pārvatī asks the temple **brahmin-priest** who will win, he answers "Śiva" — and Pārvatī wins. For the partial lie she **curses him with leprosy**. (Solah-Somvār pothī kathā) [folk] [oral tradition]
- **The apsaras' remedy** — heavenly nymphs visiting the temple pity the rotting priest and teach him the **sixteen-Monday vrata** (fast, evening pūjā, a cooked wheat-jaggery-ghee offering — *cūrmā* — shared out); on the sixteenth Monday he stands whole. (pothī kathā) [folk]
- **The chain of performers** — Pārvatī, returning and hearing how he was cured, performs the vrata herself and by it her estranged son **[Kārttikeya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/ganesha-and-kartikeya.md)** turns his heart home; Kārttikeya performs it and recovers his **lost brahmin friend**; the friend performs it and — by the rājakumārī-svayaṁvara device of the **royal elephant garlanding him** — weds a princess and rises to a throne. (pothī kathā) [folk] [oral tradition]
- **The queen who scorned it** — years on, the brahmin-king's queen refuses the vrata's observance; misfortune expels her from the palace, and every house that shelters her withers until she performs the sixteen Mondays and is restored — the genre's standard broken-vow engine. Title pages assign the kathā to the **Śiva Purāṇa**, where no such chapter is traceable. [disputed] [folk]

## Varalakṣmī Vratam — Cārumatī's dream

*Śrāvaṇa's Friday before the full moon (the second Friday), the great women's [Lakṣmī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/lakshmi.md)-vrata of Andhra, Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and parts of Maharashtra — a kalaśa dressed as the goddess ("Varalakṣmī," the boon-granting Lakṣmī) is worshipped at home.* [regional]

- **The frame: Śiva tells Pārvatī** — Pārvatī asks Śiva for the vrata by which women gain all welfare; he answers with Varalakṣmī's story, set in the city of **Kuṇḍina** (Kuṇḍinapura in Vidarbha). (Skanda Purāṇa [attrib.], Varalakṣmī-vrata-kathā) [late text]
- **Cārumatī** — the virtuous **Cārumatī**, devoted to husband and in-laws, is visited by **Mahālakṣmī in a dream** and told to worship her as Varalakṣmī on that Śrāvaṇa Friday; Cārumatī gathers the town's women and performs the rite — and with each pradakṣiṇā of the kalaśa the tokens of fortune appear on them (anklets, bracelets, ornaments of gold in the common telling), their houses filling with wealth. The kathā closes with Cārumatī honored as first of vrata-keepers. (Skanda Purāṇa [attrib.]) [late text] [regional]
- **Genre note** — like the [Kojāgarī Pūrṇimā vigil](../05-concepts-and-dharma/sharad-purnima-kojagari-lakshmi-puja.md), the rite's theology is Lakṣmī's *initiative* — the goddess seeks out the worthy householder woman, not the reverse.

## Kedāra Gaurī Vratam — the vow that made Ardhanārīśvara

*A 21-day autumn vrata (ending, in the Tamil reckoning, on Dīpāvalī/Amāvasyā of Aippasi), kept in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and Andhra; the northern toponym lives in the name — Śiva as lord of [Kedāra](../06-sacred-geography/kedarnath-kedara-kshetra.md).* [regional]

- **Gaurī's vow for half his body** — Pārvatī, desiring inseparable union with Śiva, is taught the **Kedāra vrata**; keeping its 21 days she is granted **the left half of Śiva's own body** — this kathā is the vrata-charter for **[Ardhanārīśvara](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-avatars-and-forms.md)**, rooting the icon in a woman's observance rather than a cosmic accident. (Skanda Purāṇa [attrib.], Kedāra-vrata-kathā) [late text]
  - *Variant policy:* the Purāṇic Ardhanārīśvara charters elsewhere in this corpus (Brahmā's plea for a productive creation; Bhṛṅgi's refusal to circle the goddess) are **separate myths**, not merged here — see [Śiva's forms](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-avatars-and-forms.md).
- **Punyavatī and Bhāratī** — the South Indian printed kathās append a mortal proof-pair: of two sisters (commonly **Punyavatī** and **Bhāratī**), the one who keeps the Kedāra vrata rises to fortune and royal honor, the one who neglects or mocks it falls to ruin — and is restored only when she at last performs it. Names and details vary across the Telugu/Tamil nomu-books. (regional vrata literature) [regional] [folk]

## Somvatī Amāvasyā — the washerwoman's sindūra

*Any new-moon day that falls on a **Monday** ([Soma's](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/chandra-soma.md) day) — held rare and potent; women circumambulate the **pīpal** tree (Viṣṇu's tree) 108 times for the husband's life, and bathing at tīrthas that day is credited with Gaṅgā-snāna fruit. [folk]*

- **The daughter doomed to widowhood** — a householder's daughter (a sāhūkār's, in the common Hindi telling) is blessed by every passing mendicant except with saubhāgya; a paṇḍit reads her fate: **widowhood** — unless the old washerwoman **Sonā**, a lifelong Somvatī-keeper across the sea, gives her own **sindūra** to the girl, who must then honor the pīpal on a Somvatī Amāvasyā. (oral/pothī kathā) [folk] [oral tradition]
- **The service and the gift** — the girl, brought by her youngest brother (in most tellings the sea is crossed by the grace of **grateful vultures** whose chicks she saved from a serpent), secretly does Sonā's housework before dawn until the old woman discovers her and grants the sindūra from her own parting — and at that instant **Sonā's husband dies**, the saubhāgya having passed with the vermilion. (oral/pothī kathā) [folk]
- **The 108 circumambulations** — walking home, Sonā meets a Somvatī Amāvasyā and spends the **merit of her 108 pīpal-pradakṣiṇās** to call her husband back to life — the kathā's proof that the vrata's stored puṇya is real, transferable currency. The girl, keeping the vow thereafter, escapes her written widowhood. (oral/pothī kathā) [folk] [oral tradition]
- **Attribution** — popular manuals claim a Mahābhārata warrant (Bhīṣma to Yudhiṣṭhira on the day's bathing merit); no such passage is traceable and the claim is recorded here as circulation, not source. [disputed]

## Śani Māhātmya — Saturn and King Vikramāditya

*The Saturday/Śani-propitiation kathā of Maharashtra and the Hindi belt — expanding the one-line entry in [Navagraha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md); the hero belongs to the legendary [Vikramāditya cycle](../90-literature-corpus/brihatkatha-kathasaritsagara-and-vikramaditya-cycle.md).* [late text] [regional]

- **The court debate** — in **[Ujjain](../06-sacred-geography/ujjain-avantika.md)**, King **Vikramāditya's** paṇḍits debate which of the nine grahas is greatest; the king, pressed, ranks **Śani** last and speaks slightingly of him. Śani warns the king he will learn Saturn's weight when his **sāḍe-sātī** (the 7½-year transit) begins. (Śani Māhātmya, Marathi/Hindi text) [late text]
- **The seven-and-a-half years** — Śani comes disguised as a **horse-dealer**; the king mounts a marvelous horse that bolts and strands him nameless in a far kingdom. Lodged with a merchant, he is framed when a **necklace vanishes from the wall** (swallowed, in the telling, by a painted goose/peg-figure that comes alive); the local king orders his **hands and feet cut off**. Maimed, he survives turning an **oil-presser's press**, singing ragas in the night. (Śani Māhātmya) [late text] [folk]
- **Release and the bargain** — a princess, hearing the night-song, vows to marry the crippled singer over all protest; the transit ends, **Śani appears and restores the king's limbs**, the painted bird yields up the necklace, and Vikramāditya is recognized and restored. He asks of Śani one boon — that Saturn never afflict another as harshly; Śani grants that those who **worship him on Saturdays and hear this Māhātmya** will pass his transits lightly — the text's own phala-śruti and charter. Shrines of the resulting cult: **Shani Shingnapur** and Tirunallar, catalogued at [Navagraha temples](../06-sacred-geography/navagraha-temples.md). (Śani Māhātmya) [late text] [folk] [regional]

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural/cyclic frame** — these kathās mostly float in mythic or unplaced time: the Śiva-Pārvatī dice game (Solah Somvār) and Gaurī's Kedāra vow are god-time recurrences; Kauṇḍinya, Uttaṅka's daughter, Cārumatī, Dharmapāla's son and Sonā live in the genre's "once there was" past. Two anchors touch the spine: the Ananta frame sits in **Dvāpara's end** (Kṛṣṇa counseling the dispossessed Yudhiṣṭhira), and **Vikramāditya** belongs to early-**Kali** legend (the Vikrama-era tradition of 57 BCE — legendary, not documentary [disputed]).
- **Academic/historical frame [scholarly]** — the vrata prescriptions are codified in the medieval **nibandha digests** (Hemādri's *Caturvarga-cintāmaṇi*, 13th c.; *Nirṇayasindhu*, 17th c. — see [smṛti overview](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md)), quoting a fluid "Bhaviṣyottara" whose citations often cannot be matched to any printed text ([Bhaviṣya profile](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/bhavishya-purana.md)). The kathās as now recited are **19th–20th-century printed pothīs**; the **Śani Māhātmya** in its familiar Marathi form is likewise a product of the early print era, riding the older Vikramāditya story-cycle. None of these narratives is attested in early Purāṇa manuscripts. [late text]
