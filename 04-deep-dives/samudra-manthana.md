# Samudra Manthana — The Churning of the Ocean

> Cosmic address: in the *Bhagavata Purana* frame (Canto 8, from 8.5) this episode unfolds in the prior **[Chakshusha Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-06-chakshusha.md)** (6th) — the reign of **Chakshusha Manu**, with Indra named **Mantradruma** and Vishnu appearing as the avatara **Ajita** to bear Mandara as Kurma (cf. [manvantara-06-chakshusha](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-06-chakshusha.md)); some accounts instead frame it in primordial time or the present **[Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)** (7th). [disputed: manvantara placement is text-dependent.] Narrated across the Itihasa and Puranas. It is the origin-event of **amrita** (immortality nectar), of **Lakshmi**'s emergence, of **Kurma** (the [Dashavatara](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) tortoise), and of the eternal **[deva–asura](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md)** rivalry.
> Parent: [Deep Dives](README.md)

The **Samudra Manthana** ("churning of the ocean") — also *Kshira-Sagara Manthana* (churning of the milk-ocean), *Amrita Manthana* — is one of the most widely depicted episodes in Hindu tradition. The Devas and Asuras jointly churn the cosmic ocean to extract **amrita**, using **Mount Mandara** as the churning-rod and the serpent **Vasuki** as the rope, with **Vishnu** as **Kurma** (tortoise) supporting the mountain. Fourteen (in the most common count) **ratnas** ("jewels/treasures") emerge, culminating in the nectar — which the Devas secure through Vishnu's **Mohini** ruse. The episode appears in the *Mahabharata* (Adi Parva — earliest extant version), *Ramayana* (Bala Kanda), and most Puranas (Bhagavata, Vishnu, Padma, Matsya, Agni, Brahmanda, Vayu, Kurma, Linga, Skanda, Brahma-Vaivarta, Shiva), with significant divergence in cause, sequence, and list of treasures.

---

## I. The Cause — Why the Ocean Was Churned

- **Durvasa's curse on Indra** - The irascible sage Durvasa gifts Indra a divine garland (the abode of Sri/Lakshmi); Indra carelessly drapes it on his elephant **Airavata**, who tramples it. Insulted, Durvasa curses the Devas to lose all *sri* (fortune, vigor, splendor). (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.5; *Vishnu Purana* 1.9)
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 1.9):* the garland is the **Santanaka** flower-garland from a Vidyadhari; the same trampling and curse follow — this is the locus classicus for the Durvasa frame.
- **Lakshmi withdraws into the ocean** - Stripped of fortune, the three worlds wither; Lakshmi (Sri) departs heaven and re-enters the **Kshira-Sagara** (ocean of milk) from which she will later be re-born. (*Vishnu Purana* 1.9; *Bhagavata Purana* 8.8)
- **Asuras ascend; Devas defeated** - Led by **Bali** (and Asura generals), the Asuras rout the weakened Devas, who flee Svarga. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.5–8.6)
- **Appeal to Vishnu / Brahma** - The Devas, with Brahma, approach Vishnu at the Kshira-Sagara; he counsels a **temporary truce** with the Asuras to churn the ocean for amrita, promising the Devas will ultimately keep it. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.6; *Mahabharata*, Adi Parva)
  - *Variant (Mahabharata, Adi Parva, "Astika"/Amrita-manthana section):* the framing is set on Mount Meru in primordial time, with the Devas resolving among themselves to churn for the nectar of immortality — the Durvasa curse is **absent**; the motive is simply the desire for amrita to escape death.

## II. The Apparatus — Mountain, Serpent, Tortoise

- **Mount Mandara as churning-rod** - The Devas and Asuras uproot **Mandara** (a spur of/near Mount Meru, the world-axis) to serve as the *manthana-danda* (churning staff). (*Mahabharata*, Adi Parva; *Bhagavata Purana* 8.6–8.7)
  - *Variant:* some accounts have Vishnu's mount **Garuda** carry Mandara to the ocean; in the Mahabharata the great serpents and the Devas together haul it.
- **Vasuki as the churning-rope** - The naga king **Vasuki** offers himself as the twisting-cord wound around Mandara. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.7; *Mahabharata*, Adi Parva)
  - **Side-allocation dispute** - The Devas first grasp Vasuki's head; the Asuras object to taking the tail; to flatter their pride Vishnu has the **Asuras hold the head, Devas the tail** — exposing the Asuras to Vasuki's fiery, venomous breath. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.7)
- **The mountain sinks — Kurma avatara** - Lacking a base, Mandara plunges into the ocean. **Vishnu incarnates as Kurma (the tortoise)** and dives beneath, bearing the mountain on his back/shell as the pivot. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.7; *Vishnu Purana*; *Agni*, *Kurma*, *Padma* Puranas) — see [Dashavatara](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
  - *Variant:* Vishnu is also said to enter the Asuras and Devas as strength, and to press down on the mountain's summit from above with one form while supporting it as Kurma below (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.7) — multiple simultaneous Vishnu-forms.
- **Vasuki's venom (Halahala) released** - The strain makes Vasuki exhale poison and flames; this is the immediate prelude to the first emergent (below). (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.7)

## III. The Fourteen Ratnas — Treasures of the Churning

The most-cited enumeration is **14 ratnas**; scriptural lists actually range **9–14** and differ by text. Order of emergence below follows the *Bhagavata Purana* (Canto 8) tradition, with variants noted. The poison usually comes first; the nectar (amrita) last.

- **1. Halahala / Kalakuta (the world-poison)** - A cataclysmic poison surfaces (from the ocean and/or Vasuki) threatening all creation. **Shiva** drinks it to save the cosmos; **Parvati** stays it in his throat, which turns blue — hence **Nilakantha** ("blue-throated"). (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.7; *Vishnu Purana*; *Shiva Purana*)
  - *Variant (some accounts):* a drop falls and is licked up by snakes, scorpions, and venomous creatures, explaining their venom.
  - *Variant (Mahabharata):* the poison is swallowed/absorbed by **Shiva (Rudra)** at Brahma's request; emphasis on Rudra rather than Parvati's intervention.
- **2. Kamadhenu / Surabhi** - The wish-fulfilling cow, mother of cattle; taken by the rishis for ritual (ghee/yajna). (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.8; *Mahabharata*)
- **3. Uchchaihshravas** - The pure-white (often seven-headed) celestial horse; claimed by **Bali**/the Asuras (or given to Indra in some texts). (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.8; *Mahabharata*)
- **4. Airavata** - The four-tusked white elephant (with companion elephants in some lists); becomes **Indra's** mount. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.8; *Mahabharata*)
- **5. Kaustubha** - The supreme jewel/gem; worn by **Vishnu** on his chest. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.8; *Vishnu Purana*)
- **6. Parijata / Kalpavriksha** - The celestial wish-granting tree (Parijata flower-tree, never-fading blossoms); placed in Indra's heaven. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.8 — Parijata; many lists give **Kalpavriksha/Kalpataru**)
- **7. Apsaras (Rambha et al.)** - Troupes of celestial nymphs/dancers; take residence in Svarga. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.8; *Mahabharata*)
- **8. Lakshmi (Sri)** - The goddess of fortune re-emerges radiant from the ocean and, of her own choice, garlands and weds **Vishnu** — restoring sri to the cosmos. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.8; *Vishnu Purana* 1.9) — see [Trimurti and Tridevi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md).
- **9. Varuni / Sura (Madira)** - The goddess of wine/liquor (lotus-eyed Varuni); accepted by the **Devas** (in many tellings the Asuras refuse her, hence *sura* = Devas as wine-takers and *a-sura* = those who declined her, in the traditional etymology). (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.8; *Mahabharata*)
- **10. Chandra (the Moon)** - The moon arises; **Shiva** takes it upon his head (*Chandrashekhara*). (*Vishnu Purana*; many Puranic lists; in *Bhagavata* the moon is among the celestial emergents)
- **11–13. Additional emergents (vary by text):** various lists include —
  - **Panchajanya** - Vishnu's conch. (*Vishnu Purana* tradition)
  - **Sharanga** - Vishnu's bow. (some lists)
  - **Jyeshtha / Alakshmi (Nirriti)** - Goddess of misfortune/poverty, who emerges **before** Lakshmi and is dispatched to inauspicious places. (*Padma*/*Linga* traditions)
  - **Varuna's umbrella, Indra's earrings (kundala), Nidra-devi** (goddess of sleep), and the **Tulasi** plant. ([scholarly] composite of minor Puranic variants)
- **14. Dhanvantari with the Amrita** - The divine physician **Dhanvantari** rises last, bearing the *kalasha* (pot) of **amrita** — the goal of the entire enterprise. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.8; *Mahabharata*; *Vishnu Purana*) Dhanvantari is counted an aspect/avatar of Vishnu and the patron of Ayurveda.

> **Variant on the count and order:** The *Vishnu Purana* (Wilson trans.) gives a list emphasizing Sri/Lakshmi, Sura (Varuni), Chandra, the Parijata, Kamadhenu, Dhanvantari + amrita, and the **Kaustubha**, with the poison absorbed by Shiva — totaling fewer named ratnas than the canonical 14. The Mahabharata's Adi Parva enumerates **Lakshmi, Sura, the Moon, Uchchaihshravas, Kaustubha, and Dhanvantari with amrita** as the principal emergents. The "14 ratnas" as a fixed didactic list is a **later harmonization** [scholarly] across Puranic versions; individual texts list as few as 9.

## IV. The Theft of the Nectar — Mohini

- **Asuras seize the amrita** - The moment Dhanvantari appears with the pot, the **Asuras snatch it** and flee, quarreling over precedence. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.8–8.9; *Mahabharata*)
- **Vishnu becomes Mohini** - Vishnu assumes the form of **Mohini**, an irresistibly beautiful enchantress. Bewitched, the Asuras hand her the pot and agree to let her distribute it fairly. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.9; *Mahabharata*, Adi Parva)
- **The nectar served to the Devas** - Mohini seats Devas and Asuras separately and serves the amrita **only to the Devas**, distracting/deceiving the Asuras. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.9)
- **Rahu's deception and beheading** - The Asura **Svarbhanu** disguises himself as a Deva and slips between **Surya (Sun)** and **Chandra (Moon)** to drink. The luminaries expose him; Mohini/Vishnu hurls the **Sudarshana Chakra**, severing his head — but the amrita has already reached his throat, making both pieces immortal. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.9–8.10; *Vishnu Purana*; *Mahabharata*)
  - **Rahu and Ketu** - The immortal head becomes **Rahu**, the body/tail **Ketu** — the two shadow-planets (*Navagraha*). Their eternal grudge against Surya and Chandra explains **eclipses** (Rahu/Ketu periodically "swallowing" them). (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.9; *Surya Siddhanta* tradition for the graha role)
- **Mohini and Shiva [later texts]** - In a sequel, Shiva, curious, asks to see Mohini; enchanted, their union produces **Ayyappa/Hariharaputra (Shasta)**. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.12; South Indian/Shaiva traditions) [variant — separate episode appended to the Manthana cycle]

## V. Aftermath

- **Renewed deva–asura war** - Nectar-strengthened, the Devas (with Vishnu) defeat the Asuras in the **Amrita-manthana war**; Indra is restored to Svarga and Lakshmi's fortune returns. (*Bhagavata Purana* 8.10–8.11; *Mahabharata*)
- **Spillage and the Kumbh Mela tradition** - In the popular (Puranic/folk) tradition, drops of amrita fall at four earthly sites during the contest over the *kumbha* (pot) — **Prayag, Haridwar, Ujjain, Nashik** — origin of the **Kumbh Mela**. (Puranic/folk tradition; see [Sacred Geography](../06-sacred-geography/README.md)) [the four-site etiology is largely medieval/folk, not in the early epic]
- **Cosmic significance** - Establishes amrita and the immortality of the Devas, the marriage of Vishnu–Lakshmi, Shiva as Nilakantha, the lineage of celestial treasures (Airavata, Uchchaihshravas, Kamadhenu, Kalpavriksha), and the Navagraha shadow-planets Rahu/Ketu.
