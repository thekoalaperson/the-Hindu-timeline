# Sage Śveta Saved from Kāla — the Liṅga Purāṇa's Kālāntaka Variant

> Cosmic address: a timeless Śaiva exemplum — the Liṅga Purāṇa attaches it to no yuga or reign; like the Mārkaṇḍeya rescue it is a "divine deed" told to prove that Śiva-bhakti outranks Time itself. Frame of narration: the Sūta reciting to the ṛṣis at Naimiṣāraṇya (i.e., the present Kali Yuga).
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Source text profile: [Liṅga Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/linga-purana.md) · Twin story: [Mārkaṇḍeya](markandeya.md) · Death himself: [Yama](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/yama.md)

The Mārkaṇḍeya rescue is the famous face of **Śiva Kālāntaka** ("Ender of Death"), but the **Liṅga Purāṇa carries its own, structurally independent telling** of the same theological event: Death comes for a liṅga-worshipping sage named **Śveta**, and Śiva destroys Death to save him. Here the devotee is not a boy fated to die at sixteen but an adult **royal sage (rājarṣi)**, and the assailant is named **Kāla** — Time-Death personified — rather than Yama in his ordinary bureaucratic role. The chapter is the Liṅga Purāṇa's charter-proof that **worship of the liṅga confers victory over death** (*mṛtyuṃjaya*), and it grounds Śiva's epithets **Kālakāla** ("the Time of time / Death of death") and **Kālāntaka** on a second, lesser-known devotee. This file is the canonical home for the Śveta episode; the Mārkaṇḍeya cycle has [its own deep-dive](markandeya.md).

## 1. Which Śveta this is — disambiguation first

The name Śveta ("white") is carried by several wholly unrelated figures in the corpus; none of the following is the Liṅga Purāṇa's sage:

- **Not the corpse-eating King Śveta of the Rāmāyaṇa** — in the [Uttara Kāṇḍa](ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md) the sage Agastya tells Rāma of a king Śveta who won heaven by austerity yet must return daily to a lake in the Daṇḍaka forest to **devour his own corpse**, because in life he fed no one but himself; Agastya frees him. (*Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa*, Agastya's narrative.) The same tale-type appears in the Mahābhārata's dāna-teaching — see [King Śveta and the half-golden mongoose](dana-upakhyanas-shveta-and-the-mongoose.md), which already disambiguates its own Śveta.
- **Not (necessarily) Śveta the first Yogeśvara** — the Liṅga Purāṇa's roster of the **28 yoga-avatāras of Śiva** opens with a **Śveta** who incarnates in a Kali Yuga of this manvantara (*Liṅga Purāṇa*, Pūrva-bhāga ch. 24; see the [Liṅga Purāṇa profile](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/linga-purana.md) §Yogeśvaras). Whether the rājarṣi of ch. 30 is meant to be the same figure is nowhere stated in the text as plainly as harmonizing retellings assume. [disputed]
- **Not Śvetaketu of the Upaniṣads**, nor the White Island (**Śvetadvīpa**) of the Nārāyaṇīya, nor the white directional elephant of the same name.
- **Probably related: the boy-sage Śveta of Thiruvenkadu** — Tamil sthala-tradition at **Śvetāraṇyeśvarar temple, Thiruvenkadu** ("the white forest," *śvetāraṇya*) tells of a Śveta saved from Yama by Śiva at that shrine — plausibly the same tale-type localized in the Kaveri delta. See §6 below. [regional] [folk]

## 2. The setting — a rājarṣi at the liṅga (Liṅga Purāṇa, Pūrva-bhāga ch. 30)

- **The Śveta-upākhyāna** — the episode occupies chapter 30 of the Pūrva-bhāga, told by the **Sūta** to the Naimiṣāraṇya sages within the Purāṇa's running demonstration of what Śiva-devotion accomplishes. (*Liṅga Purāṇa*, Pūrva-bhāga ch. 30.)
  - *Note (chapter numbering):* printed editions of the Liṅga Purāṇa differ in chapter divisions (the text "exists in many versions"); ch. 30 follows the numbering of the standard J. L. Shastri / AITM edition. [scholarly]
- **Śveta the devotee** — Śveta is a **royal sage (rājarṣi)** wholly given to the worship of Maheśvara: he attends upon the **liṅga**, absorbed in pūjā and japa, his mind fixed on Śiva. (*Liṅga Purāṇa*, Pūrva-bhāga ch. 30.)
- **The allotted span runs out** — Śveta's lifespan comes to its appointed end, and **Kāla** — Death as devouring Time — arrives to take him **while he is engaged in worship of the liṅga**. (*Liṅga Purāṇa*, Pūrva-bhāga ch. 30.)
  - *Naming note:* the Liṅga Purāṇa's antagonist is consistently styled **Kāla** (Time-Death); retellings and temple traditions freely call him **Yama**, since Kāla is among Yama's standard names — see [Yama](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/yama.md) for the identity of Yama/Kāla/Mṛtyu and its internal distinctions.

## 3. The confrontation — Death's boast and the devotee's defiance

- **Śveta refuses to fear** — interrupted at his worship, the sage declares himself beyond Death's reach: one whose heart holds Maheśvara, who serves the liṅga, has nothing to dread from Kāla. He continues his worship. (*Liṅga Purāṇa*, Pūrva-bhāga ch. 30.)
- **Kāla's universal claim** — Kāla answers with the classic boast of Death: **nothing in the three worlds escapes him** — all beings, however mighty, come under his sway when their time matures; even the gods are within Time's dominion. (*Liṅga Purāṇa*, Pūrva-bhāga ch. 30.)
  - The exchange is the chapter's doctrinal hinge: Kāla's claim is *true of everything except Śiva and those sheltered in Śiva* — setting up the demonstration that Śiva is **Kālakāla**, the Death of death. (*Liṅga Purāṇa*; Śaiva tradition.)
- **Death lays hands on the worshipper** — unmoved by the sanctity of the moment, Kāla proceeds to seize the sage in the very presence of the liṅga — Death asserting jurisdiction inside Śiva's own precinct. (*Liṅga Purāṇa*, Pūrva-bhāga ch. 30.)
  - *Contrast with the Mārkaṇḍeya telling:* there the offense is sharpened by the **noose falling around the liṅga itself** (*Śiva Purāṇa*; see [Mārkaṇḍeya](markandeya.md) §2); the Śveta chapter does not need that device — the assault on the absorbed devotee is offense enough. [see §5 on the two tellings]

## 4. Śiva destroys Death — and Śveta is made deathless

- **The manifestation** — Śiva manifests on the spot and **strikes Kāla down: Death himself is slain** before the sage's eyes. The god who ends the ender of all beings thereby vindicates the devotee's fearlessness. (*Liṅga Purāṇa*, Pūrva-bhāga ch. 30.)
  - *Variant (manner of the killing):* retellings and iconographic tradition differ on the instrument — the **kick of Śiva's foot** (the classic Kālāntaka-mūrti pose), the **trident**, or **burning by the glance of the third eye**; the pan-Indian Kālāntaka image was standardized around the Mārkaṇḍeya scene, and summaries of the Śveta chapter borrow its vocabulary. [disputed detail — do not press any single instrument onto ch. 30]
- **Deathlessness for the devotee** — with Death destroyed, Śveta is **freed from mortality**: his rescue is total, the fruit of liṅga-worship made literal — the worshipper of Śiva does not die. (*Liṅga Purāṇa*, Pūrva-bhāga ch. 30.)
  - Unlike Mārkaṇḍeya, Śveta is **not** counted in the standard chiranjīvi lists (see [the Chiranjīvis](the-chiranjivis.md)); his deathlessness is a proof-of-doctrine within the Liṅga Purāṇa rather than a running plot-device across the corpus.
- **Is Death restored?** — the cosmic order requires Death, and the Mārkaṇḍeya cycle regularly has Śiva **revive Yama** at the gods' plea (*Śiva Purāṇa*; see [shiva-myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) §Kālāntaka).
  - *Variant (applied to Śveta):* harmonizing retellings extend the same revival to the Śveta episode; whether the Liṅga Purāṇa's own chapter stages the restoration, or simply leaves Death slain as the demonstration's climax, is not consistently reported — treat the revival as the *cycle's* standard resolution rather than a certified detail of ch. 30. [disputed]
- **The theological yield** — the chapter grounds a cluster of Śiva-names carried in the Liṅga Purāṇa's own litanies: **Mṛtyuñjaya** (conqueror of death), **Kālakāla**, **Kālāntaka/Kālāri** — Śiva as the only power to which Time itself is subject. (*Liṅga Purāṇa*; cf. its 108- and 1,008-name litanies, see the [text profile](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/linga-purana.md).) Cross-ref the deity-side survey: [Śiva's forms](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-avatars-and-forms.md).

## 5. Śveta vs. Mārkaṇḍeya — two tellings, one theology, never merged

The corpus rule is to catalog variants, not fuse them. These are **two distinct stories** making the same claim:

- **Different devotee** — Mārkaṇḍeya is a **boy fated to die at sixteen**, his death-date fixed by the terms of a boon (*Śiva Purāṇa*; *Mahābhārata* Vana Parva); Śveta is an **adult rājarṣi** whose natural span simply ends. (*Liṅga Purāṇa*, Pūrva-bhāga ch. 30.)
- **Different antagonist-name** — Yama with his buffalo and noose in the Mārkaṇḍeya versions; **Kāla**, Time-Death in the abstract, in the Śveta chapter. (Same underlying figure; different theological register.)
- **Different mechanism of offense** — the noose looping the liṅga (Mārkaṇḍeya) versus the bare fact of Death intruding on a rapt worshipper (Śveta).
- **Different aftermath** — Mārkaṇḍeya becomes the **eternal sixteen-year-old chiranjīvi** who survives pralayas (see [Mārkaṇḍeya](markandeya.md) §3–4); Śveta's deathlessness ends his story — he generates no further corpus.
- **Same proof** — in both, Śiva-bhakti (specifically **liṅga-centered** worship) is shown stronger than the universal law of death — the Śaiva answer to the same problem the Vaiṣṇava corpus answers with [Ajāmila](ajamila.md) (the name of Hari routs the Yamadūtas) and the sapiential corpus with [Naciketas](nachiketa-katha-upanishad.md) and [Sāvitrī](savitri-satyavan.md), who face Death with knowledge and dharma rather than a rescuing god. Cross-ref [narakas and the afterlife](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/narakas-and-afterlife.md).
- **Both live in the Liṅga Purāṇa** — the text knows the Mārkaṇḍeya rescue too (it is among the sources cited for it: see [Mārkaṇḍeya](markandeya.md) §2 and [Yama](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/yama.md) §Kālāntaka); the Śveta chapter is thus not the Purāṇa's substitute for Mārkaṇḍeya but its **own additional witness** to the Kālāntaka doctrine.

## 6. Regional echo — Śvetāraṇya (Thiruvenkadu) [regional]

- **The white-forest shrine** — the **Śvetāraṇyeśvarar temple at Thiruvenkadu** (Tamil Nadu, Kaveri delta) is named for the *śveta-araṇya*, "white forest"; its sthala-legend remembers a **sage Śveta who worshipped Śiva here and was saved from Yama**, Śiva subduing Death at the shrine. [folk] [regional] (Sthala-purāṇa; see [Navagraha temples](../06-sacred-geography/navagraha-temples.md) §4.)
  - *Variant (assimilation to Mārkaṇḍeya):* the Tamil telling gives its Śveta the **boy-fated-to-die-at-sixteen** motif — i.e., it has absorbed the Mārkaṇḍeya framing onto the Śveta name; the corpus records the two strands separately rather than deciding which is "original." [disputed] [folk]
- **Relation to the Liṅga Purāṇa** — whether the Tamil Śveta descends from the Purāṇic chapter, or both draw on a shared Kālāntaka tale-type, is undetermined; the South Indian **Kālasaṃhāra/Kālāntaka** cult (with its great Mārkaṇḍeya-site at Thirukkadaiyur) provides the living devotional context. [scholarly — transmission unestablished]

## 7. The two clocks

- **Scriptural/cyclic frame** — the episode is undated in the text: a timeless demonstration, like the Liṅga Purāṇa's other divine deeds. If a harmonizer identifies the sage with **Śveta the first Yogeśvara**, that would place him in a **Kali Yuga early in the present Vaivasvata Manvantara** — but this identification is inference, not statement. [disputed] (Frame of narration: Sūta at Naimiṣāraṇya, in the present Kali Yuga.)
- **Academic/historical frame [scholarly]** — the Liṅga Purāṇa is commonly dated **c. 5th–10th century CE**, an accreted text revised over centuries, its cosmological-doctrinal Pūrva-bhāga (where ch. 30 sits) reckoned the older stratum. [late text] (See the [text profile](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/linga-purana.md) §Dating.)
