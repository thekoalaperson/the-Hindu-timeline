# Ekādaśī Māhātmyas — the Founding Legend and the Year-Cycle of Kathās

> Cosmic address: a **cross-yuga, recurring observance** — the eleventh *tithi* of every lunar fortnight, sacred to Viṣṇu — whose founding legend (the birth of the goddess Ekādaśī to slay the demon **Mura**) is set in a primordial **Satya-yuga**, and whose year-cycle of stories is narrated by **Kṛṣṇa to Yudhiṣṭhira** at the close of the **Dvāpara-yuga**. The whole māhātmya corpus belongs to the late strata of the *Padma Purāṇa* (Uttara-khaṇḍa 38–63; cf. *Kriyāyogasāra* 22–23). [late text]
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · See also: [Ambarīṣa & Durvāsā — the Ekādaśī vow lived](ambarisha-durvasa.md) · [festivals & vratas](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md) · [Padma Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/padma-purana.md) · [Nārada Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/narada-purana.md) · [Hindu calendar & pañcāṅga](../00-time-and-cosmology/hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md)

**Ekādaśī** is the most widely kept Vaiṣṇava fast: abstention (classically from grains and beans, strictly from all food and water) on the eleventh *tithi* of each fortnight — **24 observances a year, 26 in a leap (adhika-māsa) year**, each with its own *vrata-kathā* recited on the day. The canonical anthology is the **Ekādaśī-māhātmya** block of the *Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa, framed as Kṛṣṇa answering Yudhiṣṭhira month by month; a parallel cycle runs in the *Kriyāyogasāra*, and the *Nārada Purāṇa* contributes the **Rukmāṅgada** frame-legend. This file is the corpus's canonical home for the founding legend and the named year-cycle, summarized in [festivals-and-vratas.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md).

---

## The frames — who tells the kathās, and to whom

- **Kṛṣṇa to Yudhiṣṭhira (the master frame)** — In the *Padma Purāṇa*'s Uttara-khaṇḍa, **Yudhiṣṭhira asks Kṛṣṇa, month by month, the name, method, and fruit of each Ekādaśī**, and Kṛṣṇa answers with each kathā — often himself citing an older telling (Brahmā to Nārada, Vasiṣṭha to Rāma), a nested purāṇic-relay structure. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa 39–63) [late text]
- **The Kriyāyogasāra cycle** — Parallel praise of the Ekādaśī fast and its rules appears in the ***Kriyāyogasāra*** (chs. 22–23), transmitted as a khaṇḍa/appendix of the *Padma Purāṇa* in the Bengal recension — the vrata's charter circulated in more than one late-purāṇic package. (*Kriyāyogasāra* 22–23) [late text]
- **The Nārada Purāṇa's Rukmāṅgada frame** — The *Nārada Purāṇa*'s Uttarabhāga embeds the **Rukmāṅgada-carita**, the most elaborate single Ekādaśī legend (below), plus general vrata-praise declaring the Ekādaśī fast supreme among observances. → fuller text-map in [narada-purana.md](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/narada-purana.md). (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga) [late text]

## Utpannā — the founding legend: Ekādaśī born from Viṣṇu to slay Mura

- **The demon Mura** — In Satya-yuga the asura **Mura**, of the daitya line ruling from the city **Candrāvatī**, conquers the devas and drives Indra and the gods from heaven; they flee to Viṣṇu for refuge. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa 38) [late text]
- **Battle and the sleeping god** — Viṣṇu fights Mura through a long, inconclusive battle, then withdraws to a cave at **Badarikāśrama** and lies down in yogic sleep. Mura follows and raises his weapon to kill the sleeping god. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa 38) [late text]
- **The maiden-śakti is born** — From Viṣṇu's sleeping body springs a **radiant maiden**, his own śakti, who battles Mura and **slays him**; Viṣṇu wakes to find the demon dead. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa 38) [late text]
- **The boon and the name** — Pleased, Viṣṇu grants the maiden a boon; she asks to become **the most beloved of his days**, destroying the sins of all who fast on her *tithi*. Because she **arose (utpannā)** on the eleventh day of the dark fortnight of **Mārgaśīrṣa**, she is named **Ekādaśī**, and that first Ekādaśī is **Utpannā Ekādaśī** — the aetiology of the whole fast. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa 38) [late text]
  - *Variant (grain-taboo aetiology, folk/vrata-kathā retellings):* the sin expelled from Mura (or sin personified) is said to take refuge **in grains** on Ekādaśī, whence the rule of eating no grain that day. This detail belongs to popular kathā recitation rather than the critical text. [folk]
  - *Disambiguation (the two Muras):* this Mura is **not** the Mura of the Kṛṣṇa cycle — the five-headed general of Narakāsura slain by Kṛṣṇa at Prāgjyotiṣa (whence Kṛṣṇa's name *Murāri*). The shared name invites harmonization but the corpus keeps them distinct → see the roster note in [asura-wars-and-roster.md](../08-beings-and-bestiary/asura-wars-and-roster.md). [disputed]

## The year-cycle catalog — the 24 named Ekādaśīs and their kathās

Names follow the North-Indian (pūrṇimānta) month reckoning used by the *Padma Purāṇa* cycle; the amānta scheme shifts the dark-fortnight names by one month. All entries: (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa 39–63) [late text], with variants noted.

### Mārgaśīrṣa (Nov–Dec)

- **Utpannā (kṛṣṇa-pakṣa)** — the founding legend above: the goddess Ekādaśī born from Viṣṇu at Badarikāśrama slays Mura; the fast is instituted.
- **Mokṣadā (śukla-pakṣa)** — King **Vaikhānasa** of Campakanagara dreams of his **father suffering in Naraka**; on the sage Parvata's counsel he keeps the Mokṣadā fast and transfers its merit, and his father rises to the celestial worlds — the "giver of liberation," extendable even to the dead.
  - *Variant (Gītā Jayantī):* tradition identifies this same tithi as the day **Kṛṣṇa spoke the [Bhagavad-gītā](bhagavad-gita.md)** at Kurukṣetra, so Mokṣadā doubles as **Gītā Jayantī**. [traditional]
  - *Variant (Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī, South Indian solar reckoning):* in Tamil tradition the corresponding **Mārgaḻi** Ekādaśī is **Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī**, when the northern "gate of Vaikuṇṭha" (*paramapada-vāsal*) stands open in Viṣṇu temples such as Śrīraṅgam → temple-utsava dimension in [regional-temple-festivals.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/regional-temple-festivals.md). [regional]

### Pauṣa (Dec–Jan)

- **Saphalā (kṛṣṇa)** — **Lumpaka**, the dissolute cast-off son of King Māhiṣmata, living as a forest bandit, unknowingly keeps the fast (a night awake, fruit offered at a tree's root) and is restored to virtue and kingdom — "the fruit-giver," merit accruing even to unwitting observance.
- **Putradā (śukla)** — the childless King **Suketumān** of Bhadrāvatī, wandering despondent, meets sages by a lake, keeps the fast on their instruction, and gains the heroic son he lacked — the "son-giver."

### Māgha (Jan–Feb)

- **Ṣaṭtilā (kṛṣṇa)** — the Ekādaśī of **sesame six ways** (*ṣaṭ-tila*: bathing, anointing, fire-offering, offering to the dead, giving, eating); its kathā: a pious but **miserly brāhmaṇa woman** who gave no food-charity is reborn to an empty house, filled only by sesame-gifts on Ṣaṭtilā — charity, not bare austerity, completes the vrata.
- **Jayā (śukla)** — the gandharva singer **Mālyavān** and the dancer **Puṣpavatī**, distracted by desire while performing before Indra, are cursed to live as **piśācas** on Himālaya; the Jayā fast (a cold, hungry vigil kept unknowingly) frees them from ghost-hood.

### Phālguna (Feb–Mar)

- **Vijayā (kṛṣṇa)** — narrated as told to **Rāma**: standing before the impassable ocean on the march to Laṅkā, Rāma is counselled (the kathā names the sage **Vakadālbhya**) to keep the Vijayā fast, by whose merit the crossing and the **victory (vijaya) over Rāvaṇa** are won — the fast for success in dire undertakings.
- **Āmalakī (śukla)** — King **Citraratha**'s realm keeps the fast with a vigil around the **āmalakī (Indian gooseberry) tree**, dear to Viṣṇu; a starving **hunter** who joins the vigil for the leftovers is reborn a king (Vasuratha) by that accidental merit — charter of āmalakī-worship → the tree's cult in [sacred flora](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/sacred-flora-and-fauna.md).

### Caitra (Mar–Apr)

- **Pāpamocanī (kṛṣṇa)** — the ascetic **Medhāvī**, seduced from his vows by the apsarā **Mañjughoṣā**, curses her to become a **piśācī**; both the cursed nymph and the fallen ascetic are cleansed by the Pāpamocanī fast prescribed by Medhāvī's father Cyavana — "she who releases from sin."
- **Kāmadā (śukla)** — the gandharva couple **Lalita and Lalitā**: Lalita falters singing before King Puṇḍarīka (his mind on his wife) and is cursed to become a **rākṣasa**; Lalitā's Kāmadā fast, made over to her husband, restores his celestial form — "the desire-granting" Ekādaśī.

### Vaiśākha (Apr–May)

- **Varūthinī (kṛṣṇa)** — a king in austerity (the kathā names **Māndhātā** in one line of telling, **Dhundhumāra** in another [disputed]) is seized by a **wild bear** that gnaws his foot; Viṣṇu slays the bear but the foot is lost, and the fast restores the king whole — "the protectress" against mutilation and misfortune.
- **Mohinī (śukla)** — narrated by **Vasiṣṭha to Rāma**: **Dhṛṣṭabuddhi**, the wastrel son of the merchant Dhanapāla of Bhadrāvatī, ruined and outcast, is directed by the sage Kauṇḍinya to the Mohinī fast and rises purified to Vaikuṇṭha. The name is calendrical lore's echo of **Mohinī**, the form Viṣṇu assumed at the amṛta-churning → [mohini-avatara.md](mohini-avatara.md). [traditional]

### Jyeṣṭha (May–Jun)

- **Aparā (kṛṣṇa)** — King **Mahīdhvaja**, murdered by his jealous brother Vajradhvaja and buried under a **peepal tree**, haunts it as a preta; the sage Śamīka's Aparā-fast merit, made over to the ghost, releases him — "the limitless" purifier of infamy.
- **Nirjalā / Bhīmasenī (śukla)** — **Bhīma**, voracious and unable to bear fasting twice a month, asks **Vyāsa** for one observance equal to all; Vyāsa prescribes this single **absolute waterless (nir-jalā) fast**, sunrise to the next sunrise, whose merit equals **all 24 Ekādaśīs of the year** — whence **Bhīmasenī Ekādaśī**, the harshest and most famous of the cycle.

### Āṣāḍha (Jun–Jul)

- **Yoginī (kṛṣṇa)** — **Hemamālī**, a yakṣa gardener of **Kubera**, lingers with his wife and fails to deliver the god's pūjā-flowers; cursed with **leprosy** and exile, he is healed by the Yoginī fast prescribed by the sage **Mārkaṇḍeya** — the fast that cures the body and the broken duty.
- **Devaśayanī / Śayanī (śukla)** — the day **Viṣṇu lies down to his four-month cosmic sleep** on Śeṣa in the ocean of milk: the start of **Cāturmāsya**, closing the marriage season and opening the monsoon's austerity months. (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa; pan-purāṇic calendar lore)
  - *Variant (regional — Āṣāḍhī Vārī):* in Maharashtra this is **Āṣāḍhī Ekādaśī**, terminus of the great **Vārkarī pilgrimage** to Viṭṭhala at Pandharpur → [vithoba-vitthal.md](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vithoba-vitthal.md). [regional]

### Śrāvaṇa (Jul–Aug)

- **Kāmikā (kṛṣṇa)** — less a tale than a proclamation: worship of Viṣṇu with **tulasī leaves** on this day outweighs bathing at all tīrthas, and the kathā promises that even one guilty of **brāhmaṇa-slaying** who keeps Kāmikā with a ghee-lamp escapes Yama's messengers — the extreme statement of the fast's sin-destroying reach (cf. the name-alone deliverance of [Ajāmila](ajamila.md)).
- **Śrāvaṇa-Putradā / Pavitrā (śukla)** — the childless King **Mahījit** of Māhiṣmatī learns from the sage Lomaśa that a past-life sin (as a merchant he drank first from a cattle-pond, denying a thirsty cow) blocks his line; the fast's merit, kept by his whole people, gives him a son — the second "son-giver" of the year.

### Bhādrapada (Aug–Sep)

- **Ajā / Annadā (kṛṣṇa)** — **Hariścandra**, the truth-king reduced to a cremation-ground slave, keeps the Ajā fast on **Gautama's** counsel; by its merit his dead son revives and kingdom, wife, and honor return — the māhātmya's re-frame of the Hariścandra ordeal as a vrata-fruit → full tale in [hariscandra.md](hariscandra.md).
- **Pārivartinī / Pārśva / Vāmana (śukla)** — mid-Cāturmāsya, the sleeping **Viṣṇu turns over (parivartana)** onto his other side; the kathā told is that of **Vāmana and Bali** — the dwarf's three steps and Viṣṇu's abiding as doorkeeper in Bali's netherworld realm → [vamana-bali.md](vamana-bali.md); hence also "Vāmana Ekādaśī."

### Āśvina (Sep–Oct)

- **Indirā (kṛṣṇa)** — in the ancestor-fortnight (Pitṛpakṣa): King **Indrasena** of Māhiṣmatī learns from Nārada that his father languishes in **Yama's realm** for a broken vow; the Indirā fast, its merit assigned to the dead, sends him to Vaikuṇṭha — the Ekādaśī of the ancestors.
- **Pāpāṅkuśā (śukla)** — "the **goad (aṅkuśa) on sin**": its kathā tells of the cruel hunter **Krodhana**, a lifetime of violence effaced when, at death's approach, the fast is kept on a sage's word — with Kāmikā, the strongest claims of retroactive purification in the cycle.

### Kārttika (Oct–Nov)

- **Ramā (kṛṣṇa)** — Princess **Candrabhāgā** (daughter of King Mucukunda) marries the frail **Śobhana**, who dies keeping the family's severe Ekādaśī rule; the fast's merit raises him as lord of a shining celestial city on **Mandarācala**, made permanent by Candrabhāgā's own vrata-merit — the fast whose fruit outlasts death.
- **Prabodhinī / Devotthāna / Devuthani (śukla)** — **Viṣṇu wakes** (*prabodha*) from the four-month sleep begun on Devaśayanī: **Cāturmāsya ends**, the wedding season opens, and the **Tulasī-vivāha** (marriage of Tulasī to Viṣṇu/śālagrāma) is performed from this day to Kārttika-pūrṇimā → charters in [jalandhara-vrinda.md](jalandhara-vrinda.md) and [shankhachuda-tulasi.md](shankhachuda-tulasi.md).
  - *Variant (regional):* **Kārttikī Ekādaśī**, the second great Pandharpur Vārī of the Vārkarīs. [regional]

### The two intercalary Ekādaśīs (adhika-māsa, "+2")

- **Padminī / Kamalā (adhika śukla)** — kept in the leap month sacred to Viṣṇu (Puruṣottama-māsa); the kathā ties it to the birth of **Kārtavīrya Arjuna**: queen **Padminī** of the Haihaya house wins by this fast the thousand-armed son no austerity had granted. [disputed — kathā details vary widely across vrata-kathā collections]
- **Paramā (adhika kṛṣṇa)** — the poor brāhmaṇa **Sumedhā** and his wife **Pavitrā**, counselled by Kauṇḍinya, keep the Paramā fast with its five-day observance and receive inexhaustible wealth — the leap-month fast "supreme (paramā)" for ending poverty.

## The Rukmāṅgada–Mohinī frame — Ekādaśī tested to the limit [late text]

- **The king who emptied hell** — King **Rukmāṅgada** of Vidiśā enforces the Ekādaśī fast throughout his realm so effectively that **Yama's hells stand empty**, and the death-god complains to Brahmā. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga — *Rukmāṅgada-carita*)
- **Mohinī the temptress** — To break the king, Brahmā creates from his own beauty the enchantress **Mohinī** (a mind-born temptress, distinct from Viṣṇu's [Mohinī-avatāra](mohini-avatara.md)); Rukmāṅgada weds her with a rash promise to grant whatever she asks. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga)
- **The impossible price** — On Ekādaśī, Mohinī invokes the promise: **eat and break the fast — or behead your son Dharmāṅgada** with your own hand. Queen **Sandhyāvalī** and Dharmāṅgada themselves urge the king to keep the fast; as the sword falls, **Viṣṇu intervenes**, and king, queen, and prince are taken bodily to Vaikuṇṭha. (*Nārada Purāṇa*, Uttarabhāga) [late text]
- **Function of the frame** — where the Padma cycle advertises the fast's *fruits*, the Rukmāṅgada legend dramatizes its *absoluteness*: the vow outweighs a son's life — the Ekādaśī counterpart to Hariścandra's truth. → text-map: [narada-purana.md](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/narada-purana.md).

## Observance, exemplars, and the two clocks

- **The rule of the fast** — no grains/beans (strictly, no food or water) through the eleventh tithi; night vigil and Viṣṇu-worship; the fast broken (*pāraṇa*) within the **Dvādaśī window** next morning — the technicality on which the [Ambarīṣa–Durvāsā](ambarisha-durvasa.md) crisis turns; calendrical mechanics in [hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md](../00-time-and-cosmology/hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md). (*Padma Purāṇa*, Uttara-khaṇḍa; dharmaśāstra digests)
- **Canonical exemplar elsewhere** — **Ambarīṣa's** year-long Ekādaśī-Dvādaśī vrata (Bhāgavata 9.4–5) → [ambarisha-durvasa.md](ambarisha-durvasa.md).
- **Scriptural clock** — the founding Utpannā legend is placed in **Satya-yuga**; Vijayā's kathā attaches to **Rāma in Tretā**; Nirjalā's institution (Bhīma–Vyāsa) and the Kṛṣṇa–Yudhiṣṭhira frame-dialogue belong to the end of **Dvāpara**; the fast itself recurs every fortnight of **Kali-yuga** practice.
- **Academic clock [scholarly]** — the *Padma Purāṇa* is composite (core perhaps 4th–10th c. CE); its **Uttara-khaṇḍa, carrying the Ekādaśī-māhātmyas, is widely judged a late, post-1000 CE stratum**, and the *Kriyāyogasāra* and the *Nārada Purāṇa*'s Rukmāṅgada material are likewise late-medieval vrata literature — documents of the **medieval Vaiṣṇava vrata economy** (merit transfer, lay fasting) rather than early purāṇic narrative. [scholarly] [late text]
