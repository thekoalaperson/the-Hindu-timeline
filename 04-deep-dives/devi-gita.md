# Devī-Gītā: The Song of the Goddess

> *Up to [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · master [index](../README.md). The foremost **Śākta** Gītā — ~507 verses across ten chapters (Skandha VII, adhyāyas 31–40) of the [Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md), in which the Goddess as **Bhuvaneśvarī / Pārvatī** instructs the mountain-king **Himavat (Himālaya)**. The Goddess-tradition's direct counterpart to the [Bhagavad Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md), set beside the [Śiva-gītā](./shiva-gita.md), [Ṛbhu-gītā](./ribhu-gita.md), and [Avadhūta-gītā](./avadhuta-gita.md) in the [lesser-Gītās catalog](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md).*

The **Devī-Gītā** ("the Song of the Goddess") is the principal **Śākta** answer to the Bhagavad Gītā: a self-contained Vedāntic discourse in which the supreme Goddess — **Bhuvaneśvarī**, the world-mother who is also **Pārvatī**, daughter-to-be of the speaker — reveals herself as the one **Brahman**, expounds *māyā*, the three yogas of *jñāna*, *karma*, and *bhakti*, the eight-limbed *aṣṭāṅga-yoga*, grants a **Virāṭ (cosmic) theophany**, enumerates her sacred seats (*pīṭhas*), and prescribes the modes of her worship. It is embedded in the **Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Skandha VII (chs. 31–40)** but, like its Śaiva and Vaiṣṇava siblings, circulates **independently** as a recited and commented scripture in its own right. [scholarly] [Śākta]

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural time** — *In-narrative* the discourse is **cross-yuga**: it is framed as primordial *brahma-vidyā* spoken at the dawn of the world to **Himavat** (the personified Himālaya) and the assembled gods, before the Goddess takes birth from him as **Pārvatī/Satī** to wed Śiva. It is recited within the Purāṇic frame (Vyāsa to King Janamejaya; the Sūta to the sages of Naimiṣa) and claims no single yuga of "occurrence." [scriptural]
- **Manuscript / academic time** — *Text-critically* the Devī-Bhāgavata, and the Devī-Gītā within it, is a **late Śākta composition** (received form usually placed c. 11th–13th century CE, with later strata), modelled deliberately on the Bhagavad Gītā and the Devī-Māhātmya, drawing on mature Advaita and Tantric vocabulary. Keep the primeval Himavat setting distinct from this manuscript dating. [scholarly] [disputed — dating]

## Textual placement & frame

- **Location** — The Devī-Gītā occupies the **last ten chapters of Skandha (Book) VII** of the [Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md), conventionally **adhyāyas 31–40**, totalling roughly **507 verses**. (Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa VII.31–40)
  - *Variant (count):* the ten-chapter, ~500-verse span is standard; precise verse totals vary slightly by recension and are commonly cited as **c. 500–520**. [scholarly] [disputed — recension]
- **The narrative occasion** — After the slaying of the buffalo-demon and the cosmos's longing for the Goddess, the gods and **Himavat** propitiate her; pleased, the **Devī** manifests in dazzling form and Himavat asks her to reveal her true nature and the means of liberation — the Śākta mirror of Arjuna's questioning. (Devī-Bhāgavata VII.31–32)
- **Speaker and pupil** — The teacher is the Goddess as **Bhuvaneśvarī**, the supreme *Mahāmāyā*; the pupil is **Himavat**, king of mountains, who will become her father when she is born as **Pārvatī**. The intimacy of the frame (the Absolute about to become the questioner's own daughter) is distinctive to this Gītā. (Devī-Bhāgavata VII.31)
- **Reported frame** — Like the rest of the Purāṇa, the dialogue reaches the reader through **Vyāsa narrating to Janamejaya**, and beyond that the **Sūta to the Naimiṣa sages**. (Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa)

## The teaching — chapter movement

- **The Devī's self-disclosure as Brahman** — She declares that before creation she alone existed as the one, non-dual **Brahman / Cit-śakti**; from her arose *māyā* and the manifest world. The supreme reality is **feminine** — the Goddess *is* the Absolute, not a consort of it. Strong **non-dual (Advaita) identity-knowledge** cast in Śākta theology. (Devī-Gītā / DBhP VII.32)
- **Māyā and the unfolding of the world** — She expounds **māyā** as her own power: neither real nor unreal, by which the one appears as many; the *jīva*, the elements, and the cosmos evolve through her *prakṛti*, blending **Sāṃkhya** categories with Vedāntic non-duality. (DBhP VII.32–33)
- **The Virāṭ / cosmic theophany** — At Himavat's request the Goddess grants **divine sight** and reveals her **all-pervading universal form (Virāṭ-rūpa)** — the worlds, gods, and beings contained within her body — the **Śākta answer to Bhagavad Gītā ch. 11** and to the Śiva-gītā's *viśvarūpa*. The onlookers swoon and praise her. (DBhP VII.33–34)
- **The three yogas — jñāna, karma, bhakti** — She lays out the converging paths: **jñāna-yoga** (knowledge of the Self/Goddess identity as the direct cause of release), **karma-yoga** (desireless action and ritual offered to her), and **bhakti-yoga** (loving devotion), distinguishing *parā* (supreme) from *gauṇī* (preparatory) devotion. The graded synthesis closely tracks the Bhagavad Gītā's own three-fold scheme. (DBhP VII.35–37)
  - *Variant (emphasis):* the Devī-Gītā gives **supreme bhakti** to the Goddess the crowning place while insisting it ripens into liberating *jñāna* — devotion and knowledge as a single graded path, not rivals. [scholarly]
- **Aṣṭāṅga-yoga** — She teaches the **eight limbs** of yoga (*yama, niyama, āsana, prāṇāyāma, pratyāhāra, dhāraṇā, dhyāna, samādhi*), together with meditation on the Goddess, *nāḍī*/*cakra* physiology, and the rousing of **kuṇḍalinī** — the Tantric layer that marks this as a Śākta text. (DBhP VII.35)
- **Modes of worship — outer and inner** — She prescribes both **external (bāhya/vaidika–tāntrika) pūjā** — image-worship, *mantra*, *yantra* (the Śrī-cakra), *dīkṣā*, and the offering of the *navāvaraṇa* — and **internal (mānasa) worship**, culminating in worship of the Goddess as one's own Self. She also commends pilgrimage, festivals (notably the autumn **Navarātri / Durgā-pūjā**), and the building of her temples. (DBhP VII.38–39)

## The pīṭhas and Śakti-pīṭhas

- **Enumeration of sacred seats** — Among the Devī-Gītā's signal contents is a listing of the Goddess's holy **pīṭhas / kṣetras** — the places where she is to be worshipped under her many local names — woven into the worship-instruction as the geography of her presence on earth. (DBhP VII.38)
  - *Variant (lists):* the Devī-Bhāgavata's pīṭha-roster overlaps with, but is not identical to, the classic **fifty-one (or 108) Śakti-pīṭhas** of the [Satī-dismemberment myth](./dattatreya.md); different Tantras and Purāṇas give divergent counts and locations, and the Devī-Gītā's list is one such tradition rather than a fixed canon. [regional] [disputed]
- **Theological point** — The pīṭhas concretise the Gītā's metaphysics: the one formless **Cit-śakti** is simultaneously immanent across a sacred landscape, so that local Goddess-worship and supreme non-dual realisation are continuous, not opposed. [scholarly]

## Doctrine & affiliations

- **Śākta Advaita core** — The metaphysics is firmly **non-dualist with a feminine Absolute**: one **Brahman = Mahādevī**, the world as her *māyā*-appearance, *jīva*–Brahman (Goddess) identity, liberation by knowledge ripened through devotion. It is the keystone text of **Śākta Vedānta**. [scholarly]
- **Tantric–Vedic synthesis** — It harmonises **Vedic** ritual idiom with **Tantric** practice (Śrī-vidyā, the Śrī-cakra, kuṇḍalinī, *mantra-dīkṣā*), presenting Goddess-tantra as orthodox *brahma-vidyā* — a programmatic blending characteristic of the Devī-Bhāgavata. [scholarly]
- **Counterpart to the Bhagavad Gītā** — Its three-yoga structure, its *māyā* doctrine, and especially its **cosmic theophany** are conscious Śākta reworkings of the Bhagavad Gītā's templates, with the **Goddess** in Kṛṣṇa's place and **Himavat** in Arjuna's. This makes it, alongside the [Śiva-gītā](./shiva-gita.md), the closest structural twin of the Bhagavad Gītā among the Purāṇic Gītās. [scholarly]

## Place among the "great Gītās"

- **First-tier non-Mahābhārata Gītā** — On the standard *gītā-saṅgraha* rosters the Devī-Gītā stands with the **Śiva-Gītā** (Padma Purāṇa), **Gaṇeśa-Gītā** (Gaṇeśa Purāṇa), and **Rāma-Gītā** (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa) as the principal **Purāṇic** Gītās — one sectarian counterpart to the Bhagavad Gītā per major deity-stream, here the **Śākta** stream. (see [lesser Gītās catalog → Purāṇic Gītās](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md#purāṇic--independent-gītās-the-path-to-60--pointers-only)) [folk] [scholarly]
- **Independent circulation** — Like the [Ṛbhu-gītā](./ribhu-gita.md) and the Śiva- and Rāma-Gītās, the Devī-Gītā is printed, translated, commented, and recited **detached from its parent Purāṇa**, and is the best-known short Goddess-scripture after the **Devī-Māhātmya (Durgā Saptaśatī)**. [scholarly]
- **Relation to the Devī-Māhātmya** — Where the **Devī-Māhātmya** is **narrative and martial** (the slaying of Mahiṣa, Śumbha, and Niśumbha), the Devī-Gītā is its **philosophical sequel-in-spirit**: the same supreme Goddess, now expounding the *vidyā* behind the deeds. The Devī-Bhāgavata frames the Gītā precisely after recalling those victories. [scholarly]
