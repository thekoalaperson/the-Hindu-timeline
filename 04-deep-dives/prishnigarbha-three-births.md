# Pṛśnigarbha — and the Three Births of Viṣṇu in the Vasudeva–Devakī Line

> *Where this sits: the **prior-birth frame** of the Kṛṣṇa-avatāra — Viṣṇu's three successive descents into one and the same soul-couple, of which the third is [Kṛṣṇa](./krishna-lila.md) at the close of the [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga. Pṛśnigarbha is the first of the three and the theological key to why Kṛṣṇa is born to that exact couple.*
>
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · See also: [Vāmana, Bali & Trivikrama](./vamana-bali.md) · [Kṛṣṇa's Infancy Līlā](./krishna-balya-lila.md) · [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)

**Pṛśnigarbha** ("the one [carried] in the womb of Pṛśni," *pṛśni-garbha*) is the name under which the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* frames the entire Kṛṣṇa descent: at the moment of his birth, the four-armed Viṣṇu reveals to Devakī that **this is the third time** he has taken birth from her and Vasudeva. In two prior cosmic ages the same soul-pair were the ascetic couple **Sutapas and Pṛśni**, and then **Kaśyapa and Aditi** — and to each, in turn, Viṣṇu was born as their son. The first of these sons was Pṛśnigarbha; the second was [Vāmana](./vamana-bali.md); the third is Kṛṣṇa. The frame answers a question the Kaṃsa-narrative leaves open — *why this couple?* — by making Vasudeva and Devakī not a one-time accident of history but the recurring parents of God across three births, won by penance in their earliest existence. This is a **cross-yuga / cross-Manvantara** device: the three births do not sit in one age but span the cosmic calendar, which is exactly why it belongs to the "two clocks" (scriptural cosmic time, not historical time) reading of the avatāra.

## The revelation scene (SB 10.3.32–43)

- **The four-armed birth** — In the prison of Mathurā, Devakī gives birth not to an ordinary infant but to Viṣṇu in full majesty: four arms bearing **śaṅkha, cakra, gadā, padma**, dark as a rain-cloud, garlanded with the Kaustubha and Śrīvatsa (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.3.9–10.3.13*). Vasudeva and Devakī offer praise; then the Lord, before shrinking to infant form, explains who he is and why he has come to *them*.
- **"You were Pṛśni and Sutapas"** — The Lord tells the couple that in a former age (in the **Svāyambhuva Manvantara**, per the Purāṇa's frame) Vasudeva was the Prajāpati **Sutapas** and Devakī was his wife **Pṛśni**, both commanded by Brahmā to generate offspring and instead undertaking severe austerity (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.3.32–10.3.33*).
- **Twelve-thousand-year penance** — Sutapas and Pṛśni performed **tapas** for twelve thousand celestial years, enduring the seasons, living on air and on the senses withdrawn, until Viṣṇu, pleased, appeared before them in his four-armed form (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.3.34–10.3.36*).
- **The boon — and the catch** — Asked to choose a boon, the couple, captivated by the Lord's beauty, could think of nothing higher than *a son exactly like you*. Because there exists no one **equal** to the Supreme, the Lord grants the only possible fulfilment of so literal a wish: he will himself be born as their son — and will do so **three times** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.3.37–10.3.40*).
- **First birth — Pṛśnigarbha** — In that first age the Lord was accordingly born to them and became **renowned by the name Pṛśnigarbha**, "he-of-Pṛśni's-womb" (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.3.41*).
- **Second birth — Upendra / Vāmana** — In the next age the same couple were reborn as **Kaśyapa and Aditi**, and to them the Lord descended as the dwarf-brahmin **[Vāmana](./vamana-bali.md)** (also titled **Upendra**, "younger brother of Indra"), the fifth of the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.3.41–10.3.42*).
- **Third birth — Kṛṣṇa** — Now, a third time, the same souls are **Vasudeva and Devakī**, and the Lord keeps the third instalment of his old promise by being born as Kṛṣṇa — which is the explanation he gives Devakī for the four-armed self-disclosure (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.3.43*).
- **The instruction to forget** — Having explained the lineage of his births, the Lord directs the couple to treat him as an ordinary child and to carry him across the Yamunā to Gokula — so the *bālya-līlā* may unfold "humanly" despite this cosmic preface (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.3.44–47*; continued in the [infancy līlā](./krishna-balya-lila.md)).

## The prior penance of Aditi (the second-birth backstory, SB 6.18)

The Pṛśnigarbha frame compresses the *second* birth into a single verse, but the Bhāgavata elsewhere tells the matching backstory of **Aditi and Kaśyapa** at length — the penance that earns the **Vāmana** descent.

- **The Payovrata of Aditi** — When Bali's Asuras had dispossessed the Devas, **Aditi**, mother of the gods, undertook on Kaśyapa's instruction the **Payo-vrata** (milk-vow) austerity to Viṣṇu, that he be born as her son and restore her children (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 6.18 → 8.16–8.17*).
- **Viṣṇu's promise to descend** — Pleased, Viṣṇu agrees to take birth from Aditi by Kaśyapa as a portion of himself — fulfilled as **Vāmana**; this is the *same* event the 10.3 frame calls the couple's "second birth," seen from the other end (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.17; 10.3.41–42*).

## Variants & parallels

The "three births" device is distinctively the *Bhāgavata*'s; other texts narrate the same descents but frame the recurrence differently.

- *Identity of the soul-couple (Bhāgavata):* The three lives are one and the same pair of souls — Sutapas/Pṛśni, Kaśyapa/Aditi, Vasudeva/Devakī — a continuous identity across ages (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.3.32–43*).
- *Manvantara placement (Bhāgavata commentaries):* The Sutapas–Pṛśni birth is set in the **Svāyambhuva Manvantara**; the Kaśyapa–Aditi/Vāmana birth in the present **[Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)** (Vāmana being a Vaivasvata-era avatāra), and Kṛṣṇa at its 28th Dvāpara — so the three are explicitly **cross-Manvantara**. [scholarly — commentarial framing]
- *Hari-vaṃśa / Viṣṇu Purāṇa emphasis:* The epic-Purāṇic stream foregrounds the **Vasudeva–Devakī** birth and the Kaṃsa conflict and does **not** build the three-prior-births schema around Pṛśnigarbha; the recurring-couple motif is largely a Bhāgavata theological refinement. [scholarly]
- *Brahmavaivarta parallel:* The Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa develops its own elaborate Kṛṣṇa-as-Para-Brahman framing (Golokā, Rādhā, the prior-history of the divine couple) — a *parallel* theology of Kṛṣṇa's eternal precedence rather than the Bhāgavata's three-tapas-births chain. [late text / sectarian]
- *Name as epithet vs. distinct avatāra:* Tradition reads **Pṛśnigarbha** primarily as a **name of that first birth of Viṣṇu** (an *aṃśa* / *līlā-avatāra*), not as a separate item in the standard Daśāvatāra list; he is invoked among Viṣṇu's many forms but does not displace Vāmana or Kṛṣṇa in the canonical ten. [scholarly]
- *Constellation / rain-cloud reading (late, disputed):* A minority gloss links **Pṛśni** to the Vedic *pṛśni* ("dappled, speckled" — the storm-cloud cow and the Maruts' mother in the Ṛgveda), reading the womb-of-Pṛśni as the rain-cloud from which the dark Lord descends — a poetic, not narrative, identification. [folk / late gloss] [disputed]

## Why the frame matters

- **It answers "why this couple."** Without the three-births preface, Vasudeva and Devakī are merely Kṛṣṇa's biological parents trapped by Kaṃsa. With it, they are the **chosen recurring parents of God**, who earned the privilege by twelve-thousand-year penance in their first life — turning an accident of dynastic history into the payoff of ancient *tapas* (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.3.37–43*).
- **It ranks devotion above cosmic power.** The boon Sutapas and Pṛśni ask for is not liberation or lordship but *a son like Viṣṇu* — the **vātsalya** (parental love of God) that the [infancy līlā](./krishna-balya-lila.md) then dramatises; Kṛṣṇa's whole childhood becomes the fruit of a parent's wish (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.3.39–40*).
- **It binds the avatāras together.** By making **Vāmana** and **Kṛṣṇa** births of one soul-couple, the Bhāgavata stitches two of the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) into a single multi-life arc, tying the [Vāmana–Bali](./vamana-bali.md) episode and the Mathurā birth into one continuous divine biography (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 6.18, 8.17, 10.3.32–43*).
- **It is a "cosmic-clock" statement.** The three births span ages, not years; the device coheres only on the scriptural cosmic timescale (Manvantaras and Yugas) and is meaningless on a flat historical timeline — a clean instance of the two-clocks distinction. [scholarly]

## Sources

**Primary scripture**
- *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (Śrīmad Bhāgavatam), **Canto 10, Chapter 3, verses 32–43** — Viṣṇu reveals to Devakī the three births: Sutapas & Pṛśni → **Pṛśnigarbha**; Kaśyapa & Aditi → **Vāmana / Upendra**; then **Kṛṣṇa** (the *locus classicus* of the whole frame).
- *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* **6.18** and **8.16–8.17** — Aditi's **Payo-vrata** and Kaśyapa's instruction, the penance that earns the **Vāmana** (second) birth; the matching backstory to 10.3's compressed second-birth verse.
- *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* **1.3** — the enumeration of līlā-avatāras within which the Kṛṣṇa descent is placed (context for Pṛśnigarbha as an *aṃśa*/līlā form).
- *Hari-vaṃśa* and *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* (Book 5) — the parallel Vasudeva–Devakī birth narrative that does **not** carry the three-prior-births schema (contrast source).
- *Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa*, **Kṛṣṇa-janma Khaṇḍa** — the parallel (sectarian) theology of Kṛṣṇa's eternal precedence and the divine couple (contrast source). [late text]
- *Ṛgveda* — *pṛśni* ("dappled/speckled," the cloud-cow, mother of the Maruts), background for the late constellation/rain-cloud gloss on the name "Pṛśni." [philological]
