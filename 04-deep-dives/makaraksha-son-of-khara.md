# Makarākṣa, son of Khara — the vengeance duel of the Laṅkā war

> Cosmic address: [Tretā Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) of the current 28th Mahāyuga (Vaivasvata Manvantara) → the Rāma-avatāra cycle → the siege of Laṅkā, one of its minor-champion duels. Rāma is the 7th [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) of the [Sūryavaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Sits inside the [Yuddha (Laṅkā) Kāṇḍa](ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md); the backstory it avenges is in the [Araṇya Kāṇḍa](ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md).

A short, self-contained two-sarga episode of Vālmīki's **Yuddha Kāṇḍa** (vulgate sargas **78–79**) that the standard Laṅkā-war summaries usually name only in passing. After Rāvaṇa has lost Kumbhakarṇa and a string of sons, he sends out **Makarākṣa**, the son of the demon **Khara** whom Rāma had slain years earlier at Janasthāna. The son marches out expressly to avenge his father; the episode is structured as a *vengeance duel* — a demon carrying a personal, generational grievance meets the very archer who created it — and it closes with Rāma invoking the **Āgneyāstra**, the fire-weapon, to burn him down. It is one of the clearest "the past comes to collect" beats in the war-book, tying the climax at Laṅkā back to the forest killing that first set Rāvaṇa against Rāma.

## Who Makarākṣa is — name and lineage

- **The name** — *Makarākṣa* (मकराक्ष) = "makara-eyed," from *makara* (the crocodile/sea-monster, the vāhana of Varuṇa and Gaṅgā) + *akṣa* ("eye"); rendered "crocodile-eyed" or "shark-eyed," a name evoking a cold, predatory gaze (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 78).
- **Son of Khara** — his father is **Khara**, the rākṣasa overlord of **Janasthāna** in the Daṇḍaka forest, brother of Rāvaṇa and of Śūrpaṇakhā, who commanded the fourteen-thousand-strong demon colony (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 78; backstory in [Araṇya Kāṇḍa](ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md)).
- **Rāvaṇa's nephew** — since Khara is Vālmīki's brother-figure to Rāvaṇa (a son of the sage Viśravas in the rākṣasa line), Makarākṣa is Rāvaṇa's **nephew**, fighting for both king and kin — a blood-claim on the war that most of Rāvaṇa's other champions lack.
- **The wound he inherits** — in the [Araṇya Kāṇḍa](ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md), Rāma alone destroyed Khara, his generals **Dūṣaṇa** and **Triśiras**, and the entire host of fourteen thousand at Janasthāna after Lakṣmaṇa mutilated Śūrpaṇakhā (Vālmīki, Araṇya 28–30). That single-handed slaughter is the "debt" Makarākṣa comes to Laṅkā's field to repay; the episode presumes the reader knows it.

## The commission — Rāvaṇa dispatches him (sarga 78)

- **Rāvaṇa's mounting losses** — by this point in the vulgate sequence Rāvaṇa has already lost his colossal brother Kumbhakarṇa and sons/kin such as Narāntaka, Devāntaka, Triśiras, Atikāya and others; grief-struck and short of commanders, he casts about his court for a champion (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 78).
- **The king's charge** — Rāvaṇa summons Makarākṣa and orders him to take an army, go out against Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, and destroy them (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 78).
- **The son's eager vow** — Makarākṣa answers with delight, boasting that he will kill both Rāghavas that very day and thereby avenge his father Khara; he takes it not as a duty but as a long-awaited chance (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 78). The joy-before-the-fall framing is the standard Vālmīki cue that the boaster is doomed.
- **The vengeance motive made explicit** — unlike the generic "kill the invaders" orders given to earlier generals, Makarākṣa's departure is voiced as *personal* revenge for Janasthāna, which is what marks this episode out and gives it its name in tradition — "the son of Khara" rather than merely "another rākṣasa slain."

## The march and its omens (sarga 78)

- **The sortie from the gate** — Makarākṣa mounts his chariot and rides out of Laṅkā at the head of a rākṣasa force to the roar of war-drums and conches (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 78).
- **Ill omens attend him** — as with Khara's own march in the Araṇya Kāṇḍa and every doomed rākṣasa advance in the epic, evil portents appear on the way — a formulaic Vālmīki signal that the campaign is fated to fail (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 78).
- **The armies clash** — the rākṣasa host and the vānara army join battle with the usual carnage of thrown boulders, trees, arrows and maces before the two principals find each other (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 78).

## The vengeance-duel with Rāma (sargas 78–79)

- **The face-off** — Makarākṣa drives at Rāma and, before fighting, hurls a challenge, reminding him that he killed his father **Khara** in the Daṇḍaka forest; he declares he has nursed that anger and will now settle the score, calling Rāma to single combat (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 78–79).
- **Rāma's retort** — Rāma answers with a warrior's taunt, recalling that at Janasthāna he had slain Khara, Dūṣaṇa, Triśiras and fourteen thousand rākṣasas single-handed, and that Makarākṣa — "of evil deeds" — will go the same road as his father; the exchange of vaunts (the *vāg-yuddha*, war of words) precedes the arrow-duel in classic epic form (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 79).
- **The archery duel** — the two loose showers of arrows; the sky is darkened with shafts, each parrying and wounding the other in the extended arrow-exchange that Vālmīki lavishes on his set-piece single combats (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 79).
- **Chariot destroyed, fight on foot** — Rāma shatters Makarākṣa's chariot and cuts down his horses and charioteer, forcing the demon to the ground to continue the fight — the same reduction Rāma had inflicted on Khara before the kill (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 79).
- **The trident (śūla)** — dismounted and enraged, Makarākṣa takes up a great **trident/pike (śūla)**, blazing and garlanded, and flings it at Rāma; Rāma meets it in mid-air and **breaks it to pieces with his arrows** before it can strike (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 79).
  - *Note (the weapon's provenance):* Vālmīki's vulgate stresses the śūla's terror and radiance; later retellings sometimes gloss it as a boon-weapon, but the base text simply gives it as Makarākṣa's own great pike, shattered in flight — no divine grantor is named, so none should be invented.

## The Āgneyāstra and his death (sarga 79)

- **Rāma invokes the fire-weapon** — with the trident destroyed and Makarākṣa still defiant, Rāma fixes the **Āgneyāstra** — the missile presided over by **Agni**, the fire-god — to his bow (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 79). See the class of such weapons in [Divyāstras & Divine Weapons](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md).
- **The kill** — the fiery arrow pierces Makarākṣa through the chest and lays him dead on the field, the son of Khara falling as his father had fallen to Rāma's celestial shafts (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 79).
- **The rout** — with their commander slain, the surviving rākṣasas break and flee back into Laṅkā, as the vānaras exult; the episode closes and the narrative moves on (in the vulgate) to Indrajit's next sorcery — the illusory Māyā-Sītā at sarga 80 (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 79–80).
- **The thematic seal** — the vengeance is inverted: the son who came to avenge Khara instead **repeats** Khara's death, weapon for weapon (chariot broken, cast weapon shattered, celestial arrow to the body). The episode functions as a deliberate echo, closing the Janasthāna account inside the Laṅkā war.

## Placement, recension and the two clocks

- **Where it sits in the war-book** — in the **vulgate** (the widely-printed Southern/commentarial text), the Makarākṣa episode is sargas **78–79**, falling *after* the deaths of Kumbhakarṇa and Rāvaṇa's sons and *just before* Indrajit conjures the Māyā-Sītā (sarga 80) and completes his Nikumbhilā rite (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 78–80).
- **Recension caveat** — sarga numbers in the Yuddha Kāṇḍa **differ by recension**; the vulgate is longer than the **Critical Edition** (Baroda), whose numbering runs ahead differently, so "sargas 78–79" is a vulgate address and may map to neighbouring numbers elsewhere. [scholarly] Minor-champion duels of this kind are among the passages whose length and placement vary most across manuscripts. Cite at the *Yuddha Kāṇḍa* level when the exact sarga is uncertain.
- **The scriptural clock** — within the cyclic frame the whole Rāma-avatāra, and this duel with it, belongs to the **Tretā Yuga** of the 28th Mahāyuga of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara — long before the present Kali Yuga (which began 3102 BCE by the traditional reckoning).
- **The historical clock** — [scholarly] the Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa* as a text is generally dated to a core c. **5th–4th century BCE**, redacted over centuries into roughly the **1st–2nd century CE**; the battle-book's serial single combats (Makarākṣa among them) are the sort of episodic material scholars see as expanded during that long transmission. The two datings are kept distinct and never merged.

## Variants and retellings

- **A "named but skipped" episode** — because Makarākṣa is a one-duel figure, most abridged and devotional retellings compress or omit him, listing only the marquee deaths (Kumbhakarṇa, Indrajit, Rāvaṇa). He is a Vālmīki-canon episode, not a folk accretion.
- **Tulsīdās, *Rāmcaritmānas* (Avadhi, 16th c.)** — the *Laṅkākāṇḍa* of the bhakti retelling telescopes the rākṣasa champions heavily and does not give Makarākṣa a developed scene; the vengeance-for-Khara beat survives, if at all, only as part of the general slaughter of Rāvaṇa's kin. [regional]
- **Kamban's *Irāmāvatāram* (Tamil, 12th c.)** — the Tamil *Yuddha/Pōr Kāṇṭam* likewise foregrounds the great duels; minor champions such as Makarākṣa receive far less amplification than Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa and Indrajit. [regional]
- **Weapon-name slippage** — some translations render Rāma's killing shaft simply as a "flaming arrow" or "arrow of fire" rather than naming the **Āgneyāstra** as such; the underlying detail (an Agni-charged missile ending Makarākṣa) is constant. [scholarly]
- **Not to be confused** — Makarākṣa is distinct from *Makara* the sea-creature/emblem, from the *makara-toraṇa* motif, and from other "-akṣa" rākṣasas (e.g. Virūpākṣa, another of Rāvaṇa's warriors killed in the same war). He is defined solely as *Khara's son*.
