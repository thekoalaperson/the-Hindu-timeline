# Mohini: Vishnu's Enchantress Form

> Cosmic address: **Mohinī** (मोहिनी, "the enchantress/deluder," from √*muh*, "to bewilder") — **Viṣṇu's only female avatāra** and the **13th** descent in the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.3 enumeration of 22 līlā-avatars (1.3.17, between Dhanvantari and Narasiṃha). She is a manifestation of Viṣṇu's **māyā** taking female shape to disarm the asuras, principally at the [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md) in the prior Cākṣuṣa Manvantara, with a later (regional) appearance against [Bhasmāsura](bhasmasura-and-vrikasura.md). Primary scripture: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.8.36–8.9.29 and 8.12; *Mahābhārata* 1.16.38–1.17.9 (Ādi Parva, Āstīka section); shorter or divergent tellings in the *Viṣṇu*, *Agni*, *Brahmāṇḍa* and *Śiva Purāṇas*.
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · See also: [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md) · [Bhasmāsura & Vṛkāsura](bhasmasura-and-vrikasura.md) · [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)

**Mohinī** is the form Viṣṇu assumes when **delusion, not force, is the instrument of dharma**. Unique among the avatāras as the Lord's only female embodiment, she appears wherever an asura's strength or a boon makes a frontal assault futile — and undoes him by enchantment. This file consolidates the avatar-level treatment: her canonical origin distributing the **amṛta**, the **Rāhu / Svarbhānu** beheading, the **Bhasmāsura** trap and its dance-drama afterlife, the **Śiva–Mohinī** encounter and the sons later traditions give it (**Śāstā / Ayyappa**, **Hanumān**), the asura names attached to the distribution scene, her iconography, and the *mohinī*-vs-*māyā* theology of "deliverance by illusion." The episode-internal narratives are told in full at [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md) and [Bhasmāsura & Vṛkāsura](bhasmasura-and-vrikasura.md); this node draws the threads together without duplicating them.

## A. Origin — Mohinī at the Churning (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.8–8.9)

- **The crisis** — When **Dhanvantari** rises from the churned ocean bearing the pot (*kalaśa*) of **amṛta**, the **asuras snatch it by force** (8.8.31–35). The devas, dejected, take refuge in Hari, who promises to gain their end "by my own māyā" (*sādhayiṣye sva-māyayā*) (8.8.36–37). The asuras fall to quarrelling — "me first, not you" — while the weaker among them argue that the devas, who shared the labour, deserve a share (8.8.38–40). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.8.31–40) — the churning itself, with Viṣṇu as [Kūrma](kurma-avatara.md), is told at [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md).
- **Viṣṇu becomes Mohinī** — Viṣṇu assumes "an indescribable, supremely wondrous woman's form" (*yoṣid-rūpam anirdeśyaṃ … paramādbhutam*) — dark as a blue lotus (*utpala-śyāma*), jasmine in her hair, girdle and anklets jingling, kindling lust in the daitya chiefs (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.8.41–46; numbered 8.8.42 ff. in the GRETIL text). The asuras, seeing her approach, beg her to divide the nectar fairly among the sons of Kaśyapa (8.9.1–7). She laughs that they should not trust a wanton woman (*puṃścalī*) (8.9.9–10); reassured, they **hand her the pot** (8.9.11), and she takes it on condition that they accept whatever she does, "fair or unfair" (*sādhv asādhu vā*) (8.9.12–13). The name "Mohinī-mūrti" in the BBT translation of these verses is the translator's; the Sanskrit says *yoṣid-rūpa* / *māyā-yoṣid-vapus* (8.9.8). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.8.41–8.9.13)
- **The unfair distribution** — Both sides fast, bathe, make offerings and sit on kuśa facing east; she enters, pot in hand (*kalasa-pāṇiḥ*) (8.9.14–18). Judging that giving nectar to asuras, cruel by birth, would be as ill-advised as with snakes (*sarpāṇām iva durnayam*; "as dangerous as supplying milk to a snake" is the BBT translator's expansion), Viṣṇu seats devas and asuras in **separate rows** (*pṛthak paṅktīḥ*) (8.9.19–20), beguiles the asuras with blandishments and serves the **amṛta only to the devas** (8.9.21); the asuras keep silent, bound by their promise and by their infatuation (8.9.22–23). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.9.14–23)
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata 1.16.38–1.17.9, critical ed.; Ganguli Sections XVIII–XIX):* the earliest extant full telling is brief. As the dānavas clamour "it is mine," Nārāyaṇa, "resorting to the deluding māyā" (*māyām āsthito mohinīm*), "made a wondrous woman's form" (*strīrūpam adbhutaṃ kṛtvā*) and approached them; deluded, they **gave her the amṛta** (1.16.38–40). The constituted text has **no rows and no pact** (a passage found only in part of the manuscript tradition, CE 1.16.40 app. \*278, adds that "Nārāyaṇa's māyā," holding the kamaṇḍalu, served the devas while the daityas sat in a row, and gave the daityas none): Viṣṇu, **with Nara**, carries the amṛta off from the dānava chiefs as they arm for battle, and the devas drink it from him in the tumult (1.17.1–3). After Rāhu's beheading he **casts off the female form** and the war begins on the shore of the salt sea, won by Nara and Nārāyaṇa with the Sudarśana (1.17.9–28). [scholarly] (for "earliest")
  - *Variant (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, vulgate/Southern recension, Bāla Kāṇḍa 1.45.42):* Viśvāmitra's telling of the churning gives the ruse **one verse** — with everything wading into ruin, Viṣṇu "swiftly carried off the amṛta, resorting to the deluding māyā" (*amṛtaṃ so 'harat tūrṇaṃ māyām āsthāya mohinīm*). The verse does not itself say "woman"; the female form is read into it from the Mahābhārata and Purāṇic tellings (the valmikiramayan.net rendering "Mohini's illusory power" is a translator's gloss). The verse is **absent from the constituted text of the Baroda critical edition**, where the amṛta arises (1.44.24) and the kin-war follows directly (1.44.25–27). [interpolation] (critical apparatus not checked)
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.9.107–109):* a one-verse version — the daityas, "headed by **Vipracitti**" (1.9.107; GRETIL prints *vipracittirurogamāḥ*, evidently for *-purogamāḥ*), seize the kamaṇḍalu from Dhanvantari's hand; Viṣṇu, "standing in a woman's form" (*strīrūpa-saṃsthitaḥ*), deludes them by māyā, takes it and gives it to the devas, who drink and rout the daityas to Pātāla (1.9.110–111). No rows, and no Rāhu episode at this point.
  - *Variant (Agni Purāṇa 3.12–16):* the daityas, led by **Jambha** ("Jambha and the others"; GRETIL prints *janmādyāḥ*, read *jambhādyāḥ* by Mani s.v. Jambha), take the amṛta from Dhanvantari's hand — the half-line adds "having given half to the suras" (*surebhyo 'rdhaṃ pradāya*), sense unclear; seeing Viṣṇu in woman's form they ask her to **become their wife** and serve them the nectar (*bhava bhāryāmṛtaṃ gṛhya pāyayāsmān*); she agrees and serves the devas instead (3.12–14). Rāhu drinks disguised **as the Moon** (*candra-rūpa-dharaḥ*) and is pointed out by Sun and Moon (3.14).
  - *Variant (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa 3.4.10.1–26, Lalitopākhyāna; Tagare tr.):* the Śākta frame — Viṣṇu obtains the form by **worshipping and meditating on Lalitā** until he becomes her form (10.4, 10.7); she is "the enchantress of all, love's own heroine" (*sarva-saṃmohinī … śṛṅgāra-nāyikā*) (10.8). The daitya chief is named **Malaka**, and Indra fights and defeats him (10.2, 10.6, 10.24). She sets **separate rows**, stands between them (10.14–15) and serves the devas first with a golden ladle, the pot in her left hand (10.17–18); having served the devas she sets the **empty pot before the asuras and vanishes** (10.20). [late text]
- **Rāhu's infiltration and beheading** — **Svarbhānu**, disguised with the marks of a deva (*deva-liṅga-praticchannaḥ*), enters the deva assembly and drinks; **Candra and Sūrya** point him out (8.9.24). Hari cuts off his head with the razor-edged discus (*cakreṇa kṣura-dhāreṇa*); the **trunk, which the nectar had not reached, falls dead**, but the **head**, made immortal, is set among the grahas by Brahmā (*ajo graham acīkḷpat*) and in enmity attacks Sun and Moon at the parvan (new and full moon) — the eclipse (8.9.25–26). Viṣṇu then resumes his own form before the asuras (8.9.27), and the war that follows is Canto 8.10 (where Rāhu fights Soma, 8.10.30–31). The BBT translation's "Rāhu" in 8.9.24–25 is the translator's gloss for *svarbhānu*; the BBT translation says only "His disc" (the name "Sudarśana" appears only in retellings and purports), and "Brahmā" in 8.9.26 renders *aja*, "the unborn." (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.9.24–27)
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata 1.17.4–8):* the dānava is called **Rāhu**; Sun and Moon speak up when the amṛta has reached only his **throat**; Nārāyaṇa's discus severs the head, which falls like a mountain peak and shakes the earth (in the vulgate/Ganguli text the head rises roaring to the sky and the trunk falls). Rāhu's head (*rāhu-mukha*) keeps an eternal feud with Sun and Moon and "swallows them even today."
  - *Variant (Agni Purāṇa 3.15–16):* the severed head is made immortal by Hari "out of compassion," and Rāhu wins a boon: Sun and Moon will suffer eclipse by him, and gifts given at an eclipse will be **inexhaustible** (*akṣaya*).
  - *Variant (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa 3.4.10.19):* Mohinī herself cuts off Saiṃhikeya (Rāhu), seated among the devas, "**with the hand holding the ladle**" (*darvī-kareṇa ciccheda*) — not the discus; only his nectar-drunk head survives and goes to the sky. [late text]
  - *Note:* the identification of the dead **trunk as Ketu**, the shadow-graha paired with Rāhu, is **not made** in any of the tellings above (Bhāgavata, Mahābhārata, Viṣṇu, Agni, Brahmāṇḍa); in the Bhāgavata the trunk simply dies. It belongs to later astrological and retelling tradition (locus not verified) — the *graha* afterlife is catalogued at [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md). The popular placement of Rāhu "between Sūrya and Candra" in the deva row is likewise a retelling detail, not in 8.9.24.

## B. The Bhasmāsura trap (regional / late)

- **Status** — No major Purāṇa checked here (*Bhāgavata*, *Viṣṇu*, *Brahmāṇḍa*, *Matsya* 1–176, *Agni*, *Nārada*) names a **Bhasmāsura**. Its textually-anchored cousin is **Vṛkāsura** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.88), where the rescuer is Viṣṇu as a **brahmacārī boy** (*baṭuka*, 10.88.27), *not* Mohinī. The two are routinely conflated. The nearest Sanskrit attestation is a Narmadā tīrtha-legend (next bullet). [regional] [late text] — the full comparison is at [Bhasmāsura & Vṛkāsura](bhasmasura-and-vrikasura.md).
  - *Variant (Skanda Purāṇa, Revākhaṇḍa (RKS, ed. Giri 1994) 96.24–51, the Liṅgeśvara tīrtha; colophon "bhasmāsura-vadha"):* the dānava **Kālabāṣpa** wins from Śiva, by austerity in the Narmadā, the boon that whoever he lays his hand on the head of shall not live, and at once rushes at Śiva (96.26–32). Śiva flees to Keśava and hides in him (96.33–34); **Kṛṣṇa takes a woman's dress** (*strī-veṣa-dhārī*) and wanders a forest on the Narmadā bank (96.35–38). The besotted dānava asks her to be his wife; she asks him to **swear by putting his hand on his own head**, and he burns to ash "like a heap of straw" (96.39–43). **No dance**, and the text does not use the name Mohinī. The editor notes that this "genuine Revākhaṇḍa" is not the text printed as *Skanda Purāṇa* 5.3 in the standard editions. [regional] [late text]
- **The trap** — Bhasmāsura, having won from Śiva the power to **reduce to ashes** whomever he touches on the head, turns it on Śiva himself; Śiva flees to Viṣṇu, who appears as **Mohinī**. Smitten, the demon begs her hand; she consents only if he **dances with her, mirroring every pose**. In the **Muktanṛtya** ("dance of release") she lays **her own hand on her head**; copying her, Bhasmāsura touches **his own head** and is **burnt to ashes** (*bhasma*), fulfilling his name. (Popular and performance telling; Śiva's flight to Viṣṇu has a Sanskrit parallel only in the Revākhaṇḍa above.) [regional] [late text]
  - *Variant (Marathi Śivalīlāmṛta, per Vettam Mani, Purāṇic Encyclopaedia s.v. Bhasmāsura):* Mani, who says the demon is in no Sanskrit Purāṇa, summarises a shorter story: Bhasmāsura is **born from the ash on Śiva's body**, wins the head-touch boon by devotion, and becomes "a nightmare to the whole world"; Viṣṇu appears as Mohinī, "an attractive dancer," and in the **Muktanṛtya** he is forced to place his hand on his own head. Mani's summary has **no attack on Śiva, no flight and no marriage pact**. (Author Śrīdhara Svāmī and date 1718 per Wikipedia s.v. Shivlilamrut, not Mani; adhyāya locus not verified.) [regional] [late text]
- **'Bhasmāsura Mohinī' in performance** — The episode is a celebrated performance set-piece, in **Yakṣagāna**, **Bharatanāṭyam** and **Mohiniyāṭṭam** (the Kerala "dance of Mohinī," whose very name evokes her), with Mohinī roles also in Kathakali (a specific Kathakali *āṭṭakkatha* of this title: locus not verified), and recurs in Mysore/Tanjore painting and modern calendar and *Amar Chitra Katha* art — far more visually famous than the textual Vṛka. (Performance & art tradition.) [regional]

## C. Śiva–Mohinī and the sons of the encounter (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.12)

- **Śiva asks to see the form** — Hearing how Hari in woman's form had deluded the asuras, **Śiva** comes with **Umā** and his bhūta-gaṇas to Viṣṇu (8.12.1–3), praises him, and asks to see "the woman's body you assumed" (*yat te yoṣid-vapur dhṛtam*) (8.12.12–13). Viṣṇu agrees, vanishes, and Śiva sees in a flowering grove a lovely woman **playing with a ball** (*kanduka-līlā*) (8.12.14–21). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.12.1–21)
- **Śiva overpowered by māyā** — Śiva forgets himself, Umā and his attendants (8.12.22); robbed of sense, he approaches her even before Bhavānī (8.12.25), chases her, seizes her by the braid and embraces her; she slips free — "a māyā fashioned by the god" (*māyā deva-vinirmitā*) (8.12.27–31). His seed, which never goes in vain (*amogha-retas*), falls as he runs (8.12.32), and **wherever it fell on earth arose fields of silver and gold** (*rūpyasya hemnaś ca kṣetrāṇi*) (8.12.33) — stated in the verse itself. Realising he has been stupefied by the god's māyā, he turns back (8.12.35–36); seeing him **unagitated and unashamed** (*aviklavam avrīḍam*), Viṣṇu resumes his own form and praises him: who but you could cross my māyā? (8.12.37–40). Śiva returns with his gaṇas to his own abode (*svālayaṃ yayau*) and tells Bhavānī what she has witnessed (8.12.41–44). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.12.22–44)
  - *Note:* The *Bhāgavata* version itself does **not** narrate the conception of a child; it ends in Śiva's recovery, Viṣṇu's praise, and Śiva's words to Bhavānī. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.12.32–44)
  - *Variant (Agni Purāṇa 3.17–22):* Śiva asks to be shown the form; deluded, he **leaves Gaurī**, runs naked "like a madman" and seizes the woman's hair; where his seed fell arose **fields of liṅgas and of gold** (3.20); knowing it as māyā he returns to himself, and Hari tells him, "you have conquered my māyā" (3.21–22).
- **Birth of Hariharaputra (Śāstā / Ayyappa)** — *Variant (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa 3.4.10.27–77, Lalitopākhyāna; Tagare tr.):* Nārada carries the news to Kailāsa (10.27–39); Śiva comes with Pārvatī and asks to see the form (10.41–47); seeing Mohinī under a pārijāta tree playing with a ball, he leaves Umā and runs after her, while Umā stands with head bowed "in shame and jealousy" (*lajjāsūyā-samanvitā*) (10.56–72). He seizes and embraces her, his seed falls, and **from it rises Mahāśāstā** (*tataḥ samutthito devo mahāśāstā mahābalaḥ*), able to crush the pride of countless daitya lords (10.73–75); the earth touched by the drops turns silver and gold (10.76). This is the Sanskrit-Purāṇic locus for the son. [late text]
  - *Variant (South Indian / Kerala tradition):* the son is **Hariharaputra** — **Śāstā**, identified with Tamil **Aiyanār** and worshipped in Kerala as **Ayyappan** of **Śabarimala**, "son of Hari (Viṣṇu) and Hara (Śiva)." Mani s.v. Śāstā cites the union for the *Kamba Rāmāyaṇa*, Bālakāṇḍa, and adds that the story is in the "Asura Kāṇḍa of the Skanda Purāṇa" (the Tamil *Kanta Purāṇam*), which alone names the child Śāstā — a claim the Brahmāṇḍa's *mahāśāstā* (above) contradicts (Kamba and Kanta Purāṇam loci not verified). (Regional Śaiva / Ayyappa tradition.) [regional] [late text] — a *Hari-Hara* synthesis paralleling the boon-tales at [Bhasmāsura & Vṛkāsura](bhasmasura-and-vrikasura.md).
  - *Variant (Śiva Purāṇa, Śatarudra Saṃhitā 20.3–7; Shastri ed. tr.):* the seed Śiva lets fall on seeing Viṣṇu "in the form of an enchantress" is kept by the **Saptarṣis** in a leaf and poured into the ear of **Añjanī**, daughter of Gautama, "for Rāma's work"; from it Śiva is born as **Hanumān**. [late text]

## D. Who held the pot: named asuras in the distribution scene

- *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.8.35, 8.9.1):* the asuras who seize and quarrel over the pot are **unnamed** — "all the asuras." **Vṛṣaparvā** appears in this canto only in the war after the distribution, paired against the Aśvins (8.10.30–31).
- *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.9.107–108):* the daityas who seize the kamaṇḍalu from Dhanvantari are those "headed by **Vipracitti**."
- *Variant (Agni Purāṇa 3.12):* the pot is taken by "**Jambha** and the others" (reading *jambhādyāḥ*, per Mani s.v. Jambha; GRETIL prints *janmādyāḥ*).
- *Variant (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa 3.4.10.2–6, 24):* the daityas are led by **Malaka** ("Malaka and the others"), whom Indra defeats after the devas have drunk. [late text]
- *Variant (Śiva Purāṇa, Śatarudra Saṃhitā 22.30–35; Shastri ed. tr., Sanskrit checked):* the amṛta is seized by "the Daityas **Bali** and others" (*bali-prabhṛtibhiḥ*, 22.30); Viṣṇu, "at the behest of Śiva" (*śivājñayā*, 22.32), as Mohinī "bearing a woman's form" (*mohinī strī-svarūpa-dhṛk*, 22.33), makes the devas drink; the daityas beg her that "there be no break in the lines" (*mā bhūd bhedo 'tra paṅktiṣu*, 22.34) and, "deluded by **Śiva's** māyā" (*śiva-māyā-vimohitāḥ*, 22.35), hand over the nectar. [late text]
- *Earlier claim, not located:* some retellings are said to name **Vṛṣaparvā** as the asura who guards or first seizes the amṛta-pot. No such telling was found in the Bhāgavata, Mahābhārata, Viṣṇu, Agni, Brahmāṇḍa or Śiva Purāṇa passages above (locus not verified). [disputed]

## E. Iconography

- **Form** — A **young woman of supreme beauty**: slender waist, full hips and breasts, lotus-petal eyes, a jasmine-decked braid, jingling girdle and anklets — the very archetype of the enchantress (*mohinī*) in śilpa and painting, following the Purāṇic descriptions (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.8.41–46, 8.9.16–18; *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa* 3.4.10.57–70).
- **Viṣṇu-markers** — Despite the female form, Mohinī is frequently shown bearing **Viṣṇu's attributes** — four-armed with **conch and discus** (as at the Harikanyakā temple, Ariyannur, Kerala, per Wikipedia, uncited there) — and the **pot of amṛta**, which the texts themselves put in her hand (*kalasa-pāṇiḥ*, *Bhāgavata* 8.9.17; pot in the left hand and a golden ladle, *Brahmāṇḍa* 3.4.10.17–18) — marking her as Viṣṇu, not an independent goddess.
- **In the round** — Mohinī appears in temple sculpture (e.g., the **Mohinī pillar** in the navaraṅga of the Cennakeśava temple, Belur, and in many South Indian temples), in the *Bhasmāsura Mohinī* dance idiom, and lends her name to **Mohiniyāṭṭam**. Half-and-half composites also exist: at Śaṅkaranayinārkōvil (Tamil Nadu) the female half of a Hari-Hara-type image is read as Mohinī, the union of Śiva with Mohinī (Böck & Rao 2000, per Wikipedia) — though Mohinī proper is a *full* female form, not a half. [regional] [scholarly]

## F. Theology — deliverance by illusion (mohinī vs. māyā)

- **Māyā turned to dharma's service** — Mohinī is the avatar of **deception as a moral instrument**: where Vāmana uses a trick of measure and Vṛka/Bhasmāsura are undone by planted doubt or imitation, Mohinī conquers by **enchantment** (*mohana*). The same root *muh* names **Māyāmoha**, the deluding figure Viṣṇu emits from his body to lead the asuras *away* from Vedic dharma (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 3.17.41–45, 3.18); identified with the [Buddha-avatāra](buddha-avatara.md) in later tradition, he and Mohinī are read as the masculine-ascetic and feminine-seductive faces of one "deluder" function. [scholarly]
- **From epithet to name** — In the oldest layers *mohinī* is an **adjective of māyā**, "the deluding" (*māyām āsthito mohinīm*, *Mahābhārata* 1.16.39; the same phrase in the vulgate *Rāmāyaṇa* 1.45.42), and the *Bhāgavata*'s own avatar-list says only that the Lord deluded the asuras "by an enchanting woman" (*mohinyā … striyā*, 1.3.17); the *Bhāgavata* narrative calls her a "woman" (*yoṣit*, *strī*) throughout. *Mohinī* as the avatar's proper name is explicit in later texts (*mohinī-rūpam ādadhe*, *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa* 3.4.10.34; *mohinī strī-svarūpa-dhṛk*, *Śiva Purāṇa*, Śatarudra Saṃhitā 22.33; *mohinī-rūpaṃ viṣṇoḥ*, 20.3). [scholarly]
- **The asura's defect, not the trick** — In every Mohinī tale the deception succeeds because the asura is **governed by lust or greed**; Viṣṇu's *māyā* merely gives that defect its head. The *Bhāgavata* draws the moral itself: place, time, effort and aim were the same for both sides, but only those who sheltered at the Lord's feet won the fruit (8.9.28–29). The lesson parallels the boon-disasters at [Bhasmāsura & Vṛkāsura](bhasmasura-and-vrikasura.md): the power is not the problem, the **unworthy recipient** is.
- **Even Śiva is not exempt** — The 8.12 sequel makes the sharpest theological point: Viṣṇu's māyā can bewilder even the great ascetic god (Śiva himself tells Bhavānī so, 8.12.43) — yet Viṣṇu ends by praising Śiva as the one being who can cross it (8.12.39–40; cf. *Agni Purāṇa* 3.21–22, "you have conquered my māyā"). The *Śiva Purāṇa* reverses the polarity: the asuras are deluded by *Śiva's* māyā and Viṣṇu takes the form at Śiva's behest (Śatarudra Saṃhitā 22.31–35). Read sectarianly as either god's supremacy, harmonistically as the *Hari-Hara* unity that the Ayyappa cult then literalises in a shared son. [disputed]
- **Two clocks** — The *scriptural* clock places the churning-Mohinī in the prior **Cākṣuṣa Manvantara** (the sixth; Viṣṇu as Ajita churns the ocean, *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.5.7–10) and the Bhasmāsura/Ayyappa material in undated regional time; on the *historical* clock, Mohinī is a literary-theological figure whose dance-drama and Ayyappa-cult elaborations are **medieval and later** South Indian developments, not datable events. [scholarly]

## G. Other Mohinī episodes and a namesake

- **Aravān's bride** — *Variant (Tamil Draupadī / Kūttāṇṭavar cult tradition):* before **Aravān** (Irāvat, Arjuna's son by Ulūpī) is sacrificed for the Pāṇḍavas' victory he asks to be married; **Kṛṣṇa becomes Mohinī**, marries him and spends the night with him, then mourns as his widow — re-enacted in the Kūvākkam festival (Hiltebeitel, *The Cult of Draupadī* I, 1988, pp. 322–25, as cited in Wikipedia's Mohini article; not read directly). Not in the Sanskrit *Mahābhārata*, where Irāvat dies in battle (Bhīṣma Parva). [regional] [folk]
- **Not this Mohinī** — The **Mohinī** of the *Nārada Purāṇa* Uttarabhāga, Brahmā's daughter sent to test King Rukmāṅgada's Ekādaśī vow (*vidheḥ sutā*, 2.21.1; *mohinī brahmaṇaḥ sutā*, 2.24.34), is a different figure, not the avatāra.
