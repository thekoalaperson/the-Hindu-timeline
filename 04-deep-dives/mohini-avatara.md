# Mohini: Vishnu's Enchantress Form

> Cosmic address: **Mohinī** (मोहिनी, "the enchantress/deluder," from √*muh*, "to bewilder") — **Viṣṇu's only female avatāra** and the **13th** descent in the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.3 enumeration of 22 līlā-avatars (between Dhanvantari and Narasiṃha). She is a manifestation of Viṣṇu's **māyā** taking female shape to disarm the asuras, principally at the [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md) in the prior Cākṣuṣa Manvantara, with a later (regional) appearance against [Bhasmāsura](bhasmasura-and-vrikasura.md). Primary scripture: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.8–8.12; *Mahābhārata*, Ādi Parva.
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · See also: [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md) · [Bhasmāsura & Vṛkāsura](bhasmasura-and-vrikasura.md) · [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)

**Mohinī** is the form Viṣṇu assumes when **delusion, not force, is the instrument of dharma**. Unique among the avatāras as the Lord's only female embodiment, she appears wherever an asura's strength or a boon makes a frontal assault futile — and undoes him by enchantment. This file consolidates the avatar-level treatment: her canonical origin distributing the **amṛta**, the **Rāhu / Svarbhānu** beheading, the **Bhasmāsura** dance-trap and its dance-drama afterlife, the **Śiva–Mohinī** union producing **Ayyappa / Śāstā**, the **Vṛṣaparvā** variant, her iconography, and the *mohinī*-vs-*māyā* theology of "deliverance by illusion." The episode-internal narratives are told in full at [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md) and [Bhasmāsura & Vṛkāsura](bhasmasura-and-vrikasura.md); this node draws the threads together without duplicating them.

## A. Origin — Mohinī at the Churning (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.8–8.10)

- **The crisis** — When **Dhanvantari** rises from the churned ocean bearing the pot (*kalaśa*) of **amṛta**, the **asuras snatch it** and flee, quarrelling over precedence. The devas, unable to win it back by force, appeal to Viṣṇu. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.8) — the churning itself, with Viṣṇu as [Kūrma](kurma-avatara.md), is told at [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md).
- **Viṣṇu becomes Mohinī** — Viṣṇu assumes the form of an **irresistibly beautiful woman** (*strī-rūpam adbhutam*) — dark-eyed, lotus-faced, jingling with ornaments. The bewitched asuras, smitten and quarrelling, hand her the pot and agree to abide by her distribution. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.9.1–13)
- **The unfair distribution** — Mohinī seats devas and asuras in **separate rows** and, with flirtatious delay and sweet words, serves the **amṛta only to the devas** — promising the asuras their turn while ensuring it never comes. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.9.14–25)
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva — Āstīka/Amṛta-manthana):* the earliest extant version names Viṣṇu's deceiving form and the bewitchment of the asuras, but the elaboration of Mohinī's beauty and the row-by-row serving is fullest in the *Bhāgavata*. [scholarly]
- **Rāhu's infiltration and beheading** — The asura **Svarbhānu** disguises himself as a deva and slips into the deva-row between **Sūrya** and **Candra** to drink. The luminaries expose him; Mohinī/Viṣṇu hurls the **Sudarśana Cakra**, severing his head — but the nectar has already passed his throat, so **both pieces become immortal**: the head **Rāhu**, the trunk **Ketu**, the two shadow-grahas whose grudge against Sun and Moon causes **eclipses**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.9.24–26, 8.10; cf. *Mahābhārata*, Ādi Parva) — the *graha* afterlife is catalogued at [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md).

## B. The Bhasmāsura dance-trap (regional / folk)

- **Status** — A **post-Purāṇic, regional and dance-tradition** episode; **Bhasmāsura does not appear by that name in the major Purāṇas**. Its textually-anchored cousin is **Vṛkāsura** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.88), where the rescuer is Viṣṇu as a **brāhmaṇa boy**, *not* Mohinī. The two are routinely conflated. [folk] [late text] — the full comparison is at [Bhasmāsura & Vṛkāsura](bhasmasura-and-vrikasura.md).
- **The trap** — Bhasmāsura, having won from Śiva the power to **reduce to ashes** whomever he touches on the head, turns it on Śiva himself; Śiva flees to Viṣṇu, who appears as **Mohinī**. Smitten, the demon begs her hand; she consents only if he **dances with her, mirroring every pose**. In the **Muktanṛtya** ("dance of release") she lays **her own hand on her head**; copying her, Bhasmāsura touches **his own head** and is **burnt to ashes** (*bhasma*), fulfilling his name. (Regional tradition.)
- **'Bhasmāsura Mohinī' in performance** — The episode is a celebrated set-piece of **Kathakali** (the *Bhasmāsura Mohinī* āṭṭakkatha), and recurs across **Bharatanāṭyam, Mohiniyāṭṭam** (the Kerala "dance of Mohinī," whose very name evokes her), Yakṣagāna, Mysore/Tanjore painting, and modern calendar and *Amar Chitra Katha* art — far more visually famous than the textual Vṛka. (Performance & art tradition.) [regional]

## C. Śiva–Mohinī and the birth of Śāstā / Ayyappa (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.12)

- **Śiva asks to see the form** — After the churning, **Śiva**, hearing of the enchantress who deluded the asuras, comes with **Pārvatī** to Viṣṇu and asks to behold the **māyā-mohinī** form. Viṣṇu vanishes and reappears as Mohinī in a flowering garden, playing with a ball. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.12.12–35)
- **Śiva overpowered by māyā** — Disregarding Pārvatī's presence, Śiva is **maddened with desire**, pursues Mohinī, and his seed falls upon the ground — said to become deposits of **gold and silver** in some readings. The episode is the *Bhāgavata*'s demonstration that even Śiva is **not beyond Viṣṇu's māyā**, after which Śiva regains composure unashamed, knowing it as the Lord's power. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.12.31–35)
  - *Note:* The *Bhāgavata* version itself does **not** narrate the conception of a child; it ends in Śiva's recovery and praise of Viṣṇu. [textual]
- **Birth of Hariharaputra (Śāstā / Ayyappa)** — *Variant (South Indian / Śaiva tradition):* the Śiva–Mohinī union produces **Hariharaputra** — **Śāstā**, worshipped in Kerala as **Ayyappan** of **Śabarimala**, "son of Hari (Viṣṇu) and Hara (Śiva)." This is the regional theological flowering of the encounter; the conception is **implicit/appended**, not stated in the *Bhāgavata* text proper. (Regional Śaiva / Ayyappa tradition.) [regional] [late text] — a *Hari-Hara* synthesis paralleling the boon-tales at [Bhasmāsura & Vṛkāsura](bhasmasura-and-vrikasura.md).

## D. The Vṛṣaparvā variant

- *Variant (Mahābhārata-derived / Purāṇic asura-list):* some retellings name the asura who guards or first seizes the amṛta-pot as **Vṛṣaparvā** (an asura-king of the Daitya lists), with Mohinī's deception turned against him and his fellows rather than an undifferentiated mass — a minor naming-variant of the distribution scene; the mainstream *Bhāgavata* leaves the asuras collectively named. [disputed] [regional]

## E. Iconography

- **Form** — A **young woman of supreme beauty**: slender waist, full hips and breasts, lotus eyes, a flower-decked braid, jingling girdle and anklets — the very archetype of the enchantress (*mohinī*) in śilpa and painting.
- **Viṣṇu-markers** — Despite the female form, Mohinī is frequently shown bearing **Viṣṇu's attributes** — the **Sudarśana cakra**, sometimes a pot (*kalaśa* of amṛta), and Vaiṣṇava ornament — marking her unambiguously as Viṣṇu, not an independent goddess.
- **In the round** — Mohinī appears in temple sculpture (e.g., at Belur, and in many South Indian temples), in the *Bhasmāsura Mohinī* dance idiom, and lends her name to **Mohiniyāṭṭam**. She is occasionally fused iconographically with the half-female composite in the late "**Vaiṣṇava Ardhanārī**" conceit, though she is properly a *full* female form, not a half. [scholarly]

## F. Theology — deliverance by illusion (mohinī vs. māyā)

- **Māyā turned to dharma's service** — Mohinī is the avatar of **deception as a moral instrument**: where Vāmana uses a trick of measure and Vṛka/Bhasmāsura are undone by planted doubt or imitation, Mohinī conquers by **enchantment** (*mohana*). The same root *muh* names the **Mayāmoha** delusion of the [Buddha-avatāra](buddha-avatara.md), where Viṣṇu again deludes the asuras *away* from dharma — the two are the masculine-ascetic and feminine-seductive faces of one "deluder" function. [scholarly]
- **The asura's defect, not the trick** — In every Mohinī tale the deception succeeds because the asura is **governed by lust or greed**; Viṣṇu's *māyā* merely gives that defect its head. The lesson parallels the boon-disasters at [Bhasmāsura & Vṛkāsura](bhasmasura-and-vrikasura.md): the power is not the problem, the **unworthy recipient** is.
- **Even Śiva is not exempt** — The 8.12 sequel makes the sharpest theological point: Viṣṇu's *yoga-māyā* can bewilder even the great ascetic god — read sectarianly as Viṣṇu's supremacy, harmonistically as the *Hari-Hara* unity that the Ayyappa cult then literalises in a shared son. [disputed]
- **Two clocks** — The *scriptural* clock places the churning-Mohinī in the prior **Cākṣuṣa Manvantara** (per the *Bhāgavata* Canto 8 frame) and the Bhasmāsura/Ayyappa material in undated regional time; on the *historical* clock, Mohinī is a literary-theological figure whose dance-drama and Ayyappa-cult elaborations are **medieval and later** South Indian developments, not datable events. [scholarly]
