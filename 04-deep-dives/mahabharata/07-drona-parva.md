# Droṇa Parva — The Book of Drona

> Cosmic address: [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) ← the Mahābhārata war straddles the Dvāpara–Kali junction (traditional onset of Kali Yuga, 3102 BCE).
> Parent: [Mahābhārata deep-dives](README.md) · Part of the [Smṛti Itihāsa epics corpus](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md).
> This is the **7th of the 18 parvas** of the Mahābhārata. It covers the **11th through 15th days** of the 18-day Kurukṣetra war, under the second commander of the Kaurava host, the brahmin-warrior **Droṇa**. Following [Bhīṣma Parva](06-bhishma-parva.md); preceding [Karṇa Parva](08-karna-parva.md).

The Droṇa Parva is the longest of the war-books and the moral pivot of the epic. Here the "agreed rules of righteous war" (*dharma-yuddha*) break down on both sides: a boy is killed by many men at once, a man with his arm cut off is beheaded while he sits fasting to death, the truthful Yudhiṣṭhira tells a lie, and the armies fight through the night. The book's own table of contents, the Parvasaṃgraha, names **8 sub-parvas (upa-parvas)** and gives **170 adhyāyas and 8,909 ślokas** (CE 1.2.56–58, 1.2.167–168). The Bhīṣma Parva, by the same count, has 5,884 ślokas (CE 1.2.159). The BORI Critical Edition has **173 adhyāyas** and 8,152 constituted verses. Ganguli's translation, which follows the vulgate, prints section numbers up to **CCIII**.

**How the citations work.** "CE 7.x.y" is the BORI Critical Edition (ed. S. K. De), cited by chapter and verse from the GRETIL e-text. "G" is Ganguli's printed section number, in Roman numerals. A starred number such as `*0259` is a passage the CE relegated to its footnotes, and "App. I No. n" is a longer passage relegated to its first appendix. Both come from part of the manuscript tradition and were left out of the constituted text. The CE and Ganguli numberings drift apart by between −1 and about +29 chapters, so each episode below carries both.

## The Eight Sub-Parvas (upa-parvas), in order

The names and their order come from the Parvasaṃgraha (CE 1.2.56–58). The GRETIL CE text has no sub-parva headings, so the CE chapter ranges below are fixed by content and are approximate at the joins. Ganguli's running heads mark only **five** divisions: Dronabhisheka I–XXX, Abhimanyu-badha XXXI–LXXXIV, Jayadratha-vadha LXXXV–CLI, Ghatotkacha-badha CLII–CLXXXIII and Drona-vadha CLXXXIV–CCIII. They have no separate heads for the Saṃśaptaka-vadha, the Pratijñā or the Nārāyaṇāstra sections.

- **1. Droṇābhiṣeka Parva** — Karṇa returns to the field and Droṇa is consecrated as commander. Day 11 (CE 7.1–15; G I–XVI).
- **2. Saṃśaptaka-vadha Parva** — the Trigartas take a death-oath on the evening of Day 11 and draw Arjuna away on Day 12. Bhagadatta falls (CE 7.16–31; G XVII–XXX).
- **3. Abhimanyu-vadha Parva** — Day 13. Abhimanyu breaks into the cakra-vyūha and is killed (CE 7.32–50; G XXXI–LXXII, which includes the Ṣoḍaśarājakīya at G L–LXXI).
- **4. Pratijñā Parva** — that night Arjuna vows to kill Jayadratha. Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna make the dream-journey to Śiva (CE 7.51–60; G LXXIII–LXXXIV).
- **5. Jayadratha-vadha Parva** — Day 14. Jayadratha is killed at sunset (CE 7.61–121; Ganguli's head runs to CLI, which is CE 7.127).
- **6. Ghaṭotkaca-vadha Parva** — the night battle. Karṇa spends the Indra-given spear on Ghaṭotkaca (CE 7.122–158; G's head starts at CLII, which is CE 7.128, and runs to CLXXXIII).
- **7. Droṇa-vadha Parva** — the end of the night and Day 15. The "Aśvatthāman is dead" ruse and Droṇa's death (CE 7.159–165; G CLXXXIV–CXCIV).
- **8. Nārāyaṇāstra-mokṣaṇa Parva** (*mokṣo nārāyaṇāstrasya*, CE 1.2.58c) — Aśvatthāman releases the Nārāyaṇa weapon and the army survives by laying down its arms (CE 7.166–173; G CXCV–CCIII, under Ganguli's Drona-vadha head).

*(The sub-parva names agree across recensions. The chapter numbers do not, so ranges quoted without an edition are unreliable. Sacred-texts and ibiblio file numbers also differ from Ganguli's printed numbers. The printed sequence skips XXXVIII, LIV–LV, LXXV and CLXXXIX, and misprints CXXIII and CL, so file m07141 is Section CXLV.)*

## 1. Droṇābhiṣeka — Drona made commander (Day 11)

- **Karṇa at Bhīṣma's arrow-bed** - After Bhīṣma falls, Karṇa, who had stayed out of the fighting while Bhīṣma commanded, goes to the arrow-bed. Bhīṣma blesses him and sends him to fight for Duryodhana (CE 7.2–4; G II–IV).
- **Karṇa proposes Droṇa** - Duryodhana asks Karṇa who should lead next. Karṇa answers that naming any one king would offend the rest, and proposes Droṇa, the aged teacher of them all (*ayaṃ tu sarvayodhānām ācāryaḥ sthaviro guruḥ*, CE 7.5.16). Duryodhana installs him (CE 7.5.1–21; 7.6; G V–VII).
  - *Note:* in neither the CE nor Ganguli does Bhīṣma name Droṇa. That attribution belongs to later retellings.
- **Drona's boon to Duryodhana** - Droṇa offers a boon. Duryodhana asks him to take Yudhiṣṭhira **alive** (*jīvagrāhaṃ yudhiṣṭhiram*, CE 7.11.6). He explains that a captured Yudhiṣṭhira could be beaten again at dice and sent back to the forest (CE 7.11.17). Droṇa grants it with a condition: it can work only if Arjuna is drawn away from the king (CE 7.11.20–28; G XII).
- **Day 11 combat** - Droṇa repeatedly drives at Yudhiṣṭhira and the Pāṇḍavas screen their king. The capture fails because Arjuna comes back to the king's side (CE 7.12–15; G XIII–XVI).

## 2. Saṃśaptaka-vadha — the sworn warriors (Day 12)

- **The Saṃśaptaka oath** - On the evening of Day 11, the five Trigarta brothers take the death-oath: Satyaratha, Satyadharman, Satyavarman, Satyeṣu and Satyakarman. Their king Suśarman, lord of Prasthala, swears with them, as do Mālava, Tuṇḍikera, Mācellaka, Lalittha and Madraka troops. They put on garments of kuśa grass, make offerings into a fire, and swear to kill Arjuna. If they fail or turn back, they accept the worlds of liars, brahmin-killers and other sinners (CE 7.16.15–36; G XVII).
- **Arjuna draws off** - A warrior must answer a challenge, so Arjuna, with Krishna driving, goes to fight the Saṃśaptakas. This opens the gap Droṇa needs. Arjuna cuts them down in waves, but they keep re-forming (CE 7.17–18; G XVIII–XIX).
- **Bhagadatta and Supratīka** - The elephant-king Bhagadatta of Prāgjyotiṣa attacks on his great tusker Supratīka. He charges his elephant-goad with a mantra, turning it into the **Vaiṣṇavāstra**, and hurls it at Arjuna. Krishna shields Arjuna and takes the weapon on his own chest, where it becomes the Vaijayantī garland (*vaijayanty abhavan mālā*, CE 7.28.16–18). Arjuna then kills Supratīka with an arrow between its temples and Bhagadatta with a crescent-headed arrow (CE 7.28.37–43; G XXVI–XXVII).
  - **Krishna's explanation (in the text itself)** - Arjuna protests that Krishna has broken his promise not to fight. Krishna answers with a "secret". He exists in four forms (*caturmūrti*): one does austerities, one watches the world, one acts among men, and one sleeps for a thousand years. On waking, that last form granted the Earth a boon for her son Naraka, the Vaiṣṇava weapon. That weapon had passed to Naraka's son Bhagadatta. No one in the worlds, Indra and Rudra included, was safe from it, so Krishna neutralised it for Arjuna's sake (CE 7.28.19–35; G XXVII). This is constituted text, not a later gloss. Cf. [Dashāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).

## 3. Abhimanyu-vadha — the cakra-vyūha (Day 13)

The most-retold tragedy of the parva.

- **The cakra-vyūha arrayed** - Droṇa forms the *cakravyūha* (wheel array), with Duryodhana's son Lakṣmaṇa among a sworn band of ten thousand princes. Jayadratha stands at its head beside Droṇa (CE 7.33.12–20). Yudhiṣṭhira says only four men can break it: Arjuna, Krishna, Pradyumna and Abhimanyu. He asks Abhimanyu to open it (CE 7.34.14–17; G XXXIII).
- **Abhimanyu's half-knowledge** - **Abhimanyu**, son of Arjuna and Subhadrā, says his **father** taught him how to break into such an array, but he will not be able to come out if he gets into trouble (*upadiṣṭo hi me pitrā yogo 'nīkasya bhedane / notsahe tu vinirgantum*, CE 7.34.19). Yudhiṣṭhira promises that the others will follow him through the breach (CE 7.34.20). Ganguli has the same reading (G XXXIII). The Drona Parva never gives his age. It calls him *bāla* ("a boy", e.g. CE 7.41.1), and the Parvasaṃgraha calls him "not yet come of age" (*aprāptayauvanam*, CE 1.2.162d).
  - *Variant (starred passage `*0259`, CE apparatus at 7.34.18):* some manuscripts add that he heard how to enter the cakravyūha "from Kṛṣṇa's mouth" while still in the womb.
  - *Variant (popular retelling):* the full womb story, in which Subhadrā falls asleep or Krishna breaks off the account before the exit is told, is not in either edition. It is a later elaboration of that starred line.
  - *Variant (App. I No. 42 to CE Ādi 1.61.86):* Soma lends his son Varcas to be born as Abhimanyu on the condition that he stays only **sixteen years**, and foretells the cakravyūha battle. This passage is the source of the familiar "sixteen-year-old". The CE leaves it out of the constituted text.
- **The seal-off** - Abhimanyu breaks in. Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma, Sātyaki, the twins, Dhṛṣṭadyumna, Virāṭa, Drupada and others try to follow, but **Jayadratha**, king of Sindhu, fills the breach and holds them all off (CE 7.41.3–7; 7.42.6–19; G XL–XLI).
- **Jayadratha's boon** - He can do this because of a boon from **Śiva**. After Bhīma defeated and humiliated him over the abduction of Draupadī, Jayadratha did austerities to Śiva. In the Droṇa Parva's account Śiva speaks to him at the end of a dream and grants that he will hold back **the four sons of Pāṇḍu other than Arjuna** in battle (CE 7.41.11–17). The Vana Parva tells it first: there the austerities are at Gaṅgādvāra, and the boon is to hold off the Pāṇḍavas in battle, all except Arjuna (CE 3.256.24–29, see [Vana Parva](03-vana-parva.md)). See [Śiva myth-cycle](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md).
  - *Note:* neither locus (nor Ganguli XL) limits the boon to "one day". The time limit belongs to popular retellings.
- **Abhimanyu's rampage** - Alone inside, Abhimanyu drives back Duryodhana and routs the great chariot-warriors (CE 7.35–40). He kills **Lakṣmaṇa**, Duryodhana's son, with an arrow (CE 7.45.13–17; G XLIV), and then Bṛhadbala, the prince of Kosala (CE 7.46.22–24; G XLV).
- **The unfair kill** - Six chariot-warriors surround the boy: Droṇa, Kṛpa, Karṇa, Aśvatthāman, Bṛhadbala and Kṛtavarman (*ṣaḍ rathāḥ*, CE 7.45.19; 7.46.4). Bṛhadbala falls soon after. When Karṇa says he cannot stand the boy's arrows, Droṇa, speaking softly and half smiling, spells out the method. Abhimanyu's armour cannot be pierced, because Droṇa himself taught his father the art of binding it. So they must cut his bow and bowstring, his reins and horses and his two rear-guards, make him chariotless and turn him from the fight, and then strike him (CE 7.47.26–30). Karṇa cuts his bow, Kṛtavarman kills his horses, and Kṛpa kills the two rear-guards. Then the "six great chariot-warriors" pour arrows on the chariotless boy (*ṣaṇ mahārathāḥ*, CE 7.47.31–33). He fights on with sword and shield, which Droṇa and Karṇa destroy, then with a chariot wheel, which they shatter, then with a mace (CE 7.47.34–7.48.4). In a mace-duel with **Duḥśāsana's son** both fall. The other rises first and strikes Abhimanyu on the head as he is getting up (CE 7.48.9–13; G XLVI–XLVII).
  - *Variant (number of attackers):* retellings standardise "**seven mahārathis**". The CE names six, one of whom (Bṛhadbala) is killed before the end. Jayadratha's part is holding the gap. The Parvasaṃgraha says "many great chariot-warriors headed by Jayadratha" killed the boy (*jayadrathamukhā*, CE 1.2.162).
- **A war-crime acknowledged** - The text calls the killing *adharma* on both sides of the field. Yuyutsu, a son of Dhṛtarāṣṭra fighting for the Pāṇḍavas, reproaches the cheering Kauravas: "what are you roaring for, you who know no dharma? … a grievous adharma has been done" (CE 7.50.56–58). Later Arjuna and Sātyaki both cite the killing of the weaponless, chariotless boy to justify their own breaches (CE 7.118.26, 7.118.43).
- **Vyāsa's consolation: the Origin of Death and the Sixteen Kings** [App. I] - In the vulgate, Vyāsa comes to the grieving Yudhiṣṭhira at this point. He tells Nārada's story to King Akampana of how Death (Mṛtyu) was created, and then Nārada's consolation of Sṛñjaya with the "sixteen kings" who died despite their greatness: Marutta, Suhotra, Rāma Dāśarathi, Bhagīratha, Yayāti, Rantideva, Bharata, Pṛthu, Paraśurāma and others (the *Ṣoḍaśarājakīya*, G L–LXXI). The CE removes the whole block from the constituted text to **App. I No. 8**, after CE 7.49.21. The same Nārada–Sṛñjaya consolation stands in the constituted text of the [Śānti Parva](12-shanti-parva.md) (CE 12.29).

## 4. Pratijñā — Arjuna's vow

- **Arjuna's grief and oath** - Returning at dusk, Arjuna finds no music in the camp and his son not coming out to greet him, and learns of his death (CE 7.50, esp. 7.50.16; G LXXII). Yudhiṣṭhira tells him that Jayadratha held the breach (CE 7.51.1–14). Arjuna swears that if he does not kill Jayadratha tomorrow, he will go to the worlds of the worst sinners. Then he adds a second vow: if the sun sets with Jayadratha still alive, he will enter a blazing fire there and then (CE 7.51.20–37; G LXXIII). Krishna blows the Pāñcajanya and Arjuna the Devadatta (CE 7.51.41).
- **Jayadratha's panic; the war-council** - Spies report the vow. Jayadratha, terrified, asks leave to go home and hide. Duryodhana reassures him: eleven akṣauhiṇīs will guard him (CE 7.52.1–19). Droṇa tells him that Arjuna is his superior only through yoga and hardship, promises an array Arjuna cannot cross, and urges him to die, if he must, in his warrior's duty (CE 7.52.20–33; G LXXIV).
  - *Note:* Droṇa does not remind him of the Śiva-boon here.
- **Krishna's rebuke** - Krishna tells Arjuna that he made this "most rash" vow without consulting him, and that the enemy now knows of it (*tat sāhasatamaṃ kṛtam … asaṃmantrya mayā sārdham*, CE 7.53.1–9; G LXXIV–LXXVI).
- **Subhadrā; Krishna and Dāruka** - Subhadrā laments her son (CE 7.55; G LXXVIII). That night Krishna orders his charioteer **Dāruka** to have his own chariot ready, with Garuḍa banner and weapons, and to come when he blows the Pāñcajanya. He says: "whoever hates Arjuna hates me; he is half my body" (*śarīrārdhaṃ mamārjunaḥ*, CE 7.56.17–39; G LXXIX).
- **The dream-journey to Śiva** - Arjuna sleeps, and Krishna comes to him in a dream (*svapne*, CE 7.57.2). He tells him to meditate on Śiva to obtain the **Pāśupata**. In the dream the two fly north past Mandara and the Himalaya to Śiva's mountain (CE 7.57.20–34). At Śiva's bidding they go to a lake of amṛta. There two serpents turn into a bow and an arrow when the heroes recite the Śatarudriya (CE 7.57.64–73). A brahmacārin who comes from Śiva's side shows the stance and the mantra (CE 7.57.74–76). Śiva grants the Pāśupata again as the means of keeping the vow (*pratijñāyāś ca pāraṇam*, CE 7.57.79), and they wake in camp (CE 7.57.81; G LXXX–LXXXI).
  - *Note:* this is **constituted CE text** (81 verses), not a late insertion. Ganguli prints it too. The text calls the place Śiva's peak on the northern route. It does not name it Kailāsa.
- **Morning council** - At dawn Yudhiṣṭhira consults Krishna, and Arjuna reports the dream (CE 7.58–60; G LXXXII–LXXXIV).

## 5. Jayadratha-vadha — the false sunset (Day 14)

The longest sub-parva; one of the epic's set-pieces.

- **Drona's triple wall** - Droṇa places Jayadratha three *gavyūtis* behind the front. With him are Bhūriśravas, Karṇa, Aśvatthāman, Śalya, Vṛṣasena and Kṛpa, with large forces of horse, chariot, elephant and foot (CE 7.63.11–16). The array is a *cakraśakaṭa* (wheel-and-cart), twelve gavyūtis long and five wide at the rear. Behind it lies a lotus (*padma*) "womb-array", and hidden inside the lotus a needle (*sūcī*) array (CE 7.63.21–23). **Droṇa himself stands at the mouth of the cart**, with Kṛtavarman behind him (CE 7.63.28). Kṛtavarman also holds the needle's mouth (CE 7.63.24), and Jayadratha stands at the needle's eye (*sūcipāśa*), behind them all (CE 7.63.27; G LXXXVII).
- **Arjuna's all-day drive** - Arjuna breaks Durmarṣaṇa's elephant-vanguard and Duḥśāsana's division (CE 7.64–65). He passes Droṇa, who will not let himself be overcome, and goes round him rather than be held up (CE 7.66–67). He kills Śrutāyudha, whose own mace rebounds on him, and Sudakṣiṇa of Kamboja (CE 7.67–68; G XCI–XCII).
  - **Duryodhana's armour** - Droṇa binds onto Duryodhana a divine armour that came down from Śiva through Indra (CE 7.69.35–70; G XCIII). Arjuna cannot pierce it, so he shoots Duryodhana's unprotected hands (CE 7.78; G CII).
  - **Water for the horses** - When the horses tire, Arjuna splits the earth with a weapon to make a pool and builds a "house of arrows". Krishna unyokes and tends the horses in the middle of the battlefield (CE 7.74.55–7.75; G XCVIII–XCIX).
  - **Sātyaki and Bhīma sent after Arjuna** - Yudhiṣṭhira, alarmed, sends Sātyaki into the array (CE 7.85–87; G CIX–CXI). Sātyaki defeats Droṇa and Kṛtavarman and cuts through the Yavanas and Kambojas (CE 7.94–96). Then Yudhiṣṭhira sends Bhīma. Bhīma tells Droṇa, "I am not the merciful Arjuna; I am Bhīma, your enemy", and smashes his chariot with his mace (*nārjuno 'haṃ ghṛṇī droṇa bhīmaseno 'smi te ripuḥ*, CE 7.102.84–88; G CXXVI).
  - **Karṇa spares Bhīma** - In a long series of duels Bhīma kills many of Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sons (CE 7.106–114). When he finally has Bhīma weaponless, Karṇa remembers his promise to Kuntī and does not kill him, only taunting him as "beardless", "fool" and "glutton" (*vyāyudhaṃ nāvadhīc cainaṃ karṇaḥ kuntyā vacaḥ smaran*, CE 7.114.67–69; G CXXXVIII).
- **Bhūriśravas and Sātyaki** - **Bhūriśravas** overpowers the exhausted Sātyaki, drags him by the hair, kicks his chest and raises his sword (CE 7.117.50–53). Krishna points this out to Arjuna, who, though his eyes are on Jayadratha, cuts off Bhūriśravas's sword-arm with an arrow (CE 7.117.54–62). Bhūriśravas calls it a cruel (*nṛśaṃsa*) blow against a man fighting someone else (CE 7.118.3–8). Arjuna answers with Abhimanyu's killing (CE 7.118.25–26). Bhūriśravas then sits in *prāya*, the fast unto death. **Sātyaki beheads him** while Krishna, Arjuna, Bhīma, Aśvatthāman, Kṛpa, Karṇa and the whole army shout at him to stop (CE 7.118.31–37). Sātyaki defends himself: he had vowed to kill anyone who kicked him, and it was "no adharma". He even quotes a verse "sung by Vālmīki" that one should do whatever harms one's enemies (CE 7.118.42–48; G CXLI–CXLII). This is a second flagrant breach of the war-code, this time by the Pāṇḍava side.
- **Jayadratha slain; the falling-head boon** - In the constituted CE text there is **no false sunset**. As the sun nears the western mountain, Krishna urges Arjuna to cut off Jayadratha's head (CE 7.121.15–16). He explains that Jayadratha's father, **Vṛddhakṣatra**, had decreed that whoever made his son's head fall to the ground would have his own head split into a hundred pieces. Vṛddhakṣatra is now doing austerities just outside Samantapañcaka (CE 7.121.17–29). Arjuna shoots a mantra-charged arrow (not named as the Pāśupata), then keeps the severed head aloft with more arrows and drops it into the lap of Vṛddhakṣatra at his evening prayer. The old king rises, the head rolls to the ground, and his **own** head bursts (CE 7.121.30–39).
  - *Variant (the "false sunset"), in three forms the CE relegates:* (a) App. I No. 16 (after CE 7.121.15) and `*1025`: Krishna creates a darkness by yoga that looks like sunset. Jayadratha lifts his head to look at the sun, Arjuna strikes, and the darkness is withdrawn as "Vāsudeva's māyā". This is the version Ganguli prints (G CXLV). (b) `*1010`: Krishna summons the **Sudarśana discus** and orders it to hide the sun in darkness. (c) `*1009`: another darkness passage in which Jayadratha raises his head to look at the sun. Retellings in which a cloud hides the sun have no counterpart among the starred or appendix passages at CE 7.121. All the versions with a ruse agree that the "sunset" was false. The constituted text has none.

## 6. Ghaṭotkaca-vadha — the night battle

- **Battle into the night** - Duryodhana, in despair, accuses Droṇa of favouring the Pāṇḍavas, and the fighting goes on past dusk (CE 7.122–137; G CXLVI–CLXI). Aśvatthāman kills Ghaṭotkaca's son Añjanaparvan and many of Drupada's sons (CE 7.131–132). Bhīma kills Bāhlīka with his mace (CE 7.132.15), and Sātyaki kills Somadatta (CE 7.137.33). Ghaṭotkaca had already killed the rākṣasa Alambusa on Day 14 (CE 7.84.29).
  - **Lamps** - In the darkness, Duryodhana has the foot-soldiers put down their weapons and take up lamps. Both hosts are lit up across the field (CE 7.138.12–14; G CLXII).
  - **Karṇa, Kṛpa and Aśvatthāman** - Karṇa boasts that he alone will defeat the Pāṇḍavas. Kṛpa mocks him, Karṇa insults Kṛpa, and Aśvatthāman draws his sword on Karṇa for insulting his uncle until Duryodhana stops him (CE 7.133–134).
- **Ghaṭotkaca unleashed** - With Karṇa scattering the Pāṇḍava army, Krishna will not let Arjuna face him, because Karṇa still holds the Indra-given spear "kept for you" (CE 7.148.31–34). He summons **Ghaṭotkaca**, Bhīma's rākṣasa son by Hiḍimbā, who is strongest at night, and sends him against Karṇa (CE 7.148.35–47; G CLXXII–CLXXIII). Ghaṭotkaca kills the rākṣasa Alāyudha, who came to avenge Baka, Kirmīra and Hiḍimba (CE 7.151–154.1). He then fights Karṇa with illusion, rains of weapons and a *śataghnī* that kills Karṇa's horses (CE 7.150; 7.154.1–47; G CLXXV–CLXXIX). See lineage in [Pāṇḍava genealogy](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
- **Karṇa forced to spend the Śakti** - The terrified Kurus all cry out to Karṇa to use the spear (CE 7.154.48–51). Karṇa hurls the **Vāsavī Śakti**, here described as *vaijayantī* ("bringer of victory"). **Indra** had given it to him in exchange for his earrings and armour, and he had kept it for years to kill Arjuna (CE 7.154.52–54). Indra's terms were that it would kill one mighty enemy and then return to him (CE 3.294.25–35). It pierces Ghaṭotkaca's heart and rises into the night sky (CE 7.154.55–58; G CLXXIX).
  - **The falling body** - Seeing the spear in Karṇa's hand, Ghaṭotkaca flees in fear, making himself as large as a foothill of the Vindhya (*vindhyapādapramāṇam*, CE 7.154.55). Dying, he falls in that huge form and crushes "a portion of your army" (*tava sainyekadeśam*, CE 7.154.61).
  - *Variant (Ganguli/vulgate, G CLXXIX):* the falling body slays "a full Akṣauhiṇī" of Kaurava troops.
- **Krishna's grim joy** - While the Pāṇḍavas weep, Krishna roars, embraces Arjuna and dances on the chariot (CE 7.155.1–4). Asked why, he says that with the spear spent, Karṇa is as good as dead, though he can still be killed only "when his chariot-wheel has sunk" (CE 7.155.11–28). He adds that Jarāsandha, Śiśupāla and Ekalavya were each disposed of by his contrivance for Arjuna's sake. He says he would have killed Ghaṭotkaca himself, a "hater of brahmins and sacrifices", if Karṇa had not (CE 7.155.29–7.156.28; G CLXXX–CLXXXI).
- **Vyāsa's prophecy** - Yudhiṣṭhira, grief-stricken, rushes at Karṇa. Vyāsa stops him: Ghaṭotkaca's death has saved Arjuna, and "on the fifth day the earth will be yours" (*pañcame divase caiva pṛthivī te bhaviṣyati*, CE 7.158.53–62; G CLXXXIII).

## 7. Droṇa-vadha — the death of Drona (Day 15)

- **Arjuna's truce for sleep** - Late in the night both armies are blind with sleep. Arjuna calls out that they should rest where they stand and resume when the moon rises. Both sides agree and praise him, and the soldiers sleep on their horses, elephants and chariots (CE 7.159.22–40; G CLXXXIV). The fighting resumes in the last third of the night (CE 7.161.1).
- **Drona's dawn carnage** - As dawn reddens the sky (CE 7.161.2), Droṇa kills three of Drupada's grandsons, then **Drupada** (his boyhood friend and old enemy) and **Virāṭa** with two broad-headed arrows (CE 7.161.29–35; G CLXXXVI). Dhṛṣṭadyumna, in grief and anger, lays a curse on any warrior who lets Droṇa escape or turns away from him (CE 7.161.36–38), and the armies worship the rising sun (CE 7.162.1–2).
- **Krishna's stratagem** - Droṇa seems about to wipe out the Pāñcālas. Krishna says he cannot be beaten in fair fight, so they must "abandon dharma and use a stratagem". If someone tells him Aśvatthāman is dead, he will stop fighting (*āsthīyatāṃ jaye yogo dharmam utsṛjya*, CE 7.164.66–69). **Arjuna disapproves**, the others agree, and Yudhiṣṭhira agrees with difficulty (CE 7.164.70; G CXC).
- **The elephant named Aśvatthāman** - **Bhīma** kills a great elephant called Aśvatthāman in his own army, which belonged to Indravarman of Mālava. Abashed, he shouts at Droṇa that Aśvatthāman is slain, a deliberate falsehood (*mithyā*). Droṇa doubts it and fights on (CE 7.164.71–76, 101–102).
- **Drona's Brahmāstra and the rebuke of the ṛṣis** - Droṇa turns the **Brahmāstra** on the Pāñcālas and kills thousands (CE 7.164.77–85). Then the seers come, with Agni at their head: Viśvāmitra, Jamadagni, Bharadvāja, Gautama, Vasiṣṭha, Kaśyapa, Atri and others. They tell him he is fighting *adharma*, that this cruelty does not suit a brahmin, and that his time has come to lay down his weapons (CE 7.164.86–92). The rebuke comes **before** Yudhiṣṭhira speaks.
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's half-lie** - Droṇa asks Yudhiṣṭhira, who he believes would not lie even for the three worlds (CE 7.164.94–96). Krishna urges that here "a lie is better than truth; one who lies to save life is not touched by the lie" (CE 7.164.98–99). Yudhiṣṭhira, afraid of the untruth but set on victory, says **indistinctly** "the elephant is slain" (*avyaktam abravīd … hataḥ kuñjara ity uta*, CE 7.164.106). The constituted line does not have him say Aśvatthāman's name aloud. Ganguli reads that he said "Aśvatthāman is dead" distinctly and added "elephant" indistinctly (G CXCI).
  - **The chariot's fall** - Until then Yudhiṣṭhira's chariot had stayed four finger-breadths above the ground. When he spoke, his horses touched the earth (*tasya pūrvaṃ rathaḥ pṛthvyāś caturaṅgula uttaraḥ … vāhāspṛśan mahīm*, CE 7.164.107; also G CXCI). This is **constituted text** in both editions, not a folk addition.
  - *Variant (later retelling):* Krishna has drums and conches blare to drown out the word *kuñjara*. This is not in the CE, its apparatus at this point, or Ganguli. The familiar formula "*naro vā kuñjaro vā*" ("man or elephant") is likewise not the CE wording.
- **Drona lays down his arms** - Droṇa loses heart, with the seers' words in his mind (CE 7.164.108–110). Bhīma then rebukes him: a brahmin should not have killed so many men out of greed for one son, and the son is dead, as Yudhiṣṭhira says (CE 7.165.28–32). Droṇa calls out to Karṇa, Kṛpa, Duryodhana and his son, lays his weapons in the chariot, grants safety to all beings and sits in yoga (CE 7.165.33–35; G CXCII).
- **Ascent and beheading** - The one CE narrative tells both what was seen and what was done. Droṇa, in yoga, became light and passed to the Brahma-world, "like a second sun". Only five mortals saw it: Saṃjaya, Arjuna, Kṛpa, Krishna and Yudhiṣṭhira (CE 7.165.38–45). **Dhṛṣṭadyumna** was born from fire to kill Droṇa and is Drupada's son and Draupadī's brother. Cursed by all beings, he leapt down, seized the head of the body "from which life had gone" (*gatasattvasya dehinaḥ*), and cut it off with his sword (CE 7.165.36, 46–47). All the while Arjuna and the soldiers were shouting "bring the teacher alive, do not kill him!" (*jīvantam ānayācāryaṃ mā vadhīḥ*, CE 7.165.50–52; G CXCIII). Droṇa was eighty-five, dark-skinned and white-haired to the ears, yet he had fought "like a sixteen-year-old" (CE 7.165.49).
  - *Note:* "already ascended" and "a living man slain" are not rival recensions. The CE and Ganguli both narrate the ascent and the beheading of the body, in that order. The moral reading differs between the characters within the text: compare Dhṛtarāṣṭra's "killed by adharma" (CE 7.166.1).
- **A third war-crime** - Droṇa had laid down his arms (*nyastāyudha*) and sat in yoga. The next chapter opens with Dhṛtarāṣṭra asking about the brahmin "killed by adharma" (*adharmeṇa hataṃ*, CE 7.166.1). Note: *prāya*, the fast unto death, is Bhūriśravas's posture in §5, not Droṇa's.

## 8. Nārāyaṇāstra-mokṣaṇa — the Nārāyaṇa weapon

- **Aśvatthāman's fury** - Learning that his father was tricked and butchered, **Aśvatthāman** vows to destroy the Pāñcālas. He tells Duryodhana where the weapon came from. Droṇa once worshipped Nārāyaṇa, who came to him in a brahmin's form, and chose the Nārāyaṇa weapon as his boon. The god warned that it must not be used rashly. It turns back only after killing the enemy, and only fleeing, laying down weapons, entreaty or surrender can calm it (CE 7.166.43–51). No one on the Pāṇḍava side knows it (CE 7.166.41–42). He releases it (CE 7.166.60; G CXCV–CXCVI).
- **The Pāṇḍava quarrel** - As the weapon rises, the Pāṇḍava leaders quarrel bitterly. Arjuna condemns the killing of the teacher, Bhīma mocks him, and Dhṛṣṭadyumna defends himself. Sātyaki and Dhṛṣṭadyumna nearly come to blows: Bhīma holds Sātyaki back, Sahadeva pleads for peace, and Krishna and Yudhiṣṭhira finally part them (CE 7.167–169, esp. 7.169.47–62; G CXCVII–CXCIX).
- **Krishna's counter** - Krishna orders the whole army to **put down its weapons and come down from its chariots and mounts**. The weapon grows stronger the more it is resisted and spares those who submit, but it kills anyone who resists it "even in mind" (CE 7.170.40–43). All obey except **Bhīma**, who charges Aśvatthāman (CE 7.170.44–58). Arjuna covers him with the Vāruṇa weapon, and Krishna and Arjuna run unarmed into the fire, drag him from his chariot and force his weapons from him (CE 7.171.1–18; G CC–CCI).
- **The weapon spent** - With no one resisting, the weapon subsides (CE 7.171.19–22). Duryodhana begs Aśvatthāman to use it again. He answers that it cannot be used twice: invoked a second time, it would kill its user (*naitad āvartate rājann astraṃ dvir nopapadyate*, CE 7.171.24–29; G CCI).
- **Āgneyāstra and stalemate** - Aśvatthāman then hurls the **Āgneyāstra** at Arjuna, and Arjuna counters it with the **Brahmāstra** (CE 7.172.14, 32). Krishna and Arjuna come out unharmed. Aśvatthāman throws down his bow and leaves the field, and meets **Vyāsa**. Vyāsa tells him that Krishna and Arjuna are Nārāyaṇa and Nara, who worshipped Rudra, and that "this Keśava is devoted to Rudra" (*sa eṣa rudrabhaktaś ca keśavo*, CE 7.172.43–90). Aśvatthāman bows to Rudra, honours Keśava and withdraws his army, and both sides retire. Droṇa had fought for five days (CE 7.172.91–94; G CCI).
  - *Note:* the CE ends this episode with Aśvatthāman reconciled, not "vowing vengeance". His revenge comes in the [Sauptika Parva](10-sauptika-parva.md).
- **The Śatarudriya** - At the close, Arjuna asks Vyāsa about a blazing figure with a trident whom he saw going ahead of him, destroying the enemy without touching the ground. Vyāsa says it was Śiva, giver of his weapons, and recites the praise of Śiva's names, the *Śatarudriya* (CE 7.173; G CCII–CCIII). The Parvasaṃgraha's starred lines `*0149`–`*0150` mention "Rudra's greatness", "Vyāsa's arrival" and the Śatarudrīya at this point (CE 1.2 apparatus at 165d).
