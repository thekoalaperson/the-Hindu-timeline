# Droṇa Parva — The Book of Drona

> Cosmic address: [Kali Yuga](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) ← the Mahābhārata war straddles the Dvāpara–Kali junction (traditional onset of Kali Yuga, 3102 BCE).
> Parent: [Mahābhārata deep-dives](./README.md) · Part of the [Smṛti Itihāsa epics corpus](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md).
> This is the **7th of the 18 parvas** of the Mahābhārata. It covers the **11th through 15th days** of the 18-day Kurukṣetra war, under the second commander of the Kaurava host, the brahmin-warrior **Droṇa**. Following [Bhīṣma Parva](./06-bhishma-parva.md); preceding [Karṇa Parva](./08-karna-parva.md).

The Droṇa Parva is the longest of the war-books and the moral pivot of the epic: it is here that the "agreed rules of righteous war" (*dharma-yuddha*) collapse on both sides — child-killing, the slaying of a disarmed man, deception by the truthful Yudhiṣṭhira, and night-fighting all occur. It traditionally comprises **8 sub-parvas (upa-parvas)** and ~204 chapters (Critical Edition counts vary).

---

## The Eight Sub-Parvas (upa-parvas), in order

- **1. Droṇābhiṣeka Parva** — Drona's consecration as commander; opening of Day 11 (Mahābhārata Book 7, chs. 1–16).
- **2. Saṃśaptaka-vadha Parva** — Day 12; the Trigarta "sworn-to-death" warriors lure Arjuna away (chs. 17–32).
- **3. Abhimanyu-vadha Parva** — Day 13; Abhimanyu breaks into the cakra-vyūha and is killed (chs. 33–71).
- **4. Pratijñā Parva** — Arjuna's night-vow to slay Jayadratha by sunset or self-immolate (chs. 72–84).
- **5. Jayadratha-vadha Parva** — Day 14; Krishna's false sunset and the slaying of Jayadratha (chs. 85–152).
- **6. Ghaṭotkaca-vadha Parva** — the night battle; Karṇa expends his Vāsavī Śakti on Ghaṭotkaca (chs. 153–184).
- **7. Droṇa-vadha Parva** — Day 15 morning; the "Aśvatthāmā is dead" ruse and Drona's death (chs. 185–193).
- **8. Nārāyaṇāstra-mokṣaṇa Parva** — Aśvatthāmā unleashes the Nārāyaṇa weapon; the army survives by surrender (chs. 194–204).

*(Chapter ranges follow the Ganguli/standard recension; sub-parva names are uniform across recensions.)*

---

## 1. Droṇābhiṣeka — Drona made commander (Day 11)

- **Bhīṣma's counsel** - Falling on his arrow-bed (end of [Bhīṣma Parva](./06-bhishma-parva.md)), Bhīṣma names Droṇa as the man fit to lead next; Duryodhana installs the ācārya as commander-in-chief (Mbh 7, Droṇābhiṣeka).
- **Drona's boon to Duryodhana** - Drona promises to capture Yudhiṣṭhira **alive**, so Duryodhana can win the kingdom by a rigged dice-game rather than war. He warns it can only work if Arjuna is drawn away from the king (Mbh 7.11–16).
- **Day 11 combat** - Drona repeatedly drives at Yudhiṣṭhira; the Pāṇḍavas screen their king. The capture plan fails because Arjuna keeps returning.

## 2. Saṃśaptaka-vadha — the sworn warriors (Day 12)

- **The Saṃśaptaka oath** - The **Trigarta** king Suśarman and his brothers swear a death-pact: to kill Arjuna or die, never retreating. They challenge Arjuna to a separate sector of the field to pull him from Yudhiṣṭhira (Mbh 7.17–18).
- **Arjuna draws off** - Bound by warrior-code to answer a challenge, Arjuna (with Krishna driving) goes to fight the Saṃśaptakas — the deliberate gap Drona needs. He slaughters them in waves but they keep re-forming.
- **Bhagadatta and Supratīka** - The elephant-king Bhagadatta of Prāgjyotiṣa attacks on his great tusker Supratīka; he hurls the **Vaiṣṇavāstra** at Arjuna, which Krishna absorbs onto his own chest (it becomes the Vaijayantī garland). Arjuna then slays Bhagadatta (Mbh 7.25–29).
  - *Variant (regional/Vaiṣṇava gloss):* the episode is read as proof that Krishna alone can neutralize the Vaiṣṇava weapon, foreshadowing his divinity — cf. [Dashāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).

## 3. Abhimanyu-vadha — the cakra-vyūha (Day 13)

The most-retold tragedy of the parva.

- **The cakra-vyūha arrayed** - Drona forms the spinning "discus/wheel" formation, which only a handful of warriors know how to enter and exit. Yudhiṣṭhira's army cannot break it (Mbh 7.33–34).
- **Abhimanyu's half-knowledge** - **Abhimanyu**, the 16-year-old son of Arjuna and Subhadrā, knows how to *pierce in* but not how to *get out* — he learned only the entry while in the womb (Mbh 7.35).
  - *Variant (popular/folk tradition):* Krishna deliberately stopped narrating the exit-method (interrupting Subhadrā's sleep / her question) so Abhimanyu never heard it — a destiny-device absent from the terse Sanskrit but near-universal in retellings.
- **The seal-off** - Abhimanyu breaks in; the four following Pāṇḍavas (Bhīma, Yudhiṣṭhira, etc.) try to follow but **Jayadratha**, king of Sindhu, holds the gap shut, trapping Abhimanyu alone (Mbh 7.41).
- **Jayadratha's boon** - Jayadratha can do this because of a boon from **Śiva**: for one day he can hold off **all the Pāṇḍavas except Arjuna** (Mbh 7; recalled in Pratijñā Parva). See [Śiva myth-cycle](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md).
- **Abhimanyu's rampage** - Alone inside, Abhimanyu kills thousands, wounds Duryodhana, slays **Lakṣmaṇa** (Duryodhana's son), and routs major car-warriors (Mbh 7.47–48).
- **The unfair kill** - On Drona's tacit counsel, **six** warriors attack the lone boy together — Drona, Karṇa, Kṛpa, Aśvatthāmā, Bṛhadbala, Kṛtavarman. Karṇa cuts his bowstring from behind; his horses and charioteer are killed; finally **Duḥśāsana's son** clubs him to death with a mace in a ground duel (Mbh 7.48).
  - *Variant (number of attackers):* most retellings standardize "**seven mahārathis**" ganging up; the Sanskrit lists the assault piecemeal rather than as a fixed "seven."
- **A war-crime acknowledged** - The text and characters explicitly call the six-on-one slaying *adharma* — the first open breach of the war's code by the Kauravas.

## 4. Pratijñā — Arjuna's vow

- **Arjuna's grief and oath** - Returning at dusk, Arjuna learns of his son's death. He swears: **"By tomorrow's sunset I will slay Jayadratha — or enter the fire myself"** (Mbh 7.72–73). Krishna privately warns this rash vow has handed the enemy a target to merely *protect* all day.
- **Jayadratha's panic; the war-council** - Jayadratha, terrified, wants to flee the field; Duryodhana and Drona pledge to wall him off. Drona reminds him of the Śiva-boon and tells him to stand firm (Mbh 7.76–84).
- **Krishna's nocturnal aid [variant cycle]** - Several recensions and the [Hari­vaṃśa]-adjacent traditions insert Krishna and Arjuna's astral/dream journey to Śiva on Kailāsa to obtain the **Pāśupatāstra** anew this night — marked [late text]/[disputed] as it is uneven across manuscripts.

## 5. Jayadratha-vadha — the false sunset (Day 14)

The longest sub-parva; one of the epic's set-pieces.

- **Drona's triple wall** - To keep Jayadratha alive past sunset, Drona arrays **three nested formations**: the *Śakaṭa* (cart) vyūha, behind it a *Padma* (lotus) vyūha, and at its heart the *Sūcī* (needle) sub-array, with Jayadratha guarded far at the rear by Drona, Karṇa, Aśvatthāmā, Kṛpa, Bhūriśravas, Vṛṣasena (Mbh 7.86–87).
- **Arjuna's all-day drive** - Arjuna, with Krishna, smashes through formation after formation, fighting Drona himself but refusing to be detained, racing the sun.
- **Krishna's discus eclipse** - As sunset nears with Jayadratha still hidden, **Krishna covers the sun with his Sudarśana cakra** (or by yogic illusion / a cloud), feigning sunset. The Kauravas relax and Jayadratha exposes himself to gloat; Krishna says "There is your enemy — the sun returns!" and Arjuna beheads him (Mbh 7.146).
  - *Variant (mechanism):* (a) Sudarśana cakra over the sun; (b) Krishna's *yoga-māyā* creating darkness; (c) an actual cloud — Ganguli's text favors the divine illusion. All agree the "sunset" was a ruse.
- **The falling-head boon** - Jayadratha's father, the ascetic **Vṛddhakṣatra**, had decreed that whoever made his son's head fall to the ground would have his own head burst into a hundred pieces. Krishna instructs Arjuna to shoot the severed head *through the air* into the lap of the meditating Vṛddhakṣatra, who startles, lets it roll to the ground, and his **own** head explodes — the boon recoiling on its maker (Mbh 7.146).
- **Bhūriśravas and Sātyaki** - In a parallel duel, **Bhūriśravas** overpowers **Sātyaki** and is about to behead him; Krishna prompts Arjuna to shoot off Bhūriśravas's sword-arm. Bhūriśravas reproaches Arjuna for the foul stroke, sits in *prāya* (fast-unto-death yoga) — and **Sātyaki, against all protests, beheads the meditating, one-armed man** (Mbh 7.142–143). A second flagrant breach of the war-code, this time by the Pāṇḍava side.

## 6. Ghaṭotkaca-vadha — the night battle

- **Battle into the night** - Enraged at Jayadratha's death, Drona fights on past dusk; the armies, exhausted, continue by torchlight — the prohibition on night-warfare abandoned (Mbh 7.153–159).
- **Lamps and chaos** - Both sides light thousands of lamps and braziers; the fighting is described as uniquely terrible and confused.
- **Ghaṭotkaca unleashed** - **Ghaṭotkaca**, the rākṣasa son of Bhīma and Hiḍimbā, is strongest at night; he uses māyā (illusion-warfare), grows giant, rains rocks and weapons, and devastates the Kaurava host, nearly annihilating it (Mbh 7.174–179). See lineage in [Pāṇḍava genealogy](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
- **Karṇa forced to spend the Śakti** - Unable to stop Ghaṭotkaca, Duryodhana begs Karṇa to use the **Vāsavī Śakti** — the one-use infallible spear given by **Indra** (in exchange for Karṇa's natal armor) — which Karṇa had been **hoarding to kill Arjuna**. Karṇa hurls it; it kills Ghaṭotkaca instantly (Mbh 7.179–181).
- **Krishna's grim joy** - Krishna openly rejoices, revealing he had *deliberately* provoked Ghaṭotkaca's rampage so Karṇa would waste the Śakti — the only weapon certain to kill Arjuna is now spent. Krishna calls Ghaṭotkaca's death a strategic salvation (Mbh 7.181–182).
  - *Variant (falling-body detail):* the dying Ghaṭotkaca expands his body to crush a full *akṣauhiṇī* division of Kaurava troops beneath him — a flourish present in many retellings, lighter in the bare Sanskrit.

## 7. Droṇa-vadha — the death of Drona (Day 15)

- **Drona's dawn carnage** - At first light Drona, knowing his end is near, invokes the **Brahmāstra** and slaughter-vows, killing **Drupada** (his old enemy/friend), **Virāṭa**, and tens of thousands; he becomes unstoppable while he holds his bow (Mbh 7.185–190).
- **Krishna's stratagem** - Krishna tells the Pāṇḍavas Drona cannot be beaten in arms; he must be made to **lay down his weapons through grief**, and only news of his son **Aśvatthāmā's** death will do it (Mbh 7.190).
- **The elephant named Aśvatthāmā** - **Bhīma** kills a war-elephant named **Aśvatthāmā** (belonging to Indravarman/Mālava king) and shouts loudly, "**Aśvatthāmā is dead!**" (Mbh 7.190–191).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's half-lie** - Drona, doubting, will believe only Yudhiṣṭhira, who never lies. Yudhiṣṭhira says **"Aśvatthāmā hataḥ — kuñjaraḥ" ("Aśvatthāmā is dead — the elephant"),** with Krishna having drums and conches blare to drown the word *kuñjara* (elephant). Drona hears only "Aśvatthāmā is dead" (Mbh 7.191).
  - *Variant (the chariot's fall):* a widespread tradition holds that Yudhiṣṭhira's chariot, which had floated a hand's-breadth above the ground because of his truthfulness, **dropped to earth** the instant he spoke the deceitful line — a moral marker. The detail is celebrated in commentary and folk telling; treat as [folk tradition]/[late accretion].
- **Drona lays down his arms** - Crushed by grief and a divine voice (a vision of sages/the *ṛṣis* telling him his time is up and a brahmin should not have warred), Drona drops his bow and sits in yoga on his chariot (Mbh 7.192).
- **Dhṛṣṭadyumna beheads him** - **Dhṛṣṭadyumna** — born from fire expressly to kill Drona, and brother of the slain Draupadī's father Drupada — leaps up and **beheads the disarmed, meditating Drona** despite Arjuna's shouted plea to take him alive (Mbh 7.192–193).
  - *Variant (Drona's end-state):* some recensions say Drona had **already merged in yoga and ascended**, so Dhṛṣṭadyumna struck a body whose soul had departed (a softening that exculpates the killing); others present a living, defenseless man being slain — the more damning reading. Both are textual.
- **A third war-crime** - The killing of an unarmed brahmin in *prāya* is openly named *adharma*; it deepens the war's moral ruin.

## 8. Nārāyaṇāstra-mokṣaṇa — the Nārāyaṇa weapon

- **Aśvatthāmā's fury** - Learning his father was tricked and butchered, **Aśvatthāmā** invokes the **Nārāyaṇāstra**, Viṣṇu's near-omnipotent weapon that intensifies against anyone who resists it (Mbh 7.199–200).
- **Krishna's counter** - Krishna orders the entire Pāṇḍava army to **drop all weapons, dismount, and stand utterly submissive** — the only defense, since the weapon spares the unresisting. All obey except **Bhīma**, who charges in defiance and is nearly consumed until Arjuna and Krishna physically drag him down and disarm him (Mbh 7.200–201).
- **The weapon spent** - Denied resistance, the Nārāyaṇāstra dissipates harmlessly; it too is single-use (Mbh 7.201).
- **Āgneyāstra and stalemate** - Aśvatthāmā then hurls the **Āgneyāstra**; Arjuna neutralizes it with the **Brahmāstra**, and the rivals are restrained from mutual annihilation. The day — and the parva — ends with the Kaurava cause crippled and Aśvatthāmā vowing vengeance, foreshadowing the **night-massacre** of [Sauptika Parva](./10-sauptika-parva.md) (Mbh 7.202–204).
