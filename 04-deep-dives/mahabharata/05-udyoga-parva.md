# Udyoga Parva — The Book of Effort

> Cosmic address: the fifth book of the [Mahābhārata](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md), unfolding at the cusp of the [Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara; Kṛṣṇa, the 8th [Daśāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md), serves as peace-envoy. The Pāṇḍavas and Kauravas are of the [Candravaṃśa / lunar line](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
> Parent: [Mahābhārata deep-dive index](README.md) · Part of [04-deep-dives](../README.md). Follows [04 Virāṭa Parva](04-virata-parva.md); precedes [06 Bhīṣma Parva](06-bhishma-parva.md) (which opens with the [Bhagavad Gītā](../bhagavad-gita.md)). Primary source: Vyāsa's Mahābhārata, Book 5.

The **Udyoga Parva** ("Book of Effort" / "Book of Preparation"; *udyoga* = endeavour, exertion) is the fifth of the eighteen *parvas*. The thirteen-year exile is over; the Pāṇḍavas, revealed at Virāṭa's court, demand back their half-kingdom — and the Kauravas refuse. The book is the great **hinge between peace and war**: it chronicles a chain of failed embassies (Drupada's priest, Sanjaya, finally Kṛṣṇa himself), the famous *nīti* (statecraft) and *adhyātma* (metaphysics) discourses inserted into the negotiation frame (**Vidura-nīti**, **Sanatsujātīya**), the private revelation of **Karṇa's true parentage**, the muster and counting of the armies, and the naming of **Bhīṣma** and **Dhṛṣṭadyumna** as the two supreme commanders. It ends on the eve of the eighteen-day war at Kurukṣetra. (Whole-book summary: Vyāsa, Udyoga Parva; critical edition: S.K. De, BORI.)

*Citation key:* **CE** = BORI Critical Edition (book.chapter.verse), read in the GRETIL e-text; **G** = section number in K. M. Ganguli's translation of the vulgate. The two numberings drift apart: from about CE 44 the G section number runs one to four higher than the CE chapter.

## Structure — the Sub-Parvas

The **Parvasaṃgraha** (the book list in Ādi Parva) gives the Udyoga **186 adhyāyas and 6,698 ślokas** (CE 1.2.152–153); this is the source of the often-quoted "~6,700 ślokas". Ganguli's vulgate translation has **199 sections**. The **BORI critical edition** (ed. S.K. De, 1940) constitutes **197 adhyāyas** with **6,063 verses** (count of the GRETIL e-text), relegating the rest to its apparatus.
- *Variant (sub-parva lists):* the Parvasaṃgraha names the units Udyoga, Sañjayayāna, Prajāgara, Sānatsujāta, Yānasandhi, Bhagavadyāna, **Karṇa-vivāda**, Niryāṇa, Rathātirathasaṃkhyā, Ulūkadūtāgamana and Ambopākhyāna, followed by **Bhīṣmābhiṣecana** (CE 1.2.49–54). It lists the warrior-count before Ulūka, though the narrative runs the other way. The "10 sub-parvas" figure found in modern summaries merges some of these units.
- *Variant (vulgate vs critical):* the **Sanatsujātīya** is one of the places where the vulgate is much longer than the critical text. The CE constitutes 121 verses (CE 5.42–45) and records 60 starred insertions plus a 60-line Appendix I passage (No. 2). The text commented on by the bhāṣya ascribed to Śaṅkara follows this expanded Southern (Telugu/Grantha) tradition [scholarly] (Andrijanić 2025, citing De 1940: 192).

The GRETIL e-text carries no sub-parva headings, so the CE ranges below are fixed by content.
- **Sainyodyoga P.** - council at Virāṭa's court; envoys dispatched; both sides raise armies (CE 5.1–19; G 1–19).
- **Sañjaya-yāna P.** - Sañjaya's peace-mission to the Pāṇḍavas (CE 5.20–32; G 20–32).
- **Prajāgara P.** ("Sleeplessness") - Dhṛtarāṣṭra's insomnia and **Vidura-nīti** (CE 5.33–41; G 33–40).
- **Sanatsujāta P.** - the **Sanatsujātīya** metaphysical discourse (CE 5.42–45, after Vidura's invocation in 5.41; G 41–46, where Ganguli's sub-parva marker falls at section 41).
- **Yāna-sandhi P.** - Sañjaya's report; the war-council debating war vs. peace (CE 5.46–69; G 47–71).
- **Bhagavad-yāna P.** - **Kṛṣṇa's embassy** to Hāstinapura, the embedded tales, the attempted seizure and the Viśvarūpa (CE 5.70–137; G 72–139).
- **Karṇa-vivāda** (Parvasaṃgraha name) - Kṛṣṇa and then Kuntī try to win Karṇa over (CE 5.138–144; G 140–146).
- **Sainya-niryāṇa P.** - Kṛṣṇa's report, the marching-out, and the naming and consecration of commanders (CE 5.145–156; G 147–160).
- **Ulūka-dūtāgamana P.** - Ulūka's insulting message from Duryodhana (CE 5.157–161; G 161–165).
- **Rathātiratha-saṃkhyā P.** - the count/ranking of warriors (*rathī/atiratha*) (CE 5.162–169; G 166–173).
- **Ambopākhyāna P.** - the back-story of Ambā → Śikhaṇḍin (CE 5.170–193; G 174–195). The last four chapters (CE 5.194–197; G 196–199) cover the "how long to win?" question and both armies marching out at dawn.

## Opening — the Council at Upaplavya

- **The victory-assembly debates strategy** - After Abhimanyu's wedding the allied kings gather in **Virāṭa's assembly hall** (CE 5.1.1–2). The Pāṇḍavas had settled at **Upaplavya**, a town in Virāṭa's realm, at the end of the thirteenth year (CE 4.67.14). Kṛṣṇa addresses the kings: the Pāṇḍavas want their **rightful kingdom**, not war for its own sake, and an able envoy should first go to ask Duryodhana for half the kingdom (CE 5.1.24).
- **Balarāma's neutral stance** - Kṛṣṇa's elder brother Balarāma speaks for conciliation. Yudhiṣṭhira lost his kingdom by gambling heedlessly against a skilled player, he says, so Śakuni is not to blame (CE 5.2.8–11). He later declares that he will help neither side (CE 5.7.26), and on the eve of war he leaves on a pilgrimage to the **Sarasvatī** tīrthas rather than fight (CE 5.154.33–34; G 158).
- **Sātyaki rebukes Balarāma** - The Yādava Sātyaki (Yuyudhāna) angrily rejects appeasement, insisting the Pāṇḍavas' claim is just and must be backed by force if denied (CE 5.3).
- **Drupada's counsel and his priest's mission** - Drupada urges that messengers go quickly to Śalya, Bhagadatta and other kings, because "the good side with whoever asks first" (CE 5.4.8–11). His **family priest (purohita)** is sent to Hāstinapura as the first formal envoy (CE 5.6.16–18). At the Kuru court Bhīṣma receives him well, but Karṇa scorns the demand and Bhīṣma rebukes Karṇa (CE 5.20–21).
- **The race for Kṛṣṇa's aid** - Both Arjuna and Duryodhana go to Dvārakā to seek Kṛṣṇa. Duryodhana, arriving first, sits on a seat at the sleeping Kṛṣṇa's head; Arjuna stands humbly at his feet (CE 5.7.6–7). Waking, Kṛṣṇa sees Arjuna first and gives the younger man first choice (CE 5.7.8–15). The choice is **Kṛṣṇa himself, unarmed and not fighting** ("ayudhyamānaḥ … nyastaśastraḥ"), or a vast army of cowherds called the **Nārāyaṇas** (CE 5.7.16–17). Later tradition calls this army the *Nārāyaṇī sena*. Arjuna chooses Kṛṣṇa; Duryodhana gladly takes the army (CE 5.7.19–20). Balarāma refuses to help either side, and Kṛtavarman gives Duryodhana an akṣauhiṇī (CE 5.7.21–29).
  - *Note:* asked why he chose an unarmed ally, Arjuna says he has long wished Kṛṣṇa to be his **charioteer (sārathi)**, and Kṛṣṇa agrees (CE 5.7.31–35). The non-combat vow nearly breaks twice in the war, when Kṛṣṇa rushes at Bhīṣma with his discus (CE 6.55) and with his goad (CE 6.102).
- **Śalya tricked to the Kaurava side** - The Madra king Śalya is the brother of Mādrī, so the twins are his sister's sons (*svasrīyau*, CE 5.8.18). Marching to join the Pāṇḍavas, he is feted at pavilions that Duryodhana has had built along the road, and he assumes Yudhiṣṭhira's men built them (CE 5.8.7–10). Duryodhana then reveals himself, and the grateful Śalya grants him a boon: to be the leader of all his forces (CE 5.8.11–13). At Upaplavya, Yudhiṣṭhira asks him in return to break Karṇa's spirit when he serves as Karṇa's charioteer against Arjuna, and Śalya promises to do so (CE 5.8.25–32).
  - **Embedded tale — Indra-vijaya / the Vṛtra slaying** - To console Yudhiṣṭhira, **Śalya** narrates how Indra also suffered (CE 5.8.36–37). Indra kills Tvaṣṭṛ's three-headed son **Triśiras (Viśvarūpa)** and then **Vṛtra**, and goes into hiding, burdened with brahmin-murder (CE 5.9–10). The mortal king **Nahuṣa** is made Indra, lusts after Śacī, and is cast down by **Agastya's** curse; Indra is restored (CE 5.11–18; G 11–18). The text calls itself the *Indravijaya* and says that hearing it brings victory (CE 5.18.16–19) [embedded *upākhyāna*].
- **The armies gather** - Allies pour in: **seven akṣauhiṇīs** gather for the Pāṇḍavas and **eleven** for Duryodhana (CE 5.19.13, 5.19.27).

## Sañjaya's Embassy and the Sleepless Night

- **Sañjaya carries Dhṛtarāṣṭra's message** - Dhṛtarāṣṭra sends his charioteer-bard **Sañjaya** to the Pāṇḍavas at Upaplavya (CE 5.22–23). The message pleads for peace but offers no land. Sañjaya even says it would be better to live by begging in the Andhaka-Vṛṣṇi kingdom than to win a kingdom by war (CE 5.27.2) — an attempt to shame the Pāṇḍavas into giving up their claim.
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's restraint; Kṛṣṇa's rebuttal** - Yudhiṣṭhira and Kṛṣṇa expose the message's emptiness: peace without the kingdom is no peace (CE 5.26, 5.28–29). In his parting words Yudhiṣṭhira makes his famous minimum offer: give the five brothers **five villages** — **Kuśasthala, Vṛkasthala, Āsandī, Vāraṇāvata**, and any fifth Duryodhana chooses (CE 5.31.19–20).
  - *Variant (vulgate):* Ganguli's text reads **Mākandī** for Āsandī (G 31). When the list is repeated to Kṛṣṇa it reads **Aviṣṭhala** for Kuśasthala and again Mākandī for Āsandī (G 72; CE 5.70.15 keeps Kuśasthala and Āsandī).
- **Dhṛtarāṣṭra's insomnia (Prajāgara)** - Back at night, Sañjaya rebukes the king and leaves (CE 5.32). Tormented by dread of the coming war and his sons' folly, the blind king cannot sleep and summons Vidura (CE 5.33.1).

### Vidura-nīti (Udyoga 33–40)

- **Vidura's discourse on statecraft and ethics** - Vidura, the wise half-brother (born of a servant-woman, an incarnation of **Dharma** cursed by Māṇḍavya; CE 1.100.27–28), delivers a long aphoristic treatise on the duties of a king, the marks of the wise (*paṇḍita*) and the foolish, self-control, truth, forgiveness, and the management of the kingdom. He urges Dhṛtarāṣṭra to restrain Duryodhana and return the Pāṇḍavas' share (CE 5.33–40; G 33–40, the same numbers in both).
  - *Note:* The **Vidura-nīti** circulates independently as a standalone manual of *rāja-nīti* (statecraft) alongside the Cāṇakya/Kauṭilya tradition; see [Smṛti — Dharmaśāstra & Nīti](../../90-literature-corpus/agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md).
  - *Variant (recension):* the critical text of CE 5.33–40 has 530 verses. Its apparatus records **77 starred passages** and an Appendix I passage (No. 1) for these chapters, which shows how many extra gnomic verses the manuscripts carry.

### Sanatsujātīya (Udyoga 41–46)

- **Sanatsujāta on death and the eternal** - Dhṛtarāṣṭra asks Vidura for more. Vidura says that, being born of a śūdra woman, he may not teach the secret doctrine himself, and he names "the ancient, eternal youth (*kumāra*) **Sanatsujāta**" (CE 5.41.2–6). Vidura then **calls the sage by thought**, and Sanatsujāta appears (CE 5.41.8). The sage teaches that **death is heedlessness (*pramāda*)** and heedfulness is immortality (CE 5.42.4). He goes on to expound *Brahman*, true knowledge (*vidyā*) as against mere recitation of the Vedas, *brahmacarya*, and liberation (CE 5.42–45; G 42–46).
  - *Note:* the epic calls him simply a *kumāra* ("youth"). Identifying him as one of the four Kumāras (Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanātana, Sanatkumāra) belongs to later Purāṇic tradition.
  - *Note:* The **Sanatsujātīya** is one of the few Mahābhārata passages with a commentary traditionally ascribed to **Ādi Śaṅkara** (alongside the [Bhagavad Gītā](../bhagavad-gita.md) and Viṣṇu-sahasranāma). It is **not** one of the three *prasthānas* of Vedānta. Cross-ref [Ātman & Brahman](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md) and [karma, saṃsāra, mokṣa](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md).
  - *Variant (authorship) [scholarly] [disputed]:* Andrijanić (2025) argues against Śaṅkara's authorship of the Sanatsujātīya- and Viṣṇusahasranāma-bhāṣyas, from stylometry, later Vedāntic terms, and two quoted verses from a Bhṛgu-Saṃhitā that may date to about the 12th century.
  - *Variant (scholarly):* like the Gītā, the dialogue is often read as a philosophical insertion into the war-frame [scholarly]. The critical edition keeps it in the constituted text, in a shortened form (see Structure above).

## Yāna-sandhi — the Kaurava War-Council

- **Sañjaya reports back; the assembly debates** - At dawn Sañjaya reports to the assembled Kuru court, relaying Arjuna's message of war (CE 5.46–47; G 47–48). A stormy council follows (CE 5.46–69).
- **Bhīṣma and Droṇa warn Duryodhana** - Bhīṣma explains that Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna are **Nārāyaṇa and Nara**, the ancient sages, "one being made two" (CE 5.48.19–20). The elders beg Duryodhana to make peace; he is unmoved.
- **Karṇa's bravado vs. Bhīṣma's mockery** - Bhīṣma blames Duryodhana for following Karṇa, "cursed by Rāma" (CE 5.48.27). When Karṇa protests, Bhīṣma says he is not even a sixteenth part (*kalā*) of the Pāṇḍavas (CE 5.48.29–39). Later Karṇa boasts again that he can kill the Pāṇḍavas single-handed, and Bhīṣma derides him (CE 5.61.1–11). Karṇa then vows to **lay down his weapons**: the grandsire will see him only in the assembly, never in battle, and the kings will see his power once Bhīṣma is gone (CE 5.61.12–14). This is the first statement of the vow that he repeats at Bhīṣma's consecration and at the warrior-count.
- **Vidura and Dhṛtarāṣṭra waver** - Vidura counsels unity with the **fowler's parable**: birds caught in one net fly off with it together, and fall into the fowler's hands only when they start to quarrel (CE 5.62; G 64). The blind king half-wishes for peace but cannot, or will not, rein in his son (CE 5.63). Sañjaya will speak only before witnesses, so **Vyāsa and Gāndhārī** are brought in to hear him (CE 5.65.6–8), and Gāndhārī rebukes her son (CE 5.67.9). Sañjaya then expounds Kṛṣṇa's names and divinity (CE 5.66–68).

## Bhagavad-yāna — Kṛṣṇa's Peace Embassy

- **Kṛṣṇa agrees to go as the final envoy** - Yudhiṣṭhira recounts his five-village offer and asks Kṛṣṇa's counsel. Kṛṣṇa resolves to go **personally** to the Kuru assembly for both sides' sake (CE 5.70.14–16, 5.70.79). Yudhiṣṭhira at first objects to the danger, and Kṛṣṇa reassures him (CE 5.70.82–88). Each brother then gives his view. **Bhīma** surprisingly counsels peace, Kṛṣṇa taunts him, and Bhīma recovers his fury (CE 5.72–74; G 74–76). Arjuna and Nakula follow (CE 5.76, 5.78), and Sahadeva and Sātyaki call for war (CE 5.79). **Draupadī**, holding her long black hair in her left hand, reminds Kṛṣṇa of how she was dragged by it into the hall and demands that it be avenged (CE 5.80.24–34; G 82).
- **The minimal demand — five villages** - The five-village offer is Yudhiṣṭhira's, made through Sañjaya (CE 5.31.19–20) and repeated to Kṛṣṇa (CE 5.70.15–16). Draupadī recalls it (CE 5.80.7), and Duryodhana mocks Yudhiṣṭhira for begging five villages (CE 5.54.29). In the Kuru court itself Kṛṣṇa asks for the Pāṇḍavas' **ancestral share** (CE 5.93.53) — "give half to the Pārthas" (CE 5.122.60). Only in his report back does he say that his final peace offer let Duryodhana keep the whole kingdom except five villages (CE 5.148.13–16). The terms are deliberately modest, to expose Duryodhana's intransigence.
  - *Variant (named villages):* later tradition names the five as **Indraprastha, Svarṇaprastha (Sonipat), Pāṇiprastha (Panipat), Vyāghraprastha (Baghpat), and Tilaprastha (Tilpat)** [folk/late tradition]. This list differs from the epic's own list (Kuśasthala, Vṛkasthala, Āsandī, Vāraṇāvata + one; CE 5.31.19), which none of these place-names repeats.
  - *Variant (Sāraḷā Mahābhārata, Odia):* Kṛṣṇa demands five cities — Indraprastha, Yamaprastha, Hastinā, Jayanta and Bāruṇai [regional tradition] (B. N. Patnaik's reading notes on Sarala Dāsa).
- **Omens on the journey** - Kṛṣṇa sets out with Sātyaki (CE 5.81.22). On the road he meets a company of seers, for whom **Jāmadagnya (Paraśurāma)** speaks: they have come to watch the assembly (CE 5.81.60–67). Ill omens foreshadow slaughter (CE 5.82).
- **Duryodhana's plot to seize Kṛṣṇa** - Before Kṛṣṇa arrives, Duryodhana proposes to imprison him, since the Pāṇḍavas depend on him (CE 5.86.13–15). **Dhṛtarāṣṭra** forbids it, because an envoy and kinsman may not be bound, and **Bhīṣma** denounces the scheme and walks out (CE 5.86.16–23).
- **Kṛṣṇa lodges with Vidura, not Duryodhana** - Kṛṣṇa calls on Dhṛtarāṣṭra, then on Kuntī at Vidura's house, and then on Duryodhana (CE 5.87–89). He declines Duryodhana's feast: one eats another's food either out of affection or in distress, and neither applies here (CE 5.89.25). He lodges and dines at the house of the upright **Vidura** (CE 5.89.34–41; G 91), signalling his moral alignment.
- **Kuntī's lament to Kṛṣṇa** - At their first meeting Kuntī pours out her grief for her sons and Draupadī (CE 5.88; G 90). She asks Kṛṣṇa to warn Yudhiṣṭhira that his dharma is wasting away (CE 5.88.72), and to tell Arjuna and Bhīma that "the time has come for which a kṣatriya woman bears a son" (*yadarthaṃ kṣatriyā sūte*, CE 5.88.74) — effectively endorsing war if peace fails. Before Kṛṣṇa leaves she sends a fuller message, repeating that line (CE 5.135.9): the tale of **Vidulā** (spelled Vidurā in the critical text, CE 5.131.3–4), a queen who goads her defeated son, himself named **Sañjaya** (not the bard), back into battle (CE 5.130–135; G 132–137).
- **Kṛṣṇa's address in the Kaurava court** - Before the full assembly Kṛṣṇa makes an eloquent plea for peace and for the Pāṇḍavas' ancestral share (CE 5.93). Duryodhana replies that he will not yield even **"as much land as the point of a sharp needle (*sūcī*) can pierce"** (CE 5.125.26; G 127).
  - **Embedded tales told to Duryodhana** - Three cautionary stories are told in the court. **Paraśurāma** tells of the proud king **Dambhodbhava**, humbled by Nara and Nārāyaṇa (CE 5.94; G 96). **Kaṇva** tells of **Mātali's search** for a husband for his daughter **Guṇakeśī**, which ends in the nāga Sumukha and Garuḍa's humbling (CE 5.95–103; G 97–105). **Nārada** tells of **Gālava**, who must pay his guru Viśvāmitra 800 white horses each with one black ear, and of Yayāti's daughter **Mādhavī** (CE 5.104–121; G 106–123).
- **Duryodhana walks out; Kṛṣṇa urges his arrest** - Kṛṣṇa rebukes Duryodhana. Duḥśāsana warns that the elders may bind Duryodhana and hand him over, and Duryodhana storms out of the hall (CE 5.126.21–27). Kṛṣṇa then advises the elders to **bind Duryodhana, Karṇa, Śakuni and Duḥśāsana** and give them to the Pāṇḍavas, citing the Yādavas' own abandonment of Kaṃsa. "For the family abandon one man; for the village, the family; for the country, the village" (CE 5.126.36–49). **Gāndhārī** is summoned and rebukes her son (CE 5.127).
- **The attempted arrest and the Viśvarūpa** - Duryodhana's faction plots to seize Kṛṣṇa by force. **Sātyaki** detects the plot and warns Kṛṣṇa and the court (CE 5.128.5–13). Kṛṣṇa laughs aloud and manifests his **Viśvarūpa / cosmic universal form** (CE 5.129.4). Gods issue from his body — Brahmā on his brow, Rudra on his chest — along with Arjuna and Balarāma at his arms and the Pāṇḍavas and Vṛṣṇis about him (CE 5.129.4–9). The kings shut their eyes in terror. Only **Droṇa, Bhīṣma, Vidura, Sañjaya and the seers** behold it, because Kṛṣṇa gives them divine sight (CE 5.129.12–13; G 131). Kṛṣṇa then leaves, the embassy having formally failed.
  - *Variant (Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sight) [interpolation]:* some manuscripts add that the blind Dhṛtarāṣṭra was also given divine eyes and saw the form. The critical edition relegates this to Appendix I (No. 8), and Ganguli's text lacks it too.
  - *Note:* this Viśvarūpa is distinct from, and prefigures, the more famous theophany in the [Bhagavad Gītā](../bhagavad-gita.md), Chapter 11 (Bhīṣma Parva).

### Karṇa's parentage revealed

- **Kṛṣṇa's private offer to Karṇa** - On leaving, Kṛṣṇa takes Karṇa onto his chariot and reveals the secret: Karṇa is **Kuntī's first-born by Sūrya**. Under the dharmaśāstra rule that a maiden's son (*kānīna*) belongs to the man she later marries, he is **Pāṇḍu's son** and the eldest Pāṇḍava (CE 5.138.7–9). Kṛṣṇa offers to consecrate him king, with Yudhiṣṭhira as heir-apparent, and says Draupadī will come to him "at the sixth time" (CE 5.138.15–18; G 140).
- **Karṇa's refusal out of loyalty** - Karṇa declines. Duryodhana's friendship raised him from obscurity, and he will not betray it (CE 5.139; G 141). He asks Kṛṣṇa to keep his birth secret: if Yudhiṣṭhira knew, he would not take the kingdom, and Karṇa would hand it to Duryodhana (CE 5.139.20–23). He foresees defeat and pictures the war as Duryodhana's great **weapon-sacrifice** (*śastra-yajña*). Kṛṣṇa will be its knower and Arjuna its hotṛ, and Karṇa's own death at Arjuna's hands will be its "re-piling" (*punaś-citi*) (CE 5.139.29–51). Kṛṣṇa names the day for battle — the new moon (*amāvāsyā*) seven days on (CE 5.140.18; G 142). Karṇa recounts dreams of doom, including Yudhiṣṭhira climbing a thousand-pillared palace (CE 5.141.27–29; G 143).
- **Kuntī's appeal on the Ganges bank** - After Kṛṣṇa's departure **Vidura** comes to Kuntī in distress at the failure of peace (CE 5.142.1–4). Kuntī then decides **on her own** to go to Karṇa (CE 5.142.25–26). She finds him on the Gaṅgā bank at prayer, facing east with arms raised, and waits behind him in the heat until he has finished (CE 5.142.26–29). Karṇa greets her as "Rādheya, son of Adhiratha" (CE 5.143.1). She confesses she is his mother and begs him to join his brothers. **Sūrya** speaks from the solar disc, confirming her words and urging him to heed her, but Karṇa does not waver (CE 5.144.1–3; G 144–146).
- **Karṇa's vow — "you shall still have five sons"** - Karṇa reproaches Kuntī for abandoning him at birth (CE 5.144.4–10), but grants a boon. He will **not kill her other sons** even when they are in his power. Only between **him and Arjuna** will it be to the death, so that whatever happens Kuntī will still have **five sons**: with Karṇa and without Arjuna, or with Arjuna if Karṇa falls (CE 5.144.20–22).
  - *Variant (timing of Karṇa's curses/handicaps):* the loss of his Sūrya-given armour-and-earrings (*kavaca-kuṇḍala*) to Indra is told in the Vana Parva (CE 3.284–294) — see [03 Vana Parva](03-vana-parva.md). The curses of **Paraśurāma** (to forget the brahmāstra at the decisive moment) and of the **brahmin** whose sacrificial cow he killed (that the earth will swallow his chariot-wheel) are narrated later: by Karṇa himself to Śalya in the Karṇa Parva (CE 8.29.4–7, 31–39), and at length by Nārada in the Śānti Parva (CE 12.2–3). The two tellings differ in a detail: Karṇa says he shot the cow's calf (CE 8.29.33), while Nārada says he killed the cow (CE 12.2.20). Here they are only alluded to: by Bhīṣma (CE 5.48.27, 5.165.6), and by Karṇa himself, who admits Rāma's curse (CE 5.61.2–3).
  - *Variant (Sāraḷā Mahābhārata, Odia) [regional tradition]:* in Sarala Dāsa's Odia retelling, Karṇa knows from childhood that the Pāṇḍavas are his brothers. He rejects Kṛṣṇa's appeal and vows to kill Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa, and Kuntī insists to Kṛṣṇa that the war take place (B. N. Patnaik's reading notes, 2024).

## Sainya-niryāṇa — Marching to Kurukṣetra & the Commanders

- **Kṛṣṇa's report** - Back at Upaplavya, Kṛṣṇa reports the counsel of Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Vidura, Gāndhārī and Dhṛtarāṣṭra (CE 5.145–148, listed at 5.148.6; G 147–150). He has tried conciliation, division and a gift, so only force (*daṇḍa*) remains (CE 5.148.7–17).
- **The armies move to Kurukṣetra** - Both hosts march to the sacred plain. Kurukṣetra is also called Samanta-pañcaka, where Paraśurāma once filled five lakes with the blood of kṣatriyas (CE 1.2.3–9). The Pāṇḍavas camp by the river **Hiraṇvatī** (CE 5.149.63–73). The famous **18 akṣauhiṇīs** muster — **7** for the Pāṇḍavas, **11** for the Kauravas (CE 5.19.13, 27; 5.148.5).
- **Dhṛṣṭadyumna made Pāṇḍava commander** - Seven leaders are named for the seven akṣauhiṇīs: Drupada, Virāṭa, Dhṛṣṭadyumna, Śikhaṇḍin, Sātyaki, Cekitāna and Bhīma (CE 5.149.4–5). For supreme commander Sahadeva proposes Virāṭa, Nakula proposes Drupada, Arjuna proposes Dhṛṣṭadyumna, and Bhīma proposes Śikhaṇḍin. Yudhiṣṭhira leaves the choice to Kṛṣṇa, who praises all the candidates and urges the march without singling one out (CE 5.149.8–47; G 151). Drupada's son **Dhṛṣṭadyumna** (born of fire, fated to slay Droṇa) is then formally installed as *senāpati* of all the forces. **Arjuna** is made "lord of the commanders" (*senāpatipati*), and Kṛṣṇa is Arjuna's guide and charioteer (CE 5.154.11–14; G 158).
- **Bhīṣma made Kaurava commander** - Duryodhana names the eleven leaders of his akṣauhiṇīs: Kṛpa, Droṇa, Śalya, Jayadratha, Sudakṣiṇa, Kṛtavarman, Aśvatthāman, Karṇa, Bhūriśravas, Śakuni and Bāhlīka (CE 5.152.27–29). The grand-patriarch **Bhīṣma** is consecrated as their first *senāpati* (CE 5.153; G 157); he accepts on conditions.
  - **Bhīṣma's two conditions** - He will fight for Duryodhana but (1) he will **not destroy the sons of Pāṇḍu**, killing ten thousand of their warriors a day instead (CE 5.153.21–22). (2) **Either Karṇa fights first, or he does** (CE 5.153.24). Karṇa answers that he will not fight while Bhīṣma lives, only after Bhīṣma falls (CE 5.153.25, at Bhīṣma's consecration; repeated in the Rathātiratha, CE 5.165.27).
- **Rukmin rejected by both sides** - **Rukmin**, Bhīṣmaka's son and lord of Bhojakaṭa, offers to save Arjuna "if you are afraid". Arjuna refuses, and Duryodhana too declines him, so Rukmin, like Balarāma, takes no part in the war (CE 5.155.21–36; G 159).
- **Rules of war (dharma-yuddha) agreed** [not in Udyoga] - The conventions of fair combat are settled at the start of the **Bhīṣma Parva**, not in this book (CE 6.1.26–33). Fight only matched opponents (chariot against chariot, elephant against elephant). Do not strike one who withdraws, is engaged with another, is unprepared, has turned away, or has lost weapon or armour. Do not strike charioteers, draught animals, weapon-carriers or drummers. These rules are systematically broken as the war degrades.
- **Yuyutsu defects to the Pāṇḍavas** [not in Udyoga] - Just before the first day's battle, Yudhiṣṭhira invites any who wish to change sides. **Yuyutsu**, Dhṛtarāṣṭra's son by a vaiśya woman, crosses over (CE 6.41.90–95, Bhīṣma Parva).

## Ulūka-dūtāgamana — the Insulting Embassy

- **Ulūka delivers Duryodhana's taunt** - With both armies encamped, Duryodhana sends Śakuni's son **Ulūka** to the Pāṇḍava camp (CE 5.157; G 161). He carries a deliberately humiliating message mocking Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma, Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa — Arjuna's year with his hair in a braid at Virāṭa's court, Bhīma's as a cook in Virāṭa's kitchen — to enrage them past any last hope of peace (CE 5.157–158; 5.158.31–32).
  - *Variant (vulgate):* the vulgate adds a fable of a hypocritical **cat** posing as an ascetic to eat the mice, aimed at Yudhiṣṭhira's piety. The critical edition relegates it to Appendix I (No. 9).
- **The Pāṇḍavas' furious replies** - Yudhiṣṭhira only bids Ulūka speak without fear (CE 5.158.3). In the critical text the replies come from **Kṛṣṇa** and Arjuna: Kṛṣṇa will drive Arjuna's chariot at Yudhiṣṭhira's bidding (CE 5.159.5–11). Then **Arjuna** vows to bring down Bhīṣma first and relays Bhīma's vow against Duḥśāsana (CE 5.160.2–23). Ulūka reports back, and Duryodhana orders the army readied before dawn (CE 5.160.24–29). Yudhiṣṭhira moves his army out under Dhṛṣṭadyumna, posting **Śikhaṇḍin** against Bhīṣma (CE 5.161.7).
  - *Variant (vulgate):* Ganguli's text gives longer counter-messages from more warriors, including Bhīma, Dhṛṣṭadyumna and Śikhaṇḍin (G 163–164).

## Rathātiratha-saṃkhyā — Counting the Warriors

- **Bhīṣma ranks the Kaurava warriors** - At Duryodhana's request Bhīṣma grades the champions of both hosts as *ardharatha* ("half a chariot-warrior"), *ratha*, *atiratha* or *mahāratha* (CE 5.162–169; G 166–173). An example is "Sudakṣiṇa of Kamboja is a ratha of single worth" (CE 5.163.1). The epic gives no numerical definitions of these grades; the fixed equivalences quoted in modern retellings come from later glosses.
- **Bhīṣma demotes Karṇa to "half-ratha" (ardha-ratha)** - Bhīṣma calls Karṇa harsh, boastful and base, "neither a full ratha nor an atiratha". He has lost his inborn armour and earrings and lies under the curses of Rāma and the brahmin, so Bhīṣma counts him an **ardharatha** who will not escape Arjuna alive (CE 5.165.3–6).
- **Droṇa's counter-assessment** - **Droṇa concurs** with Bhīṣma: Karṇa is arrogant, turns away in battle and is careless, so he too counts him a half-ratha (CE 5.165.7–8). Furious, Karṇa retorts that it is Bhīṣma who is the half-ratha (CE 5.165.9–11). He again vows not to fight while Bhīṣma lives (CE 5.165.27). Bhīṣma then goes on to rank the Pāṇḍava champions (CE 5.166–169).

## Ambopākhyāna — Why Bhīṣma Will Not Fight Śikhaṇḍin

- **Bhīṣma names his one exception** - Bhīṣma says he will never kill a woman or one who was formerly a woman. **Śikhaṇḍin** "was a maiden and became a man", so Bhīṣma will not fight him (CE 5.169.19–20). Asked why, Bhīṣma tells the long back-story (CE 5.170.1 ff.; G 174).
- **The abduction of the three Kāśī princesses** - Long ago Bhīṣma carried off **Ambā, Ambikā, and Ambālikā** from their *svayaṃvara* for his half-brother Vicitravīrya (CE 5.170.9–22; back-story to [01 Ādi Parva](01-adi-parva.md)).
- **Ambā's release and rejection** - Ambā tells Bhīṣma she had already chosen **Śālva** in her heart, and he lets her go (CE 5.171.6–8). But Śālva spurns her as one who belonged to another (*anyapūrvā*) and sends her back to Bhīṣma (CE 5.172.4–6). Ruined and vengeful against Bhīṣma, she goes to the forest hermits (CE 5.173–174).
- **Ambā's tapas and Paraśurāma's duel** - In the forest her maternal grandfather, the royal sage **Hotravāhana**, sends her to his friend **Paraśurāma**, the son of Jamadagni (CE 5.174.14–5.175.5). Paraśurāma was also Bhīṣma's own teacher. Paraśurāma orders Bhīṣma to take Ambā back, and Bhīṣma refuses because of his vow. They fight at Kurukṣetra for **twenty-three days** (CE 5.183.27). On the last day the seers, led by Nārada, stop Bhīṣma from loosing the *prasvāpa* weapon, and Rāma's ancestors and Gaṅgā make Rāma lay down his arms (CE 5.186.1–30). Rāma tells Ambā he cannot defeat Bhīṣma (CE 5.187.2–6).
- **The boon of Śiva and Ambā's self-immolation** - Ambā performs fierce austerities on the Yamunā bank (CE 5.187.18). **Gaṅgā** curses her, and by that austerity half of her becomes the crooked, seasonal river **Ambā** in the land of the Vatsas while half remains a maiden (CE 5.187.33–40). **Śiva** appears and grants that she will kill Bhīṣma and **become a man in time** (CE 5.188.7–14). She builds a pyre by the Yamunā and enters the fire "for Bhīṣma's death" (CE 5.188.16–18). She is reborn as Drupada's child, after Śiva tells Drupada he will have a daughter who becomes a man (CE 5.189.3–7).
- **Śikhaṇḍin's sex-change** - Born a daughter (**Śikhaṇḍinī**) but raised as a son, Śikhaṇḍin is married to the daughter of **Hiraṇyavarman**, king of Daśārṇa. When the truth comes out, Hiraṇyavarman threatens war (CE 5.190–191). In despair Śikhaṇḍinī goes into the forest, where the *yakṣa* **Sthūṇākarṇa** exchanges sexes with her, becoming female while she becomes male (CE 5.192–193.8). **Kubera** punishes the yakṣa by fixing the exchange: Sthūṇa will regain his male form only **when Śikhaṇḍin is killed in battle** (CE 5.193.44–46; G 195). So the change holds for Śikhaṇḍin's lifetime, and Śikhaṇḍin is the destined instrument before whom Bhīṣma will lay down arms in the [Bhīṣma Parva](06-bhishma-parva.md).
  - *Variant (gender):* in the critical text the exchange lasts for Śikhaṇḍin's life (CE 5.193.46), yet Bhīṣma still treats Śikhaṇḍin as "formerly a woman" (*strīpūrva*, CE 5.169.19–20). The tension between these two passages is much discussed in modern scholarship on gender in the epic [scholarly].
- **"How long to win?"** - Next morning Duryodhana asks his commanders how long each would need to destroy the Pāṇḍava host (CE 5.194; G 196). Bhīṣma says **one month**, killing ten thousand warriors a day, and Droṇa likewise; Kṛpa says **two months**, Aśvatthāman **ten nights**, and Karṇa **five nights** (CE 5.194.12–19). Hearing of this, Yudhiṣṭhira asks Arjuna. With Kṛṣṇa beside him, Arjuna says, he could destroy the three worlds in an instant with the **Pāśupata** weapon Śiva gave him. But it is not right to kill ordinary men with divine weapons, so they will win by fair fighting (CE 5.195.7–15; G 197).
- **The book closes on the eve of war** - Both armies march out at dawn and take their positions (CE 5.196–197; G 198–199). With commanders named, warriors counted, and every embassy failed, the two armies stand arrayed at Kurukṣetra. The narrative passes to the [Bhīṣma Parva](06-bhishma-parva.md), where Arjuna's despondency opens the [Bhagavad Gītā](../bhagavad-gita.md).
