# Sauptika Parva — The Book of the Night Raid

> Cosmic address: [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) → the Mahābhārata war at the Dvāpara–Kali junction (war traditionally ~3138 BCE; Kali begins 3102 BCE).
> Parent: [Mahābhārata deep-dives](README.md) · Previous: [09-Śalya Parva](09-shalya-parva.md) · Next: [11-Strī Parva](11-stri-parva.md)
> The tenth of the eighteen parvas. It covers the **night after Day 18 and the following morning**: Aśvatthāman's night massacre of the sleeping Pāñcāla camp and of Draupadī's five sons, his Brahmaśiras duel with Arjuna, the weapon sent into the wombs of the Pāṇḍava women, Kṛṣṇa's curse, and Kṛṣṇa's closing account of how Śiva made the massacre possible.

The Sauptika ("of the sleepers") Parva is the shortest of the war-books. The Parvasaṃgraha gives it **18 adhyāyas and 870 ślokas** (Mbh CE 1.2.189–190: *aṣṭādaśāsminn adhyāyāḥ … śatāny aṣṭau … ślokāś ca saptatiḥ*). The Pune Critical Edition (CE) constitutes the same 18 adhyāyas with **772 verses**, and Ganguli's translation has 18 sections that correspond one-to-one with the CE adhyāyas. It turns the heroic war into atrocity: the war is already over and won when the last three Kaurava survivors slaughter men who are asleep or unarmed. See the [Suryavaṃśa & Chandravaṃśa lineages](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) for the combatants' descent, the [Droṇa Parva](07-drona-parva.md) for Aśvatthāman's father's death (the motive), the character page [Aśvatthāman](../characters/ashvatthama.md), and the [Mahābhārata corpus entry](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md).

Citations below are to the CE by book.adhyāya.verse (e.g. 10.8.14). "Starred" (`*nnnn`) and "App. I" passages are lines found in some manuscripts that the CE editors printed outside the constituted text; they are recorded here as variants, never merged into the main narrative.

## Structure — the two sub-parvas (upa-parvas)

- **The names** — The Parvasaṃgraha's list of the hundred sub-parvas names two here: *sauptikaṃ parva* and *aiṣīkaṃ parva* (Mbh CE 1.2.60–61). Its summary of Book 10 also speaks of mutual curses involving Aśvatthāman and Vyāsa and of the water-offerings and the secret of Karṇa's birth (1.2.178–188). In the CE those last two episodes fall in the [Strī Parva](11-stri-parva.md), not here.
- **Sauptika Parva proper** — the flight of the three survivors, the owl-omen, the debate, the guardian at the gate, the self-offering to Śiva, the night massacre, and Duryodhana's death. This is conventionally CE adhy. 1–9.
- **Aiṣīka Parva** ("of the reed") — the news reaching Yudhiṣṭhira, Draupadī's fast, the pursuit, the Brahmaśiras released from a reed, Vyāsa and Nārada's intervention, the weapon sent into the wombs, the surrender of the gem, Kṛṣṇa's curse, and Kṛṣṇa's account of Śiva's power. This is conventionally CE adhy. 10–18. It is named for the *iṣīkā*, the stalk of grass or reed that Aśvatthāman charges as a missile (10.13.17, 10.13.20).
  - *Note:* the GRETIL CE e-text carries no sub-parva headings, so the 1–9 / 10–18 split is the conventional division by content.

## The three survivors flee to the forest

- **Only three Kaurava warriors remain** — The sole surviving Kaurava warriors are **[Aśvatthāman](../characters/ashvatthama.md)** (Droṇa's son), **[Kṛpa](../characters/kripa.md)** (his maternal uncle) and **[Kṛtavarman](../characters/kritavarma.md)** (the Sātvata/Bhoja chief). Aśvatthāman says so himself: *vayam eva trayaḥ śiṣṭāḥ* (10.1.63).
- **Aśvatthāman made commander (end of Śalya Parva)** — Before Book 10 begins, the three find Duryodhana lying with broken thighs. Aśvatthāman vows to send all the Pāñcālas to Yama's house "while Vāsudeva watches". Duryodhana orders Kṛpa to fetch a pot of water, and **Kṛpa, at the king's command, consecrates Aśvatthāman as senāpati** (Mbh CE 9.64.35–41; see [Śalya Parva](09-shalya-parva.md)). The night raid is thus, formally, the act of the last Kaurava commander. Book 10 opens with the three already riding away south (10.1.1).
- **Where the Pāṇḍavas were** — That night the Pāṇḍavas, Sātyaki and Kṛṣṇa were not in their camp. Kṛṣṇa had said "we must stay outside the camp, for auspiciousness" (*maṅgalārthāya*), and they spent the night by the Oghavatī river (9.61.35–37). Sañjaya later gives this absence of the Pārthas, Keśava and Sātyaki as the reason Aśvatthāman could do what he had not done before (10.8.146–147).
- **The three rest under a banyan** — They hear the victors' shouts, fear pursuit, and turn east. At nightfall they enter a dense forest and halt under a huge nyagrodha (banyan). Kṛpa and Kṛtavarman fall asleep on the bare ground; Aśvatthāman, "breathing like a snake" with rage, cannot sleep (10.1.1–6, 10.1.16–32).

## The owl-omen — the moral hinge of the parva

- **The owl slaughters the sleeping crows** — Sleepless, Aśvatthāman sees thousands of crows roosting in the banyan. A huge, terrible owl (*ulūka*, *kauśika*) comes on suddenly, gives a soft cry, settles stealthily on a branch and kills the sleeping crows one by one, tearing off wings, heads and feet, until the ground under the tree is strewn with bodies (10.1.34–42; *śabdaṃ mṛduṃ kṛtvā līyamāna iva*, 10.1.38).
- **The lesson he draws** — He reads the owl's "deceitful deed" (*sopadhaṃ karma*) as instruction. In a fair fight he would only die; by stratagem (*chadmanā*) a great destruction of the enemy is possible. He reasons that a kṣatriya may do what the world condemns, since the Pāṇḍavas have done blameworthy things at every step (10.1.43–49). He then cites two verses "sung of old by those who reflect on dharma" (*dharmacintakaiḥ*). They permit striking an enemy army when it is weary, wounded, eating, marching out or coming in, "afflicted with sleep at midnight", leaderless, or divided (10.1.50–52).
  - *Variant (starred passage \*0005, after 10.1.52):* some manuscripts add lines saying that no dharma need be looked for in killing an enemy, and that one should kill him by any means.
  - *Text-internal note:* the parva states both sides of the question. Kṛpa calls the killing of sleepers adharma (10.5.9–12). At the gate Aśvatthāman himself admits his resolve was "turned to adharma" (10.6.30), and the camp's own soldiers say Arjuna would never strike a sleeping or unarmed man (10.8.118).
- **The debate with Kṛpa and Kṛtavarman** — Aśvatthāman wakes the two; ashamed, they at first make no reply (10.1.54–55). The exchange then runs over four adhyāyas:
  - Aśvatthāman laments Duryodhana's fall. He calls it unfair that Bhīma touched an anointed king's head with his foot (10.1.56–66).
  - Kṛpa speaks on destiny (*daiva*) and human effort. He urges that they go and ask **Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Gāndhārī and Vidura** what to do (10.2.31).
  - Aśvatthāman answers that he was born a brāhmaṇa but follows kṣatriya dharma by ill luck. He will make the Pāñcālas sleeping in their camp follow Duryodhana, and he will move among them "like Rudra wielding the Pināka" (10.3.19–34).
  - Kṛpa offers to fight beside him openly at dawn, and urges him to rest tonight (10.4.1–3).
  - Kṛpa then gives the dharma objection directly. Those who are asleep, have laid down their arms, have left their chariots, say "I am yours", or seek refuge must not be killed. Whoever attacks the trusting, sleeping Pāñcālas "will sink in a bottomless hell" (10.5.9–12).
  - Aśvatthāman replies with the Pāṇḍavas' own breaches: Droṇa killed after laying down his weapons, Karṇa with his wheel sunk, Bhīṣma disarmed, Bhūriśravas in prāya, and Duryodhana struck unfairly (10.5.16–24). He does not care if he is reborn a worm or an insect (10.5.25).
- **Kṛpa and Kṛtavarman follow** — When Aśvatthāman yokes his horses and sets off, the two say they share his joy and sorrow and follow him (10.5.28–37). He tells them to arm and wait. He intends to kill Dhṛṣṭadyumna "like a beast" (*paśuvat*), so that the prince will not reach the worlds won by those slain by weapons (10.5.32–35). At the camp the two stand at the gate (*śibiradvāri*, 10.8.5). Aśvatthāman tells them they are equal to destroying all the kṣatriyas, let alone the sleeping remnant, and says he will enter and move "like Death" so that no man escapes him alive (10.8.6–8). The explicit charge that the two must see to this is a starred line (\*0028, after 10.8.8), which Ganguli's vulgate translates.
  - *Variant (starred passage \*0016, after 10.5.38):* some manuscripts insert a compact between Vāsudeva and Śiva (*vāsudevavṛṣadhvajau*) to destroy the Pāñcālas. This is not in the constituted text.

## The gate of the camp — the divine guardian

- **A towering being blocks the gate** — At the camp gate Aśvatthāman meets a huge being (*bhūta*), bright as sun and moon. It wears a blood-dripping tiger-skin and a black-antelope upper garment, has a great snake for its sacred thread, and holds many raised weapons. Its face is set with **thousands of eyes**, and flames pour from its mouth, nostrils, ears and eyes. From those flames issue **hundreds and thousands of Hṛṣīkeśas** (Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa forms) bearing conch, discus and mace (10.6.3–9).
- **His weapons fail against it** — Aśvatthāman showers it with celestial weapons, and it swallows his arrows "as the submarine fire swallows the ocean's waters". His flaming chariot-spear (*rathaśakti*) strikes it and shatters. His gold-hilted sword reaches it and vanishes "like a tuft of cotton" (*vilayaṃ tūlavad yayau*). It swallows his mace. Disarmed, he sees the whole sky filled with **Janārdanas** (10.6.10–17).
- **Aśvatthāman's remorse and turn to Śiva** — Recalling Kṛpa's advice, he lists those against whom one may not use weapons, including the sleeping and the newly woken. He concludes that his resolve has turned to adharma and that this being is its fruit, "a rod of divine punishment" (*daivadaṇḍa*). He says he cannot recognise it, and resolves to take refuge in **Mahādeva** (10.6.18–34).
- **The hymn and the self-offering** — Aśvatthāman praises Śiva with a long string of epithets (10.7.2–11) and vows to sacrifice himself. A **golden altar appears of itself** with a blazing fire on it (10.7.13). Hosts of Śiva's *gaṇas*, beast-faced, blood-drinking and armed, crowd round to test him (10.7.14–49). He offers himself with bows as fuel, arrows as the sacred grass and his own self as the oblation (10.7.50–57). Then he climbs onto the altar and sits down in the fire (10.7.58).
- **Śiva's explanation, the sword, and the possession** — Mahādeva appears "as if smiling". Kṛṣṇa has worshipped him perfectly, so no one is dearer to him than Kṛṣṇa. "Honouring him, and **testing you**, I suddenly guarded the Pāñcālas and made many illusions (*māyāḥ*)." But now they are overcome by Time and have no life left (10.7.60–63). Śiva then **enters Aśvatthāman's body** and gives him a spotless sword. Blazing with that god-made energy, Aśvatthāman goes to the camp with invisible beings and rākṣasas around him, "like the Lord himself" (10.7.64–66).
  - *Who was the guardian? (text-internal):* the narrator never names the gate-being, and Aśvatthāman says he cannot recognise it (10.6). Śiva's own words claim the protective illusions as his (10.7.62). That is the textual basis for reading the guardian as Śiva's manifestation; the flood of Hṛṣīkeśas pouring from it also ties it to Kṛṣṇa. Śiva's possession of Aśvatthāman is likewise constituted CE text (10.7.64).
  - *Agency:* the text deliberately doubles the agent. The massacre is Aśvatthāman's vengeful crime, and it is also Rudra's work. Kṛṣṇa states the second view explicitly at the end of the book: "it was not done by Droṇa's son; it was Mahādeva's grace" (10.18.26; see below).

## The night massacre (the Sauptika Parva proper)

- **Entry** — Aśvatthāman enters the camp "by a place where there was no gate" (*advāreṇa*, 10.8.9) and goes softly to Dhṛṣṭadyumna's quarters (10.8.10).
- **[Dhṛṣṭadyumna](../characters/dhrishtadyumna.md) killed first** — The Pāñcāla prince sleeps on a great bed spread with white linen, flowers and incense. Aśvatthāman **wakes him with a kick**, seizes him by the hair as he rises and grinds him into the ground. Then he **tramples his throat and chest** and kills him "with the death of a sacrificial beast" (*paśumāram*) while he roars and writhes (10.8.12–18). Dhṛṣṭadyumna claws at him and gasps, "Son of my teacher, kill me with a weapon, do not delay", so that he may reach the worlds of the righteous. Aśvatthāman answers that there are no such worlds for a teacher's killer, so he does not deserve death by a weapon, and finishes him with heel-blows to the vitals (10.8.19–21). The women and guards, waking, take the killer for some supernatural being (*bhūta*) and dare not cry out. Afterwards the women say they cannot tell whether he is a rākṣasa or a man (10.8.22–30). When warriors come running at the women's cries, Aśvatthāman cuts them down with the **Rudrāstra** (10.8.31).
  - *Variant (starred passage \*0030, after 10.8.17):* some manuscripts have Aśvatthāman unstring his bow, tie the bowstring round Dhṛṣṭadyumna's throat and **strangle** him. The constituted CE has death by trampling, without any weapon.
- **Uttamaujas and Yudhāmanyu** — He kills **Uttamaujas** in his bed in the same way, by trampling his throat and chest. **Yudhāmanyu**, thinking a rākṣasa had killed his friend, runs up and strikes Aśvatthāman in the chest with a mace. Aśvatthāman throws him down and kills him "like a beast" (10.8.32–35). He then cuts down sleeping chariot-warriors and whole encampments with the sword (10.8.36–43).
- **The Upapāṇḍavas (Draupadeyas) slain — awake and fighting** — The din wakes Draupadī's sons, who learn that Dhṛṣṭadyumna is dead and **shower Aśvatthāman with arrows**; Śikhaṇḍin and the Prabhadrakas join them (10.8.44–46). Aśvatthāman leaps down with his shield of a thousand moons and his sword (10.8.47–49):
  - **Prativindhya** is struck in the belly (10.8.50).
  - **Sutasoma** wounds him with a lance and charges; Aśvatthāman cuts off his sword-arm and then strikes him in the side (10.8.51–52).
  - **Śatānīka**, Nakula's son, hits him in the chest with a chariot-wheel and is beheaded (10.8.53–54).
  - **Śrutakarman** rushes at him with a spiked club (*parigha*) and strikes him hard on the left (CE *savye … phalake*; Ganguli: "on the left part of his forehead"). He is struck in the face with the sword (10.8.55–56).
  - **Śrutakīrti** attacks with arrows, which Aśvatthāman stops with his shield before beheading him (10.8.57–58).
  - *Note:* popular retellings often have the boys killed in their sleep. In the CE's narration of the raid they wake and fight, and they are killed in combat inside a camp surprised in its sleep. The CE's own later reports speak more loosely: the charioteer says Draupadī's sons were killed "careless and asleep" (10.10.2), and Draupadī speaks of "the killing of the sleeping" (10.11.13). The version in which they are beheaded asleep is the Bhāgavata's (below).
  - *Variant (text-internal — the Draupadeyas' names and fathers):* the Ādi Parva itself gives three different lists.
    - CE 1.57.102–103: Prativindhya (Yudhiṣṭhira), Sutasoma (Bhīma), **Śrutakīrti** (Arjuna), Śatānīka (Nakula), **Śrutasena** (Sahadeva).
    - CE 1.90.82: Arjuna's son is **Śrutakīrti** and Sahadeva's is **Śrutakarman**.
    - CE 1.213.72–78: Arjuna's son is **Śrutakarman** and Sahadeva's is **Śrutasena**.
    - The Sauptika killing-list names Śrutakarman and Śrutakīrti but not Śrutasena (10.8.55–58).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.7.14):* here Aśvatthāman cuts off the heads of Draupadī's sons **while they sleep** (*svapatāṃ śirāṃsi*). He thinks it will please his master Duryodhana, but in fact it displeases him (*vipriyam eva tasya*).
  - *Later retelling, not in the CE:* the idea that the boys were **mistaken for the Pāṇḍavas** in the dark appears in modern retellings; neither the CE nor Ganguli has it.
- **[Śikhaṇḍin](../characters/amba-shikhandi.md) killed** — **Śikhaṇḍin** (the cause of Bhīṣma's fall — see [Bhīṣma Parva](06-bhishma-parva.md)) hits Aśvatthāman between the eyebrows with an arrow and is **cut in two** with the sword (10.8.59–60). Aśvatthāman then falls on the Prabhadrakas and on what remains of Virāṭa's force (10.8.61).
- **Drupada's house and the general host** — He makes a terrible slaughter of Drupada's sons, grandsons and allies, singling them out one by one (10.8.62–63). Then he moves on to the Pāṇḍu–Sṛñjaya soldiers, who have no armour or weapons (10.8.70–80). The contingents the text names are Pāñcālas, Sṛñjayas, Somakas, Prabhadrakas and the remnant of the Matsyas/Virāṭa's force (10.8.61, 10.8.141, 10.8.150).
- **Panic in the dark** — Elephants and horses break their tethers and trample men. The dust doubles the darkness, men kill their own comrades without recognising them, and the guards abandon the gates (10.8.81–98). Some soldiers say that this cannot be the Dhārtarāṣṭras' work, only that of rākṣasas, and that it is happening because the Pārthas are away, since Arjuna never strikes a sleeping or unarmed man (10.8.115–119).
- **The gate becomes a killing-ground** — **Kṛpa and Kṛtavarman** stand **at the gate** (*dvāradeśe*). They kill everyone who flees the camp and spare no one, even men with joined hands and loosened hair (10.8.100–102). To please Aśvatthāman they also **set the camp on fire in three places** (*triṣu deśeṣu*, 10.8.103). By the firelight he goes on killing with the sword (10.8.104–124).
- **Rudra, Kāla and the Night of Death** — The parva repeatedly casts the slaughter as cosmic destruction:
  - Aśvatthāman is "the Ender sent by Time" (*kālasṛṣṭa ivāntakaḥ*, 10.8.71). He fells men "as Paśupati fells beasts" (10.8.122). At dawn he blazes "like the fire at the end of the yuga" that has burnt all beings to ash (10.8.137).
  - The soldiers see **Kālarātri**, the Night of Death. She is a dark woman with red mouth and eyes, red garlands and red garment, a noose in her hand, smiling and leading men, horses and elephants away bound. The leading warriors had seen her and Aśvatthāman in their dreams every night since the war began. Now they recognise the dream, and the text says they had been "struck down by fate beforehand" (10.8.64–69). Men who rush at Aśvatthāman are "offered to Kālarātri" (10.8.78).
  - Rākṣasas and piśācas feast on the dead (10.8.126–135).
- **Timing** — Within half the night (*rajanyās tv ardhena*) he has sent the great Pāṇḍava army to Yama's house (10.8.125). At first light (*pratyūṣakāle*) he leaves the camp, his sword-hilt so caked with blood that hand and sword seem one, his grief for his father gone (10.8.136–138).
- **The three report to Duryodhana** — They meet, exchange congratulations and embrace. Aśvatthāman says that all the Pāñcālas, Draupadī's sons, the Somakas and the remnant of the Matsyas are dead, and they go to find the king (10.8.148–150). Kṛpa and Aśvatthāman lament over Duryodhana, who is dying amid beasts of prey. Aśvatthāman asks him to tell Droṇa in the next world that Dhṛṣṭadyumna has been killed (10.9.1–45).
  - Aśvatthāman: "**Seven** are left on the Pāṇḍava side — the five brothers, Vāsudeva and Sātyaki — and three of us." Draupadī's sons, Dhṛṣṭadyumna's children, all the Pāñcālas and the remnant of the Matsyas are slain: "the Pāṇḍavas are sonless" (10.9.47–51).
  - Duryodhana: "What neither Gaṅgā's son, nor Karṇa, nor your father did for me, you have done … I count myself equal to Maghavan (Indra)." Then: "We shall meet again in heaven", and he dies (10.9.52–55).
  - *Variants (starred passages \*0046–\*0048, after 10.9.55–56):* some manuscripts add Duryodhana's spirit rising like lightning, or the three seeing him in a celestial chariot surrounded by Indra's hosts.
- **Sañjaya loses his divine sight** — At dawn Sañjaya hurries to the city. With Duryodhana gone to heaven, the divine vision given him by the ṛṣi (Vyāsa) leaves him (10.9.57–58). That ends his eyewitness narration of the war; Vaiśaṃpāyana narrates the rest.

## Aftermath in the camp — discovery

- **The lone survivor brings the news** — When the night is over, **Dhṛṣṭadyumna's charioteer** (*dhṛṣṭadyumnasya sārathiḥ*) reports the massacre to Yudhiṣṭhira. He says that Draupadī's sons and Drupada's sons were killed while careless and asleep, and that he alone escaped, slipping past Kṛtavarman while the Bhoja was distracted (10.10.1–6).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's lament on heedlessness** — Yudhiṣṭhira collapses and is held up by Sātyaki. Then he laments: "Being defeated, they conquer; conquering, we are conquered" (*jīyamānā jayanty anye jayamānā vayaṃ jitāḥ*, 10.10.10). His sons escaped Karṇa, Droṇa and Bhīṣma only to die of **heedlessness** (*pramāda*), "like merchants who cross the ocean and drown in a shallow stream". There is no greater cause of death than pramāda (10.10.9–23). He sends Nakula to bring Draupadī and goes to the field to see the bodies (10.10.26–30).
- **Draupadī's grief and vow** — Nakula brings **[Draupadī](../characters/draupadi.md)** from **Upaplavya**, and she collapses; Bhīma lifts her (10.11.4–9). She taunts Yudhiṣṭhira bitterly, "By good luck you have the earth …", and **vows to fast unto death (prāya)** unless Aśvatthāman and his followers are killed (10.11.10–15). When Yudhiṣṭhira asks how she can be sure of his death in a distant forest, she names the **gem born on his head** (*sahajo maṇiḥ śirasi*). She will live if it is brought to her and bound on Yudhiṣṭhira's head (10.11.20–21). Then she urges Bhīma, recalling how he saved them at Vāraṇāvata, from Hiḍimba, and from Kīcaka at Virāṭa's city (10.11.22–25).
  - *Variant (App. I No. 1, after 10.11.11):* some manuscripts expand her speech into a long lament. In it she demands a funeral pyre and threatens poison, rope or a fall, and she calls Aśvatthāman's deed the work of fate "in the form of Droṇa's son".
- **Bhīma sets out** — Bhīma takes his bow and sets off along Aśvatthāman's track, with **Nakula as his charioteer** (10.11.26–30).
- **Kṛṣṇa's warning and the story of the discus** — Kṛṣṇa tells Yudhiṣṭhira that Bhīma is in danger.
  - Droṇa first taught the **Brahmaśiras**, a weapon that could burn the earth, to Arjuna. Then, at his son's insistence and not gladly, he taught it to Aśvatthāman, ordering him never to use it on human beings even in the gravest danger. Later Droṇa added, "you will not stand in the path of the good", and the son wandered the earth in despair (10.12.1–10).
  - Kṛṣṇa then tells a story of his own. While the Pāṇḍavas were in exile, Aśvatthāman came to **Dvārakā** and found Kṛṣṇa alone on the seashore. He offered him the Brahmaśiras in exchange for **the discus**. Kṛṣṇa told him to take whatever weapon he could wield. Aśvatthāman could not even move the discus, with his left hand or with his right (10.12.11–24). Kṛṣṇa remarked that not Arjuna, nor Pradyumna, nor Balarāma had ever asked for it. Aśvatthāman admitted he had wanted it in order to fight Kṛṣṇa himself, and left with horses and gems (10.12.25–39). "He knows the Brahmaśiras; therefore Vṛkodara must be protected" (10.12.40).
- **The pursuit** — Kṛṣṇa, Arjuna and Yudhiṣṭhira follow on Kṛṣṇa's chariot. They catch up with Bhīma but cannot stop him (10.13.1–11).

## The Aiṣīka Parva — the Brahmaśiras duel

- **Aśvatthāman found on the Gaṅgā bank** — Bhīma reaches the bank of the **Bhāgīrathī (Gaṅgā)**. There he sees **Vyāsa** sitting by the water among ṛṣis, and beside him Aśvatthāman, smeared with ghee, wearing a garment of kuśa-grass, his hair covered with dust. Bhīma rushes at him, bow drawn, crying "Stand! Stand!" (10.13.12–15). (The text says nothing of a hermitage.)
- **The Brahmaśiras on a reed** — Seeing Bhīma, and Kṛṣṇa's chariot with the two brothers behind him, Aśvatthāman thinks his hour has come. He calls the celestial weapon to mind and takes up a **reed/grass-stalk (iṣīkā)** in his left hand. Crying "**So that there be no Pāṇḍavas!**" (*apāṇḍavāya*), he releases it "to bewilder all the worlds". A fire springs up in the reed as if to burn the three worlds (10.13.16–20). This act names the sub-parva (Aiṣīka).
  - *Weapon name (text-internal):* the text calls this weapon the **Brahmaśiras** (10.12.4, 10.12.14, 10.12.40, 10.15.19, 10.15.23) and in the same breath **brahmāstra** (10.15.21). In Vyāsa's speech Arjuna's counter-weapon is also the Brahmaśiras (10.15.19).
- **Arjuna counters** — Kṛṣṇa reads Aśvatthāman's intent from his gestures and tells Arjuna to release the weapon Droṇa taught him, "to protect yourself and your brothers" (10.14.1–3). Arjuna steps down from the chariot and wishes well first to his teacher's son, then to himself and his brothers. He bows to the gods and his elders, thinks of what is auspicious and releases his weapon with the words "**Let the weapon be quelled by the weapon**" (10.14.4–6). The two weapons blaze like the fire at the end of a yuga; thunder rolls, meteors fall and the earth shakes (10.14.7–10).
- **Vyāsa and Nārada intervene** — **Nārada** and **Vyāsa** ("grandsire of the Bhāratas") appear and stand **between the two blazing weapons** to calm the two warriors (10.14.11–15). Their rebuke is that the great warriors who have died, though they knew many weapons, **never used this one on human beings** (10.14.16).
- **Arjuna can withdraw his; Aśvatthāman cannot** — Arjuna, with joined palms, explains that he released his weapon only to quell the other, and **withdraws it** (10.15.1–5). The narrator explains that even the gods find this hard. The weapon is "born of brahman-energy"; no one but a **brahmacārin** of disciplined self can recall it, and if anyone else tries, it cuts off his head and those of his kin. Arjuna, truthful, a brahmacārin and obedient to his elders, never used it even in extreme danger (10.15.5–10).
  - Aśvatthāman **cannot** recall his. He says he released it from fear of Bhīma, because Bhīma had acted unrighteously in killing Duryodhana, and that it is now set on killing all the Pāṇḍavas (10.15.11–18).
  - *Note (text-internal):* the brahmacarya test is stated in the text itself (10.15.7–10).
- **Vyāsa's terms** — Vyāsa answers on two points:
  - Arjuna released the weapon not in anger but only to quell Aśvatthāman's. Wherever the **Brahmaśiras is struck down by another supreme weapon, the rain-god sends no rain on that kingdom for twelve years**, which is why Arjuna did not destroy Aśvatthāman's weapon with his own (10.15.19–24). The warning is Vyāsa's alone, not part of the two ṛṣis' joint rebuke.
  - Vyāsa tells Aśvatthāman to withdraw the weapon, and to **give the Pāṇḍavas the gem on his head; in return they will grant him his life** (10.15.25–27).
- **The gem's powers (in his own words)** — Aśvatthāman says the gem is worth more than all the riches of Pāṇḍavas and Kauravas. Whoever wears it need not fear **weapons, disease or hunger**, nor **gods, dānavas or nāgas**, nor **rākṣasas or thieves** (10.15.28–30).
- **The weapon sent into the wombs, with Vyāsa's leave** — "Here is the gem, here am I — but the reed will fall." He cannot recall it, so it will fall into **the wombs of the Pāṇḍava women** (10.15.31). **Vyāsa sanctions this**: "Do so, and have no other purpose; release it into the wombs of the Pāṇḍavas and stop." Aśvatthāman releases it (10.15.32–33).
  - *Variant (starred passage \*0067, after 10.15.33):* here Kṛṣṇa tells him to spare one future child, the one who will offer the ancestral piṇḍa, and to send the weapon into the other wombs.
- **Kṛṣṇa and Uttarā's child** — Kṛṣṇa, "rejoicing", recalls a prophecy. A brāhmaṇa who saw **Uttarā** (Virāṭa's daughter, Arjuna's daughter-in-law, Abhimanyu's widow — see [Droṇa Parva](07-drona-parva.md)) at Upaplavya told her: "When the Kurus are destroyed (*parikṣīṇeṣu kuruṣu*), a son will be born to you; hence the child in the womb will be **Parikṣit**" (10.16.1–4).
  - The name is **explained in the text** from *pari-kṣī* ("dwindled away"). It means the son born when the Kurus had dwindled away, and **a brāhmaṇa gives it, not Kṛṣṇa**. (The popular gloss "the tested one" comes from *parīkṣ-* "to examine, test". It is the Bhāgavata's explanation, that he examines (*parīkṣeta*) every man for the one he saw while in the womb (BhP 1.12.30), and it is not this text's.)
  - Aśvatthāman retorts that his weapon will fall on Uttarā's foetus, the one Kṛṣṇa wants to protect (10.16.5–7).
  - Kṛṣṇa answers: "The fall of the supreme weapon will not be in vain. The child **will be born dead and will then gain long life**" (10.16.8). He adds that Parikṣit will learn all weapons from Kṛpa and rule for sixty years, and will become king of the Kurus "before your very eyes" (10.16.13–15).
  - *Variant (starred passages \*0068 and \*0070, after 10.16.8 and 10.16.15):* some manuscripts, followed by Ganguli's vulgate, add Kṛṣṇa's explicit promise: "though he be burnt by your weapon's fire, I shall revive him".
  - The actual revival of the stillborn Parikṣit is narrated in the [Āśvamedhika Parva](14-ashvamedhika-parva.md) (cf. Parvasaṃgraha 1.2.207).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.8.8–16):* the weapon pursues Uttarā later, as Kṛṣṇa is about to leave for Dvārakā, and the Pāṇḍavas see five blazing shafts coming at them. Kṛṣṇa guards the Pāṇḍavas with his Sudarśana and **covers Uttarā's embryo from within by his own māyā**, so the Brahmaśiras is quelled on meeting Vaiṣṇava energy. In this version he protects the child in the womb, where the Mbh has him revive it after a stillbirth.

## The surrender of the gem and Kṛṣṇa's curse

- **Kṛṣṇa's curse** — Kṛṣṇa calls Aśvatthāman a coward and a sinner, "a repeated evildoer, a killer of children", and curses him (10.16.9–12):
  - *trīṇi varṣasahasrāṇi cariṣyasi mahīm imām* — **for three thousand years** he will wander this earth;
  - he will find no one to speak with, and will go alone and without companions through empty lands, with no place among men;
  - he will **stink of pus and blood**, live in impassable wilds, and carry **every disease** (*sarvavyādhisamanvitaḥ*).
  - The curse names no head-wound; that belongs to the later tradition (below).
- **Vyāsa confirms the curse** — In the constituted text (10.16.16–17) Vyāsa says that Aśvatthāman acted with contempt for them, and that such conduct is unworthy of a brāhmaṇa. So what Devakī's son has said will undoubtedly happen: "go quickly from here".
- **Aśvatthāman's reply** — "I will stay among men together with you, holy one; may the Lord and this Puruṣottama be true to their word" (10.16.18).
- **The gem is handed over, not cut out** — Aśvatthāman **gives** the gem (*pradāya … maṇim*) to the Pāṇḍavas and goes off dejected into the forest as they all watch (10.16.19).
  - *Three layers:* popular retellings have the gem cut from his forehead, leaving an unhealing wound. The Mahābhārata CE has **no cutting and no wound**: the gem is demanded by Vyāsa (10.15.27), not by Kṛṣṇa, and handed over. The traditions are layered as follows:
    - *Mahābhārata (CE 10.15.27–31, 10.16.19):* the gem is surrendered on Vyāsa's terms, in exchange for his life.
    - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.7.16–57):* Arjuna alone pursues him with Kṛṣṇa as charioteer (1.7.17). Aśvatthāman looses the Brahmaśiras, Arjuna answers with his own, and Arjuna himself withdraws both (1.7.19–32). Then Arjuna **binds him with a rope "like a sacrificial beast"** and brings him to the camp. Draupadī, moved, bows and pleads: "Let him go — he is a brāhmaṇa, our guru" (1.7.42–48). Bhīma wants him killed; Kṛṣṇa says a *brahmabandhu* must not be killed, though an aggressor deserves death (1.7.53). So **Arjuna cuts the gem from his head together with his hair** (*maṇiṃ jahāra mūrdhanyaṃ … sahamūrdhajam*, 1.7.55) and expels him from the camp. The text adds that shaving the head, taking his wealth and expelling him is the "death" of a brahmabandhu (1.7.57). In this version Vyāsa and Nārada do not intervene, and there is no 3,000-year curse.
    - *[folk] / [oral tradition]:* the belief that he still roams with a **festering, unhealing forehead-wound** is not found in either text. It is popular belief (see [Aśvatthāman](../characters/ashvatthama.md) and [The Cirañjīvis](../the-chiranjivis.md)).
- **The gem brought to Draupadī** — The Pāṇḍavas, with Kṛṣṇa, Vyāsa and Nārada at their head, hurry back to Draupadī, who is keeping her fast (10.16.20–24).
  - With the king's leave **Bhīma** hands her the gem: "This is your gem; your sons' killer is conquered." He reminds her of her fierce words to Kṛṣṇa before the peace mission, "I have no husbands, no sons, no brothers …". He says they let Aśvatthāman go **because he is a brāhmaṇa and out of respect for their teacher**: his fame is destroyed, only his body is left, and he has been parted from his gem and made to lay down his weapons (10.16.25–32).
  - Draupadī: "I have only wanted the debt repaid; **the teacher's son is my teacher** (*guruputro gurur mama*). Let the king bind this gem on his head" (10.16.33).
  - **Yudhiṣṭhira** wears it "as the teacher's gift" and shines "like a mountain with the moon on it". Draupadī rises and ends her fast (10.16.34–36).
- **Duration of the curse** — Both the CE and the vulgate translated by Ganguli give **3,000 years** (10.16.10).
  - No starred or appendix passage in the GRETIL CE e-text extends the term to the end of Kali Yuga. (The e-text does not carry the full apparatus of variant readings.)
  - The belief that **Aśvatthāman still wanders India** belongs to the later **cirañjīvin** tradition. It rests on the popular verse *aśvatthāmā balir vyāso hanūmāṃś ca vibhīṣaṇaḥ / kṛpaḥ paraśurāmaś ca saptaite cirajīvinaḥ*, which is a later smṛti verse and not part of the Sauptika text [late text] [folk]. See [The Cirañjīvis](../the-chiranjivis.md).
- **Aśvatthāman's divine portions** — The Ādi Parva says Droṇa's son was born from the union in one person of portions of **Mahādeva and Antaka (Death), of Kāma and Krodha** (*mahādevāntakābhyāṃ ca kāmāt krodhāc ca … ekatvam upapannānāṃ jajñe*, CE 1.61.66–67).

## Kṛṣṇa explains the massacre — Rudra's power (adhy. 17–18)

- **Yudhiṣṭhira's question** — How could "the petty, sinful" Aśvatthāman, no great fighter, alone kill his sons, Drupada's sons and Dhṛṣṭadyumna, whom even Droṇa could not face (10.17.1–6)?
- **Kṛṣṇa's answer** — Aśvatthāman had taken refuge with **Mahādeva**, who, when pleased, can give even immortality (10.17.6–9). Kṛṣṇa then tells two Rudra stories:
  - *Rudra in the waters (10.17.10–26).* Brahmā asks Rudra to create creatures, but Rudra plunges into the waters to do tapas. Brahmā makes another creator, who produces **seven Prajāpatis, Dakṣa first** (10.17.15), and they populate the world. When Rudra rises and finds creation already done, he angrily **tears off his liṅga and sets it in the earth** (10.17.21–23). Then he goes to the foot of Mount Muñjavat to do tapas (10.17.26).
  - *Rudra and the gods' sacrifice (10.18.1–24).* At the end of the age of the gods (*devayuga*), the gods arrange a sacrifice and give Rudra no share. Armed with a bow fashioned from the "world-sacrifice" (*lokayajña*), with the *vaṣaṭ* call as its string (10.18.4–7), he pierces Sacrifice in the heart, and it flees with Agni in the form of a deer (10.18.13). He breaks Savitṛ's arms, Bhaga's eyes and Pūṣan's teeth (10.18.16). Once appeased, he throws his anger into the waters, where it burns as fire, and restores the gods' limbs and the sacrifice; the gods then assign him the oblations (10.18.21–23).
  - *Note:* Dakṣa is not named as the sacrificer in this telling.
- **The verdict** — "When he was angry the whole world was disturbed; when he was pleased it was well again — and he was pleased with Aśvatthāman. That is why your sons and the Pāñcālas were killed. Do not dwell on it: **it was not done by Droṇa's son; it was Mahādeva's grace**. Do what must be done next" (10.18.24–26). The book ends here.
- *[scholarly]* Alf Hiltebeitel (*The Ritual of Battle*, 1976) argues that the **mythic model of the whole Sauptika episode is Śiva's destruction of Dakṣa's sacrifice**. On this reading Aśvatthāman, like Śiva, is the excluded outsider. Denied his share in the "sacrifice of battle" (*raṇayajña*), he takes it after the rite is over, while Kṛṣṇa is absent from the camp by design, as Viṣṇu is absent when Śiva destroys. (This summary follows Gregory Bailey's review of Hiltebeitel in *Religious Traditions*, citing Hiltebeitel pp. 315, 317.)

## The eight survivors

- **What the text counts: seven and three** — At the close of the night Aśvatthāman counts **seven** left on the Pāṇḍava side: the **five Pāṇḍavas**, **Vāsudeva (Kṛṣṇa)** and **Sātyaki**. He counts **three** on the Kaurava side: **himself**, **Kṛpa** and **Kṛtavarman** (10.9.47–48). The Parvasaṃgraha likewise says the five Pārthas and Sātyaki were saved by relying on Kṛṣṇa's strength, and the rest died (1.2.182). This gives seven + three = ten surviving warriors.
  - *Yuyutsu, the conventional eighth:* this page's heading follows a common count of "eight" that adds **Yuyutsu**, Dhṛtarāṣṭra's son who fought for the Pāṇḍavas. He is not in Aśvatthāman's count. He is named entering the captured Kaurava camp with the Pāṇḍavas (9.61.2) but not among those who went out with Kṛṣṇa for the night (9.61.36). Earlier, during Day 18, he had escorted the royal women to Hāstinapura (9.28.75–92). He is alive afterwards, at the funeral rites (11.26.25).
  - *Detail:* the parva marks the true end of the fighting. What follows in the [Strī Parva](11-stri-parva.md) is mourning, not battle.
