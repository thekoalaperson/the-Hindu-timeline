# Mahāprasthānika Parva — The Book of the Great Journey

> **Where this sits:** Cosmic address → Shveta-Varaha Kalpa › 7th (Vaivasvata) [Manvantara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md) › 28th Mahāyuga › end of [Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) sliding into [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).
> Book 17 of 18 in the [Mahābhārata](README.md). The penultimate parva: the Pāṇḍavas renounce the throne and walk north to die one by one. Follows the Mausala Parva (the destruction of the Yādavas); leads into the final [Svargārohaṇa Parva](18-svargarohana-parva.md) (Yudhiṣṭhira in heaven).

The shortest book of the epic — **3 adhyāyas (chapters), no upa-parvas, ~106 ślokas (Critical Edition)** — yet doctrinally one of the densest. It dramatizes *prāyopaveśa / mahāprasthāna* (the sanctioned "great departure," walking unto death as a final renunciation) and stages the epic's last and starkest test of dharma: Yudhiṣṭhira and a dog. It is set precisely on the hinge between the ages — 36 years after Kurukṣetra, after Kṛṣṇa's departure has already triggered the onset of [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) (3102 BCE by tradition).

---

## I. The Renunciation and Succession (Adhyāya 1, opening)

- **Vyāsa's counsel / the prompt to leave** - At the close of the preceding Mausala Parva, the sage Vyāsa (and, in the broader frame, news of Kṛṣṇa's death and Dvārakā's submergence) advises the brothers that their life's purpose is fulfilled and they should renounce the kingdom. Arjuna conveys this to Yudhiṣṭhira; the brothers and Draupadī all assent (Mbh, Mahāprasthānika Parva 1).
  - *Variant (framing):* Some retellings make the decisive trigger Yudhiṣṭhira's grief at Kṛṣṇa's passing and the dissolution of the Yādavas, rather than an explicit instruction from Vyāsa [folk/retelling tradition].
- **Coronation of Parīkṣit** - Yudhiṣṭhira installs Parīkṣit — grandson of Arjuna, son of Abhimanyu and Uttarā, the lone Kuru heir revived by Kṛṣṇa in the womb — as King of Hastināpura. Kṛpa(ācārya) is appointed his preceptor (Mbh 1).
- **Yuyutsu as regent / steward** - Yuyutsu (Dhṛtarāṣṭra's son by a vaiśya woman, the one Kaurava who crossed over to the Pāṇḍavas before the war) is left as caretaker/regent of the realm (Mbh 1).
- **Vajra crowned at Indraprastha** - Vajra, the surviving Yādava prince (great-grandson of Kṛṣṇa, grandson of Aniruddha), is enthroned over Indraprastha / the Śūrasena (Mathurā) lands — preserving the Yādava line after the Mausala fratricide (Mbh 1).
- **Final rites and distribution** - Yudhiṣṭhira performs the śrāddha (death-rites) for the departed, distributes wealth to brāhmaṇas and dependents, and takes leave of the citizens (Mbh 1).
- **Casting off royalty / the sannyāsa garb** - The five brothers and Draupadī remove their ornaments and royal dress, don bark-cloth/ascetic garments, kindle the sacred fire one last time, and set out — this is the *mahāprasthāna*, the renunciant's walk toward death with no intention of return (Mbh 1).
- **The retinue follows then turns back** - The citizens, women of the palace, and Subhadrā (Arjuna's wife, Kṛṣṇa's sister) accompany them part of the way weeping; they are sent back. Subhadrā remains to look after Parīkṣit's house [as drawn from the Mausala/Mahāprasthānika frame].

## II. The Pilgrimage Route (Adhyāya 1)

- **Departure eastward; the six plus the dog** - The seven set out (the five Pāṇḍavas + Draupadī), faces first turned **east**, walking in *yoga* (yogic absorption), renouncing food beyond what sustains the walk. A **dog** attaches itself to the company and follows throughout (Mbh 1).
- **The surrender of Gāṇḍīva** - They reach the **Lauhitya / "red sea" (lauhityaṃ salilārṇavam** — traditionally identified with the Brahmaputra, or a red-watered ocean). Agni, the fire-god, appears blocking the path and demands Arjuna return the celestial bow **Gāṇḍīva** and the two inexhaustible quivers to Varuṇa, from whom they had been borrowed at the burning of the Khāṇḍava forest. Arjuna casts bow and quivers into the waters (Mbh 1).
  - *Significance:* The disarming of the epic's greatest archer marks the formal end of the kṣatriya phase of their lives.
- **The great circuit of India** - From there they turn **south** to the sea, then up the **west** coast, passing **Dvārakā — found submerged beneath the ocean** (per the Mausala Parva's flooding). They then turn **north**, crossing past Rishikesh region into the **Himālaya** (Mbh 1).
  - *Variant (route detail):* The Vyāsa-online prose and the Critical Edition give a terse account and do not spell out the full south–west–north circuit; the elaborated itinerary (sea → Dvārakā → Himalaya) is drawn out by Wikipedia/commentarial summaries [scholarly synthesis].
- **Crossing the sands toward Mount Meru/Sumeru** - Beyond the Himālaya they cross a vast desert of sand and behold mighty **Mount Meru (Sumeru)**, the cosmic mountain, their final ascent toward Svarga (Mbh 1–2).

## III. The Fall of the Six (Adhyāya 2)

As they cross the high snow and the slopes of Meru, the travelers fall one by one. Bhīma, walking last, each time asks Yudhiṣṭhira why the faultless one has fallen; Yudhiṣṭhira — never looking back, never breaking stride — names the single fault that bars each from a bodily ascent. The order is **Draupadī, Sahadeva, Nakula, Arjuna, Bhīma**, then Yudhiṣṭhira alone with the dog.

- **Draupadī falls first** - Yudhiṣṭhira explains: though she was wedded to all five equally, she harbored **excessive partiality (pakṣapāta) for Arjuna (Dhanañjaya)**; she reaps the fruit of that favoritism today (Mbh 2).
- **Sahadeva falls second** - His fault: **pride in his own wisdom** — he thought no one equaled him in learning/intelligence (Mbh 2).
- **Nakula falls third** - His fault: **vanity over his beauty** — he believed himself the handsomest man alive (Mbh 2).
- **Arjuna falls fourth** - Yudhiṣṭhira's stated reason **varies by source/recension**:
  - *Variant A (vow of arrogance — Critical Edition / Vyāsa-online):* Arjuna had **boasted he would destroy all the enemy in a single day** and failed to make good the vow; his arrogance condemns him (Mbh 2).
  - *Variant B (general pride in prowess — Ganguli/Wikipedia summary):* Arjuna suffered the **pride and vanity of thinking himself the supreme hero**, disdaining all other warriors (Mbh 2).
  - *Variant C (combined):* Many retellings fold both together — pride in being the greatest archer *and* the unfulfilled boast.
- **Bhīma falls fifth** - His faults: **gluttony** (he ate to excess, heedless of others' hunger) **and pride/boasting about his great strength** (Mbh 2).
  - *Note:* Yudhiṣṭhira's serene refusal to turn back or mourn each fall is itself a doctrinal point — detachment (vairāgya) consummated.
- **Why Yudhiṣṭhira alone does not fall** - He is the only one with no clinging fault to drag the body down; he walks on to attempt the rare feat of ascending to heaven **in his mortal body (sa-śarīra)** (Mbh 2).

## IV. Yudhiṣṭhira, the Dog, and Indra (Adhyāya 3)

- **Indra's chariot descends** - Indra (Śakra) arrives in his celestial chariot amid thunder, offering to carry Yudhiṣṭhira to Svarga directly (Mbh 3).
- **"Not without my brothers and Draupadī"** - Yudhiṣṭhira first refuses, unwilling to enter heaven while his brothers and wife lie dead. Indra reassures him: they have already attained heaven, having shed their bodies; Yudhiṣṭhira alone is granted entry **in the flesh** (Mbh 3).
  - *Cross-link:* This sets up the [Svargārohaṇa Parva](18-svargarohana-parva.md), where Yudhiṣṭhira finds his kin — and a final illusory test of heaven and hell.
- **"Not without my dog"** - Indra forbids the dog to board the chariot, declaring dogs have no place in heaven (and that dogs defile the sacrificial offerings). Yudhiṣṭhira refuses to abandon the faithful animal that has shared his entire journey (Mbh 3).
- **The argument over loyalty** - Indra challenges the logic: he abandoned his fallen brothers and Draupadī, yet clings to a beast. Yudhiṣṭhira answers that there is **neither friendship nor enmity with the dead** — he left them only because they had died, not from disloyalty — but the **living, devoted** creature cannot be cast off; to forsake one who has sought his protection (a *bhakta* / *śaraṇāgata*) would be among the gravest sins, equal to (or worse than) killing a brāhmaṇa (Mbh 3).
- **The dog reveals himself as Dharma** - The dog transforms into **Dharma (Yama Dharmarāja), Yudhiṣṭhira's own divine father**, who praises him: he has passed the test of compassion and fidelity, just as he once passed the Yakṣa-praśna (the questions at the lake, Vana Parva) and the test in the cattle-pen (Virāṭa Parva). This is the third such trial by Dharma (Mbh 3).
  - *Variant (identity):* Universally the dog is Dharma; some popular retellings additionally gloss the dog as a symbol of Yudhiṣṭhira's own conscience/karma [folk gloss].
- **Ascent in the body** - With the test passed, Yudhiṣṭhira mounts Indra's chariot and **ascends to heaven in his living, mortal body** — a feat the parva (and the sage Nārada, who meets him en route) declares unprecedented even among the great royal sages (rājarṣis) (Mbh 3).
  - *Detail:* Nārada remarks he has known no other to reach Svarga sa-śarīra; the line underscores Yudhiṣṭhira's singular dharmic stature.

## Themes and Doctrine

- **Mahāprasthāna / prāyopaveśa** - The sanctioned renunciant's death-walk: dharmaśāstra recognizes the "great departure" (and fasting/walking unto death) as a legitimate end for one whose worldly duties are complete. See [karma, saṃsāra & mokṣa](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md) and [āśrama / varṇa](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md) for the renunciation framework.
- **Karma made visible** - Each fall externalizes an inner fault; the parva is a compact catechism on how subtle vices (partiality, pride, vanity, boastfulness, gluttony) bind even the near-perfect. Cf. [purushārthas](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md).
- **Compassion over reward** - Yudhiṣṭhira chooses fidelity to a "low" creature over heaven itself — dharma above svarga-phala (the fruit of heaven).
- **Age-hinge** - The book is staged at the very turn into [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md); the [dharma-bull](../../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md) now stands on one leg, and the last great upholders of dharma exit the world.
