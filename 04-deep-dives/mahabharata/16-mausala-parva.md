# Mausala Parva — The Book of the Clubs

> Cosmic address: Shveta-Varaha Kalpa › 51st year of Brahma › 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara › 28th Mahayuga › **end of Dvapara Yuga**, on the cusp of Kali.
> The 16th of the 18 books (parvas) of the Mahabharata. ↑ Parent: [Mahabharata deep-dives](./README.md) · See also [03-dvapara-yuga](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and [04-kali-yuga](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).

The shortest and bleakest book of the epic. Thirty-six years after the Kurukshetra war, the victorious Yadava (Vrishni-Andhaka) clan annihilates itself in a drunken brawl using clubs (*musala* / *mausala*) sprung from a sage's curse; Balarama and Krishna depart the earth; Dwarka is swallowed by the sea; the divine descent of [Vishnu's Krishna avatar](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) ends. Krishna's departure is traditionally the marker for the **onset of Kali Yuga (3102 BCE)**. The book takes its name from the iron clubs (*musala*). Critical edition: 8 adhyayas / ~273 shlokas; vulgate (Gita Press / Ganguli): 9 chapters; no sub-parvas.

## The seeds of doom — two curses

- **Gandhari's curse on Krishna** - After Kurukshetra, the bereaved Kuru queen Gandhari, having lost all hundred sons, curses Krishna: because he allowed the slaughter, his own Vrishni-Andhaka kinsmen will perish in fratricidal strife and he himself will die ignobly, alone in the wilderness, 36 years hence (Mausala Parva 1; this curse is first uttered in the [Stri Parva, book 11](./11-stri-parva.md)).
  - *Variant (Mahabharata, Mausala 1):* Krishna accepts the curse calmly, saying he already knows the Yadavas can be destroyed by none but themselves, and that the curse merely confirms his own will.
- **The sages' curse on Samba's iron club** - At the holy spot Pindaraka (Pindataraka, near Dwarka/Prabhasa), idle Yadava princes try to mock visiting rishis: they dress Krishna's son **Samba** as a pregnant woman and ask the sages what "she" will bear. The enraged sages curse that Samba will deliver an iron club (*musala*) that will destroy the whole Vrishni and Andhaka race; Krishna alone the curse cannot touch except by his own choosing (Mausala Parva 1).
  - *Variant (Mahabharata):* the offended sages are named as **Vishvamitra, Kanva, and Narada**.
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 5.37; later listings):* a larger company of rishis is named — including Vishvamitra, Durvasa, Narada, Kanva and others — visiting at Pindaraka.
  - *Variant (general Puranic gloss):* the prank is sometimes told as the princes asking whether the "woman" will bear a son or a daughter, the sages answering with the club-curse to punish their arrogance and irreverence.

## The pulverised club and the eraka grass

- **The iron club is born and ground to dust** - Next day Samba indeed produces an iron club. The terrified Yadava assembly, on King Ugrasena's order, has it filed/ground into powder and the powder cast into the sea at Prabhasa, hoping to avert the curse (Mausala Parva 1).
- **The unground fragment swallowed by a fish** - One last lump of iron will not grind down; it is also thrown into the sea, where a fish swallows it. The fish is caught, and the iron splinter inside it is taken by the hunter **Jara**, who fixes it as an arrowhead (Mausala Parva 1; **Vishnu Purana 5.37** preserves the fish detail prominently).
- **The powder becomes eraka grass** - The iron filings wash ashore and grow into the sharp rush-like reed **eraka** along the Prabhasa coast — a botanical time-bomb that will later turn to iron in the Yadavas' hands (Mausala Parva 1).

## Omens — the avatar's instruments withdraw

- **Dwarka's portents multiply** - Rats infest homes and gnaw sleeping men's hair and nails; pots crack of themselves; predatory birds shriek; the day of the new/full moon falls wrongly; women dream of a black figure with white teeth dragging them off; dharma collapses and shamelessness spreads (Mausala Parva 2).
- **Krishna recognises the hour** - Reading the omens, Krishna understands Gandhari's and the sages' curses are ripening and the 36th year has come; he orders the Yadavas to make pilgrimage to Prabhasa to bathe and propitiate the gods — a fatal gathering (Mausala Parva 2).
- **The divine weapons depart** - Krishna's **Sudarshana Chakra**, the conch **Panchajanya**, his chariot and horses, and Balarama's **plough (hala)** and pestle/club weapon all rise and vanish into the sky/sea before the end — the instruments of the avatar leaving first (Mausala Parva; emphasised in Puranic retellings).

## The brawl at Prabhasa

- **The drunken festival** - At Prabhasa the Yadava heroes and their women feast and drink *maireya* liquor by the sea (drinking having earlier been banned in Dwarka; here the prohibition lapses) (Mausala Parva 3).
- **Satyaki insults Kritavarma** - Inflamed by wine, **Satyaki** taunts **Kritavarma** for the night-massacre of the sleeping Pandava camp ([Sauptika Parva, book 10](./10-sauptika-parva.md)) and his role in Bhurishravas's death; he vows no warrior who slew sleeping men deserves to live (Mausala Parva 3).
- **Pradyumna and the counter-taunt** - Krishna's son **Pradyumna** backs Satyaki; Kritavarma retorts by mocking Satyaki for killing the unarmed, fasting Bhurishravas in battle. The quarrel turns lethal (Mausala Parva 3).
- **Satyaki beheads Kritavarma** - Satyaki springs up and cuts off Kritavarma's head; the Bhojas and Andhakas turn on Satyaki (Mausala Parva 3).
- **Krishna's helplessness** - The mob falls on Satyaki and Pradyumna and kills them before Krishna's eyes. Enraged, Krishna himself seizes a handful of eraka grass — which turns to an iron club in his hand — and begins to kill (Mausala Parva 3).
- **Eraka becomes iron clubs** - Every Yadava who grabs the eraka reeds finds it transformed into a deadly iron *musala*; brother slays brother, father slays son, in blind drunken fury until almost the entire Vrishni-Andhaka race lies dead on the shore — the curse fulfilled exactly (Mausala Parva 3).
- **The handful of survivors** - Only a few escape the slaughter: **Krishna**, **Balarama**, the charioteer **Daruka**, and **Babhru** (Vabhru) survive the field (Mausala Parva 3).
  - *Sub-episode:* Babhru is killed immediately afterward — a stray (or hunter's) bolt/club strikes him dead as he stands, so that even the survivors of the brawl do not all live (Mausala Parva 3-4).

## The deaths of Balarama and Krishna

- **Krishna sends warnings to Dwarka** - Krishna dispatches Daruka to Hastinapura to fetch Arjuna, and goes to comfort his father Vasudeva, asking him to protect the women until Arjuna comes (Mausala Parva 3-4).
- **Balarama's yogic departure** - Krishna finds **Balarama** seated against a tree in yoga; Balarama gives up his body. A great white serpent issues from his mouth and glides into the sea, received by Ocean and the Nagas — Balarama returning to his nature as **Shesha/Ananta**, the cosmic serpent (Mausala Parva 4).
  - *Variant ([scholarly]/doctrinal):* this is the textual ground for the Vaishnava identification of Balarama as a *purna/amsha* incarnation of Shesha-Ananta on whom Vishnu reclines; see [dashavatara](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Krishna lies down in meditation** - Alone, Krishna lies on the ground in deep yoga, knowing his hour, one foot resting raised over the other knee (Mausala Parva 4).
- **The hunter Jara's arrow** - The hunter **Jara**, ranging the forest, mistakes Krishna's reddish raised foot/sole for a deer and looses his arrow — tipped with the very iron splinter from the cursed club (recovered from the fish). The arrow pierces the foot (Mausala Parva 4).
  - *Variant (traditional gloss):* the wound is to the heel/sole of the left foot — the one mortal spot — echoing the "vulnerable foot" motif; the spot is identified with **Bhalka Tirtha** near Somnath, Gujarat [folk tradition / sthala-purana].
  - *Variant ([folk tradition]):* Jara is said in some traditions to be the reborn **Vali (Bali)**, the monkey-king slain by Rama in the [Ramayana](../ramayana/README.md) with an arrow from concealment — closing a karmic circle across the two avatars; treated as devotional folklore, not in the critical Mausala text.
- **Jara's grief and Krishna's blessing** - Jara rushes up, sees the four-armed god, and falls in terror begging forgiveness. Krishna consoles him, blesses him, and (in devotional tellings) grants him heaven, then withdraws into his own supreme abode, his body ascending in radiance (Mausala Parva 4).
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana 11.30-31):* the fullest devotional account — Krishna deliberately wills his departure (*svadhama-gamana*), the gods and Brahma come to witness, and he reabsorbs Dwarka and his earthly form; the violence is framed as the Lord's own play to lighten the earth and end the age.
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 5.37-38):* concise; stresses the curses' inevitability and Krishna's voluntary acceptance.

## Daruka, Arjuna, and the fall of Dwarka

- **Daruka brings the news** - Daruka reaches Hastinapura and tells the Pandavas the Vrishnis have destroyed themselves and Krishna and Balarama are gone. Arjuna sets out at once for Dwarka (Mausala Parva 5-6).
- **Vasudeva's death** - At Dwarka, Krishna's father **Vasudeva**, hearing of his sons' deaths, gives up his life in yoga. Arjuna performs his funeral; **four of Vasudeva's wives (Devaki, Rohini, Bhadra, Madira)** mount his pyre as sati (Mausala Parva 6-7).
- **Arjuna cremates the slain** - Arjuna performs the last rites for Balarama, Krishna, and the fallen Yadava heroes; some of Krishna's and Balarama's wives also enter the fire as sati (Mausala Parva 7).
- **Dwarka swallowed by the sea** - On Krishna's earlier instruction, Arjuna leads out the surviving Yadava women, children and elders. As the last of them quit the city, the **sea rises and submerges Dwarka** behind them, reclaiming every part except the spot where Krishna had dwelt (Mausala Parva 7).
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 5.38):* "The ocean rose and submerged the whole of Dwaraka, save only the dwelling of Krishna" — the relic-house alone spared.
  - *Note [scholarly]:* the submergence and the Prabhasa setting have drawn archaeological interest (offshore investigations near Dwarka/Bet Dwarka, Gujarat); treated as legend with possible memory of coastal inundation, not confirmed history.

## Arjuna's humiliation — the power passes with the age

- **The Abhira robbers** - Escorting the column toward Indraprastha, Arjuna is ambushed at the **Panchanada (Punjab) of the Saraswati region** by **Abhira** cowherd-bandits who set upon the defenceless Yadava women (Mausala Parva 7-8).
- **The Gandiva fails** - Arjuna draws his great bow **Gandiva** but finds his strength gone; he cannot string it as before, his celestial astras (divine missiles) will not come at his call, and his quivers run empty. He beats the robbers with the bow-end but cannot protect the women (Mausala Parva 8).
- **The women lost** - Many of the Vrishni-Andhaka widows are carried off by the bandits; others go willingly. Arjuna reaches Kurukshetra with only a remnant and settles the survivors — Krishna's grandson **Vajra** is installed as king at Indraprastha (Mausala Parva 8).
  - *Significance:* the loss of Arjuna's invincibility signals that the divine age has closed; the very weapons that served him "no longer have need of him."
- **Vyasa's counsel** - The grief-stricken Arjuna goes to the sage **Vyasa**, who explains that all has happened by destiny and the ripening of curses; the Yadavas' time, and Krishna's earthly purpose, were complete. Vyasa tells Arjuna the heroes' age is over and counsels the Pandavas that the time has come to **renounce the kingdom and depart** — directly setting up the [Mahaprasthanika Parva (book 17)](./17-mahaprasthanika-parva.md) (Mausala Parva 8-9).

## Why it matters in the timeline

- **End of Dvapara / start of Kali** - Krishna's departure is the conventional pivot: tradition fixes **Kali Yuga's onset at 3102 BCE**, dated from the day Krishna left the earth (a few weeks/months before by some reckonings). See [yugas-and-mahayuga](../../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md).
- **Dharma's collapse** - The lawlessness, drunkenness and shamelessness portrayed prefigure the moral conditions of Kali Yuga (the dharma-bull now on a single leg). See [04-kali-yuga](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).
- **The avatar's withdrawal** - Marks the close of the [Krishna avatar](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) and the [Chandravamsha's](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) Yadava (Vrishni) branch.
