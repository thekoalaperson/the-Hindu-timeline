# Mausala Parva — The Book of the Clubs

> Cosmic address: Shveta-Varaha Kalpa › 51st year of Brahma › 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara › 28th Mahayuga › **end of Dvapara Yuga**, on the cusp of Kali.
> The 16th of the 18 books (parvas) of the Mahabharata. ↑ Parent: [Mahabharata deep-dives](README.md) · See also [03-dvapara-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and [04-kali-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).

The shortest and bleakest book of the epic. In the thirty-sixth year after the Kurukshetra war, the Yadava clans (Vrishni, Andhaka, Bhoja, Kukura) annihilate one another in a drunken brawl at Prabhasa, with clubs (*musala*) that spring from a sages' curse; Balarama and Krishna leave the earth; the sea floods Dvaraka; Arjuna, escorting the survivors, finds his strength gone. The book's name, *mausala*, means "of the clubs". Later tradition (not this book) dates the **onset of Kali Yuga** from the day Krishna left the earth (see the last section).

- **Size.** The BORI Critical Edition (CE) constitutes **9 adhyayas, 273 verses** (11, 20, 22, 46, 25, 15, 22, 74, 38) (GRETIL e-text of CE Book 16). The epic's own table of contents gives **8 adhyayas and 300 shlokas** (CE 1.2.229; a starred line, *0171, adds "and twenty", i.e. 320). Ganguli's translation (vulgate) has **8 sections**. The Parvasamgraha lists Mausala as a single sub-parva (CE 1.2.68).
- **Concordance.** Ganguli §1 = CE 1–2 · §2 = CE 3 · §3 = CE 4 · §4 = CE 5 · §5 = CE 6 · §6 = CE 7 · §7 = CE 8 · §8 = CE 9. Below, all citations are **CE adhyaya.verse**. Passages that the CE prints only as starred lines (`*nnnn`) or appendix passages are marked **[CE star passage]**: they appear in some manuscripts (many of them also in the vulgate that Ganguli translates), but the CE does not accept them into its text.
- **Frame.** The book opens on Yudhishthira's side. In the 36th year he sees portents, then hears of the slaughter of the Vrishnis in the affair of the clubs (*mausale*). He hears that Vasudeva and Rama are *vimukta*, "released" (Ganguli: "had escaped with life"), and the Pandavas cannot believe Krishna's death, likening it to the ocean drying up (CE 16.1.1–11). Janamejaya then asks how it happened, and Vaishampayana narrates (CE 16.2.1–3).

## The seeds of doom — two curses

- **Gandhari's curse on Krishna (told in Book 11, recalled here)** — In the [Stri Parva, book 11](11-stri-parva.md), the bereaved Gandhari blames Krishna for letting Kurus and Pandavas destroy each other although he was able to prevent it. She curses him by the merit of her service to her husband: because he ignored the slaughter of kinsmen, he will kill his own kinsmen; when the 36th year comes, he will die by an ignoble means, having lost his kin, ministers and sons and wandering in the forest; and the Yadava women will lament as the Bharata women do now (CE 11.25.36–42). Mausala itself only recalls the curse: at CE 16.3.19 Krishna sees it coming true, and at CE 16.5.17 he remembers it before he dies.
  - *Krishna's reply (CE 11.25.43–45):* half-smiling, he says he already knew this. No one but he can destroy the Vrishnis; men, gods and Danavas cannot kill them, so they will perish at one another's hands.
- **The sages' curse on Samba** — The Vrishni youths, led by **Sarana**, see the sages **Vishvamitra, Kanva and Narada** arrive at **Dvaraka**. "Afflicted by the rod of the gods", the youths dress Krishna's son **Samba** as a woman and ask: "This is the wife of Babhru, who wants a son. Sages, do you know what she will bear?" (CE 16.2.4–6). The angry sages answer that Samba will bear a terrible iron club (*musalam ghoram ayasam*) to destroy the Vrishnis and Andhakas. With it the youths will exterminate their whole race **except Rama and Janardana**. Balarama will cast off his body and go into the sea, and a hunter, **Jara**, will pierce Krishna as he lies on the ground (CE 16.2.8–11).
- **Krishna lets the curse stand** — The sages go to Krishna. He tells the Vrishnis that it will happen as foretold, and enters his house: "the lord of the world did not wish to make fate otherwise" (CE 16.2.12–14).
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 5.37.6–10):* the same three sages, Vishvamitra, Kanva and Narada, but at the great tirtha **Pindaraka**, not in Dvaraka. Samba is called Jambavati's son, and the youths ask what this woman, "desirous of a son", will bear.
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana 11.1.11–16):* the sages, sent by Krishna himself acting as Time, gather at **Pindaraka**. The list is longer: Vishvamitra, Asita, Kanva, Durvasa, Bhrigu, Angiras, Kashyapa, Vamadeva, Atri, Vasishtha, Narada and others. Samba is Jambavati's son, presented as a dark-eyed pregnant woman too shy to ask for herself. In this version the club is found at once, when the youths unwrap the disguise (11.1.17), not born the next day.

## The pulverised club and the eraka grass

- **The club is born and ground to powder** — Next day Samba gives birth to the club, "like a giant servant of death". The news is brought to the king, who in dismay has it ground to fine powder, and men cast the powder into the sea on his order (CE 16.2.15–17). The CE text does not say where the powder was thrown; it does not name Prabhasa.
- **Prohibition of liquor** — By order of **Ahuka (Ugrasena)** (a starred line, *0008, adds Janardana, Rama and Babhru), it is proclaimed in Dvaraka that from that day no Vrishni or Andhaka household may make *sura* or *asava*. Anyone who secretly makes a drink is to be impaled alive with his kin, and the citizens bind themselves to the rule (CE 16.2.17–20).
- **The powder becomes eraka grass** — The CE text never explains where the eraka comes from. The grass is simply there at Prabhasa when Krishna seizes it (CE 16.4.34). The clubs are later called "born of eraka" (*erakodbhava*, CE 16.8.30), and the transformation is put down to "the brahmins' rod" (*brahmadanda*, CE 16.4.38). A starred line after CE 16.2.17 (*0007) links the powder thrown into the ocean with the erakas **[CE star passage]**.
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 5.37.12; Bhagavata Purana 11.1.22):* both state plainly that the iron powder became eraka. ViP says only that the powder thrown into the ocean turned into eraka; BhP adds that the waves carried the powder to the shore, where it took root as eraka.
- **The unground fragment, the fish and Jara's arrow (Purana tradition only)** — This episode is **absent from the Mahabharata** (both the CE and Ganguli §1). In the Puranas, one lump of iron cannot be ground and is thrown into the sea. A fish swallows it; fishermen net the fish; and the hunter **Jara** takes the iron from its belly and makes it his arrowhead (Vishnu Purana 5.37.13–14, where the lump is "lance-shaped"; Bhagavata Purana 11.1.21–23). Both Puranas add that Krishna knew all this but did not wish to undo the curse (ViP 5.37.15; BhP 11.1.24).

## Omens — the avatar's instruments withdraw

- **Time walks the houses** — Kala (Time/Death) roams the Vrishni houses every day as a terrible, bald, black-and-tawny man. He looks in and then vanishes (CE 16.3.1–2). That archers shoot arrows at him by the hundred thousand, in vain, is a starred line (*0011) **[CE star passage]**.
- **Portents in Dvaraka** — Rats multiply in the streets; pots crack; *sarika* birds chatter day and night; cranes hoot like owls and goats howl like jackals; pale, red-footed birds and pigeons haunt the houses. Asses are born of cows, cats of bitches, mice of mongooses (CE 16.3.4–7). The Vrishnis sin without shame and despise brahmins, ancestors, gods and elders; only Rama and Janardana do not. Wives and husbands deceive each other; fire bends to the left with blue-red flames; the sun appears ringed by headless trunks; worms appear in cooked food; unseen feet run past; the planets strike the asterisms; and when Panchajanya is blown, asses bray from every side (CE 16.3.8–15). That rats gnaw the hair and nails of sleepers at night is a starred line (*0012) **[CE star passage]**.
- **The calendar goes wrong** — Krishna sees the new moon fall on the thirteenth lunar day, and says Rahu has again made the fourteenth into the fifteenth, as at the time of the Bharata war. He judges that the 36th year has come and that Gandhari's words, and what Yudhishthira said when he saw the omens before the battle, are coming true (CE 16.3.16–20). Wishing to make Gandhari's words true, he has a pilgrimage to the sea proclaimed (CE 16.3.21–22).
- **Dreams** — At night a black woman with white teeth enters the houses laughing and runs through Dvaraka, robbing the women in their dreams. Terrible Rakshasas are seen carrying off ornaments, parasols, banners and armour (CE 16.4.1–2). Vultures devouring the Vrishnis in dreams is a starred passage (*0015).
- **The divine instruments depart** — Krishna's **discus**, the gift of Agni, rises into the sky as the Vrishnis watch. His sun-bright chariot is carried off over the sea by its four horses while Daruka looks on. The two great banners, Balarama's **palm-tree (*tala*)** and Krishna's **Garuda (*suparna*)**, are carried up by Apsarases, who cry day and night, "Go on pilgrimage!" (CE 16.4.3–5). In the Mahabharata, **Panchajanya does not depart** (it is still blown at CE 16.3.15), and Balarama's plough and pestle are not mentioned.
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 5.37.51–52):* only **after** the slaughter, the chariot runs off across the sea while Daruka watches. Krishna's discus, mace, bow Sharnga, quivers, conch and sword circle him and leave by the path of the sun.
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana 11.30.44–45):* after Krishna is wounded, his Garuda-bannered chariot and horses fly into the sky, and his divine weapons follow, while Daruka watches.
- **Uddhava leaves** — When the Yadavas have camped by the sea, **Uddhava**, learned in yoga, takes leave. Knowing the Vrishnis' end is near, Krishna salutes him with joined hands and does not stop him, and the doomed Yadavas watch him go, his radiance filling heaven and earth (CE 16.4.10–12).
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 5.37.31–37):* Krishna sends Uddhava to the Nara-Narayana ashrama at **Badari** on Gandhamadana to attain perfection there.
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana 11.6–29):* before the end, Krishna teaches Uddhava at length (the *Uddhava Gita*); Uddhava leaves for Badari (11.29), and the narrative resumes "after Uddhava had gone to the forest" (11.30.1).

## The brawl at Prabhasa

- **The drinking feast** — The Yadavas prepare food and many kinds of drink, liquor and meat. They leave the city "attached to liquor" (*sidhu*) and camp at **Prabhasa** with their wives (CE 16.4.7–9). They give monkeys the food cooked for brahmins, mixed with liquor, and a great drinking-bout begins among trumpets, actors and dancers (CE 16.4.13–14). **In Krishna's presence, Rama drinks** with Kritavarma, Yuyudhana (Satyaki), Gada and Babhru (CE 16.4.15).
- **Satyaki's taunt** — Drunk, Satyaki mocks **Kritavarma**: "What kshatriya would kill men asleep, as good as dead? The Yadavas do not forgive what you did." **Pradyumna** applauds (CE 16.4.16–18). The taunt is about the night massacre of the [Sauptika Parva, book 10](10-sauptika-parva.md) only; Bhurishravas is raised by Kritavarma, not Satyaki.
- **Kritavarma's counter-taunt** — Furious, Kritavarma points at Satyaki contemptuously with his left hand. "How could you, a hero, so cruelly kill Bhurishravas, his arm cut off, sitting in *praya* on the battlefield?" (CE 16.4.19–20). Krishna glances at Kritavarma sideways in anger (CE 16.4.21).
- **The Syamantaka grievance** — Satyaki reminds Krishna of the affair of the **Syamantaka** jewel, which had belonged to Satrajit. **Satyabhama**, Satrajit's daughter, goes weeping to Krishna's lap and inflames his anger (CE 16.4.22–23). (The text only alludes to the story; the Puranas tell it in full.)
- **Satyaki beheads Kritavarma** — Satyaki rises and swears by truth that Kritavarma will follow the five sons of Draupadi, Dhrishtadyumna and Shikhandin, whom Kritavarma killed in their sleep with Drona's son. Then he rushes at Kritavarma and **cuts off his head with a sword** close to Krishna (CE 16.4.24–27).
- **Satyaki and Pradyumna killed** — Satyaki goes on striking others, and Krishna runs to stop him (CE 16.4.28). The Bhojas and Andhakas close around Satyaki, and Krishna, knowing the turn of time, does not grow angry (CE 16.4.29–30). Drunk, they beat Satyaki with the leftover food vessels. **Pradyumna** (Rukmini's son) runs to rescue him, and both are killed before Krishna's eyes by sheer numbers (CE 16.4.31–33).
- **Eraka becomes iron** — Seeing his son and Satyaki dead, Krishna angrily seizes a handful of **eraka** grass. It becomes an iron club like a thunderbolt, and he kills everyone in front of him (CE 16.4.34–35). Andhakas, Bhojas, Shaineyas and Vrishnis then strike one another. Whoever grabs eraka holds a thunderbolt; even grass becomes a club, "all made by the brahmins' rod" (CE 16.4.36–39). Son kills father and father kills son; they fall like moths into fire, and no one thinks of flight (CE 16.4.40–41).
- **Krishna finishes the slaughter** — Seeing **Samba, Charudeshna, Pradyumna and Aniruddha** dead, and **Gada** lying on the ground, Krishna in fury leaves no one alive (CE 16.4.42–44). **Babhru** and **Daruka** say to him: "Lord, you have destroyed almost everyone. Let us go where Rama has gone" (CE 16.4.45–46).
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 5.37.40–50):* the quarrel begins with drunken boasting over whose food was tastier (5.37.42). They fight with weapons until the weapons are spent, and only then pull up eraka (5.37.43–45). When Krishna tries to restrain them, the Yadavas take him for their enemies' ally and go on killing one another (5.37.48). Afterwards **only Krishna and Daruka** survive (5.37.53); Babhru is not mentioned.
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana 11.30.12–24):* they drink *maireya*. A full battle with bows, swords, maces and chariots comes before the eraka. Named pairs fight: Pradyumna and Samba, Akrura and Bhoja, Aniruddha and Satyaki, Subhadra and Sangramajit, the two Gadas, Sumitra and Suratha (11.30.16). Krishna's maya bewilders them, and the deluded Yadavas attack Krishna and Balarama as well, who then kill with eraka clubs (11.30.22–23).

## The deaths of Balarama and Krishna

- **Daruka sent to Arjuna** — Krishna, Daruka and Babhru follow Rama's tracks and find him seated against a tree, deep in thought, in a solitary place (CE 16.5.1). Krishna sends **Daruka** to the Kurus to tell Arjuna of the slaughter "by the brahmins' curse" and to bring him quickly (CE 16.5.2–3).
- **Babhru's death** — Krishna sends **Babhru**, still drunk, to guard the women from robbers. As Babhru hurries off, a club "cursed by the brahmins", released from a **hunter's trap** (*kutonmuktam musalam lubdhakasya*), kills him (CE 16.5.4–5). (Ganguli renders the phrase as an iron bolt "attaching itself to a mallet in the hands of a hunter".)
- **Krishna's farewell to Vasudeva** — Krishna asks Rama to wait. In Dvaraka he asks his father **Vasudeva** to protect the women until Arjuna comes. He has seen the destruction of the Yadus, as before of the Kurus, says he cannot look on the city without them, and will go to the forest to practise austerities with Rama. Hearing the women wail, he turns back to tell them that Arjuna will come and free them from grief (CE 16.5.6–11).
- **Balarama's departure** — Krishna finds Rama alone in the forest, sunk in yoga. From his mouth comes a great **white serpent**, thousand-headed, huge as a mountain, red-mouthed, which leaves the human body and goes to the sea. Ocean, divine Nagas and holy rivers receive it. The text names the welcoming Nagas: Karkotaka, Vasuki, Takshaka, Prithushravas, Varuna, Kunjara, Mishri, Shankha, Kumuda, Pundarika, Dhritarashtra, Hrada, Kratha, Shitikantha, Chakramanda, Atishanda, Durmukha and Ambarisha, with King Varuna himself. They greet it with *arghya* and water for the feet (CE 16.5.11–15).
  - *Doctrinal ground:* this image fits the epic's own statement in the Adi Parva that "Baladeva of great strength was a portion of the Naga Shesha" (*sheshasyamshas tu nagasya*, CE 1.61.91). Vaishnava doctrine builds on this to identify Balarama with Shesha-Ananta; see [dashavatara](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 5.37.54–56; Bhagavata Purana 11.30.26):* ViP has the same serpent leaving from the mouth, honoured by Siddhas and serpents, with Ocean bringing *arghya*. BhP says only that Rama, by yoga at the seashore, joined his self to the Self and left the human world.
- **Krishna recalls the curses** — Wandering the empty forest, Krishna sits on the bare ground. He remembers Gandhari's words and **what Durvasas said when Krishna was smeared with the leftover *payasa*** (CE 16.5.16–17). Thinking of the end of the Andhakas, Vrishnis and Kurus, he decides it is time to go, restrains his senses, speech and mind, and **lies down in great yoga** (CE 16.5.18–19). A starred passage (*0026) adds that, though a god, he wanted a cause for his death to keep the world's order and to make Durvasas's words true **[CE star passage]**.
  - *The Durvasas episode (Anushasana Parva, CE 13.144):* Krishna tells Yudhishthira, repeating what he once told Pradyumna (CE 13.144.2–6), how he hosted the irascible Durvasas. The sage demanded hot *payasa* and ordered Krishna to smear it on his limbs. Pleased, Durvasas promised that Krishna had no fear of death wherever he was smeared, but asked why he had not smeared the **soles of his feet** (CE 13.144.38–39). This is the epic's own reason for the fatal wound in the foot. See [Anushasana Parva](13-anushasana-parva.md).
- **Jara's arrow** — The fierce hunter **Jara** comes looking for deer. Taking the reclining yogi for a deer, he shoots and pierces him **in the sole of the foot** (*padatale*). Coming up to seize his prey, he sees a man in yoga, **in yellow robes, with many arms** (*anekabahum*) (CE 16.5.19–20). In the CE the arrow is simply a *sayaka* (arrow); the iron-splinter tip is from the Puranas.
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 5.37.66–71):* honouring Durvasas's word, Krishna lies in yoga (*shete sma*, 5.37.66) **with his foot placed on his knee** (5.37.67). Jara's arrow is tipped with the **iron left over from the club**; he takes the foot for a deer, pierces the sole, then sees a **four-armed** man and begs forgiveness.
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana 11.30.27–37):* Krishna sits under an **ashvattha (pippala)** tree in his radiant four-armed form, **one lotus foot resting on his right thigh** (11.30.32). Jara, his arrow made from the club's leftover iron, mistakes the foot for a deer's face (11.30.33), then falls at his feet and asks to be killed for his sin (11.30.34–37).
  - *Folk/sthala tradition:* the spot is identified with **Bhalka Tirtha** near Somnath (Prabhas Patan/Veraval, Gujarat). The Mahabharata names only Prabhasa and a forest. See [Prabhasa–Somnath](../../06-sacred-geography/prabhasa-somnath-tirtha.md) and [Dvaraka](../../06-sacred-geography/dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md). [folk]
  - *Variant ([folk tradition]):* Jara is said to be the reborn **Vali**, the monkey-king Rama shot from concealment in the [Ramayana](../ramayana/README.md), closing a karmic circle across two avatars. This is **not found** in the CE (16.5.19–21) or in Ganguli's vulgate (§4); it is devotional folklore.
- **Krishna comforts Jara and ascends** — Thinking himself an offender, Jara grasps Krishna's feet with his head. Krishna **comforts him** and rises upward, filling heaven and earth with splendour (CE 16.5.21). In heaven he is met by Indra (Vasava), the Ashvins, Rudras, Adityas, Vasus, Vishvedevas, sages, Siddhas, Gandharvas and Apsarases. As Narayana, the master of yoga, he reaches **his own immeasurable abode**, honoured by gods, rishis, Charanas, Siddhas and Sadhyas, while Indra rejoices (CE 16.5.22–25). The constituted CE text does not say that Jara goes to heaven. A four-line starred passage (*0028) does seem to: remorseful for his deed and his birth, having seen the god, he casts off his body and is brought to heaven by the gods. That passage and a long hymn of the gods (Appendix passage @001, after 16.5.25) are **[CE star passage]**.
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 5.37.72–75; Bhagavata Purana 11.30.39–40):* Krishna tells Jara not to fear, "this was my own wish" (BhP), and sends him to heaven. A *vimana* comes, and Jara ascends.
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana 11.31.1–10):* Brahma, Shiva, Indra and the gods gather to watch the departure. Krishna joins his self to the Self and enters his own abode unseen; even the gods cannot follow his path. Verse 11.31.6 can be read two ways: *yoga-dharanayagneyya-dagdhva* splits either as "having burnt" his body by the fiery yogic concentration (*dagdhva*) or, as traditional commentators such as Shridhara take it, "without burning" it (*adagdhva*). The page does not decide between them. Before this, Daruka finds him and is sent to Dvaraka, not Hastinapura, with word that the sea will flood the city and the Yadavas must go to Indraprastha under Arjuna's protection (11.30.41–50).

## Daruka, Arjuna, and the fall of Dwarka

- **Daruka brings the news** — Daruka tells the Pandavas that the Vrishnis have destroyed one another, and the Pandavas are stricken. Arjuna takes leave of his brothers and sets out to see his maternal uncle Vasudeva, saying "this is no more" (*nedam asti*; Ganguli: "destruction would soon overtake everything") (CE 16.6.1–3).
- **The widowed city** — With Daruka, Arjuna sees Dvaraka "like a woman whose husband has died". The **16,000 women** of Krishna's household cry out when they see him, and he cannot look at them through his tears (CE 16.6.4–7). In an extended metaphor the city is a river: Vrishnis its water, horses its fish, Rama and Krishna its crocodiles. Now it looks to Arjuna like the Vaitarani, the river of the dead, caught in Time's noose (CE 16.6.8–11). Arjuna falls weeping; **Satyabhama** and **Rukmini** fall beside him, seat him on a golden seat, and he consoles the women (CE 16.6.12–15).
- **Vasudeva's lament and Krishna's message** — Vasudeva (Anakadundubhi) embraces Arjuna and weeps. He blames the misconduct of **Pradyumna and Yuyudhana**, the two atirathas Arjuna used to boast of, as "the mouth of the Vrishnis' ruin". Yet he blames no one but the curse, and wonders that Krishna stood by (CE 16.7.1–11). (That Krishna had revived Arjuna's grandson Parikshit yet would not save his own kin is a starred passage, *0036.) He passes on Krishna's last instructions (CE 16.7.12–18):
  - "Know that I am Arjuna and Arjuna is I"; do whatever Arjuna says;
  - Arjuna will look after the women and children and perform Vasudeva's funeral rites;
  - once Arjuna leaves, the sea will flood the city, walls and towers included.
  Vasudeva then says he will neither eat nor live (CE 16.7.19–22).
- **Arjuna's orders** — Arjuna says he cannot bear the earth without Krishna. He, the king, Bhima, Sahadeva, Nakula and Draupadi "are six of one mind", and their own time to depart is at hand. First he will take the Vrishni women, children and old people to Indraprastha (CE 16.8.1–5). In the **Sudharma** assembly hall he announces: the sea will flood the city; prepare the carriages and wealth; **Vajra will be your king at Shakraprastha**; and on the seventh day, at sunrise, they will set out (CE 16.8.6–13).
- **Vasudeva's death and the four wives** — Arjuna spends the night in Krishna's house. Next morning Vasudeva "yoked himself" and reached the highest state (CE 16.8.14–15). His wives **Devaki, Bhadra, Rohini and Madira** resolve to follow him. Arjuna carries the body out in a great man-borne carriage, preceded by Vasudeva's ashvamedha parasol and sacred fires. At the place Vasudeva had loved in life, the funeral is held, and the **four wives mount the pyre** (CE 16.8.16–25). Vajra and the Vrishni boys offer water (CE 16.8.27).
- **Rites for the slain** — Arjuna goes to the place of slaughter and performs the rites for those killed "by clubs born of eraka", in order of seniority. He has the bodies of **Rama and Vasudeva (Krishna)** found and cremated by trusted men, then performs the after-death rites (CE 16.8.28–32). The CE text says nothing here of Krishna's queens. That eight chief queens led by Rukmini, and Balarama's wife **Revati**, enter the fire with the two bodies is a starred passage (*0040) **[CE star passage]**; it is the main text of Vishnu Purana 5.38.2–3.
- **The sea takes Dvaraka** — On the seventh day Arjuna leads out the column in chariots drawn by horses and by oxen, asses (*khara*) and camels: the Vrishni widows, servants, horsemen, citizens, the 16,000 women of Krishna's household, and **Vajra**, whom the text calls Krishna's **pautra** (CE 16.8.32–39). As soon as the people have left, the ocean, "home of sea-monsters", floods Dvaraka, still full of jewels, and the citizens hurry on crying "Wonderful is fate!" (CE 16.8.40–41).
  - *[CE star passages, after 16.8.40]:* the sea covers each piece of ground as soon as it is left behind (*0041). In *0042, the Ocean speaks: the city with its houses is now his to guard for Hari until a future descent in the Krita age, and he is its treasurer.
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 5.37.36, 5.38.9–11):* the sea floods the empty city **but cannot pass over Vasudeva's house alone**, where Keshava is ever present. Merely seeing it frees one from sin. In ViP 5.38.4, **Ugrasena**, Vasudeva, Devaki and Rohini all enter the fire.
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana 11.31.18–25):* Devaki, Rohini and Vasudeva die of grief **at Prabhasa**; the wives of Rama, of Vasudeva, of Krishna's sons and of Krishna (Rukmini and the others) enter the pyres. The sea floods Dvaraka **except the Lord's dwelling** (11.31.23), where Madhusudana is ever present.
  - *Note [scholarly]:* for the offshore finds at Dvaraka and Bet Dvaraka (S. R. Rao and the NIO, 1983–1990) and the dispute over dating them, see [Dvaraka](../../06-sacred-geography/dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md). The scriptural flood and the archaeological record are kept on separate clocks.

## Arjuna's humiliation — the power passes with the age

- **The Abhira attack in Panchanada** — Arjuna travels by stages, resting the women in forests, on hills and by rivers. In **Panchanada** ("five rivers", the Punjab) he camps in a land rich in cattle and grain (CE 16.8.42–43). Seeing the lordless women escorted by one bowman, the **Abhiras**, "sinful" and "of ill omen", plot together and attack in thousands, armed with **staves** (*yashti*) (CE 16.8.44–48). Arjuna turns back and, smiling, warns them off; they ignore him (CE 16.8.49–51).
- **The Gandiva fails** — Arjuna begins to string the ageless divine bow **Gandiva**, and **manages it only with great difficulty** in the confusion. He calls his divine weapons to mind, but he cannot remember them (CE 16.8.52–53), and he is ashamed of the change in his arms and the loss of his astras (CE 16.8.54). The Vrishni warriors cannot bring back the people being carried off. The column is too large to guard, and the women are dragged off on every side, **while others go of their own will** (*kamat*) (CE 16.8.55–57). His once-inexhaustible arrows are used up in a moment; he strikes at the robbers with the **tip of his bow**; and the "Mlecchas" go off with the best of the Vrishni and Andhaka women (CE 16.8.58–61). Arjuna sees it as fate and turns back, saying "this is no more" (*nedam asti*; Ganguli: "he had not the power which he had before") (CE 16.8.62–64).
  - *[CE star passages]:* the strung bow goes slack again, and his arrows do not even break the skin (*0046). The slack bow is also in the main text of Vishnu Purana 5.38.22. ViP 5.38.23 seems instead to say the arrows did no more than break the skin (the GRETIL line is corrupt).
- **Settling the survivors** — With the rest of the women and wealth, Arjuna reaches **Kurukshetra** and settles the survivors in different places (CE 16.8.65–66):
  - **Kritavarma's son** at the city of **Martikavata**, with the surviving women of the Bhoja king (CE 16.8.67);
  - the old, the children and the remaining women at **Shakraprastha** (CE 16.8.68);
  - **Satyaki's son** by the **Sarasvati** (CE 16.8.69);
  - **Vajra** given the kingdom of **Indraprastha** (CE 16.8.70).
- **The queens** — **Akrura's wives** leave to become ascetics, though Vajra begs them to stay (CE 16.8.70). **Rukmini, Gandhari, Shaibya, Haimavati and Jambavati enter the fire** (CE 16.8.71). **Satyabhama** and other wives of Krishna go to the forest to practise austerities (CE 16.8.72). A starred line (*0050) adds that they lived on fruits and roots and settled at **Kalapa-grama** beyond the Himalaya **[CE star passage]**. Arjuna distributes the men from Dvaraka to Vajra (CE 16.8.73). In the CE, then, the queens enter the fire here, after the march, not at the Dvaraka cremation, and Satyabhama does not enter it.
  - *Vajra's descent:* the CE calls Vajra Krishna's *pautra* (CE 16.8.37), a word that usually means grandson but can mean descendant. In the Puranic genealogy he is Aniruddha's son by Subhadra (Rukmin's granddaughter), i.e. Krishna's great-grandson (Vishnu Purana 4.15.38–41, 5.32.6). Aniruddha is named among the dead at CE 16.4.43.
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana 1.15.39):* Yudhishthira installs Vajra as lord of the Shurasenas at **Mathura**. BhP 11.31.25, like the Mahabharata, has Arjuna consecrate him at Indraprastha.
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 5.38.34):* Arjuna makes Vajra king at Indraprastha.
- **Vyasa's counsel** — In his hermitage Vyasa sees Arjuna's dejection and asks whether he has approached a woman in her courses (*avirajo 'bhighatah*, as Ganguli renders it), killed a brahmin, or been defeated in battle (CE 16.9.4–6). The further question, whether he has been sprinkled with water from nails, hair or a jar, is a starred line (*0051) **[CE star passage]**. Arjuna tells him that Krishna and Rama have left their bodies. **Five hundred thousand** Yadava warriors killed one another with eraka grass at Prabhasa, and Abhiras from Panchanada carried off thousands of Vrishni women while his strength, astras and arrows failed (CE 16.9.7–24). Vyasa answers (CE 16.9.25–36):
  - the Vrishnis were consumed by the brahmins' curse, as was destined; Krishna allowed it, though he could have changed the three worlds, let alone a curse;
  - the four-armed one who drove before Arjuna's chariot has lightened the earth's burden and gone to his own supreme place;
  - Arjuna and his brothers have done the gods' great work and are fulfilled, and now **it is time for them to depart**;
  - strength, understanding and energy arise in times of good fortune and vanish in adversity, for Time is the root of all;
  - Arjuna's astras, their purpose done, have gone back where they came from, and will return to his hand when Time brings them.
- **Return to Hastinapura** — With Vyasa's leave, Arjuna goes to the City of the Elephant (Hastinapura) and tells Yudhishthira all that happened to the Vrishnis and Andhakas (CE 16.9.37–38). This leads directly into the [Mahaprasthanika Parva (book 17)](17-mahaprasthanika-parva.md); the epic's table of contents sums it up: "urged by Vyasa's words", Arjuna goes to the Dharma-king and chooses *samnyasa* (CE 1.2.228).
  - *Variant (Vishnu Purana 5.38.70–84):* Vyasa explains the abduction. The sage **Ashtavakra**, standing neck-deep in water, was praised by Apsarases. He granted the wish of some of them to have the Purushottama as husband, but when he came out of the water, crooked in eight places, some of them laughed. He cursed them to fall into the hands of robbers after winning that husband. The Vrishni women carried off by the Abhiras were those Apsarases. The same explanation survives in a starred line in the Mahabharata (*0059, after CE 16.9.27) **[CE star passage]**. In ViP 5.38.89–92, Vyasa tells the Pandavas to go to the forest, and they crown **Parikshit** and leave.
  - *Significance:* Vyasa's own words, "Time is the seed of the world", explain Arjuna's lost power. It is not defeat but the closing of the age in which the astras were needed (CE 16.9.32–35).

## Why it matters in the timeline

- **End of Dvapara / start of Kali** — The Mausala Parva **does not itself date Kali**. The doctrine that **Kali began on the very day Krishna left the earth** is Puranic: "on the day Krishna went to heaven, on that very day Kali-yuga set in, say the knowers of the past" (Bhagavata Purana 12.2.33; also 1.15.36 and 1.18.6), and Kali "descended on the day Hari left the earth" (Vishnu Purana 5.38.8). The calendrical epoch of **3102 BCE** (midnight 17/18 February) belongs to the astronomical tradition of Aryabhata and the Surya Siddhanta [scholarly]; see [yugas-and-mahayuga](../../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md). Within the epic, the only time-mark is the **36th year** after the war, foretold by Gandhari (CE 11.25.41) and confirmed at CE 16.1.1, 16.2.2 and 16.3.18.
  - *Variant (Bhagavata Purana 12.2.29–31):* Kali entered the world when Krishna went to heaven, but could not prevail while his feet still touched the earth. It is reckoned to start when the Seven Sages (Saptarshi) were in the asterism Magha.
- **Dharma's collapse** — The shamelessness, contempt for brahmins and elders, and broken marriages listed among the omens (CE 16.3.8–9) prefigure the conditions of Kali Yuga. The image of the dharma-bull standing on one leg is from Bhagavata Purana 1.16–17, not from this book. See [04-kali-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).
- **The avatar's withdrawal** — Marks the close of the [Krishna avatar](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md). The [Chandravamsha's](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) Yadava (Vrishni) branch continues only through Vajra (CE 16.8.70) and the sons of Kritavarma and Satyaki (CE 16.8.67, 16.8.69).
