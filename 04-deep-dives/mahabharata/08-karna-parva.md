# Karṇa Parva — The Book of Karna

> Cosmic address: [Brahma's current life](../../README.md) › [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md) › [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md) › **[Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** — days 16–17 of the Kurukṣetra War at the very junction of Dvāpara and [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).

The **Karṇa Parva** ("Book of Karṇa") is the **eighth of the 18 parvas** of the [Mahābhārata](README.md) and the **third of the six war-books** (Bhīṣma → Droṇa → **Karṇa** → Śalya → Sauptika → Strī). It narrates the **two days (16th and 17th)** of the Kurukṣetra War during which **Karṇa**, the eldest secret son of [Kuntī](01-adi-parva.md) and Sūrya, serves as the **third Kaurava commander-in-chief** after the fall of Droṇa. It is the shortest of the great war-books and the most relentlessly tragic: it converges Karṇa's lifelong burden of **curses** (Paraśurāma's, the Brāhmaṇa's cow-curse, the Earth-goddess's) onto a single afternoon, ending with Karṇa's chariot-wheel sunk in the earth and his beheading by Arjuna — the war's true turning point, after which Kaurava defeat is certain. In the Bhandarkar **Critical Edition** it runs to **69 adhyāyas (~3,871 ślokas)**; the older **Vulgate** numbers **96 chapters**.

- *Variant [scholarly]:* the Critical Edition (BORI) reduces the Vulgate's ~96 chapters to ~69 adhyāyas; verse counts and chapter divisions differ widely between recensions and translations.

## The 2 upaparvas (sub-books)

| # | Upaparva | Chapters (approx., Vulgate) | Core content |
|---|----------|-----------------------------|--------------|
| 1 | **Karṇa-abhiṣeka Parva** ("consecration of Karṇa") | 1–56 | Karṇa made commander; Śalya forced to be charioteer; the 16th day; the crow-and-swan quarrel; Karṇa spares the lesser Pāṇḍavas |
| 2 | **Karṇa-vadha Parva** ("the slaying of Karṇa") | 57–96 | The 17th day; Duḥśāsana's death; Yudhiṣṭhira wounded and the Arjuna quarrel; the final Karṇa–Arjuna duel; the wheel, the curses, the beheading |

- *Variant [scholarly]:* sub-parva names and chapter boundaries vary by edition; some lists fold the whole parva into a single book or split the consecration material differently. Wikipedia/Critical-Edition usage gives the two-fold division (Karṇa Parva chs. 1–56, Karṇa-vadha chs. 57–96).

## 1. The consecration of Karṇa (day 16 opens)

- **Droṇa's fall reported to Dhṛtarāṣṭra** - The parva opens with **Sañjaya** (his [divine sight](06-bhishma-parva.md) still granted) bringing the blind king word that **Droṇa is dead**, beheaded by Dhṛṣṭadyumna at the end of the [Droṇa Parva](07-drona-parva.md); Dhṛtarāṣṭra swoons, and Sañjaya begins the account of the next two days (Karṇa Parva 1–5).
- **Karṇa consecrated commander (senāpati)** - With Bhīṣma and Droṇa both fallen, **Duryodhana installs Karṇa** as the **third commander-in-chief** of the Kaurava host; Karṇa is ritually consecrated (*abhiṣeka*) and vows to slay Arjuna or die (Karṇa Parva 6–9).
  - *Note:* this is the moment Karṇa has waited the whole war for — he had withdrawn from the field for the ten days under Bhīṣma (who slighted him as a *sūta-putra* / "charioteer's son"); see the [Bhīṣma–Karṇa reconciliation](06-bhishma-parva.md) on the arrow-bed.
- **Karṇa demands a charioteer equal to Kṛṣṇa** - Karṇa argues that to defeat Arjuna — who has Kṛṣṇa as charioteer — he needs a driver of equal skill, and names **Śalya**, king of Madra and master of horses, as the only man fit to rein his steeds (Karṇa Parva 26–32).

---

## 2. Śalya as charioteer — the great quarrel

- **Śalya's bitter consent** - **Śalya**, the **maternal uncle of Nakula and Sahadeva** (a Pāṇḍava kinsman tricked onto the Kaurava side in the [Udyoga Parva](05-udyoga-parva.md)), is mortally insulted at being asked to drive for a "*sūta*"; he agrees only after Duryodhana flatters him by comparing him to Kṛṣṇa, and on the secret condition (some say) that he may speak as he pleases (Karṇa Parva 26–32).
  - *Variant:* the framing of the deal differs — in one telling Śalya extracts from Duryodhana the right to "say whatever I wish," knowing he will use it to demoralize Karṇa; in others he is simply browbeaten into the role and turns hostile on his own.
- **Śalya undermines Karṇa from the driver's seat** - True to a (secret) prior promise to **Yudhiṣṭhira** to sap Karṇa's spirit, Śalya repeatedly **praises Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa**, belittles Karṇa, predicts his death, and refuses to encourage him — a psychological sabotage running through both days (Karṇa Parva 27–42).
- **The crow-and-swan parable (haṃsa-kāka)** - When Karṇa boasts he will kill Arjuna, **Śalya mocks him with the fable of the arrogant crow** who challenged a swan (haṃsa) to a flying contest over the sea, exhausted itself, and would have drowned but for the swan's pity — casting Karṇa as the strutting crow and Arjuna as the effortless swan (Karṇa Parva 41–43).
  - *Variant:* the parable is sometimes paired with a second insulting tale; the exchange of barbs (Śalya also slandering the Madra people, Karṇa retorting) is elaborated at length in the Vulgate and trimmed in the Critical Edition.

---

## 3. The 16th day — Karṇa's ascendancy (Karṇa-abhiṣeka Parva)

- **Aśvatthāman's renewed fury & the Pāṇḍya king** - **Aśvatthāman** (Droṇa's son, now bent on vengeance) rampages and **slays the Pāṇḍya king Malayadhvaja**, a mighty Pāṇḍava ally, after a long archery duel — cutting off his head and arms (Karṇa Parva 16–22).
- **Karṇa breaks the Pāṇḍava ranks** - Karṇa fights with terrible power, scattering the Pāṇḍava army and routing **Sātyaki**, the **Draupadeyas** (Draupadī's sons), and others (Karṇa Parva 33–48).
- **Karṇa defeats Nakula and Sahadeva — but spares them** - Karṇa overpowers **Nakula** (shattering his chariot) and **Sahadeva**, yet does not kill them — honoring his **promise to Kuntī** that he would slay none of her sons but Arjuna; he taunts Nakula and lets him go (Karṇa Parva 21, 33).
  - *Variant:* in some accounts **Śalya physically restrains Karṇa** from finishing Nakula/Sahadeva (his own nephews); in the dominant reading it is Karṇa's own vow to Kuntī that stays his hand. See the [Karṇa–Kuntī meeting](05-udyoga-parva.md) in the Udyoga Parva.
- **Karṇa defeats and spares Yudhiṣṭhira** - Karṇa worsts **Yudhiṣṭhira** himself, mocks him as unfit for war, and spares his life (again by the Kuntī-vow); the wounded king withdraws toward the camp (Karṇa Parva 49 ff.).

## 4. The 17th day — toward the slaying (Karṇa-vadha Parva)

### Yudhiṣṭhira wounded; the Arjuna quarrel

- **Yudhiṣṭhira retreats wounded** - Battered by Karṇa, **Yudhiṣṭhira leaves the field** and lies wounded in his tent; thinking Arjuna has at last killed Karṇa, he waits for the good news (Karṇa Parva 49–67).
- **Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa visit the tent** - When **Arjuna** comes (Karṇa still alive), Yudhiṣṭhira, humiliated and in pain, **bitterly reviles Arjuna** for not yet killing Karṇa and insults the **Gāṇḍīva** bow (Karṇa Parva 68–69).
- **Arjuna nearly kills Yudhiṣṭhira; Kṛṣṇa intervenes** - Arjuna has a **secret vow to kill anyone who insults Gāṇḍīva**, and draws his sword on his own brother; **Kṛṣṇa stops him**, resolving the dilemma by counsel — Arjuna "kills" Yudhiṣṭhira *figuratively* by speaking disrespectfully to an elder (death-equivalent), then, repentant at having shamed his king, turns the blade on himself until Kṛṣṇa teaches him to "praise himself" (an equivalent suicide) — satisfying both vows without bloodshed (Karṇa Parva 69).
  - *Variant:* the exact casuistry of how Kṛṣṇa lets Arjuna keep both vows (kill the insulter / never harm an elder) is told with differing detail; the core is Kṛṣṇa's teaching that disrespectful speech to an elder equals "killing" him, and self-praise equals "killing" oneself.
- **Arjuna's counter-vow over Abhimanyu** - Re-entering the battle, Arjuna swears he will **slay Karṇa this day** to avenge **Abhimanyu** (Arjuna's son, killed by an unfair gang-attack in the [Droṇa Parva](07-drona-parva.md)), declaring he will first kill Karṇa's son and then Karṇa (Karṇa Parva 70 ff.).

### The death of Duḥśāsana

- **Bhīma kills Duḥśāsana** - **Bhīma** seizes **Duḥśāsana** (the Kaurava who had dragged and disrobed [Draupadī](02-sabha-parva.md) in the dice-hall), smashes him down, **tears open his chest, and drinks his blood** — fulfilling the twin vows he swore at the dice-game: to break Duḥśāsana's chest and drink his blood (Karṇa Parva 61 / 83).
- **Draupadī's hair washed in the blood** - Bhīma carries the blood to **Draupadī**, who at last **binds up the hair she had left loose since the dice-hall outrage**, washing/dressing it in Duḥśāsana's blood — the long-deferred vengeance completed (Karṇa Parva, vadha section).
  - *Variant:* whether Draupadī literally washes her hair in the blood or Bhīma simply brings it to her as proof varies by retelling; the loosed-hair vow is from the [Sabhā Parva](02-sabha-parva.md).
- **Karṇa's son Vṛṣasena slain** - Keeping his fresh vow, **Arjuna kills Karṇa's son Vṛṣasena** before Karṇa's eyes — cutting off his bow, arms, and head with razor-arrows — to avenge Abhimanyu and to break Karṇa's spirit; the grief-stricken Karṇa charges Arjuna in fury (Karṇa Parva 85).

## 5. The final duel — Karṇa's death

- **The single combat of Karṇa and Arjuna** - The two greatest archers meet at last; the gods themselves are said to take sides watching, and the duel is described as world-shaking. Karṇa fights superbly and at moments gains the upper hand (Karṇa Parva 87–91).
- **The Nāgāstra and Aśvasena** - Karṇa looses a deadly serpent-arrow (**Nāgāstra**), animated by the snake **Aśvasena** (whose mother Arjuna had killed in the burning of [Khāṇḍava](01-adi-parva.md), seeking revenge); **Kṛṣṇa presses the chariot down with his foot**, so the arrow strikes Arjuna's diadem instead of his head. Karṇa, bound by warrior-honor, refuses to fire the same arrow twice when Aśvasena offers to return (Karṇa Parva 90).
- **Karṇa's chariot-wheel sinks** - At the crisis Karṇa's left **chariot-wheel sinks into the soft earth** — the **Earth-goddess's (Bhūmi's) curse** taking effect. Karṇa leaps down to free it and, invoking the rules of fair war, asks Arjuna to **pause** (Karṇa Parva 91).
- **Karṇa forgets the Brahmāstra** - Trying to summon his ultimate weapon, Karṇa finds the **incantation gone from his memory** — **Paraśurāma's curse** striking home (his guru had cursed that the knowledge would fail him when he most needed it, after Karṇa lied about being a Brāhmaṇa to learn it) (Karṇa Parva 91).
- **Kṛṣṇa rejects Karṇa's appeal to dharma** - When Karṇa cites the rules of war, **Kṛṣṇa rebukes him**, listing Karṇa's own past breaches of dharma — the disrobing of Draupadī, the cheating dice, the unfair killing of the unarmed boy Abhimanyu — and tells Arjuna to strike (Karṇa Parva 91).
- **The Añjalika beheads Karṇa** - As Karṇa struggles with the mired wheel (weaponless and helpless — the **Brāhmaṇa's cow-curse** fulfilled, that he would die helpless as the cow he once slew), **Arjuna looses the Añjalika-astra and severs Karṇa's head** (Karṇa Parva 91).
  - *Variant:* sources differ on the exact sequence and number of curses converging — most catalog **three** (Paraśurāma's forgotten-mantra curse, the Brāhmaṇa's "wheel-sink/helpless-death" curse for killing the cow, and the Earth-goddess's wheel curse); some traditions merge the wheel-curse and the cow-curse, others keep Bhūmi's curse separate. Catalogued here as distinct.
- **The light leaves Karṇa** - At his death a radiance (his solar nature) is said to leave his body and merge into the sun; even the Kauravas mourn, and Sūrya, his father, is grieved (Karṇa Parva 91–93).

---

## 6. Aftermath (close of the parva)

- **Kaurava rout and despair** - With Karṇa fallen the Kaurava army breaks; **Duryodhana** is plunged into grief, his last great champion gone; the Pāṇḍavas exult (Karṇa Parva 93–96).
- **Śalya named next commander** - As the parva closes the demoralized Kauravas turn to **Śalya** — the very man who had broken Karṇa's spirit — as the **fourth and last** generalissimo, leading into the **[Śalya Parva](09-shalya-parva.md)** (transition to Karṇa Parva's end / Śalya Parva's start).
- **The secret of Karṇa's birth** - The full revelation that Karṇa was **Kuntī's firstborn**, the eldest Pāṇḍava, is grieved over later — Yudhiṣṭhira learns it only after the war (in the [Strī](11-stri-parva.md) / Śānti parvas), turning his victory bitter; foreshadowed by the [Kuntī–Karṇa](05-udyoga-parva.md) and [Bhīṣma–Karṇa](06-bhishma-parva.md) meetings.

## Variant traditions & afterlives

- *Variant (Karṇa as tragic hero):* later and devotional/popular tradition reframes Karṇa as the great tragic figure of the epic — loyal, generous (*dānavīra Karṇa*, who never refused a suppliant), wronged by birth-caste and by fate's stacked curses; his death by a sunk wheel while weaponless is read as the gods' acknowledgment that he could not be beaten fairly. [folk/devotional]
- *Variant (Indra's earrings & armour):* Karṇa's vulnerability traces to the [Vana Parva](03-vana-parva.md) episode where **Indra**, disguised as a Brāhmaṇa, begs away Karṇa's inborn **kavaca (armour) and kuṇḍala (earrings)** that made him invincible — given Karṇa the one-use **Vāsava/Śakti dart** in exchange (which Karṇa was forced to spend on **Ghaṭotkaca** in the Droṇa Parva, leaving him mortal against Arjuna).
- *Variant (regional vernacular epics) [folk tradition]:* the Telugu **Andhra Mahābhāratamu** (Tikkana's war-books), the Tamil **Villipāratam**, and other regional versions re-color the Karṇa–Śalya exchanges and Karṇa's death; folk and modern retellings (e.g. *Mṛtyuñjaya*, *Rashmirathi*) center Karṇa sympathetically.
- *Variant (date of the war):* traditional astronomy places the war at the Dvāpara–Kali junction (**3102 BCE** by Kali-era reckoning; **~3138 BCE** by some eclipse readings) [disputed]; academic dating treats the war-books as composed over centuries BCE–CE [scholarly]. See [Mahābhārata overview — Dating](README.md).
