# Sabhā Parva — The Book of the Assembly Hall

> Cosmic address: [Brahma's current life](../../README.md) › [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md) › [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md) › **[Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** — the Pāṇḍavas at their zenith, then their fall through the dice.
>
> Up: [Mahābhārata overview](README.md) · Previous: [01 — Ādi Parva](01-adi-parva.md) · Next: [03 — Vana Parva](03-vana-parva.md). Parent corpus: [Smṛti — Itihāsa & Epics](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md).

The **Sabhā Parva** ("Book of the Assembly Hall") is the **second of the 18 parvas** of the [Mahābhārata](README.md). In the Bhandarkar **Critical Edition** it runs to ~**81 adhyāyas** in **10 upaparvas** (sub-books); the older Vulgate (Nīlakaṇṭha) count is higher. It is the pivot of the whole epic: it carries the Pāṇḍavas to the summit of imperial glory (the *Rājasūya* coronation) and then, in a single night of dice, strips them of everything — kingdom, freedom, and the dignity of their wife — converting a dynastic rivalry into an irreparable blood-feud. The disrobing of Draupadī (*vastraharaṇa*) and her unanswered question to the assembly are the moral fulcrum on which the war later turns.

## 1. Sabhākriyā — Maya's wondrous hall

- **Maya Dānava's debt** - At the burning of the Khāṇḍava forest (end of [Ādi Parva](01-adi-parva.md)), Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa spared the *asura* architect **Maya**, who in gratitude offers to build Yudhiṣṭhira a peerless assembly hall (Sabhā Parva 1).
- **The illusory hall (*māyā-sabhā*)** - Maya raises a hall of dazzling optical illusion at **Indraprastha** — crystal floors mistaken for water, real pools mistaken for solid floor, jewelled pillars, a thousand cubits square. It is the most beautiful sabhā on earth, rivalling the celestial assemblies (Sabhā Parva 3).
- **The materials from Bindusaras** - Maya fetches a great mace (later Bhīma's), the conch *Devadatta* (later Arjuna's), and gem-stone building material from Lake **Bindusaras** in the Himālaya, a treasure once of King Vṛṣaparvan (Sabhā Parva 3).
  - *Note:* the famous later jibe — Duryodhana falling into water mistaking it for floor, with Draupadī (or her maids) laughing, "the blind man's son is blind" — is told in the **Dyūta** section, not here, and is itself a flashpoint for Duryodhana's envy. *Variant [scholarly]:* this mockery scene is much expanded in the Vulgate and in folk/televised retellings; the Critical Edition's version is terse.

## 2. Lokapāla-sabhākhyāna — Nārada's catalogue of the divine assemblies

- **Nārada's visit** - The sage **Nārada** arrives, and on seeing the new hall describes by comparison the assembly-halls of the four **Lokapālas** (world-guardians) and of Brahmā, modelling ideal kingship (Sabhā Parva 5–11).
- **The mirror of good governance** - Nārada catechises Yudhiṣṭhira with the **rāja-dharma praśna**: scores of probing questions on statecraft — fair taxation, irrigation, spies, justice for the weak, treatment of brāhmaṇas, soldiers' pay, fort defence — a compact *nīti-śāstra* embedded in narrative (Sabhā Parva 5). See [Concepts: puruṣārthas & rāja-dharma](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md).
- **The five celestial sabhās** - Nārada describes the courts of **Indra** (Devasabhā), **Yama**, **Varuṇa**, **Kubera** (Vaiśravaṇa), and **Brahmā**, naming the kings, ṛṣis, and beings who attend each (Sabhā Parva 6–11). See [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
- **Hariścandra and the Rājasūya seed** - In Indra's sabhā (Devasabhā) only one human king sits among the gods — **Hariścandra** — because he alone completed the **Rājasūya**. Yudhiṣṭhira learns his father **Pāṇḍu** longs for him to perform that very sacrifice; this plants the ambition (Sabhā Parva 11–12).

## 3. Rājasūyārambha — the Rājasūya proposed; the Jarāsandha obstacle

- **The Rājasūya defined** - The *Rājasūya* is the imperial consecration by which a king becomes **samrāṭ / cakravartin** (universal sovereign); it requires that all other kings either submit or be subdued (Sabhā Parva 12–13).
- **Kṛṣṇa summoned for counsel** - Yudhiṣṭhira sends for **Kṛṣṇa**, who endorses the rite but identifies the one insurmountable obstacle: **Jarāsandha**, king of **Magadha**, who blocks any cakravartin claim (Sabhā Parva 13–14).
- **Jarāsandha's tyranny** - Jarāsandha has imprisoned **86 (var. 100) captive kings** in his mountain fortress, intending to sacrifice them to **Rudra/Śiva** when he reaches 100 — i.e., human sacrifice. Kṛṣṇa frames his removal as a dharmic necessity, not mere conquest (Sabhā Parva 14).
- **Jarāsandha's birth & invulnerability** - Born in two lifeless halves to two queens of King Bṛhadratha and joined by the *rākṣasī* **Jarā** (hence *Jarā-sandha*, "joined by Jarā"); he can only be killed by being **torn apart** along that seam (Sabhā Parva 16–17). [folk/etiological motif]
- **Kṛṣṇa's enmity** - Jarāsandha is father-in-law of the slain **Kaṃsa**; his repeated sieges of Mathurā drove Kṛṣṇa's Yādavas to relocate to **Dvārakā** — personal motive layered over the dharmic one (Sabhā Parva 13–14).

## 4. Jarāsandha-vadha — the duel of Bhīma

- **The brāhmaṇa disguise** - Kṛṣṇa, **Bhīma**, and **Arjuna** travel to Magadha disguised as snātaka brāhmaṇas, enter unconventionally (breaking through, not by the gate), and reveal themselves as kṣatriya foes demanding combat (Sabhā Parva 20–21).
- **The choice of opponent** - Jarāsandha, scorning Kṛṣṇa (a cowherd-trader) and the younger Arjuna, chooses to wrestle the strongest — **Bhīma** (Sabhā Parva 22).
- **The 13-/27-day wrestling match** - The two wrestle for days (texts give **13**, some **27** days); Jarāsandha's halves keep rejoining whenever Bhīma throws him (Sabhā Parva 24).
- **Kṛṣṇa's hint and the kill** - Kṛṣṇa picks up a twig (or a leaf), splits it lengthwise and casts the halves in opposite directions, signalling the secret. Bhīma tears Jarāsandha in two and **flings the halves apart** so they cannot rejoin (Sabhā Parva 24).
  - *Variant (Vulgate / folk):* Bhīma "reads" the leaf-sign directly; in some tellings Kṛṣṇa throws the two halves in opposite directions himself, in others it is Bhīma alone.
- **The kings freed** - The imprisoned kings are released and become Yudhiṣṭhira's allies; Jarāsandha's son **Sahadeva** (of Magadha) is installed as a vassal king (Sabhā Parva 24).

## 5. Digvijaya — conquest of the four quarters

- **The four-direction campaign** - To establish universal sovereignty the four younger Pāṇḍavas subdue the four directions, bringing back tribute (Sabhā Parva 25–29):
  - **Arjuna — North** - Subdues the Himālayan and trans-Himālayan kingdoms (Kuluta, Kashmir, Bāhlīka, Kāmboja, even the Uttarakurus' border) (Sabhā Parva 25).
  - **Bhīma — East** - Conquers Pāñcāla, Videha, Magadha's borderlands, Aṅga, Vaṅga and the eastern realms (Sabhā Parva 27).
  - **Sahadeva — South** - Goes deep into the Deccan and Tamil south (Vidarbha, Kiṣkindhā, Māhiṣmatī, Pāṇḍya, and contact with **Vibhīṣaṇa of Laṅkā**, who sends tribute) (Sabhā Parva 28).
  - **Nakula — West** - Subdues the western Sindhu lands, the Mlecchas, and desert kingdoms (Sabhā Parva 29).
- **Wealth pours into Indraprastha** - Tribute is so vast the treasury cannot count it; this magnificence is the very thing that ignites Duryodhana's envy (Sabhā Parva 30–31).

## 6. Rājasūyika — the sacrifice performed

- **The grand assembly** - Vyāsa officiates as chief priest; all the great houses — Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Kṛpa, Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Vidura, Karṇa, the Kauravas, the Yādavas — attend (Sabhā Parva 33).
- **Tasks distributed** - Each guest is given an office; pointedly, **Duryodhana** is put in charge of the **treasury/receipt of tribute**, where he witnesses the Pāṇḍavas' wealth at close range (Sabhā Parva 33–34).
- **The rite completed** - Yudhiṣṭhira is consecrated **samrāṭ**; the sacrifice succeeds — but the consecration triggers the *agra-pūjā* (first-honour) dispute (Sabhā Parva 34).

## 7. Arghyāharaṇa — the first-honour dispute

- **Bhīṣma's nomination** - At the question of who receives the **arghya** (the foremost guest-honour), **Bhīṣma** advises that **Kṛṣṇa** is the most worthy of all present; Sahadeva performs the honour to Kṛṣṇa (Sabhā Parva 35–36).
- **Śiśupāla's objection** - **Śiśupāla**, king of Cedi, erupts: Kṛṣṇa is no king, no priest, no elder — merely a cowherd — and honouring him insults the assembled monarchs. He abuses Bhīṣma and storms toward leaving with allied kings (Sabhā Parva 37–38).

## 8. Śiśupāla-vadha — the slaying of Śiśupāla

- **The 100-offences boon** - Kṛṣṇa explains he had promised Śiśupāla's mother (his own aunt) to **forgive 100 offences**; Śiśupāla, born with three eyes and four arms that vanished on Kṛṣṇa's lap, was destined to die by Kṛṣṇa's hand (Sabhā Parva 40). [etiological birth-prophecy]
- **The 101st offence** - When Śiśupāla's insults exceed the hundredth, Kṛṣṇa releases the **Sudarśana Cakra** and beheads him in the very assembly hall (Sabhā Parva 42).
- **The light merging into Kṛṣṇa** - A radiance rises from Śiśupāla's body and merges into Kṛṣṇa — the **jaya-vijaya / gatekeeper-curse** payoff: Śiśupāla was the third and final birth of Viṣṇu's doorkeeper **Jaya** (after Hiraṇyakaśipu and Rāvaṇa), liberated by death at the Lord's hand (Sabhā Parva 42; the three-births frame is fullest in *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 7.1). See [Daśāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
  - *Variant ([Bhāgavata Purāṇa]):* explicitly names the **Jaya–Vijaya curse** of Sanaka-kumāras and the three-life sequence; the Mahābhārata gives the birth-prophecy but is lighter on the doorkeeper frame.
  - *Note:* the standalone Sanskrit *mahākāvya* **Śiśupāla-vadha** of **Māgha** [late text, ~7th c. CE] retells this single episode in 20 ornate cantos.
- **The Rājasūya concludes** - With the disturbance ended, the consecration is sealed and the kings depart. Yudhiṣṭhira stands as undisputed emperor — the high-water mark of Pāṇḍava fortune (Sabhā Parva 44).

## 9. Dyūta — the game of dice

- **Duryodhana's envy** - Touring the *māyā-sabhā*, Duryodhana stumbles (mistaking crystal for water, water for crystal) and is laughed at; consumed by jealousy of the Pāṇḍavas' wealth and the humiliation, he falls into despair before his father (Sabhā Parva 43, 45).
  - *Variant (Vulgate / Peria Purāṇam-style folk):* Draupadī herself laughs and taunts "*andhasya putraḥ andhaḥ*" ("the blind man's son is also blind"). *Variant [scholarly]:* this taunt is **absent from the Critical Edition** and likely a later dramatic addition; the CE attributes the laughter to servants and Bhīma/Arjuna/the twins, not to Draupadī.
- **Śakuni's plot** - Duryodhana's uncle **Śakuni** of Gāndhāra counsels that the Pāṇḍavas cannot be beaten in war or by fair means, but Yudhiṣṭhira's one weakness is his love of dice; Śakuni, a master (cheat) of the dice, will play in Duryodhana's stead (Sabhā Parva 45–46).
- **Dhṛtarāṣṭra consents** - Over Vidura's misgivings, the blind king permits the invitation, rationalising it as fate/destiny; a gambling hall is built at Hāstinapura (Sabhā Parva 45–46).
- **Vidura's warning ignored** - **Vidura** is sent to deliver the invitation but openly protests it will breed ruin; Yudhiṣṭhira, bound by kṣatriya custom not to refuse a challenge, comes anyway, foreseeing disaster (Sabhā Parva 51–52).
- **Śakuni wins by foul dice** - Playing for Duryodhana, Śakuni wins every throw. Yudhiṣṭhira stakes and loses in escalating order: **jewels and gold → chariots and horses → slaves and cattle → the whole kingdom and city → his brothers (Nakula, Sahadeva, then Arjuna, then Bhīma) → himself → finally Draupadī** (Sabhā Parva 53–58).
  - *Variant [scholarly]:* the Critical Edition gives a leaner list of stakes; the Vulgate elaborates each wager with set speeches.
- **Draupadī staked and "won"** - At Śakuni's prompting Yudhiṣṭhira wagers **Draupadī** as his last stake and loses. Duryodhana orders her brought to the hall as a won slave (Sabhā Parva 58).
- **Vidura's protest** - Vidura denounces the act as adharma and refuses to summon her; the **Prātikāmin** (herald) is sent instead and is rebuffed by Draupadī (Sabhā Parva 60).
- **Draupadī's question (the *praśna*)** - Draupadī sends back the searing question that paralyses the assembly: **"Did Yudhiṣṭhira lose himself first, or me? If he had already lost himself and was no longer his own master, by what right did he stake me?"** The hall cannot answer (Sabhā Parva 60).
- **Duḥśāsana drags her in** - **Duḥśāsana** seizes Draupadī by the hair — she menstruating and in a single garment — and hauls her into the men's assembly, taunting that she is now a slave (Sabhā Parva 60).
- **The elders' paralysis** - **Bhīṣma** equivocates: the question of dharma is "subtle" (*sūkṣma*) — a slave's wife may be a stake, yet a man who has lost himself may own nothing; he cannot resolve it (Sabhā Parva 60–62).
- **Vikarṇa's lone defence** - The Kaurava **Vikarṇa** alone speaks for dharma, arguing on three grounds that Draupadī was **not lawfully won**: (1) Yudhiṣṭhira had already lost himself first; (2) he was provoked/cheated into the game; (3) Draupadī is the common wife of five and could not be staked by one alone (Sabhā Parva 61).
- **Karṇa's brutal rebuttal** - **Karṇa** crushes Vikarṇa's defence, declares Draupadī a slave with no claim to modesty, and notoriously calls her a **"*bandhakī*" (a woman of loose virtue)** for having five husbands — ordering her and the Pāṇḍavas stripped of their fine clothes (Sabhā Parva 61).
- **The disrobing (*vastraharaṇa* / *cīraharaṇa*)** - At Duḥśāsana's hands Draupadī's sari is pulled — and as each length is torn away, **another garment endlessly appears**, so that she cannot be stripped; Duḥśāsana tires beside a heap of cloth (Sabhā Parva 61).
  - *Variant (Vulgate / popular):* Draupadī cries out to **Kṛṣṇa (Govinda)**, who from afar miraculously supplies the endless cloth — the cherished *akṣaya-vastra* ("inexhaustible garment") scene of bhakti tradition and all televised retellings.
  - *Variant [scholarly] (Critical Edition):* the CE **omits Draupadī's prayer to Kṛṣṇa and any named agent of the miracle** — the garments simply renew themselves; the Sabhāparvan editor **F. Edgerton** read it as **"cosmic justice / dharma itself" automatically preventing the stripping of a chaste woman**, with no Kṛṣṇa-intervention in the oldest layer.
  - *Variant [scholarly]:* a minority argument holds the whole disrobing is a late interpolation absent from the earliest stratum; mainstream textual scholarship retains the miracle (in the impersonal form) but treats the explicit Kṛṣṇa-rescue as the accretion.
- **The terrible vows** - In the hall the Pāṇḍavas swear oaths that drive the rest of the epic: **Bhīma vows to break Duryodhana's thigh** (which Duryodhana had bared, slapping it and inviting Draupadī to sit on it) and **to drink Duḥśāsana's blood / tear open his chest**; Draupadī vows to **leave her hair unbound until it is washed in Duḥśāsana's blood** (Sabhā Parva 61–63).
- **The omens** - Jackals howl and asses bray at Dhṛtarāṣṭra's hall; frightened by these ill omens (and Vidura's warning), **Dhṛtarāṣṭra intervenes** (Sabhā Parva 63).
- **Draupadī's boons** - Dhṛtarāṣṭra grants Draupadī boons: she frees **Yudhiṣṭhira** from slavery, then her other husbands and their weapons/kingdom; offered a third boon she declines, saying greed is unbecoming — restoring the Pāṇḍavas to liberty and their realm (Sabhā Parva 63–64).
  - *Variant:* the number and exact content of the boons vary slightly across recensions; the core (freeing the husbands) is constant.

## 10. Anudyūta — the second dice game and the exile

- **Duryodhana's complaint** - Furious that the boons undid his victory, Duryodhana pressures Dhṛtarāṣṭra to allow **one more game** with a single, decisive stake (Sabhā Parva 66–67).
- **The single wager** - The loser of one throw must go into **exile for 12 years in the forest** plus a **13th year incognito (*ajñāta-vāsa*)** — if discovered in the 13th year, the term restarts (12 + 1 = 13 years total) (Sabhā Parva 67).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira recalled and loses** - Already departed, the Pāṇḍavas are summoned back; Yudhiṣṭhira (again bound by custom, again knowing the dice are crooked) plays Śakuni and loses the single throw (Sabhā Parva 67).
- **The exile decreed** - The five Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī don deerskins/bark garments and depart Hāstinapura for the forest, beginning the **13-year exile** that fills the [Vana Parva](03-vana-parva.md) and [Virāṭa Parva](04-virata-parva.md) (Sabhā Parva 68–72).
- **Departing portents and threats** - As they leave, Draupadī and Bhīma signal by gesture and word the vengeance to come; **Vidura** shelters their mother **Kuntī**; sages and citizens lament. **Dhṛtarāṣṭra**, fearful of the omens, questions Vidura on the future — and Vidura/Sañjaya foretell ruin for the Kuru house (Sabhā Parva 70–72).
- **The seed of Kurukṣetra** - The parva closes with the kingdom morally bankrupt though the Pāṇḍavas are physically exiled: the dice-hall injustice, not the battlefield, is repeatedly named in later parvas as the true cause of the war (Sabhā Parva 72; echoed in [Udyoga](05-udyoga-parva.md) and [Strī](11-stri-parva.md) parvas).
