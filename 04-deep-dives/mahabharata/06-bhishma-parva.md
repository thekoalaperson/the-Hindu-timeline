# Bhīṣma Parva — The Book of Bhishma

> Cosmic address: [Brahma's current life](../../README.md) › [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md) › [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md) › **[Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** — the opening of the 18-day Kurukṣetra War at the very junction of Dvāpara and [Kali Yuga](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).
>
> Up: [Mahābhārata overview](README.md) · Previous: [05 — Udyoga Parva](05-udyoga-parva.md) · Next: [07 — Droṇa Parva](07-drona-parva.md). Parent corpus: [Smṛti — Itihāsa & Epics](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md).

The **Bhīṣma Parva** ("Book of Bhīṣma") is the **sixth of the 18 parvas** of the [Mahābhārata](README.md) and the **first of the six war-books** (Bhīṣma → Droṇa → Karṇa → Śalya → Sauptika → Strī). It is the threshold of the great battle: it begins with the cosmic prologue narrated by **Sañjaya** through his divinely granted second sight (*divya-dṛṣṭi*), embeds the entire **[Bhagavad Gītā](../bhagavad-gita.md)** as Arjuna freezes at the moment of attack, then runs **the first ten days of the war** under the Kaurava generalissimo **Bhīṣma** — closing with his fall on the **bed of arrows** (*śara-śayyā*), pierced down by Arjuna from behind the screen of **Śikhaṇḍin**. In the Bhandarkar **Critical Edition** it runs to ~**117 adhyāyas** in **4 upaparvas** (~5,380–5,884 ślokas depending on count); the older **Vulgate** numbers ~124 chapters.

- *Variant [scholarly]:* the Critical Edition strips a large body of "spurious and corrupted" verses from the Vulgate's ~124 chapters down to ~117; verse totals therefore differ widely between editions (commonly cited ~5,381 vs ~5,884).

---

## The 4 upaparvas (sub-books)

| # | Upaparva | Chapters (approx., Vulgate) | Core content |
|---|----------|-----------------------------|--------------|
| 1 | **Jambū-khaṇḍa-vinirmāṇa** ("forming of the Jambū continent") | 1–10 | Sañjaya granted divine sight; rules of war fixed; cosmic-geographical prologue |
| 2 | **Bhūmi Parva** ("the Earth") | 11–12 | Sañjaya describes the earth, *dvīpas*, and heavenly bodies — the shortest sub-book |
| 3 | **Bhagavad-gītā Parva** | 13–42 (Gītā = 25–42) | Both armies array; Arjuna's despondency; the **18-chapter Bhagavad Gītā** |
| 4 | **Bhīṣma-vadha Parva** ("the slaying of Bhīṣma") | 43–124 | The first ten days of battle and Bhīṣma's fall — the longest sub-book |

- *Variant [scholarly]:* sub-parva names and numbering vary; the first is sometimes given as **Karṇa-khaṇḍa / Senodyoga** material, and the Gītā chapters are numbered **25–42** within the parva (= chapters 13–30 of the Bhagavad-gītā Parva sub-book).

## 1. Prologue — divine sight, the rules of war, and the cosmic ground (Jambū-khaṇḍa & Bhūmi)

- **Sañjaya's divya-dṛṣṭi** - **Vyāsa** offers the blind king **Dhṛtarāṣṭra** the boon of seeing the war himself; he refuses to watch his kin die, so Vyāsa grants the eye of the gods (*divya-cakṣus*) to his charioteer-bard **Sañjaya**, who will report every event — making the whole parva (and the Gītā) Sañjaya's eyewitness narration to Dhṛtarāṣṭra (Bhīṣma Parva 2).
- **The omens of catastrophe** - Sañjaya and Vyāsa recount terrible portents — bloody rain, earthquakes, eclipses, headless trunks, beasts weeping — marking the war as a world-ending event at the Dvāpara–Kali junction (Bhīṣma Parva 2–3).
- **The rules of righteous war (dharma-yuddha)** - Before fighting the elders fix conventions: combat ceases at sunset; equals fight equals; no striking one who has surrendered, fled, is disarmed, or turns away; charioteer fights charioteer, foot-soldier foot-soldier; no killing of non-combatants (Bhīṣma Parva 1).
  - *Note:* nearly every one of these rules is broken before the war ends (Abhimanyu's encirclement, the killing of the sleeping camp, the thigh-strike on Duryodhana) — the parva sets the standard precisely so the later transgressions land.
- **The geography of Jambūdvīpa & Bhārata** - At Dhṛtarāṣṭra's request Sañjaya describes **Jambūdvīpa**, Mount **Meru/Sumeru**, the *varṣas* (continental zones), and **Bhārata-varṣa** itself — locating the battlefield within the [cosmographic scheme](../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md) (Bhīṣma Parva 5–11).
- **The Earth and the heavens (Bhūmi Parva)** - Sañjaya continues with the *dvīpas* (island-continents), oceans, Śāka and Śālmali lands, the moon, sun and stars — a compact cosmology preceding the slaughter (Bhīṣma Parva 11–12).

## 2. The two armies array & Arjuna's collapse (Bhagavad-gītā Parva, pre-Gītā)

- **The akṣauhiṇīs drawn up** - The 11 Kaurava and 7 Pāṇḍava *akṣauhiṇīs* (18 in all) array on the field of **Kurukṣetra** / **Dharmakṣetra**; conch-shells blast — Kṛṣṇa's **Pāñcajanya**, Arjuna's **Devadatta**, Bhīma's **Pauṇḍra**, etc. (Bhīṣma Parva 25 / Gītā 1).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira crosses to honour the elders** - In a famous gesture, **Yudhiṣṭhira** lays down his weapons and walks unarmed across to the enemy line to touch the feet of **Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Kṛpa and Śalya** and seek their leave to fight them; each blesses him with victory while bound by duty to Duryodhana (Bhīṣma Parva 43).
  - *Variant:* it is in this same crossing that **Bhīṣma, Droṇa and Kṛpa** are said to hint at how they may be overcome — a foreshadow of the death-secrets revealed on day 10. [varies by recension]
- **Yuyutsu changes sides** - **Yuyutsu**, Dhṛtarāṣṭra's son by a *vaiśya* woman, alone accepts Yudhiṣṭhira's open invitation and **crosses over to the Pāṇḍavas** — the sole Kaurava prince to survive the war (Bhīṣma Parva 43).
- **Arjuna asks to see both hosts** - Arjuna has **Kṛṣṇa** drive his chariot between the armies; seeing grandfathers, teachers, uncles, cousins and friends arrayed to kill one another, he is overcome with grief and pity (*viṣāda*), his limbs failing, **Gāṇḍīva** slipping from his hand (Bhīṣma Parva 25 / Gītā 1).

## 3. The Bhagavad Gītā (Bhīṣma Parva 25–42)

- **The Song of the Lord** - Arjuna's refusal to fight triggers **Kṛṣṇa's 700-verse teaching**, the **[Bhagavad Gītā](../bhagavad-gita.md)** — 18 chapters embedded as adhyāyas 25–42 of the Bhīṣma Parva, reported by Sañjaya. Kṛṣṇa resolves Arjuna's paralysis through the imperishable *ātman*, *niṣkāma-karma* (action without attachment to fruit), the yogas of *jñāna*, *karma* and *bhakti*, and the doctrine of *svadharma* — climaxing in the **Viśvarūpa-darśana** (chapter 11), the terrifying universal form (Bhīṣma Parva 25–42).
  - *See the dedicated deep-dive:* **[Bhagavad Gītā](../bhagavad-gita.md)** — chapter-by-chapter, with the variant readings and the 18-chapter / 3-yoga structure.
- **Arjuna's resolve restored** - At the end Arjuna declares his delusion gone (*naṣṭo mohaḥ*) and takes up Gāṇḍīva; the war can begin (Gītā 18 / Bhīṣma Parva 42).
- **Bhīṣma named commander** - On the Kaurava side **Bhīṣma** is consecrated supreme commander (*senāpati*), accepting on condition he will not slay the Pāṇḍavas and will not fight Śikhaṇḍin (whom he regards as born a woman); he vows to fell **10,000 warriors a day** (Udyoga/Bhīṣma Parva juncture).
  - *Variant:* the figure given for Bhīṣma's daily kill-count and the exact terms of his command vary between recensions.

## 4. The first ten days of war — Bhīṣma-vadha Parva

> Daily structure: each side forms a *vyūha* (battle-array) at dawn, fights to sunset, then withdraws to camp; Sañjaya reports each day to Dhṛtarāṣṭra. Casualty figures are hyperbolic and inconsistent across recensions.

- **Day 1 — the opening slaughter** - Bhīṣma forms a great array; the first day goes badly for the Pāṇḍavas. **Bhīṣma slays Śveta** (Sveta), son of Virāṭa, with a celestial (Brahma) weapon, and **Śalya slays Uttara** (the prince of the Virāṭa Parva) — two of Virāṭa's sons fall in a single day (Bhīṣma Parva 45).
  - *Variant:* some retellings reverse the pairings or have **Bhagadatta** kill one of the Virāṭa princes; the Northern and Southern recensions differ on details of day 1.
- **Day 2 — Bhīṣma checked** - Arjuna and **Dhṛṣṭadyumna** rally the Pāṇḍavas; Bhīma drives deep into the Kaurava ranks and Arjuna duels Bhīṣma, forcing a Kaurava retreat by evening (Bhīṣma Parva 46–52).
- **Day 3 — the Garuḍa & crescent arrays; Kṛṣṇa's first fury** - Bhīṣma forms the **Garuḍa-vyūha**, Arjuna the crescent (*ardha-candra*). Arjuna spares his grandfather; seeing Arjuna fight half-heartedly while Bhīṣma devastates the army, **Kṛṣṇa breaks his vow not to take up arms — leaps down, seizes a chariot-wheel** (or whip-and-wheel) and rushes at Bhīṣma, who joyfully welcomes death at the Lord's hand; **Arjuna restrains him**, clasping his legs, and swears to fight in earnest (Bhīṣma Parva 55–59).
  - *Variant:* the tradition places **Kṛṣṇa's wheel-charge** sometimes on **day 3** and sometimes on **day 9** — and several recensions narrate it on **both** days as two separate outbursts. Catalogued here for both days.
- **Day 4 — Bhīma's rampage; Bhagadatta's elephant** - **Bhīma** slays many of Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sons (commonly **eight or fourteen** Kauravas), routs elephant divisions, and is wounded; **Bhagadatta** of Prāgjyotiṣa attacks on his great elephant **Supratīka**, wounding Bhīma and Ghaṭotkaca; **Ghaṭotkaca** (Bhīma's rākṣasa son) deploys illusions to scatter the Kauravas (Bhīṣma Parva 64).
- **Day 5 — the Makara & Śyena arrays** - Bhīṣma forms the **Makara-vyūha**, the Pāṇḍavas the **Śyena (hawk)**; heavy fighting; **Sātyaki** and his sons distinguish themselves and Bhīma again breaks the line (Bhīṣma Parva 66–69).
- **Day 6 — Krauñca & Maṇḍala arrays** - Bhīma penetrates the Kaurava formation and is briefly surrounded; **Dhṛṣṭadyumna** fights **Droṇa**; the day's exchange is indecisive with great loss (Bhīṣma Parva 70–74).
- **Day 7 — the Maṇḍala vs Vajra** - Massed combats: **Virāṭa**, **Droṇa**, **Aśvatthāman**, **Sātyaki**, the twins **Nakula & Sahadeva** (who worst **Śalya**); **Saṅkha**, another son of Virāṭa, is slain (Bhīṣma Parva 75–82).
- **Day 8 — the death of Irāvān; Ghaṭotkaca ascendant** - **Irāvān** (Arjuna's son by the Nāga princess **Ulūpī**) fights brilliantly but is **beheaded by the rākṣasa Alambuṣa** (sent by Duryodhana); **Bhīma** slays more of Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sons (commonly **eight or seventeen**); **Ghaṭotkaca** dominates the field with terrifying illusions, nearly breaking the Kauravas before nightfall (Bhīṣma Parva 83–91).
  - *Variant:* the number of Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sons Bhīma kills on each day (and the running total) differs between recensions; Bhīma's vow is to kill **all 100** before the war ends.
- **Day 9 — Bhīṣma unleashed; Kṛṣṇa's wheel again** - Bhīṣma fights without restraint, mowing down the Pāṇḍava army; **Abhimanyu** and **Sātyaki** counter; **Alambuṣa** uses illusion-warfare and is checked. In despair at Arjuna's continued reluctance to kill his grandfather, **Kṛṣṇa again leaps from the chariot, snatches up a wheel** and charges Bhīṣma; Arjuna restrains him a second time and vows to slay Bhīṣma the next day (Bhīṣma Parva 92–106).
- **The night council — the Pāṇḍavas ask Bhīṣma how to kill him** - Unable to overcome the invincible Grandsire, the despairing Pāṇḍavas (with Kṛṣṇa) go by night to **Bhīṣma's own tent**; the dharmic Bhīṣma, who **cannot be defeated while he chooses to fight**, tells them the secret: he **will not raise a weapon against a woman, one formerly a woman, or one bearing a woman's name** — so let them set **Śikhaṇḍin** before him and Arjuna may strike from behind that screen (Bhīṣma Parva 107).
  - *Note on Śikhaṇḍin / Ambā:* **Śikhaṇḍin**, child of **Drupada**, is the reincarnation of **Ambā** — the princess of Kāśī whom Bhīṣma had abducted (for his brother) and then, when rejected, refused to marry, who immolated herself vowing to cause his death and was reborn to that end. Śikhaṇḍin was born/raised female and became male (by a yakṣa's gift of sex, in the common telling). Bhīṣma's vow against fighting "a woman" is thus the exact hinge of his doom — Ambā's revenge fulfilled. See the [Sūrya/Candra-vaṃśa lineages](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) and the [Ādi Parva](01-adi-parva.md) backstory.
  - *Variant (Ambā's rebirth):* in some accounts Ambā is reborn directly as a daughter of Drupada and transformed to male; in others she does penance for Śiva's boon that she will slay Bhīṣma; the yakṣa **Sthūṇākarṇa** sex-exchange episode is elaborated differently across recensions.

## 5. Day 10 — the fall of Bhīṣma (Śara-śayyā)

- **Śikhaṇḍin set before Bhīṣma** - On the tenth day **Arjuna places Śikhaṇḍin at the front** of his chariot; true to his vow, Bhīṣma **lowers his weapons** and will not fight Śikhaṇḍin, smiling at the device (Bhīṣma Parva 114–119).
- **Arjuna strikes from behind the screen** - **Śikhaṇḍin** pelts Bhīṣma with arrows while **Arjuna**, shielded, looses a ceaseless storm that cuts Bhīṣma's bow again and again and pierces him over his whole body (Bhīṣma Parva 119).
- **The bed of arrows (śara-śayyā)** - Bhīṣma falls from his chariot but, so densely pierced, his body is **held off the ground by the arrows themselves** — the celebrated **bed of arrows**; the gods and ṛṣis hail him (Bhīṣma Parva 120).
- **The pillow of arrows** - When Bhīṣma's head hangs unsupported and he asks for a pillow, Arjuna **fires three arrows into the ground to cradle his head** — refusing the soft cushions the kings offer — which Bhīṣma praises as the only fit pillow for a warrior (Bhīṣma Parva 120).
- **The arrow-spring of water (Parjanya-astra)** - Thirsting, Bhīṣma asks for water; Arjuna shoots an arrow into the earth (by some accounts invoking the **Parjanya** water-weapon) and **a jet of pure water** rises to Bhīṣma's mouth — water Bhīṣma declares is from Gaṅgā, his own mother (Bhīṣma Parva 120).
- **The choice of death-by-will (icchā-mṛtyu)** - By the boon of his father **Śāntanu**, Bhīṣma can choose the hour of his death; he resolves **not to die until the sun turns north (uttarāyaṇa)**, the auspicious season — so he lingers on the arrow-bed through the rest of the war, **fifty-eight nights** (commonly cited), to expire only in the later parvas (Bhīṣma Parva 120).
  - *Variant:* the count of days Bhīṣma lay on the bed of arrows before death is variously given (often **58 nights**, sometimes **51 days**); the actual passing is narrated in the **[Anuśāsana Parva](13-anushasana-parva.md)**, after his great discourses in the **[Śānti Parva](12-shanti-parva.md)**.
- **Bhīṣma's last plea for peace** - From the arrow-bed Bhīṣma counsels both sides to **stop the war and make peace**; **Duryodhana refuses**, and the battle resumes under the new commander **Droṇa** (Bhīṣma Parva 121–124).
- **Karṇa's reconciliation** - With Bhīṣma down, **Karṇa** — who had withdrawn rather than serve under a general who slighted him — comes to the arrow-bed; Bhīṣma reveals he knew Karṇa was Kuntī's eldest son and a Pāṇḍava by birth, and urges him to join his brothers or at least fight nobly; Karṇa, loyal to Duryodhana, returns to the field (Bhīṣma Parva 122–124).
  - *Variant:* the placement of the Bhīṣma–Karṇa reconciliation differs — some editions set it here at the close of the Bhīṣma Parva, others fold it into the opening of the [Droṇa Parva](07-drona-parva.md).

## Variant traditions & afterlives

- *Variant (regional vernacular epics) [folk tradition]:* the Telugu **Andhra Mahābhāratamu** (Tikkana's war-books), the Tamil **Villipāratam**, Bengali, Malayāḷam and the Indonesian *Bhīṣma-parwa* localize and re-color the ten days; Tikkana in particular is celebrated for his battle narration.
- *Variant (devotional reframing):* in **Vaiṣṇava bhakti** the parva is read for two supreme moments — Kṛṣṇa as the speaker of the [Gītā](../bhagavad-gita.md), and Kṛṣṇa **breaking his own vow** (twice charging Bhīṣma) to protect his devotee, taken as proof that the Lord prizes devotion over his own word. See [Dashāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) and the [Kṛṣṇa-līlā deep-dive](../krishna-lila.md).
- *Variant (Bhīṣma as exemplar):* the dying Bhīṣma becomes, in the **[Śānti](12-shanti-parva.md)** and **[Anuśāsana](13-anushasana-parva.md)** parvas, the mouthpiece for the epic's longest didactic teaching (*rāja-/āpad-/mokṣa-dharma* and the **Viṣṇu Sahasranāma**); the arrow-bed is iconic in temple art and recitation.
- *Variant (date of the war):* traditional astronomy places the war at the Dvāpara–Kali junction (**3102 BCE** by the Kali-era reckoning; **~3138 BCE** by some eclipse readings) [disputed]; academic dating treats the war-books as composed over centuries BCE–CE [scholarly]. See [Mahābhārata overview — Dating](README.md).
