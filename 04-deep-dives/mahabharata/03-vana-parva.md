# Vana (Āraṇyaka) Parva — The Book of the Forest

> Cosmic address: the third book of the [Mahābhārata](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md), unfolding at the cusp of the [Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara; Kṛṣṇa is the 8th [Daśāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md). The Pāṇḍavas are of the [Candravaṃśa / lunar line](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
> Parent: [Mahābhārata deep-dive index](README.md) · Part of [04-deep-dives](../README.md). Follows [02 Sabhā Parva](02-sabha-parva.md); precedes [04 Virāṭa Parva](04-virata-parva.md). Primary source: Vyāsa's Mahābhārata, Book 3.

The **Vana Parva** ("Book of the Forest"; in the critical edition **Āraṇyakaparvan**) is the third of the eighteen *parvas* and, after the Śānti, the longest by chapters. It chronicles the **twelve years of forest exile** (*vanavāsa*) of the Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī — the penalty exacted after Yudhiṣṭhira lost the second dice-game of the [Sabhā Parva](02-sabha-parva.md) — and ends as they prepare the thirteenth year of disguise (*ajñātavāsa*), which belongs to the next book (CE 3.299; G 313). Outwardly a chronicle of wandering, the book is the epic's great reservoir of **embedded tales** (*upākhyāna*), told to console and instruct the brothers: it holds the *Mahābhārata*'s own short Rāmāyaṇa, the love-stories of Nala–Damayantī and Sāvitrī–Satyavān, Mārkaṇḍeya's vision of the dissolution, Arjuna's winning of the divine weapons, and the closing riddle-test of the Yakṣa.

## Structure — the Sub-Parvas

**How this page cites.** "CE 3.x.y" is the BORI Critical Edition (Sukthankar et al.), Āraṇyakaparvan, chapter x, verse y, as on GRETIL. "G n" is section n of K.M. Ganguli's translation of the vulgate. The two numberings diverge by up to 14 chapters, so every locator below gives both. Material the CE prints only in its footnotes (starred passages, `*nnnn`) or in Appendix I ("App. I No. n") is marked as such. Such material is not in the critical text. Most passages of this kind cited below are also in Ganguli's vulgate, and their Ganguli section is given; the one exception (App. I No. 28, in the Rāmopākhyāna) is flagged where it is cited.

- **Counts.** The CE has **299 adhyāyas**; Ganguli has **313 sections**. The epic's own table of contents, the Parvasaṃgraha, claims 269 adhyāyas and 11,664 ślokas for this book (CE 1.2.128–129), a count neither edition matches.
- **The sixteen sub-parvas of the Parvasaṃgraha (CE 1.2.42–48):** Āraṇyaka, Kirmīravadha, Kairāta, Indralokābhigamana, Tīrthayātrā, Jaṭāsuravadha, Yakṣayuddha, Ājagara, Mārkaṇḍeyasamasyā, Draupadī–Satyabhāmā-saṃvāda, Ghoṣayātrā, Mṛgasvapnabhaya, Vrīhidrauṇika, Draupadīharaṇa, Kuṇḍalāharaṇa, Āraṇeya. Arjunābhigamana, Nalopākhyāna, Nivātakavacayuddha, Rāmopākhyāna and Pativratāmāhātmya are not on this list. They are episodes inside it, although some editions and translations print them as sub-parvas of their own.
  - *Variant (Ganguli's divisions):* Ganguli's index uses only 13 headings. It folds everything from the pilgrimage to the python into one "Tirtha-yatra Parva" (G 80–180), and it calls the last section "Aranya Parva" (G 309–313).

Episode ranges, in narrative order:
- **Āraṇyaka** — departure, Sūrya's boon, Vidura's banishment and recall, Maitreya (CE 3.1–11; G 1–10).
- **Kirmīravadha** — Bhīma kills Kirmīra (CE 3.12; G 11).
- **Arjunābhigamana** *(a vulgate heading)* — Kṛṣṇa's visit and the Śālva story; the move to Dvaitavana; the debates on anger, fate and effort; Vyāsa's counsel (CE 3.13–37; G 12–37).
- **Kairāta** — Arjuna's tapas and the fight with Śiva-as-Kirāta (CE 3.38–41; G 38–41).
- **Indralokābhigamana** — the Lokapālas' weapons and Arjuna's stay in heaven (CE 3.42–49; G 41–51; Ganguli's own heading starts at G 42). It contains the **Nalopākhyāna** (CE 3.50–78; G 52–79).
- **Tīrthayātrā** — the pilgrimage catalogues and the circuit with Lomaśa (CE 3.79–153; G 80–155).
- **Jaṭāsuravadha** (CE 3.154; G 156) · **Yakṣayuddha**, with Arjuna's return and his account of the Nivātakavaca war (CE 3.155–172; G 157–174) · **Ājagara** (CE 3.173–178; G 175–180).
- **Mārkaṇḍeyasamasyā** (CE 3.179–221; G 181–230).
- **Draupadī–Satyabhāmā-saṃvāda** (CE 3.222–224; G 231–233).
- **Ghoṣayātrā** (CE 3.225–243; G 234–255) · **Mṛgasvapnabhaya** (CE 3.244; G 256) · **Vrīhidrauṇika** (CE 3.245–247; G 257–259).
- **Draupadīharaṇa** — the abduction itself (CE 3.248–256; G 262–270), the **Rāmopākhyāna** (CE 3.257–276; G 271–290) and Sāvitrī (CE 3.277–283; G 291–297).
- **Kuṇḍalāharaṇa** (CE 3.284–294; G 298–308) · **Āraṇeya** (CE 3.295–299; G 309–313).
- *Variant (vulgate vs critical):* the critical text drops a good deal that the vulgate carries. Among the losses are the Urvaśī episode, Karṇa's world-conquest, Durvāsas's visit to the Pāṇḍavas, and the most-quoted Yakṣa riddles. Each is flagged at its place below.

## Into Exile — Kāmyaka and the First Trials

- **Departure for the forest** — The citizens follow the exiles out of Hāstinapura. At the great banyan called Pramāṇa, on the Gaṅgā, Yudhiṣṭhira persuades them to turn back (CE 3.1.11–40; G 1). The priest **Dhaumya** goes with the brothers from the start. Travelling by way of Kurukṣetra, they reach the **Kāmyaka forest** on the bank of the Sarasvatī (CE 3.6.2–3; G 5).
- **Sūrya's boon (the "Akṣaya-pātra")** — On **Śaunaka**'s advice and with a hymn taught by Dhaumya, Yudhiṣṭhira worships **Sūrya**. The god promises food for twelve years: four kinds of food that will never run out (CE 3.4.2–3; G 3).
  - *Variant (vulgate):* in the vulgate, Sūrya also gives a **copper vessel** (*piṭharaṃ tāmram*), and the food lasts as long as Pāñcālī has not yet eaten from it. This is the familiar *akṣaya-pātra*. The CE prints these lines only as a starred passage (CE *0021, after 3.4.3). Ganguli translates them (G 3).
- **Vidura sent away and recalled** — Dhṛtarāṣṭra rejects Vidura's advice to make peace and tells him to go or stay as he likes (CE 3.5.19). Vidura goes to the Pāṇḍavas in Kāmyaka. The remorseful king then sends Sañjaya to bring him back (CE 3.5–7; G 4–6).
- **Maitreya's curse on Duryodhana** — The sage **Maitreya** urges Duryodhana to make peace with the Pāṇḍavas. Duryodhana slaps his thigh, smiles and scratches the ground with his foot in contempt, and Maitreya curses him: **Bhīma will break that thigh** with blows of his mace in the war (CE 3.11.28–34; G 10). The curse echoes Bhīma's vow at the dice-game.
- **Kirmīra-vadha** — Dhṛtarāṣṭra asks, and Vidura tells, how **Kirmīra** blocked the exiles' road on their way to Kāmyaka. Kirmīra is the **brother of Baka** and a **friend (*sakhā*) of Hiḍimba**, both of whom Bhīma had killed. Bhīma fights him with uprooted trees, then strangles him "as one kills a sacrificial beast" (CE 3.12.22–35, 3.12.39–64; G 11).
- **Kṛṣṇa's visit and Draupadī's lament** — The Bhojas, Vṛṣṇis and Pāñcālas come to the forest. Draupadī recites her humiliation in the hall, and **Kṛṣṇa** vows that the wives of those she is angry with will weep in the same way. She will be a queen of kings again, he says, and his word will not fail even if the ocean dries up (CE 3.13.114–117; G 12). He explains that he was absent from the dice-game because he was destroying **Śālva**'s flying city **Saubha** (CE 3.14–23; G 13–22).
- **Dvaitavana** — The exiles move to the Dvaitavana lake (CE 3.24–26; G 23–25), and the sage **Mārkaṇḍeya** pays a first visit (CE 3.26).
- **Draupadī vs Yudhiṣṭhira on anger, fate and effort** — Draupadī cites the old dialogue of **Prahlāda and Bali** on when to forgive (CE 3.29; G 28). She reproaches the Ordainer (CE 3.31; G 30). Yudhiṣṭhira calls her talk unbelief (*nāstikya*) and defends patience and forgiveness (CE 3.30, 3.32; G 29, 31). In reply she argues for human exertion (*pauruṣa*) (CE 3.33; G 32). Bhīma urges immediate war (CE 3.34–36; G 33–35). This is the parva's central ethical tension.
- **Vyāsa's counsel** — **Vyāsa** appears. He teaches Yudhiṣṭhira the *pratismṛti* knowledge, to be passed on to Arjuna so that he can win divine weapons (CE 3.37.20–35; G 36).

## Kairāta — Arjuna's Tapas and the Divine Weapons

- **The quest for celestial astras** — Yudhiṣṭhira passes Vyāsa's *pratismṛti* on to Arjuna, who departs alone for the Himālaya. At **Indrakīla** a matted-haired ascetic, **Indra** in disguise, promises him weapons once he has seen Śiva (CE 3.38; G 37).
- **The Kirāta duel** — Arjuna performs fierce tapas, standing on his toes with arms raised and living on air (CE 3.39.23). The demon **Mūka**, a son of Diti, attacks him in the form of a boar. Arjuna and **Śiva, disguised as a mountain hunter (Kirāta)**, shoot it at the same moment and quarrel over the kill (CE 3.40.7–14). Arjuna's arrows fail. The hunter **swallows the Gāṇḍīva bow**, and the two fight with fists until Arjuna falls senseless (CE 3.40.39–51; G 39).
  - *Variant (vulgate):* in the vulgate, Arjuna makes an earthen altar or clay image of Śiva and worships it with a garland, then sees the garland on the Kirāta's head and recognises the god (G 39, "a clay image"). The CE prints this only as a starred passage, *0171 (*mṛnmayaṃ sthaṇḍilaṃ kṛtvā mālyenāpūjayad bhavam*). Neither text calls the image a liṅga.
- **Grant of the Pāśupata** — Śiva reveals himself and tells Arjuna that in a former body he was **Nara**, Nārāyaṇa's companion at Badarī (CE 3.41.1). Arjuna asks for "the terrible Pāśupata, called Brahmaśiras". Śiva grants it and gives back the Gāṇḍīva (CE 3.41.7–25; G 40).
  - *Later retellings:* Bhāravi's classical mahākāvya **Kirātārjunīya** (6th century) elaborates this episode far beyond the epic [late text].
- **Gifts of the Lokapālas** — The world-guardians appear. **Yama** gives his staff (*daṇḍa*), **Varuṇa** his nooses (*pāśa*) and **Kubera** the weapon of vanishing (*antardhāna*). **Indra** tells Arjuna to ready himself for heaven, where Mātali's chariot will take him (CE 3.42.23–38; G 41).

## Indralokābhigamana — Arjuna in Heaven

- **Ascent to Amarāvatī** — **Mātali**, Indra's charioteer, carries Arjuna to **Indra's city**. At Indra's bidding he shares Indra's own seat (CE 3.44.20–27; G 42–43). Arjuna receives Indra's own **vajra** and the roaring thunderbolts (*aśani*) (CE 3.45.4; G 44).
- **Citrasena and music** — On Indra's instruction, the gandharva **Citrasena** teaches Arjuna **dance, song and instrumental music** (CE 3.45.6–8, 3.45.32; G 44). These skills will serve his disguise as the dancing-master Bṛhannaḍā in the [Virāṭa Parva](04-virata-parva.md).
- **Five years in heaven** — Arjuna stays in Indra's world for five years (CE 3.171.9). Meanwhile Lomaśa brings news of him to his brothers (CE 3.45, 3.89–90).
- **Urvaśī's curse** *[vulgate; CE App. I No. 6]* — Indra notices Arjuna gazing at the apsaras **Urvaśī** and sends her to him. Arjuna refuses her, honouring her as the mother of the Paurava line. Spurned, she curses him to live as a eunuch (*ṣaṇḍha*). Indra limits the curse to **one year**, to be served in the thirteenth year of exile (G 45–46; App. I No. 6, lines 149–152). The CE removes the whole episode to Appendix I No. 6, inserted after 3.45.8 (*apumān iti vikhyātaḥ ṣaṇḍhavad vicariṣyasi*).
- **Nivātakavaca and Hiraṇyapura** — As his teacher's fee (*gurvartha*), Indra asks Arjuna to destroy the **Nivātakavaca** dānavas, who live in the sea (CE 3.165.7–11). On the way back Arjuna also destroys **Hiraṇyapura**, a flying city that moves at will, held by the **Paulomas and Kālakeyas** (CE 3.170.2–66). Arjuna tells all this to his brothers after his return (CE 3.164–171; G 167–173).

## Nalopākhyāna — Nala and Damayantī

- **Frame** — The sage **Bṛhadaśva** visits, and Yudhiṣṭhira laments that no king was ever so unfortunate. Bṛhadaśva tells him of a king who lost his kingdom at dice and won it back (CE 3.49; G 52; the frame is announced at CE 1.2.108–109).
- **The wooing** — **Nala** of Niṣadha and **Damayantī** of Vidarbha fall in love by report. Gold-feathered **geese** (*haṃsa*) carry their messages (CE 3.50.18–24). The Lokapālas **Indra, Agni, Varuṇa and Yama** come to woo her and make Nala their messenger. At the **svayaṃvara** each takes Nala's form, but Damayantī tells the mortal from the gods and chooses Nala (CE 3.51–54; G 53–57).
- **Kali's possession and the dice** — **Kali**, the personified losing throw and age, arrives too late with **Dvāpara**, and the two vow revenge (CE 3.55). After twelve years Kali finds his opening: Nala does his twilight rite without washing his feet after urinating, and Kali enters him (CE 3.56.3). Nala gambles away everything to his brother **Puṣkara** (CE 3.56–58; G 59–61).
- **Abandonment** — Nala tries to catch some birds with his only cloth. The birds fly off with it, saying that they are the dice themselves (CE 3.58.13–16). The couple then share **Damayantī's single garment**. Nala cuts it in half while she sleeps and **abandons her** (CE 3.59.3–20; G 62). She escapes a python, and then a hunter who rescued her and tried to force her, whom she curses dead (CE 3.60–61.1; G 63–64). She joins a caravan bound for Cedi (CE 3.61.106–125), and wild elephants trample it in the night (CE 3.62.6–12; G 65). At **Cedi** she becomes the *sairandhrī* of the **queen mother**, who later turns out to be her mother's sister (CE 3.62.21–43, 3.66.12; G 65, 69).
- **The serpent and disguise** — Nala saves the nāga **Karkoṭaka** from a forest fire, and the nāga's bite deforms him. Under the name **Bāhuka** he becomes charioteer to King **Ṛtuparṇa** of Ayodhyā, who is master of dice and numbers. Karkoṭaka gives him a divine pair of garments that will restore his form (CE 3.63–64; G 66–67).
- **The exchange of sciences** — Damayantī's agents find Bāhuka. She announces a **second svayaṃvara** as a lure (CE 3.68.21–23). Ṛtuparṇa races to Vidarbha behind Bāhuka's horses. On the way he gives Bāhuka the **heart of dice** (*akṣahṛdaya*) and takes the **heart of horses** (*aśvahṛdaya*) as a deposit to be taught later. At that moment **Kali leaves Nala's body**, vomiting Karkoṭaka's venom, and begs not to be cursed (CE 3.70.21–38; G 72).
- **Reunion** — Damayantī tests Bāhuka through her maid Keśinī (CE 3.72–74). Nala puts on the serpent's garment, regains his form and is reunited with her (CE 3.75–76). With his new mastery of dice he wins his kingdom back from Puṣkara. He spares Puṣkara and keeps him as a brother (CE 3.77.18–26; G 78–79).

## Tīrtha-yātrā — The Great Pilgrimage

- **Nārada's catalogue (Pulastya's teaching)** — **Nārada** arrives (CE 3.80.2). He repeats the long tīrtha-catalogue that the sage **Pulastya** once gave to **Bhīṣma**, covering Puṣkara, Kurukṣetra, Prayāga and the rest (CE 3.80.11–3.83; G 81–85). It is an early [sacred-geography](../../06-sacred-geography/) gazetteer.
- **Dhaumya's catalogue by quarters** — Then, at Yudhiṣṭhira's request, **Dhaumya** gives his own list of holy places in the east, south, west and north (CE 3.84–88; G 86–90). The two catalogues are separate recitations, not alternatives.
- **Pilgrimage with Lomaśa** — The sage **Lomaśa** arrives from Indra's world with messages from Indra and Arjuna. He guides the brothers on their circuit while Arjuna is away (CE 3.89–90; G 91–92).
- **Agastya** — Lomaśa tells how **Agastya** fashioned **Lopāmudrā** and married her (CE 3.94–95). He tells how Agastya digested the demon **Vātāpi**, whom his brother **Ilvala** served up as meat (CE 3.94, 3.97; G 96–99). Then come the slaying of **Vṛtra** (CE 3.98–99; G 100–101), the **Kāleyas** hiding in the sea and Agastya **drinking the ocean** to expose them (CE 3.100–103; G 102–105), and the check on the **Vindhya** mountain (CE 3.102; G 104).
- **Sagara and the descent of Gaṅgā** — King Sagara wins **sixty thousand sons** (CE 3.104.14), who are burnt to ashes by the sage Kapila. His descendant **Bhagīratha**, by tapas, brings the **Gaṅgā** down, with Śiva bearing her fall, to purify them (CE 3.104–108; G 106–109).
- **Ṛśyaśṛṅga** — The horned, chaste son of the sage Vibhāṇḍaka has never seen a woman. A courtesan sent by King **Lomapāda of Aṅga** lures him to the kingdom, and rain returns to the drought-struck land. He marries the princess **Śāntā** (CE 3.110–113; G 110–113).
- **Tales along the route** — **Paraśurāma** and Kārtavīrya, told at Mount Mahendra (CE 3.115–117; G 115–117). A meeting with the Vṛṣṇis at Prabhāsa (CE 3.118–120). **Cyavana and Sukanyā**, and the Aśvins' share of soma (CE 3.122–125; G 122–125). **Māndhātṛ** (CE 3.126; G 126). **Somaka and his son Jantu** (CE 3.127–128; G 127–128). The hawk (Indra) and the dove (Agni) test King **Uśīnara** (CE 3.130.17–3.131; G 130–131). The Parvasaṃgraha names the king as Śibi (CE 1.2.115). **Aṣṭāvakra**'s debate with Bandin (CE 3.132–134; G 132–134). **Yavakrīta** (CE 3.135–139; G 135–138). The pilgrims also pass the **Ṛṣabha peak** (CE 3.109; G 110).
- **Ghaṭotkaca carries them** — Draupadī collapses on the mountain road. At Yudhiṣṭhira's request Bhīma summons his son **Ghaṭotkaca**, whose rākṣasas carry the party to Badarī (CE 3.144–145; G 143–144).
- **Saugandhika quest — Bhīma and Hanumān** — Draupadī asks Bhīma for more of the fragrant **saugandhika** flowers. On the way he finds an old monkey lying across the path, whose **tail he cannot lift**. It is his elder brother **Hanumān**, like him a son of Vāyu (CE 3.146–147; G 145–147). Hanumān tells him of Rāma and the yugas. At Bhīma's insistence he shows **the form he took to leap the ocean** (CE 3.149.2; G 149). He promises to roar from **Arjuna's banner** in battle (CE 3.150.15; G 150).
- **The lotus pool** — Bhīma takes the flowers from **Kubera's pool**. He fights the rākṣasas called **Krodhavaśas** who guard it (CE 3.151.10, 3.152–153; G 152–154), and his brothers join him there.

## Himālayan Episodes — Jaṭāsura, the Yakṣa, and Nahuṣa

- **Jaṭāsura-vadha** — The rākṣasa **Jaṭāsura**, disguised as a brāhmaṇa, carries off Yudhiṣṭhira, the twins and Draupadī while Bhīma is out hunting. **Bhīma** returns and kills him (CE 3.154; G 156).
- **Ārṣṭiṣeṇa's hermitage** — The brothers wait for Arjuna at the hermitage of the royal sage **Ārṣṭiṣeṇa** on Gandhamādana (CE 3.155–156; G 157–158).
- **Battle with the Yakṣas** — On Gandhamādana, Bhīma slaughters Kubera's yakṣas and rākṣasas and kills their leader **Maṇimat**, Kubera's friend (CE 3.157.52–69; G 159). **Kubera** comes, and instead of punishing Bhīma he thanks him. Agastya had once cursed Kubera, and Maṇimat with him, and the curse would end when a man killed Maṇimat (CE 3.158.46–59; G 160).
- **Arjuna's return** — After **five years** in Indra's world (CE 3.171.9), Arjuna comes down in Mātali's chariot to rejoin his family on Gandhamādana (CE 3.161–163; G 163–166). He recounts the Kirāta fight, his stay in heaven, and the Nivātakavaca and Hiraṇyapura wars (CE 3.164–171; G 167–173).
- **Ājagara — Nahuṣa the python** — A huge python seizes **Bhīma** (CE 3.175–176; G 177–178). It is King **Nahuṣa**. While ruling heaven in Indra's place he grew arrogant and had a thousand brahmin seers carry his palanquin. When he **touched Agastya with his foot**, the sage cursed him to become a serpent (CE 3.177.6–10, 3.178.36–37). Yudhiṣṭhira's answers on who is a true brāhmaṇa win Bhīma's release and end Nahuṣa's curse. Yudhiṣṭhira argues that brahminhood lies in conduct, not birth (CE 3.177.16–30; G 179–180).

## Mārkaṇḍeya-samāsyā — Cosmology and Moral Tales

Back in Kāmyaka, **Kṛṣṇa** arrives with **Satyabhāmā**, and the very long-lived sage **Mārkaṇḍeya** discourses at length (CE 3.180–181; G 182). This block is the parva's encyclopedic heart.
- **The Fish (Matsya) and Manu** — **Manu Vaivasvata** rescues a tiny fish, which grows until only the ocean can hold it. It warns him of the flood and tells him to build a boat and board it with the Seven Ṛṣis and all seeds. It appears **horned** and tows the boat to the highest Himālayan peak, which is thereafter called **Naubandhana**, "the binding of the boat" (CE 3.185.29–47; G 186). The fish then reveals itself: **"I am Prajāpati Brahmā"** (*ahaṃ prajāpatir brahmā*, CE 3.185.48; Ganguli: "I am Brahma, the Lord of all creatures"). This is one of the oldest Indian flood narratives.
  - *Variant (later Purāṇic):* the Purāṇas make the fish **Viṣṇu's first avatāra** ([Daśāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)). The Āraṇyaka's own fish is Brahmā.
- **The vision of pralaya** — Mārkaṇḍeya describes wandering in the shoreless flood after a dissolution of the world. There he sees a great **banyan** (*nyagrodha*), and **on one of its branches, on a divine couch, a child** (CE 3.186.81–83; G 187). The child tells him to enter his body. Mārkaṇḍeya goes in through the child's mouth and sees the whole universe inside (CE 3.186.87–114). The child names himself **Nārāyaṇa** (CE 3.187.3–4; G 188). Mārkaṇḍeya then tells the Pāṇḍavas that this god is **the Kṛṣṇa sitting beside them**, their kinsman Janārdana (CE 3.187.50–52). This is a foundational [pralaya](../../00-time-and-cosmology/kalpa-and-pralaya.md) image.
  - *Variant (later Purāṇic):* the child lying **on a leaf**, the Vaṭapatraśāyī or Bāla-Mukunda of devotion, belongs to later texts. For example, the Bhāgavata has him "lying in a cup of a leaf" (*śayānaṃ parṇa-puṭake*, BhP 12.9.21) and sucking his toe (12.9.25) [late text]. The epic has a branch and a couch.
- **The yugas and the end of the age** — Mārkaṇḍeya foretells the decay of **[Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)**. At its end, he says, a brahmin named **Kalki Viṣṇuyaśas** will be born, destroy the barbarians, and give the earth to the brāhmaṇas at a horse-sacrifice (CE 3.188.89, 3.189.1–5; G 189–190). See [Daśāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **The frog-king's daughter** — **Parikṣit** of Ayodhyā marries a beautiful woman he finds by a lake. She turns out to be the daughter of **Āyu, king of the frogs**. Their son's theft of **Vāmadeva**'s horses follows (CE 3.190, in prose; G 191).
- **Indradyumna** — The royal sage **Indradyumna** has fallen from heaven because no one on earth remembers him. Mārkaṇḍeya does not know him, and neither does the owl **Prākārakarṇa** (Ganguli: Pravarakarna) or the crane **Nāḍījaṅgha** of Lake Indradyumna. The tortoise **Akūpāra** does remember him, and the king is restored to heaven (CE 3.191, in prose; G 198). Ganguli's next section (G 199), a discourse on gifts, has no counterpart in the CE's constituted text.
- **Dhundhumāra** — **Kuvalāśva**, prompted by the sage **Uttaṅka**, kills the demon **Dhundhu** in the sands (CE 3.192–195; G 200–203).
- **Kauśika, the faithful wife, and the righteous butcher** — The brahmin **Kauśika** kills a **crane** with an angry glance when its droppings fall on him; the housewife later calls the crane "burnt" (CE 3.197.3–5, 3.197.30). Next a housewife keeps him waiting while she serves her husband. She knows about the crane, and she sends him to learn dharma from a **butcher (Dharmavyādha) in Mithilā** (CE 3.197; G 205). The butcher teaches him that one's own duty done without pride is the highest dharma; this is the **Vyādha-gītā** (CE 3.198–206; G 206–215).
- **The fires and the birth of Skanda (Kārttikeya)** — A genealogy of the fires (CE 3.207–212; G 216–221) leads to the birth of **Skanda**. Agni desires the wives of the Seven Ṛṣis. **Svāhā** takes the forms of six of them; she cannot take Arundhatī's. Six times she casts Agni's seed into a golden vessel on the **White Mountain** (CE 3.213–214). The ṛṣis then put away the six wives, who become the **Kṛttikās** (CE 3.215.5, 3.219.11). Skanda is also called a son of Rudra (CE 3.218.27–30). He becomes general of the gods, marries **Devasenā** (CE 3.218.47), and kills the dānava **Mahiṣa** with his spear (CE 3.221.52–66; G 222–230).
  - *Variant (other tellings):* in the Purāṇic and Kālidāsa tradition, Skanda is born to kill **Tāraka** (cf. the Śiva myth-cycle, [deities](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md)). The Āraṇyaka's account names Mahiṣa as his foe, and Tāraka does not appear in this book.
- **Other discourses** — The greatness of brāhmaṇas, with King **Vainya** (CE 3.182–183), and the dialogue of Sarasvatī and Tārkṣya (CE 3.184) appear here. The praise of *satya*, *dāna* and wifely fidelity runs through the whole session.
  - *Variant (vulgate):* Ganguli's text has six more tales in this block (G 192–197). They are about the long-lived Baka Dālbhya, Suhotra, Yayāti, Vṛṣadarbha and Seduka, Śibi tested by Agni and Indra, and Aṣṭaka with his brothers. The CE removes all of them to App. I No. 21 (after 3.190.82).

## Draupadī–Satyabhāmā and the Ghoṣa-yātrā

- **Draupadī–Satyabhāmā dialogue** — Kṛṣṇa's queen **Satyabhāmā** asks Draupadī what vow, spell or drug keeps her five husbands devoted. Draupadī rejects spells and drugs: a husband who learns that his wife uses them fears her (CE 3.222.6–12). Her answer is attentive service, and the stewardship of a vast household, whose income and outlay she alone kept (CE 3.222.48–56; G 231). She then advises Satyabhāmā on winning Kṛṣṇa's heart (CE 3.223; G 232). This is a much-cited and much-debated text on wifely conduct.
- **Ghoṣa-yātrā (the cattle expedition)** — **Karṇa** and **Śakuni** urge Duryodhana to flaunt his fortune before the exiles at Dvaitavana, using an inspection of the royal herds as a pretext (CE 3.226–229; G 235–238). Duryodhana's men quarrel with the gandharvas over the lake. The **gandharva Citrasena** routs Karṇa and **takes Duryodhana alive** (*jīvagrāham*, CE 3.231.6; G 240).
- **The Pāṇḍavas rescue their enemy** — Bhīma gloats, but Yudhiṣṭhira orders his brothers to free the Kauravas (CE 3.232–233; G 241–242). Arjuna, Citrasena's friend from heaven, wins Duryodhana's release (CE 3.234–235; G 243–244).
- **Duryodhana's fast and the dānavas** — Shamed, Duryodhana resolves to fast to death (*prāyopaveśa*). The dānavas of the underworld have a **kṛtyā** (a conjured being) carry him to them by night. They tell him that other asuras will enter Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Kṛpa and the rest, and that the soul of the slain Naraka has entered Karṇa. Then they send him back (CE 3.236–240, 3.239.18–26, 3.240.11–32; G 245–250).
- **The Vaiṣṇava sacrifice and Karṇa's vow** — Duryodhana cannot perform a Rājasūya while Yudhiṣṭhira lives, so he performs the equivalent **Vaiṣṇava** sacrifice instead (CE 3.241.19–32, 3.242; G 253–255). At its close **Karṇa** vows **not to have his feet washed until Arjuna is slain** (*pādau na dhāvaye tāvad yāvan na nihato 'rjunaḥ*, CE 3.243.15; G 255).
  - *Variant (vulgate):* in Ganguli, Karṇa also vows to abstain from meat and keep an "āsura vow" (G 255). The CE has these lines only as starred passage *1167. Before the sacrifice the vulgate also has Karṇa's **conquest of the four quarters** (*digvijaya*) (G 252–253), which the CE removes to App. I No. 24 (after 3.241.15).
- **The deer-dream (Mṛgasvapnabhaya)** — The surviving deer of Dvaitavana appear to Yudhiṣṭhira in a dream and beg the brothers to spare them. The Pāṇḍavas move back to Kāmyaka, near Lake Tṛṇabindu (CE 3.244; G 256).
- **Mudgala and the measure of grain (Vrīhidrauṇika)** — **Vyāsa** visits in the eleventh year (CE 3.245.1–10; G 257). He tells of the gleaner **Mudgala**, who gathers a *droṇa* of rice each fortnight. Mudgala gives it away even when **Durvāsas** comes six times and eats it all (CE 3.246; G 258). A messenger from the gods offers him heaven in his own body. He first asks what heaven is like, hears of its faults, and refuses it for the lasting goal of liberation (CE 3.246.29–3.247; G 258–259).
  - *Variant (vulgate):* Ganguli has a further tale here (G 260–261; his "Draupadi-harana Parva" heading begins partway through it, at G 261). Duryodhana sends **Durvāsas** and his disciples to demand a meal after Draupadī has eaten and the vessel is empty. **Kṛṣṇa** eats a single leaf of vegetable left in it, and the sages feel so full that they slip away. The CE removes this tale to App. I No. 25 (after 3.247.47). It is therefore not part of the critical text.

## Draupadī-haraṇa — The Abduction by Jayadratha

- **The approach** — While the brothers hunt, **Jayadratha**, king of Sindhu and Sauvīra and husband of the Kauravas' sister Duḥśalā, passes the hermitage. He sends his companion **Koṭikāśya** to ask who Draupadī is (CE 3.248–250; G 262–264).
- **The abduction** — Draupadī receives him as a guest and then rebuffs him. Jayadratha drags her onto his chariot. She salutes Dhaumya's feet, and the priest follows on foot among the soldiers (CE 3.251–252, 3.252.22–27; G 265–266).
- **Pursuit and capture** — The Pāṇḍavas overtake him, scatter his army and capture him (CE 3.253–256; G 267–270). Yudhiṣṭhira asks them to spare his life, remembering **Duḥśalā and Gāndhārī** (CE 3.255.43). **Arjuna** restrains Bhīma for the same reason (CE 3.256.6).
- **The humiliation** — With a crescent-headed arrow Bhīma shaves Jayadratha's head into **five tufts**. He spares him on condition that he declare himself the Pāṇḍavas' **slave** in every assembly (CE 3.256.9–11). Yudhiṣṭhira orders him freed, and Draupadī agrees (CE 3.256.15–21).
- **Śiva's boon** — Jayadratha does tapas at Gaṅgādvāra. He asks Śiva for the power to defeat all five Pāṇḍavas. Śiva refuses, and grants instead that he will **hold them back in battle, except Arjuna** (CE 3.256.25–28; G 270).
  - *Note (wording):* the CE's constituted text sets no limit of "one day". The limit is in starred passages *1227 (*dinaikam*, "for one day") and *1228 (*ekadā*, "once"), which belong to the vulgate; Ganguli renders it "(once)". The boon pays off on the day of Abhimanyu's death in the [Droṇa Parva](07-drona-parva.md).
  - *Variant (vulgate):* after this, Śiva gives Jayadratha a long account of Viṣṇu's avatāras (Varāha, Narasiṃha, Vāmana, Kṛṣṇa). The CE removes it to App. I No. 27 (after 3.256.28).

## Rāmopākhyāna — The Rāmāyaṇa Retold

- **Frame** — Yudhiṣṭhira asks whether any man was ever more unlucky than he. Mārkaṇḍeya answers with the story of **Rāma**, who also lost his kingdom and had his wife carried off (CE 3.257–258; G 271–272).
- **The compressed epic** — The telling begins with the descent of **Rāma**'s line and the birth of **Rāvaṇa** and his brothers from Viśravas, followed by their boons (CE 3.258–259; G 272–273). The gods prepare to be born as monkeys and bears (CE 3.260). Next come the exile, the abduction of **Sītā**, Jaṭāyu, the alliance with **Sugrīva** and **Hanumān**, and Nala's **bridge** across the sea (CE 3.267.41–45; G 281). The war follows, and Rāvaṇa dies (CE 3.274; G 288). See the dedicated [Rāmāyaṇa deep-dive](../ramayana/) for the full kāṇḍa-by-kāṇḍa account.
- **Sītā's vindication** — Rāma tells Sītā to go where she pleases (CE 3.275.10). She swears by the elements that she is innocent (CE 3.275.23–24). **Vāyu, Agni, Varuṇa** and **Brahmā** testify to her purity, and **Daśaratha** appears (CE 3.275.17–35; G 289). The critical text and Ganguli have no entry into fire; some manuscripts add one, in which Sītā has Lakṣmaṇa light a fire and circles it (App. I No. 28, after 3.275.16; not in Ganguli). The story ends with the return to Ayodhyā and the coronation (CE 3.275.60ff).
  - *Variant (vs Vālmīki):* this **Rāmopākhyāna** is much shorter than Vālmīki's poem. It gives Rāvaṇa's ancestry, material that Vālmīki holds in the Uttarakāṇḍa, but it ends before Sītā's banishment. Scholars have long disputed whether it depends on Vālmīki. Jacobi, Sukthankar (1939, 1941) and Raghavan argued that it is derived from the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa; Hopkins, Ludwig, Vaidya and van Buitenen (1975) dissented [scholarly]. The scholars and their positions are listed in notes 71–75 of the introduction to the Clay Sanskrit Library *Rāmāyaṇa* Book I. Its note 160 concludes that the Rāmopākhyāna is "later than and derived from the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa".

## Pativratā-māhātmya — Sāvitrī and Satyavān

- **Frame** — Yudhiṣṭhira asks whether any woman has ever been as devoted as Draupadī. **Mārkaṇḍeya** answers with the story of Sāvitrī (CE 3.277; G 291).
- **The fated choice** — **Sāvitrī**, daughter of King Aśvapati of Madra, chooses **Satyavān**, son of the blind and exiled king **Dyumatsena**. **Nārada** warns that Satyavān will die within a year, but she will not change her choice (CE 3.278.22–24; G 292).
- **The vigil** — As the day approaches, Sāvitrī keeps a **three-night vow**, standing day and night (CE 3.280.3; G 294). She goes with Satyavān into the forest. When he dies, **Yama** binds and draws out his soul, and she follows Yama (CE 3.281; G 295).
- **The five boons** — Yama, pleased by her words, grants a boon each time she speaks, always excepting Satyavān's life (CE 3.281.25–45). In order she asks:
  - sight and strength for her father-in-law (CE 3.281.26);
  - his lost kingdom (CE 3.281.31);
  - a hundred sons for her own father (CE 3.281.37);
  - a hundred sons for herself, **by Satyavān** (CE 3.281.44).

  Finally Yama offers a boon without that exception, and she asks **that Satyavān live**. A promise of sons by Satyavān cannot be kept without him (CE 3.281.50–53). Satyavān revives, and Dyumatsena regains his sight and kingdom (CE 3.282–283; G 296–297).

## Kuṇḍala-āharaṇa — Karṇa's Sacrifice

- **Sūrya's warning** — Lomaśa had brought word that Indra would deal with Yudhiṣṭhira's fear of Karṇa (CE 3.284.1). **Sūrya** then appears to Karṇa in a dream. He warns that **Indra**, Arjuna's father, will come in the guise of a brāhmaṇa to beg for his earrings, and he tells Karṇa not to give them. Karṇa refuses to break his vow of never refusing a brāhmaṇa who asks (CE 3.284.12–30, 3.285–286; G 298–300).
- **The backstory** — Here the book tells Karṇa's birth. Young **Kuntī** serves a fierce brāhmaṇa guest, who gives her a mantra that summons gods; he is unnamed in this book, and the Ādi Parva calls him Durvāsas. She calls **Sūrya** and bears a son already wearing the god's **armour (*kavaca*) and earrings (*kuṇḍala*)**. She casts him adrift, and the sūta **Adhiratha** and his wife Rādhā find and raise him (CE 3.287–293; G 301–307).
- **The exchange** — Indra comes as a brāhmaṇa, and Karṇa **cuts the armour and earrings from his own body**. In return he asks for Indra's **unfailing spear** (*amoghā śakti*), which he calls the *vāsavī* (CE 3.294.34). It will kill one enemy and then return to Indra; if Karṇa hurls it carelessly, it will fall on him. Indra also promises that Karṇa's body will not be disfigured (CE 3.294.20–36; G 308). Karṇa will be forced to spend the spear on Ghaṭotkaca rather than Arjuna in the [Droṇa Parva](07-drona-parva.md).
  - *Later in the epic:* in the [Udyoga Parva](05-udyoga-parva.md), Kṛṣṇa and then Kuntī reveal Karṇa's birth to him. He promises Kuntī to spare four of her sons and fight only Arjuna. This is a thread set up here, not a variant of this book.

## Āraṇeya — The Yakṣa Praśna (Yakṣa's Questions)

- **The lost fire-sticks** — A deer rubs against a tree, and a brāhmaṇa's fire-drill (*araṇi*) catches in its antlers. It runs off with it, and the Pāṇḍavas give chase until, exhausted and thirsty, they rest (CE 3.295.8–16; G 309).
- **The deadly lake** — Nakula climbs a tree, spots water and goes to fetch it. A voice forbids him to drink until he has answered its questions. One by one **Nakula, Sahadeva, Arjuna and Bhīma** ignore the warning, drink and **fall dead** (CE 3.296; G 310). The speaker first appears as a **crane** that lives on moss and fish (CE 3.297.11; G 311).
- **The riddle-test** — Yudhiṣṭhira answers the Yakṣa's catechism on the sun, the earth, dharma and right living (CE 3.297.26–61; G 311–312). One example: "What, when given up, makes one loved? Pride. What, given up, frees one from grief? Anger. What makes one rich? Desire. What makes one happy? Greed" (CE 3.297.56–57).
  - *Variant (vulgate):* the two most-quoted exchanges are in Ganguli (G 311) but are App. I No. 32 in the CE (after 3.297.61):
    - *"What is the greatest wonder?"* — "Day after day creatures go to Yama's abode, and those who remain want to live for ever" (*ahany ahani bhūtāni gacchantīha yamālayam / śeṣāḥ sthāvaram icchanti kim āścaryam ataḥ param*).
    - *"What is the path?"* — "Reasoning has no firm ground, the scriptures differ, the truth of dharma lies hidden in a cave: the path is the one the great have walked" (*mahājano yena gataḥ sa panthāḥ*).
- **The revival and the chosen brother** — The Yakṣa offers to revive **one** brother, and Yudhiṣṭhira chooses **Nakula**. He explains that Kuntī and Mādrī should both have a living son, and that he will not abandon non-cruelty (*ānṛśaṃsya*) (CE 3.297.66–73). Pleased, the Yakṣa revives all four (CE 3.298.1; G 312).
- **Dharma revealed** — The Yakṣa is **Dharma**, Yudhiṣṭhira's divine father, who says that Vidura too is a portion of himself (CE 3.298.10, 3.298.21). He took the fire-drill in deer form to test him, and returns it (CE 3.298.13, 3.298.20). His boons follow: no one in the three worlds will recognise the Pāṇḍavas while they live in disguise in **Virāṭa's city**, and Yudhiṣṭhira will master greed, delusion and anger (CE 3.298.17–23; G 312). The Pāṇḍavas then plan the thirteenth year with their companions (CE 3.299; G 313), which leads into the [Virāṭa Parva](04-virata-parva.md).
  - *Note (count of questions):* in the CE constituted text the questions run from 3.297.26 to 3.297.64. The Yakṣa speaks 25 times in the chapter, and many of his speeches hold several questions. Vulgate recensions add more (App. I No. 32 alone adds a long run of them), so popular counts of "the Yakṣa's questions" are not fixed by the text [scholarly].
