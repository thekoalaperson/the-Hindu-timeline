# Vana (Āraṇyaka) Parva — The Book of the Forest

> Cosmic address: the third book of the [Mahābhārata](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md), unfolding at the cusp of the [Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara; Kṛṣṇa is the 8th [Daśāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md). The Pāṇḍavas are of the [Candravaṃśa / lunar line](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
> Parent: [Mahābhārata deep-dive index](./README.md) · Part of [04-deep-dives](../README.md). Follows [02 Sabhā Parva](./02-sabha-parva.md); precedes [04 Virāṭa Parva](./04-virata-parva.md). Primary source: Vyāsa's Mahābhārata, Book 3.

The **Vana Parva** ("Book of the Forest"; also **Āraṇyaka Parva**) is the third and one of the longest of the eighteen *parvas*. It chronicles the **twelve years of forest exile** (*vanavāsa*) of the Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī — the penalty exacted after Yudhiṣṭhira lost everything at the rigged dice-game of the [Sabhā Parva](./02-sabha-parva.md) — a thirteenth year of disguise (*ajñātavāsa*) being kept for the next book. Outwardly a chronicle of wandering ascetics, the book is the epic's great reservoir of **embedded tales** (*upākhyāna*), told to console and instruct the brothers: it holds the *Mahābhārata*'s own miniature Rāmāyaṇa, the immortal love-stories of Nala–Damayantī and Sāvitrī–Satyavān, the cosmology of Mārkaṇḍeya, Arjuna's winning of the divine weapons, and the closing riddle-test of the Yakṣa. (Whole-book summary: Vyāsa, Vana Parva; critical edition: V.S. Sukthankar et al., BORI.)

## Structure — the Sub-Parvas

The vulgate (Calcutta/Bombay) divides the book into **21 *upaparvas* / ~324 adhyāyas**; the **BORI critical edition** reduces it to **16 sections / 299 adhyāyas (~10,239 ślokas)**, having pruned interpolations.
- *Variant (vulgate vs critical):* the vulgate's larger Mārkaṇḍeya and Tīrtha-yātrā blocks are heavily padded with material the critical edition marks as later accretion [scholarly] (Sukthankar, BORI Prolegomena).

- **Āraṇyaka P.** - the Pāṇḍavas reach the Kāmyaka forest; citizens follow them out of Hāstinapura (Vana 1–10).
- **Kirmīra-vadha P.** - Bhīma slays the rākṣasa Kirmīra (Vana 11).
- **Arjunābhigamana P.** - Kṛṣṇa and the Pāñcālas visit; counsel and lament (Vana 12–37).
- **Kairāta P.** - Arjuna's tapas and the duel with Śiva-as-Kirāta (Vana 38–41).
- **Indralokābhigamana P.** - Arjuna ascends to Indra's heaven (Vana 42–51).
- **Nalopākhyāna P.** - the tale of Nala and Damayantī (Vana 52–79).
- **Tīrtha-yātrā P.** - the great pilgrimage with Lomaśa (Vana 80–156).
- **Jaṭāsura-vadha, Yakṣa-yuddha, Nivātakavaca-yuddha, Ājagara** - Himālayan episodes and the serpent Nahuṣa (Vana 157–181).
- **Mārkaṇḍeya-samāsyā P.** - the sage Mārkaṇḍeya's cosmological discourses and tales (Vana 182–232).
- **Draupadī-Satyabhāmā-saṃvāda P.** - the queens' dialogue on wifely duty (Vana 233–234).
- **Ghoṣa-yātrā P.** - Duryodhana's cattle-expedition and capture by gandharvas (Vana 235–256).
- **Mṛgasvapnabhava, Vrīhidrauṇika** - the deer-dream and sage Mudgala (Vana 257–260).
- **Draupadī-haraṇa / Jayadratha-vimokṣaṇa P.** - Jayadratha abducts Draupadī (Vana 261–271).
- **Rāmopākhyāna P.** - the Rāmāyaṇa retold (Vana 272–291).
- **Pativratā-māhātmya P.** - Sāvitrī and Satyavān (Vana 292–298).
- **Kuṇḍala-āharaṇa P.** - Karṇa surrenders his armour to Indra (Vana 299–309).
- **Āraṇeya P.** - the Yakṣa Praśna at the lake (Vana 310–315; critical ed. closes here).

## Into Exile — Kāmyaka and the First Trials

- **Departure for the forest** - The Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī, clad in deerskins, leave Hāstinapura for the **Kāmyaka forest** on the Sarasvatī; grieving citizens follow until Yudhiṣṭhira turns them back; Vidura, then Dhaumya the priest, accompany them (Vana 1–10).
- **The Akṣaya-pātra (inexhaustible vessel)** - Yudhiṣṭhira worships **Sūrya**, who grants a copper vessel that yields unlimited food each day until Draupadī has eaten — so the exiles may feed visiting brāhmaṇas (Vana 3).
- **Maitreya's curse on Duryodhana** - The sage Maitreya warns Duryodhana to make peace; mocked, he curses that **Bhīma will smash Duryodhana's thigh** in war — sealing the Sabhā-Parva oath (Vana 10–11).
- **Kirmīra-vadha** - In the Kāmyaka woods the man-eating rākṣasa **Kirmīra** (brother of Baka, kin of Hiḍimba) attacks; **Bhīma** kills him bare-handed (Vana 11).
- **Kṛṣṇa's visit and Draupadī's lament** - Kṛṣṇa (absent during the dice-game, fighting Śālva) arrives and vows the Kauravas' destruction; Draupadī, hair still unbound, pleads for vengeance; debates on the slowness of dharma and Yudhiṣṭhira's forbearance (Vana 12–37).
- **Draupadī vs Yudhiṣṭhira on fate and effort** - Draupadī argues for human exertion (*pauruṣa*) and even rails against the Ordainer; Yudhiṣṭhira defends patience and non-anger; Bhīma urges immediate war — the parva's central ethical tension (Vana 28–36).

## Kairāta — Arjuna's Tapas and the Divine Weapons

- **The quest for celestial astras** - On Vyāsa's and Kṛṣṇa-Draupadī's counsel, Arjuna departs alone to win divine weapons for the coming war, journeying to the Himālaya and Mt. Indrakīla (Vana 37–38).
- **The Kirāta duel** - Performing fierce tapas, Arjuna and **Śiva — disguised as a mountain hunter (Kirāta)** — both shoot the same boar (the demon **Mūka** in pig form); they quarrel and fight; Arjuna's arrows are exhausted and his bow-blows fail against the hunter (Vana 38–40).
- **Grant of the Pāśupatāstra** - Recognising the god in his foe, Arjuna worships a clay liṅga and is honoured; Śiva reveals himself and bestows the **Pāśupata / Brahmaśiras** weapon, supreme of all astras (Vana 40–41).
- *Variant (later poetics):* this episode is the seed of Bhāravi's classical mahākāvya **Kirātārjunīya** [late text/scholarly], which elaborates the debate and combat far beyond the epic.
- **Gifts of the Lokapālas** - The world-guardians appear and arm Arjuna: **Yama** gives the *daṇḍa* (mace/staff weapon), **Varuṇa** the noose (*pāśa*), **Kubera** the *antardhāna* (vanishing) weapon, **Indra** invites him to heaven (Vana 41–42).

## Indralokābhigamana — Arjuna in Heaven

- **Ascent to Amarāvatī** - Mātali, Indra's charioteer, carries Arjuna to **Indra's heaven**; he is seated beside his father Indra and learns the *vajra* and other celestial weapons (Vana 42–46).
- **Citrasena and music** - The gandharva **Citrasena** teaches Arjuna song and dance — skills that will serve his disguise as eunuch-dancer Bṛhannaḍā in the Virāṭa Parva (Vana 44–45).
- **Urvaśī's curse** - The apsarā **Urvaśī** approaches Arjuna; he refuses her, honouring her as an ancestress of the Pauravas; spurned, she curses him to be a eunuch — Indra softens it so it lasts **only one year, of his choosing** (Vana 45–46).
- **Nivātakavaca and Hiraṇyapura** - As *guru-dakṣiṇā* to Indra, Arjuna destroys the **Nivātakavaca** dānavas of the sea and the flying demon-city **Hiraṇyapura** (the Paulomas and Kālakeyas), wielding the new weapons (Vana 168–173, recounted to his brothers on his return).

## Nalopākhyāna — Nala and Damayantī

- **Frame** - To console Yudhiṣṭhira — who has likewise lost a kingdom at dice — the sage **Bṛhadaśva** tells of a king who suffered the same and recovered all (Vana 52–53).
- **The wooing** - **Nala** of Niṣadha and **Damayantī** of Vidarbha fall in love by reputation; a golden goose (*haṃsa*) carries messages; even the gods Indra, Agni, Varuṇa, Yama woo her, but at her **svayaṃvara** she chooses the mortal Nala (Vana 53–57).
- **Kali's possession and the dice-fall** - The demon **Kali** (spirit of the age), angered, possesses Nala; Nala gambles away his kingdom to his brother **Puṣkara** and is exiled with Damayantī (Vana 58–59).
- **Abandonment** - Half-mad, Nala cuts away half his single garment and **abandons the sleeping Damayantī**; she wanders, survives a python and a caravan disaster, and is sheltered as a maid by the queen of Cedi (Vana 60–65).
- **The serpent and disguise** - Nala saves the nāga **Karkoṭaka**, whose bite transforms him into the ugly dwarf-charioteer **Bāhuka**; he serves King **Ṛtuparṇa** of Ayodhyā, master of the secret of dice and numbers (Vana 66–67).
- **Reunion** - Damayantī stages a second svayaṃvara as a lure; Ṛtuparṇa races there with Bāhuka, exchanging the **science of dice (akṣa-vidyā) for the science of horses (aśva-vidyā)**; Kali is expelled, Nala restored to form, the couple reunited and the kingdom won back at a return throw (Vana 68–79).

## Tīrtha-yātrā — The Great Pilgrimage

- **Nārada's catalogue** - The sage **Nārada** (or Dhaumya) recites the merits of India's holy fords (*tīrthas*) — Puṣkara, Prayāga, Gaṅgā, Yamunā, Gayā, Kurukṣetra, etc. — an early **[sacred-geography](../../06-sacred-geography/) gazetteer** (Vana 80–88).
- **Pilgrimage with Lomaśa** - The sage **Lomaśa** (sent by Indra at Arjuna's request) guides the brothers on a vast circuit of tīrthas while Arjuna is in heaven (Vana 89–156).
- **Agastya and Vātāpi** - Lomaśa tells how **Agastya** digested the demon **Vātāpi** (served as a meal by his brother Ilvala), drank the ocean dry to expose the Kālakeyas, and married **Lopāmudrā** (Vana 96–99).
- **Ṛśyaśṛṅga** - The tale of the horn-headed celibate boy-sage lured by a courtesan to bring rain to Aṅga and marry princess Śāntā (Vana 110–113).
- **Bhagīratha and the descent of Gaṅgā** - How Bhagīratha's tapas brought the **Gaṅgā** to earth to redeem the 60,000 ashen sons of Sagara — the Gaṅgāvataraṇa (Vana 104–109).
- **Ṛṣabha / Cyavana, Mandhātā, Jantu** - Cycle of royal and ascetic legends along the route (Vana 121–135).
- **Saugandhika quest — Bhīma and Hanumān** - Draupadī covets a celestial **saugandhika lotus**; Bhīma storms toward Kubera's lake and meets an aged monkey blocking the path whose tail he cannot lift — his half-brother **Hanumān** (both sons of Vāyu). Hanumān shows his **cosmic Setu-bandha form**, blesses Bhīma, and promises to roar on Arjuna's banner in battle (Vana 146–153).
- **Bhīma vs the yakṣas** - Bhīma seizes the lotuses from Kubera's pool, fights the **yakṣas/krodhavaśas**, and is forgiven by Kubera, who recalls a curse now lifted (Vana 152–155).

## Himālayan Episodes — Jaṭāsura, the Yakṣa, and Nahuṣa

- **Jaṭāsura-vadha** - The rākṣasa **Jaṭāsura**, disguised as a brāhmaṇa, abducts Yudhiṣṭhira, the twins, and Draupadī; **Bhīma** returns and slays him (Vana 157).
- **Battle with the Yakṣas** - At Kubera's domain Bhīma again clashes with yakṣa hordes before reconciliation (Vana 158–164).
- **Arjuna's return** - Arjuna rejoins his family on Mt. Gandhamādana after five years in heaven and recounts the Nivātakavaca war (Vana 165–175).
- **Ājagara — Nahuṣa the python** - A great serpent (the fallen king **Nahuṣa**, cursed from Indra's throne for arrogance) seizes **Bhīma**; he will release him only if Yudhiṣṭhira answers questions on dharma, the soul, and the true brāhmaṇa. Yudhiṣṭhira's answers free both Bhīma and Nahuṣa from the curse (Vana 176–181).

## Mārkaṇḍeya-samāsyā — Cosmology and Moral Tales

The deathless sage **Mārkaṇḍeya** — survivor of past dissolutions — discourses at length; this block is the parva's encyclopedic heart.
- **The vision of pralaya** - Mārkaṇḍeya describes surviving the cosmic dissolution and beholding **Viṣṇu as a divine infant asleep on a banyan leaf** upon the shoreless flood, entering the child's body to see the entire universe within — a foundational [pralaya](../../00-time-and-cosmology/kalpa-and-pralaya.md) image (Vana 186–188).
- *Variant (later Purāṇic):* the child on the leaf is identified as **Bāla-Mukunda / Vaṭapatraśāyī Kṛṣṇa** in later devotional texts [late text] (cf. Bhāgavata, Matsya traditions).
- **The yugas and the end of the age** - Mārkaṇḍeya foretells the degeneration of **[Kali Yuga](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** and the future advent of **Kalki**, the destroyer-restorer at the age's end (Vana 188–190) — see [Daśāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **The Fish (Matsya) and Manu** - The deluge story: **Manu** rears a tiny fish that grows into the giant **Matsya** ([1st avatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)), who tows his boat to safety through the flood (Vana 185) — one of the oldest Indian flood narratives.
- **Story of the pious crane / Dharma-vyādha** - The arrogant brāhmaṇa **Kauśika**, shamed by a faithful housewife and then taught by a virtuous **butcher (Dharma-vyādha)** of Mithilā, learns that duty done without pride — even a butcher's — is the highest dharma (the **Vyādha-gītā**) (Vana 205–216).
- **The birth of Skanda (Kārttikeya)** - Agni and the Kṛttikās rear the war-god **Skanda**, born to slay **Tāraka**; he becomes commander of the gods' army (Vana 213–221) — cf. the Śiva myth-cycle ([deities](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md)).
- **Indradyumna** - A king fallen from heaven seeks the oldest living being to prove his fame endures, finding only an immortal owl, crane, and tortoise — a parable on the impermanence of glory (Vana 191–199).
- **The frog-princess and other tales** - Cycle on truthfulness, fate, and the power of women's chastity (*pātivratya*), preparing the Sāvitrī tale (Vana 196–204).
- **Praise of the brāhmaṇa and of patient women** - Repeated exaltation of *satya*, *dāna*, and wifely fidelity threads the whole discourse.

## Draupadī–Satyabhāmā and the Ghoṣa-yātrā

- **Draupadī–Satyabhāmā dialogue** - Kṛṣṇa's queen **Satyabhāmā** asks Draupadī how she keeps five husbands devoted; Draupadī answers with a discourse on service and household dharma — a much-cited (and debated) text on wifely conduct (Vana 222–224).
- **Ghoṣa-yātrā (cattle expedition)** - Duryodhana, Karṇa, and Śakuni go to inspect cattle near the Pāṇḍavas' forest to flaunt their fortune; they quarrel with the **gandharva Citrasena**, who routs and **captures Duryodhana** (Vana 239–243).
- **The Pāṇḍavas rescue their enemy** - On Yudhiṣṭhira's order Bhīma and Arjuna free Duryodhana — a humiliation that drives Duryodhana to vow death-by-fasting (*prāyopaveśa*), from which the dānavas secretly recall him (Vana 243–251).
- **Vaiṣṇava-yajña / Karṇa's vow** - Stung, Karṇa vows not to wash his feet until Arjuna is slain and undertakes a conquest tour (Vana 252–254).
- **The deer-dream (Mṛgasvapna)** - Deer appear to Yudhiṣṭhira in a dream begging the brothers not to exhaust the game; they move back to Kāmyaka (Vana 257).
- **Mudgala and the measure of grain** - The ascetic **Mudgala**, who lived by gleaning, refuses even heaven when offered, choosing liberation over impermanent paradise (Vana 258–260).

## Draupadī-haraṇa — The Abduction by Jayadratha

- **The abduction** - While the brothers hunt, **Jayadratha** (king of Sindhu, the Kauravas' brother-in-law) carries off **Draupadī** from the hermitage; the sage Dhaumya follows the chariot (Vana 263–267).
- **Pursuit and capture** - The Pāṇḍavas overtake Jayadratha; Bhīma would kill him but Yudhiṣṭhira (and Draupadī, mindful of Duḥśalā) spares his life (Vana 268–271).
- **The humiliation** - Bhīma shaves Jayadratha's head leaving five tufts and makes him declare himself a slave; released, Jayadratha performs tapas and wins a boon from Śiva to **check four Pāṇḍavas (not Arjuna) one day** — the boon that enables Abhimanyu's death at Kurukṣetra (Vana 271–272).

## Rāmopākhyāna — The Rāmāyaṇa Retold

- **Frame** - To console Yudhiṣṭhira that even a god-king (Rāma) suffered loss of kingdom and abduction of his wife, **Mārkaṇḍeya** narrates the whole **Rāmāyaṇa** in brief (Vana 272–291).
- **The compressed epic** - Rāvaṇa's boon, the births of Rāma and brothers, exile, the abduction of **Sītā**, the alliance with **Sugrīva** and **Hanumān**, the **Setu** bridge, the slaying of Rāvaṇa, and the recovery of Sītā — see the dedicated [Rāmāyaṇa deep-dive](../ramayana/) for the full kāṇḍa-by-kāṇḍa account.
- *Variant (vs Vālmīki):* this **Rāmopākhyāna** is an independent, terser telling; it **omits the agni-parīkṣā controversies and the entire Uttara-kāṇḍa**, and scholars debate whether it draws on Vālmīki or a shared older tradition [scholarly] (cf. critical-edition studies).

## Pativratā-māhātmya — Sāvitrī and Satyavān

- **Frame** - When Yudhiṣṭhira asks if any woman equalled Draupadī, the sage **Mārkaṇḍeya** answers with Sāvitrī (Vana 291).
- **The fated choice** - Princess **Sāvitrī** chooses the exiled prince **Satyavān** though Nārada warns he is doomed to die in one year (Vana 293–294).
- **The vigil** - On the appointed day Sāvitrī keeps a three-day fast; when **Yama** comes and bears off Satyavān's soul, she follows on foot (Vana 296).
- **The four boons** - Yama, charmed by her wisdom and devotion, grants boons: her father-in-law's sight and kingdom restored, sons for her father, and — by a clever wording (a boon of *children for herself*) — she compels Yama to **return Satyavān to life** (Vana 297–298).

## Kuṇḍala-āharaṇa — Karṇa's Sacrifice

- **The backstory** - Karṇa was born wearing **natural golden armour (*kavaca*) and earrings (*kuṇḍala*)** from his father Sūrya, making him invulnerable (Vana 300–302).
- **Sūrya's warning** - In a dream Sūrya warns Karṇa that **Indra** (Arjuna's father) will come disguised as a brāhmaṇa to beg the armour; Karṇa, bound by his vow never to refuse a brāhmaṇa at his charity hour, resolves to give it anyway (Vana 302–308).
- **The exchange** - Indra begs and Karṇa **cuts the armour and earrings from his own body**; in compensation Indra gives the one-use infallible dart **Vāsavī Śakti / Ekāghnī** — which Karṇa will be forced to spend on Ghaṭotkaca rather than Arjuna (Vana 308–309).
- *Variant (Kuntī's plea):* in the later [Udyoga Parva](./05-udyoga-parva.md) Kuntī reveals she is Karṇa's mother and extracts his vow to spare four Pāṇḍavas — a thread set up here.

## Āraṇeya — The Yakṣa Praśna (Yakṣa's Questions)

- **The lost fire-sticks** - A deer carries off a brāhmaṇa's fire-churning sticks (*araṇi*); the Pāṇḍavas pursue it and, parched, come to a lake (Vana 311–312).
- **The deadly lake** - A **Yakṣa** forbids drinking until his questions are answered; one by one Nakula, Sahadeva, Arjuna, and Bhīma drink heedlessly and **fall dead** (Vana 312).
- **The riddle-test (Yakṣa Praśna)** - Yudhiṣṭhira answers the Yakṣa's catechism of riddles on dharma, the soul, and right living — including the famous exchange:
  - *"What is the greatest wonder?"* — "Day after day men die, yet the living believe themselves immortal." (Vana 313)
  - *"What is the path?"* — "Reason is no ground; scripture differs; truth is hidden in caves; the path is the road great souls have trodden (*mahājano yena gataḥ sa panthāḥ*)."
- **The revival and the chosen brother** - Pleased, the Yakṣa offers to revive **one** brother; Yudhiṣṭhira chooses **Nakula** (a son of Mādrī, so both wives keep a son) — proof of his impartial dharma; the Yakṣa revives all four (Vana 314).
- **Dharma revealed** - The Yakṣa is **Dharma**, Yudhiṣṭhira's own divine father (echoing Vidura), who grants a boon enabling their coming **disguise (ajñātavāsa)** to go undetected — the bridge to the [Virāṭa Parva](./04-virata-parva.md) (Vana 314–315).
- *Variant (count of questions):* traditions number the Yakṣa's questions variously (commonly ~124 in vulgate enumerations); the figure is not fixed in the critical text [scholarly].
