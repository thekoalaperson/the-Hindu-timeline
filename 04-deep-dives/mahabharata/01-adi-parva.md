# Ādi Parva — The Book of the Beginning

> Cosmic address: the Mahābhārata unfolds at the cusp of the [Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and the [Kali Yuga](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara; Kṛṣṇa is the 8th [Daśāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) of [Viṣṇu](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md). The Kurus belong to the [Candravaṃśa / lunar line](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
> Parent: [Mahābhārata deep-dive index](./README.md) · Part of [04-deep-dives](../README.md). Primary source: Vyāsa's [Mahābhārata](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md), Book 1.

The **Ādi Parva** ("Book of the Beginning") is the first of the eighteen *parvas* of the Mahābhārata — the longest single book, divided traditionally into **19 *upaparvas* (sub-parvas)**, spanning ~236 adhyāyas in the Calcutta/Bombay vulgate. It establishes the frame narration, the cosmic background, the genealogy of the [Kuru house](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md), the births of the Kaurava and Pāṇḍava cousins, their rivalry, the attempt to burn them in the lac house, their forest exile, the winning of Draupadī, and the founding of Indraprastha sealed by the burning of the Khāṇḍava forest. (Whole-book summary: Vyāsa, Ādi Parva; critical edition: V.S. Sukthankar, ed., BORI.)

## The Frame & The 19 Sub-Parvas

The book is told in nested frames: **Ugraśravas Sauti** narrates to the ṛṣis at the **Naimiṣa forest** the Mahābhārata that **Vaiśampāyana** earlier recited to King **Janamejaya** (great-grandson of Arjuna) during his snake sacrifice, which Vyāsa himself had composed (Vyāsa, Ādi 1).

- **Anukramaṇikā / Sangraha** - Sauti's prologue, the boast that "what is here is everywhere, what is not here is nowhere," and a chapter-by-chapter table of contents of all 18 parvas (Ādi 1–2).
- **Pauṣya, Pauloma** - Tales of sage Dhaumya's disciples, the Bhṛgu lineage, birth of Cyavana — framing the Bhārgava transmission (Ādi 3–12).
- **Āstīka** - The [Samudra Manthana](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/) backstory of Garuḍa and the serpents, and how the boy-sage **Āstīka** halts Janamejaya's *Sarpa Satra* (snake sacrifice), saving **Takṣaka** and the nāgas — the very rite at which the epic is recited (Ādi 13–58).
- **Aṃśāvataraṇa ("descent of portions")** - Devas and asuras incarnate on earth to relieve its burden; key heroes are declared portions of gods (Ādi 59–64).
- **Sambhava ("origins")** - The vast genealogy: Duṣyanta & Śakuntalā, **Bharata** (eponym of the dynasty), Yayāti, and the births of the Kaurava/Pāṇḍava generation (Ādi 65–142).
- **Jatugṛha (lac house), Hiḍimba-vadha, Baka-vadha** - The Vāraṇāvata plot and the forest exile (Ādi 143–166).
- **Caitraratha, Svayaṃvara, Vaivāhika** - Journey to Pāñcāla and the winning and marriage of Draupadī (Ādi 167–201).
- **Vidurāgamana–Rājyalābha, Arjuna-vanavāsa, Subhadrā-haraṇa, Haraṇāharaṇa** - Indraprastha founded; Arjuna's pilgrimage, marriages, and the carrying-off of Subhadrā (Ādi 202–223).
- **Khāṇḍava-dāha, Maya-darśana** - The burning of Khāṇḍava and Maya's offer to build the assembly hall (Ādi 224–236).

## Origins of the Kuru Line (Sambhava Parva)

- **Bharata and the dynasty's name** - From Duṣyanta and Śakuntalā is born **Bharata**, after whom the land (*Bhārata-varṣa*) and the *Mahā-bhārata* are named (Vyāsa, Ādi 68–74).
- **Yayāti and Pūru** - Yayāti's youngest son **Pūru** accepts his father's old age, founding the **Paurava** line from which the Kurus descend; the ancestor **Kuru** gives the clan its name (Ādi 75–95).
- **Śantanu and Gaṅgā** - King **Śantanu** of Hastināpura weds the river-goddess **Gaṅgā**, who drowns her first seven sons (the cursed **Vasus**) and surrenders the eighth, **Devavrata**, when Śantanu breaks his vow not to question her (Vyāsa, Ādi 96–100).
- **Bhīṣma's terrible vow** - Śantanu desires the fisher-maiden **Satyavatī**; her father demands her sons rule. Devavrata renounces the throne *and* vows lifelong celibacy so her line may inherit — earning the name **Bhīṣma** ("the terrible-oathed") and the boon of death-at-will (*icchā-mṛtyu*) (Vyāsa, Ādi 100).
- **Citrāṅgada & Vicitravīrya** - Satyavatī bears Śantanu two sons; **Citrāṅgada** dies young fighting a gandharva, **Vicitravīrya** becomes king (Ādi 101–102).
- **The abduction of the Kāśi princesses** - Bhīṣma seizes **Ambā, Ambikā, Ambālikā** at their svayaṃvara for Vicitravīrya. **Ambā**, in love with King Śālva, is released, rejected by him, and — vowing vengeance on Bhīṣma — reincarnates as **Śikhaṇḍin** (Ādi 102–103).
- **Niyoga and the three births** - Vicitravīrya dies heirless. Satyavatī summons her firstborn (by sage Parāśara before her marriage), the sage **Vyāsa**, to father children on the widows by *niyoga* (levirate):
  - **Dhṛtarāṣṭra** — born **blind** because Ambikā shut her eyes at Vyāsa's fearsome appearance (Ādi 105–106).
  - **Pāṇḍu** — born **pale/sickly** because Ambālikā turned pale (*pāṇḍu*) (Ādi 105–106).
  - **Vidura** — born wise and whole to a maidservant sent in the queen's place; he is an incarnation of **Dharma** (cursed by sage Māṇḍavya to be born a śūdra) (Ādi 106–107).

## Drona, Drupada, Karṇa & the Princes' Training

- **Kṛpa and Droṇa as teachers** - The princes are taught arms by **Kṛpācārya** and then the master **Droṇa**, born of a pot (*droṇa*) to sage Bharadvāja (Vyāsa, Ādi 121–131).
- **Droṇa vs Drupada** - Droṇa, humiliated by his boyhood friend **Drupada** (king of Pāñcāla), demands as *guru-dakṣiṇā* that his pupils defeat Drupada; Arjuna captures him, Droṇa takes half his kingdom — sowing Drupada's hunger for a son who will kill Droṇa (Ādi 128, 137–138).
- **Ekalavya's thumb** - The Niṣāda prince **Ekalavya**, self-taught before a clay image of Droṇa, surpasses Arjuna; Droṇa demands his right thumb as dakṣiṇā to preserve Arjuna's supremacy (Vyāsa, Ādi 123).
- **The graduation tournament** - At the arms-display Arjuna dazzles all; **Karṇa** appears, matches him, but is mocked as a charioteer's son. **Duryodhana** crowns Karṇa king of Aṅga, forging their lifelong alliance (Ādi 134–136).
- **Karṇa's secret birth [revealed later]** - Karṇa is actually Kuntī's firstborn by the sun-god **Sūrya** (via the **Durvāsas mantra**), set adrift on the river as an infant and raised by the charioteer **Adhiratha** — established here in seed, fully disclosed in later parvas (Vyāsa, Ādi 67, 111).

## Births of the Kaurava–Pāṇḍava Generation

- **Gāndhārī's vow** - **Gāndhārī**, wed to the blind Dhṛtarāṣṭra, blindfolds herself for life to share his condition (Vyāsa, Ādi 109–110).
- **The hundred Kauravas** - Gāndhārī's two-year pregnancy yields a lump of flesh; Vyāsa divides it into **101 pieces** incubated in ghee-jars, producing **Duryodhana** (born amid evil omens — jackals howl, and Vidura urges his abandonment), **Duḥśāsana**, and 98 more sons, plus one daughter **Duḥśalā** (Vyāsa, Ādi 114–115).
  - *Variant (number):* texts speak of "a hundred sons"; the daughter Duḥśalā makes **101 children**, the extra jar-piece. A son **Yuyutsu** is also born to Dhṛtarāṣṭra by a vaiśya serving-woman during Gāndhārī's pregnancy (Ādi 115).
- **Kuntī's boon** - As a girl **Kuntī (Pṛthā)** pleased sage **Durvāsas** and received a mantra to summon any deva to father a child; she tested it on **Sūrya** (begetting Karṇa) before marriage (Vyāsa, Ādi 111).
- **Pāṇḍu's curse** - Pāṇḍu, while hunting, kills the sage **Kindama** coupling in deer-form; the dying sage curses him to die if he ever embraces a wife — so Pāṇḍu cannot father children himself (Vyāsa, Ādi 118).
- **The three eldest Pāṇḍavas (by Kuntī)** - Using Durvāsas' boon at Pāṇḍu's request:
  - **Yudhiṣṭhira** — by **Dharma/Yama** (embodiment of righteousness) (Ādi 123).
  - **Bhīma** — by **Vāyu**, the wind-god (matchless strength; as an infant he crushes a rock when dropped) (Ādi 123).
  - **Arjuna** — by **Indra**, king of the gods (the supreme archer) (Ādi 123).
- **The twins (by Mādrī)** - Pāṇḍu's second wife **Mādrī** uses the mantra once to invoke the twin **Aśvins**, bearing **Nakula** and **Sahadeva** (Ādi 124).
- **Pāṇḍu's death & Mādrī's satī** - Overcome by desire in spring, Pāṇḍu embraces Mādrī and dies by the curse; Mādrī immolates herself, entrusting her twins to Kuntī. The five are brought to Hastināpura (Vyāsa, Ādi 125).
- **Childhood rivalry** - Bhīma's strength provokes the Kauravas; **Duryodhana poisons him** and throws him bound into the Gaṅgā, but he sinks to the **nāga realm**, where serpent-nectar gives him the strength of ten thousand elephants (Vyāsa, Ādi 119–120, 127).

## The House of Lac — Lākṣāgṛha (Jatugṛha Parva)

- **Kaṇika's counsel** - Fearing the popular Pāṇḍavas, Duryodhana wins his blind father's tacit consent; the brahmin-minister **Kaṇika (Kaṇinka)** advises pitiless, treacherous statecraft to destroy rivals (Vyāsa, Ādi 140).
- **Exile to Vāraṇāvata** - On the pretext of a festival, the Pāṇḍavas and Kuntī are sent to **Vāraṇāvata**, where Duryodhana's agent **Purocana** has built a mansion of **lac, resin, and combustibles** to be set ablaze (Vyāsa, Ādi 142–143).
- **Vidura's coded warning** - The far-seeing **Vidura** warns Yudhiṣṭhira in *mlecchā* (a secret tongue) that "the consumer of straw and wood" will strike; he sends a **miner** who digs a secret **escape tunnel** beneath the house (Vyāsa, Ādi 144–145).
- **The counter-plot and the fire** - The Pāṇḍavas feign ignorance for a year, then host a feast. A **Niṣāda (tribal) woman and her five sons**, drunk, fall asleep in the house; the Pāṇḍavas fire the mansion themselves (over Purocana, who also burns), escape through the tunnel, and flee into the forest (Vyāsa, Ādi 147–148).
  - *Variant (who lights it):* some retellings say **Bhīma** sets the fire; the critical text has the Pāṇḍavas burning Purocana within it as he sleeps.
  - *Note [scholarly]:* the six charred bodies make the Kauravas believe the Pāṇḍavas and their mother dead — a deception sustained until the svayaṃvara.
- **Vyāsa's guidance** - The sage **Vyāsa** meets the survivors and directs them to live disguised as brahmins in **Ekacakrā** at a brahmin's house (Ādi 148, 152).

## Forest Episodes: Hiḍimbā & Baka

- **Bhīma slays Hiḍimba** - In the woods the rākṣasa **Hiḍimba** sends his sister **Hiḍimbā** to lure the sleeping brothers; she falls in love with Bhīma instead, and Bhīma kills Hiḍimba in combat (Vyāsa, Ādi 154–155).
- **Birth of Ghaṭotkaca** - With Kuntī's and Yudhiṣṭhira's leave, Bhīma weds **Hiḍimbā**; she bears the mighty bald-headed rākṣasa son **Ghaṭotkaca**, who vows to come whenever needed (and later dies heroically in the war) (Vyāsa, Ādi 155).
- **The slaying of Baka** - At Ekacakrā the cannibal-demon **Bakāsura** extorts a cartload of food and a human victim daily; **Bhīma**, going in a brahmin's place, eats the food and kills Baka, freeing the town (Vyāsa, Ādi 159–166).
- **Birth of Draupadī foretold** - A brahmin guest tells of Drupada's fire-altar daughter; Vyāsa appears and directs the Pāṇḍavas toward Pāñcāla and Draupadī's svayaṃvara (Vyāsa, Ādi 166).

## Toward Pāñcāla (Caitraratha Parva)

- **The gandharva Citraratha** - Crossing the Gaṅgā at night, Arjuna fights and defeats the gandharva king **Aṅgāraparṇa (Citraratha)**, sparing him; in gratitude the gandharva teaches him the *cākṣuṣī* lore and gifts celestial horses (Vyāsa, Ādi 169–172).
- **The need for a priest & the tale of Tapatī, Vasiṣṭha, Aurva, Viśvāmitra** - The gandharva counsels the brotherhood to take a *purohita*; he recounts the lineage-tales of **Tapatī**, **Vasiṣṭha vs Viśvāmitra**, and **Aurva** — explaining why the Pāṇḍavas lack a guiding priest (Ādi 173–185). They engage **Dhaumya** as family priest (Ādi 182).

## Draupadī's Svayaṃvara (Svayaṃvara Parva)

- **The contest** - **Drupada**, hoping Arjuna lives, sets a near-impossible test: string a mighty bow and shoot a revolving fish-target (*matsya-yantra*) by looking only at its reflection in water below, to win **Draupadī (Kṛṣṇā / Pāñcālī)** (Vyāsa, Ādi 186–188).
- **The kings fail; Karṇa rebuffed** - Assembled monarchs cannot lift or string the bow. **Karṇa** rises to attempt it but is rejected by Draupadī as a *sūta* (charioteer's son) — a wound that fuels his hatred (Ādi 188–190).
  - *Variant (Karṇa's attempt):* in some recensions Karṇa actually strings and nearly shoots before Draupadī's refusal; the vulgate has her stop him beforehand. [textual variant]
- **Arjuna wins** - Disguised as a brahmin, **Arjuna** strings the bow and pierces the target; Draupadī garlands him. Kṛṣṇa, present with Balarāma, recognizes the Pāṇḍavas (Vyāsa, Ādi 188–190).
- **The brawl** - Outraged kings attack Drupada; **Bhīma uproots a tree** and Arjuna fights them off (notably **Karṇa** and **Śalya**) before the brahmins withdraw with their prize (Ādi 190–192).

## The Marriage & Polyandry (Vaivāhika Parva)

- **Kuntī's fateful words** - Returning, the brothers call out "we have brought alms"; Kuntī, unseeing, replies **"share it among yourselves"** — and her word, once spoken, must be honored (Vyāsa, Ādi 191, 196).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's ruling** - To keep their mother's word and avoid mutual envy, Yudhiṣṭhira declares Draupadī shall be **wife to all five brothers** (Ādi 196).
- **Drupada's objection & Vyāsa's resolution** - Drupada protests polyandry as *adharma*; **Vyāsa** sanctions it, revealing destinies (Vyāsa, Ādi 197–198):
  - *Justification (former life):* Draupadī was an ascetic who five times begged **Śiva** for a husband possessing all virtues; Śiva granted **five husbands** in her next birth (Ādi 197).
  - *Justification (divine identity):* she is an incarnation of **Śrī / Lakṣmī**, destined for the five Indras (the brothers being portions of Indra) — Vyāsa grants Drupada **divine sight** to see this (Ādi 197).
  - *Justification (the five former Indras):* a tale of five Indras confined in a cave by Śiva, reborn as the Pāṇḍavas, with Śrī as their shared consort (Ādi 197).
- **The wedding** - Draupadī marries the five in succession; by Vyāsa's grace she regains virginity before each. She bears each husband a son — the **Upapāṇḍavas (Draupadeyas)**: Prativindhya, Sutasoma, Śrutakarma, Śatānīka, Śrutasena (Ādi 198–199; sons named in later parvas).

## Indraprastha & Arjuna's Exile (Vidurāgamana / Arjuna-Vanavāsa)

- **The Pāṇḍavas revealed; partition of the kingdom** - News of the marriage reveals the Pāṇḍavas alive. Bhīṣma and Vidura counsel peace; Dhṛtarāṣṭra grants them the wild tract **Khāṇḍavaprastha**, which they transform into the splendid capital **Indraprastha** (Vyāsa, Ādi 199–207).
- **Nārada's rule for Draupadī** - To prevent strife over the shared wife, **Nārada** tells the tale of **Sunda and Upasunda** (asura brothers who destroyed each other over the apsarā Tilottamā) and sets a rule: any brother intruding on Draupadī with another shall accept **twelve years' exile** (Vyāsa, Ādi 204–212).
- **Arjuna's self-exile & marriages** - Arjuna breaks the rule (entering for his weapons to aid a brahmin) and exiles himself, undertaking a pilgrimage; he weds the nāga princess **Ulūpī** (mother of Irāvān) and the Maṇipura princess **Citrāṅgadā** (mother of Babhruvāhana) (Vyāsa, Ādi 213–219).
- **Abduction of Subhadrā** - At Dvārakā, with **Kṛṣṇa's** blessing, Arjuna carries off Kṛṣṇa's sister **Subhadrā**; Balarāma is angered but pacified, and the marriage is solemnized. Their son is **Abhimanyu** (Vyāsa, Ādi 220–223).

## The Burning of Khāṇḍava (Khāṇḍava-dāha Parva)

- **Agni's hunger** - The fire-god **Agni**, sickened by over-consuming oblations at King **Śvetaki's** twelve-year sacrifice, is told by Brahmā he can be cured only by devouring the **Khāṇḍava forest** with all its creatures — but **Indra** repeatedly quenches the flames to protect his friend the nāga **Takṣaka** who dwells there (Vyāsa, Ādi 224–225).
- **The divine pact** - Agni, disguised as a brahmin, begs **Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna** (resting by the Yamunā) to guard the forest's edges so nothing escapes. They agree, and Agni equips them (Vyāsa, Ādi 225–226):
  - **Arjuna** receives from Varuṇa (via Agni) the **Gāṇḍīva bow**, two inexhaustible quivers, and a monkey-bannered chariot (later the *Kapidhvaja*).
  - **Kṛṣṇa** receives the **Sudarśana cakra** and the **Kaumodakī mace**.
- **The conflagration** - The two heroes ring the blaze and slaughter every creature trying to flee; Arjuna even repels **Indra** and the host of gods who rain water and hurl mountains (Vyāsa, Ādi 226–227).
- **The survivors** - Of all Khāṇḍava only a handful escape (Vyāsa, Ādi 227–229):
  - **Takṣaka** himself is absent (at Kurukṣetra); his son **Aśvasena** is borne to safety by his mother (and bears a grudge resurfacing in the war).
  - The asura-architect **Maya Dānava** is spared by Arjuna at Kṛṣṇa's word; in gratitude he will build the magnificent **Maya-sabhā** (assembly hall) at Indraprastha.
  - The four young **Śārṅgaka birds**, sons of the sage **Mandapāla** and the bird **Jaritā**, survive by their father's foresight (a poignant inset on Mandapāla, Jaritā, and Lapitā) (Ādi 229–234).
- **Indra's blessing & the curse of Mandapāla's tale** - Indra, defeated, befriends Arjuna and promises him celestial weapons in time. Agni, satisfied, grants Arjuna boons. The parva (and book) closes with **Maya's offer** to serve the Pāṇḍavas — leading into the **Sabhā Parva** (Vyāsa, Ādi 234–236).
