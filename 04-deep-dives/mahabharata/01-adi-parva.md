# Ādi Parva — The Book of the Beginning

> Cosmic address: the Mahābhārata unfolds at the cusp of the [Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and the [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara; Kṛṣṇa is the 8th [Daśāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) of [Viṣṇu](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md). The Kurus belong to the [Candravaṃśa / lunar line](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
> Parent: [Mahābhārata deep-dive index](README.md) · Part of [04-deep-dives](../README.md). Primary source: Vyāsa's [Mahābhārata](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md), Book 1.

The **Ādi Parva** ("Book of the Beginning") is the first of the eighteen *parvas* of the Mahābhārata and one of its longest: **225 adhyāyas** in the BORI Critical Edition and **236 sections** in the vulgate as translated by Ganguli. (The longest books are the Śānti Parva, 353 CE adhyāyas, and the Āraṇyaka, 299.) The book's own table of contents counts 218 adhyāyas and 7,984 ślokas for it (CE 1.2.95–96). It establishes the frame narration, the cosmic background, the genealogy of the [Kuru house](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md), the births of the Kaurava and Pāṇḍava cousins, their rivalry, the attempt to burn them in the lac house, their forest exile, the winning of Draupadī, and the founding of Indraprastha, closing with the burning of the Khāṇḍava forest.

**How references work on this page:** "G 134" is a section of Ganguli's translation, which follows the Calcutta/Bombay vulgate. "CE 1.123.5" is adhyāya and verse in the BORI Critical Edition (V.S. Sukthankar, ed., 1933). The two numberings drift apart by as many as fifteen chapters (G 154 is CE 1.139). "CE App. I No. n" is a long passage that the critical editors found only in some manuscripts. They printed it in Appendix I, outside the constituted text. A "*" passage is a shorter insertion printed in the footnotes. Episodes that are only in the vulgate are flagged here because they are not part of the reconstructed text, even when they are famous.

## The Frame & The 19 Sub-Parvas

The book is told in nested frames. **Ugraśravas Sauti**, son of Lomaharṣaṇa, arrives at the **Naimiṣa forest** during **Śaunaka's twelve-year sacrifice** (CE 1.1.1). He recites to the ṛṣis the Mahābhārata that **Vaiśampāyana**, Vyāsa's pupil, had recited at Vyāsa's bidding to King **Janamejaya**, Arjuna's great-grandson, during Janamejaya's snake sacrifice (CE 1.54; G 60).

- *Variant (Gaṇeśa as scribe) [interpolation]:* in the vulgate, Brahmā visits Vyāsa and tells him to think of Gaṇeśa. Gaṇeśa agrees to write the poem down provided his pen never stops. Vyāsa counters that Gaṇeśa must understand each verse before writing it, and slips in knotty verses to buy time (G 1). The CE places the whole Brahmā–Gaṇeśa complex in App. I No. 1 (Sukthankar). Sukthankar calls the Gaṇeśa story "a very late interpolation in some inferior Devanāgarī manuscripts". He notes that it is entirely missing from the Southern manuscripts and that the Bengali version has "not the remotest trace" of the Brahmā–Gaṇeśa complex (*Prolegomena*).
- **The famous claim** "what is here may be found elsewhere; what is not here is nowhere" (*yad ihāsti tad anyatra yan nehāsti na tat kva cit*) is made about the four aims of life. In the critical text it is spoken by Vaiśampāyana as he begins the full recitation (CE 1.56.33; G 62). Some manuscripts also put it in Sauti's prologue; those occurrences are App. I Nos. 3 and 5 in the CE, and they are not in Ganguli's G 1–2. The line recurs at the close of the epic, in the Svargārohaṇa Parva.
- **The name.** The text offers its own pun: the poem is called *Mahā-bhārata* "because of its greatness (*mahattva*) and its weight (*bhāravattva*)" (CE 1.1.209; G 1).

The sub-parva list below follows Ganguli's vulgate divisions and section numbers. The *Parvasaṃgraha*, the epic's own table of contents in adhyāya 2, names **twenty** headings for this book, listing Mayadarśana separately (CE 1.2.34–39). The vulgate and the critical edition both work with **nineteen**, folding Mayadarśana into Khāṇḍavadāha.

- **Anukramaṇikā / Parvasaṃgraha** - Sauti's prologue: a summary of the whole story, and then the table of contents. The table lists the 100 sub-parvas, summarises each of the 18 books, and gives each book's adhyāya and śloka counts (G 1–2; CE 1.1–2).
- **Pauṣya** - King Janamejaya's brothers beat the dog-pup of **Saramā**, and she curses him. The sage **Āyoda Dhaumya** tests his three pupils: Āruṇi Pāñcālya, **Upamanyu** and Veda. Veda's pupil **[Uttaṅka](../uttanka.md)** fetches Queen Pauṣya's earrings as his teacher's fee, the nāga **Takṣaka** steals them, and Uttaṅka goes to Janamejaya to urge revenge on Takṣaka, the killer of his father Parikṣit (G 3; CE 1.3). The Parvasaṃgraha sums this book up as "the greatness of Uttaṅka" (CE 1.2.72).
- **Pauloma** - The Bhṛgu lineage. The demon Puloman carries off **Bhṛgu's** pregnant wife **Pulomā**. Her son **Cyavana** falls from her womb and his radiance burns the demon to ash. Bhṛgu curses **Agni**, who had identified her to Puloman, to devour everything. **[Ruru](../ruru-pramadvara.md)** gives half his own life to revive his bride Pramadvarā, then turns on snakes until a harmless *ḍuṇḍubha* snake (a sage under a curse) teaches him non-violence (G 4–12; CE 1.4–12).
- **[Āstīka](../astika-sarpa-satra.md)** - The serpent and bird mothers **Kadrū** and **Vinatā** bet on the colour of the horse **Uccaiḥśravas**. The horse came out of the [churning of the ocean](../samudra-manthana.md), which is narrated here. Kadrū cheats, Vinatā is enslaved, and her son **Garuḍa** steals the *amṛta* from the gods to free her. The ascetic **Jaratkāru** fathers **Āstīka** on the nāga **Vāsuki's** sister. Parikṣit dies of Takṣaka's bite under Śṛṅgin's curse, and Janamejaya holds a *sarpa-satra* to burn all snakes. Āstīka halts it and saves Takṣaka, who is left hanging in mid-air (G 13–58; CE 1.13–53).
- **Ādivaṃśāvataraṇa** ("account of the first lineage"; the Parvasaṃgraha's summary mentions the *aṃśāvataraṇa*, the descent of divine portions, under the Sambhava heading, CE 1.2.75–76) - Vyāsa arrives at the sacrifice; Vaiśampāyana gives a summary of the whole war-story. Devas and asuras take birth on earth to relieve its burden, and each hero is declared a portion of some god (G 59–64; CE 1.54–61).
- **Sambhava ("origins")** - The vast genealogy: Duṣyanta and Śakuntalā, **Bharata**, Yayāti, Śantanu and Bhīṣma, and the births and training of the Kaurava–Pāṇḍava generation (G 65–142).
- **Jatugṛha (lac house), Hiḍimba-vadha, Baka-vadha** - The Vāraṇāvata plot, the escape, and the forest years in disguise (G 143–166).
- **Caitraratha, Svayaṃvara, Vaivāhika** - The journey to Pāñcāla and the winning and marriage of Draupadī (G 167–201).
- **Vidurāgamana, Rājyalābha, Arjuna-vanavāsa, Subhadrā-haraṇa, Haraṇāharaṇa** - Indraprastha is founded; Arjuna's twelve-year exile, his marriages, and the carrying-off of Subhadrā (G 202–223).
- **Khāṇḍava-dāha (with Maya-darśana)** - The burning of Khāṇḍava, and the sparing of the asura Maya, who will build the assembly hall in the next book (G 224–236; CE 1.214–225).

## Origins of the Kuru Line (Sambhava Parva)

- **Bharata and the dynasty's name** - The king **Duṣyanta** (CE spelling *Duḥṣanta*; Ganguli *Dushmanta*) marries **[Śakuntalā](../shakuntala-bharata.md)**, the daughter of Viśvāmitra and the apsaras Menakā, in Kaṇva's hermitage. He later disowns her and their son in open court, until a voice from the sky confirms the boy is his (G 68–74; CE 1.62–69). The boy is **Bharata**. "From Bharata comes the Bhārata fame, and this Bhārata lineage" (CE 1.69.49), so the Kurus are *Bhāratas* after him.
  - *Variant (the land's name):* the Bhāgavata Purāṇa names **Bhārata-varṣa** after a different Bharata, the eldest son of Ṛṣabha (BhP 5.4.9). This Ādi verse speaks of the clan, not the land.
- **Yayāti and Pūru** - **[Yayāti](../yayati.md)** marries **Devayānī**, daughter of the asura-preceptor Śukra, whose love had been refused by **[Kaca](../characters/kacha-devayani.md)**. He also secretly takes the princess **Śarmiṣṭhā**. Śukra's curse makes him old. Only the youngest son, **Pūru**, takes on his father's old age, and he inherits the kingdom, founding the **Paurava** line from which the Kurus descend (G 75–95; CE 1.70–90). Further down that line, Saṃvaraṇa and Tapatī's son **Kuru** gives his name to Kurujāṅgala and makes Kurukṣetra holy (CE 1.89.42–43).
- **Śantanu and Gaṅgā** - The king **Mahābhiṣa** is reborn as **Śantanu** for having stared at Gaṅgā in Brahmā's court (G 96; CE 1.91). Śantanu weds the river-goddess **Gaṅgā** on condition that he never questions her. She drowns their first seven sons, and when he protests at the eighth, she leaves, taking the child with her. The sons are the eight **Vasus**, cursed by Vasiṣṭha (*Āpava*) for stealing his cow; seven are freed at birth, and the eighth, the one who stole the cow, must live a long human life (G 97–99; CE 1.92–93). Gaṅgā later returns the grown boy, **Devavrata**.
- **Bhīṣma's terrible vow** - Śantanu desires the fisher-maiden **[Satyavatī](../characters/satyavati.md)**, whose father, the fisher-chief (*dāśa*), demands that her sons rule. Devavrata renounces the throne *and* vows lifelong celibacy so that no son of his can contest it. He is hailed as **Bhīṣma** ("the terrible"), and Śantanu grants him the boon of dying only when he chooses (*svacchanda-maraṇa*, CE 1.94.94) (G 100; CE 1.94).
- **Citrāṅgada & Vicitravīrya** - Satyavatī bears Śantanu two sons. **Citrāṅgada** is killed in a three-year duel by a gandharva of the same name, and **Vicitravīrya** becomes king (G 101; CE 1.95).
- **The abduction of the Kāśi princesses** - Bhīṣma seizes **Ambā, Ambikā, Ambālikā** at their svayaṃvara for Vicitravīrya, and defeats King **Śālva** when Śālva pursues him. **Ambā** says she had already chosen Śālva in her heart, and Bhīṣma lets her go to him (G 102; CE 1.96.25–51). Vicitravīrya dies childless of consumption (G 102; CE 1.96).
  - *Later in the epic:* Śālva's rejection of Ambā, her austerities against Bhīṣma and her rebirth as **Śikhaṇḍin** are told by Bhīṣma in the Udyoga Parva (the *Ambopākhyāna*), not in the Ādi. See [Ambā–Śikhaṇḍin](../characters/amba-shikhandi.md).
- **Niyoga and the three births** - Bhīṣma refuses to break his vow. As precedents, he cites Paraśurāma's annihilation of the kṣatriyas and the sage [Dīrghatamas](../dirghatamas.md), who fathered sons for a king (G 103–104; CE 1.97–98). Satyavatī then reveals her firstborn: the sage **[Vyāsa](../vyasa.md)**, born to her of **Parāśara** before her marriage, on an island in the Yamunā. She herself was born from a fish, the cursed apsaras Adrikā, and King Uparicara Vasu (G 63; CE 1.57). Vyāsa fathers children on the widows by *niyoga* (levirate) (G 105–106; CE 1.99–100):
  - **Dhṛtarāṣṭra** — born **blind** because Ambikā shut her eyes at Vyāsa's fearsome appearance.
  - **Pāṇḍu** — born **pale** (*pāṇḍu*) because Ambālikā turned pale.
  - **Vidura** — born wise and whole to a slave-woman whom Ambikā sent in her place. He is the god **Dharma**, cursed by the sage **Aṇī-Māṇḍavya** to be born of a śūdra womb (G 106–108; CE 1.100–101). Māṇḍavya had been impaled as a suspected thief, and Dharma had told him it was the punishment for torturing an insect as a child.

## Drona, Drupada, Karṇa & the Princes' Training

- **Kṛpa and Droṇa as teachers** - The princes are taught arms first by **Kṛpa** and then by **Droṇa**, son of the sage Bharadvāja. Droṇa is born in a vessel (*droṇa*) from the sage's seed, which fell at the sight of the apsaras Ghṛtācī (G 130–131; CE 1.120–121). In Droṇa's test of the bird's eye, only Arjuna sees nothing but the target (G 135; CE 1.123).
- **Droṇa vs Drupada** - Droṇa, poor, goes to his boyhood friend **Drupada**, now king of Pāñcāla, and is told that a king cannot be the friend of a beggar (G 132; CE 1.122). Years later he demands Drupada's capture as his *guru-dakṣiṇā*. Droṇa keeps the northern half of Pāñcāla (Ahicchatra) and returns the land south of the Gaṅgā to Drupada. Drupada turns to sacrifice for a son who will kill Droṇa (G 140; CE 1.128).
  - *Variant (who takes Drupada):* in the vulgate the Kauravas attack first and are routed, and **Arjuna** leaps onto Drupada's chariot and seizes him (G 140). In the critical text the pupils take him together in a few verses (CE 1.128.4–5), though the chapter closes by saying Pārtha won Ahicchatra for Droṇa in battle (CE 1.128.18). The vulgate's battle narrative is App. I No. 78.
- **[Ekalavya's](../ekalavya.md) thumb** - The Niṣāda prince **Ekalavya**, son of Hiraṇyadhanus, is refused as a pupil by Droṇa. He teaches himself before a clay image of Droṇa and surpasses Arjuna. When Arjuna complains, Droṇa demands Ekalavya's right thumb as his fee (G 134; CE 1.123).
- **The graduation tournament** - At the display of arms Arjuna dazzles all. **[Karṇa](../karna.md)** enters, matches every feat and challenges him. Kṛpa asks his lineage, and **Duryodhana** at once makes him king of **Aṅga**. When the old charioteer **Adhiratha** totters in and Karṇa bows to him, Bhīma mocks him as a *sūta's* son (G 136–139; CE 1.124–127).
- **Karṇa's birth** - The narrator tells the audience everything at the point of Kuntī's girlhood (G 111; CE 1.104). The sage **Durvāsas** gives Kuntī a mantra to call any god. She tests it on **Sūrya**, and Karṇa is born with natural armour and earrings. Her virginity is restored, and she casts the baby into the river. The sūta, "husband of Rādhā", adopts him and names him **Vasuṣeṇa**. When **Indra**, disguised as a brahmin, begs his armour and earrings, Karṇa cuts them from his body, earning the name *Vaikartana* and an infallible spear. Only the characters are kept in ignorance. Kṛṣṇa and Kuntī tell Karṇa in the Udyoga Parva, and the Pāṇḍavas learn it after the war, in the Strī Parva.

## Births of the Kaurava–Pāṇḍava Generation

- **Kuntī and Mādrī** - **Kuntī (Pṛthā)**, daughter of the Yādava Śūra, is given in adoption to **Kuntibhoja**. She chooses Pāṇḍu at her svayaṃvara (G 111–112; CE 1.104–105). Bhīṣma buys **Mādrī** of Madra as Pāṇḍu's second wife (G 113; CE 1.105).
- **Gāndhārī's vow** - **[Gāndhārī](../characters/gandhari.md)**, wed to the blind Dhṛtarāṣṭra, blindfolds herself for life (G 110; CE 1.103).
- **Pāṇḍu's curse** - Hunting, Pāṇḍu kills the sage **Kindama** (CE *Kiṃdama*), who was coupling with his wife in the form of a deer. The dying sage curses Pāṇḍu to die in the act of love. Pāṇḍu renounces the kingdom and lives as an ascetic on Mount Śataśṛṅga (G 118–119; CE 1.109–110).
- **The three eldest Pāṇḍavas (by Kuntī)** - Pāṇḍu urges Kuntī to bear sons by others, citing old precedents (G 120–122; CE 1.111–113). She uses the Durvāsas mantra:
  - **Yudhiṣṭhira** — by **Dharma** (G 123; CE 1.114).
  - **Bhīma** — by **Vāyu**. Kuntī, startled by a tiger, jumps up; the infant falls from her lap and his body shatters the rock beneath into a hundred pieces. He is born on the same day as Duryodhana (G 123; CE 1.114.11–14).
  - **Arjuna** — by **Indra** (G 123; CE 1.114).
- **The twins (by Mādrī)** - Kuntī shares the mantra once with **Mādrī**, who invokes the twin **Aśvins** and bears **Nakula** and **Sahadeva** (G 124; CE 1.115).
- **The hundred Kauravas** - Vyāsa grants Gāndhārī a hundred sons, but her pregnancy lasts two years. When she hears that Kuntī has borne a son, she strikes her own womb in grief and delivers a hard ball of flesh. Vyāsa has it sprinkled with water. It splits into **101 pieces**, each the size of a thumb-joint, which he places in pots of ghee (G 115–116; CE 1.107.7–23). **Yudhiṣṭhira is the eldest by birth** (CE 1.107.24). At **Duryodhana's** birth jackals howl, and Vidura and the brahmins urge Dhṛtarāṣṭra to abandon the child for the sake of the family; he refuses (G 115; CE 1.107.25–33). The hundred sons and one daughter, **Duḥśalā**, are born within a month. **Yuyutsu** is born to Dhṛtarāṣṭra by a vaiśya woman who served him during Gāndhārī's long pregnancy (G 115–117; CE 1.107.34–37).
- **Pāṇḍu's death & Mādrī's death** - One spring Pāṇḍu embraces Mādrī and dies by the curse. Mādrī entrusts her twins to Kuntī and ascends his funeral pyre (G 125; CE 1.116.29–31). The ascetics bring the five boys, with the bodies, to Hastināpura, and the funeral rites are held there (G 126–127; CE 1.117–118).
- **Childhood rivalry** - Bhīma's strength provokes the Kauravas. **Duryodhana** poisons him at a water-sport, binds him and throws him into the Gaṅgā (G 128; CE 1.119).
  - *Critical text:* Bhīma wakes in the water, snaps his bonds and climbs out. The snakes set on him cannot pierce his skin, and he kills them. He digests a second dose of fresh *kālakūṭa* poison, and **Yuyutsu**, "the son of the vaiśya woman", warns the Pāṇḍavas (CE 1.119.35–40).
  - *Vulgate (CE App. I Nos. 72–73):* Bhīma sinks to the **nāga realm**. The serpent-king Vāsuki, at the urging of the nāga Āryaka, Bhīma's maternal great-great-grandfather (*dauhitra-dauhitra*), lets him drink eight bowls of elixir, each holding the strength of a thousand elephants. The nāgas say he will now have the might of ten thousand (G 128–129).

## The House of Lac — Lākṣāgṛha (Jatugṛha Parva)

- **Kaṇika's counsel** - Anxious at the Pāṇḍavas' growing might, **Dhṛtarāṣṭra** asks the brahmin-minister **Kaṇika** for advice and is taught a pitiless statecraft for destroying rivals (G 142). *[interpolation]* The whole Kaṇika episode is CE App. I No. 81, outside the constituted text. In the critical text, Duryodhana, Karṇa and Śakuni plot, and Duryodhana, stung by the citizens' wish to crown Yudhiṣṭhira, works on his father directly (CE 1.129.1–12).
- **Exile to Vāraṇāvata** - On the pretext of a festival of Śiva, the Pāṇḍavas and Kuntī are sent to **Vāraṇāvata**. There Duryodhana's agent **Purocana** has built a mansion of hemp, resin and lac, plastered with clay mixed with ghee, oil and lac (the vulgate adds fat), to be set ablaze (G 143–146; CE 1.130–132).
- **Vidura's coded warning** - As they leave, **Vidura** warns Yudhiṣṭhira in riddling speech that only the two of them understand (*pralāpa*, CE 1.133.18–23). His riddle: there is a weapon not made of iron (fire); the destroyer of thickets and of cold cannot harm the dweller in a burrow. The vulgate calls this "the Mlechchha tongue" (G 147). In the CE the miner later recalls Vidura's *mleccha-vācā*, "barbarian speech" (CE 1.135.6). Vidura then sends a **miner**, who digs a secret tunnel under the house (G 149; CE 1.135).
- **The counter-plot and the fire** - The Pāṇḍavas live there a year, feigning trust. Yudhiṣṭhira plans to burn Purocana and leave six bodies behind (CE 1.136.4). Kuntī gives a night feast for brahmins, to which a **Niṣāda woman and her five sons** come begging. They drink, and fall asleep in the house. In a high wind **Bhīma** sets the fire where Purocana sleeps, and the family escapes through the tunnel (G 150; CE 1.136.5–9). The vulgate and the critical text agree that Bhīma lights it. Bhīma carries his mother and brothers into the forest, and a boatman sent by Vidura ferries them across the Gaṅgā (G 150–151; CE 1.136–137).
  - The six burnt bodies make Hastināpura believe the Pāṇḍavas and their mother dead. The text itself frames this as deliberate (CE 1.136.4, "leaving six creatures here"), and the deception lasts until the svayaṃvara.

## Forest Episodes: Hiḍimbā & Baka

- **Bhīma slays Hiḍimba** - In the woods the cannibal rākṣasa **Hiḍimba** sends his sister **[Hiḍimbā](../characters/hidimba-hidimbi.md)** to lure the sleeping family. She falls in love with Bhīma instead, and Bhīma kills Hiḍimba in combat (G 154–156; CE 1.139–142).
- **Birth of Ghaṭotkaca** - With Kuntī's and Yudhiṣṭhira's leave, Bhīma lives with **Hiḍimbā** by day and returns to his family at night, until a son is born. The son is called **[Ghaṭotkaca](../characters/ghatotkacha.md)** because his head is shaped like a pot (*ghaṭa*) and bare of hair (*utkaca*). He promises to come whenever his fathers need him (G 157; CE 1.143.34–37). He dies in the war in the Droṇa Parva.
- **Vyāsa's guidance** - Only now does **Vyāsa** meet the wanderers. He comforts them and settles them, disguised as brahmins, in a brahmin's house at **Ekacakrā** (G 158; CE 1.144).
- **The slaying of Baka** - Near Ekacakrā lives the rākṣasa **Baka**. Each household must take turns sending him his wage: a cartload of rice, two buffaloes, and the man who drives them (CE 1.148.6). Kuntī volunteers **Bhīma** in her host's place. He eats the food himself and breaks Baka's back over his knee (G 159–166; CE 1.145–152).
- **Draupadī's birth foretold** - A travelling brahmin tells the Pāṇḍavas about Drupada's sacrifice by the priests **Yāja** and **Upayāja**. From the fire rose **Dhṛṣṭadyumna**, armoured, the son destined to kill Droṇa, and from the altar rose **Kṛṣṇā (Draupadī)** (G 167–169; CE 1.153–155). Vyāsa returns and tells them of Draupadī's former life: a ṛṣi's daughter who five times asked Śiva for a husband (see below). He sends them to Pāñcāla (G 171; CE 1.157).

## Toward Pāñcāla (Caitraratha Parva)

- **The gandharva Citraratha** - Crossing the Gaṅgā at night, the brothers are challenged by the gandharva king **Aṅgāraparṇa**. Arjuna burns his chariot with the *āgneya* weapon and spares his life. The gandharva renames himself **Dagdharatha** ("he of the burnt chariot", formerly Citraratha). He gives Arjuna the **cākṣuṣī** lore of seeing anything one wishes, which Manu had given to Soma, and gandharva horses, and takes the fire-weapon in exchange (G 172; CE 1.158.26–55).
- **The need for a priest, and the tales** - The gandharva says he attacked them because they had no *purohita*. He calls Arjuna *Tāpatya*, descendant of Tapatī, and explains with a chain of tales: **Tapatī**, the Sun's daughter, and King Saṃvaraṇa, the Kurus' ancestor (G 173–175; CE 1.160–163); the feud of **[Vasiṣṭha and Viśvāmitra](../vishwamitra-vasishtha.md)**, with **[Kalmāṣapāda](../kalmashapada-saudasa.md)** and the birth of Parāśara (G 176–184; CE 1.164–173); and **Aurva**, the Bhārgava whose anger became the submarine fire (G 180–182; CE 1.169–171). On his advice the brothers take **Dhaumya**, the younger brother of Devala, as their family priest (G 185; CE 1.174).

## Draupadī's Svayaṃvara (Svayaṃvara Parva)


- **The contest** - The Pāṇḍavas lodge with a potter and live by begging. Drupada secretly hopes to give **[Draupadī](../characters/draupadi.md) (Kṛṣṇā / Pāñcālī)** to Arjuna. He has a bow made that is almost impossible to string, and sets a golden target (*lakṣya*) on an artificial device (*yantra*) high in the air. The suitor must string the bow and send five arrows "through the opening of the device" into the target (G 187; CE 1.176.8–11, 34).
  - *Later retellings:* the familiar picture, a revolving **fish** shot by looking at its **reflection** in water or oil below, is not in the critical text or in Ganguli's vulgate. The Bhāgavata Purāṇa does speak of a fish (*matsya*) at Draupadī's svayaṃvara, but it gives the reflected, hidden fish to a different contest, that of Kṛṣṇa's wife **Lakṣmaṇā**. Its commentator Śrīdhara notes that Draupadī's target was only partly covered and could be seen by looking straight up (BhP 10.83.19 with Śrīdhara).
- **The kings fail; Karṇa** - The assembled kings cannot even string the bow (G 189; CE 1.178.15–17). On Karṇa, the texts give three different scenes:
  - *Vulgate:* **Karṇa** strings the bow and fits the arrows. Draupadī cries out "I will not choose a *sūta*", and Karṇa, with a bitter laugh and a glance at the sun, throws down the bow already drawn to a circle (G 189).
  - *Critical text:* that scene is the rejected passage 1827*. In the constituted text Karṇa is simply among the great kings who could not bend the bow (CE 1.179.4, "*karṇa-śalya-pramukhaiḥ … nānatam*").
  - *Other manuscripts:* further rejected passages (1830*, 1834*) have Karṇa fail by "a hair's breadth" (*romamātre*).
- **Arjuna wins** - Disguised as a brahmin, **Arjuna** strings the bow and brings down the target, and Draupadī garlands him (G 190; CE 1.179).
- **The brawl** - The kings, outraged that a brahmin has won, attack Drupada. **Bhīma** uproots a tree and wields it as a club. Arjuna fights **Karṇa**, who withdraws rather than fight a brahmin he cannot beat, and Bhīma throws down **Śalya**. Watching, **Kṛṣṇa** tells Balarāma "this is Arjuna… if I am Vāsudeva" (G 191–192; CE 1.180–181).

## The Marriage & Polyandry (Vaivāhika Parva)

- **Kuntī's fateful words** - Bhīma and Arjuna announce Draupadī to their mother as the day's alms. Kuntī, inside and not looking, says **"enjoy it all together"**, and then sees the girl and is dismayed (G 193; CE 1.182.1–2). That night Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma visit the potter's house (CE 1.183).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's ruling** - Seeing that all his brothers desire her, and to keep their mother's word from being false, Yudhiṣṭhira rules that Draupadī shall be **wife to all five** (G 193; CE 1.182).
- **Drupada's objection & Vyāsa's resolution** - Drupada calls polyandry against custom. Yudhiṣṭhira cites precedents: **Jaṭilā** of the Gautama family, who married seven ṛṣis (CE 1.188.14), and **Vārkṣī**, who married the ten Pracetas (vulgate; in the CE this is passage 1910*) (G 196–198). **Vyāsa** then justifies the marriage privately to Drupada (G 198–199; CE 1.188–189):
  - *The five Indras (Pañcendropākhyāna):* at Naimiṣa the gods see a golden lotus on the Gaṅgā. Indra follows a weeping woman whose tears turn into golden lotuses, and finds a youth playing dice on the Himālaya. He is Śiva. Indra insults him, and Śiva shuts him in a cave with four former Indras. All five must be born as men: they are the Pāṇḍavas, with Arjuna the portion of the present Indra. The weeping woman is **Śrī/Lakṣmī**, ordained to be their common wife, and she is Draupadī (CE 1.189.9–33). The same tale says that Balarāma and Kṛṣṇa came from a white hair and a black hair of Nārāyaṇa (CE 1.189.31). Vyāsa gives Drupada **divine sight**, and he sees the Pāṇḍavas in their former divine bodies (CE 1.189.35–39).
  - *The former birth:* Vyāsa adds that Draupadī was once a ṛṣi's daughter who could not find a husband. She asked Śiva five times for "a husband with every virtue". Śiva answered that because she had asked five times, she would have five husbands in her next birth (CE 1.189.41–46; first told to the Pāṇḍavas at CE 1.157.6–14; G 171, 199).
  - *Variant (Nāḷāyanī) [Southern recension]:* Southern manuscripts give her former life as **Nāḷāyanī (Indrasenā)**, devoted wife of the aged sage Maudgalya, also called Mudgala (CE App. I No. 100, after 1.188.22). This is a different episode from that of the gleaner-sage Mudgala in the Vana Parva. Sukthankar cites it as a Southern-only passage (*Prolegomena*).
- **The wedding** - Draupadī weds the brothers on successive days in order of age, and a seer (*viprarṣi*) reports that she became a virgin again after each day (G 200; CE 1.190.13–14). Her five sons, the **Upapāṇḍavas (Draupadeyas)**, are born later in Indraprastha and named within this book: **Prativindhya** by Yudhiṣṭhira, **Sutasoma** by Bhīma, **Śrutakarman** by Arjuna, **Śatānīka** by Nakula and **Śrutasena** by Sahadeva (G 223; CE 1.213.71–79).
  - *Variant (name):* the genealogy chapter calls Arjuna's son **Śrutakīrti** (CE 1.57.102; G 95 "Srutakriti"), against Śrutakarman at CE 1.213.72.

## Indraprastha & Arjuna's Exile (Vidurāgamana / Arjuna-Vanavāsa)

- **The Pāṇḍavas revealed; partition of the kingdom** - Spies bring news of the marriage, and the Pāṇḍavas are known to be alive. Duryodhana and Karṇa argue for force. Bhīṣma, Droṇa and Vidura argue for a share of the kingdom. Vidura brings the Pāṇḍavas home, and Dhṛtarāṣṭra gives them half the realm, the wilderness of **Khāṇḍavaprastha**, where they build **Indraprastha** (G 202–209; CE 1.192–199).
- **Nārada's rule for Draupadī** - **Nārada** visits them. To prevent strife over their shared wife, he tells the tale of **Sunda and Upasunda**, asura brothers who killed each other over the apsaras **Tilottamā** (G 210–214; CE 1.200–204). The brothers then agree that whoever intrudes on Draupadī with another must live **twelve years in the forest as a brahmacārin** (CE 1.204.28).
- **Arjuna's exile & marriages** - Thieves steal a brahmin's cattle. Arjuna's weapons are in the room where Yudhiṣṭhira is with Draupadī, and he goes in to fetch them. Yudhiṣṭhira waives the fault, but Arjuna insists on the exile (G 215; CE 1.205). On his pilgrimage:
  - He spends a night with the nāga princess **Ulūpī**, daughter of Kauravya of the Airāvata line (G 216; CE 1.206). Their son Irāvat is not named in the Ādi's critical text or in Ganguli's G 216. Here he appears only in the rejected passages 904* and 2025*; he enters the story in the Bhīṣma Parva (see [Aravan/Irāvān](../characters/aravan-iravan.md)).
  - He marries **[Citrāṅgadā](../characters/chitrangada.md)**, daughter of Citravāhana, on condition that her son will be her father's heir. The son is **[Babhruvāhana](../characters/babhruvahana.md)** (G 217, 219; CE 1.207, 1.209.24). Her city is **Maṇalūra** in the CE and **Maṇipura** in the vulgate.
  - At five sacred bathing-places in the south, he frees five apsarases, led by **Vargā**, who had been cursed to live there as crocodiles (G 218–219; CE 1.208–209).
- **Abduction of [Subhadrā](../characters/subhadra.md)** - At Prabhāsa and the Raivataka festival, Arjuna falls for Kṛṣṇa's sister **Subhadrā**. Kṛṣṇa advises him to carry her off, as a kṣatriya may, and Yudhiṣṭhira consents by messenger (G 220–222; CE 1.210–212). The Vṛṣṇis rage, and **Balarāma** most of all. Kṛṣṇa pacifies them, the marriage is solemnised, and Arjuna returns to Indraprastha at the end of the twelve years (G 223; CE 1.213). Their son is **[Abhimanyu](../characters/abhimanyu.md)** (CE 1.213.59–60).

## The Burning of Khāṇḍava (Khāṇḍava-dāha Parva)

- **Agni's hunger** - Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna are picnicking by the Yamunā when a huge brahmin asks them for food. He is **Agni**, and he wants to eat the **Khāṇḍava forest**. **Indra** always puts out his fire with rain, to protect his friend, the nāga **Takṣaka**, who lives there (G 224–225; CE 1.214–215).
  - *Variant (why Agni is sick) [interpolation]:* in the vulgate, King **Śvetaki's** endless sacrifices, completed with Śiva's help by the sage Durvāsas, have glutted Agni with ghee. He has lost his vigour, and Brahmā prescribes the creatures of Khāṇḍava as the cure (G 225–226). In the CE this is App. I No. 118; the constituted text has only Agni's request.
- **The divine pact** - Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa agree, but ask for weapons fit for the task (G 225–227; CE 1.215–216):
  - At Agni's request **Varuṇa** gives Arjuna the **Gāṇḍīva** bow, made long ago by Brahmā, two inexhaustible quivers, and a chariot with white gandharva horses and an **ape on its banner** (G 227; CE 1.216.1–20).
  - **Kṛṣṇa** receives from Agni a discus with a thunderbolt hub (*vajranābha*), which returns to his hand after every throw. From Varuṇa he receives the mace **Kaumodakī** (G 227; CE 1.216.21–25). The name *Sudarśana* is not used here in the critical text or in Ganguli; it occurs only in the rejected passage 2117*.
- **The conflagration** - The two heroes ride round the burning forest and kill every creature that tries to escape. Arjuna holds back **Indra's** rain with his arrows, and he and Kṛṣṇa beat back the gods (G 228–230; CE 1.217–219). A voice from the sky tells Indra that Takṣaka is away at **Kurukṣetra**, and that his opponents are the ancient gods **Nara and Nārāyaṇa**. Indra withdraws (G 230; CE 1.219.13–15).
- **The survivors** - Only **six** creatures escape: Aśvasena, Maya and the four Śārṅgaka birds (G 230; CE 1.219.40):
  - **Aśvasena**, Takṣaka's son. His mother swallows him head first and flies up while still swallowing his tail, and Arjuna cuts off her head. Indra dazes Arjuna with a storm and the young snake gets away. Arjuna, Agni and Kṛṣṇa curse him to be *apratiṣṭha*, "without a resting place" (G 229; CE 1.218.5–11). He reappears in the Karṇa Parva.
  - The asura **Maya**, Namuci's brother, flees from Takṣaka's home with Agni and Kṛṣṇa's discus close behind. He cries to Arjuna for help, and Arjuna grants him safety (G 230; CE 1.219.35–39).
  - The four young **Śārṅgaka** birds: Jaritāri, Sārisṛkva, Stambamitra and Droṇa. Their father is the sage **Mandapāla**. Denied the heavenly worlds because he had no sons, he became a śārṅgaka bird and fathered them on **Jaritā**. He then left them for another bird, **Lapitā**, but first won Agni's promise to spare them with a hymn. A tale of the mother's anguish and the father's jealous second wife follows (G 231–236; CE 1.220–225).
- **Indra's blessing, and the end of the book** - Indra, pleased, offers Arjuna a boon. Arjuna asks for all of Indra's weapons, and Indra promises them for the time when Śiva is pleased with him. Kṛṣṇa asks for eternal friendship with Arjuna (G 236; CE 1.225.7–13). Agni, sated, lets them go. Arjuna, Kṛṣṇa and Maya sit down together on a river bank, and the book ends (G 236; CE 1.225.15–19). Maya's offer to build the assembly hall opens the **Sabhā Parva**.
  - *Variant (duration):* the critical text has the fire burn for **six** days, "five and one" (CE 1.225.15). Ganguli's vulgate says "five and ten days" (G 230, 236).
