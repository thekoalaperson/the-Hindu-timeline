# Virāṭa Parva — The Book of Virata

> Cosmic address: [Brahma's current life](../../README.md) › [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md) › [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md) › **[Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** — the 13th year of the Pāṇḍavas' exile, lived incognito (*ajñāta-vāsa*) at the Matsya court.
>
> Up: [Mahābhārata overview](README.md) · Previous: [03 — Vana Parva](03-vana-parva.md) · Next: [05 — Udyoga Parva](05-udyoga-parva.md). Parent corpus: [Smṛti — Itihāsa & Epics](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md).

The **Virāṭa Parva** ("Book of Virāṭa") is the **fourth of the 18 parvas** of the [Mahābhārata](README.md). It covers the **single climactic year of concealment** (*ajñāta-vāsa*) imposed by the dice-bond of the [Sabhā Parva](02-sabha-parva.md): after 12 forest years (Vana Parva), the Pāṇḍavas must spend a 13th year undetected, on pain of restarting the whole exile if recognised. They serve in disguise at the court of **King Virāṭa of the Matsya kingdom**. The parva is the epic's one extended pause from cosmic grandeur — a chamber-drama of hidden identity, sexual menace (Kīcaka), and at last a single dazzling display of Arjuna's full divine arsenal in the cattle-raid, after which the disguises fall and the road to Kurukṣetra opens. In the Bhandarkar **Critical Edition** it runs to ~**67 adhyāyas** in **4 upaparvas** (older Vulgate counts ~72); ~1,736 verses.

- *Variant [scholarly]:* the 2nd-c. CE **Spitzer Manuscript** contents-list omits the Virāṭa (and Udyoga) parva, prompting some scholars to argue these are later insertions; this is a minority view and is not followed by the Bhandarkar Critical Edition, which treats the parva as integral.

## The 4 upaparvas (sub-books)

| # | Upaparva | CE chapters (approx.) | Core content |
|---|----------|-----------------------|--------------|
| 1 | **Pāṇḍava-praveśa** ("entry of the Pāṇḍavas") | 1–13 | Choosing disguises; hiding the weapons; taking service at the Matsya court |
| 2 | **Kīcaka-vadha** ("slaying of Kīcaka") | 14–24 | Kīcaka's harassment of Sairandhrī; his death by Bhīma; the Upakīcakas |
| 3 | **Go-grahaṇa** / Go-harana ("seizing of the cattle") | 25–62 | The Trigarta and Kaurava cattle-raids; Arjuna revealed; Uttara |
| 4 | **Vaivāhika** ("the marriage") | 63–67 | Identities revealed; Abhimanyu weds Uttarā |

- *Variant [scholarly]:* upaparva boundaries and the total chapter count differ between the Critical Edition (~67) and the Vulgate/Nīlakaṇṭha recension (~72). Some lists give the third book as **Go-harana**, others **Go-grahaṇa**; the names are interchangeable.

## 1. Pāṇḍava-praveśa — entering the Matsya court in disguise

- **The deliberation on where to hide** - As the 12 forest years close, Yudhiṣṭhira asks his brothers where they can pass the 13th year unrecognised; they settle on the prosperous, virtuous **kingdom of Matsya** ruled by aged **King Virāṭa** (Virāṭa Parva 1).
- **Hiding the weapons in the śamī tree** - Before entering the city, the Pāṇḍavas wrap their celestial weapons — Arjuna's **Gāṇḍīva** bow and inexhaustible quivers, Bhīma's mace, the others' bows and swords — in a corpse-shroud and bind the bundle high in a **śamī (śamī/jammi) tree** near a cremation ground, telling onlookers it is a relative's body so none will touch it (Virāṭa Parva 5).
  - *Note:* the śamī-tree hiding-place is why the tree is venerated on **Vijayadaśamī / Daśahrā** in many regional traditions — the day Arjuna is said to have retrieved his arms. [folk tradition]
- **Yudhiṣṭhira → Kaṅka** - The eldest takes the name **Kaṅka** (a brāhmaṇa once of King Yudhiṣṭhira's court), serving as Virāṭa's courtier, dice-companion and counsellor (Virāṭa Parva 1, 7).
- **Bhīma → Vallabha (Ballava)** - Becomes the royal **cook and wrestler/strongman**, also called **Jayanta/Vallava** in variants, dazzling the court by defeating challengers and beasts (Virāṭa Parva 2, 8).
- **Arjuna → Bṛhannalā** - The great archer adopts the **eunuch / third-gender** identity **Bṛhannalā** (Bṛhannaḍā), in women's dress and bangles, teaching **song and dance** to Virāṭa's daughter **Uttarā** and the harem women — a disguise that fulfils the curse of the apsarā **Ūrvaśī** (laid in the Vana Parva, granting one year of eunuch-hood) (Virāṭa Parva 2, 10).
  - *Variant:* the spelling and exact nature of Bṛhannalā's third-gender status is treated differently across recensions and regional retellings — sometimes a literal eunuch, sometimes a celibate dance-master in disguise.
- **Nakula → Granthika (Damagranthi)** - Serves as the **horse-keeper / groom**, expert in equine science (Virāṭa Parva 2, 11).
- **Sahadeva → Tantipāla (Ariṣṭanemi)** - Serves as the **cowherd / keeper of the royal cattle**, expert in bovine lore (Virāṭa Parva 2, 12).
- **Draupadī → Sairandhrī (Mālinī)** - Disguised as **Sairandhrī** ("skilled chamber-maid"), personal name **Mālinī**, she becomes lady's-maid and hairdresser to **Queen Sudeṣṇā**, on condition she handle no leftover food and never wash feet — and she claims to be protected by five invisible **gandharva husbands** who slay any who molest her (Virāṭa Parva 3, 9).
- **The pact of secrecy and signals** - The six agree on rendezvous and recognition-signs and a vow to reveal nothing for a year, accepting whatever menial roles fortune assigns (Virāṭa Parva 4–5).

## 2. Kīcaka-vadha — the slaying of Kīcaka

- **Kīcaka, the queen's brother** - **Kīcaka** (Kīchaka), commander-in-chief of Virāṭa's army and brother of Queen Sudeṣṇā, is the real power behind the throne; he becomes inflamed with lust for Sairandhrī (Virāṭa Parva 14).
  - *Variant [Southern recension / Tamil tradition]:* Kīcaka and his 105 brothers (the Upakīcakas) are said to be a partial incarnation of the asura **Bāṇa** / of the Kālakeya demons — giving the human commander a demonic origin absent from the Northern recension. [disputed]
- **The harassment** - Kīcaka begs Sudeṣṇā to send Sairandhrī to his house with wine; Draupadī warns him of her gandharva husbands, but he persists and assaults her (Virāṭa Parva 15–16).
- **The kick in the sabhā** - Fleeing to Virāṭa's assembly hall, Sairandhrī is caught and **kicked / dragged by the hair before the whole court**; Virāṭa, fearing his commander, does not act, and even **Kaṅka (Yudhiṣṭhira)** restrains himself, counselling patience lest the disguise break — to her bitter reproach (Virāṭa Parva 16).
  - *Note:* this public outrage deliberately mirrors the earlier disrobing in the [Sabhā Parva](02-sabha-parva.md) — Draupadī twice shamed before silent kings — and her fury at Yudhiṣṭhira's passivity is among the parva's most powerful scenes.
- **Draupadī goes to Bhīma by night** - She wakes **Vallabha (Bhīma)** in the kitchen, pours out her humiliation and her exhaustion with exile, and demands Kīcaka's death; Bhīma consoles her and devises the trap (Virāṭa Parva 18–20).
- **The assignation in the dancing-hall** - Sairandhrī lures Kīcaka to a tryst in the dark **nartana-śālā (dance-hall)** at night; **Bhīma lies on the couch in her place** (Virāṭa Parva 21).
- **The killing** - In the dark Bhīma seizes Kīcaka; after a tremendous wrestling-fight he **crushes him into a shapeless ball of flesh** — head, hands and feet pounded into the trunk — leaving a "creature without limbs" (Virāṭa Parva 22).
  - *Variant (dramatic / Yakṣagāna & Kathakaḷi traditions):* the Bhīma–Kīcaka duel is elaborated into a long set-piece combat; the mangled corpse is a famous theatrical image. [folk tradition]
- **The 105 Upakīcakas** - Kīcaka's kinsmen, blaming Sairandhrī (the "gandharva-wife") for his death, seize her to **burn her alive on his funeral pyre**; Bhīma, uprooting a tree, charges in and **slays all 105 Upakīcakas**, freeing her (Virāṭa Parva 23).
  - *Variant:* the number is usually 105 brothers; some recensions give round figures or simply "Kīcaka's kinsmen."
- **The court's terror** - Awed and frightened by the slaughter, Virāṭa and the people credit Sairandhrī's invisible gandharva husbands; Sudeṣṇā asks Sairandhrī to leave, but she begs 13 more days, the year being nearly done (Virāṭa Parva 23–24).

## 3. Go-grahaṇa — the twin cattle-raids and the unveiling of Arjuna

- **The Kaurava war-council** - Spies report that the Pāṇḍavas cannot be found and that Kīcaka is dead; with the exile nearly expired, the Kuru court debates. **Karṇa** urges harder searching, **Droṇa** counsels peace, **Bhīṣma** reasons that the Pāṇḍavas must be wherever the land is most prosperous and just (he names Matsya), and **Kṛpa** advises arming for war (Virāṭa Parva 25–30).
- **Suśarmā's Trigarta raid (southern attack)** - **King Suśarmā of the Trigartas**, a Kaurava ally long humiliated by Matsya, proposes raiding Virāṭa's famous cattle-herds to draw out and weaken him; the Trigartas attack, **loot the herds and capture King Virāṭa** himself (Virāṭa Parva 30–32).
- **The four Pāṇḍavas to the rescue** - Yudhiṣṭhira (Kaṅka), Bhīma (Vallabha), Nakula (Granthika) and Sahadeva (Tantipāla) join Virāṭa's defence; **Bhīma routs the Trigartas, rescues Virāṭa, and captures Suśarmā** — whom Yudhiṣṭhira mercifully has freed (Virāṭa Parva 32–33).
- **The Kaurava raid (northern attack)** - The same day, **Duryodhana, Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Karṇa, Kṛpa, Aśvatthāman and Duḥśāsana** strike the now-undefended capital from the north to rustle the rest of the cattle (the *Go-grahaṇa* proper) and to flush out the Pāṇḍavas (Virāṭa Parva 34).
- **Prince Uttara's boast** - With the army gone south, only the young, untried prince **Uttara (Uttara Kumāra / Bhūmiñjaya)** remains; he boasts he would scatter the Kurus single-handed if only he had a charioteer (Virāṭa Parva 35).
- **Bṛhannalā volunteers as charioteer** - At Uttarā's and Sairandhrī's prompting, the "eunuch" **Bṛhannalā (Arjuna)** offers to drive; the women laughingly accept (Virāṭa Parva 35–36).
- **Uttara's panic and flight** - Seeing the vast Kuru host with Bhīṣma's and Droṇa's banners, Uttara is terrified and **leaps from the chariot to flee**; Bṛhannalā chases him down, drags him back by the hair, and forces him to take the reins (Virāṭa Parva 36–37).
- **The retrieval of the weapons** - Arjuna drives to the **śamī tree**, has the trembling Uttara climb up and fetch the hidden arms, recites the lineage of each great bow and weapon, then **strings the Gāṇḍīva** — its twang like thunder — and blows the conch **Devadatta**, the sound shaking the Kuru ranks (Virāṭa Parva 38–42).
- **Arjuna reveals himself** - Arjuna discloses his true identity (and his ten secret names) to the astonished prince, who now joyfully serves as his charioteer (Virāṭa Parva 39, 42).
- **Single-handed battle against the Kuru host** - Alone, Arjuna fights **Karṇa, Kṛpa, Droṇa, Aśvatthāman, Bhīṣma and Duryodhana** in succession, defeating each: he wounds and humbles Karṇa, drives off Kṛpa, honours then routs Droṇa, and stands off Bhīṣma (Virāṭa Parva 47–60).
- **The Sammohana-astra** - To end the fight without slaughter, Arjuna invokes the **Sammohana ("stupefying/hypnotic") astra**, which throws the entire Kuru army into a swoon (Virāṭa Parva 61).
- **The taking of the garments** - At Arjuna's order Uttara strips the upper garments from the unconscious lords — **white from Bhīṣma, etc.** (colours vary by recension) — for the princesses' dolls, a teasing trophy of victory (Virāṭa Parva 61).
  - *Variant:* the precise colours attributed to Duryodhana, Karṇa and Duḥśāsana differ across manuscripts; the doll-cloth motif itself is constant.
- **The cattle recovered** - The Kurus, recognising Arjuna (and so confirming the exile is in fact already completed, by the lunar/solar reckoning), withdraw; the stolen cattle are driven back to Matsya (Virāṭa Parva 62).
- **The thirteen-year reckoning [disputed]** - Bhīṣma argues the 13 years are complete by the calendar of intercalary (*adhika*) months, so the Pāṇḍavas have not been "discovered" prematurely; this calendrical defence is a famous point of debate, raised again in later parvas.

## 4. Vaivāhika — the unmasking and the marriage

- **Virāṭa's gratitude and his blunder** - Believing his own son Uttara won the battle, Virāṭa rejoices; when **Kaṅka (Yudhiṣṭhira)** says it was Bṛhannalā's doing, the king in anger **hurls a dice and strikes Yudhiṣṭhira's face, drawing blood** — which Draupadī catches in a vessel so it not fall to the ground (lest the earth suffer drought, by her vow) (Virāṭa Parva 63).
- **Uttara's correction** - Returning, Prince Uttara rebukes his father: it was the mighty Bṛhannalā, "a god or gandharva," who won; he hides the warrior's true name as instructed (Virāṭa Parva 64).
- **The revelation in the sabhā** - On the third day the five brothers, bathed and robed as kings, take the royal seats; the astonished Virāṭa is told by Arjuna that **Kaṅka is Yudhiṣṭhira, Vallabha is Bhīma, Bṛhannalā is Arjuna, Granthika and Tantipāla are the twins, and Sairandhrī is Draupadī** (Virāṭa Parva 65).
- **Virāṭa's homage** - Mortified at having struck and employed the emperor, Virāṭa begs forgiveness, offers his whole kingdom, and proposes alliance (Virāṭa Parva 66).
- **The marriage of Abhimanyu and Uttarā** - Virāṭa offers his daughter **Uttarā** to Arjuna; Arjuna declines her as a wife (having been her dance-teacher, he treats her as a daughter) and instead takes her **as daughter-in-law**, marrying her to his son **Abhimanyu**, who arrives with Kṛṣṇa, Balarāma and the Yādavas; a great wedding is celebrated at Virāṭa's capital **Upaplavya** (Virāṭa Parva 67).
  - *Note:* this union produces **Parīkṣit** (born after the war), through whom the Kuru line — and the audience-frame of the whole epic, narrated to his son Janamejaya — continues. See the [Ādi Parva](01-adi-parva.md) frame and [Sūrya/Candra-vaṃśa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
- **The springboard to war** - The parva closes with the kingdoms of Matsya, the Yādavas and allies gathered at Upaplavya — the assembly that opens the **[Udyoga Parva](05-udyoga-parva.md)** and the diplomacy that fails into the Kurukṣetra war (Virāṭa Parva 67).

## Variant traditions & afterlives

- *Variant (Bhāsa, *Pañcarātra* & related plays) [late text]:* Sanskrit dramatists reworked Virāṭa episodes for the stage; the Kīcaka-vadha became a favourite dramatic and dance subject far beyond the epic text.
- *Variant (Tamil *Bhārata Veṇbā* of Peruntēvanār) [regional]:* the medieval Tamil rendering localises the Kīcaka episode's ethics for a Tamil audience, with heightened poetic-justice framing.
- *Variant (Madhva/Dvaita tradition):* commentators in the **Madhva** lineage give the Virāṭa Parva special devotional importance, reading Bhīma's slaying of Kīcaka and protection of Draupadī as exemplary of Vāyu/Bhīma's role; the parva is recited for protection and the removal of distress. [folk/sectarian]
- *Variant (performance traditions):* **Yakṣagāna, Kathakaḷi, Kūṭiyāṭṭam** and Telugu/Kannada folk theatre all stage *Kīcaka-vadha* and the *Go-grahaṇa* as standalone spectacles, often expanding the Bhīma–Kīcaka and Arjuna–Kaurava combats far beyond the epic. [folk tradition]
- *Variant (cinema):* the 1916 Tamil silent film **Keechaka Vadham** was among the earliest South Indian feature films — index of the episode's enduring popularity.
