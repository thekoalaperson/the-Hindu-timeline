# Anuśāsana Parva — The Book of Instructions

> Cosmic address: [Kali Yuga](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) → the Dvāpara–Kali junction, on the field of Kurukṣetra in the weeks after the war (war traditionally ~3138 BCE; Kali begins 3102 BCE). Bhīṣma still lies on the bed of arrows, dying by his own will at the turn of the year.
> Parent: [Mahābhārata deep-dives](./README.md) · Previous: [12-Śānti Parva](./12-shanti-parva.md) · Next: [14-Aśvamedhika Parva](./14-ashvamedhika-parva.md)
> The thirteenth of the eighteen parvas. The didactic continuation of the Śānti Parva: Bhīṣma's last instructions to Yudhiṣṭhira on dāna (charity), dharma, women, ancestors and the worship of Viṣṇu and Śiva — closing with Bhīṣma's chosen death at the winter solstice (uttarāyaṇa) and his ascent to heaven.

The Anuśāsana Parva ("anuśāsana" = instruction, precept, governance; ~6,500+ ślokas in the Pune Critical Edition, ~12,000 in the vulgate, the single longest book of the epic alongside Śānti) is the second half of Bhīṣma's deathbed teaching. Where [Śānti Parva](./12-shanti-parva.md) concentrated on rāja-dharma (statecraft), mokṣa-dharma and the great philosophical dialogues, Anuśāsana is overwhelmingly a manual of **dāna-dharma** (the law of giving) and applied ethics — what to give, to whom, when, the merit of each gift, the duties of women, the cult of the ancestors (śrāddha), expiation (prāyaścitta), and the supreme efficacy of devotion to Viṣṇu and Śiva. It embeds two of Hinduism's most-recited liturgies — the **Viṣṇu Sahasranāma** and the **Śiva Sahasranāma** — and ends with Bhīṣma's nirvāṇa. See [karma–saṃsāra–mokṣa](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md), the [puruṣārthas](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md), [ahiṃsā within yoga/ethics](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/yoga-paths-and-patanjali.md), [Viṣṇu](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md), [Śiva myth-cycles](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md), and the [Mahābhārata corpus entry](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md).

## Structure — the sub-parvas (upa-parvas)

- **Two-fold scheme (Critical Edition / standard):** the parva divides into **Dāna-dharma Parva** (the great body of teaching, ~chapters 1–152 in the CE numbering) and **Bhīṣma-svargārohaṇa Parva** ("Bhīṣma's Ascent to Heaven," the final 2 chapters), totalling ~154 adhyāyas. (Wikipedia; Critical Edition)
  - *Variant (vulgate / Sacred-Texts–Ganguli numbering):* the printed vulgate runs to ~168 chapters because of additional/expanded sections; the two upa-parvas are the same in substance. (Sacred-Texts / Ganguli)
- *Note:* Anuśāsana is widely regarded by scholars as the most heavily **interpolated** book of the Mahābhārata — large dāna-stuti, tīrtha and stotra blocks were added over centuries; the Critical Edition excises much of it. [scholarly] (Wikipedia; Pune CE)

## Events and teachings — in narrative order

### Frame: Yudhiṣṭhira's lingering remorse (Dāna-dharma, opening)

- **Yudhiṣṭhira still grief-stricken** - Despite all of Śānti Parva, the king remains crushed by guilt over the slaughter; Bhīṣma resumes teaching to lift him out of sorrow, opening the discourse on fate, effort and the workings of dharma. (Mbh XIII.1)
- **Destiny vs. exertion (the Gautamī / serpent dialogue)** - A famous opening parable: a child bitten by a snake; the bereaved mother Gautamī refuses to let the fowler Arjunaka kill the snake. The serpent blames Death (Mṛtyu); Death blames Time/Kāla; the resolution is that **karma** is the true cause and no single agent is to be blamed — vengeance is futile. (Mbh XIII.1, "Gautamī-saṃvāda")
- **Free will and the seed (Vasiṣṭha–brāhmaṇa dialogue)** - Effort (puruṣakāra) and destiny (daiva) are likened to seed and soil: fruit needs both present exertion and ripened karma. (Mbh XIII.6)

### Caste, expiation and the malleability of varṇa

- **Viśvāmitra becomes a brāhmaṇa** - the kṣatriya sage rises to brahminhood by tapas — adduced to show varṇa can be transcended by conduct/austerity. (Mbh XIII; cf. [varṇa/āśrama](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md))
- **Origin and ranking of the four varṇas; intermixture (saṅkara)** - Bhīṣma lays out the duties of each order, the offspring of mixed unions, and rules of expiation (prāyaścitta) for transgressions. (Mbh XIII; Dāna-dharma)
  - *Variant emphasis:* the same parva also contains strongly birth-determinist passages — the contradiction (conduct-based vs. birth-based varṇa) reflects multiple textual layers. [scholarly]

### The duties, nature and rights of women

- **The Aṣṭāvakra and the apsaras / the old woman (Diśā)** - the sage Aṣṭāvakra, sent to test marriage, debates a celestial woman on whether a woman is independent or always under guardianship (father, husband, sons); the discourse on strī-svabhāva and strī-dharma. (Mbh XIII.19–21)
- **Umā–Maheśvara saṃvāda on women's duties** - Śiva, questioned by Umā (Pārvatī), expounds the ideal conduct of the wife: sweet speech, devotion, treating the husband as god, refuge and friend. (Mbh XIII.146)
  - *Variant (counter-current):* elsewhere the same parva affirms a **daughter's right to inherit** and equates her with a son in certain respects — Bhīṣma's teaching is not monolithic on women's status. [scholarly] (Wikipedia)
- **On the praise and the danger of women** - mixed passages: women extolled as the household's Lakṣmī and as sources of dharma, alongside cautionary/ascetic verses warning of attachment — again reflecting layered authorship. (Mbh XIII; Dāna-dharma)
- **Bhaṅgāśvana — the king who became a woman** - a king transformed into a woman bears children as both sexes and, asked to choose, keeps the female form, declaring a mother's love and a woman's pleasure greater — a striking dialogue on gender. (Mbh XIII.12)

### The law of giving (dāna-dharma) — the heart of the parva

- **What gifts yield what merit** - Bhīṣma catalogues the fruits of giving food, water, land, gold, cattle, sesame, lamps, houses, umbrellas, sandals and more — with food and water praised as the highest everyday gifts. (Mbh XIII, Dāna-dharma; TemplePurohit; Vyāsa Online)
- **The supreme gift of the cow (go-dāna)** - extensive eulogy of the cow and of giving cows (go-dāna), the worlds attained thereby, and the sanctity of cattle and Brahmins. (Mbh XIII, "go-dāna" sections)
- **Gift of land (bhūmi-dāna) and of gold (suvarṇa-dāna)** - land-grant as among the highest gifts; the origin-myth of gold from Agni and the merit of giving it. (Mbh XIII)
- **Tīrtha-yātrā and the merit of pilgrimage** - listing of sacred fords and the punya of visiting them (overlaps the Vana Parva tīrtha-list); links to [sacred geography](../../06-sacred-geography/README.md). (Mbh XIII)
- **To whom, when and how to give** - the worthy recipient (pātra), the proper time (especially during eclipses, solstices, sacred days), and the spirit of giving (with faith, śraddhā). (Mbh XIII, Dāna-dharma)

### Ahiṃsā, diet and conduct

- **Ahiṃsā paramo dharmaḥ** - the celebrated declaration: "Non-violence is the highest dharma; non-violence is the highest self-control; non-violence is the highest gift; non-violence is the highest austerity." Bṛhaspati counsels universal compassion. (Mbh XIII.117)
- **Discourse on meat-eating (māṃsa-bhakṣaṇa)** - a sustained passage praising abstention from meat and the merit of vegetarianism, while acknowledging sanctioned exceptions (sacrifice, emergency) — one of the strongest pro-ahiṃsā/vegetarian texts in the epic. (Mbh XIII.115–116)
- **On truth, anger, forgiveness, and the conduct of the good** - clusters of ethical instruction (sadācāra) on speech, restraint, hospitality and gratitude. (Mbh XIII)

### Ancestors, fate and the unseen

- **Śrāddha (rites for the ancestors / pitṛs)** - the proper performance of śrāddha, what foods please the pitṛs, the days and the worthy Brahmins to feed; the doctrine that offspring and offerings sustain the dead. (Mbh XIII, Dāna-dharma; links to [karma–saṃsāra](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md))
- **The legend of Nachiketā (Nāciketa)** - the boy who visited the realm of Yama and returned with knowledge of the afterlife and of right gifting — a Mahābhārata recension of the tale famous from the *Kaṭha Upaniṣad*. (Mbh XIII; cf. [Upaniṣads](../../90-literature-corpus/shruti-vedas-and-upanishads.md))
- **Story of Chyavana and the fish / the Niṣādas** - the sage Cyavana, caught in a fisherman's net, insists on sharing the fate (and value) of the fish, teaching the worth of all life and the dharma of the Niṣādas. (Mbh XIII)
- **The pigeon and the fowler (kapota-upākhyāna)** - a hunted pigeon's mate immolates herself in hospitality to the fowler who killed her husband; the fowler renounces violence — a parable of ahiṃsā and ātithya (hospitality). (Mbh XIII)

### The two great hymns (sahasranāmas)

- **Viṣṇu Sahasranāma — the Thousand Names of Viṣṇu [key episode]** - Yudhiṣṭhira asks Bhīṣma the supreme refuge and easiest path to liberation; Bhīṣma answers that meditating on and praising **Viṣṇu/Vāsudeva** — the highest being — is best, and recites the 1,000 names. This is the single most-recited Vaiṣṇava stotra in living Hindu practice. (Mbh XIII.135 [vulgate ~149]; Sacred-Texts)
  - *Variant (chapter number):* cited variously as adhyāya 134, 135 or 149 depending on edition/numbering. [late text / editional]
- **Śiva Sahasranāma — the Thousand Names of Śiva [key episode]** - Yudhiṣṭhira asks for the names of Śiva; Bhīṣma defers, and **Kṛṣṇa** recites them, having himself learned them from the sage **Upamanyu** (whose Śiva-bhakti story is told here). (Mbh XIII; Anuśāsana)
  - *Variant (other recension):* a different Śiva Sahasranāma appears in the *Liṅga Purāṇa*; the two lists differ — catalog both. [variant text] (Hindupedia)
- **Equivalence of Viṣṇu and Śiva** - the parva repeatedly identifies the two as one supreme reality (Hari-Hara), a deliberate sectarian-harmonizing thread. (Mbh XIII)
- **Upamanyu's penance and Kṛṣṇa's boon from Śiva** - Kṛṣṇa narrates how he obtained sons and boons from Śiva through austerity, framing the Śaiva material. (Mbh XIII)

### Snakes, omens and miscellany

- **Praise of Brahmins and the power of their curse/blessing** - tales (e.g. the duel of curses, the brāhmaṇa-kṣatriya conflicts) illustrating the supremacy of Brahminical merit. (Mbh XIII)
- **On rākṣasas, serpents (nāgas) and propitiation** - protective and expiatory material woven into the dāna sections. (Mbh XIII)

### Bhīṣma's death and ascent (Bhīṣma-svargārohaṇa Parva)

- **Bhīṣma awaits the solstice** - having fallen on the tenth day of war (Mārgaśīrṣa, his fall in [Bhīṣma Parva](./06-bhishma-parva.md)), Bhīṣma uses his boon of icchā-mṛtyu (death-at-will, granted for his vow of celibacy) to delay death until the sun turns north (**uttarāyaṇa**), the auspicious gate to the deathless worlds. (Mbh XIII.153–154)
- **The teaching ends; permission to depart** - Bhīṣma tells Yudhiṣṭhira his instruction is complete and that the sun has reached the solstice; he asks leave of Kṛṣṇa, the Pāṇḍavas, Vidura, Dhṛtarāṣṭra and the assembled sages to release his life. (Mbh XIII.153)
- **Bhīṣma's death by yoga** - withdrawing his life-breaths (prāṇas) by yogic concentration, region by region of the body, Bhīṣma casts off the mortal frame; the arrows fall away, flowers rain from heaven, drums sound, and his soul rises like a meteor into the sky. (Mbh XIII.154; Sacred-Texts m13)
- **Cremation and water-rites on the Ganges** - the Pāṇḍavas and Dhṛtarāṣṭra perform the funeral; **Gaṅgā**, Bhīṣma's mother, rises grieving from her river, and Kṛṣṇa consoles her, revealing Bhīṣma's true nature as one of the **Vasus** (he was the Vasu Dyaus/Prabhāsa, cursed to be born human — see [Ādi Parva](./01-adi-parva.md)). (Mbh XIII; Sacred-Texts)
- **The day commemorated** - Bhīṣma's passing is observed as **Bhīṣma Aṣṭamī**, on the eighth lunar day of the bright fortnight of Māgha (Māgha-śukla-aṣṭamī, Jan–Feb), with tarpaṇa offered to him. (tradition; Bhishma Ashtami)

### Variants on the duration on the bed of arrows

- *Variant (58 nights — most common):* Bhīṣma fell on the 10th day of an 18-day war and lay on the arrow-bed until Māgha-śukla-aṣṭamī, counted by many traditions as **58 nights** (the figure widely cited from Anuśāsana 13.167/273). (Bharath Gyan; popular tradition)
- *Variant (51 days):* some retellings give **51 days** on the bed before his death. [folk tradition]
- *Variant (chronological problem):* astronomers and Indologists note the 10th-day fall (Mārgaśīrṣa) to Māgha-śukla-aṣṭamī span and the uttarāyaṇa timing do not reconcile cleanly across systems; the "58 days" has been challenged as a later harmonization. [scholarly] (Nilesh Oak; Jayasree Saranathan)
- *Note on uttarāyaṇa:* the text makes Bhīṣma die *after* the sun's northward turn; precession means the calendrical solstice has drifted from Māgha-śukla over millennia, fuelling Mahābhārata-dating debates. [scholarly]

## Why this parva matters

- It is, with Śānti, the dharmaśāstric core of the Mahābhārata — a near-encyclopedia of giving, expiation, ancestor-worship and household ethics absorbed into later [Dharmaśāstra](../../90-literature-corpus/agamas-tantras-dharmashastras.md).
- It supplies two of the most-recited liturgies in daily Hindu worship (Viṣṇu and Śiva Sahasranāmas) and the canonical *ahiṃsā paramo dharmaḥ* verse.
- It narrates the death of the epic's great patriarch and ties him back to the cosmic frame as a fallen Vasu, completing his arc from [Ādi Parva](./01-adi-parva.md).
