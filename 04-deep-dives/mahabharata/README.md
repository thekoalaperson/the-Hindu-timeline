# The Mahābhārata — Overview & Recensions

> Cosmic address: [Brahma's current life](../../README.md) › [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md) › [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md) › **[Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** — the Kurukṣetra War is traditionally placed at the very end of the Dvāpara Yuga; Kṛṣṇa's departure marks the dawn of [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) (3102 BCE).
>
> This is the overview node for the Mahābhārata deep-dive. Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Parent epic-corpus: [Smṛti — Itihāsa & Epics](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md). Sister epic: [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md).

The **Mahābhārata** ("the Great [story] of the Bhārata dynasty") is the longer of the two great Itihāsas — the world's longest epic poem, traditionally **~100,000 ślokas** (plus the Harivaṃśa appendix), roughly four times the [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md) and over seven times the *Iliad* and *Odyssey* combined. It narrates the dynastic struggle between the **Pāṇḍavas** and **Kauravas** culminating in the 18-day Kurukṣetra War, and frames within it the entire moral-philosophical universe of classical Hinduism — including the [Bhagavad Gītā](../bhagavad-gita.md). It is called **"the fifth Veda"** and proclaims of itself: *"What is here may be found elsewhere; what is not here is nowhere"* (Ādi Parva / Svargārohaṇa Parva).

## Authorship & the Vyāsa–Gaṇeśa tradition

- **Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana Vyāsa** - Ascribed to the sage **Vyāsa** ("the arranger/compiler"), who is also a *character* in his own epic: biological grandfather (via niyoga) of both the Pāṇḍavas and Kauravas, and the *vyāsa* ("divider") of the Vedas. He is counted a partial *avatāra* of Viṣṇu (Ādi Parva).
- **Gaṇeśa as scribe** - By tradition Vyāsa dictated the epic and **Gaṇeśa** agreed to write it on condition that Vyāsa never pause; Vyāsa countered that Gaṇeśa must understand each verse before writing it, and so composed deliberately knotty *kūṭa-ślokas* ("tangle-verses") to buy thinking-time. When Gaṇeśa's stylus broke he snapped off his own tusk to continue (Ādi Parva 1) — explaining his one-tusked (*ekadanta*) iconography.
  - *Variant [scholarly]:* The Gaṇeśa-scribe episode is **absent from the Critical Edition** and judged a later interpolation; the Bhandarkar editors relegated it to the apparatus.
- **The "fifth Veda" claim** - The text repeatedly positions itself as Veda accessible to all *varṇas* and to women, unlike the śruti (Ādi Parva; Bhīṣma Parva).

## The frame narrative — nested tellings

The epic is built as **stories within stories**, each with its own narrator and audience:

- **Outer frame — Ugraśravas Sauti → Śaunaka** - The bard **Ugraśravas "Sauti"** (son of Lomaharṣaṇa) arrives at the **Naimiṣa Forest**, where ṛṣis led by **Śaunaka** are performing a 12-year sacrifice; invited to tell wonders, he recites the whole Mahābhārata as he heard it at Janamejaya's snake-sacrifice (Ādi Parva, Section I).
- **Middle frame — Vaiśampāyana → Janamejaya** - At the **Sarpa Satra** ("snake sacrifice") of King **Janamejaya** (great-grandson of Arjuna), held to avenge his father Parīkṣit's death by the serpent Takṣaka, Vyāsa's pupil **Vaiśampāyana** narrates the epic at Vyāsa's bidding (Ādi Parva, Pauṣya/Pauloma/Āstīka sub-parvas).
- **Inner frame — Sañjaya → Dhṛtarāṣṭra** - The blind king **Dhṛtarāṣṭra** "watches" the war through his charioteer-bard **Sañjaya**, granted divine sight (*divya-dṛṣṭi*) by Vyāsa; Sañjaya's eyewitness report constitutes the six war-parvas (Bhīṣma–Śalya). The Bhagavad Gītā is itself reported *by Sañjaya* (Bhīṣma Parva).
- **Deeper embeddings** - Within these sit countless sub-tales (*upākhyānas*) — Nala-Damayantī, Sāvitrī, Śakuntalā, the Rāmopākhyāna (a Rāmāyaṇa-in-miniature), etc. — narrated to console or instruct the characters.

## The 18 Parvas (deep-dive files)

The epic is divided into **18 parvas** ("books"), themselves subdivided into ~100 *upaparvas* (sub-books) and ~2,000 chapters (*adhyāyas*). The number 18 deliberately mirrors the 18 *akṣauhiṇī* armies, the 18 days of war, and the 18 chapters of the Gītā.

- **[01 — Ādi Parva](01-adi-parva.md)** - "Book of the Beginning": the three frame narratives, origin of the epic, the lineage (Śantanu, Bhīṣma's vow, Vyāsa's birth), birth and childhood of the Pāṇḍavas & Kauravas, the lac-house (*lākṣāgṛha*), Draupadī's *svayaṃvara* and polyandrous marriage, founding of Indraprastha. (Includes the Āstīka, Śakuntalā sub-tales.)
- **[02 — Sabhā Parva](02-sabha-parva.md)** - "Book of the Assembly Hall": Maya Dānava builds the wondrous hall; Yudhiṣṭhira's *Rājasūya*; slaying of Jarāsandha & Śiśupāla; the rigged **game of dice** (Śakuni), loss of kingdom, the *vastraharaṇa* (disrobing of Draupadī, foiled by Kṛṣṇa), and the 13-year exile sentence.
- **[03 — Vana / Āraṇyaka Parva](03-vana-parva.md)** - "Book of the Forest" (one of the longest): 12 years of forest exile; Arjuna's penance and the *Pāśupatāstra* from Śiva; pilgrimage tales — **Nala-Damayantī, Sāvitrī, Rāmopākhyāna**; the Yakṣa-praśna at the lake (Yudhiṣṭhira's riddles).
- **[04 — Virāṭa Parva](04-virata-parva.md)** - "Book of Virāṭa": the 13th year in disguise at King Virāṭa's court (Yudhiṣṭhira as Kaṅka, Bhīma as cook, Arjuna as the eunuch Bṛhannalā, Draupadī as Sairandhrī); Kīcaka's death; the cattle-raid and Arjuna's revelation.
- **[05 — Udyoga Parva](05-udyoga-parva.md)** - "Book of the Effort": failed peace embassies; **Kṛṣṇa's peace mission** rejected by Duryodhana; the choosing of Kṛṣṇa's army vs. Kṛṣṇa-unarmed; Karṇa's parentage revealed; the *Vidura-nīti* and *Sanatsujātīya* didactic sections; both sides muster.
- **[06 — Bhīṣma Parva](06-bhishma-parva.md)** - "Book of Bhīṣma": opens with the **[Bhagavad Gītā](../bhagavad-gita.md)**; first 10 days of war under commander Bhīṣma; his fall on the bed of arrows (*śara-śayyā*) via Śikhaṇḍin.
- **[07 — Droṇa Parva](07-drona-parva.md)** - "Book of Droṇa": days 11–15 under Droṇa; the *cakra-vyūha* and death of **Abhimanyu**; Jayadratha slain; the deception "Aśvatthāmā is dead" and Droṇa's beheading by Dhṛṣṭadyumna; the Nārāyaṇāstra.
- **[08 — Karṇa Parva](08-karna-parva.md)** - "Book of Karṇa": days 16–17 under Karṇa; his duel with Arjuna; chariot wheel sinking; **Karṇa slain** by Arjuna.
- **[09 — Śalya Parva](09-shalya-parva.md)** - "Book of Śalya": day 18 under Śalya; the **mace duel** between Bhīma and Duryodhana (thigh-strike below the belt); Duryodhana mortally wounded — war effectively ends.
- **[10 — Sauptika Parva](10-sauptika-parva.md)** - "Book of the Sleeping (Night-attack)": **Aśvatthāmā, Kṛpa & Kṛtavarmā** raid the sleeping Pāṇḍava camp, slaughtering Dhṛṣṭadyumna, Śikhaṇḍin and the five Upapāṇḍavas; the **Brahmaśiras** weapon duel; Aśvatthāmā cursed to wander.
- **[11 — Strī Parva](11-stri-parva.md)** - "Book of the Women": **Gāndhārī, Kuntī** and the widows lament over the dead; Gāndhārī curses Kṛṣṇa (foreshadowing the Yādava ruin); funeral rites.
- **[12 — Śānti Parva](12-shanti-parva.md)** - "Book of Peace" (the **longest** book): Yudhiṣṭhira crowned; the dying **Bhīṣma's discourse** on *rāja-dharma*, *āpad-dharma* and *mokṣa-dharma* — a vast treatise on kingship, ethics and liberation.
- **[13 — Anuśāsana Parva](13-anushasana-parva.md)** - "Book of Instruction": Bhīṣma's continued teaching on *dāna* (giving), duties, the **Viṣṇu Sahasranāma**, and his final passing (*icchā-mṛtyu*) at the winter solstice.
- **[14 — Āśvamedhika Parva](14-ashvamedhika-parva.md)** - "Book of the Horse-Sacrifice": Yudhiṣṭhira's **Aśvamedha**; Arjuna's world-circuit following the horse (incl. battle with his son Babhruvāhana); the **Anu-Gītā**; birth/revival of Parīkṣit.
- **[15 — Āśramavāsika Parva](15-ashramavasika-parva.md)** - "Book of the Hermitage": Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Gāndhārī and Kuntī retire to the forest and **perish in a wildfire**; Vidura's death.
- **[16 — Mausala Parva](16-mausala-parva.md)** - "Book of the Clubs": the **Yādava clan self-destructs** in a drunken brawl at Prabhāsa (Gāndhārī's curse fulfilled); Kṛṣṇa shot in the heel by the hunter Jara and departs; Dvārakā sinks beneath the sea — the formal **end of the Dvāpara Yuga**.
- **[17 — Mahāprasthānika Parva](17-mahaprasthanika-parva.md)** - "Book of the Great Departure": the Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī make their final pilgrimage toward the Himālaya/Meru; all fall on the way except Yudhiṣṭhira and the faithful dog (Dharma in disguise).
- **[18 — Svargārohaṇa Parva](18-svargarohana-parva.md)** - "Book of the Ascent to Heaven": Yudhiṣṭhira's final test (refusing heaven without his dog/brothers), the vision of hell and heaven, and the family's reunion in the celestial realm.

> **Harivaṃśa (appendix / *khila*)** - A "supplement" of ~16,000 verses treating the genealogy of Hari (Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa), creation accounts, and especially **Kṛṣṇa's birth and childhood** (material the epic proper omits) — see [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](../krishna-lila.md). Counted as the last 2 of the 100 upaparvas and traditionally regarded as part of the Mahābhārata corpus though formally outside the 18 parvas. (Critical Edition of the Harivaṃśa: P. L. Vaidya, 1969–71.)

## Recensions & the Critical Edition

The Mahābhārata survives in **over 1,200 manuscripts** in many scripts, falling into two broad recensions:

- **Northern recension** - Includes the **Śāradā (Kashmiri)**, Nepālī, Maithilī, Bengali and Devanāgarī sub-versions; the Devanāgarī "vulgate" (with **Nīlakaṇṭha's** 17th-c. commentary) is the basis of most popular printed editions (~100,000 verses).
- **Southern recension (Dākṣiṇātya)** - In **Grantha, Telugu, Malayāḷam, Kannaḍa** scripts; generally fuller and more systematically ordered than the Northern, with additional episodes.
- **The Critical Edition (Pune / BORI)** - Prepared at the **Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute** (1919–1966), under **V. S. Sukthankar**, then S. K. Belvalkar, S. K. De, R. N. Dandekar and others, collating ~1,259 manuscripts to reconstruct the **"constituted text"** — a hypothesized common archetype. It runs to **~73,900 ślokas** (the *constituted* text; ~89,000+ incl. apparatus), in **19 volumes (22 books)**, deliberately *shorter* than the vulgate because demonstrably late accretions (e.g. the Gaṇeśa-scribe story) are demoted to the apparatus.

## The layered growth of the text [scholarly]

- **Jaya ("Victory")** - A hypothesized core of **~8,800 verses** focused narrowly on the war and the Pāṇḍavas' triumph.
- **Bhārata** - Expanded to **~24,000 verses** recounting the full Bhārata-dynasty history (the form Vaiśampāyana recites).
- **Mahābhārata** - Swollen to **~100,000 verses** with the great didactic and philosophical strata (Śānti, Anuśāsana, the Gītā, the *upākhyānas*) — the encyclopaedic "fifth Veda."

## Dating — tradition vs. scholarship

- *Traditional (scriptural):* The Kurukṣetra War falls at the junction of **Dvāpara and Kali Yugas**; Kali begins with Kṛṣṇa's departure, conventionally **3102 BCE** (Āryabhaṭa / Sūrya Siddhānta reckoning). By the [yuga framework](../../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md) Dharma stands on **2 legs** in Dvāpara, collapsing to 1 in Kali — see [Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and [recorded history](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md).
  - *Variant (traditional astronomical):* other epic-internal eclipse/planetary readings have been used to argue dates such as ~3138 BCE for the war [disputed].
- *Academic (scholarly):* The text grew over centuries — oldest layers **~400 BCE**, the bulk compiled **c. 3rd century BCE – 3rd/4th century CE**, with the framing and didactic books latest. The constituted archetype is dated by some editors to **c. 300 CE** [scholarly].

---

## Why so many variants

- *Accretion, not single authorship:* the epic explicitly invites continuation; its "encyclopaedic" self-image (*"what is not here is nowhere"*) drew in independent tracts (Gītā, the *nīti* texts, the Sahasranāma, the Anu-Gītā).
- *Regional & sectarian reframing:* Southern manuscripts add episodes; later vernacular retellings (Telugu Tikkana/Nannaya/Errapragada *Andhra Mahābhāratamu*, Tamil *Villipāratam*, Bengali, Malayāḷam, the Indonesian *Kakawin* parvas, etc.) localize and re-color the story [folk tradition].
- *Bhakti elevation of Kṛṣṇa:* the Harivaṃśa and Gītā strata transform Kṛṣṇa from ally-king into supreme deity — paralleling the Viṣṇu-avatāra layering in the [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md). See [Daśāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
