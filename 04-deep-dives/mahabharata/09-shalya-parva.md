# Śalya Parva — The Book of Shalya

> Cosmic address: [Kali Yuga](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) → the Mahābhārata war at the Dvāpara–Kali junction (war traditionally ~3138 BCE; Kali begins 3102 BCE).
> Parent: [Mahābhārata deep-dives](./README.md) · Previous: [08-Karṇa Parva](./08-karna-parva.md) · Next: [10-Sauptika Parva](./10-sauptika-parva.md)
> The ninth of the eighteen parvas. It covers **Day 18** of Kurukṣetra — the last day of open battle — culminating in the mace-duel that ends Duryodhana and effectively ends the war.

The Śalya Parva (4 sub-parvas; ~64–65 adhyāyas in the Critical/vulgate editions) recites the death of Śalya, the fall of Śakuni, Duryodhana's flight into the lake, and the great gadā-yuddha (mace duel) between Bhīma and Duryodhana. See the [Suryavaṃśa & Chandravaṃśa lineages](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) for the combatants' descent, and the [Mahābhārata corpus entry](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md).

## Structure — the four sub-parvas (upa-parvas)

- **Śalya-vadha Parva** - appointment and death of Śalya; collapse of Kaurava resistance on Day 18 (Mbh IX, adhy. ~1–22).
- **Hrada-praveśa Parva** - "Entering the Lake": the rout of the survivors; Duryodhana hides under the waters of Lake Dvaipāyana (Mbh IX, ~28–29).
- **Gadā-yuddha Parva** - "The Mace-Duel": Balarāma's return, the duel itself, the foul thigh-blow, Duryodhana mortally wounded (Mbh IX, ~30–65).
- *Note (numbering variant):* editions differ — the vulgate (Nīlakaṇṭha) counts 65 adhyāyas; the Pune Critical Edition counts 64. Some tables of contents list **Śalya Parva** and **Saraswata/Tīrtha-yātrā Parva** as further subdivisions inside the above. (Wikipedia; Vyāsa Online)

## Day 18 — Śalya as Commander and his death

- **Śalya made fourth commander-in-chief** - After Karṇa's death (end of [Karṇa Parva](./08-karna-parva.md)), Duryodhana appoints Śalya, king of Madra and maternal uncle of Nakula–Sahadeva, as the fourth and final daytime senāpati of the Kaurava host. He accepts out of kṣatriya duty despite kinship with the Pāṇḍavas (Mbh, Śalya-vadha P.).
- **Śalya's morning rampage** - Śalya fights with great fury, scattering the Pāṇḍava ranks and wounding Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma, Sātyaki and the twins; he is hailed as fighting "like Yama himself" (Mbh IX).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's vow to kill Śalya** - Kṛṣṇa had earlier told Yudhiṣṭhira that Śalya was the one fated to fall to him; Yudhiṣṭhira, normally restrained, takes the foremost role (Mbh, Karṇa/Śalya P.). This is among Yudhiṣṭhira's rare personal kills.
- **Death of Śalya** - At midday Yudhiṣṭhira hurls a great gold-decked **dart/spear (śakti)** consecrated with mantras; it pierces Śalya's chest and he falls dead, "like a banner-staff sacred to Indra let go" (Mbh IX, Śalya-vadha P.).
  - *Variant (weapon):* most retellings and the Sanskrit text describe a hurled **śakti (dart/javelin)**; some popular summaries loosely say "in fierce battle" or attribute the killing blow to arrows. The canonical instrument is the dart. (Wikipedia; Hindu-Blog; draupadiparashakti)
  - *Detail:* Śalya's younger brother and son also fall soon after; Madra's contingent is broken.

## Collapse of the Kaurava army

- **Death of Śakuni** - **Sahadeva** confronts Śakuni Saubala, the dice-master architect of the war. After an arrow-duel Sahadeva severs Śakuni's head (some passages: cleaves his body) — fulfilling Sahadeva's own dice-hall vow (Mbh IX, Śalya-vadha P.).
  - *Detail:* Śakuni's son **Ulūka** is also slain in the same engagement (by Sahadeva). (Wikipedia)
  - *Variant (agent):* the slaying of Śakuni is universally Sahadeva's; this is one of the few vows in the epic whose assigned slayer never wavers across recensions.
- **Duḥśāsana already dead** - (Slain by Bhīma in the [Karṇa Parva](./08-karna-parva.md), blood-drinking vow.) By the close of Day 18 nearly all hundred Kaurava brothers have perished.
- **The rout and the surviving four** - Of the eleven akṣauhiṇīs Duryodhana once commanded, after Day 18 only a handful of the Kaurava side survive: **Duryodhana** (soon mortally wounded), **Aśvatthāman**, **Kṛpa** and **Kṛtavarman** (Mbh IX, Hrada-praveśa P.).
  - *Detail:* On the Pāṇḍava side the **seven survivors** are the five Pāṇḍavas plus Kṛṣṇa and Sātyaki (the count of war-survivors is usually given as these plus Yuyutsu = the standard "18 survivors of the war" tally is reached across later events).

## Hrada-praveśa — Duryodhana hides in the lake

- **Duryodhana flees alone** - With his army destroyed, Duryodhana takes up his mace and walks/flees from the field toward **Lake Dvaipāyana (Dvaipāyana-hrada)** (Mbh IX, Hrada-praveśa P.).
- **Stambhana — solidifying the waters** - By his yogic/māyā power he congeals the water and enters the lake, lying hidden beneath the surface to rest and regather strength (Mbh IX). This is the episode that names the sub-parva.
  - *Variant (means):* the text frames it as a magical "stambhana" (freezing/solidifying) of water by Duryodhana; some retellings rationalize it merely as Duryodhana submerging and holding his breath. The scriptural version is the supernatural solidification.
- **The Pāṇḍavas track him down** - Hunters/spies and then the Pāṇḍavas with Kṛṣṇa locate him. Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma and the others stand at the lake's edge (Mbh IX).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's taunt and challenge** - Yudhiṣṭhira calls Duryodhana out, mocking him for hiding after causing such slaughter, and offers a single condition: **Duryodhana may choose any one Pāṇḍava and any weapon for single combat**, and if he wins he keeps the kingdom (Mbh IX, Gadā-yuddha P.).
  - *Kṛṣṇa's rebuke [scholarly point of tension]:* Kṛṣṇa is dismayed at Yudhiṣṭhira's reckless wager — Duryodhana, fresh and rested and the finest mace-fighter alive, could have killed any of them. The narrative treats Yudhiṣṭhira's offer as imprudent (Mbh IX).
- **Duryodhana rises** - Stung by the taunts, Duryodhana emerges from the water, accepts single combat with the **mace (gadā)**, and naturally chooses **Bhīma** as his opponent — the long-promised rematch (Mbh IX).

## Balarāma's return — the Saraswatī pilgrimage frame

- **Balarāma's neutrality** - Both Bhīma and Duryodhana were Balarāma's mace-students; refusing to fight against either kinsman, Balarāma had left on a **tīrtha-yātrā (pilgrimage) along the Saraswatī River** for the war's duration (recounted in the Saraswata/Tīrtha section within Śalya Parva).
- **Named tīrthas on the route** - The pilgrimage catalogues sacred fords along the (now partly dried) Saraswatī, e.g. **Prabhāsa, Camasodbheda, Udapāna/Vinaśana** (where the river vanishes), **Pṛthūdaka (Pehowa), Plakṣa-prasravaṇa**, and tīrthas named for Indra, Rāma, Āditya and others — a major source of Saraswatī sacred geography (Mbh IX, Saraswata P.). See [sacred-geography](../../06-sacred-geography/README.md).
- **Timely arrival** - Balarāma returns to Kurukṣetra exactly in time to witness his two pupils' final duel and presides as the senior referee (Mbh IX, Gadā-yuddha P.).

## The Gadā-yuddha — Bhīma vs Duryodhana

- **The two champions** - Both are supreme mace-warriors; Duryodhana is technically the more skilled (better trained, fresher), Bhīma the stronger. A genuine, evenly matched single combat ensues, watched by both armies, Kṛṣṇa, and Balarāma (Mbh IX, Gadā-yuddha P.).
- **The maṇḍala-display** - The duel opens with the formal circling and the display of mace-postures/movements (the named gada techniques — āyasa, the maṇḍala wheelings, leaps and feints) before they close (Mbh IX).
- **A long, indecisive contest** - They strike sparks, wound each other, knock one another down and rise; for a long time neither can win by fair technique — Duryodhana's skill matches Bhīma's force (Mbh IX).
- **Kṛṣṇa's prompt to Bhīma** - Seeing Bhīma tiring and that Duryodhana cannot be beaten fairly, **Kṛṣṇa reminds Bhīma of his vow** and signals by **slapping his own thigh** — the cue that Bhīma must break Duryodhana's thigh, where he is vulnerable (Mbh IX, Gadā-yuddha P.).
  - *Background — Bhīma's two dice-hall vows:* (1) to drink Duḥśāsana's blood [fulfilled, Karṇa P.]; (2) **to break the thigh(s) of Duryodhana**, the thigh he had bared and slapped to insult [Draupadī](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) in the gambling hall (Sabhā Parva). The thigh-blow is the payment of this oath.
- **The foul blow — breaking the thighs** - Bhīma feints, leaps, and brings his mace down **below the navel on Duryodhana's thighs**, shattering them — a stroke forbidden by the rules of fair mace-combat (a blow below the waist is adharmic) (Mbh IX, Gadā-yuddha P.).
  - *Variant (one thigh or both):* the Sanskrit and most translations say Bhīma broke **both thighs**; the popular phrasing "the thigh" derives from Bhīma's vow naming the specific thigh Duryodhana bared. (Ganguli tr.; Wikipedia)
- **Bhīma's insult to the fallen king** - Bhīma kicks Duryodhana's head/crown with his foot and reproaches him for Draupadī's disrobing and the wax-house — a much-noted breach of decorum toward a fallen warrior (Mbh IX).
  - *Variant (kick):* present in the vulgate; some retellings soften or omit it.

## Aftermath at the lake — wrath, justification, the dying curse

- **Balarāma's fury** - Balarāma is enraged at the illegal thigh-blow, declaring Bhīma a foul fighter; he raises his plough-weapon to attack Bhīma and has to be physically restrained/pacified by **Kṛṣṇa** (Mbh IX, Gadā-yuddha P.).
  - *Kṛṣṇa's justification:* Kṛṣṇa argues the blow was just retribution for Duryodhana's own adharma (the dice-game, the bared thigh, the attempts to kill the Pāṇḍavas) and that Bhīma was bound by his sacred vow; the age (Kali approaching) and the weight of Duryodhana's sins warranted it. Balarāma, unconvinced of its propriety but unable to overturn it, leaves declaring Bhīma will be remembered as a "crooked fighter" and Duryodhana as one who fought fair. (Mbh IX)
- **Duryodhana's accusation of treachery** - The mortally wounded Duryodhana reviles Kṛṣṇa, listing the **unfair means** by which the Pāṇḍavas won every major kill: Bhīṣma felled behind Śikhaṇḍin, Droṇa by the lie of "Aśvatthāman is dead," Karṇa shot while lifting his chariot wheel, Bhūriśravas slain while seated in prāya, and now himself by a foul blow (Mbh IX).
  - *Kṛṣṇa's reply:* Kṛṣṇa answers that these warriors could never have been defeated by fair means alone, and that each fall was a consequence of the Kauravas' own greater crimes; he reminds Duryodhana of his cumulative adharma. (Mbh IX)
- **Heavenly honour for Duryodhana** - As Duryodhana speaks, the gods rain flowers and gandharvas sing — celestial honours for a kṣatriya dying on the field, which shames the Pāṇḍavas momentarily (Mbh IX).
- **Duryodhana left to die slowly** - The Pāṇḍavas depart; Duryodhana is left mortally wounded but **still alive** at the lake — he survives into the night to appoint **Aśvatthāman** as the last commander, setting up the night massacre of the [Sauptika Parva](./10-sauptika-parva.md) (Mbh IX → X).

## Significance & cross-links

- This parva marks the **effective end of the war**: with Duryodhana fallen, organized battle ceases. What follows is the nocturnal revenge-raid ([Sauptika Parva](./10-sauptika-parva.md)).
- It crystallizes the epic's central moral problem — **dharma achieved through adharmic means** — debated through Balarāma's wrath and Kṛṣṇa's apologetics; a touchstone for [karma and dharma](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/karma-samsara-moksha.md) discussions.
- The Saraswatī tīrtha-yātrā embedded here is a primary scriptural source for [sacred river geography](../../06-sacred-geography/README.md).
- Era: events sit at the Dvāpara–Kali junction; see [Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and [Kali Yuga / recorded history](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/recorded-history.md).
