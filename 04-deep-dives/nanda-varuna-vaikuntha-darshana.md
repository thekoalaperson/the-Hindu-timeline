# Nanda seized by Varuṇa — Krishna in Varuṇa-loka and the vision of Vaikuṇṭha

> *Where this sits: a quiet Vṛndāvana līlā of [Kṛṣṇa](./krishna-lila.md) told in **[Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md) 10.28**, set in the closing [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga. It sits between the [Govardhana-dhāraṇa](./govardhana-dharana.md) (10.24–27) and the [Rāsa-līlā](./rasa-lila.md) (10.29–33) — the still, revelatory counterpart to the loud defeat of Indra.*
>
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Up to: [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md) · Companions: [Varuṇa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/varuna.md) · [Bhāgavata Tales Catalog](./bhagavata-tales-catalog.md) · [Ekādaśī Māhātmyas](./ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md)

The episode of **Nanda's rescue from Varuṇa** (SB 10.28: *"Kṛṣṇa Rescues Nanda Mahārāja from the Abode of Varuṇa"*) is a short, seventeen-verse chapter of the tenth canto's Vṛndāvana cycle. Its two joined halves make it structurally important out of all proportion to its length. In the first half **Nanda**, Kṛṣṇa's foster-father, is carried off to the undersea court of **Varuṇa**, lord of the waters, for a ritual lapse — bathing at a forbidden hour — and Kṛṣṇa descends to fetch him, only to be **worshipped** by the sea-god as the Supreme Person. In the second half the cowherds, hearing Nanda's astonished report of the honour paid to their boy, silently wonder whether Kṛṣṇa can grant them his own world; and Kṛṣṇa, reading their hearts, dips them in the **Brahma-hrada** and shows them the **effulgent abode beyond material darkness (Vaikuṇṭha / Brahman)**, where he is praised by the personified Vedas. It is the deliberate mirror-image of [Govardhana](./govardhana-dharana.md): there a god is humbled by force, here a god bows in love; there the cowherds are sheltered *under* a mountain, here they are lifted *into* the spiritual sky.

---

## Placement in the tenth-canto sequence

- **Immediately after the Govardhana cycle (10.24–27)** — In the received order of the Bhāgavata, Kṛṣṇa's public humbling of Indra and his consecration as *Govinda* (10.27) is followed at once by this private, tender episode. Where the Govardhana story ends with a god (Indra) and the wish-cow Surabhi anointing Kṛṣṇa under compulsion of defeat, the Varuṇa story opens with a god *volunteering* worship — the same theological point (Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme) reached by the opposite mood (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.27–28).
- **Immediately before the Rāsa-līlā (10.29–33)** — The vision of Vaikuṇṭha at the Brahma-hrada is the last thing that happens before the autumn-moon [Rāsa-dance](./rasa-lila.md). Commentators read 10.28 as the necessary "credential-check": having confirmed (through Nanda's testimony and their own darśana) that Kṛṣṇa is God, the Vraja-folk are ready for the supreme intimacy of the rāsa (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28–29; Gauḍīya commentarial reading). [scholarly]
- **Absent from the older Krishna biographies** — The episode is a Bhāgavata development; the **Harivaṃśa** and **Viṣṇu Purāṇa Book 5**, which carry the Govardhana and Kāliya tales, do **not** record Nanda's abduction by Varuṇa or the Brahma-hrada vision as a discrete episode. This marks 10.28 as part of the Bhāgavata's characteristic layering of *aiśvarya* (majesty-revealing) proofs onto the pastoral līlā. [scholarly]

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic frame** — The event falls in the boyhood of Kṛṣṇa at **Vṛndāvana**, in the closing years of the [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of the **28th Mahāyuga** of the present **[Vaivasvata (7th) Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)** — traditionally just before the onset of Kali Yuga in **3102 BCE** (Kṛṣṇa's departure being its marker).
- **Academic / historical frame** — The **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** in its extant form is a mature composition generally dated **[scholarly] c. 9th–10th century CE** (South India). The Varuṇa-and-Vaikuṇṭha episode belongs to this developed devotional stratum, not to the archaic Vedic Varuṇa material. Keep the two frames distinct: the *event* is Dvāpara, the *text* is early-medieval.

## Part I — Nanda seized by Varuṇa (SB 10.28.1–10)

- **The Ekādaśī–Dvādaśī vow** — Having worshipped **Janārdana (Viṣṇu)** and fasted on the **Ekādaśī** (eleventh lunar day), Nanda broke the fast on the **Dvādaśī** (twelfth day) and, still in the dark of the last watch of night, entered the water of the **Kālindī (Yamunā)** to bathe as part of the observance (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.1). The setting is thus a specifically **Vaiṣṇava-vrata** dawn, the same *brāhma-muhūrta* window prized for such rites. [Ekādaśī-vrata context: [Ekādaśī Māhātmyas](./ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md).]
- **The forbidden hour** — Because Nanda entered the water during an **inauspicious (asura-natured) portion of the night**, disregarding the impropriety of the time, he committed a technical breach of the water-lord's domain (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.1). The offence is ritual, not moral — a lapse of *muhūrta*, not of intent.
- **The servant of Varuṇa** — A **servant of Varuṇa** — the text calls him a *puruṣa* of **demoniac (asura)** character, acting **out of ignorance** — seized Nanda and carried him down to his master's court, presenting him as one who had bathed at a proscribed time (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.2).
  - *Note on the seizer:* the Bhāgavata makes the servant, not Varuṇa himself, the offender; Varuṇa is throughout portrayed as blameless and devout. The abduction is thus the fault of a subordinate's zeal, setting up Varuṇa's apology. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.2, 10.28.8)
- **The cowherds' cry** — The cowherds, finding Nanda gone, cried out **"Kṛṣṇa! Rāma!"** in alarm. Kṛṣṇa, the almighty Lord "who makes his devotees fearless," understood at once that his father had been taken to **Varuṇa** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.3–4).
- **Descent to Varuṇa-loka** — Kṛṣṇa went to the **abode of Varuṇa** (the watery world, *Varuṇa-loka*) to recover Nanda (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.4). The motif inverts the ordinary Purāṇic hierarchy: a *loka*-guardian (a *lokapāla*, one of the eight world-protectors) does not summon the boy; the boy comes down into the guardian's own realm.
- **Varuṇa's rapture and worship** — Seeing the Lord arrive, **Varuṇa was overwhelmed with joy** and worshipped him with elaborate honour. He declared that today his birth and his very body were successful, that his existence had borne fruit; "those who take shelter at your feet reach the end of all journeys," he said, and offered obeisance to Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Soul, transcendental to the *guṇas* (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.5–7).
- **The apology** — Varuṇa begged forgiveness: his servant, "a fool, ignorant of his duty," had seized Nanda in error; he prayed Kṛṣṇa to be gracious and to reclaim his father. Varuṇa's whole speech is a hymn of **śaraṇāgati from a god** — the water-lord, himself a master of an entire *loka*, confessing utter dependence (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.8–9).
- **The return** — Kṛṣṇa took Nanda and returned to Vraja, where his kinsmen, who had been anxious, rejoiced (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.10).

## Part II — The vision of Vaikuṇṭha at the Brahma-hrada (SB 10.28.11–17)

- **Nanda's astonished report** — Back among the cowherds, Nanda described the **great opulence and reverence** he had witnessed — how Varuṇa, a *lokapāla*, had personally worshipped his son. The Vraja-folk were struck with wonder (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.11).
- **The silent wish of the cowherds** — Hearing this, the cowherds began to think that **Kṛṣṇa might be able to grant them the supreme destination** — his own abode. Their longing is unspoken; the Bhāgavata makes their unvoiced desire the trigger for what follows (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.12).
- **Kṛṣṇa's compassion** — The Lord, **knowing the hearts of all**, perceived that his cowherd-devotees, "bewildered by the pursuit of ordinary life," yearned to see his world. Out of compassion he resolved to reveal it to them (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.13).
- **The Brahma-hrada** — Kṛṣṇa led them to a pool of the Yamunā, the **Brahma-hrada** ("the lake of Brahman"), and had them **bathe / dip beneath the water**; drawing them up again, he showed them the transcendental realm. The Bhāgavata specially notes this is the **same lake where Akrūra** — later, on his journey with Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma to Mathurā — **would behold his own vision of the Absolute** (SB 10.39). The Brahma-hrada is thus a fixed *tīrtha* of revelation in the tenth canto (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.14–15; cross-ref 10.39).
- **The abode beyond darkness** — The cowherds beheld **Brahman / Brahmaloka — the effulgent spiritual sky beyond material darkness (tamas)**, "realized by great sages in mystic trance," infinite, self-luminous, pure consciousness, unmodified and imperishable (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.15–16).
  - *Reading (Gauḍīya / Vaiṣṇava commentators):* Śrīdhara Svāmī and Viśvanātha Cakravartī gloss the revealed realm as Kṛṣṇa's **own eternal abode — Vaikuṇṭha / the para-vyoma**, not a merely impersonal effulgence; the presence of the personified Vedas offering personal prayers (below) is adduced as proof that a *personal* transcendental world, not featureless Brahman, is shown. [scholarly]
  - *Reading (Advaitic / literal):* the verse's own vocabulary — *satyaṃ jñānam anantam*, beyond destruction and change — reads as the impersonal **Brahman** realized in *samādhi*; the two readings are catalogued, not merged. [disputed]
- **Kṛṣṇa praised by the personified Vedas** — Within that realm the cowherds were **especially amazed to see Kṛṣṇa himself present there**, surrounded by the **personified Vedas (Śrutis)** offering him hymns of praise — the same *Veda-stuti* motif that recurs at the summit of the Bhāgavata (cf. the Śruti-gītā, SB 10.87). Nanda and the cowherds felt the **highest bliss (paramānanda)** at the sight (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.16–17).
- **The return to ordinary vision** — Kṛṣṇa then brought them back to Vraja; the vision closes as quietly as it opened, and the narrative moves directly into the Rāsa-līlā (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.28.17 → 10.29.1).

## The mirror to Govardhana: two proofs of divinity

- **Force versus love** — The [Govardhana-dhāraṇa](./govardhana-dharana.md) proves Kṛṣṇa's supremacy by **subduing** a proud god (Indra) who then submits under duress; the Varuṇa episode proves the same supremacy by a god who **submits willingly, in devotion**, without any contest. The Bhāgavata brackets the pair to teach that the Lord is Lord whether he is opposed or adored. [scholarly]
- **Shelter below versus shelter above** — At Govardhana the Vrajavāsīs are sheltered **beneath** a lifted mountain from Indra's flood — a rescue of the *body*. At the Brahma-hrada they are lifted **above** the material world into Vaikuṇṭha — a rescue of the *soul*, a foretaste of their final destination. The spatial inversion (down under the hill / up into the sky) is deliberate.
- **Public miracle versus private darśana** — Govardhana is a seven-day public spectacle witnessed by all Vraja and the gods; the Vaikuṇṭha-darśana is a silent, interior granting, given because of an *unspoken* wish. Together they frame the Bhāgavata's twin registers of *aiśvarya* (majesty) and *mādhurya* (sweetness). [scholarly]
- **Parallel to Gajendra** — Both episodes end with a devotee being shown / taken to **Vaikuṇṭha**; the [Gajendra Mokṣa](./gajendra-moksha.md) grants the abode to one surrendered soul at the point of death, while the Brahma-hrada grants a preview to the whole community of cowherds in life. The Bhāgavata repeatedly makes water — a lake, an ocean — the threshold of the vision of the Lord's world.

## Varuṇa: the Vedic sovereign turned devotee

- **From cosmic sovereign to lokapāla** — In the **Ṛgveda**, Varuṇa is the great *asura* of moral order (*ṛta*), the sky-sovereign who binds sinners with his noose; by the Purāṇic period he has been demoted to **god of waters and oceans**, one of the eight **lokapālas** (guardian of the **west**). SB 10.28 belongs to this later phase and dramatizes the demotion theologically: the once-supreme judge of the cosmos now bows to a cowherd boy. [scholarly] For the full arc see [Varuṇa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/varuna.md).
- **The devotional inversion** — The episode is one of the clearest Purāṇic statements that even a *lokapāla* is a *jīva* — a dependent soul — before the Supreme. Varuṇa's confession ("my life is now successful") is the model for the god-as-devotee, a stock figure the Bhāgavata uses repeatedly to relativize the entire Vedic pantheon beneath Kṛṣṇa. [scholarly]
- **Not the same as Varuṇa's other Purāṇic roles** — This Varuṇa is the water-lord of the *lokapāla* system; do not conflate the episode with the Vedic Varuṇa's binding-and-releasing of sinners, nor with his appearances in the Rāmāyaṇa (the *setu*-crossing) or the Mahābhārata (gifting weapons). The Bhāgavata here uses only his identity as sovereign of the waters into which Nanda strayed.

## Ritual and devotional afterlife

- **Ekādaśī / Kārttika observance** — Because the frame is an Ekādaśī–Dvādaśī vow broken by a pre-dawn Yamunā bath, the episode is cited in **Vaiṣṇava vrata literature** to underline both the merit of the observance and the danger of bathing at an improper *muhūrta* — the very act that triggered Nanda's seizure. [regional / liturgical]
- **The Brahma-hrada as tīrtha** — Vraja-maṇḍala pilgrimage tradition identifies a **Brahma-hrada / Brahma-kuṇḍa** as the site of this vision (and of Akrūra's), incorporating it into the Braj-*parikramā* circuit. [regional] [folk]
- **A minor episode, a major proof** — In liturgical reckoning the Varuṇa episode is rarely recited on its own (unlike the Gajendra-stuti or the Gopī-gītā), but commentators weight it heavily as one of the Bhāgavata's compact "Kṛṣṇa-is-the-Supreme" demonstrations, cited alongside the vision of the universe in Kṛṣṇa's mouth and the Brahma-vimohana (the theft of the calves and boys, SB 10.13). See [Brahma-vimohana](./brahma-vimohana.md) for the adjacent "confounding of Brahmā" proof.
