# Shankhachuda and Tulasi: The Conch-Demon and the Chastity Deception

> Cosmic address: a **chastity-deception war-myth** structurally twinned with [Jalandhara & Vṛndā](jalandhara-vrinda.md) but with its own canonical cast — the asura **Śaṅkhacūḍa** ("conch-crest"), a former Kṛṣṇa-attendant reborn as a daitya, made invincible by **Viṣṇu's Kavaca** and by the *pativratya* of his wife **Tulasī/Vṛndā**, undone when Viṣṇu impersonates him to break her vow, then slain by **Śiva**. In the *Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa* it is **Śaṅkhacūḍa — not Jalandhara —** who is the canonical demon of the **Tulasī** cycle, the origin of the **śālagrāma–tulasī** pairing, the **Tulsī-Vivāha** festival, and the **conch (śaṅkha)** used in Viṣṇu-worship.
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · See also: [Jalandhara & Vṛndā](jalandhara-vrinda.md) · [Asura Wars & Roster](../08-beings-and-bestiary/asura-wars-and-roster.md) · [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md)

The **Śaṅkhacūḍa–Tulasī** narrative is the *Brahma-Vaivarta* and *Devī Bhāgavata* form of the same paradox that animates the Jalandhara myth: an asura who **cannot be killed while his wife's chastity is intact**, so the gods win by *māyā*, not weapons. The two tales are close twins and are often conflated — but the major texts keep them distinct in *cast and provenance*. Where the *Śiva Purāṇa* anchors the cycle on **Jalandhara** (born of Śiva's brow-fire) and his wife **Vṛndā**, the **Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa (Prakṛti-khaṇḍa)** anchors it on **Śaṅkhacūḍa** and his wife **Tulasī**, giving a fuller Vaiṣṇava/Śākta cosmology of prior births and salvation. All variants are catalogued below; the traditions are kept separate, not merged.

---

## A. Prior births — Sudāmā and Tulasī

- **Śaṅkhacūḍa as the cursed Sudāmā** — In the *Brahma-Vaivarta*, the demon is the rebirth of **Sudāmā**, a *gopa*-attendant of **Kṛṣṇa** in Goloka, cursed by **Rādhā** (for an offence in the divine pleasance) to be born as an asura. He becomes **Śaṅkhacūḍa**, son of the daitya **Dambha**, who won him by long penance to Viṣṇu. (*Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa*).
- **Tulasī as the cursed apsaras / Goloka maiden** — His wife **Tulasī** is in her prior birth a **Goloka maiden** (or in some tellings the daughter of **Dharmadhvaja** / a princess of penance), born on earth and herself bearing a destiny to wed Viṣṇu — her devotion having been pledged to Nārāyaṇa. The two cursed beings are fated to marry on earth. (*Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa*). [sectarian]
  - *Variant (Tulasī = Vṛndā):* The *Brahma-Vaivarta* uses **Tulasī** as the proper name and treats **Vṛndā** of the Śaiva myth as the same goddess under another body — making this text the bridge that fuses the two cycles into one Tulasī-theology. [regional / sectarian]

---

## B. The marriage and the double armor

- **Marriage by gandharva rite** — Śaṅkhacūḍa weds **Tulasī**; their union is sanctioned (in the text) by **Brahmā**, who foretells both their greatness and Tulasī's eventual divinity. Her **unbroken fidelity** becomes the **invisible armor** that makes her husband unkillable. (*Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa*).
- **The Viṣṇu-Kavaca and the conch** — Beyond Tulasī's chastity, Śaṅkhacūḍa is protected by the **Śrī-Kṛṣṇa-Kavaca** (the protective amulet/mantra given by Viṣṇu in reward for Dambha's penance) and carries on his person a brilliant **conch-jewel (śaṅkha)** — the emblem from which he takes his name. He becomes **king of the asuras**, conquers the three worlds, dispossesses the gods, and rules as a near-righteous sovereign. (*Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa*).
- **The gods dispossessed** — As with Jalandhara, the devas are driven from heaven and appeal first to **Brahmā**, then to **Śiva**, for relief. (*Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa*; *Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa*).

---

## C. Śiva's war and the twin lock of invincibility

- **The embassy and the war** — Brahmā and the gods bring the petition to **Śiva**, who agrees to destroy Śaṅkhacūḍa. A great battle follows; but Śiva's weapons fail to kill the demon while **two protections** hold: the **Viṣṇu-Kavaca** and **Tulasī's *pativratya***. (*Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa*).
- **Viṣṇu must undo his own boon** — Because it is Viṣṇu's own armor and a *pativratā*'s vow that shield the asura, only **Viṣṇu** can break the lock. The gods turn to him; the deception falls to him to perform — a structural mirror of the Jalandhara tale, where the morally costly stratagem is again Viṣṇu's. (*Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa*).
- **Viṣṇu obtains the Kavaca by a beggar's ruse** — In a step distinctive to this cycle, Viṣṇu approaches Śaṅkhacūḍa **disguised as an old brāhmaṇa mendicant** and begs the demon — true to his righteous nature, never refusing a brāhmaṇa — to **give away the Viṣṇu-Kavaca** as alms. The demon yields his amulet, stripping the first layer of protection. (*Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa*).

---

## D. The chastity deception — Viṣṇu impersonates Śaṅkhacūḍa

- **Viṣṇu takes Śaṅkhacūḍa's form** — While the demon is on the battlefield, Viṣṇu assumes his **exact form** and approaches **Tulasī** as her returning, living husband. Believing him her lord, she **receives/embraces him** — and in that instant her vow of sole-fidelity is broken; her *pativratya*-power lapses. (*Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa*; *Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Book IX*).
  - *Variant (degree of violation):* As in the twin myth, tellings range from a touch/embrace that nullifies the vow to fuller cohabitation; the more decorous Purāṇic versions stress that the **disguise and intent**, not consummation, break the *pativratya*. [disputed]
- **The simultaneous slaying** — At the very moment Tulasī's chastity fails — both layers of protection now gone — **Śiva** strikes down **Śaṅkhacūḍa** on the field with his trident (*triśūla*), reducing the demon's body to ash. (*Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa*).
  - *Variant (the weapon):* Where the Śaiva *Jalandhara* telling uses a **cakra struck from Śiva's toe**, the *Brahma-Vaivarta* Śaṅkhacūḍa telling has Śiva slay him with the **triśūla**; the demon's bones are said to become the conches (*śaṅkha*) of the world. [regional]

---

## E. The conch-jewel passes to Krishna / Vishnu

- **Origin of the worship-conch** — From the slain Śaṅkhacūḍa's body comes the **śaṅkha (conch)**: the *Brahma-Vaivarta* makes this episode the **etiology of the ritual conch** used in Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa worship — water poured from a conch is sacred to Viṣṇu **because** it is the demon's transformed substance, while (by the same logic) it is *forbidden* in the worship of Śiva, Sūrya, and Gaṇeśa, who slew or are unconnected to him. (*Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa*).
- **The conch-jewel and Kṛṣṇa** — The brilliant **conch-jewel** that named Śaṅkhacūḍa passes, in the Vaiṣṇava reading, into the keeping of **Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa** — folding the asura's defeated emblem into the symbolic regalia of the very god (Kṛṣṇa, whose conch is **Pāñcajanya**) from whose retinue the demon had once fallen as Sudāmā. The cycle thus closes back on [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md): the cursed *gopa* returns, transformed, to his lord. [scholarly]
  - *Note (vs. Pāñcajanya):* The Bhāgavata's own conch-demon is **Pañcajana** (slain by Kṛṣṇa, his shell becoming Pāñcajanya); the *Brahma-Vaivarta* Śaṅkhacūḍa is a **distinct** figure, though the two conch-demon motifs resonate. [disputed]

---

## F. Tulasī's curse, death, and the basil-plant

- **Tulasī discovers the fraud and curses Viṣṇu** — When the disguise drops, Tulasī realizes she has lain with **Viṣṇu**, not her husband, and **curses him** to become a **stone (the śālagrāma)** and (in many versions) to be **heartless / separated from his wife**. Viṣṇu, contrite, accepts the curse and **blesses her in return**. (*Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa*; *Padma Purāṇa*).
- **Her body becomes the Gaṇḍakī; her hair the Tulasī plant** — Viṣṇu declares that **Tulasī** shall leave her mortal body and join him as the goddess **Tulasī/Lakṣmī-like consort**; her body becomes the **Gaṇḍakī river** (whose bed yields the śālagrāma stones), and her **hair** springs up as the sacred **Tulasī (holy basil)** plant, **dearest to Viṣṇu above all leaves and flowers**. (*Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa, Prakṛti-khaṇḍa*).
  - *Variant (single vs. twin etiology):* The *Padma Purāṇa* attaches the same basil-rebirth to **Vṛndā** (Jalandhara's wife); the *Brahma-Vaivarta* attaches it to **Tulasī** (Śaṅkhacūḍa's wife). The plant, the stone, and the curse are constant; **only the demon's name and provenance differ** between the two cycles. [scholarly]

## G. Charter myths and the śālagrāma–tulasī / Tulsī-Vivāha traditions

- **Tulsī-Vivāha** — This episode is, alongside the Jalandhara version, the charter for **Tulsī-Vivāha** — the ceremonial **marriage of the Tulasī plant to Viṣṇu (as śālagrāma or Kṛṣṇa)** on **Prabodhinī/Devotthāna Ekādaśī (Kārttika, śukla-pakṣa)**, closing *Cāturmāsya* and opening the wedding season. Tulasī's union with Viṣṇu — promised at her cursing — is ritually re-enacted. (Festival tradition, rooted in *Brahma-Vaivarta* & *Padma Purāṇa*).
- **The śālagrāma–tulasī pairing** — The myth grounds the inseparable **pairing of the tulasī-leaf with the śālagrāma-stone** in Viṣṇu-pūjā: by Tulasī's curse Viṣṇu *is* the stone, and by his boon she *is* the leaf, so their worship is eternally joint. (Ritual tradition.)
- **The conch in worship** — Uniquely, the Śaṅkhacūḍa cycle further licenses the **conch (śaṅkha)** as a Vaiṣṇava worship-vessel, and explains its **exclusion** from Śaiva/Saura/Gāṇapatya rites — a sectarian boundary-marker drawn from the demon's death. (*Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa*.) [sectarian]

## H. Theological significance and the "two demons" problem

- **Twin myths, distinct canon** — The Śaṅkhacūḍa–Tulasī and [Jalandhara–Vṛndā](jalandhara-vrinda.md) tales share a single plot-skeleton (invincible asura → chastity-lock → Viṣṇu's impersonation → Śiva's killing blow → wife reborn as Tulasī). They are **not the same story told twice of one demon**: the *Śiva Purāṇa* canon names **Jalandhara/Vṛndā**, the *Brahma-Vaivarta* canon names **Śaṅkhacūḍa/Tulasī**, with different births, weapons, and added motifs (the Kavaca-by-alms, the conch). The conflation is later/popular; the texts keep them apart. [scholarly]
- **Victory by deception, not force** — As with [Mohinī](samudra-manthana.md), Vāmana, and the Bhasmāsura tales, the asura is undone by **Viṣṇu's *māyā***; and as with Jalandhara, the casualty is an innocent *pativratā*, so the tradition registers the wrong via Tulasī's curse, which **rebounds on Viṣṇu** (his stone-form, his loss of a wife → the Rāma/Sītā resonance). [scholarly]
- **Salvation arc** — Distinctive to the *Brahma-Vaivarta* is the **redemptive frame**: Śaṅkhacūḍa is the **cursed Sudāmā** returning to Kṛṣṇa, and Tulasī ascends to permanent union with Viṣṇu — the war is, beneath the violence, a roundabout **homecoming** for two exiled Goloka beings. [sectarian]
- **Two clocks** — Scripturally the war belongs to **primordial / past-manvantara** asura-cosmology (its prior-birth frame even reaches into the Kṛṣṇa/Goloka mythos); historically it is undatable myth. But its **ritual residue** — Tulasī worship, the śālagrāma–tulasī pair, Tulsī-Vivāha, the conch's place in pūjā — is **living, observable practice**. The narrative clock and the festival clock stay distinct.

## Sources

**Primary scripture**
- *Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa*, **Prakṛti-khaṇḍa** — the fullest and **canonical** Śaṅkhacūḍa–Tulasī account: the prior births (Sudāmā cursed by Rādhā; Tulasī as a Goloka/Dharmadhvaja maiden), the marriage, the Viṣṇu-Kavaca and the conch, Śiva's war, Viṣṇu's begging away of the Kavaca and his impersonation of Śaṅkhacūḍa, Śiva's slaying with the triśūla, Tulasī's curse and rebirth (Gaṇḍakī river / śālagrāma; hair → tulasī plant), and the etiology of the **worship-conch**.
- *Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa*, **Book IX** — Śākta retelling of the Śaṅkhacūḍa war and of Tulasī as goddess; the chastity-lock and Viṣṇu's deception within a Devī-centred cosmology.
- *Padma Purāṇa* — the **tulasī/śālagrāma** etiology and **Tulsī-Vivāha** charter (attached in this text to **Vṛndā**), the parallel that the *Brahma-Vaivarta* re-anchors on **Tulasī**.
- *Śiva Purāṇa*, **Rudra-saṃhitā, Yuddha-khaṇḍa** — the **twin** Śaiva myth of **Jalandhara/Vṛndā**, included for contrast; see [Jalandhara & Vṛndā](jalandhara-vrinda.md).

**Tradition & ritual**
- Festival tradition — **Tulsī-Vivāha** on **Prabodhinī Ekādaśī (Kārttika)**; daily worship of the *tulsī-vṛndāvana* and the joint śālagrāma–tulasī pūjā.
- Vaiṣṇava ritual lore — the **conch (śaṅkha)** as a Viṣṇu-worship vessel and its exclusion from Śaiva/Saura/Gāṇapatya rites, traced to this episode.
