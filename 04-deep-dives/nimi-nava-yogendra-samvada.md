# Nimi & the Nine Yogendras — the Nava-Yogendra Saṃvāda (Bhāgavata 11.2–5)

> Cosmic address: [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md) → a dialogue set at a Videha sacrifice in the deep past, but *narrated* by Nārada to Vasudeva at Dvārakā on the eve of Kṛṣṇa's departure (Dvāpara's end), and aimed prophetically at **Kali-yuga**. Parent: [04-deep-dives](./) · Text home: [Bhāgavata Purāṇa skandha map](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md) · The nine speakers are sons of [Ṛṣabhadeva](rishabha-bharata-jada.md); the royal questioner belongs to the [Videha line of Mithilā](../03-lineages-vamsha/videha-mithila-line-janaka.md).

The doctrinal *opening* of Skandha 11: before Kṛṣṇa's own farewell teaching (the [Uddhava-gītā](uddhava-gita.md), 11.6–29), the Purāṇa lays out **bhāgavata-dharma** — the religion of devotion — through nine questions of **King Nimi of Videha** answered in turn by the nine **avadhūta** sons of Ṛṣabha, the **Nava-Yogendras**. Its final answer, Karabhājana's account of the **yuga-avatāras**, contains **Bhāg. 11.5.32**, the single most-quoted proof-text of the Gauḍīya (Caitanya) tradition.

---

## I. The frame — Nārada to Vasudeva at Dvārakā (Bhāg. 11.1–2)

- **The curse on the Yadus sets the stage (Bhāg. 11.1)** — Skandha 11 opens with the sages' curse on Sāmba and the iron club that will destroy the Yadu clan at Prabhāsa; Kṛṣṇa's departure is imminent. Canonical home of that thread: [Mausala-parva](mahabharata/16-mausala-parva.md). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.1)
- **Vasudeva's question (Bhāg. 11.2.1–11)** — [Nārada](narada.md), a frequent guest at Dvārakā, is approached by **Vasudeva**, Kṛṣṇa's father, who confesses that in a former life he worshipped the Lord **desiring a son rather than liberation** — and so got the Lord *as* his son while remaining bound. He now asks to hear the **bhāgavata-dharmas**, the practices that free the fearful from fear. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.2)
- **Nārada reaches for an old dialogue (Bhāg. 11.2.12–14)** — Nārada praises the question as purifying the whole world and answers not in his own voice but by retelling an ancient conversation: how **King Nimi of Videha** questioned the **nine Yogendras**. The Purāṇa thus wraps its bhakti catechism in a double frame — Śuka to Parīkṣit, containing Nārada to Vasudeva, containing the nine to Nimi. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.2)

## II. The nine speakers and the royal questioner

- **The Nava-Yogendras — nine avadhūta sons of Ṛṣabha (Bhāg. 5.4.11; 11.2.15–23)** — Of [Ṛṣabhadeva's](rishabha-bharata-jada.md) hundred sons, nine — **Kavi, Havi (Hari), Antarīkṣa, Prabuddha, Pippalāyana, Āvirhotra, Drumila, Camasa, Karabhājana** — took no kingdom but became naked **paramahaṃsa** wanderers, seeing the universe as the Lord's form, moving unchecked among the worlds of gods, siddhas and men. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 5.4; 11.2)
- **Arrival at Nimi's sacrifice (Bhāg. 11.2.24–33)** — The nine appear, effulgent, at a great **sattra (sacrificial session)** being performed by **Nimi**, king of **Videha**. The king, his priests and the assembly rise, worship them with seats and honors, and Nimi asks his first question: seeing them as direct associates of the Lord, he begs to know **the supreme good (ātyantika-kṣema)** for embodied beings, for whom even half a moment of sādhu-saṅga is a treasure. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.2)
- **Which Nimi? [disputed]** — The Bhāgavata elsewhere (9.13) tells of **Nimi son of Ikṣvāku**, founder of the Videha line, whose body fell by Vasiṣṭha's curse and who thereafter lives "in the blink of every eye" — full treatment in [the Videha line file](../03-lineages-vamsha/videha-mithila-line-janaka.md). Whether the questioner of 11.2–5 is that dynastic founder or a later Maithila king bearing the ancestral name is left open by the text itself.
  - *Variant (commentarial):* traditional commentators note the chronological wrinkle — the Yogendras descend from Svāyambhuva Manu's line (via Priyavrata → Nābhi → Ṛṣabha) while Nimi belongs to Vaivasvata's — and resolve it by the nine's status as liberated siddhas who **wander freely across realms and ages**, so the meeting needs no single-era anchor. [disputed]

## III. The nine questions and nine answers — bhāgavata-dharma unfolded (Bhāg. 11.2–5)

- **Q1 — Kavi on bhāgavata-dharma itself (Bhāg. 11.2.33–43)** — The supreme good is to worship the Lord's feet with everything one does: **whatever one does with body, speech, mind, senses or intelligence should be offered to Nārāyaṇa**. Fear itself is diagnosed in the famous verse *bhayaṁ dvitīyābhiniveśataḥ syāt* — **fear arises from absorption in "a second thing,"** the soul's turning from the Lord under māyā (11.2.37); therefore a sober person serves the Lord, taking the guru as the Self's beloved deity. These dharmas are so framed that **even the ignorant can walk them without stumbling**: one who has taken shelter of the Lord "does not trip even running with eyes closed" (11.2.35). The practitioner who chants the beloved names develops attachment, and — heart melted — **laughs, weeps, cries out, sings and dances like a madman, indifferent to public opinion** (11.2.40). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.2)
- **Q2 — Havi on the grades of devotees (Bhāg. 11.2.44–55)** — Asked the marks of a **bhāgavata**, Havi gives the classic three-tier typology: the **uttama** (highest) devotee **sees the Lord in all beings and all beings in the Lord**; the **madhyama** (intermediate) loves the Lord, befriends His devotees, shows mercy to the innocent and disregards the inimical; the **prākṛta / kaniṣṭha** (materialistic beginner) worships the Deity in the temple with faith **but not the Lord's devotees or other beings**. The chapter closes praising the devotee whom māyā cannot hold, whose one ornament is remembrance of the Lord's feet. Concept home: [bhakti-rasa & the bhāvas](../05-concepts-and-dharma/bhakti-rasa-and-the-bhavas.md). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.2.45–47)
- **Q3 — Antarīkṣa on māyā (Bhāg. 11.3.1–16)** — Nimi asks about the Lord's **māyā**, "bewildering even the great." Antarīkṣa answers with a compact cosmogony-and-dissolution: the Lord evolves the elements, enters the created bodies as their indwelling enjoyer, and beings — driven by māyā through desire-laden karma — transmigrate; at the end, the cosmic **pralaya** (drought, fire, wind, flood) withdraws everything back into the unmanifest. Māyā is thus the Lord's own power of creation, deception and dissolution. Concept home: [māyā & avidyā](../05-concepts-and-dharma/maya-and-avidya.md). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.3)
- **Q4 — Prabuddha on crossing māyā (Bhāg. 11.3.17–33)** — Asked how one **easily crosses** this māyā, Prabuddha first punctures the householder's mirage — happiness sought through wealth, home and family is as futile as watering a mirage — and then delivers the parampara charter-verse: ***tasmād guruṁ prapadyeta jijñāsuḥ śreya uttamam*** — "therefore one who seeks the highest good should surrender to a **guru**" learned in śabda and established in Brahman (11.3.21). From the guru one learns bhāgavata-dharma: satsaṅga, service, hearing the Lord's stories, and mutual devotion among devotees who **remind one another of Hari, thrilling, weeping and dancing** in shared ecstasy. Concept home: [guru–śiṣya paramparā](../05-concepts-and-dharma/guru-shishya-parampara.md). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.3)
- **Q5 — Pippalāyana on Nārāyaṇa/Brahman (Bhāg. 11.3.34–40)** — Asked the nature of the **Supreme (para-brahman, Nārāyaṇa)**, Pippalāyana answers apophatically: that which is the cause of the universe's creation, maintenance and destruction yet itself uncaused; present in waking, dream and deep sleep and beyond them; that which **mind and word turn back from** — knowable only when the heart is purified by devotion. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.3)
- **Q6 — Āvirhotra on karma, akarma and vikarma (Bhāg. 11.3.41–55)** — Asked about **karma-yoga**, Āvirhotra explains that prescribed action, inaction and forbidden action are understood only from the **Veda**, whose indirect (parokṣa) idiom is easily misread: ritual injunctions are the Lord's device for weaning beings *off* karma, as a father coaxes a child with sweets. He recommends offering all prescribed action to the Lord and describes worship of the **arcā (Deity form)** with Vedic and tantric rites — mantra, nyāsa, upacāras — as the swift purifier. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.3)
- **Q7 — Drumila on the avatāras (Bhāg. 11.4)** — Asked what deeds the Lord has done and will do, Drumila answers that counting His qualities is counting the dust-grains of the earth, then surveys the descents: **Nārāyaṇa-ṛṣi** (with Nara) performing tapas at Badarī — see [Nara-Nārāyaṇa](nara-narayana.md) — who, when Indra sends Kāmadeva with apsarases to break his vow, remains unshaken and **manifests from his own person the nymph Urvaśī**, more beautiful than the temptresses, presenting her to them (Bhāg. 11.4.6–16; Urvaśī's later career: [Purūravas & Urvaśī](pururavas-urvashi.md)). He then names, among others, [Haṃsa](hamsa-avatar.md), [Dattātreya](dattatreya.md), the [Kumāras](the-four-kumaras.md), their own father **Ṛṣabha**, [Hayagrīva](hayagriva.md), [Matsya](matsya-pralaya.md), [Kūrma](kurma-avatara.md), [Nṛsiṁha](prahlada-narasimha.md), [Mohinī](mohini-avatara.md), [Vāmana](vamana-bali.md), [Paraśurāma](parashurama.md), Rāma, **Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma**, and the future [Buddha](buddha-avatara.md) and [Kalki](kalki.md). Roster home: [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.4)
- **Q8 — Camasa on the fate of non-devotees (Bhāg. 11.5.1–18)** — Asked the destination of those who do **not** worship, Camasa recalls the four varṇas born of the cosmic Puruṣa's face, arms, thighs and feet, and warns: *ya eṣāṁ puruṣaṁ sākṣād… na bhajanty avajānanti sthānād bhraṣṭāḥ patanty adhaḥ* — those who neglect or disrespect their own source **fall from their position** (11.5.3). He then delivers the Bhāgavata's sharpest ritual-critique: since the urges for **sex, meat and wine** exist naturally, the scriptural licenses (marriage, sacrifice, sautrāmaṇī) exist to **restrict** them, not to encourage them (11.5.11) — condemning those who kill animals or drink in the name of the Veda while ignoring its intent. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.5)
- **Q9 — Karabhājana on the yuga-avatāras (Bhāg. 11.5.19–42)** — the celebrated finale, given its own section below.

## IV. Karabhājana's answer — colors, names and dharmas of the four yugas (Bhāg. 11.5.20–31)

Nimi's last question: **in what colors and forms does the Lord appear in each yuga, and by what method is He worshipped?** Karabhājana's schema:

- **Satya (Kṛta) Yuga — white (śukla)** — the Lord is **four-armed, matted-locked, clad in bark**; people are naturally peaceful and worship Him by **meditation (dhyāna)**; His names include **Haṁsa, Suparṇa, Vaikuṇṭha, Dharma, Yogeśvara, Amala**. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.5.21–23)
- **Tretā Yuga — red (rakta)** — the Lord appears **golden-haired, embodying the Veda**, with the sacrificial ladle among His emblems; He is worshipped through **Vedic sacrifice (yajña)**; names include **Viṣṇu, Yajña, Pṛśnigarbha, Sarvadeva, Urukrama, Vṛṣākapi, Jayanta, Urugāya** — for Pṛśnigarbha see [Pṛśnigarbha & the three births](prishnigarbha-three-births.md). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.5.24–26)
- **Dvāpara Yuga — dark (śyāma)** — the Lord wears **yellow garments**, bears His weapons and the śrīvatsa; He is worshipped **as the great King**, with Vedic and tantric rites, hailed through the **catur-vyūha** mantra: obeisance to **Vāsudeva, Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna, Aniruddha**. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.5.27–30)
- **Kali Yuga — the saṅkīrtana verse (Bhāg. 11.5.32)** — *kṛṣṇa-varṇaṁ tviṣākṛṣṇaṁ sāṅgopāṅgāstra-pārṣadam / yajñaiḥ saṅkīrtana-prāyair yajanti hi su-medhasaḥ* — "In Kali, the intelligent worship, by **sacrifices consisting chiefly of saṅkīrtana** (congregational chanting), Him who is *kṛṣṇa-varṇa* yet of a luster **not black** (*tviṣā akṛṣṇam*), accompanied by His limbs, ornaments, weapons and associates." **Nāma-saṅkīrtana is thus the yuga-dharma of Kali.** (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.5.32)
  - *Variant (Gauḍīya reading):* the Caitanya school takes the verse as the Bhāgavata's own prophecy of **Śrī Caitanya** — *kṛṣṇa-varṇa* read as "He who chants 'Kṛṣṇa' / is Kṛṣṇa Himself," *tviṣā akṛṣṇa* as His **golden (gaura)** luster, the pārṣadas as Nityānanda, Advaita and the rest; the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* (Ādi 3) quotes it as the charter of the **channa (hidden) avatāra**. Sectarian home: [Nimbārka, Vallabha & Caitanya](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md). [disputed]
  - *Variant (plain/pre-Caitanya reading):* read without the Gauḍīya key, the verse simply describes the Kali-age worship of **Kṛṣṇa** ("of the Kṛṣṇa category, of dark-yet-brilliant luster") by chanting — the sense carried by commentators before the 16th century. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Garga's color-list, Bhāg. 10.8.13):* at Kṛṣṇa's naming ceremony Garga gives the sequence **white, red, yellow (pīta), now black** — differing from 11.5's white–red–dark–"non-black." Gauḍīyas reconcile the lists by assigning **yellow to Kali** (the golden avatāra) and black to Dvāpara; the two enumerations are cataloged here, not merged. [disputed]
- **Kali praised for its one virtue (Bhāg. 11.5.36–37)** — *kaliṁ sabhājayanty āryāḥ* — discerning people **praise Kali-yuga**, for in it the whole aim of existence is gained by **saṅkīrtana alone**; what meditation, sacrifice and temple-worship won in the earlier yugas, Kali grants for the mere singing of the Name. Even those of the prior yugas desire birth in Kali, says the text, because Kali will be full of the Lord's devotees. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.5)
- **The Draviḍa-deśa prophecy (Bhāg. 11.5.38–40)** — those Kali devotees will appear **especially in the south**: "great souls, O King, are born here and there in Kali, but **abundantly along the Draviḍa rivers** — the **Tāmraparṇī, Kṛtamālā (Vaigai), Payasvinī, the most-holy Kāverī, and the westward Mahānadī** — and those who drink their waters become, for the most part, pure-hearted devotees of Vāsudeva." (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.5.38–40)
  - *Variant (Śrīvaiṣṇava reading):* the verse is cherished as the Bhāgavata's **prophecy of the Āḻvārs**, the Tamil poet-saints born on precisely those rivers — see [Āḻvārs & Nāyaṉārs](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/alvars-and-nayanars.md). [regional]

## V. Closing the frames (Bhāg. 11.5.41–55)

- **The debt-canceling verse (Bhāg. 11.5.41)** — *devarṣi-bhūtāpta-nṛṇāṁ pitṝṇāṁ na kiṅkaro nāyam ṛṇī ca rājan* — one who has wholly surrendered to **Mukunda** is "no servant or debtor of the gods, the sages, other beings, kinsmen, mankind or the ancestors" — the classic proof-text releasing the śaraṇāgata from the standard five debts. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.5.41)
- **Nimi's attainment and the nine's departure** — Nimi, thus instructed in bhāgavata-dharma, honors the Yogendras, who vanish as freely as they came; the king, practicing what he heard, attains the supreme destination. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.5)
- **Nārada's point to Vasudeva (Bhāg. 11.5.46–52)** — Nārada turns the old dialogue on its hearer: **the Absolute described by the nine is the boy in your own house** — do not think of Kṛṣṇa merely as your son; Vasudeva and Devakī, hearing this, are freed from delusion and fixed in devotion. The stage is set for Kṛṣṇa's own last teaching, the [Uddhava-gītā](uddhava-gita.md) (11.6–29), and His departure ([Mausala-parva](mahabharata/16-mausala-parva.md); Bhāg. 11.30–31). (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.5)

## VI. Two-clocks note

- **Scriptural clock** — the *narration* sits at the very end of **Dvāpara-yuga** (Dvārakā, just before Kṛṣṇa's departure; Kali begins 3102 BCE on the traditional count); the *dialogue narrated* belongs to a much earlier Videha generation (if the founder-Nimi, the dawn of the current cycle — see the [Videha line](../03-lineages-vamsha/videha-mithila-line-janaka.md) two-clocks note); and Karabhājana's answer looks *forward* into **Kali-yuga** as prophecy. Three time-layers in one text is the passage's deliberate design.
- **Academic clock [scholarly]** — modern scholarship dates the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa's** composition to roughly the **8th–10th centuries CE**, very likely in the Tamil south — the warmth of 11.5.38–40 toward the Draviḍa rivers, echoing Āḻvār geography, is a standard argument for the southern provenance. The Gauḍīya deployment of 11.5.32 as a Caitanya proof-text dates from the **16th century CE** (Caitanya 1486–1533; *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* c. 1610s). [scholarly]
