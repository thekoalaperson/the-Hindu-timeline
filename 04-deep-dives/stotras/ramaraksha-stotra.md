# Ramaraksha Stotra

> *Up one level: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · The premier protective armour-hymn (**kavaca**) of Rāma, revealed in a dream to the sage **Budha-Kauśika**, recited daily across North India to "fence in" the body limb-by-limb with the name of Rāma; sibling to the battlefield Sun-hymn [Āditya-hṛdaya](../aditya-hridayam.md) and to the [Hanumān Cālīsā](hanuman-chalisa.md), and rooted in the [Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md).*

The **Rāma-rakṣā Stotra** ("Hymn of Rāma's Protection," from *rakṣā*, "guarding, shield"), opening *caritaṃ raghunāthasya śata-koṭi-pravistaram...* ("The deeds of Raghunātha, spread out in a hundred crores [of verses]...") and culminating in the celebrated benediction *rāmo rāja-maṇiḥ sadā vijayate* ("Rāma, the jewel among kings, is forever victorious"), is a Sanskrit **kavaca-stotra** of roughly **thirty-eight ślokas**. It is structured as a literal **armour**: verse by verse it sets Rāma, the Rāmāyaṇa heroes, and the *rāma-nāma* upon each limb of the reciter, so that the body is wholly enclosed in the name. It is among the most widely recited Rāma hymns in living North-Indian practice, chanted daily, at dawn, before sleep, and as apotropaic protection against fear, illness, and ill fortune. [scholarly]

---

## The frame: the dream-revelation to Budha-Kauśika

- **Authorship by Budha-Kauśika** — The hymn names its author/seer in its colophon: it was "seen" (revealed) by the **ṛṣi Budha-Kauśika** (also given as Bodhāyana / Budha-Kauśika), whose name appears in the closing dedication *ity ārṣe śrīmad-rāmāyaṇe... budha-kauśikena viracitam*. The text is thus framed not as ordinary authorship but as a **revealed mantra-stotra**. (*Rāma-rakṣā Stotra*, phala-śruti) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (the dream of Śiva):* the dominant tradition holds that **Śiva himself appeared to Budha-Kauśika in a dream (svapna) and dictated the hymn**, which the sage wrote down on waking — making the stotra a divine composition transmitted through the sleeping seer. The internal line *budha-kauśika ṛṣiṃ... yaḥ kaṇṭhe dhārayet* and the dream-frame are the basis of its prestige. (devotional tradition) [folk]
  - *Variant (identity of the ṛṣi):* "Budha-Kauśika" is sometimes glossed as an epithet of **Viśvāmitra** (a Kauśika by gotra) or treated as a distinct later seer; the hymn's date is undetermined and likely **post-epic / medieval**, despite its claim to ārṣa (sagely, Vedic-style) origin. [disputed]

## The kavaca: armouring the body limb-by-limb

- **The aṅga-nyāsa structure** — The doctrinal heart is an **aṅga-rakṣā** ("limb-protection"): the reciter invokes Rāma and his retinue to guard each part of the body in sequence, so that no opening is left undefended. This is the classic **kavaca** form shared with the Saura [Āditya-hṛdaya](../aditya-hridayam.md) and other rakṣā-stotras. (*Rāma-rakṣā Stotra*, vv. 4–11) [scholarly]
- **The limb-litany** — Rāma is set on the **head**; *daśaratha-putra* (Daśaratha's son) on the **brow**; *kausalyeya* (Kausalyā's son) on the **eyes**; *viśvāmitra-priya* (dear to Viśvāmitra) on the **ears**; the **nose, mouth, tongue, throat, shoulders, arms, hands, heart, navel, hips, knees, feet** each receive a different epithet of Rāma — *jāmadagnya-jaya* (victor over Paraśurāma), *bharatāgraja* (Bharata's elder), *jānakī-vallabha* (Sītā's beloved), and so on. The whole body is thereby "named" with Rāma. (*Rāma-rakṣā Stotra*, vv. 4–9) [scholarly]
- **The enclosing seal** — Having armoured the limbs, the hymn declares that one who wears this *rāma-kavaca* — "fashioned by Budha-Kauśika" — becomes long-lived, happy, progeny-blessed, victorious, and humble, and is freed from every fear. (*Rāma-rakṣā Stotra*, vv. 10–13) [scholarly]

## The Rāma-nāma and the famous benedictions

- **The acrostic / name-meditation** — A celebrated cluster of verses dwells on the **power of the bare name "Rāma"**: the reciter who bears the *rāma-nāma* on the tongue, in the heart, on the head, is fenced about by the name itself, which is identified with the whole protective force of the hymn. The *rāma-nāma* is treated as a complete kavaca in its own right. (*Rāma-rakṣā Stotra*, vv. 13–14) [scholarly]
- **"Rāmo rāja-maṇiḥ sadā vijayate"** — The single most-quoted verse declines the name **"Rāma" through all eight Sanskrit grammatical cases** (*vibhakti*): *rāmo rāja-maṇiḥ sadā vijayate, rāmaṃ rameśaṃ bhaje, rāmeṇābhihatā niśācara-camūḥ, rāmāya tasmai namaḥ...* — a tour-de-force in which the very **declension of the name** becomes the prayer, ending *rāme cittalayaḥ sadā bhavatu me, bho rāma, mām uddhara* ("may my mind be ever fixed on Rāma; O Rāma, lift me up"). (*Rāma-rakṣā Stotra*, v. 14) [scholarly]
- **The sleeping lions** — The much-loved closing image *rāmaṃ dūrvā-dala-śyāmaṃ... siṃhāv iva nirgatau* / *rāmaṃ lakṣmaṇa-pūrvajaṃ raghu-varaṃ...* meditates on Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa as **two brothers like lions striding forth**, bows in hand, ever protecting the devotee — culminating in the verse that begins *āpadām apahartāraṃ dātāraṃ sarva-sampadām* ("remover of calamities, giver of all wealth"). (*Rāma-rakṣā Stotra*, vv. 30–33) [scholarly]
- **Hanumān as guardian** — The hymn closes by invoking **Hanumān** — *mano-javaṃ māruta-tulya-vegam... vātātmajaṃ vānara-yūtha-mukhyam, śrī-rāma-dūtaṃ śaraṇaṃ prapadye* ("swift as thought, equal to the wind... son of the wind, chief of the monkey host, Rāma's messenger — I take refuge in him") — tying the Rāma-shield to its Cālīsā companion. (*Rāma-rakṣā Stotra*, v. 38; cf. [Hanumān Cālīsā](hanuman-chalisa.md)) [scholarly]

## The Rāmāyaṇa substratum

- **Epithets presupposing the epic** — Each protective epithet compresses a Rāmāyaṇa episode: *viśvāmitra-priya* (Rāma's tutelage and demon-slaying under [Viśvāmitra](../vishwamitra-vasishtha.md) in the [Bāla-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md)); *jāmadagnya-jaya* (the encounter with Paraśurāma); *daṇḍakāraṇya-vihārin* (the forest exile of the [Araṇya-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md)); *paulastya-vadha* (the slaying of Rāvaṇa, son of Pulastya's line). The shield thus rehearses the whole epic in epithet-form. (*Rāma-rakṣā Stotra*, vv. 4–9) [scholarly]
- **Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa woven in** — Rāma is repeatedly named through his relations — *jānakī-vallabha* (beloved of [Sītā](../characters/sita.md)), *lakṣmaṇa-pūrvaja* (elder brother of [Lakṣmaṇa](../characters/lakshmana.md)), *bharatāgraja* — so that the protective field includes the heroes of the [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md) as a family. [scholarly]

## The recitation tradition

- **Daily and apotropaic use** — The Rāma-rakṣā is recited **daily**, especially at dawn and before sleep, and as a **protective shield** worn (mentally or in amulet form) against fear, disease, enemies, evil spirits, and ill fortune — exactly the kavaca function its verses promise. (living North-Indian practice) [regional]
- **Phala-śruti promises** — The closing fruit-of-recitation verses pledge that one who recites or wears the hymn gains **long life, health, progeny, victory, fearlessness, and final union with Rāma**; the merit is framed as Śiva-given through the dream-revelation. (*Rāma-rakṣā Stotra*, phala-śruti) [scholarly]
- **Place in the Rāma corpus** — Within living practice it stands beside the [Hanumān Cālīsā](hanuman-chalisa.md), the battlefield [Āditya-hṛdaya](../aditya-hridayam.md), and the retelling-hymns of the [Rāmcaritmānas](../ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md) as one of the core daily Rāmabhakti recitations. [regional]

## Two clocks: scriptural time vs. composition date

- **Scriptural / narrative time** — The *content* belongs to the **Tretā-yuga** world of the Rāmāyaṇa — Rāma's deeds, his brothers, Sītā, and Hanumān — which the hymn treats as eternally protective and ever-present. [scholarly]
- **Historical / composition time** — The *text* is a **post-epic Sanskrit kavaca-stotra**, undated but most plausibly **medieval**, framed as a dream-revelation to Budha-Kauśika and shaped by the mature **kavaca / aṅga-nyāsa** liturgical genre rather than by epic-period composition. Keep the eternal narrative subject distinct from the datable hymn. [scholarly] [disputed]

## Sources

**Primary text**
- *Rāma-rakṣā Stotra*, attributed to the ṛṣi **Budha-Kauśika** — Sanskrit kavaca-stotra of ~38 ślokas, opening *caritaṃ raghunāthasya...*, containing the limb-by-limb aṅga-rakṣā, the eight-case *rāma-nāma* verse (*rāmo rāja-maṇiḥ sadā vijayate...*), the *āpadām apahartāram* benediction, and the closing Hanumān invocation (*mano-javaṃ māruta-tulya-vegam...*); the phala-śruti names Budha-Kauśika and the dream/Śiva-revelation frame.

**Narrative substratum**
- **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa** — the epic whose episodes the protective epithets compress (see [Rāmāyaṇa overview](../ramayana/README.md), [Bāla-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md), [Araṇya-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md)).

**Tradition / context**
- Living North-Indian Rāmabhakti recitation: daily/dawn/apotropaic use, amulet-wearing, vow-based repetition (folk/regional, oral tradition).
- Sibling daily hymns of the Rāma corpus: [Hanumān Cālīsā](hanuman-chalisa.md), [Āditya-hṛdaya](../aditya-hridayam.md), [Rāmcaritmānas](../ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md).
