# Aditya Hridayam

> *Up one level: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · The **Āditya-Hṛdayam** ("Heart of the Sun"), the battlefield Sūrya-hymn that the sage [Agastya](../agastya.md) teaches a war-weary [Rāma](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md) on the eve of his final duel with Rāvaṇa — a canonical stotra extracted from the [Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md) and recited daily across the Hindu world, sibling to the [Hanumān Cālīsā](hanuman-chalisa.md) among living devotional texts.*

The **Āditya-Hṛdayam** (आदित्यहृदयम्, "Heart / Essence of Āditya [the Sun]") is a short Sanskrit hymn of roughly **thirty ślokas** addressed to **Sūrya**, the Sun-god. Unlike most independent stotras, it is **embedded in narrative scripture**: it appears as **sarga 105** of the **Yuddha-kāṇḍa** of Vālmīki's **Rāmāyaṇa** (sarga number varies slightly by recension), spoken by the ṛṣi **Agastya** to **Rāma** at the climactic moment of the war against Rāvaṇa. It is among the most widely recited Sūrya-hymns in living Hindu practice, chanted especially at **sunrise** and on **Sundays** (Ravivāra) and in the month of vows, and it is the most famous textual source for the worship of the Sun as the supreme, victory-giving deity. [scholarly]

## The frame-story (Yuddha-kāṇḍa 105)

- **The exhausted Rāma** — On the battlefield before Laṅkā, after days of combat, **Rāma stands fatigued and despondent (*viṣaṇṇa*)**, facing **Rāvaṇa** who advances afresh on his chariot for the final duel; Rāma is *cintā-paraḥ*, sunk in anxious thought, weary of the unending war. (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha-kāṇḍa 105.1–4) [scholarly]
- **Agastya approaches** — The sage **Agastya**, present among the gods who have come to witness the war, **draws near Rāma** and addresses him, beginning *rāma rāma mahābāho śṛṇu guhyaṃ sanātanam* ("Rāma, mighty-armed Rāma, hear this eternal secret"). (Yuddha-kāṇḍa 105.1) [scholarly]
- **The teaching** — Agastya gives Rāma the **Āditya-Hṛdayam**, declaring that by it Rāma will **conquer all enemies in battle** (*yena sarvān arīn vatsa samare vijayiṣyasi*); he frames the hymn as the means to victory, not mere devotion. (Yuddha-kāṇḍa 105.2–3) [scholarly]
- **The instruction to recite** — Agastya bids Rāma **purify himself (*ācamya*), face the rising Sun, and recite the hymn three times (*triḥ japtvā*)**, promising he will then prevail. (Yuddha-kāṇḍa 105.24–25) [scholarly]
- **Rāma's worship and victory** — Rāma takes up water, recites the hymn with **gladdened heart**, gazes at the Sun thrice, and is filled with resolve (*paraṃ harṣam avāptavān*); he then takes up his bow, advances on Rāvaṇa, and slays him. **Sūrya himself**, in the heavens, looks upon Rāma and urges him on with *tvarasva* ("make haste"). (Yuddha-kāṇḍa 105.26–31) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (placement):* in the **vulgate / popular recensions** the Āditya-Hṛdayam is a full standalone sarga (105); the **Critical Edition** of the Rāmāyaṇa treats the hymn as a **later interpolation** into the Yuddha-kāṇḍa, absent from or marked in several manuscript traditions — so the hymn is textually a devotional layer atop the war-narrative, however ancient and beloved. [scholarly] [disputed]

## Structure and content of the hymn

The hymn's body is a dense litany of Sūrya's names, identifications, and glories — a stotra of *nāma-saṃkīrtana* (praise by enumerated names) rather than narrative.

- **Opening promise** — The first ślokas establish the hymn as **secret and supreme (*guhyaṃ sanātanam*)**, *sarva-maṅgala-māṅgalyam* (the auspicious of all auspicious things), destroyer of all enemies, bestower of victory, imperishable, the supreme auspicious thing (*paramaṃ śivam*). (Yuddha-kāṇḍa 105.3–5) [scholarly]
- **The Sun as all gods (*sarva-devātmaka*)** — Sūrya is hailed as **the soul of all the gods**, the self-luminous one (*raśmimantaṃ samudyantam*), worshipped by gods and asuras alike, who **nourishes and warms the worlds with his rays** and is the source of all beings. (Yuddha-kāṇḍa 105.6–9) [scholarly]
- **Identification with the great deities** — The Sun is identified with **Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Śiva (Rudra), Skanda, Prajāpati**, with **Indra, Kubera, Kāla (Time), Yama, Soma (the Moon), and Varuṇa** — the one luminary behind all divine functions. (Yuddha-kāṇḍa 105.10–13) [scholarly]
- **The litany of names** — A cascade of epithets: **Āditya, Savitṛ, Sūrya, Khaga, Pūṣan, Gabhastimān, Suvarṇa-sadṛśa, Bhānu, Hiraṇya-retas, Divākara**; **Haridaśva** (he of the tawny horses), **Sahasra-arci** (thousand-rayed), **Sapta-saptiḥ** (seven-horsed), **Marīcimān, Timironmathana** (churner of darkness), **Śambhu, Tvaṣṭṛ, Mārtaṇḍa, Aṃśumān**. (Yuddha-kāṇḍa 105.14–18) [scholarly]
- **The cosmic functions** — Sūrya is praised as the maker of **day and the seasons**, lord of the **twelve months and the year**, source of **rain (he draws up and pours down water)**, dispeller of cold, darkness, fear and disease; *hiraṇya-garbhaḥ śiśiras tapano bhāskaro raviḥ*. (Yuddha-kāṇḍa 105.19–22) [scholarly]
- **The phala-śruti (fruit of recitation)** — The closing ślokas promise that **whoever recites this hymn is never overwhelmed in adversity**, that it **drives away all grief, anxiety and danger (*sarva-vyādhi / saṅkaṭa*)**, and that recitation **before battle, in distress, or in any peril grants victory and protection**. (Yuddha-kāṇḍa 105.23–25) [scholarly]
- **Adoration formula** — The hymn is punctuated with the refrain of homage — *namaḥ* / *jaya* to the Sun in his many aspects, east and west, rising and setting — a structure echoed in later Sūrya liturgy. (Yuddha-kāṇḍa 105, refrain ślokas) [scholarly]

## Place in worship and tradition

- **Canonical standalone stotra** — Though embedded in the epic, the Āditya-Hṛdayam is **recited as an independent hymn**, printed in stotra-collections and chanted apart from any reading of the Rāmāyaṇa; it is the principal liturgical hymn of **Sūrya-upāsanā** (Sun-worship). (See [Sūrya and his family](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/surya-and-his-family.md).) [scholarly]
- **Daily and weekly recitation** — Devotees recite it at **sunrise facing the Sun**, often after **Sūrya-namaskāra**; **Sunday (Ravivāra)** and the festival **Ratha-saptamī** (Sūrya's "birthday") are especially associated with its recitation. (living practice) [regional]
- **Apotropaic and victory use** — Following Agastya's own framing, the hymn is invoked for **success in undertakings, removal of disease and obstacles, mental strength in adversity, and "victory" in trials** — the same protective register that drives daily use of the [Hanumān Cālīsā](hanuman-chalisa.md). (living practice) [folk]
- **Health and the Sun** — Because Sūrya is the Vedic lord of **health, sight and vitality**, the hymn is recited for **recovery from illness and for eye-ailments** in particular, a folk-medical extension of the scriptural *timironmathana* ("churner of darkness") epithet. (living practice) [folk]
- **Agastya as transmitter** — That the hymn is given by **Agastya** — the southern sage, ocean-drinker and culture-hero — links it to his role as transmitter of sacred knowledge; the episode is a fixture of his hagiography. (See [Agastya](../agastya.md).) [scholarly]

## Two clocks: scriptural time vs. textual layer

- **Scriptural / narrative time** — Within the story, the teaching of the Āditya-Hṛdayam happens in the **Tretā-yuga**, on the **last day of the Laṅkā war**, immediately before Rāma kills Rāvaṇa — a single, datable-within-the-epic moment of the Rāma-avatāra cycle. [scholarly]
- **Textual / compositional layer** — As a *text*, the hymn is widely regarded by scholars as a **devotional interpolation** woven into the Yuddha-kāṇḍa — an embedded stotra reflecting the rise of organized **Sūrya / Saura worship**, later than the core war-narrative though transmitted as part of it. Keep the eternal narrative event distinct from the history of the hymn-as-text. [scholarly] [disputed]

## Sources

**Primary text**
- **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa**, *Yuddha-kāṇḍa*, **sarga 105** (sarga number varies by recension) — the Āditya-Hṛdayam proper: Agastya's address to the despondent Rāma, the ~30-śloka hymn enumerating Sūrya's names and glories, the instruction to recite thrice facing the Sun, and Rāma's resulting victory over Rāvaṇa. The **Critical Edition** flags the passage as a likely later interpolation. (See [Yuddha-kāṇḍa deep-dive](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md).)

**Tradition / context**
- Sūrya / Saura worship and the deity's names, family and cosmic role (see [Sūrya and his family](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/surya-and-his-family.md)).
- Agastya hagiography and his role as transmitter of sacred knowledge (see [Agastya](../agastya.md)).
- Living recitation practice: sunrise/Sunday/Ratha-saptamī observance, victory- and health-oriented use (folk/regional, oral tradition); companion in daily devotion to the [Hanumān Cālīsā](hanuman-chalisa.md).
