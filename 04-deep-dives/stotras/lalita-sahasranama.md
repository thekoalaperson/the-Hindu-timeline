# Lalita Sahasranama: The Thousand Names of Lalita Tripurasundari

> *Up one level: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · The supreme Śākta name-litany — **a thousand names of [Lalitā Tripurasundarī](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/lalita-bhandasura.md)**, composed by the eight **Vāg-devatās** at the Goddess's own command and transmitted by **[Hayagrīva](../hayagriva.md)** to the sage **[Agastya](../agastya.md)** in the **Lalitopākhyāna** of the [Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa](../../90-literature-corpus/puranas/brahmanda-purana.md) — sibling to the [Viṣṇu Sahasranāma](vishnu-sahasranama.md) and [Śiva Sahasranāma](shiva-sahasranama.md), and the liturgical heart of Śrī Vidyā alongside the [Soundarya Laharī](soundarya-lahari.md). The myth-frame is cyclic/timeless; the text belongs to the medieval Śākta stratum [scholarly].*

The **Lalitā Sahasranāma** (ललितासहस्रनाम, "the Thousand Names of Lalitā") is the litany of **one thousand names of the Great Goddess as Lalitā Mahātripurasundarī**, embedded in the **Lalitopākhyāna** — the Śākta crown appended to the **Uttara-bhāga of the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa**. Where the Viṣṇu Sahasranāma is spoken by a dying warrior and the Śiva names taught in the epic's books of instruction, Lalitā's thousand names carry a double divine pedigree: they were **composed by the eight Vāg-devatās (goddesses of speech) at Lalitā's own command** and sung before her throne, then **narrated by the horse-headed Viṣṇu-form Hayagrīva to Agastya**. The corpus's own survey of the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa calls it "the single most influential **Śākta stotra**"; it is simultaneously a devotional hymn, a condensed theology of Śiva–Śakti non-duality, and an encoded manual of **Śrī-cakra** and **Pañcadaśī-mantra** worship, fixed for the tradition by **Bhāskararāya's** commentary. [scholarly]

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic time** — The names have no yuga-date: Lalitā's manifestation from the **Cidagni-kuṇḍa**, her marriage to **Kāmeśvara**, and the destruction of **Bhaṇḍāsura** are framed as a timeless deva–asura event of the cosmic order (see [Lalitā & the War on Bhaṇḍāsura](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/lalita-bhandasura.md)); the composition of the names happens in the Goddess's eternal court at **Śrīpura**, and the Hayagrīva–Agastya transmission is a ṛṣi-frame outside any fixed manvantara chronology. (*Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*, Lalitopākhyāna) [scriptural]
- **Manuscript / academic time** — Text-critically the **Lalitopākhyāna is a late appendix** to the old Vāyu-shared core of the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, reflecting the **medieval Śrī-Vidyā efflorescence**; the corpus's Purāṇa survey places the Lalitopākhyāna stratum commonly at **~13th–16th c. CE** [late text] [interpolation], while some scholars argue the Sahasranāma itself circulated earlier (estimates ranging back toward the **9th–12th c. CE**) — the dating is genuinely unsettled. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **The commentarial date** — The one hard date in the hymn's history is **Bhāskararāya Makhin (1690–1785 CE)**, whose *Saubhāgya-bhāskara* commentary (traditionally completed **c. 1728 CE**) fixed the received text and its Śrī-Vidyā reading. (see [Śākta & Tantra lineages](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/shakta-and-tantra-lineages.md)) [scholarly]

## The frame-story — Hayagrīva and Agastya

- **The teacher and the student** — In the Lalitopākhyāna, the sage **[Agastya](../agastya.md)**, grieved at the spiritual darkness of beings, approaches **[Hayagrīva](../hayagriva.md)** — Viṣṇu's horse-headed embodiment of *jñāna* and guardian of sacred knowledge — who narrates to him the whole story of Lalitā: Bhaṇḍāsura's birth from Kāma's ashes, the Goddess's rise from the Cidagni-kuṇḍa, her marriage to Kāmeśvara, the war, and the glories of **Śrīpura**. (*Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*, Lalitopākhyāna) [scriptural]
- **The withheld secret** — Tradition's telling of the Sahasranāma's own preface: having taught everything else, Hayagrīva **passes over the thousand names in silence**, because they are the most secret (*rahasya*) of teachings, not to be uttered unasked. Agastya notices the omission and presses him; Hayagrīva — satisfied of Agastya's fitness, and (in the received telling) prompted by the Goddess's own grace — then reveals the **Rahasya-nāma-sāhasra**, "the secret thousand names." (Lalitā Sahasranāma, *pūrva-bhāga*) [scriptural]
  - *Variant (status of the preface):* the framing chapters (*pūrva-bhāga*) and the *phala-śruti* travel with the hymn in the manuscript and liturgical tradition, but as with the [Viṣṇu Sahasranāma](vishnu-sahasranama.md)'s apparatus their exact wording and extent vary by recension. [disputed]
- **A charter of initiation** — The frame's insistence that the names must be *asked for* and given only to the fit student is not decoration: it encodes the **Śrī-Vidyā rule of guru-transmission**, and the Agastya–Hayagrīva dialogue serves the lineage as its founding charter, with Agastya and his wife **Lopāmudrā** counted among the archetypal Śrī-Vidyā adepts. (see [Śākta & Tantra lineages](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/shakta-and-tantra-lineages.md)) [scholarly]

## Composition by the Vāg-devatās

- **The command of the Goddess** — Unlike almost every other sahasranāma, this one is presented as **commissioned by its own deity**: Lalitā herself commands the **eight Vāg-devatās** — the goddesses of speech headed by **Vaśinī** (the *Vaśiny-ādi-vāg-devatās*) — to compose a hymn of her thousand secret names, and they sing it before her lion-throne in the assembly of Śrīpura. (*Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*, Lalitopākhyāna / Lalitā Sahasranāma *pūrva-bhāga*) [scriptural]
- **The eight goddesses of speech** — The received list of the eight is **Vaśinī, Kāmeśvarī, Modinī, Vimalā, Aruṇā, Jayinī, Sarveśvarī, Kauliṇī** — in Śrī-Vidyā ritual they are the deities of the **eighth enclosure (āvaraṇa) of the Śrī-cakra**, presiding over the groups of Sanskrit phonemes; the hymn is thus literally the work of the powers of language itself. (Lalitopākhyāna; Śrī-cakra *āvaraṇa* tradition) [scholarly]
- **Why speech composes the names** — Commentators draw the theology out of the frame: because the Vāg-devatās govern all speech, a hymn composed by them is *perfect* speech — hence the tradition's claims for the text's formal flawlessness (below). (Bhāskararāya, *Saubhāgya-bhāskara*) [scholarly]

## Structure of the hymn

- **The received shape** — In liturgical use the text has three parts: the **pūrva-bhāga** (the Agastya–Hayagrīva frame and *nyāsa*/*dhyāna* apparatus), the **stotra proper** — the thousand names in **anuṣṭubh** metre, conventionally counted as **182½ / 183 ślokas** — and the **uttara-bhāga** (*phala-śruti*). (Lalitā Sahasranāma, received text) [scholarly]
- **The opening names** — The litany opens **Śrī-mātā** ("the auspicious Mother"), **Śrī-mahārājñī** ("the great Empress"), **Śrīmat-siṃhāsaneśvarī** ("Sovereign of the lion-throne") — creation, protection, dissolution read into the first three names — and then plunges straight into the myth: **Cid-agni-kuṇḍa-sambhūtā** ("born from the fire-pit of consciousness"), **Deva-kārya-samudyatā** ("arisen for the gods' cause"). (Lalitā Sahasranāma, vv. 1–2) [scriptural]
- **The narrative block** — A long opening sequence of names simply *narrates* the Lalitopākhyāna in vocative compression — her weapons (noose, goad, sugarcane bow, five flower-arrows), her ministers **Mantriṇī/Śyāmalā** and **Daṇḍanāthā/Vārāhī**, the chariots, the burning of **Śūnyaka**, the slaying of **Bhaṇḍāsura**, and the revival of **Kāmadeva** — so the hymn carries its own myth inside itself. (Lalitā Sahasranāma; cf. [the full war-narrative](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/lalita-bhandasura.md)) [scriptural]
- **The closing names** — The litany culminates in the famous final run: **Śrī-cakra-rāja-nilayā** ("whose abode is the king of cakras"), **Śrīmat-Tripurasundarī**, **Śrī-Śivā**, **Śiva-śakty-aikya-rūpiṇī** ("whose form is the oneness of Śiva and Śakti"), ending on **Lalitāmbikā** — the whole theology of Śrī Vidyā compressed into the last five names. (Lalitā Sahasranāma, final verse) [scriptural]
- **Formal perfection claims** — Tradition, following Bhāskararāya, prizes the hymn's form: **no name is repeated** (unlike the Viṣṇu and Śiva litanies, where commentators must justify recurrences), and the thousand names fill the metre **without filler particles** (*ca*, *api*, *tu* etc.) — read as proof of the Vāg-devatās' authorship. (Bhāskararāya, *Saubhāgya-bhāskara*, introduction) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (counting):* as with every sahasranāma, parsing compounds into exactly 1,000 names involves commentarial choices; enumerations differ slightly across editions. [disputed]

## Nyasa, ritual use, and the phala-shruti

- **Nyāsa before recitation** — The hymn is classically recited as a **mantra-text, not mere poetry**: preceded by *ṛṣy-ādi-nyāsa* (installing Hayagrīva as ṛṣi, anuṣṭubh as metre, Lalitā as devatā), *kara-nyāsa* and *aṅga-nyāsa* (placing syllables on the hands and body), and a **dhyāna** visualizing the Goddess red as dawn, holding noose, goad, bow and arrows. (received liturgical apparatus) [scholarly]
- **The phala-śruti** — The *uttara-bhāga* promises the fruits of recitation — destruction of sin and disease, protection, prosperity, and finally liberation — and famously **subordinates all other worship to it**: japa of the names is declared equal to full ritual worship, and the worship of Devī is called incomplete without it. It equally hedges the gift with **secrecy rules**: the names are not to be given to the undevoted or the uninitiated. (Lalitā Sahasranāma, *phala-śruti*) [scriptural]
- **Prescribed days** — The *phala-śruti* and living custom single out **Fridays**, full-moon days (*pūrṇimā*), **Navarātri**, and the Goddess's special tithis for recitation; in South Indian temple and household practice the Friday Lalitā-sahasranāma-pārāyaṇa is a fixture, and the hymn is recited over **kuṅkuma-arcana** (offering vermilion at each name). [folk] [regional]
- **The sibling Triśatī** — In the same Lalitopākhyāna frame, Hayagrīva also transmits the **Lalitā Triśatī** (300 names) — held by the tradition to be even more esoteric, its names arranged **twenty to each of the fifteen syllables of the Pañcadaśī mantra**. (*Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*, Lalitopākhyāna; Triśatī tradition) [scholarly]

## Shri-chakra and the Panchadashi/Shodashi encoding

- **A mantra-manual in disguise** — For Śrī Vidyā, the Sahasranāma is not only praise: names describe the Goddess in her **gross form** (*sthūla* — the head-to-foot descriptions), her **subtle form** (*sūkṣma* — as the **Pañcadaśī mantra** itself), and her **transcendent form** (*para* — as the Śrī-cakra and pure consciousness); Bhāskararāya reads the whole litany through this graded scheme. (Bhāskararāya, *Saubhāgya-bhāskara*) [scholarly]
- **The mantra as her body** — Names such as **Mūla-mantrātmikā** ("whose self is the root-mantra") and **Mūla-kūṭa-traya-kalevarā** ("whose body is the three kūṭas of the root-mantra") explicitly identify the Goddess with the **fifteen-syllable Pañcadaśī** in its three sections — *vāgbhava-kūṭa* (her face), *kāmarāja-kūṭa* (neck to hip), *śakti-kūṭa* (hip to feet). (Lalitā Sahasranāma; Bhāskararāya *ad loc.*) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Kādi vs Hādi):* the "same" Pañcadaśī is extracted differently in the **Kādimata** and **Hādimata** schools; the **Ṣoḍaśī** (sixteen-syllable) form adds a further secret syllable, given only in initiation — see [Mantra-śāstra](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/mantra-shastra.md) for the coded *uddhāra* conventions. [scholarly]
- **The Śrī-cakra names** — **Śrī-cakra-rāja-nilayā** and the sequence around it place the Goddess in the **nine-enclosure Śrī-cakra**, whose *āvaraṇa*-deities (including the Vāg-devatās themselves, and the **Nityā** goddesses of the fifteen lunar tithis) appear as names; the Lalitopākhyāna's city **Śrīpura is itself laid out as the Śrī-cakra**, so hymn, yantra, and myth mirror one another. (*Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*, Lalitopākhyāna; cf. [Daśa Mahāvidyā](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md)) [scholarly]
- **The Kuṇḍalinī sequence** — A celebrated run of names traces the Goddess as **Kuṇḍalinī** ascending the six cakras from **Mūlādhāraika-nilayā** ("dwelling in the mūlādhāra") through the piercing of the three knots (**Brahma-granthi**, etc.) to the thousand-petalled lotus, where as **Sudhā-sārābhi-varṣiṇī** she rains down nectar — the yogic itinerary embedded mid-litany. (Lalitā Sahasranāma, Kuṇḍalinī sequence) [scriptural]
- **Within the Mahāvidyā scheme** — As **Tripurasundarī/Ṣoḍaśī**, the hymn's deity is the third and (for Śrīkula) supreme of the **[ten Mahāvidyās](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dasha-mahavidya.md)**; the Sahasranāma functions as that cult's central liturgical text, as the [Soundarya Laharī](soundarya-lahari.md) is its central praise-poem. [scholarly]

## Bhaskararaya's commentary and the exegetical tradition

- **The Saubhāgya-bhāskara** — **Bhāskararāya Makhin (Bhāsurānanda-nātha, 1690–1785)**, the great systematizer of Śrī Vidyā, wrote the standard commentary, the ***Saubhāgya-bhāskara*** (traditionally dated **c. 1728 CE**), glossing every name — often at multiple levels (literal, mantric, yogic, non-dual) — and buttressing each with citations from Śruti, Purāṇa, and Tantra. It is to this hymn what Śaṅkara's bhāṣya is to the [Viṣṇu Sahasranāma](vishnu-sahasranama.md). (Bhāskararāya, *Saubhāgya-bhāskara*; see [Śākta & Tantra lineages](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/shakta-and-tantra-lineages.md)) [scholarly]
- **Doctrinal fixing** — Bhāskararāya's readings — the no-repetition claim, the sthūla/sūkṣma/para scheme, the identification of names with Śrī-cakra *āvaraṇas* and with the **108 deities** he fixed within the cakra — made the Sahasranāma the doctrinal spine of the living **Bhāskararāya-sampradāya** and of modern Śrī-Vidyā practice generally. (see [Śākta & Tantra lineages](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/shakta-and-tantra-lineages.md)) [scholarly]
- **Other glosses** — Bhāskararāya's is not the only commentary — he himself engages earlier and rival interpretations, and later Śrī-Vidyā teachers produced further glosses — but his is the one the tradition treats as definitive; modern English expositions (e.g. those of the Ramakrishna Math line) largely translate through him. [scholarly]

## Living use

- **The Śākta counterpart of the great litanies** — With the [Viṣṇu Sahasranāma](vishnu-sahasranama.md) and [Śiva Sahasranāma](shiva-sahasranama.md), the Lalitā Sahasranāma completes the triad of the most-recited thousand-name hymns; it is chanted daily or weekly in **Smārta and Śākta** households, is central to **Navarātri** observance, and anchors temple liturgy at the great South Indian Devī shrines (Kāñcī Kāmākṣī, Madurai Mīnākṣī, and the Śrī-cakra-centred temples). [folk] [regional]
- **From secrecy to print** — Though its own *phala-śruti* restricts transmission, the hymn today circulates universally in print and recording; formal Śrī-Vidyā practice nonetheless still embeds it within initiated **navāvaraṇa-pūjā**, preserving the old boundary between public recitation and esoteric use. [scholarly] [regional]
