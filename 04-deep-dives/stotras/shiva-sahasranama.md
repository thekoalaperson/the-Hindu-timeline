# Shiva Sahasranama: The Thousand Names of Shiva

> *Up one level: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · The supreme litany of **a thousand names of Śiva/Rudra**, recited not by the dying [Bhīṣma](../characters/bhishma.md) but by **Kṛṣṇa** — who learned them from the sage **Upamanyu** — in the [Anuśāsana Parva](../mahabharata/13-anushasana-parva.md) of the Mahābhārata; the Śaiva counterpart and structural sibling to the [Viṣṇu Sahasranāma](vishnu-sahasranama.md), and kin to the [Śiva-mahimnaḥ stotra](shiva-mahimna-stotra.md) and the doctrinal [Śiva-Gītā](../shiva-gita.md).*

The **Śiva Sahasranāma** (शिवसहस्रनाम, "the Thousand Names of Śiva") is a litany of **one thousand names and epithets of Śiva/Rudra**, the Śaiva counterpart to the [Viṣṇu Sahasranāma](vishnu-sahasranama.md). The best-known recension is embedded in the **Anuśāsana Parva** (Book 13) of the **Mahābhārata**, but its frame differs sharply from the Vaiṣṇava hymn: when **Yudhiṣṭhira** asks for the names of Śiva, the dying **Bhīṣma defers** and it is **Kṛṣṇa** who recites them, having himself received them from the ascetic **Upamanyu**. A wholly distinct thousand-name list circulates in the **Liṅga Purāṇa**; the two recensions are *not* the same text and must never be merged. [scholarly]

---

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural / narrative time** — The recitation is delivered in the **Dvāpara Yuga**, in the aftermath of the **Kurukṣetra war**, during the days **Bhīṣma** lies dying on the **bed of arrows (śara-talpa)** and instructs Yudhiṣṭhira before relinquishing his life at the winter solstice (*uttarāyaṇa*). It sits within the [Anuśāsana Parva](../mahabharata/13-anushasana-parva.md), the great post-war book of instruction, immediately after the Viṣṇu litany. [scriptural]
- **Manuscript / academic time** — Text-critically the hymn is part of the heavily **interpolated dāna-stotra mass** of Anuśāsana, generally regarded as **later than the war-narrative core**; the Mahābhārata reached near its received shape around the turn of the Common Era (roughly **c. 400 BCE – 400 CE**). The Liṅga-Purāṇa recension is a separate, Purāṇic-period composition. Keep the in-story Dvāpara setting distinct from the historical layering of the texts. [scholarly] [disputed]

---

## The frame-story — Yudhiṣṭhira's question and Kṛṣṇa's answer

- **Yudhiṣṭhira asks for Śiva's names** — Having heard the **Viṣṇu Sahasranāma** from Bhīṣma, Yudhiṣṭhira now asks for the thousand names of **Mahādeva (Śiva/Rudra)** — the supreme ascetic god — wishing to praise Him too. (Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana Parva XIII) [scriptural]
- **Bhīṣma defers to Kṛṣṇa** — Distinctively, the grandsire **does not recite this hymn himself**; he declares that Śiva's glory is beyond his own power to expound and that **Kṛṣṇa (Vāsudeva)**, present at the bedside, is the fit teacher of Śiva's names — a striking structural contrast with the Viṣṇu hymn, which Bhīṣma delivers directly. (Anuśāsana Parva XIII) [scriptural]
- **Kṛṣṇa's source: the sage Upamanyu** — Kṛṣṇa explains that he himself learned the thousand names and the worship of Śiva from the ṛṣi **Upamanyu**, the great Śiva-devotee, during his own austerities on Mount **Himavat / Kailāsa**; he then recites the names as Upamanyu taught them. (Anuśāsana Parva XIII) [scriptural]
- **Kṛṣṇa's penance and boon from Śiva** — The frame embeds Kṛṣṇa's own narrative of having propitiated Śiva by severe tapas to obtain **sons (notably Sāmba) and boons**, anchoring even the avatāra of Viṣṇu as a worshipper of Śiva — a deliberate **Hari–Hara harmonizing** gesture. (Anuśāsana Parva XIII) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (the reciter):* in some retellings the emphasis is that **Upamanyu himself** first hymns Śiva and Kṛṣṇa repeats it; in the standard Anuśāsana text the **thousand names proper are spoken by Kṛṣṇa**. [regional]

## The Upamanyu charter — the devotee who saw the milk-ocean

- **Upamanyu's childhood vow** — The embedded backstory tells of **Upamanyu**, a boy who, tasting milk only once and craving it, is told by his mother that milk is the gift of Śiva's grace alone; he resolves to win it by **austerity directed to Śiva**. (Anuśāsana Parva XIII, Upamanyu legend) [scriptural]
- **The ocean of milk as boon** — By terrible penance — in some tellings preparing to offer his own body into fire — Upamanyu compels Śiva's appearance; Śiva (testing him first in the guise of **Indra**) grants him an **ocean of milk** and the boon of unwavering devotion, making him the archetypal Śiva-bhakta. (Anuśāsana Parva XIII) [scriptural] [folk]
- **The narrative charter** — This Upamanyu episode functions as the **named narrative charter** of the hymn: it establishes the unbroken **lineage of transmission** (Śiva → Upamanyu → Kṛṣṇa → Yudhiṣṭhira) that licenses the recitation, exactly as the Bhīṣma frame licenses the Viṣṇu hymn. [scholarly]

## Structure of the Mahābhārata (Anuśāsana) recension

- **The thousand Rudra-names** — The body lists a thousand names heavy with **Rudra-Śaiva theology** — among them **Sthāṇu, Hara, Mahādeva, Śaṃkara, Bhava, Śarva, Īśāna, Tryambaka, Nīlakaṇṭha, Kapardin, Paśupati, Maheśvara** — fusing the Vedic terrible **Rudra** with the classical auspicious **Śiva**, and including names drawn from the cremation-ground, ascetic, and cosmic-destroyer registers. (Anuśāsana Śiva Sahasranāma, main body) [scholarly]
- **The "eight-thousand" frame** — A celebrated conceit of this recension is that Śiva's names are in truth **innumerable** — sometimes glossed as **aṣṭa-sahasra / eight thousand** or beyond — of which only a thousand are here uttered as a representative garland; the hymn signals its own incompleteness before the *aparimeya* (immeasurable) god, a note it shares with the [Śiva-mahimnaḥ stotra](shiva-mahimna-stotra.md). (Anuśāsana frame) [scholarly]
- **The phala-śruti** — A closing *phala-śruti* ("hearing of the fruit") promises that one who hears or recites the names gains release from sin and sorrow, long life, progeny, and final union with Śiva — paralleling the fruit-promise of the Viṣṇu hymn. (Anuśāsana Parva XIII, *phala-śruti*) [scriptural]

## The distinct Liṅga Purāṇa recension

- **A separate thousand names** — The **Liṅga Purāṇa** preserves its own **Śiva Sahasranāma**, with a different selection and ordering of names and its own frame; it is the most-cited *non-Mahābhārata* recension and is widely used in temple and *liṅga* worship. (*Liṅga Purāṇa*, Śiva-sahasranāma) [scholarly]
- **Many recensions, not one** — Beyond these two, distinct thousand-name lists of Śiva appear in the **Śiva Purāṇa, Vāyu Purāṇa, Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, Mahābhārata Śānti Parva (a second, shorter Śaiva name-list), and the Padma Purāṇa** — tradition counts on the order of **eight** recognized recensions. The names overlap heavily but the texts are independent; **never merge them into a single canonical list**. (Purāṇic corpus) [scholarly] [disputed]
  - *Variant (numbering & parsing):* as with the Viṣṇu hymn, commentators differ on how to split compounds to reach **exactly 1,000**, and counts vary by edition; the totals are **recension-dependent**. [disputed]

## Themes and devotional use

- **Rudra-to-Śiva synthesis** — The litany's theological work is to bind the fierce Vedic **Rudra** (of the Śatarudrīya / Rudram) to the classical, gracious **Śiva**, presenting destroyer, ascetic, healer, and supreme lord as one — the name-hymn equivalent of the synthesis dramatized in the [Śiva myth-cycle](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md). [scholarly]
- **Hari–Hara reconciliation** — Because **Kṛṣṇa (Viṣṇu's avatāra)** is its reciter and a Śiva-worshipper within the frame, the hymn is read as a charter of **sectarian harmony**, the Śaiva mirror of the Pārvatī–Śiva "Rāma equals the thousand" coda appended to the [Viṣṇu Sahasranāma](vishnu-sahasranama.md). [scholarly]
- **Living recitation** — The Śiva Sahasranāma is chanted in **Śiva temples and homes**, especially on **Mondays (Somavāra), pradoṣa, and Mahā-śivarātri**, often during **abhiṣeka** of the liṅga with the names recited one per offering of bilva-leaf or water (*sahasranāma-arcana*); it stands beside the [Śiva-mahimnaḥ stotra](shiva-mahimna-stotra.md) and the Rudram in Śaiva liturgy. [folk] [regional]
