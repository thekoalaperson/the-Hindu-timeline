# Vishnu Sahasranama: The Thousand Names of Vishnu

> *Up one level: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · The supreme litany of **a thousand names of Viṣṇu/Vāsudeva**, taught by the dying [Bhīṣma](../characters/bhishma.md) to Yudhiṣṭhira in the [Anuśāsana Parva](../mahabharata/13-anushasana-parva.md) of the Mahābhārata — the most-recited Vaiṣṇava stotra in living practice, sibling to the Śaiva [Śiva Sahasranāma](shiva-sahasranama.md) and to the Advaita song [Bhaja Govindam](bhaja-govindam.md).*

The **Viṣṇu Sahasranāma** (विष्णुसहस्रनाम, "the Thousand Names of Viṣṇu") is a litany of **one thousand names (and epithets) of Viṣṇu**, embedded in the **Anuśāsana Parva** (Book 13) of the **Mahābhārata**. It is framed as the answer the dying patriarch **Bhīṣma**, lying on his **bed of arrows (śara-talpa)**, gives to **Yudhiṣṭhira** when the new king asks him for the highest refuge and the easiest dharma. Recited daily across **Vaiṣṇava and Smārta** households and temples, glossed by a famous *bhāṣya* of **Ādi Śaṅkara**, it stands as the foremost name-litany (*nāma-stotra*) of the Vaiṣṇava world. [scholarly]

---

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural / narrative time** — The teaching is delivered in the **Dvāpara Yuga**, in the aftermath of the **Kurukṣetra war**, during the days Bhīṣma lies dying and discourses to Yudhiṣṭhira before relinquishing his life at the winter solstice (*uttarāyaṇa*). It sits within the Mahābhārata's [Anuśāsana Parva](../mahabharata/13-anushasana-parva.md), the great post-war book of instruction. [scriptural]
- **Manuscript / academic time** — Text-critically the stotra is a layer of the **Śānti–Anuśāsana didactic mass** of the epic, generally regarded as **later than the war-narrative core**; the Mahābhārata reached something near its received shape in the centuries around the turn of the Common Era (roughly **c. 400 BCE – 400 CE**). Śaṅkara's commentary (traditionally **8th c. CE**) is the first major exegesis. Keep the in-story Dvāpara setting distinct from the historical-period composition of the text. [scholarly] [disputed]

## The frame-story — Yudhiṣṭhira's question to Bhīṣma

- **The setting on the bed of arrows** — After the **Kurukṣetra war**, the grandsire **Bhīṣma**, pierced and resting on his *śara-talpa*, awaits the auspicious northern solstice to die; **Yudhiṣṭhira**, grief-struck and burdened by kingship, approaches him with **Kṛṣṇa** present, to receive instruction on dharma, governance, and liberation. (Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana Parva) [scriptural]
- **The six questions** — Tradition crystallizes Yudhiṣṭhira's inquiry into a set of questions, chief among them: *Kim ekaṃ daivataṃ loke?* — "**What is the one supreme deity in the world? What is the highest goal? By praising and worshipping whom does a mortal attain the auspicious? Which is, in your view, the greatest dharma (the easiest, surest path)?**" (Anuśāsana Parva XIII.135) [scriptural]
- **Bhīṣma's answer** — Bhīṣma replies that the supreme refuge is the **Lord of all, Viṣṇu / Vāsudeva** — the eternal, the Puruṣottama; that praising Him with the **thousand names** is the highest and easiest dharma, by which a man is freed from all sorrow. He then recites the **sahasranāma**. (Anuśāsana Parva XIII.135) [scriptural]
- **The "easiest dharma" emphasis** — A defining note of the frame is *accessibility*: against the elaborate ritual and ascetic disciplines, Bhīṣma names the **simple recitation of the divine names** as the path open to all — the seed of the later Vaiṣṇava theology of *nāma-saṅkīrtana* (salvation by the Name). [scholarly]
  - *Variant (vulgate numbering):* in the **vulgate / Kumbhakonam** editions the chapter is commonly cited as **Anuśāsana Parva, adhyāya ~149**; the **Pune Critical Edition** places the hymn in adhyāya **135**. Chapter numbers thus differ by recension — both point to the same text. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (the introductory verses):* the prefatory and concluding stanzas (the *pūrva-* and *uttara-bhāga*, including the meditation verses and *phala-śruti*) are partly **interpolations standardized in liturgical use**, not all present uniformly in every manuscript line. [disputed]

## Structure of the hymn

- **The thousand names** — The body is **107 (or so) ślokas** in *anuṣṭubh* metre listing the names — among them **Viśvam, Viṣṇu, Vaṣaṭkāra, Bhūta-bhavya-bhavat-prabhu, Bhūta-kṛt, Bhūta-bhṛt, Bhāva, Bhūtātmā**, opening the litany, and culminating in epithets such as **Śrī-vatsa-vakṣas, Śrī-vāsa, Śrī-pati, Sarva-pratāpana**. Many names recur or overlap, and commentators differ on how to parse compounds into exactly one thousand. (Viṣṇu Sahasranāma, main body) [scholarly]
- **The Vāsudeva refrain** — The most-quoted single line is the closing summary, *Vāsudevāśrayo martyo Vāsudevaparāyaṇaḥ / sarva-pāpa-viśuddhātmā yāti brahma sanātanam* — "the mortal who takes refuge in Vāsudeva, devoted to Vāsudeva, purified of all sin, attains the eternal Brahman." (Viṣṇu Sahasranāma, *uttara-bhāga*) [scholarly]
- **The framing apparatus (liturgical add-ons)** — In daily practice the names are wrapped in a standardized frame: an opening **dhyāna (meditation verse)** on Viṣṇu reclining on Śeṣa, the **pūrva-bhāga** (Yudhiṣṭhira–Bhīṣma dialogue), the names proper, the **phala-śruti**, and a closing **dialogue of Pārvatī and Śiva / hymn to the Name**. (received liturgical text) [scholarly]

## The phala-shruti — the promised fruits

- **Freedom from sorrow and sin** — The *phala-śruti* (the "hearing of the fruit") promises that one who **hears or recites** the thousand names is released from all grief, fear, and sin, and gains long life, fame, prosperity, health, and strength. (Viṣṇu Sahasranāma, *phala-śruti*) [scriptural]
- **The name above all names** — A celebrated verse declares that **uttering the single name "Rāma" (or contemplating Viṣṇu) equals reciting the thousand** — *śrī-rāma rāma rāmeti … sahasra-nāma tat tulyam* — fusing the Viṣṇu litany with **Rāma-nāma** devotion. (concluding verses) [folk] [late text]
- **Śiva's praise of the Name** — A framing dialogue has **Pārvatī ask Śiva** the easy way to recite the thousand names, and Śiva answer that the single name **"Rāma"** is equal to the whole — a deliberate harmonizing of Śaiva and Vaiṣṇava devotion within the stotra's apparatus. [folk] [regional]
  - *Variant:* this Pārvatī–Śiva colloquy and the "Rāma equals the thousand" verse are **appended frame-material** popularized in recitation manuals; they are not part of the core Anuśāsana-Parva names-list. [disputed]

## Ādi Śaṅkara's commentary

- **The standard bhāṣya** — **Ādi Śaṅkarācārya** wrote the most influential commentary, the **Viṣṇu-sahasranāma-bhāṣya**, glossing each name — frequently giving **multiple Advaitic meanings** for a single epithet and reading the whole as praise of the **one non-dual Brahman** under the form of Viṣṇu. It is the principal reason the hymn is canonical for **Smārtas** as well as Vaiṣṇavas. (Śaṅkara, *Viṣṇu-sahasranāma-bhāṣya*) [scholarly]
- **Sectarian afterlife** — Later **Vaiṣṇava ācāryas** produced their own glosses: **Parāśara Bhaṭṭa's *Bhagavad-guṇa-darpaṇa*** in the **Śrī-Vaiṣṇava (Viśiṣṭādvaita)** tradition is the most important, reading the names through Rāmānuja's theology rather than Advaita. The same thousand names thus carry **rival philosophical readings** — never merge them. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Madhva tradition):* **Dvaita** commentators likewise expound the names within Madhva's dualist framework, stressing Viṣṇu's supremacy and difference from the soul. [scholarly]

## Daily liturgical use

- **The most-recited Vaiṣṇava stotra** — The Viṣṇu Sahasranāma is chanted **daily in homes and temples** across India, especially on **Saturdays, Ekādaśī, and during Dhanur-māsa**, and is a fixture of **Smārta and Vaiṣṇava** observance alike; learning it by heart is a common act of lifelong devotion. [folk]
- **Modern fixing in the ear** — The recording by **M. S. Subbulakshmi** is the canonical popular rendition, learned and played in countless households; its measured chant set the standard cadence for modern recitation. [regional]
- **Sibling litanies** — It is the Vaiṣṇava counterpart to the **[Śiva Sahasranāma](shiva-sahasranama.md)** (itself also taught in the epic's didactic books) and to the many *sahasranāma* of the Goddess (Lalitā-sahasranāma); within the Mahābhārata it belongs to Bhīṣma's great post-war [Anuśāsana](../mahabharata/13-anushasana-parva.md) teaching. [scholarly]
