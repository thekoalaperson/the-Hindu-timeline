# Sri Suktam (Lakshmi Sukta)

> *Up one level: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · The **Śrī Sūkta** (श्रीसूक्तम्, also **Lakṣmī Sūkta**), the principal Vedic hymn to **[Śrī–Lakṣmī](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/lakshmi.md)**, goddess of fortune, abundance, and sovereignty — a **khila** (appended) hymn to **Ṛgveda maṇḍala 5**, daily liturgical staple of Lakṣmī worship, and the Vedic charter on which all later Lakṣmī devotion is hung.*

The **Śrī Sūkta** is the oldest sustained praise of the goddess **Śrī / Lakṣmī** in the Vedic corpus and the seed-text of her entire cult. It is not part of the received *saṃhitā* proper but belongs to the **Ṛgveda khilāni** — a small body of "appendix" hymns transmitted alongside the Ṛgveda, this one attached after the hymns of **maṇḍala 5**. In roughly **fifteen to sixteen ṛcs** (verses) it invokes Śrī as **golden-hued (hiraṇya-varṇā), lotus-borne, moist with abundance, attended by elephants and the radiance of gold**, and asks her to drive out her opposite, **Alakṣmī** (misfortune, want). Recited as one of the **pañca-sūkta** abhiṣeka set and at the heart of every formal **Lakṣmī pūjā**, it converts the abstract Vedic notion of *śrī* ("splendour, prosperity, glory") into a goddess one can bathe, seat, and feed. It complements the deity treatment in **[Lakṣmī](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/lakshmi.md)**. [scholarly]

## Canonical placement — a khila of the Ṛgveda

- **A khila hymn, not a saṃhitā hymn** — The Śrī Sūkta belongs to the **Ṛgveda khilāni**, the corpus of supplementary hymns preserved (notably in the Kashmir/Bāṣkala recension) outside the 1,028 hymns of the received Śākala Ṛgveda; it is conventionally placed as a **khila to maṇḍala 5**. (*Ṛgveda khilāni*) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (numbering):* editions assign it variously — commonly **RVKh 5.87** territory or printed simply as *Śrī Sūkta* — and most printed liturgical texts give **fifteen ṛcs**, some recensions **sixteen**, with **phala-śruti** (fruit-of-recitation) verses appended later. [disputed]
- **Mixed and late-archaic register** — Its metre and language are **post-archaic** relative to the family books; scholars read it as a **late-Vedic / early-classical** composition that nonetheless became liturgically "Vedic," which is why a fully personified, lotus-and-elephant goddess appears here rather than in the older maṇḍalas. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Ṛṣi and devatā** — Liturgical tradition assigns the hymn the **deity Śrī (Lakṣmī)** and, in the *viniyoga*, the **ṛṣi Ānanda-Kardama-Ciklīta** and **Śrī herself / a "son of" lineage** — the "children of Śrī" named within the hymn becoming its seers, a hallmark of khila *viniyoga*. (recitation *viniyoga*) [regional]

## The imagery — golden, lotus-borne, elephant-attended

- **Hiraṇya-varṇā — the golden goddess (v. 1)** — The opening *ṛc* invokes Agni to summon **Śrī who is gold-coloured, gold-complexioned, wearing gold and silver, garlanded, moon-like (candrāṃ)** — fortune imagined as luminous precious metal. (*Śrī Sūkta* 1) [scholarly]
- **Tāṃ ma āvaha — "bring her to me"** — The refrain asks **Agni** (then Jātavedas) to **convey the goddess near**, from whom one obtains **cattle, horses, gold, and attendants** — the hymn's mechanism is *invitation*: Śrī is a goddess who must be **brought and seated**. (*Śrī Sūkta* 2–3) [scholarly]
- **Aśva-pūrvāṃ ratha-madhyāṃ hasti-nāda-prabodhinīm** — Śrī is **"preceded by horses, throned amid chariots, awakened by the trumpeting of elephants"** — the **gaja / elephant motif** that becomes Gaja-Lakṣmī, the goddess lustrated by elephants. (*Śrī Sūkta* 3) [scholarly]
- **Padma-sthitāṃ padma-varṇām — the lotus** — She is **lotus-seated, lotus-hued, lotus-eyed, lotus-born (padme-sthitā)**; the **padma** is her throne, her colour, and her emblem at once — the single most enduring icon of Lakṣmī. (*Śrī Sūkta* 5, 8, 13) [scholarly]
- **Ārdrāṃ … karīṣiṇīm — moist and dung-rich** — Śrī is called **"moist" (ārdrā), abounding in dung/manure (karīṣiṇī), nourishing** — strikingly agrarian imagery: fortune as **fertile, wet, fecund earth**, not mere coin. (*Śrī Sūkta* 11–12) [scholarly]
- **The driving-out of Alakṣmī** — Several verses ask Śrī to **expel her dark sister Alakṣmī / Nirṛti** — "**hunger, thirst, and want** I drive from me" — establishing the auspicious/inauspicious polarity (Lakṣmī vs Alakṣmī, Jyeṣṭhā) central to her cult. (*Śrī Sūkta* 6–8) [scholarly]
- **Ciklīta and the household-children of Śrī** — The hymn names **Kardama, Ciklīta, Ānanda** as "**sons of Śrī**" whom the worshipper invites to **dwell in his lineage and home** — the Vedic root of installing Lakṣmī in the household. (*Śrī Sūkta* 9–10) [scholarly]

## Place in liturgy — the pañca-sūkta and abhiṣeka

- **A core pañca-sūkta** — The Śrī Sūkta is one of the standard **five sūktas** (**Puruṣa, Nārāyaṇa, Viṣṇu/Bhū, Śrī, and Durgā / Nīla** sūktas, sets vary) chanted in temple and home **abhiṣeka** — the ritual bathing of the deity-image with water, milk, and the rest. (abhiṣeka *paddhati*) [regional]
  - *Variant (set membership):* the precise pañca-/pañcāyatana-sūkta roster differs by tradition (Śrīvaiṣṇava, Smārta, āgamic temple use); the Śrī Sūkta is near-universally included where **Lakṣmī or prosperity** is the aim. [regional] [disputed]
- **The spine of Lakṣmī pūjā** — In **Vara-Lakṣmī Vrata, Dīpāvalī Lakṣmī Pūjā, and Kojāgarī**, the Śrī Sūkta is the principal **Vedic** recitation, often with each ṛc accompanying an offering (*āvāhana*, *snāna*, *dhūpa*, *dīpa*) onto the image, **kalaśa**, or coin/gold. (Lakṣmī pūjā *vidhi*) [regional]
- **Homa and japa** — It is the standard mantra-base for **Śrī Sūkta homa** (oblations into fire, each ṛc with *svāhā*) and for repeated **japa** for wealth, attended by its own **phala-śruti** promising **prosperity, freedom from want, and the favour of Lakṣmī**. (homa *prayoga*) [regional]
- **Two clocks** — Devotionally the hymn is **timeless Vedic śruti**, co-eternal with the goddess; historically it is a **late-Vedic khila** layered onto an older Ṛgveda — the usual scriptural-vs-historical distinction. [scholarly]

## Relation to Lakṣmī's wider lore

- **The Vedic charter behind the Purāṇic goddess** — The Śrī Sūkta supplies the **iconography** (gold, lotus, elephants, *karīṣiṇī* fertility) that the Purāṇas later attach to the Lakṣmī who **rises from the churned ocean**; see **[The Churning of the Ocean (Samudra Manthana)](../samudra-manthana.md)** for her emergence and choice of Viṣṇu. [scholarly]
- **Beside the Viṣṇu hymns** — As Viṣṇu's consort, Lakṣmī's Vedic hymn is recited alongside the great Vaiṣṇava praise-texts; see the **[Viṣṇu Sahasranāma](./vishnu-sahasranama.md)**, with which the Śrī Sūkta is often paired in Vaiṣṇava nitya practice. [scholarly]
- **And the Śākta hymn-corpus** — Where the **[Saundarya-laharī](./soundarya-lahari.md)** is the great *tantric* praise of the Goddess as Lalitā, the Śrī Sūkta is the great *Vedic* praise of the Goddess as Fortune — the two ends of the hymn-spectrum for the Devī. [scholarly]

## Sources

**Primary text**
- *Śrī Sūkta* (Lakṣmī Sūkta), a **khila** hymn of the **Ṛgveda** appended to **maṇḍala 5** — c. **15–16 ṛcs** invoking **Agni/Jātavedas** to bring **Śrī**, described as *hiraṇya-varṇā* (golden), *padma-sthitā* (lotus-seated), *hasti-nāda-prabodhinī* (elephant-attended), *ārdrā / karīṣiṇī* (moist, fertile), with the expulsion of **Alakṣmī** and the naming of **Kardama, Ciklīta, Ānanda** as children of Śrī. [scholarly]

**Tradition / liturgy**
- The **Ṛgveda khilāni** corpus (Kashmir/Bāṣkala-associated supplements) — the hymn's non-saṃhitā, late-Vedic placement and its varying ṛc-count and numbering. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **Pañca-sūkta abhiṣeka** practice and **Lakṣmī pūjā vidhi** (Vara-Lakṣmī Vrata, Dīpāvalī, Kojāgarī) — the Śrī Sūkta as core abhiṣeka and pūjā recitation, with per-ṛc offerings. [regional]
- **Śrī Sūkta homa / japa** with appended **phala-śruti** — fire-oblation and repetition for prosperity and the removal of want. [regional]
- Dating and personification — the developed lotus-and-elephant goddess marks the hymn as a **late-Vedic / early-classical** layer treated liturgically as śruti. [scholarly] [disputed]
