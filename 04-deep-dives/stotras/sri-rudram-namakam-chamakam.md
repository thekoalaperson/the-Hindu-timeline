# Sri Rudram / Satarudriya (Namakam and Chamakam)

> *Up one level: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · The **Śrī Rudram** (Śatarudrīya), the supreme Vedic litany of **Rudra–Śiva** from the Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda (Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.5 + 4.7); foremost Śaiva abhiṣeka-chant and the matrix of the **Pañcākṣara — *Oṃ Namaḥ Śivāya***. Sibling to the [Śiva Mahimna Stotra](shiva-mahimna-stotra.md), the [Śiva Sahasranāma](shiva-sahasranama.md), and the [Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra](shiva-tandava-stotra.md).*

The **Śrī Rudram**, also called the **Śatarudrīya** (शतरुद्रीय, "the hymn of the hundred Rudras") and **Rudra-praśna**, is the central Śaiva text of the **Kṛṣṇa- (Black) Yajurveda**, embedded in the **Taittirīya Saṃhitā** as two complementary sets of *anuvākas* — the **Namakam** (TS 4.5, eleven anuvākas) and the **Chamakam** (TS 4.7, eleven anuvākas). Unlike the classical praise-poems, this is not a medieval *stotra* but **śruti** — Vedic mantra of high antiquity — yet it is recited daily and on festivals like any hymn, and is **the** scriptural source of the most famous of all Śaiva utterances, ***Oṃ Namaḥ Śivāya***, whose words sit at the heart of the Namakam. [scholarly]

---

## What the text is and where it sits

- **A litany inside the Yajurveda** — The Śatarudrīya is a hymn to **Rudra** that names, propitiates, and salutes the god in his countless aspects. The Taittirīya recension (TS 4.5/4.7) is the most recited; parallel Śatarudrīya texts exist in the **Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā 16** (Śukla-Yajurveda) and the **Maitrāyaṇī** and **Kāṭhaka** Saṃhitās — the same core hymn transmitted across Yajurvedic schools. (Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.5, 4.7; Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā 16) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Śukla recension):* the Vājasaneyi/Mādhyandina Śatarudrīya (VS 16) orders and words the salutations somewhat differently and is chanted in north-Indian Śukla-Yajurvedic lineages; the Taittirīya is standard in the south. Never conflate the two recensions. [regional]
- **"Hundred Rudras"** — *Śata-rudrīya* means the hymn of the **hundred (śata = "a hundred / innumerable") Rudras**, i.e. Rudra recognised in a hundredfold of forms and persons; the number is plenitude, not a literal count. (Śatarudrīya, title) [scholarly]
- **Two parts, two movements** — The **Namakam** (from *namaḥ*, "salutation") pacifies and praises Rudra; the **Chamakam** (from *ca me*, "and to me") petitions for boons. Together they enact the full arc: first **propitiation**, then **prayer**. (TS 4.5 + 4.7)

## The Namakam (TS 4.5) — salutation and pacification of the fierce-and-gracious Rudra

- **The opening prayer for grace** — The first anuvāka opens *Oṃ namaste rudra manyava utota iṣave namaḥ* ("salutation to thy wrath, O Rudra, and to thine arrow"), beseeching Rudra to turn aside his fearsome weapon and become *śiva* — auspicious, benign. The hymn's first move is to **disarm the terrible god by reverence**. (TS 4.5.1)
- **The seat of *Oṃ Namaḥ Śivāya*** — Within the Namakam stands the line ***namaḥ śivāya ca śivatarāya ca*** ("salutation to the auspicious one, and to the more auspicious") — the scriptural womb from which the **Pañcākṣara mantra *Oṃ Namaḥ Śivāya*** ("five-syllabled" *na-maḥ-śi-vā-ya*) is drawn. The supreme Śaiva mantra is thus a **Vedic phrase**, not a later coinage. (TS 4.5; Pañcākṣara) [scholarly]
- **The dual face: Rudra the fierce and Śiva the kind** — The hymn's theology is that the **destroyer-archer** and the **gracious healer** are one god; it repeatedly asks the wrathful Rudra to show his **śiva (benign)** aspect, the very root of the name "Śiva." (TS 4.5)
- **Rudra in every place and person** — The middle anuvākas salute Rudra as immanent in **all things and all roles** — the blue-necked (*nīlagrīva*), the thousand-eyed, lord of thieves and of householders, of forests and of paths, in trees and waters, in the carpenter and the potter, the soldier and the hunter — a radical vision of the god **present in every being and trade**. (TS 4.5.2–9)
- **The arrow, the bow, and the plea to lay them down** — Recurrent imagery of Rudra's **bow (*dhanus*) and arrows**: the worshipper begs him to unstring the bow, blunt the shafts, and be *aghora* (not-terrible). (TS 4.5)
- **The Tryambaka / Mṛtyuñjaya seed** — The Namakam's later portion carries the **Tryambaka mantra** strand (*tryambakaṃ yajāmahe...*, the three-eyed one) associated with healing and release from death — the Vedic root behind the **Mahā-Mṛtyuñjaya** prayer. (TS 4.5; cf. Ṛgveda 7.59.12) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (mantra-count):* the Namakam is traditionally said to contain a structured set of salutations and mantras; printed and pāṭha traditions count its mantras differently (the round figures cited in pūjā-paddhatis are conventional, not text-critical). [disputed]

## The Chamakam (TS 4.7) — the "ca me" enumeration of boons

- **The petition after the praise** — Having pacified Rudra, the worshipper now **asks**: each clause of the Chamakam ends *...ca me yajñena kalpantām* ("...and to me, may these be granted through the sacrifice"). It is a vast **litany of desiderata** offered in the same breath as the Vedic *yajña*. (TS 4.7)
- **Everything a human life could want** — The boons run from the material to the spiritual: **food and waters, prosperity and progeny, health and long life, cattle and grain, intelligence and *ojas* (vigour), faith and truth, and finally the sacrificial and cosmic powers** — a comprehensive prayer for total well-being. (TS 4.7.1–11)
- **The number-litany (the famous odd/even sequence)** — One anuvāka enumerates the **odd numbers (1, 3, 5 ... 33)** and the **even numbers (4, 8, 12 ... 48)** as *ca me* requests, asking that all numerical orders of reality, the **33 Vedic gods** among them, be made favourable through sacrifice. (TS 4.7.11) [scholarly]
- **Complement to the Namakam** — Where the Namakam **negates and pacifies** (*namaḥ*, "not-this, be gentle"), the Chamakam **affirms and requests** (*ca me*, "and to me"); the pair is held to be incomplete if either is chanted alone in formal worship. (TS 4.5 + 4.7)

## Ritual life: abhiṣeka, Rudra-japa, and the nyāsa frame

- **The supreme abhiṣeka-chant** — The Śrī Rudram is the premier text for **Rudrābhiṣeka** — the ceremonial bathing (*abhiṣeka*) of the Śiva-liṅga with water, milk, honey, ghee, curd, and other substances while the Namakam (and often the Chamakam) is intoned. It is **the** Vedic accompaniment to liṅga-worship in Śaiva temples. (living liturgical tradition) [scholarly]
- **Rudra, Ekādaśa-rudra, Laghu- and Mahā-rudra** — Recitation is graded by **multiplication**: one **Namakam with eleven Chamakam anuvākas interleaved** is a **Rudra (Ekādaśa-rudri)**; **eleven such Rudras = a Laghu-rudra**; **eleven Laghu-rudras (121 cycles) = a Mahā-rudra**; **eleven Mahā-rudras = an Ati-rudra** — escalating tiers of the same hymn used for great temple yajñas. (Rudra-yajña paddhati) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (interleaving order):* in the **Ekādaśa-rudri** the single Namakam is followed by the **first Chamakam anuvāka**, the cycle repeating so each of the eleven Chamakam anuvākas pairs once with a Namakam recitation; sequencing differs slightly by śākhā and ritual manual. [regional]
- **The nyāsa and aṅga frame** — Formal recitation is bracketed by the **Rudra-praśna nyāsa / laghu-nyāsa** — the assignment (*nyāsa*) of mantras and deities to the parts of the body to consecrate the reciter — and concludes with the **Śānti** (peace invocation); these envelopes turn the śruti hymn into a structured *japa*. (pūjā-paddhati; laghu-nyāsa) [scholarly]
- **Festival and daily use** — The Rudram is chanted daily in Śaiva temples and especially on **Mahā-śivarātri**, **Pradoṣa**, **Kārttika** and **Śrāvaṇa** Mondays, and at Rudra-homa/yajña — the same calendar that carries the [Bilvāṣṭaka](bilvashtaka.md) bilva-offering and the [Śiva Mahimna Stotra](shiva-mahimna-stotra.md). (devotional tradition)
- **Phala and standing** — The tradition holds the Śatarudrīya among the most potent of all Vedic recitations, a remover of sin, disease, and fear; the **Śatapatha** and later texts already glorify Rudra-japa, and the Purāṇas treat the Śatarudrīya as the highest hymn to Śiva. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa; Purāṇic māhātmya) [scholarly]

## Cosmic placement — the "two clocks"

- **Text-critical time** — The Śatarudrīya is **genuine Vedic śruti** of the Saṃhitā layer (Kṛṣṇa- and Śukla-Yajurveda), among the **oldest** continuously recited Hindu texts — far older than the classical *stotra* corpus. Here the scriptural clock and the historical clock nearly coincide: this is **not** a medieval composition with a Vedic frame, but Vedic antiquity itself. [scholarly]
- **In-narrative time** — The hymn is **not a yuga-bound narrative**; it is timeless liturgy addressed to the eternal Rudra. Its only "story" is the implicit drama of **pacifying the wrathful god into his gracious form** — a cross-yuga, ever-present cultic act rather than a dated event. [scholarly]
- **Keep the clocks distinct** — Where the [Śiva Mahimna Stotra](shiva-mahimna-stotra.md) and [Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra](shiva-tandava-stotra.md) are classical poems traditionally ascribed to Puṣpadanta and Rāvaṇa, the Rudram needs no such ascription: it is **anonymous Vedic revelation**, and its prestige is precisely that it is *śruti*, not *smṛti*. [scholarly]

## Sources

**Primary text (śruti)**
- **Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda, Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.5** — the **Namakam** (Śrī Rudra-praśna), eleven anuvākas; opening *Oṃ namaste rudra manyava...*, containing *namaḥ śivāya ca* (the seed of *Oṃ Namaḥ Śivāya*), the Rudra-in-all-forms salutations, the bow-and-arrow pacification, and the Tryambaka strand.
- **Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda, Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.7** — the **Chamakam**, eleven anuvākas; the *...ca me yajñena kalpantām* enumeration of boons, including the odd/even number-litany and the 33 gods.
- **Śukla-Yajurveda, Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā 16** — the parallel **Śatarudrīya** of the Mādhyandina school (north-Indian recension). [regional]
- Parallel Śatarudrīya texts in the **Maitrāyaṇī** and **Kāṭhaka** Saṃhitās (other Yajurvedic schools). [scholarly]

**Tradition / context**
- **Tryambaka / Mahā-Mṛtyuñjaya** lineage — TS 4.5 Tryambaka mantra; cf. **Ṛgveda 7.59.12** (*tryambakaṃ yajāmahe*). [scholarly]
- **Rudra-yajña paddhati** — the graded recitation tiers (Rudra / Ekādaśa-rudri / Laghu-rudra / Mahā-rudra / Ati-rudra) and the Namakam–Chamakam interleaving; the **laghu-nyāsa / Rudra-praśna nyāsa** and concluding Śānti. [scholarly] [regional]
- **Rudrābhiṣeka** — Śatarudrīya as the Vedic accompaniment to liṅga-bathing in Śaiva temple liturgy; Mahā-śivarātri, Pradoṣa, Kārttika/Śrāvaṇa Monday use. (living tradition) [scholarly]
- Glorification of Rudra-japa in the **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa** and Purāṇic māhātmya. [scholarly]

**Note on variants**
- Recension differences (Taittirīya vs. Vājasaneyi), conventional mantra-counts in pūjā-paddhatis, and the exact interleaving order in Ekādaśa-rudri are catalogued as **regional/textual variants**, not as a single fixed original. [disputed]
