# Nirvana Shatkam / Atma Shatkam

> *Up one level: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Ādi Śaṅkara's six-verse signature of pure Advaita — "I am not mind, intellect, ego… I am the form of Consciousness-Bliss; I am Śiva, I am Śiva" (*Chidānanda-rūpaḥ Śivo'ham Śivo'ham*) — the lyric distillation of *nirguṇa* Brahman and *neti-neti*; companion to his [Bhaja Govindam](bhaja-govindam.md), [Saundarya-laharī](soundarya-lahari.md), [Śiva-mahimna-stotra](shiva-mahimna-stotra.md) and the [Dakṣiṇāmūrti](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dakshinamurti.md) hymn, rooted in the [Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad](../../90-literature-corpus/upanishads/mandukya-upanishad.md) and the [Brahma-sūtras](../../90-literature-corpus/darshanas/brahma-sutras.md).*

The **Nirvāṇa Ṣaṭkam** (निर्वाण षट्कम्; also **Ātma Ṣaṭkam**, "Six [Verses] on the Self," and known from its refrain as **Śivo'ham** / **Ātma-ṣaṭka-stotram**) is a six-verse Sanskrit hymn ascribed to **Ādi Śaṅkarācārya**, the systematizer of **Advaita Vedānta** (see [Ādi Śaṅkara](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/adi-shankara.md) and the [Ācāryas & Sampradāyas](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/README.md) overview). In six tightly-built stanzas — each closing on the refrain **"Chidānanda-rūpaḥ Śivo'ham Śivo'ham"** ("I am the form of Consciousness-and-Bliss; I am Śiva, I am Śiva") — it negates every layer of body, mind, emotion, relationship, ritual and cosmos to declare the Self as the one non-dual **Brahman** identified with **Śiva**. It is arguably the single most-quoted short Vedāntic *stotra*, prized as a pure poetic enactment of **neti-neti** ("not this, not this") and of **nirguṇa** (attributeless) Brahman. [scholarly]

---

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural / hagiographic time** — In its traditional legend the hymn is spoken *in situ* by the boy or young **Śaṅkara** at his first meeting with his guru **Govinda Bhagavatpāda** (or Gauḍapāda's line) on the banks of the **Narmadā**; the frame belongs to the saint's biography (*Śaṅkara-vijaya* literature) and carries no yuga-claim beyond "within our present age." [hagiographic]
- **Manuscript / academic time** — Text-critically the poem is a **post-Vedic, Kali-yuga historical-period stotra**: if by Śaṅkara himself it dates to the traditional **8th c. CE** (some scholars place Śaṅkara 7th–early 8th c.); as with most short hymns in his name, the ascription is devotional and not all academics accept it as autograph. Either way it sits in **recorded history**, not mythic time — keep the saintly ascription distinct from the manuscript record. [scholarly] [disputed]

---

## The frame-story — "Who are you?"

- **The guru's question** — In the most-told legend, **Govinda Bhagavatpāda**, meditating in a Narmadā cave, is roused by the young Śaṅkara and asks **"Who are you?"** (*ko'si tvam*). The six verses are Śaṅkara's reply: not a biographical answer but a thoroughgoing **negation of every false identification**, ending each time in his true identity as Śiva-Consciousness. (traditional frame) [folk]
  - *Variant (the interlocutor):* some retellings make the questioner **Gauḍapāda** or simply an unnamed sage; the point of the frame is identical — the Self cannot be named by body or biography. [folk] [disputed]
  - *Variant (no frame at all):* many editions transmit the hymn with **no narrative frame**, treating it purely as a meditation-text on the **mahāvākya** "*ahaṃ brahmāsmi*" / "*tat tvam asi*"; the cave-story is a later devotional setting. [scholarly]
- **Why "Nirvāṇa"** — The title **Nirvāṇa** ("extinction, blowing-out") marks the hymn's goal: the extinguishing of the ego-sense (*ahaṅkāra*) and of all dualistic limitation, leaving only the *chidānanda* Self. The alternate title **Ātma Ṣaṭkam** simply names it "the six [verses] on the Self." (traditional)

## Verse-by-verse — the ladder of negation

- **Verse 1 — not the inner instrument, not the senses** — *Mano-buddhy-ahaṅkāra-cittāni nāham* — "I am not mind, intellect, ego or memory; not the ears, tongue, nose or eyes; not ether, earth, fire or air — **I am Consciousness-Bliss; I am Śiva.**" Negates the **antaḥkaraṇa** (inner organ), the **jñānendriyas**, and the **five elements**. (Nirvāṇa Ṣaṭkam 1)
- **Verse 2 — not the vital airs, not the sheaths** — *Na ca prāṇa-saṃjño na vai pañca-vāyuḥ* — "I am not the *prāṇa* nor the five vital airs, nor the seven *dhātus* (bodily constituents), nor the five *kośas* (sheaths); not speech, hands, feet or organs of action." Negates the **prāṇic body** and the **pañca-kośa** of Vedāntic anatomy. (Nirvāṇa Ṣaṭkam 2) [scholarly]
- **Verse 3 — not the emotions, not the pairs of opposites** — *Na me dveṣa-rāgau na me lobha-mohau* — "I have no aversion or attachment, no greed or delusion, no pride or envy; **neither *dharma* nor *artha*, neither *kāma* nor *mokṣa*** [as objects external to me]." Negates the **passions** and even the **four puruṣārthas** as binding aims. (Nirvāṇa Ṣaṭkam 3)
- **Verse 4 — not merit and sin, not ritual** — *Na puṇyaṃ na pāpaṃ na saukhyaṃ na duḥkham* — "No merit or sin, no pleasure or pain; **no mantra, no holy place, no Veda, no sacrifice (*yajña*)**; I am neither the eaten, the eating, nor the eater." Negates **karma, ritual and the entire apparatus of religious means** — the radical Advaita point that the realized Self transcends even scripture-as-injunction. (Nirvāṇa Ṣaṭkam 4) [scholarly]
- **Verse 5 — not death, not caste, not kin** — *Na me mṛtyu-śaṅkā na me jāti-bhedaḥ* — "I have no fear of death, no distinction of caste; **no father, no mother, no birth**; no friend or kinsman, no guru and no disciple." Negates **mortality, social identity, and all relationship** — including, strikingly, the guru-disciple bond within which the hymn is supposedly spoken. (Nirvāṇa Ṣaṭkam 5)
- **Verse 6 — the positive declaration** — *Ahaṃ nirvikalpo nirākāra-rūpaḥ* — "I am beyond all thought-distinction (*nirvikalpa*), formless (*nirākāra*); I pervade and underlie all the senses; **ever even-minded, neither bound nor free** — I am Consciousness-Bliss; I am Śiva." After five verses of *neti-neti*, the closing stanza states the **non-dual all-pervasiveness** of the Self directly. (Nirvāṇa Ṣaṭkam 6) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (verse count and order):* though "Ṣaṭkam" fixes the number at **six**, a handful of manuscripts and chant-traditions circulate the stanzas in slightly different order or with a minor textual variant in the negation-lists; the six-verse core is stable. [disputed]

## Doctrine — nirguna Brahman in song

- **Neti-neti embodied** — The hymn is the most concise lyric form of the Upaniṣadic **neti-neti** ("not this, not this") method (Bṛhadāraṇyaka): the Self is reached not by description but by stripping away every describable layer until only the witnessing Consciousness remains. [scholarly]
- **Nirguṇa over saguṇa** — Its "Śiva" is not the *saguṇa* deity of mythology but **Brahman as pure Consciousness** — Śiva read as *śivam*, the auspicious attributeless Absolute. The refrain *Śivo'ham* is thus a **mahāvākya-equivalent**, voicing "*ahaṃ brahmāsmi*" ("I am Brahman") in Śaiva vocabulary. (Advaita reading) [scholarly]
- **The pañca-kośa and turīya frame** — The negations track the **five sheaths** (annamaya through ānandamaya) and culminate in the witness of waking, dream and deep sleep — the **turīya** ("fourth") of the [Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad](../../90-literature-corpus/upanishads/mandukya-upanishad.md), the metaphysical backbone Śaṅkara inherits from Gauḍapāda. [scholarly]
- **Bhakti and jñāna fused** — Unlike the *vairāgya* of [Bhaja Govindam](bhaja-govindam.md) or the devotion of the [Śiva-mahimna-stotra](shiva-mahimna-stotra.md), the Nirvāṇa Ṣaṭkam is a **pure jñāna-stotra**: it has no praise of a deity, no petition, no narrative — its "worship" *is* the act of self-identification with Brahman. It stands beside the silent teaching of [Dakṣiṇāmūrti](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dakshinamurti.md) and the radical non-dual *avadhūta* voice of the [Avadhūta Gītā](../avadhuta-gita.md). [scholarly]

## Recitation tradition and reception

- **A staple of Advaita and Yoga practice** — The Nirvāṇa Ṣaṭkam is **memorized and chanted across Advaita maṭhas, Yoga schools, and modern Vedānta movements** as a meditation on the Self; its singable metre (*bhujaṅga-prayāta*, the "serpent's glide") and hypnotic refrain make it a favourite for *japa* and group recitation. [folk]
- **The most-cited short Vedāntic stotra** — Among the many hymns ascribed to Śaṅkara (alongside [Bhaja Govindam](bhaja-govindam.md), the [Kanakadhārā-stotra](kanakadhara-stotram.md), and the Śākta [Saundarya-laharī](soundarya-lahari.md)), the Ātma Ṣaṭkam is the one most often quoted to state **nirguṇa Advaita in a single breath** — invoked in commentaries, satsaṅgs and popular teaching wherever the bare doctrine "I am Brahman" needs a poetic voice. [scholarly]
- **Musical settings** — The hymn has been set and recorded widely in the modern period; its refrain *Śivo'ham Śivo'ham* is among the most recognizable lines of devotional-philosophical Sanskrit chant. [regional]

## Sources

- ***Nirvāṇa Ṣaṭkam / Ātma Ṣaṭkam***, attributed to **Ādi Śaṅkarācārya** — the primary six-verse text (refrain *Chidānanda-rūpaḥ Śivo'ham Śivo'ham*). Sanskrit text and translations widely available (e.g. sanskritdocuments.org; standard Śaṅkara-stotra anthologies). [scholarly]
- **Upaniṣadic and Vedānta background** — the **Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad** (with Gauḍapāda's *Kārikā*) for *turīya*; the **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad** for *neti-neti*; the **Taittirīya Upaniṣad** for the *pañca-kośa* — the conceptual scaffolding the hymn versifies. [scholarly]
- Traditional ***Śaṅkara-vijaya*** hagiographies (Mādhava-Vidyāraṇya's *Śaṅkara-dig-vijaya* et al.) — for the Narmadā / Govinda Bhagavatpāda frame-story and Śaṅkara's biography. [hagiographic]
- *Note on authorship:* the ascription to Śaṅkara and the cave-meeting frame are **devotional traditions**; academic opinion on Śaṅkara's date (traditionally 8th c. CE) and on which short stotras are genuinely his is divided. Borderline points flagged [disputed] above. [scholarly]
