# Purusha Sukta (Rigveda 10.90) — The Hymn of the Cosmic Man

> *Up one level: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · The **Puruṣa Sūkta** (पुरुषसूक्तम्), hymn **10.90 of the [Ṛgveda](../../90-literature-corpus/vedas/rigveda.md)** — the vision of the **Cosmic Man (Puruṣa)** whose primordial **sacrifice by the gods** yields the universe, the Vedas, the animals, the sun and moon, and the **four varṇas**. On the cosmic clock it narrates the moment **before time**, at the head of creation itself; on the historical clock it is a **late hymn of maṇḍala 10** [scholarly].*

The **Puruṣa Sūkta** is arguably the single most consequential hymn in the Vedic corpus: the Ṛgveda's fullest statement that **the cosmos is the body of one Person**, brought forth not by a craftsman's making but by a **sacrifice in which the victim, the offering, and the recipient are all the same primal being**. Its sixteen ṛcs became the seed of Hindu cosmogony (Virāj, the *virāṭ-puruṣa* of the Purāṇas), of **varṇa ideology** (its twelfth verse is the only Ṛgvedic mention of the four varṇas), of **Puruṣa–Nārāyaṇa theology** in the Vaiṣṇava traditions, and of a liturgical career — abhiṣeka, homa, image-installation — matched by no other sūkta. It complements the text-profile in **[Ṛgveda](../../90-literature-corpus/vedas/rigveda.md)** and the comparative survey in **[Cosmogony — the creation accounts](../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md)**. [scholarly]

## Canonical placement and attribution

- **Ṛgveda 10.90, sixteen ṛcs** — In the received Śākala saṃhitā the hymn stands as **maṇḍala 10, hymn 90**, with **sixteen verses**, fifteen in **anuṣṭubh** metre and the final verse in **triṣṭubh**. (*Ṛgveda* 10.90) [scholarly]
- **Ṛṣi Nārāyaṇa, devatā Puruṣa** — The **Sarvānukramaṇī** (traditional index) assigns the hymn the seer **Nārāyaṇa** and the deity **Puruṣa** — a ṛṣi-name that later tradition read as no accident, binding the hymn to Nārāyaṇa-theology from the index itself. (*Sarvānukramaṇī* on RV 10.90)
- **A late layer of a late maṇḍala [scholarly] [late text]** — Maṇḍala 10 is broadly agreed to be among the **youngest strata** of the Ṛgveda, and 10.90 is widely judged late even within it — on grounds of language, metre, its developed sacrificial speculation, and its unique varṇa scheme. Indologists from **Colebrooke and Max Müller** onward flagged it as comparatively modern; a common academic bracket for its composition is roughly **c. 1200–1000 BCE**, versus c. 1500–1200 BCE (or earlier) for the family books. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (traditional view):* to the tradition the hymn is **apauruṣeya śruti** — authorless, eternal, merely "seen" by the ṛṣi Nārāyaṇa — so questions of lateness do not arise; the two clocks are kept distinct here by design.
- **Parallel recensions across the Vedas** — Versions of the hymn recur, with variant verse-order and readings, in the **Śukla-Yajurveda (Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā 31)**, the **Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda (Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 3.12–13)**, the **Sāmaveda**, and the **Atharvaveda (19.6)** — an unusually wide transmission that signals its early liturgical importance. (*Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā* 31; *Taittirīya Āraṇyaka* 3; *Atharvaveda* 19.6) [scholarly]

## The hymn, verse by verse

- **vv. 1–2: The thousand-headed Puruṣa** — **"Sahasraśīrṣā puruṣaḥ sahasrākṣaḥ sahasrapāt"** — the Puruṣa has a thousand heads, a thousand eyes, a thousand feet; he **envelops the earth on every side and extends ten fingers' breadth beyond it**. He is **"all this — whatever has been and whatever is to be"**, the **lord of immortality (amṛtatvasyeśānaḥ)**, who grows yet greater by food. (*Ṛgveda* 10.90.1–2)
- **vv. 3–4: One quarter here, three quarters beyond** — Great as this cosmos is, it measures only the Puruṣa's magnitude: **all beings are one quarter (pāda) of him; three quarters are the immortal in heaven**. With one quarter he **"came down here again"** and spread out over what eats and what does not eat — transcendence stated as arithmetic. (*Ṛgveda* 10.90.3–4)
- **v. 5: Puruṣa and Virāj beget each other** — **"From him Virāj was born; from Virāj, again, the Puruṣa"** — a deliberately circular double birth. Born, he stretched beyond the earth behind and before. The feminine **Virāj** ("the wide-ruling") becomes, in later cosmology, the **virāṭ / cosmic-body principle** elaborated by the Purāṇas. (*Ṛgveda* 10.90.5)
- **vv. 6–7: The gods stake out the sacrifice** — The **devas performed a sacrifice with Puruṣa himself as the offering (puruṣeṇa haviṣā)**: **spring was its ghee, summer its fuel, autumn its oblation**. They consecrated as victim on the sacred grass the Puruṣa **born at the very beginning**; with him **the gods, the Sādhyas, and the ṛṣis** sacrificed. (*Ṛgveda* 10.90.6–7)
- **vv. 8–10: From the sacrifice, living things and sacred speech** — From that all-offered sacrifice the **clotted ghee (pṛṣadājya)** was gathered, and from it came **the creatures of air, forest, and village**. From it were born **the ṛcs and sāmans, the metres (chandas), and the yajus** — the Vedas themselves are products of the primal rite. From it came **horses and all beasts with two rows of teeth, cattle, goats, and sheep**. (*Ṛgveda* 10.90.8–10)
- **v. 11: The dismemberment question** — "When they divided the Puruṣa, **into how many portions did they apportion him? What was his mouth called, what his arms, what his thighs and feet?**" — the riddle-form question that verse 12 answers. (*Ṛgveda* 10.90.11)
- **v. 12: The varṇa verse [late text] [disputed]** — **"The Brāhmaṇa was his mouth; the Rājanya was made his two arms; his thighs became the Vaiśya; from his feet the Śūdra was born."** See the dedicated section below. (*Ṛgveda* 10.90.12)
- **vv. 13–14: The cosmos from his limbs** — **From his mind the moon; from his eye the sun; from his mouth Indra and Agni; from his breath Vāyu.** From his **navel the midspace (antarikṣa)**, from his **head the sky**, from his **feet the earth**, from his **ear the directions** — "thus they fashioned the worlds." (*Ṛgveda* 10.90.13–14)
- **v. 15: The ritual geometry** — The sacrifice had **seven enclosing-sticks (paridhi)** and **thrice-seven (21) fuel-sticks** when the gods, extending the sacrifice, **bound the Puruṣa as the victim (paśu)** — the cosmic act described in the exact idiom of the śrauta rite. (*Ṛgveda* 10.90.15)
- **v. 16: "With sacrifice the gods sacrificed to sacrifice"** — **"Yajñena yajñam ayajanta devāḥ; tāni dharmāṇi prathamāny āsan"** — the sacrifice offered to Sacrifice itself; **"these were the first ordinances (dharmas)"**; by them the mighty ones reach heaven, where the ancient **Sādhyas** and gods dwell. The verse is among the most-quoted lines of the Veda, closing several other hymns as well (cf. RV 1.164.50). (*Ṛgveda* 10.90.16)

## The cosmic-sacrifice cosmogony

- **Creation as sacrifice, not fabrication** — Unlike the craftsman-cosmogonies (Viśvakarman, RV 10.81–82) or the agnostic hymn of origins (Nāsadīya, RV 10.129), the Puruṣa Sūkta grounds the cosmos in **yajña**: the world exists because a primal offering was made, and every subsequent sacrifice re-enacts that first act. It thereby becomes the charter myth of the whole śrauta system. (*Ṛgveda* 10.90; compared in [Cosmogony — creation accounts](../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md)) [scholarly]
- **The bootstrap paradox is deliberate** — The gods who sacrifice the Puruṣa are themselves produced from him (v. 13), and Puruṣa and Virāj generate each other (v. 5): the hymn presents creation as **self-referential**, an idea the Brāhmaṇas develop into Prajāpati's self-emptying and self-restoration. (*Ṛgveda* 10.90.5, 13) [scholarly]
- **Brāhmaṇa elaboration — Puruṣa-Nārāyaṇa's sattra** — The **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa** tells of **Puruṣa Nārāyaṇa** who, desiring to surpass all beings and become the universe, performed the **pañcarātra sattra** (five-day sacrificial session) and thereby **became all this** — an early, explicit welding of the hymn's Puruṣa to the name **Nārāyaṇa**. (*Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* 13.6.1)
- **Purāṇic reuse — the virāṭ-puruṣa** — The Purāṇas absorb the hymn's scheme into the **virāṭ-puruṣa / cosmic-form** cosmology: the Bhāgavata's account of the universal form whose limbs are the worlds and whose apertures birth the devas is a systematic expansion of RV 10.90.13–14. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 2.5–2.6; see [Cosmogony — creation accounts](../../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md)) [scholarly]
- **Sāṃkhya echo, not identity [disputed]** — The technical **puruṣa** of Sāṃkhya (pure consciousness over against prakṛti) shares the name and the transcendence-motif, but scholars caution that the Sūkta's Puruṣa is a **cosmic body**, not the Sāṃkhya witness-self; the traditions themselves later braided the two. [scholarly] [disputed]

## Verse 12 — the varṇa verse and its status

- **The locus classicus of varṇa** — RV 10.90.12 is the **only passage in the entire Ṛgveda** that names all four varṇas together (Brāhmaṇa, Rājanya/Kṣatriya, Vaiśya, Śūdra) and the **first occurrence of the word Śūdra** in the corpus. Every later charter of varṇa hierarchy — e.g. **Manusmṛti 1.31**, where Brahmā emits the four varṇas from mouth, arms, thighs, and feet — descends from this verse. (*Ṛgveda* 10.90.12; *Manusmṛti* 1.31) [scholarly]
- **[late text] [disputed] — the interpolation question** — Because the four-varṇa scheme appears nowhere else in the Ṛgveda, many Indologists since **Colebrooke** and **Max Müller** have treated the hymn — or at least verse 12 — as a **late composition or insertion**, reflecting a stratified society post-dating the family books; others hold the whole hymn is simply late-Vedic and internally coherent, needing no excision. The lateness of the *hymn* is broad consensus; the verse-level "interpolation" claim is contested. [scholarly] [disputed]
  - *Variant (traditional exegesis):* the śrauta and Vedāntic commentators (e.g. **Sāyaṇa**) read the verse as an organic part of eternal śruti describing the **functional** origin of the varṇas from the divine body, with no historical layering implied.
  - *Variant (reformist readings):* modern interpreters from **Dayānanda Sarasvatī** onward have read the verse as describing **aptitude-based division of labour, not birth-based caste**; critics of caste, notably **B. R. Ambedkar**, engaged the same verse as the mythic root of hierarchy — the verse's meaning is itself a live dispute within tradition. [disputed]
- **Body-symbolism cuts both ways** — Apologetic readings stress that all four varṇas issue from the **one body** (organic unity); critical readings stress the **head-to-feet gradient** (rank). Both readings are attested in the tradition and are cataloged, not resolved, here; see **[Varṇa, āśrama and saṃskāras](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md)**. [disputed]

## Yajurveda transmission and the Uttaranārāyaṇa appendix

- **Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā 31 — the 22-verse form** — The **Śukla-Yajurveda** transmits the hymn as **adhyāya 31 of the Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā**: the sixteen Ṛgvedic verses (with variant order/readings) **plus a six-verse appendix (VS 31.17–22)** known as the **Uttaranārāyaṇa** ("the latter Nārāyaṇa section"). (*Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā* 31) [scholarly]
- **The Uttaranārāyaṇa's content [late text]** — The appendix turns the cosmogonic hymn toward **theistic praise**: it declares **"vedāham etaṃ puruṣaṃ mahāntam āditya-varṇaṃ tamasaḥ parastāt"** — "I know this great Puruṣa, sun-coloured, beyond darkness; only by knowing him does one pass over death; there is no other path" — a verse also taken up at **Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 3.8**. Its final verse names **Hrī and Lakṣmī as the Puruṣa's consorts (hrīś ca te lakṣmīś ca patnyau)** — the earliest liturgical pairing of the Puruṣa with **Lakṣmī**, cherished by Śrīvaiṣṇavas. (*Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā* 31.18, 31.22; *Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad* 3.8) [late text]
- **Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda / South Indian recitation form** — The **Taittirīya Āraṇyaka (3.12–13)** carries the version that dominates South Indian temple recitation, commonly chanted with **eighteen ṛcs plus śānti**, followed in practice by the **Nārāyaṇa Sūkta** and often the **[Śrī Sūkta](./sri-suktam.md)** — a fixed liturgical chain of Puruṣa → Nārāyaṇa → Śrī. (*Taittirīya Āraṇyaka* 3; recitation *krama*) [regional]
- **The Nārāyaṇa Sūkta as deliberate sequel** — The Nārāyaṇa Sūkta (in the Taittirīya tradition, within the **[Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad](../../90-literature-corpus/upanishads/mahanarayana-upanishad.md)** corpus) opens **"sahasraśīrṣaṃ devam"** — pointedly re-keying the Puruṣa Sūkta's first word to name the thousand-headed one **as Nārāyaṇa**, "Hari, the supreme, dwelling in the lotus of the heart." The juxtaposition in recitation *is* the theological argument. (*Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad* / Nārāyaṇa Sūkta) [late text]

## Liturgical use — abhiṣeka, homa, pratiṣṭhā

- **Head of the pañca-sūkta set** — The Puruṣa Sūkta customarily leads the **pañca-sūkta** group (Puruṣa, Nārāyaṇa, Viṣṇu, Śrī, Bhū/Nīlā sūktas; rosters vary by tradition) chanted during **abhiṣeka**, the ritual bathing of the deity image, in both temple and domestic worship. (abhiṣeka *paddhati*; cf. [Śrī Sūkta](./sri-suktam.md)) [regional]
- **Ṣoḍaśopacāra mapping** — In widespread pūjā practice the hymn's **sixteen verses are mapped one-to-one onto the sixteen upacāras** (offerings — āvāhana through puṣpāñjali) of formal worship, each ṛc accompanying one service to the deity. (pūjā *viniyoga*) [regional]
- **Puruṣa-sūkta homa and image installation** — The hymn is the mantra-base of the **Puruṣa-sūkta homa** and is prescribed in Vaiṣṇava **Āgama/Pāñcarātra** ritual for **mūrti-pratiṣṭhā** (consecration of images) and daily ārādhana — fitting, since the hymn itself narrates spirit pervading a body/form. (Pāñcarātra *prayoga*) [regional]
- **Vaiṣṇava theology of the hymn** — For **Śrīvaiṣṇava** and other Vaiṣṇava traditions the Sūkta (with Uttaranārāyaṇa and Nārāyaṇa Sūkta) is proof-text that the **Puruṣa of the Veda is Nārāyaṇa/Viṣṇu** — the supreme person of whom the cosmos is the body, a reading systematized in **Rāmānuja's** body-of-God (śarīra-śarīri) doctrine; see [Viṣṇu — forms and vyūhas](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md) and the later praise-tradition in [Viṣṇu Sahasranāma](./vishnu-sahasranama.md). [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Śaiva/Smārta use):* the hymn is not sectarian property — it is likewise recited in Śaiva and Smārta abhiṣeka (alongside **[Śrī Rudram](./sri-rudram-namakam-chamakam.md)**) and for any deity's consecration, the Puruṣa there read as the impersonal-supreme or as Śiva. [regional]

## Two clocks

- **Scriptural frame** — The hymn narrates the **first event of all**: the primal sacrifice **before ordered time**, from which yugas, worlds, gods, Vedas, and society descend; as śruti it is itself eternal and authorless. (*Ṛgveda* 10.90 with traditional exegesis)
- **Historical frame [scholarly]** — Composition within the **late Ṛgvedic stratum, c. 1200–1000 BCE**; incorporation with the Uttaranārāyaṇa appendix into the **Yajurveda saṃhitās (c. 1200–800 BCE)**; Upaniṣadic uptake (Śvetāśvatara, Mahānārāyaṇa, latter half of the 1st millennium BCE); Purāṇic virāṭ-puruṣa elaboration and Āgamic liturgical codification in the common era. [scholarly]
