# Tripura-dāhana — The Burning of the Three Cities

> Cosmic address: a first-tier *standalone Śaiva cosmic-warrior myth* in which [Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md) becomes **Tripurāntaka** ("ender of the three cities") and incinerates the three flying fortresses of the asura brothers **Tārakākṣa, Vidyunmālī, and Kamalākṣa** (sons of Tāraka) with a *single* arrow — the entire pantheon collapsing into the components of his war-machine. Undated within the Manvantara frame; the texts set it in primordial deva-time after the asuras win their boon.
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · See also: [Śiva myth-cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) · [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md)

The **Tripura-dāhana** (also *Tripura-saṃhāra*, "destruction of the Tripura") is the signature demonstration of **Śaiva primacy**: a battle that the whole divine order cannot win until Śiva consents to fight, and which he then ends in a *single instant* with *one* arrow. Its theological argument is built into its imagery — every other god is reduced to a *part* of Śiva's chariot or weapon. Brahmā becomes his charioteer, Viṣṇu his arrow, the Earth his car, the Vedas his horses, Meru his bow. The episode is one of the most-depicted in Śaiva art, generating the **Tripurāntaka-mūrti** iconographic type. The fullest narratives are in the *Śiva Purāṇa* (Rudra-saṃhitā / Yuddha-khaṇḍa), the *Liṅga Purāṇa*, the *Matsya Purāṇa*, and the *Mahābhārata* (Karṇa Parva, where Kṛṣṇa recounts it to inspire Arjuna); Kālidāsa alludes to it.

---

## I. The Boon and the Building of the Three Cities

- **Origin of the brothers** — After the slaying of the asura **Tāraka** (by Skanda/Kārttikeya), his three sons **Tārakākṣa**, **Vidyunmālī**, and **Kamalākṣa** undertake fierce penance to gain power and avenge their father. (*Śiva Purāṇa* Rudra-saṃhitā; *Matsya Purāṇa*)
- **Brahmā's boon** — Brahmā, pleased by the austerities, refuses outright immortality. The brothers instead ask for three impregnable cities and a near-unbreakable condition for their fall: the cities can be destroyed only by a *single arrow* loosed at the one instant in a thousand years when all three align on a single line/star. (*Śiva Purāṇa*; *Liṅga Purāṇa*)
- **The architect Maya builds Tripura** — The asura-artificer **Maya** (Mayāsura) constructs the three *pura* (cities/fortresses): one of **gold**, one of **silver**, one of **iron** — set respectively in **heaven (svarga), the sky/air (antarikṣa), and the earth (bhūmi)**. (*Śiva Purāṇa*; *Matsya Purāṇa* 129–140)
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata, Karṇa Parva 8.24):* the three cities are described as one of gold, one of silver, one of iron, ranging the heavens; Maya is their builder and the alignment-condition is stressed.
  - *Variant (metal/loka assignment):* texts differ on which metal sits in which world; the gold city is most often placed in heaven and the iron city on/near earth, but the silver–air pairing is not fixed. [scholarly: assignment is text-dependent]
- **The flying, mobile fortresses** — The three *pura* roam the three worlds independently and re-merge only rarely; their mobility plus the alignment-clause is what makes them effectively indestructible. (*Śiva Purāṇa*; *Liṅga Purāṇa*)
- **A thousand years of asura rule** — Empowered, the brothers and their people grow mighty; in time they abandon dharma, oppress the worlds and the devas, and provoke the gods to seek their destruction. (*Śiva Purāṇa* Rudra-saṃhitā)

## II. The Corruption of Dharma — Viṣṇu as Māyāmoha / the Deluding Monk

- **Why the cities cannot simply be attacked** — While the Tripura asuras remain devout Śiva-worshippers and dharmic, they are protected; the gods cannot break the boon's conditions. A stratagem is needed to first *corrupt* them. (*Śiva Purāṇa*; *Matsya Purāṇa*)
- **Viṣṇu generates a deluding teacher (Māyāmoha)** — Viṣṇu emanates a heretic preacher — a naked/shaven mendicant teaching an anti-Vedic, nihilistic or "Jain/Buddhist-like" doctrine — who enters Tripura and seduces the asuras away from Vedic dharma and Śiva-worship. Stripped of their merit and protection, the cities become vulnerable. (*Śiva Purāṇa*; *Matsya Purāṇa*) [the Māyāmoha figure is the Purāṇic prototype of the later **Buddha-avatāra** of Viṣṇu]
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu as a bull / a man at the city wall):* in some tellings Viṣṇu creates a strange bull, or a heterodox man, drawing the asuras into impious practice. (*Śiva Purāṇa*)
- **Connection to the [Mohinī](samudra-manthana.md)/deluder pattern** — This is the same Vaiṣṇava "deceive-to-win" device seen in the Samudra Manthana and the [Bhasmāsura](bhasmasura-and-vrikasura.md) tales — Viṣṇu wins by guile, Śiva by force. [scholarly: a recurring sectarian division of labor]

## III. Śiva's Cosmic Chariot — The Pantheon as Weapon-System

The most distinctive feature of the myth: the gods cannot defeat the cities, so they *become* Śiva's war-machine. Even with this apparatus assembled, Śiva needs no more than a fraction of his power.

- **The Earth as chariot (ratha)** — The goddess **Pṛthivī / Bhūmi (the Earth)** becomes the great chariot on which Śiva rides; **Meru** and the mountains form its frame; the sun and moon are its wheels. (*Mahābhārata* Karṇa Parva; *Śiva Purāṇa*)
- **Sun and Moon as wheels (cakra)** — **Sūrya** and **Candra** become the two wheels of the chariot. (*Mahābhārata* Karṇa Parva 8.24)
- **The Vedas as horses; Gāyatrī/Sāvitrī as reins** — The four **Vedas** become the horses yoked to the car, with the sacred meters/Sāvitrī as the bridle. (*Śiva Purāṇa*; *Mahābhārata*)
- **Brahmā as charioteer (sārathi)** — The creator **Brahmā** takes the reins as Śiva's driver. (*Mahābhārata* Karṇa Parva; *Śiva Purāṇa*)
- **Mount Meru as the bow (dhanus)** — **Meru**, the world-axis, becomes Śiva's bow; the great serpent **Vāsuki** (or Śeṣa/Ananta) becomes the bowstring. (*Śiva Purāṇa*; *Liṅga Purāṇa*)
- **Viṣṇu as the arrow (bāṇa/śara)** — **Viṣṇu** himself becomes the single arrow; **Agni** is its tip/flame, **Vāyu** its feathering, and **Yama** (or Kāla) is set in the shaft. (*Mahābhārata* Karṇa Parva; *Śiva Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (Liṅga Purāṇa):* the arrow is composed of Viṣṇu (shaft), Agni (point), and Soma (feathers) — a tri-divine missile mirroring the three cities.
- **The pramatha-gaṇas and the army** — Śiva's gaṇas, Nandin, Vīrabhadra, and the assembled host accompany him, though the killing-stroke is his alone. (*Śiva Purāṇa* Yuddha-khaṇḍa)

## IV. The Single Arrow — Alignment and Destruction

- **The thousand-year alignment** — Śiva waits, fully armed, for the rare moment (in some texts the conjunction of the three cities under **Puṣya** nakṣatra / a single instant) when the three *pura* fall into one line. (*Śiva Purāṇa*; *Matsya Purāṇa*)
- **The smile that pierces — or the loosed arrow** — At the aligned instant Śiva releases the Viṣṇu-arrow; it incinerates all three cities and their asuras simultaneously in a single blaze. (*Mahābhārata* Karṇa Parva; *Śiva Purāṇa*; *Liṅga Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (the bow not drawn / the laugh):* a celebrated motif holds that Śiva, having assembled the whole cosmos as his weapon, destroys Tripura merely **with a smile (or laugh)** — or by drawing the bow only slightly — underscoring that the apparatus was unnecessary to his power. [late text / devotional emphasis; *Śiva Purāṇa* and stotra tradition]
- **The spared devotee — Maya and others** — Because Maya (and in some tellings a remnant of true Śiva-devotees within Tripura) had not abandoned Śiva, he is spared and allowed to depart; Śiva preserves his bhaktas amid the conflagration. (*Śiva Purāṇa*) [regional emphasis]
- **The devas' arrogance rebuked** — In several tellings the gods, presuming the victory theirs, are humbled — Śiva (or his laughter) reminds them that he alone destroyed the cities; this caps the Śaiva-primacy argument. (*Śiva Purāṇa* Yuddha-khaṇḍa) [Śaiva-primacy motif]

## V. Tripurāntaka-mūrti and Cultic Afterlife

- **Tripurāntaka / Tripurāri iconography** — The deed crystallizes into a fixed icon: Śiva (often eight-armed) standing in *ālīḍha* archer's stance, drawing a bow, mounted on or beside the cosmic chariot — the **Tripurāntaka-mūrti**, prominent in Cōḻa bronze and South Indian temple sculpture. (Śilpa/Āgama tradition; *Kāraṇāgama*) [iconographic]
- **Epithets** — From this act Śiva bears the names **Tripurāntaka, Tripurāri, Tripura-ghna, Purāntaka** ("ender of the city/cities"). (Purāṇic / stotra usage)
- **Kālidāsa allusion** — Kālidāsa references Śiva's burning of the three cities in his court poetry (e.g., the Tripura-conqueror's bow in the *Kumārasaṃbhava*/*Raghuvaṃśa* milieu), attesting the myth's classical-age currency. (*Kālidāsa* corpus) [allusive]
- **Kārttikai Dīpam association** — In Tamil tradition the festival of **Kārttikai Dīpam** (lighting of lamps / the great beacon on Tiruvaṇṇāmalai, Kārttikai month) is widely linked to the burning of Tripura — the lit flame commemorating Śiva's destroying fire. (Tamil Śaiva sthala/folk tradition) [regional / folk]
- **Contrast with Viṣṇu's mode** — As in [Gajendra Mokṣa](gajendra-moksha.md) (Viṣṇu rescues by grace) and the Manthana (Viṣṇu wins by Mohinī's guile), Tripura-dāhana is the Śaiva counter-paradigm: deliverance by overwhelming, instantaneous *force*. [scholarly: sectarian patterning]

## Sources

**Primary (scriptural):**
- *Śiva Purāṇa*, Rudra-saṃhitā / Yuddha-khaṇḍa — fullest narrative: the boon, Maya's three cities, Māyāmoha corruption, the cosmic chariot, the single arrow, sparing of devotees.
- *Liṅga Purāṇa* — the cosmic-chariot and Meru-bow / Viṣṇu-arrow apparatus; destruction of the three *pura*.
- *Matsya Purāṇa*, chs. ~129–140 — building of the cities by Maya; the deluding-teacher device.
- *Padma Purāṇa* — additional Tripura-saṃhāra material and the Māyāmoha/heretic-preacher motif.
- *Mahābhārata*, Karṇa Parva (8.24, Kṛṣṇa's account to Arjuna) — the earliest epic version of the cosmic chariot, Brahmā as charioteer, Earth as car, Sun/Moon as wheels, Viṣṇu as arrow.
- *Kālidāsa* (Kumārasaṃbhava / Raghuvaṃśa allusions) — classical-age poetic reference to Śiva the Tripura-conqueror.
