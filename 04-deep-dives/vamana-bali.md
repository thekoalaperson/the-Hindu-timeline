# Vāmana, Bali & Trivikrama — the Three Strides

> Cosmic address: narrated by Śuka to Parīkṣit in the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Canto 8, Chapters 15–23** (SB 8.15–8.23), set in the present **[7th — Vaivasvata — Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)** during the reign of the seventh Indra. The **fifth** of the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md), Vāmana is the first avatāra of fully human (dwarf-brahmin) form and the only one who conquers by *yācñā* (begging) rather than by force.
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · See also: [Samudra Manthana](./samudra-manthana.md) · [The Cirañjīvis](./the-chiranjivis.md) · [The Gurus — Bṛhaspati & Śukra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md)

The **Vāmana–Bali** episode tells how Viṣṇu, born as a tiny brahmin dwarf, begs three paces of land from the world-conquering Asura king **Bali**, then swells into the cosmic **Trivikrama** ("three-strides") form to measure the whole universe in two steps — reclaiming the three worlds for the Devas while honouring Bali's unbroken word. It is at once a cosmogonic myth (the Vedic three strides of Viṣṇu), a parable of *dāna* (generous giving) outweighing victory, and the charter-myth of the South Indian harvest festival **Onam**. The principal narrative is the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (8.15–23); fuller mythic detail appears in the *Vāmana Purāṇa*; the cosmogonic kernel goes back to **Ṛgveda 1.154**.

---

## I. Background — Bali's Conquest of the Three Worlds

- **Bali's lineage and resurrection** — **Bali** (Mahābali) is grandson of **Prahlāda**, son of **Virocana**, king of the Asuras. Slain earlier in the deva–asura wars, he is revived and empowered by his preceptor **Śukrācārya** and the Bhṛgu brahmins through a *viśvajit* sacrifice. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.15) — see [The Gurus — Bṛhaspati & Śukra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md).
- **Conquest of Svarga** — Strengthened, Bali storms heaven; on **Bṛhaspati's counsel** ("all of you should now quit the celestial region and go in hiding," 8.15.30) **Indra** and the Devas abandon Svarga without battle. Bali becomes sovereign of all three worlds and performs one hundred Aśvamedhas to secure his rule. From the Viśvajit fire had come his golden chariot, lion banner, bow and armor — and **Prahlāda gave him an unfading wreath**, Śukra a conch. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.15.8–9, 8.15.30)
- **Connection to the churning** — This episode follows directly from the **[Samudra Manthana](./samudra-manthana.md)** frame in Canto 8: Bali is the same Asura general who led the churning-side and lost the amṛta to Mohinī; his renewed dominion sets the stage for Vāmana. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.6–8.15)
- **Aditi's distress and the Payovrata** — The Devas' mother **Aditi** grieves at her sons' dispossession. Her husband, the sage **Kaśyapa**, instructs her in the **Payovrata** (a twelve-day milk-vow to Viṣṇu). Pleased, Viṣṇu promises to be born as her son. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.16–8.17)

## II. The Birth and Approach of Vāmana

- **Birth of Vāmana** — Viṣṇu is born to **Aditi and Kaśyapa** as a brilliant dwarf-brahmin (*vāmana* = "dwarf"), bearing conch, discus, mace and lotus at birth, then assuming the modest form of a *brahmacārin*. His sacred-thread (*upanayana*) ceremony is performed; he receives the staff, deerskin, umbrella and water-pot (*kamaṇḍalu*). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.18)
  - *Variant (Vāmana Purāṇa):* the text expands the divine boyhood, the gifts of the gods (Bṛhaspati's thread, Bhṛgu's girdle, the Sun's umbrella, etc.), and frames the avatāra within a longer Bhārgava-Asura context. [late text]
- **Bali's Aśvamedha** — Bali, advised by the Bhṛgu priests, undertakes a great **Aśvamedha** (horse-sacrifice) on the north bank of the **Narmadā** at *Bhṛgukaccha* (Bharuch). The boy Vāmana arrives at the sacrificial ground. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.18)
- **The dwarf welcomed** — Bali, famed for never refusing a petitioner, honours the radiant child, washes his feet, and offers him anything — land, gold, villages, cattle, a kingdom. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.18–8.19)

## III. The Boon of Three Paces

- **The request: tri-pada-bhūmi** — Vāmana asks for only **three paces of land, measured by his own small steps** (*trīṇi padāni* / *tri-pada-bhūmi*), declining all greater gifts — a true brahmin, he says, asks no more than he needs. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.19)
- **Śukra's warning** — **Śukrācārya**, perceiving by his yogic sight that the dwarf is Viṣṇu himself come to dispossess Bali, **forbids** the king to grant the boon, warning that the "three steps" will swallow all the worlds. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.19–8.20)
- **Bali's resolve** — Bali refuses to retract a promise already made: "though cursed by his preceptor, the great Bali did not swerve from the truth," and he pours the gift-water with due formality. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.20.16) The famous extended reply — that deceiving a begging brahmin is the worst of sins, feared more than hell itself — is a **popular-tradition elaboration**: it does not appear at SB 8.19–20 in the translations checked. [popular tradition] Bali's explicit invocation of his ancestor **Prahlāda** comes later, while bound: he cites Prahlāda's endurance of Hiraṇyakaśipu's tortures as his precedent for accepting suffering from Viṣṇu's hand. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.22.8)
- **Śukra's curse** — Angered at being defied, Śukra **curses** Bali to lose his royal splendour for disobeying his guru. Bali accepts the curse rather than break his word. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.20)
- **The kamaṇḍalu and Śukra's eye** — As Bali prepares to seal the gift by pouring the consecration-water from his **kamaṇḍalu** (water-pot), Śukra, in a last effort, shrinks himself and **lodges in the spout to block the water**. Vāmana, knowing this, pokes a blade of **kuśa-grass** into the spout to clear it — and **pierces Śukra's eye**, which is why Śukra (the planet Venus) is thereafter one-eyed. **NOT in the Bhāgavata**: SB 8.19–8.20 narrate the water-pouring with no blocking or blinding episode at all; the story belongs to the **Nṛsiṁha Purāṇa** and regional/folk retellings, where the details vary (kuśa / darbha / dūrva grass; Śukra shrinks himself or becomes an insect/wasp). A widely-cited aetiology for Venus's single eye and the name **Uśanas Kāvya**'s single sight. [Nṛsiṁha Purāṇa / regional]
- **The gift confirmed** — Bali pours the water, **formally granting the three paces**; his queen **Vindhyāvali** stands by as he fulfils the *saṃkalpa*. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.20–8.21)

## IV. Trivikrama — Measuring the Worlds

- **The cosmic expansion** — The instant the gift is sealed, Vāmana abandons the dwarf-body and grows into the immeasurable **Trivikrama** (also *Urukrama*, "wide-strider") — the universal *Virāṭ* form filling sky and space. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.20)
- **The first two strides** — With **one step he covers the entire earth and the lower worlds (Bhūrloka); with the second he spans the heavens and all the upper worlds** up to **Satyaloka / Brahmaloka**, his foot piercing the shell of the universe. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.20–8.21)
  - **Origin of the Gaṅgā** — Where his uplifted toe pierces the cosmic shell, the waters of the **Causal Ocean** stream in as the celestial **Gaṅgā** (Viṣṇupadī, "from Viṣṇu's foot"), later to descend to earth. The Canto 8 chapters do not narrate this in-scene; the full treatment is the retrospective **SB 5.17** ("The Descent of the River Ganges"), referring back to the Trivikrama act. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.17, re: 8.20–21; cf. *Vāmana Purāṇa*)
- **Nowhere for the third stride** — With two strides Trivikrama has measured **all** of Bali's three worlds; **there is no space left** for the promised third pace. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.21–8.22)
- **The Vedic kernel** — This is the mythic flowering of **Ṛgveda 1.154**, where **Viṣṇu strides across the cosmos in three steps** (*trīṇi padā vi cakrame*), the third resting in his own highest abode (*paramaṃ padam*) beyond mortal sight — the oldest layer of the whole legend. (*Ṛgveda* 1.154; cf. 1.22, 7.99) [scholarly]

## V. Bali's Surrender and Exaltation

- **Bali offers his own head** — Asked where to place the third step, Bali — bound by **Garuḍa with Varuṇa's nooses** (8.21.26), having himself stopped his own army from fighting (8.21.18) — hears Viṣṇu close chapter 21 with a sentence of hell for the unkept word. Chapter 22 then opens on Bali's reply: he will be true to his word — "**Be pleased to place your third step on my head**." (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.22.2; the binding and sentence at 8.21.26–34)
- **Prahlāda and Vindhyāvali intercede** — Bali's grandfather **Prahlāda** and queen **Vindhyāvali** praise Viṣṇu and plead Bali's cause; the Lord, moved by Bali's truthfulness, relents. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.22)
- **Granted Sutala** — Viṣṇu sets his foot on Bali's head and sends him not to punishment but to **Sutala**, the most beautiful of the subterranean Pātāla realms — a domain richer than Indra's heaven, free of disease and anxiety. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.22)
  - *Variant:* many tellings simply say Bali is "pushed down to **Pātāla**" (the nether worlds generally); *Bhāgavata* specifies **Sutala** in particular. [disputed: which sub-loka]
- **Viṣṇu becomes Bali's gatekeeper** — Two adjacent boon-moments, often conflated: to **Bali** Viṣṇu promises constant protective presence — "I shall protect you along with your followers... You will always find me present there" (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.22.35–36); the vivid **mace-in-hand doorkeeper image** is then spoken to **Prahlāda** — "You will always see me there standing with a mace in hand" (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.23.10) — after which **Prahlāda escorts Bali into Sutala** together (8.23.11–12). The name "Gadādhara" for this role is a devotional convention, not confirmed in the verse text checked.
- **Bali, a future Indra** — Viṣṇu declares that Bali, by his merit, will become **Indra in the coming Sāvarṇi (8th) Manvantara**; until then he reigns immortal in Sutala. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.22) Thus Bali is counted among the **cirañjīvis** (the deathless ones) — see [The Cirañjīvis](./the-chiranjivis.md).
- **Svarga restored to Indra** — With Bali honourably retired, Aditi's son returns the three worlds to **Indra** and the Devas; the proper cosmic order is re-established. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.23)
- **Brahmā and the Vedas honour Vāmana** — The episode closes with Brahmā installing Vāmana as protector (*Upendra*, "younger Indra"); reciting this *Trivikrama-carita* is praised as supremely purifying. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.23)

## VI. Onam — the Living Festival

- **Mahābali's annual return** — In **Kerala** folk-tradition, the beloved king **Māveli (Mahābali)** ruled a golden, casteless, sorrow-free age; granted a boon by Viṣṇu, he is permitted to **revisit his people once a year**. His homecoming is celebrated as **Onam** (Tiruvōṇam), the great harvest festival, with *pūkkaḷam* flower-carpets, the *Oṇasadya* feast, and *vallam kali* boat-races. (Kerala festival tradition) [regional][folk]
  - *Note:* the Onam "golden age of Māveli" framing is a **regional devotional inflection** that recasts the Asura king as an ideal, beloved ruler — a notably sympathetic reading of Bali not foregrounded in the Sanskrit Purāṇas. [scholarly]

## Sources

- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Canto 8, Chapters 15–23** (SB 8.15–8.23) — the principal narrative: Bali's conquest, the Payovrata, Vāmana's birth, the three-pace boon, Śukra's warning and blinding, the Trivikrama form, and Bali's exaltation to Sutala.
- **Vāmana Purāṇa** — expanded mythic detail on the avatāra's birth, the divine gifts, and the Bhārgava–Asura background. [late text]
- **Ṛgveda 1.154** (with 1.22, 7.99) — the archaic "three strides of Viṣṇu" (*trīṇi padā vi cakrame*), the Vedic root of the Trivikrama myth.
- Kerala **Onam** festival tradition — the regional Māveli folk-cycle. [regional][folk]
