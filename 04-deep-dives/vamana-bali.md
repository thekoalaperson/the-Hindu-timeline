# Vāmana, Bali & Trivikrama — the Three Strides

> Cosmic address: narrated by Śuka to Parīkṣit in the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Canto 8, Chapters 15–23** (SB 8.15–8.23), set in the present **[7th — Vaivasvata — Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)** during the reign of that manvantara's Indra, **Purandara** (SB 8.13.4–6, which names Vāmana as this manvantara's avatāra). The **fifth** of the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md), Vāmana is the first avatāra of fully human (dwarf-brahmin) form and the only one who conquers by *yācñā* (begging) rather than by force.
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · See also: [Samudra Manthana](./samudra-manthana.md) · [The Cirañjīvis](./the-chiranjivis.md) · [The Gurus — Bṛhaspati & Śukra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md)

The **Vāmana–Bali** episode tells how Viṣṇu, born as a tiny brahmin dwarf, begs three paces of land from the world-conquering Asura king **Bali**, then swells into the cosmic **Trivikrama** ("three-strides") form to measure the whole universe in two steps — reclaiming the three worlds for the Devas while honouring Bali's unbroken word. It is at once a cosmogonic myth (the Vedic three strides of Viṣṇu), a parable of *dāna* (generous giving) outweighing victory, and the charter-myth of the South Indian harvest festival **Onam**. The principal narrative is the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (8.15–23); fuller mythic detail appears in the *Vāmana Purāṇa*; the cosmogonic kernel goes back to the Ṛgvedic three strides (**Ṛgveda 1.22.16–21, 1.154–155**), and the dwarf form already appears in the **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa (1.2.5)**.

---

## I. Background — Bali's Conquest of the Three Worlds

- **Bali's lineage and resurrection** — **Bali** (Mahābali) is grandson of **Prahlāda**, son of **Virocana**, king of the Asuras. Felled by Indra's thunderbolt in the war after the churning, he is revived by his preceptor **Śukrācārya** (8.11.48, 8.15.3; cf. the *Saṃjīvanī* mantra with which Śukra revives the dead Asura soldiers, 8.11.47), and the Bhṛgu brahmins then empower him through a *viśvajit* sacrifice. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.11.12, 8.11.46–48; 8.15.3–4) — see [The Gurus — Bṛhaspati & Śukra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md).
- **Conquest of Svarga** — Strengthened, Bali storms heaven; on **Bṛhaspati's counsel** to wait until the enemy's fortunes are reversed ("you should all leave this heavenly planet and go elsewhere, where you will not be seen," 8.15.30, BBT), with his warning that Bali will fall when he insults the brahmins (8.15.31), **Indra** and the Devas abandon Svarga without battle. Bali becomes sovereign of all three worlds and the Bhṛgus engage him in one hundred Aśvamedhas (8.15.34). From the Viśvajit fire had come his golden chariot, lion banner, bow and armor — and **Prahlāda gave him an unfading wreath**, Śukra a conch. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.15.5–6, 8.15.28–34)
- **Connection to the churning** — This episode follows directly from the **[Samudra Manthana](./samudra-manthana.md)** frame in Canto 8: Bali is the same Asura king who agreed to the churning truce, took the horse Uccaiḥśravas and the goddess Vāruṇī from the ocean, and lost the amṛta to Mohinī; his renewed dominion sets the stage for Vāmana. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.6.28–32, 8.8.3, 8.8.30, 8.11–8.15)
- **Aditi's distress and the Payovrata** — The Devas' mother **Aditi** grieves at her sons' dispossession. Her husband, the sage **Kaśyapa**, instructs her in the **Payovrata** (a twelve-day milk-vow to Viṣṇu in the bright fortnight of Phālguna, ending on Dvādaśī), as Brahmā had taught him. Pleased, Viṣṇu promises to be born as her son. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.16.24–25, 8.17)

## II. The Birth and Approach of Vāmana

- **Birth of Vāmana** — Viṣṇu is born to **Aditi and Kaśyapa** as a brilliant dwarf-brahmin (*vāmana* = "dwarf"), bearing conch, discus, mace and lotus at birth, then — before his parents — assuming the modest form of a *brahmacārin*. He is born on **Śravaṇa-dvādaśī**, with the moon in the *nakṣatra* Śravaṇa, at the *muhūrta* Abhijit, the sun at the meridian (8.18.5–6); the month, the bright fortnight of Bhādra, is the BBT translator's bracketed gloss on 8.18.5, not verse text. At his sacred-thread (*upanayana*) the gods give the gifts: the Sun utters the Gāyatrī, **Bṛhaspati the sacred thread**, Kaśyapa the straw girdle, Earth the deerskin, the Moon the staff, Aditi the loincloth, the deity of the sky the umbrella, **Brahmā the water-pot (*kamaṇḍalu*)**, the Seven Sages kuśa, Sarasvatī a rudrākṣa string, Kubera the begging-bowl, and Bhagavatī (Pārvatī) his first alms. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.18.1–17)
  - *Variant (Vāmana Purāṇa, ch. 89 in the Sanskrit–English edition checked; chapter numbering varies by edition):* the *upanayana* is performed by **Bharadvāja**, and the gifts come from sages and kings instead — Pulaha the sacred thread, Pulastya two white garments, Agastya the deerskin, Bharadvāja the girdle (*mekhalā*), Marīci the palāśa staff, Vasiṣṭha the rosary (*akṣasūtra*), Aṅgiras a kuśa brush, King Raghu the umbrella, Nṛga sandals, and **Bṛhaspati the kamaṇḍalu**. The Purāṇa also frames the avatāra within a longer Bhārgava–Asura context (Prahlāda curses Bali to lose his throne for belittling Viṣṇu while Viṣṇu is in Aditi's womb — told in chs. 29–30 and again in ch. 77 of the edition checked). [late text]
- **Bali's Aśvamedha** — Bali, advised by the Bhṛgu priests, undertakes a great **Aśvamedha** (horse-sacrifice) on the north bank of the **Narmadā** at *Bhṛgukaccha* (Bharuch). The boy Vāmana arrives at the sacrificial ground. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.18.20–25)
  - *Variant (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa 1.28.2–11 in the Baroda critical edition = 1.29 in the Southern/vulgate text):* Viśvāmitra shows Rāma the **Siddhāśrama**, "the former hermitage of Vāmana." At the Devas' appeal while Bali, who has conquered the gods, is performing his sacrifice and refuses no petitioner, Viṣṇu is born of **Aditi**, begs three strides, strides across the worlds and returns them to Indra after **restraining Bali by his might** (*niyamya balim ojasā*) — no Narmadā, no Śukra, no Sutala. The vulgate's longer version (1.29.2–18) adds Viṣṇu's own long austerities at the hermitage, Kaśyapa's *tapas* with Aditi, and Kaśyapa's boon-request that Viṣṇu be born as their son, "the younger brother of Indra."
- **The dwarf welcomed** — Bali, famed for never refusing a petitioner, honours the radiant child, washes his feet, and offers him anything — a cow, gold, a furnished house, a brahmin bride, villages, horses, elephants, chariots — and, mindful that Śiva bears the Gaṅgā from Viṣṇu's toe, places the foot-washing water on his own head. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.18.26–32)

## III. The Boon of Three Paces

- **The request: tri-pada-bhūmi** — Vāmana asks for only **three paces of land, measured by his own small steps** (*trīṇi padāni* / *tri-pada-bhūmi*), declining all greater gifts — a true brahmin, he says, asks no more than he needs; Bali chides the boy for bargaining so poorly and offers him an entire island. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.19.16–27)
- **Śukra's warning** — **Śukrācārya**, perceiving by his yogic sight that the dwarf is Viṣṇu himself come to dispossess Bali, **forbids** the king to grant the boon, warning that the "three steps" will swallow all the worlds — and even argues that a promise not sealed with *oṃ* may lawfully be broken, listing the occasions on which a lie is blameless. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.19.29–43)
- **Bali's resolve** — Bali refuses to retract a promise already made, in a long reply to his guru: "**I am the grandson of Prahlāda** — how can I withdraw my promise out of greed, like an ordinary cheat, and to a brāhmaṇa?" (8.20.3); "there is nothing more sinful than untruth — Mother Earth said she can bear any weight except a liar" (8.20.4); "**I do not fear hell, poverty, an ocean of distress, loss of position or even death as much as I fear cheating a brāhmaṇa**" (8.20.5). He cites Dadhīci and Śibi, who gave their lives (8.20.7), and declares that even if the boy is Viṣṇu come to punish him, he will give and will not retaliate (8.20.11–13). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.20.2–13) Prahlāda is invoked again later, while Bali is bound: he cites his grandfather's endurance of Hiraṇyakaśipu's tortures as his precedent for accepting suffering from Viṣṇu's hand. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.22.8–10)
- **Śukra's curse** — Angered at being defied, Śukra **curses** Bali: "you shall very soon be bereft of all your opulence." Bali accepts the curse rather than break his word: "though cursed by his own guru, the great one did not swerve from truth" (*satyān na calito mahān*), and gives the land, preceded by the offering of water. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.20.14–16)
- **The kamaṇḍalu and Śukra's eye** — As Bali prepares to seal the gift by pouring the consecration-water from his **kamaṇḍalu** (water-pot), Śukra, in a last effort, shrinks himself and **lodges in the spout to block the water**. Vāmana, knowing this, pokes a blade of **kuśa-grass** into the spout to clear it — and **pierces Śukra's eye**, which is why Śukra (the planet Venus) is thereafter one-eyed. **NOT in the Bhāgavata**: SB 8.19–8.20 narrate the water-pouring with no blocking or blinding episode at all (Mani's *Purāṇic Encyclopaedia* retells it under "Bhāgavata, Skandha 8," which the verse text does not bear out), and the translator of the Sanskrit–English *Vāmana Purāṇa* notes it is "nowhere mentioned" in that Purāṇa either. The story is usually assigned to the **Nṛsiṁha Purāṇa** (locus not verified in this check) and to regional/folk retellings, where the details vary (kuśa / darbha / dūrva grass; Śukra shrinks himself or becomes an insect/wasp). A widely-cited aetiology for Venus's single eye and the name **Uśanas Kāvya**'s single sight. [Nṛsiṁha Purāṇa / regional]
- **The gift confirmed** — Bali pours the water, **formally granting the three paces**; his queen **Vindhyāvali**, wearing a pearl necklace, has a large **golden water-pot** brought for washing the Lord's feet, and Bali washes them and takes the water on his head while the celestials shower flowers and praise his gift. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.20.16–20)

## IV. Trivikrama — Measuring the Worlds

- **The cosmic expansion** — The instant the gift is sealed, Vāmana abandons the dwarf-body and grows into the immeasurable **Trivikrama** (also *Urukrama*, "wide-strider") — the universal *Virāṭ* form filling sky and space; Bali sees the lower worlds such as Rasātala on the soles of its feet. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.20.21–33)
- **The first two strides** — With **one step he covers the surface of the earth (Bhūrloka), with his body the sky and with his arms the directions; with the second he covers the heavens**, his foot reaching beyond Mahar-, Jana-, Tapo- and **Satyaloka** — the effulgence of his toenails dims Brahmā's own world. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.20.33–34, 8.21.1, 8.21.31)
  - **Origin of the Gaṅgā** — the Bhāgavata gives two images, side by side:
    - *In-scene (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.21.4):* **Brahmā washes the uplifted foot with water from his kamaṇḍalu**, and that water, purified by the touch, becomes the **Gaṅgā** flowing down from the sky to purify the three worlds. *Skanda Purāṇa* (Māheśvara-khaṇḍa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa 19) tells it the same way.
    - *Retrospective (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 5.17.1):* the **nail of his left big toe pierces the covering of the universe**, and the waters of the **Causal Ocean** stream in as the Gaṅgā (Viṣṇupadī, "from Viṣṇu's foot"), later to descend to earth.
- **Nowhere for the third stride** — With two strides Trivikrama has measured **all** of Bali's three worlds; **there is no space left** for the promised third pace. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.20.34, 8.21.29–31)
- **The Vedic kernel** — This is the mythic flowering of the Ṛgvedic hymns in which **Viṣṇu strides across the cosmos in three steps**: "Viṣṇu, the guardian no one deceives, **strode three steps** (*trīṇi padā vi cakrame*)" (*Ṛgveda* 1.22.18, repeated 8.12.27); the wise ever see his **highest step** (*tad viṣṇoḥ paramaṃ padam*, 1.22.20); the hymn 1.154 praises him who "measured out the earthly realms" in three wide strides, with a spring of honey in his highest step (1.154.1–5); and in 1.155.5 a mortal can behold **two** of his steps, but **no one dares approach the third — not even the winged birds**. These hymns contain no dwarf, no Bali and no begging — the oldest layer of the legend is the cosmic striding alone. (*Ṛgveda* 1.22.16–21, 1.154, 1.155; cf. 7.99) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 1.2.5.1–9):* an early Vedic-prose **dwarf** telling. The Asuras, having worsted the gods, divide the earth among themselves with ox-hides; the gods come with **Viṣṇu — "the sacrifice" — at their head** and ask a share; the Asuras grudgingly grant "**as much as this Viṣṇu lies upon**." "**Now Viṣṇu was a dwarf.**" The gods enclose him with the Gāyatrī, Triṣṭubh and Jagatī metres, place Agni to the east, and so win the whole earth — the etiology of the *vedi* (altar). Here there is no Bali, no three strides and no gift; the trick is ritual, not a vow. (Eggeling trans., SBE 12; Eggeling's note, after Muir, calls this "the germ of the Dwarf Incarnation") [scholarly]

## V. Bali's Surrender and Exaltation

- **Bali offers his own head** — Asked where to place the third step, Bali — bound by **Garuḍa with Varuṇa's nooses** (8.21.26), having himself stopped his own army from fighting (8.21.18) — hears Viṣṇu close chapter 21 with a sentence of hell for the unkept word. Chapter 22 then opens on Bali's reply: he will be true to his word — "**Be pleased to place your third step on my head**." (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.22.2; the binding and sentence at 8.21.26–34)
- **Prahlāda, Vindhyāvali and Brahmā intercede** — Bali's grandfather **Prahlāda** arrives and praises the Lord for both giving and taking away Bali's kingdom (8.22.12–17); queen **Vindhyāvali** declares that the world is the Lord's and no one else can claim to give it (8.22.19–20); and **Brahmā** asks that Bali, who has given everything including his body, be released (8.22.21–23). The Lord, moved by Bali's truthfulness under every loss, relents. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.22.12–30)
- **Granted Sutala** — Viṣṇu sends Bali not to punishment but to **Sutala**, built by Viśvakarmā at his order and guarded by him — "desired even by the demigods," a domain richer than Indra's heaven, free of disease, fatigue and anxiety. Released from Varuṇa's ropes, Bali bows to Hari, Brahmā and Śiva and enters Sutala himself. The Bhāgavata has Bali **offer** his head for the third step (8.22.2; cf. 2.7.18) but **does not narrate the foot actually being placed** on it. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.13.14, 8.22.31–34, 8.23.3)
  - *Variant (popular tradition):* Vāmana **sets his foot on Bali's head and presses him down** into the nether world — the image of most retellings and of Onam lore; Mani's *Purāṇic Encyclopaedia* gives it under "Bhāgavata, Skandha 8," but the verse text does not contain it. [popular tradition]
  - *Variant (Skanda Purāṇa, Māheśvara-khaṇḍa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa 19.42–48):* **Vindhyāvali offers the three steps herself** — one on her own head, one on her son Bāṇa's, one on her husband's — and the delighted Lord sends Bali to Sutala.
  - *Variant:* many tellings simply say Bali is "pushed down to **Pātāla**" (the nether worlds generally); *Bhāgavata* specifies **Sutala** in particular. [disputed: which sub-loka]
- **Viṣṇu becomes Bali's gatekeeper** — Two adjacent boon-moments, often conflated: to **Bali** Viṣṇu promises constant protective presence — "I shall protect you along with your followers... You will always find me present there" (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.22.35–36); the vivid **mace-in-hand doorkeeper image** is then spoken to **Prahlāda** — "You will always see me there standing with a mace in hand" (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.23.10) — after which **Prahlāda escorts Bali into Sutala** together (8.23.11–12). The name "Gadādhara" for this role is a devotional convention, not confirmed in the verse text checked.
- **Bali, a future Indra** — Viṣṇu declares that Bali, by his merit, will become **Indra in the coming Sāvarṇi (8th) Manvantara**; until then he reigns in Sutala. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.22.31–33; 8.13.12–14) Thus Bali is counted among the **cirañjīvis** (the deathless ones) — see [The Cirañjīvis](./the-chiranjivis.md).
- **Svarga restored to Indra** — With Bali honourably retired, Aditi's son returns the three worlds to his brother **Indra** and the Devas; Śukra, at Viṣṇu's request, makes good the flaws in Bali's sacrifice; the proper cosmic order is re-established. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.23.4, 8.23.13–19, 8.23.24–25)
- **Brahmā and the Vedas honour Vāmana** — The episode closes with Brahmā and the gods installing Vāmana as protector of the Vedas, dharma and all the worlds (*Upendra*, "younger Indra"); reciting this *Trivikrama-carita* is praised as supremely purifying. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.23.20–31)

## VI. Onam — the Living Festival

- **Mahābali's annual return** — In **Kerala** folk-tradition, the beloved king **Māveli (Mahābali)** ruled a golden, casteless, sorrow-free age; granted a boon by Viṣṇu, he is permitted to **revisit his people once a year**. His homecoming is celebrated as **Onam** (Tiruvōṇam), the great harvest festival, with *pūkkaḷam* flower-carpets, the *Oṇasadya* feast, and *vallam kali* boat-races. (Kerala festival tradition) [regional][folk]
  - *Note:* the Onam "golden age of Māveli" framing is a **regional devotional inflection** that recasts the Asura king as an ideal, beloved ruler — a notably sympathetic reading of Bali not foregrounded in the Sanskrit Purāṇas. [scholarly]
