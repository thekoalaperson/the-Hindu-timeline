# Vyāsa humbled at Kāśī — the paralysed arm and the ban from the city

> A Śaiva humbling-of-the-sage tale set in [Kāśī / Vārāṇasī](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md), the *mokṣa-purī* on Śiva's trident, at the Dvāpara–Kali junction of the present 28th Mahāyuga. The **Skanda Purāṇa, Kāśī-khaṇḍa** records how **Vyāsa** — arranger of the Veda — was checked twice in the city: his arm paralysed over a doctrinal boast, and, failing to get alms, his curse against Kāśī turned back on him until Śiva-as-householder and Pārvatī-as-Annapūrṇā fed him and **barred him from residing in the city except on the eighth and fourteenth *tithi***. This is the charter-legend of **Vyāsakāśī / Ramnagar** across the Gaṅgā.
> ⬆ Parent: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Master index: [README](../README.md) · Canonical sage: [Vyāsa](vyasa.md) · City node: [Kāśī / Vārāṇasī](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md)

The **Kāśī-khaṇḍa** of the [Skanda Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/skanda-purana-khanda-map.md) devotes two consecutive chapters of its *Uttarārdha* (latter half) to the discomfiting of **[Vyāsa](vyasa.md)** — the very sage the tradition credits with arranging the Vedas, composing the Mahābhārata, and redacting the eighteen Purāṇas (including this one). It is a pointed **Śaiva *māhātmya*** move: the city that dying men enter to win *mokṣa* will not be dictated to, cursed, or out-argued, not even by the greatest of ṛṣis. The two incidents are distinct in the text — (1) an **arm paralysed** when Vyāsa tries to proclaim Viṣṇu's supremacy at Jñānavāpī (ch. 95), and (2) an **attempted curse and a banishment** when, denied alms, his anger is turned back on him (ch. 96) — though popular retellings fuse them into a single "raised his arm to curse Kāśī and it froze" story. Both are catalogued below, kept apart.

---

## Where it sits — the two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic frame** — The episode belongs to the era of **Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana Vyāsa**, the 28th Veda-Vyāsa, at the **Dvāpara–Kali junction** of the current [Mahāyuga](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md) — the same lifetime in which he divides the Veda and compiles the epic. As a [chiranjīvi](the-chiranjivis.md) his presence at Kāśī is not fixed to a single reign; the city itself is held to stand outside ordinary yuga-time, borne aloft on Śiva's *triśūla*. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa*)
- **Academic / historical frame** — The **Kāśī-khaṇḍa** is a **late stratum of the Skanda Purāṇa**, dated **[scholarly] c. 13th–14th century CE**, systematising older Vārāṇasī pilgrimage-lore. The banishment tale is therefore best read as a **medieval Śaiva sectarian statement** — asserting Kāśī's Śiva-supremacy over a Vaiṣṇava-leaning Vyāsa figure — not as an early or pan-Purāṇic tradition. [late text] The **Vyāsa temple at Ramnagar** that anchors the legend on the ground is an **18th-century foundation (c. 1750)**. [scholarly]

## Chapter 95 — the arm paralysed at Jñānavāpī (the doctrinal humbling)

- **The provocation at Naimiṣāraṇya** — At [Naimiṣāraṇya](../06-sacred-geography/naimisharanya-chakra-tirtha.md), among 88,000 Śiva-devoted ascetics, Vyāsa insists that **Hari (Viṣṇu) alone** grants salvation across all scriptures; challenged, he is told to make his proclamation in the presence of **Viśveśvara** at Vārāṇasī, the city's supreme Śiva-liṅga. (*Skanda Purāṇa, Kāśī-khaṇḍa, Uttarārdha* 95)
- **The raised hand at Jñānavāpī** — Vyāsa arrives, worships Viṣṇu-forms at **Ādikeśava**, then at the **Jñānavāpī** (the "well of wisdom" beside Viśvanātha) begins reciting verses of Viṣṇu-supremacy, **raising his right hand** (and then the left) to swear the point. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa* 95.44–47)
- **Nandin freezes the arm** — **Nandin / Śailādi** (Nandi, son of Śilāda, Śiva's gaṇa-chief) **paralyses the raised hand "playfully" (līlayā)**, and Vyāsa's speech is stunned mid-verse. The lord of the city will not let the boast be completed within his walls. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa* 95.46–47)
  - *Popular conflation (folk):* many retellings and temple pamphlets say the arm was paralysed **because Vyāsa raised it to curse Kāśī over the alms-refusal** — collapsing this doctrinal episode into the alms-story of ch. 96. In the Purāṇa the two are **separate incidents**; the paralysis here is over Viṣṇu-vs-Śiva supremacy, not over food. [folk]
- **Viṣṇu himself corrects Vyāsa** — **Viṣṇu appears secretly** and tells Vyāsa that **Śiva is the supreme reality**; he restores the sage's speech by touching his throat. Vyāsa, undeceived, sings the **Vyāsāṣṭaka** (eight verses in praise of Śiva) and turns Śaiva. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa* 95)
- **The Vyāseśvara liṅga** — Reconciled, Vyāsa **installs the Vyāseśvara Liṅga** (at Ghaṇṭākarṇa-hrada), his own Śiva-shrine within the *kṣetra* — the doctrinal humbling ending not in exile but in worship. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa* 95)

## Chapter 96 — no alms, a curse turned back, and the ban (the moral humbling)

- **Śiva sets a test** — Now a devout resident bathing daily at [Maṇikarṇikā](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md#v-manikarnikā--the-theology-of-cremation-moksha) and worshipping Viśveśvara, Vyāsa instructs his disciples in *dharma* and *prāyaścitta*. To test him, **Śiva orders Pārvatī to see that no one in Kāśī gives Vyāsa alms**. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa, Uttarārdha* 96)
- **The wandering and the failed *bhikṣā*** — Vyāsa and his disciples (the text gives a hyperbolic **ten thousand**) wander the city house to house and **receive nothing** — every door, by the goddess's decree, withheld. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa* 96)
- **The attempted curse against Kāśī** — Enraged, Vyāsa **curses the city**: *"May learning not endure here for three generations! May wealth not endure for three generations! May liberation not be continuous here!"* — blaming the citizens' **arrogance of scholarship and of wealth**. This is the curse the paralysed-arm folk-version imagines being physically raised. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa* 96)
  - *On the curse's force:* the Purāṇa's whole point is that the curse **does not take** — Kāśī is *nitya* and *avimukta*, and an *ātithya* (a guest's) resentment cannot bind it. The curse rebounds on the curser. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa* 96) [late text]
- **Pārvatī / Annapūrṇā feeds him** — At evening, **Pārvatī, disguised as a householder-wife**, invites Vyāsa to eat at her home, where her husband (**Śiva in householder form**) hosts the sage and all his disciples to a **lavish feast** — the deity of the never-empty foodbowl overturning the very grievance that provoked the curse. This is the same feeding-theology as **Annapūrṇā ("the full-of-food")**, queen of Kāśī, at whose bowl even Śiva begs. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa* 96)
  - *Identity of the feeder (variant):* the Purāṇa names the disguised hostess **Pārvatī**; secondary summaries of the Kāśī-khaṇḍa give her as **Viśālākṣī** ("the wide-eyed," the city's [Śakti-pīṭha](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md#iii-the-central-deities-of-the-kṣetra) goddess), and popular Vārāṇasī tradition calls her **Annapūrṇā** — three names of the one goddess of the *kṣetra*, all of whom "feed the ungrateful guest." [regional]
- **The householder exposes the hypocrisy** — After feeding him, the householder-Śiva **turns the sage's own teaching on him**: *"If a man, failing to get his selfish end for want of fortune, curses in anger — on whom does that curse fall?"* Vyāsa, who had just been lecturing on *dharma*, is caught out. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa* 96)
- **The banishment** — The lord declares Vyāsa **"not worthy to dwell here"** and expels him from the *kṣetra*, styling him a **Rudra-piśāca** (a Rudra-goblin, i.e. one who may haunt the edge of the holy field but not reside in it). (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa* 96)

## The compromise — residence only on the eighth and fourteenth *tithi*

- **Pārvatī brokers the mercy** — Vyāsa takes refuge with **Pārvatī**, who intercedes with Śiva and wins him a concession: *"Grant him leave to enter this holy spot on the **eighth (aṣṭamī)** and **fourteenth (caturdaśī)** lunar days."* Śiva assents — *"So be it."* (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa* 96)
- **Why those two *tithi*** — Both are **Śaiva days**: caturdaśī (esp. Kṛṣṇa-caturdaśī) is Śiva's own *tithi* (Māsa-Śivarātri), and aṣṭamī a Bhairava/goddess day — so the exiled Viṣṇu-partisan sage may re-enter only on days sacred to the god who expelled him. The rest of the month he is kept out. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa* 96) [scholarly]
- **The exile across the Gaṅgā** — Vyāsa **settles on the eastern bank of the Gaṅgā, south-east of Lolārka**, where "day and night he gazed at the holy spot not far from his sight, and on the eighth and fourteenth *tithi* he would enter it." The bank-of-exile is the site later called **Vyāsakāśī**. (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa* 96)

## Vyāsakāśī / Ramnagar — the legend on the ground

- **The one east-bank ghāṭ** — Vārāṇasī's ghāṭs almost all line the **western** bank in a great crescent; the single notable **eastern-bank** ghāṭ sits below the **Vyāsa temple at Ramnagar**, directly facing the city — the geography read as Vyāsa's enforced vantage, "always looking at Kāśī he could not live in." [regional]
- **The Vyāsakāśī temple (Ramnagar Fort)** — Inside/beside the **Ramnagar Fort** (seat of the Kāśī Nareś, the Maharaja of Banaras) stands the **Vyāsa Kāśī / Vyāseśvara temple**, its present structure a **c. 1750 CE** foundation of the local ruler. Its sanctum holds **three liṅgas in a copper casing**, glossed as **Vyāsa, his son Śuka(deva)**, and **Kāśī Viśvanātha** — the exiled sage kept in liṅga-form beside the very lord who barred him. [scholarly] [regional]
- **"Kāśī-yātrā is incomplete without Vyāsa"** — Local tradition holds that Śiva, relenting, promised Vyāsa a boon: a **temple would stand where he dwelt, and Kāśī-darśana would be incomplete without crossing the river to visit it** — turning the banishment into a mandatory coda of the pilgrimage. [folk] [regional]
  - *Note:* this "pilgrimage-incomplete" clause is **temple / oral tradition**, not stated in the Kāśī-khaṇḍa chapters, which end only with the aṣṭamī/caturdaśī concession and the settling south-east of Lolārka. Keep the two apart. [folk]

## Reading the tale — what the Śaiva redactor is doing

- **Humbling the author of the Purāṇas** — The sharpest irony is that Vyāsa is, by tradition, the **compiler of the very Skanda Purāṇa** that records his defeat; the Kāśī-khaṇḍa makes the arranger of all scripture **submit in his own book** to the city and its god. [scholarly]
- **A sectarian statement** — Ch. 95 stages and settles a **Vaiṣṇava-vs-Śaiva** supremacy debate on Śaiva ground (Viṣṇu himself concedes Śiva); ch. 96 punishes a Vaiṣṇava-partisan sage's **entitlement** — together a **medieval Śaiva Vārāṇasī charter** asserting the primacy of Viśveśvara's city. [scholarly] [late text]
- **The anna / hospitality theology** — The engine of ch. 96 is **food**: a guest denied alms curses; the goddess who *is* food (Annapūrṇā) feeds him lavishly and thereby shames the curse. It dramatises the Kāśī doctrine that **the city never starves a guest**, and that resentment over alms is itself the disqualifying sin. See [anna, naivedya & prasāda](../05-concepts-and-dharma/anna-naivedya-and-prasada.md). (*Kāśī-khaṇḍa* 96)
- **The curse-that-recoils motif** — Like other Purāṇic tales where a great ṛṣi's curse fails against a greater sanctity, Vyāsa's malediction cannot bind Kāśī and rebounds as his own exile — the **[pāpa / prāyaścitta](../05-concepts-and-dharma/papa-punya-and-prayaschitta.md)** logic that an unjust curse falls on the curser. [scholarly]
