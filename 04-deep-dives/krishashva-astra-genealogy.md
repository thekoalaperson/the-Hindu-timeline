# The sons of Kṛśāśva — the Rāmāyaṇa's astra-genealogy

> Where this sits: the **Rāmāyaṇa's own theory of what a divine weapon is**. Arguing a frightened Daśaratha into letting Rāma go, **Vasiṣṭha** explains that the missiles [Viśvāmitra](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md) commands are not techniques but **persons** — the hundred sons of **Kṛśāśva Prajāpati** by **Jayā** and **Suprabhā**, daughters of Dakṣa: fifty formless strike-astras born to destroy the asura hosts, and fifty named **Saṃhāras**, the withdrawers ([Bāla Kāṇḍa](ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md) **21.13–18**). Six sargas later they are handed to Rāma and stand before him in bodily form, asking for orders (**Bāla 27–28**).

Almost every treatment of divine weaponry in this corpus and outside it is drawn from the **Mahābhārata**, where astras are knowledge — mantras acquired, remembered, and forgotten. The **Rāmāyaṇa's** account is different in kind and older in its own frame: the weapons are **begotten**, they have a father, two mothers and a paternal grandfather-in-law, they are counted as a family of a hundred, they arrive in person when transferred, they speak, and they are dismissed to live in the memory of the man who owns them until he thinks of them again. This file is the canonical home of that genealogy and of the two transfer-scenes that realize it. The object-by-object registry — which astra does what, where each is used, which are Rāmāyaṇa-only — lives at [Divyāstras & divine weapons](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md).

It also answers a question the epic never asks aloud: **why a brahmarṣi, not a kṣatriya war-guru, arms Rāma.** Viśvāmitra is not a teacher of archery. He is the custodian of an inherited arsenal, and the Bāla Kāṇḍa is built around the transaction in which he hands it on.

## The genealogy — Bāla Kāṇḍa 21

- **The occasion** — Daśaratha, having promised Viśvāmitra whatever he asks, refuses when the ask turns out to be Rāma; Viśvāmitra's anger shakes the earth, and **Vasiṣṭha** intervenes with a speech whose whole purpose is to tell the king that his son will be *safer* with this sage than at home. The genealogy is offered as reassurance to a frightened father — its rhetorical setting, and the reason it is delivered at all. (*Bāla* 21)
- **The weapons are Kṛśāśva's children** — *sarvāstrāṇi kṛśāśvasya putrāḥ paramadhārmikāḥ*: "all the astras are the sons of Kṛśāśva, supremely righteous." They were given to **Kauśika** (Viśvāmitra) long ago, "when he was still ruling his kingdom" — that is, in his kṣatriya life, before the brahmarṣi career. (*Bāla* 21.13)
  - *On the donor:* the verse names **no giver** — it is a passive, "were given to Kauśika formerly." The standard commentarial reading, followed by this corpus, supplies **Śiva**, on the strength of Bāla 55–56, where Viśvāmitra deploys astras won from Śiva by austerity. Worth flagging: the donor is inference, the parentage is text. [scholarly]
- **What kind of beings** — *naikarūpā mahāvīryā dīptimanto jayāvahāḥ*: "of no single form, of great valour, blazing, victory-bringing." They are described as sons of a Prajāpati's daughters' husband, not as spells — the epic's grammar treats them throughout as animate. (*Bāla* 21.14)
- **The two mothers** — **Jayā** and **Suprabhā**, daughters of **Dakṣa Prajāpati**, between them bore **a hundred** supremely radiant *astra-śastra*. The hundred is the text's own total. (*Bāla* 21.15)
- **Jayā's fifty — the strike-astras** — Jayā, having obtained a boon, bore fifty excellent sons "for the slaughter of the asura armies," *aprameyān arūpiṇaḥ* — **immeasurable and formless**. These are the weapons of assault. (*Bāla* 21.16)
- **Suprabhā's fifty — the Saṃhāras** — Suprabhā bore fifty more, *saṃhārān nāma durdharṣān durākramān balīyasaḥ* — "named the **Saṃhāras**, unassailable, unconquerable, exceedingly strong." *Saṃhāra* is withdrawal, recall, the calling-back of a loosed weapon. The Rāmāyaṇa's arsenal is symmetrical by birth: **every strike has a sibling that ends it**. (*Bāla* 21.17)
- **And Viśvāmitra can make more** — He knows the hundred "exactly as they are," and — *apūrvāṇāṃ ca janane śaktaḥ* — is able to **generate weapons that never existed before**. The line is easy to skip and hard to overstate: the armorer is not merely a holder of stock. (*Bāla* 21.18)
- **Kṛśāśva himself** — Titled **Prajāpati** here and given no story, no lineage above him and no other appearance in the epic. He exists in the Rāmāyaṇa solely as the father of the weapons. Neither **Jayā** nor **Suprabhā** appears in the standard Purāṇic rosters of Dakṣa's daughters in this role; the pair belongs to this genealogy alone and does not fit the Dakṣa-daughter lists at [The Prajāpatis](../08-beings-and-bestiary/the-prajapatis.md). [scholarly]

## The handing-over — Bāla 27 (the strike-roster)

- **Why it happens where it happens** — Viśvāmitra confers the arsenal *after* Tāṭakā is killed and *before* the sacrifice is guarded: the boy has proved he will shoot, and is armed for what is coming. The transfer is a reward and a provisioning, not a curriculum. (*Bāla* 27.1–3)
- **The roster** — Some four dozen named missiles are recited in sequence, among them the discs of **Daṇḍa, Dharma, Kāla, Viṣṇu and Indra**; the **Vajra**; Śiva's **śūla**; **Brahmaśiras**, **Aiṣīka** and the **Brahmāstra**; the maces **Modakī** and **Śikharī**; the nooses of **Dharma, Kāla and Varuṇa**; **Pināka** and **Nārāyaṇa**; the **āgneya** and **vāyavya**; **Hayaśiras**, **Krauñca**, **Mohana**, **Prasvāpana**, **Praśamana**, **Varṣaṇa**, **Śoṣaṇa**, **Santāpana**, **Vilāpana**, **Mādana**, **Mānava**, **Satya**, **Māyādhara**, **Tejaḥprabha**, **Śiśira**, **Tvāṣṭra**. (*Bāla* 27.4–20)
- **They arrive in person** — At the close of the recitation "all those venerable missiles came near and stood accessible to Rāghava in their astral forms" — some blazing like fire, some like smoke, some moon-like, some like coals. (*Bāla* 27.21–24)
- **They ask for orders** — Standing with folded hands, the astras address him: *here are your thralls, we are here to accomplish whatever you bid us do.* (*Bāla* 27.25–26)
- **Rāma dismisses them into memory** — He tells them to **manifest in his cognisance whenever he needs them**, and they consent and depart. The weapons are not carried; they are *remembered*. (*Bāla* 27.27) — this single instruction is the epic's operative definition of an astra.

## The counter-arsenal — Bāla 28 (the saṃhāra-roster)

- **Rāma asks for it** — He asks Viśvāmitra outright for the means of **recalling** what he has just been given — *saṃhāra*. That the disciple asks for the brakes rather than the sage volunteering them is a small, deliberate touch of characterization. (*Bāla* 28.1–3)
- **The roster** — Around forty are named, a wholly separate onomastic register from the strike-list — no elemental deities, no discs, but abstractions, body-parts and numbers: **Satyavān, Satyakīrti, Dhṛṣṭa, Rabhasa, Pratihāratara, Parāṅmukha, Avāṅmukha, Lakṣya, Alakṣya, Dṛḍhanābha, Sunābha, Daśākṣa, Śatavaktra, Daśaśīrṣa, Śatodara, Padmanābha, Mahānābha, Dundunābha, Svanābha, Jyotiṣa, Śakuna, Nairāśya, Vimala, Yaugandhara, Vinidra, Daitya, Pramathana, Śucibāhu, Mahābāhu, Niṣkali, Virucī, Sārcirmālī, Dhṛtimālī, Vṛttimān, Rucira, Pitrya, Saumanasa, Vidhūta, Makara, Karavīrakara, Dhana, Dhānya**. (*Bāla* 28.4–10)
- **The same protocol** — They too appear in person — "here we are, tiger among men; what shall we do for you?" — and Rāma sends them likewise to **dwell in his memory** and come when thought of, then go where they will. (*Bāla* 28.11–14)
- **The count does not come to a clean hundred** — Bāla 21.15 says a hundred; the two rosters as printed yield roughly forty-six and roughly forty, and the totals shift with how the dvandva compounds are resolved and with the recension. The **hundred is the genealogy's figure**, and the enumerated lists approximate rather than exhaust it; fifty-and-fifty is a total, not an itemized count. [textual]

## What the genealogy is doing

- **Why Viśvāmitra is the armorer** — The Bāla Kāṇḍa's whole first movement is a transfer of the Kṛśāśva inheritance from a sage who cannot use it to a boy who can. Viśvāmitra is under a **dīkṣā-vow** that bars him from striking at Mārīca and Subāhu himself ([Bāla](ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md) 19) — he holds a hundred weapons and is forbidden to raise one. Rāma is the hands the arsenal has been waiting for. (*Bāla* 19, 21, 27–28) — see [Viśvāmitra & Vasiṣṭha](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md)
- **The first use, immediately** — Two sargas later Rāma flings **Mārīca** a hundred yojanas into the sea with the **Mānava** astra and kills **Subāhu** with the **āgneya** — the newly-transferred stock proved on the very demons it was transferred to fight. (*Bāla* 30) — see [Mārīca](characters/maricha.md), [Subāhu](characters/subahu.md)
- **And their limit, in the same book** — In Śatānanda's narration of Viśvāmitra's own past, the king Kauśika hurls astra after astra at Vasiṣṭha's hermitage and **Vasiṣṭha's bare brahmadaṇḍa swallows every one, the Brahmāstra included** ([Bāla](ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md) 55–56). The Bāla Kāṇḍa gives the arsenal and then, forty sargas later, shows what it cannot do — the argument that drives Viśvāmitra out of kingship and into tapas. (*Bāla* 55–56)
- **Not the source of every weapon Rāma carries** — Keep the Kṛśāśva stock distinct from the arsenal **Agastya** gives him in the forest: Viṣṇu's bow, **one** Brahmā-given arrow, **two** Indra-given quivers and a gold-sheathed sword ([Araṇya](ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md) 12). It is that single Agastya-transmitted **Brahmā-arrow** — not a Kṛśāśva astra — that kills Rāvaṇa at [Yuddha](ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md) 108, and Indra's quiver-arrows that kill Khara at *Araṇya* 30. Two donors, two systems, never merged. (*Araṇya* 12, 30; *Yuddha* 108)
- **The theology of it** — Because the weapons are *children*, loosing one is a summons and recalling one is a dismissal; because each strike has a birth-sibling that undoes it, the epic's arsenal is closed and symmetric rather than escalatory; and because they must be *remembered* to exist, an astra can be **forgotten** — the premise on which several later crises in Sanskrit epic turn. [scholarly]

## Two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic** — Kṛśāśva is a Prajāpati of the creation-era; the transfer to Kauśika belongs to Viśvāmitra's kingly life, generations before Daśaratha; the conferral on Rāma falls in the **Tretā Yuga** of the current 28th Mahāyuga, days after Tāṭakā's death.
- **Academic / text-critical** — Bāla 21 and 27–28 lie in the **Bāla Kāṇḍa**, which historical-critical scholarship treats along with the Uttara as a later frame around the older core books; within that frame the personified-astra conception is nevertheless a distinct and un-Mahābhārata-like stratum of thought, and it is the only place in the two epics where divine weapons are given a **parentage**. Do not read the Rāmāyaṇa's ontology back into the Mahābhārata's, where astras are mantric knowledge. [scholarly]
