# Karṇa — the Tragic Son of the Sun

> Where this sits: a **Dvāpara-era life** spanning the whole [Mahābhārata](mahabharata/), from a secret birth before [Kuntī's](mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md) marriage to a death on the 17th day of the Kurukṣetra War. Karṇa belongs to the [Mahābhārata deep-dives](../04-deep-dives/) and to the [28th Mahāyuga, Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md), at the very junction of Dvāpara and the [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).
> Up: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Parent epic: [Mahābhārata](mahabharata/) · His death-book: [08 — Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md)

**Karṇa** (Sanskrit *Karṇa*, "ear" — for the earrings he was born with) is the great anti-hero of the [Mahābhārata](mahabharata/): the eldest, unacknowledged son of **[Kuntī](mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md)** by the sun-god **Sūrya**, born before her marriage and cast away on the river; raised a **sūta** (charioteer-bard caste) by **Adhiratha** and **Rādhā**; the only warrior who could rival **Arjuna**; the lifelong friend and warlord of **Duryodhana**; and a man so loaded with **curses** (Paraśurāma's, the Brāhmaṇa's cow-curse, the Earth-goddess's) that his magnificence is doomed from birth. He is the epic's study in *dāna* (charity), loyalty, wounded pride, and the cruelty of caste and fate. His many names — **Vasuṣeṇa, Vaikartana, Rādheya, Sūtaputra, Aṅgarāja, Karṇa** — each mark a chapter of that fate.

---

## I. Birth and abandonment (before the Pāṇḍava generation; Mbh Ādi Parva & Vana Parva)

- **The boon of Durvāsa** - As a young unmarried princess of Kuntibhoja, **Pṛthā/Kuntī** serves the irascible sage **Durvāsa** for a year; pleased, he grants her a **mantra (the *atharvaśiras* / Durvāsa-boon)** by which she may summon any deity and beget a child sharing his nature (Mbh Vana Parva ~305; Ādi Parva 1.104).
- **Testing the mantra on Sūrya** - Out of girlish curiosity, Kuntī invokes the **sun-god Sūrya**; bound by his own boon's power, Sūrya appears and, despite her terror and protest, begets a son in her, restoring her virginity afterward so no shame attaches (Mbh Vana Parva ~305–306).
  - *Variant:* the tellings differ on consent and force — some present a tender theophany, others stress Kuntī's fright and Sūrya's compulsion; the restoration of her maidenhood is constant.
- **Born armored** - The child is born **already wearing a golden *kavacha* (breastplate/armor) fused to his skin and golden *kuṇḍala* (earrings)** — pieces of Sūrya's own radiance, dipped in *amṛta*, making him effectively **invulnerable while he keeps them** (Mbh Vana Parva ~308; Ādi Parva). The earrings give him the name **Karṇa**.
- **Cast on the river** - Unwed and afraid of disgrace, Kuntī seals the infant in a **wicker basket/box (mañjūṣā)** and floats him down the **Aśvanadī → Charmaṇvatī → Yamunā → Gaṅgā** (Mbh Vana Parva ~308). This is the secret she carries until the war's eve.
- **Found by the sūta couple** - The basket is recovered at Campā by **Adhiratha**, a **sūta** (charioteer) of Dhṛtarāṣṭra's court, and his wife **Rādhā**, who are childless; they raise him as their own, naming him **Vasuṣeṇa** ("born with wealth/armor") and **Vṛṣa** (Mbh Vana Parva ~308; Ādi Parva).
  - This adoption fixes his social brand as **Sūtaputra** ("charioteer's son") and **Rādheya** ("Rādhā's son") — slurs that will dog him among kṣatriyas his whole life.

## II. Education and the curses (youth; Mbh Vana Parva / Śānti Parva back-stories)

- **Rejected by Droṇa, drawn to Paraśurāma** - Wanting brahmāstra-grade weapons that **Droṇa** reserves for kṣatriya princes, Karṇa goes to **Paraśurāma** (the kṣatriya-hating avatāra of Viṣṇu), claiming to be a **Brāhmaṇa** to be accepted as a pupil (Mbh Śānti/Vana back-narratives; Critical Edition appendix material).
- **Paraśurāma's curse — the forgotten astra** - One day Paraśurāma sleeps with his head on Karṇa's thigh; a **worm/insect (*alarka*, sometimes a bee) bores into Karṇa's thigh**, but Karṇa endures the agony in silence rather than wake his guru. The flowing blood wakes Paraśurāma, who deduces no Brāhmaṇa could bear such pain — **only a kṣatriya**. Feeling deceived, he curses Karṇa: **at the decisive moment of his greatest need, his memory of the supreme astra (the brahmāstra) will fail him** (Mbh; widely retold).
  - *Variant:* the boring creature is variously a worm, an *alarka*-beetle, or a bee said to be a demon (Dansha) in disguise.
- **The Brāhmaṇa's cow-curse — the sinking wheel** - Practicing archery, Karṇa **accidentally kills a Brāhmaṇa's cow** (mistaking it for game). The grieving Brāhmaṇa curses that **the wheel of his chariot will be swallowed by the earth, leaving him helpless, exactly when he most needs to be steady in battle** (Mbh Karṇa Parva back-reference; [08 — Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md)).
  - *Variant [folk]:* a later popular telling replaces the cow with the **Earth-goddess Bhūmi**: Karṇa, helping a weeping girl recover spilled ghee, **squeezes the soil so hard that he wrings ghee from it**, hurting Bhūmi, who curses that the earth will one day **seize his chariot-wheel** at his hour of need. This Bhūmi-variant is absent from the critical text [late text].
- **A third curse [variant]** - Some regional traditions add that Karṇa, while disguising his birth, is cursed by his guru that his learning will desert him "at the end" — usually conflated with Paraśurāma's curse [folk].

These curses are the gears of his doom; they all converge in the [Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md).

## III. The tournament and the kingship of Aṅga (Mbh Ādi Parva ~134–137)

- **The graduation tournament** - At the martial display of the **Kuru princes** in Hastināpura, **Arjuna** dazzles the crowd; an unknown youth, **Karṇa**, strides in and **matches every feat of Arjuna's**, then challenges him to single combat (Mbh Ādi Parva ~135).
- **Barred by caste** - **Kṛpa** demands Karṇa's lineage, for a prince may not duel a man of low birth; Karṇa, shamed and silent, is publicly humiliated as a **Sūtaputra** (Mbh Ādi Parva ~136).
- **Duryodhana's gift — King of Aṅga** - Seizing the moment, **Duryodhana** crowns Karṇa **King of Aṅga (Aṅgarāja)** on the spot, making him a king fit to fight Arjuna and binding Karṇa to him in **undying gratitude and friendship** (Mbh Ādi Parva ~136). The duel is then deferred by nightfall — and by Adhiratha's arrival, which re-exposes Karṇa's sūta status.
- **The lifelong bond** - From here Karṇa is **Duryodhana's truest ally**, repaying the one man who honored him when birth and the world despised him — the emotional spine of his tragedy.

## IV. Rivalry with Arjuna and the Pāṇḍava years

- **Draupadī's svayaṃvara** - At **Pāñcāla**, Karṇa rises to attempt **Drupada's bow-test**; in the main tradition **Draupadī refuses him as a Sūtaputra** ("I will not wed a charioteer's son"), a public wound that hardens his hatred of the Pāṇḍava circle (Mbh Ādi Parva ~178–179).
  - *Variant [scholarly]:* the **Critical Edition (BORI)** treats Draupadī's explicit rejection of Karṇa as a **later interpolation**; in the oldest layer he simply fails (or does not attempt) the test, and the famous insult belongs to the epic's growth, not its core.
- **The Sabhā Parva dice-game** - When Yudhiṣṭhira gambles away the kingdom and **Draupadī is dragged into the assembly**, Karṇa is among her tormentors — calling her a **whore (since she has five husbands)** and endorsing the order to **disrobe her** ([02 — Sabhā Parva](mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md); Mbh Sabhā Parva). It is the act he most regrets, and which seals Pāṇḍava enmity.
- **The digvijaya of Karṇa** - As Aṅgarāja, Karṇa is said to **conquer the four quarters for Duryodhana's *Rājasūya/Vaiṣṇava* sacrifice**, subduing many kings — testimony to real martial supremacy, not just talk (Mbh Vana/Sabhā references).
- **The standing duel-rivalry** - Through the [Virāṭa Parva](mahabharata/04-virata-parva.md) cattle-raid and the war-councils of the [Udyoga Parva](mahabharata/05-udyoga-parva.md), Karṇa is repeatedly cast as the man who will "kill Arjuna" — a vow that defines and destroys him.

## V. Stripped of his armor: the kavacha-kuṇḍala (Mbh Vana Parva ~300–310)

- **Indra's beggary** - Knowing Karṇa's invulnerable **kavacha-kuṇḍala** make Arjuna's death certain, **Indra** (Arjuna's divine father) comes **disguised as a Brāhmaṇa** to ask them as alms, exploiting Karṇa's vow that he **never refuses a Brāhmaṇa at his midday Sūrya-worship** (Mbh Vana Parva ~308–310).
- **Sūrya's warning** - Sūrya warns Karṇa in a dream that the beggar is Indra in disguise and that surrender means death; Karṇa answers that **his fame as the supreme giver (*dānavīra*) outweighs his life** (Mbh Vana Parva ~308).
- **The flaying gift** - Karṇa **cuts the armor and earrings from his own body** and gives them away, earning the new name **Vaikartana** ("the cut one") and the epithet **Vasuṣeṇa**; the wounds heal but he is now **mortal** (Mbh Vana Parva ~310).
- **The Śakti in exchange** - Refusing to let so great a giver be cheated, Indra grants in return the **Vasava-śakti** (also *Śakti*/*Ekaghnī*), an infallible dart that **kills one target unfailingly — but only once** (Mbh Vana Parva ~310). Karṇa hoards it for Arjuna — but is forced to spend it on **Ghaṭotkaca** in the [Droṇa Parva](mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md), the night-battle that secretly saves Arjuna's life.

## VI. Kuntī's revelation and the vow (Mbh Udyoga Parva ~140–146)

- **Kṛṣṇa's secret offer** - On the eve of war, **Kṛṣṇa** privately tells Karṇa the truth of his birth and offers him **the throne and the Pāṇḍavas' allegiance** if he switches sides — even Draupadī as wife. Karṇa **refuses**, unwilling to betray Duryodhana, and asks Kṛṣṇa to keep the secret lest Yudhiṣṭhira, learning he is the eldest, hand him the crown (Mbh Udyoga Parva ~141–144).
- **Kuntī's plea** - **Kuntī** herself comes to the riverbank where Karṇa worships and reveals she is his mother, begging him to join his brothers (Mbh Udyoga Parva ~144–146).
- **The vow to spare four** - Karṇa reproaches her abandonment but grants one mercy: he will **fight only Arjuna**, and **spare Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma, Nakula, and Sahadeva** — so that whatever the outcome, **Kuntī will still have five living sons** (himself or Arjuna among them) (Mbh Udyoga Parva ~146). This vow shapes the whole [Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md).

## VII. The war and death (Mbh — chiefly the [Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md))

- **Sidelined under Bhīṣma** - Slighted by **Bhīṣma** as a *sūta* and rated only "half a *ratha*," Karṇa **refuses to fight for the ten days Bhīṣma commands**, entering battle only after Bhīṣma falls ([06 — Bhīṣma Parva](mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md)); the two reconcile on the arrow-bed.
- **Commander-in-chief** - After Droṇa's death, **Duryodhana consecrates Karṇa as the third Kaurava senāpati** for days 16–17, with **Śalya** as his reluctant, sabotaging charioteer ([08 — Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md)).
- **Sparing the lesser Pāṇḍavas** - True to his vow, Karṇa **defeats but spares Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma, Nakula, and Sahadeva** in turn, taunting them but letting each live ([Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md)).
- **The duel and the convergence of curses** - In the final duel with **Arjuna**, the three curses strike at once: his **chariot-wheel sinks into the earth** (the cow/Bhūmi curse), and as he steps down to free it his **memory of the brahmāstra fails** (Paraśurāma's curse). Pleading the rules of fair combat, he is answered by **Kṛṣṇa's recital of his own past cruelties** (the dice-hall, the killing of Abhimanyu, etc.) ([Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md)).
- **The beheading** - On Kṛṣṇa's word, **Arjuna looses the *Añjalika* arrow** and **decapitates the defenseless Karṇa** — the war's true turning point, after which Kaurava defeat is certain ([Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md)).
- **Posthumous revelation** - Only after the war, at the funerals, does **Kuntī reveal to the grieving Pāṇḍavas that Karṇa was their eldest brother**; Yudhiṣṭhira, anguished, curses all women to **never again keep a secret** ([11 — Strī Parva](mahabharata/11-stri-parva.md); [Śānti Parva](mahabharata/12-shanti-parva.md)). His surviving son **Vṛṣaketu** is taken under Arjuna's protection and features in the [Aśvamedhika Parva](mahabharata/14-ashvamedhika-parva.md).

## VIII. Family — names, wives, and sons

- **Names/epithets** - **Karṇa** (earrings) · **Vasuṣeṇa** (born with wealth/armor) · **Vaikartana** ("the cut," for the flayed armor) · **Rādheya** (son of Rādhā) · **Sūtaputra** (charioteer's son) · **Aṅgarāja / Aṅgādhipa** (King of Aṅga) · **Vṛṣa** (Mbh, various).
- **Wives [variant]** - The **critical Mahābhārata does not name Karṇa's wife/wives**, calling them only women of the **sūta** community. The widely-known names **Vṛṣālī** and **Supriyā** are **20th-century literary creations** of Shivaji Sawant's Marathi novel ***Mṛtyuñjaya*** (1967); "Vṛṣālī" appears coined to echo "Pāñcālī" [late text/modern].
- **Sons** - Tradition gives Karṇa several sons — commonly listed as **Vṛṣaseṇa, Vṛṣaketu, Citrasena, Satyasena, Suṣeṇa, Śatruñjaya, Dvipata, Bāṇaseṇa (Banasena), Prasena, and (in some lists) Sudāman** — most of whom die in the war; **Vṛṣaketu** alone survives as the lineage-bearer (Mbh; lists vary by recension).
  - *Variant:* a "**Sudāman**, son of Karṇa, slain at Draupadī's svayaṃvara" appears in some retellings but **not in Vyāsa's core text** [folk/late].

## IX. Themes & afterlife

- **Caste and merit** - Karṇa is the epic's sharpest indictment of **birth-over-worth**: a king's son raised low, denied his due, and defined by the slur *sūtaputra* — yet the equal of any kṣatriya.
- **Dāna (charity) as identity** - His refusal to refuse a petitioner — giving away the very armor that guards his life — makes him the archetypal **dānavīra**; the same virtue is the lever Indra uses to doom him.
- **Loyalty vs. dharma** - His fidelity to **Duryodhana** over his own brothers, and his choice of his adoptive mother **Rādhā** over his birth-mother Kuntī, dramatize the conflict between gratitude/loyalty and "rightful" dharma.
- **Fate (daiva)** - More than any figure in the epic, Karṇa is **cursed from the womb**; the [Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md) is the machinery of *daiva* closing on a great man.
- **Afterlife** - Hugely sympathetic in modern reception: Shivaji Sawant's ***Mṛtyuñjaya*** (Marathi, 1967) [late text], Ramdhari Singh 'Dinkar's ***Rashmirathi*** (Hindi) [late text], countless TV serials and folk-theatre cycles recast him as the *real* hero of the Mahābhārata [folk].
