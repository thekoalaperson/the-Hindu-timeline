# Karṇa — the Tragic Son of the Sun

> Where this sits: a **Dvāpara-era life** spanning the whole [Mahābhārata](mahabharata/), from a secret birth before [Kuntī's](mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md) marriage to a death on the 17th day of the Kurukṣetra War. Karṇa belongs to the [Mahābhārata deep-dives](../04-deep-dives/) and to the [28th Mahāyuga, Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md), at the very junction of Dvāpara and the [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).
> Up: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Parent epic: [Mahābhārata](mahabharata/) · His death-book: [08 — Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md)

**Karṇa** is the great anti-hero of the [Mahābhārata](mahabharata/): the eldest, unacknowledged son of **[Kuntī](mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md)** by the sun-god **Sūrya**, born before her marriage and cast away on the river; raised a **sūta** (charioteer-bard caste) by **Adhiratha** and **Rādhā**; the archer whom Yudhiṣṭhira himself judged to have no equal on earth (Mbh 1.127.24); the lifelong friend and warlord of **Duryodhana**; and a man so loaded with **curses** (Paraśurāma's and the Brāhmaṇa's in the epic; the Earth-goddess's in later folk telling [folk]) that his magnificence is doomed from birth. He is the epic's study in *dāna* (charity), loyalty, wounded pride, and the cruelty of caste and fate. His many names — **Vasuṣeṇa, Vṛṣa, Vaikartana, Rādheya, Sūtaputra, Aṅgarāja, Karṇa** — each mark a chapter of that fate.

*Citation note:* numbers in the form 3.289.20 are the Pune **Critical Edition** (CE; book.adhyāya.verse). "Ganguli §" is a section of K. M. Ganguli's translation of the Vulgate, whose numbering differs. Passages marked *\** or "App." are in the CE apparatus, not its constituted text.

## I. Birth and abandonment (before the Pāṇḍava generation; Mbh Ādi 1.104 & Vana 3.287–293)

- **The boon of Durvāsas** - **Pṛthā**, daughter of the Yādava **Śūra** (Vasudeva's father), is given in adoption to his childless cousin **Kuntibhoja** (hence "Kuntī") (Mbh 1.104.1–3). In Kuntibhoja's house she serves an irascible Brāhmaṇa guest for a full year; pleased, he gives her a **mantra-set "heard in the Atharvaśiras"** that compels any god she calls (Mbh 3.287–289, esp. 3.289.12–20; Ganguli Vana §CCCIII). The Ādi Parva names him **Durvāsas** and says he gave the spell foreseeing a coming emergency, and that each god she called would give her a son (Mbh 1.104.4–7). In the Vana Parva telling the Brāhmaṇa is unnamed.
- **Testing the mantra on Sūrya** - Out of curiosity, Kuntī invokes the **rising Sun**; Sūrya comes, bound by the spell, and will not leave without giving her a son. When she begs him to go, he threatens to burn her father and the Brāhmaṇa who gave the spell, and she consents only after he promises she will remain a maiden and that the son will be born with immortal earrings and armour (Mbh 3.290.6–26, 3.291.7–22; Ganguli Vana §§CCCIV–CCCV).
  - *Variant (Mbh Vana 3.291.23–28 vs Ādi 1.104.10–12):* in the Vana Parva, Sūrya enters her **by yoga and touches her navel**, and "did not defile her" (*na caivaināṃ dūṣayām āsa*), so there is no bodily union. The Ādi Parva says he begot the child and then **gave her maidenhood back** (*prādāc ca tasyāḥ kanyātvaṃ punaḥ*). Both versions agree that she stays a virgin.
- **Born armoured** - The child is born **wearing his father's armour (*kavaca*) and bright golden earrings (*kuṇḍala*)**, "arisen from *amṛta*", which make him **unkillable while he keeps them** (Mbh 3.292.4–5, 3.292.27, 3.294.10; 1.104.10–11). The earrings were Aditi's, given to Sūrya (Mbh 3.291.21).
  - The popular gloss that the earrings give him the name *Karṇa* ("ear") is a conventional etymology, not a verse of the epic [folk etymology]. The epic's own gloss is in the names bullet in §VIII.
- **Cast on the river** - Fearing her kin, Kuntī lays the newborn in a **wax-sealed, well-lined basket (*mañjūṣā*)**, with the help of her nurse, and sets it on the **Aśvanadī** at night. The basket drifts into the **Carmaṇvatī**, then the **Yamunā** and the **Gaṅgā**, and reaches **Campā** in the sūtas' country (Mbh 3.292.6–26; Ganguli Vana §CCCVI). This is the secret she carries until the war's eve.
- **Found by the sūta couple** - The basket is spotted on the Gaṅgā by **Rādhā**, the childless wife of the **sūta Adhiratha**, "a friend of Dhṛtarāṣṭra". They raise the boy as a gift from the gods (Mbh 3.293.1–10; 1.104.14). The Brāhmaṇas name him **Vasuṣeṇa** for the "wealth" (armour and earrings) he was born with, and he is also called **Vṛṣa** (Mbh 3.293.12–13). In the Ādi summary it is the couple themselves who name him (Mbh 1.104.15); Karṇa later says Adhiratha had the name given by Brāhmaṇas (Mbh 5.139.10). Rādhā later bears natural sons too (Mbh 3.293.11), and Kuntī keeps track of him through spies (Mbh 3.293.14).
  - This adoption fixes his social brand as **Sūtaputra** ("charioteer's son") and **Rādheya** ("Rādhā's son"). Sūtaputra becomes a slur that dogs him among kṣatriyas all his life.

## II. Education and the curses (youth; told by Nārada in Mbh Śānti 12.2–3, and by Karṇa himself in Karṇa Parva 8.29)

- **Droṇa's pupil, then Paraśurāma's** - Karṇa **studies under Droṇa alongside the Kuru princes**, rivalling Arjuna and siding with Duryodhana (Mbh 1.122.47; 12.2.5–8). He later gains weapons from Droṇa, Kṛpa and Rāma (Mbh 3.293.16–17). When he secretly asks Droṇa for the **brahmāstra**, Droṇa refuses: only a Brāhmaṇa of proper vows or a kṣatriya ascetic may know it (Mbh 12.2.9–13). Karṇa then goes to **Paraśurāma (Rāma Jāmadagnya)** on **Mount Mahendra**, introduces himself as **"a Brāhmaṇa of the Bhṛgu line"** (*brāhmaṇo bhārgavo 'smi*), and is accepted (Mbh 12.2.14–18; Ganguli Śānti §II).
- **The Brāhmaṇa's curse — the sinking wheel** - While he lives at Rāma's hermitage, Karṇa wanders alone near the sea-shore and **unknowingly kills the *homa*-cow of a Brāhmaṇa** devoted to the Agnihotra. He confesses and begs forgiveness, but the Brāhmaṇa curses him: **while he fights the rival he strives against, the earth will swallow his chariot-wheel, and the enemy will strike off his head while he is distracted, just as Karṇa carelessly killed the cow**. No gifts can undo the curse (Mbh 12.2.19–29; Ganguli Śānti §II).
  - *Variant (Mbh Karṇa Parva 8.29.31–34):* in Karṇa's own account to Śalya, he shot the **calf** of the *homa*-cow, carelessly, as it wandered in a lonely place, and the wheel is to "fall into a pit". He offered the Brāhmaṇa 700 tusked elephants and hundreds of servants, but the Brāhmaṇa would not relent.
  - *Variant (Mbh Strī 11.21.11):* in the lament of Karṇa's wife, as Gāndhārī reports it, the earth swallowing his wheel is put down to **"the teacher's curse"** (*ācāryaśāpa*; Ganguli Strī §21: "thy preceptor's curse"), not the Brāhmaṇa's.
  - *Variant [folk] — the Earth-goddess's curse:* later popular tellings add a third curse. Karṇa helps a weeping girl who has spilt her ghee (in some versions, milk) and **squeezes it back out of the soil**, hurting **Bhūmi**, who vows to **seize his chariot-wheel** at his hour of need. The episode is **absent from the Critical Edition**: the epic attributes the sinking wheel to the Brāhmaṇa's curse (Mbh 12.2.24; 8.66.42), or once to the teacher's (11.21.11), never to Bhūmi. Its regional origin is a locus not verified.
- **Paraśurāma's curse — the failing astra** - Rāma, now pleased with him, teaches Karṇa the **brahmāstra with its withdrawal** (Mbh 12.3.1–3). Weak from fasting, Rāma sleeps with his head in Karṇa's lap. A **blood-eating worm (*kṛmi*) named Alarka**, eight-legged and bristly, bores into Karṇa's thigh. Karṇa bears the pain without moving, for fear of waking his guru. The blood wakes Rāma, who kills the worm with a glance (Mbh 12.3.4–14). From the worm rises a rākṣasa, the former asura **Gṛtsa**, who had abducted Bhṛgu's wife, was cursed by Bhṛgu, and is now freed (Mbh 12.3.15–24). Rāma declares that no Brāhmaṇa could bear such pain. Karṇa admits he is a **sūta**, "born between Brāhmaṇa and kṣatriya", and pleads that a teacher is a father. Rāma curses him: **because he lied out of greed for the weapon, the brahmāstra will come to his mind at every time except the time of his death, when he faces his equal**. Rāma adds that **no kṣatriya on earth will equal him in battle** (Mbh 12.3.24–33; Ganguli Śānti §III).
  - *Variant (Vulgate / Ganguli Śānti §III):* the freed asura is called **Daṃśa** ("Dansa") instead of the CE's **Gṛtsa**.
  - *Variant (Mbh Karṇa Parva 8.29.4–6):* in Karṇa's own account, the creature was **Indra himself**, entering the body of an insect (*kīṭa*) to hinder him for Arjuna's sake. Popular retellings make the insect a **bee** sent by Indra [folk].
- **Nārada's own tally of the causes** - Summing up for Yudhiṣṭhira, Nārada says Karṇa fell because of **the Brāhmaṇa's curse, Rāma's curse, the boon Kuntī won from him, Indra's trickery, Bhīṣma's slight in ranking him a half-chariot, Śalya's sapping of his spirit (*tejovadha*), and Kṛṣṇa's strategy**, and because Arjuna came armed with the divine weapons of Rudra, Indra, Yama, Varuṇa, Kubera, Droṇa and Kṛpa (Mbh 12.5.11–14).

These curses are the gears of his doom; they converge in the [Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md).

## III. The tournament and the kingship of Aṅga (Mbh Ādi 1.124–127)

- **The graduation tournament** - At the display of the **Kuru princes'** skill in Hastināpura, **Arjuna** dazzles the crowd. **Karṇa** strides in, born armour and earrings shining, greets Droṇa and Kṛpa only coolly, and with Droṇa's leave **repeats every feat of Arjuna's**. Duryodhana embraces him, Karṇa asks for his friendship and a **duel with Arjuna**, and the two arm for combat (Mbh 1.126.1–22). **Kuntī recognises her son and faints**; Vidura revives her (Mbh 1.126.27–29).
- **Barred by caste** - **Kṛpa** tells Karṇa to declare his mother, father and lineage, since Arjuna, a prince, will fight only an equal. Karṇa hangs his head "like a rain-battered lotus" (Mbh 1.126.30–33).
- **Duryodhana's gift — King of Aṅga** - **Duryodhana** answers that kingship can come by birth, valour or leadership, and has Karṇa **consecrated King of Aṅga (Aṅgarāja)** on the spot. Karṇa asks what he can give in return, and Duryodhana asks only for "boundless friendship" (Mbh 1.126.34–39).
- **Adhiratha and Bhīma's taunt** - The old charioteer **Adhiratha** totters into the arena. Karṇa, his hair still wet from the consecration, bows to him as his father (Mbh 1.127.1–4). **Bhīma** jeers: the sūtaputra is not fit to die at Arjuna's hand and should "take up the whip" that suits his family; Aṅga suits him no more than the sacrificial cake suits a dog. Duryodhana retorts that the births of heroes, like the sources of rivers, are hard to trace (Mbh 1.127.5–17). **The sun sets**, the duel never happens, and Duryodhana leads Karṇa away by torchlight (Mbh 1.127.18–19).
- **The lifelong bond** - From here Karṇa is **Duryodhana's truest ally**, repaying the one man who honoured him when birth and the world despised him. This bond is the emotional spine of his tragedy (Mbh 1.126.38–39; 5.139.13–17).

## IV. Rivalry with Arjuna and the Pāṇḍava years

- **Draupadī's svayaṃvara** - At **Pāñcāla**, in the Vulgate, Karṇa strings **Drupada's bow** and sets the arrows, and **Draupadī cries out, "I will not choose a sūta"**. Karṇa laughs bitterly at the sun and throws the bow down (Mbh CE 1.178.17, star passage \*1827; Ganguli Ādi §CLXXXIX). It is a public wound that hardens his hatred of the Pāṇḍava circle.
  - *Variant (Mbh CE 1.179.4) [scholarly]:* the **Critical Edition** keeps the rejection only in its apparatus (\*1827, after 1.178.17). In its constituted text, Karṇa is simply among the famous kings, "Karṇa, Śalya and others", who **could not bend the bow** (1.179.4). Other manuscripts (\*1830, \*1834) have him **fail by a hair's breadth**.
- **The Sabhā Parva dice-game** - When Yudhiṣṭhira gambles everything away and **Draupadī is dragged into the assembly**, Karṇa argues that a woman with **many husbands is a *bandhakī* (an unchaste woman)**, so bringing her in, in one garment or naked, is no outrage. He then **orders Duḥśāsana to strip the Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī of their clothes** (Mbh 2.61.35–38; [02 — Sabhā Parva](mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md)). Before the war he tells Kṛṣṇa that he **burns with regret for the harsh words he spoke to the Pāṇḍavas** to please Duryodhana (Mbh 5.139.45).
- **The Vaiṣṇava sacrifice and the foot-washing vow** - After the Ghoṣayātrā, Duryodhana cannot perform a Rājasūya while Yudhiṣṭhira lives (nor while his father Dhṛtarāṣṭra lives), so he is steered to the **Vaiṣṇava sacrifice**, its equal (Mbh 3.241.25–33). Karṇa then vows **not to wash his feet until Arjuna is slain** (Mbh 3.243.15). In the same book, the Dānavas say the **soul of the slain asura Naraka has entered Karṇa** to set him against Arjuna (Mbh 3.240.19–21, 3.240.32).
  - *Variant (Vulgate; Mbh CE Vana App. I no. 24, after 3.241.15) [interpolation]:* Karṇa's **digvijaya**, his conquest of the four quarters to raise tribute for that sacrifice, survives only in the Vulgate (Ganguli Vana §CCLII). The Critical Edition relegates it to an appendix.
- **Kaliṅga and Jarāsandha** - At the svayaṃvara of the daughter of King **Citrāṅgada of Kaliṅga** at Rājapura, Duryodhana carries the princess off and Karṇa, guarding his rear, cuts down the pursuing kings' bows and routs them (Mbh 12.4.11–21). Later **Jarāsandha** challenges him to single combat. Karṇa, wrestling, splits the seam where Jarā had joined his body, and the pleased king gives him the town of **Mālinī**; Karṇa also rules **Campā** among the Aṅgas (Mbh 12.5.1–7; Ganguli Śānti §§IV–V).
- **The standing duel-rivalry** - Through the [Virāṭa Parva](mahabharata/04-virata-parva.md) cattle-raid and the war-councils of the [Udyoga Parva](mahabharata/05-udyoga-parva.md), Karṇa is repeatedly cast as the man who will kill Arjuna. That vow defines and destroys him.

## V. Stripped of his armour: the kavaca-kuṇḍala (Mbh Vana 3.284–294; Ganguli Vana §§CCXCVIII–CCCVIII)

- **Sūrya's warning** - In the thirteenth year of the Pāṇḍavas' exile, **Sūrya appears to Karṇa in a dream as a Vedic Brāhmaṇa**. He warns that **Indra** will come disguised as a Brāhmaṇa to beg for the armour and earrings, and that giving them means death (Mbh 3.284.5–19). Karṇa replies that **fame matters more than life**: he will not refuse (Mbh 3.284.20–32; Ganguli Vana §CCXCVIII, printed "CCLXLVIII" in the archive.org text). Sūrya then advises him to demand **Indra's unfailing *śakti*** in exchange (Mbh 3.286.10–16; Ganguli Vana §CCC).
- **Indra's beggary** - **Indra** (Arjuna's divine father) comes **disguised as a Brāhmaṇa** at the hour when Karṇa, standing in the water at **midday worshipping the Sun, refuses Brāhmaṇas nothing** (Mbh 3.293.21–23). He turns down Karṇa's offers of women, villages, cattle and a kingdom, and asks only for the **armour and earrings** (Mbh 3.294.1–13).
- **The bargain for the Śakti** - Karṇa says he has recognised Indra and asks for an **exchange** (*vinimaya*) (Mbh 3.294.13–17). Indra offers anything except his *vajra*, and Karṇa chooses the **unfailing *śakti*** (Mbh 3.294.19–21). Indra sets the terms. In Indra's hand the śakti slays hundreds and returns. In Karṇa's it will kill **one** mighty foe and then go back to Indra. If Karṇa hurls it carelessly while he still has other weapons, it will strike Karṇa himself. And the one foe Karṇa means it for is **guarded by Kṛṣṇa** (Mbh 3.294.22–34). It is the **Vāsavī śakti** (3.294.34), described as *vaijayantī*, "victory-bringing" (Mbh 7.154.52) and **Ekaghnī**, "one-slayer" (Mbh 7.158.2).
  - *Variant (Mbh Ādi 1.104.17–20):* the Ādi summary has Indra give the śakti unasked, "in wonder", after Karṇa hands over the armour. Sūrya's dream advice appears there only in the apparatus (App. I no. 60, after 1.104.17).
- **The flaying gift** - Karṇa first asks that his body not be left disfigured, and Indra promises he will keep his father's lustre without a scar (Mbh 3.294.30–32). Then Karṇa **cuts the armour from his body and the earrings from his ears**, smiling, as the gods' drums sound. "By that deed" he is called **Vaikartana** (Mbh 3.294.35–38). Indra departs laughing at having tricked him (Mbh 3.294.39).
- **Spent on Ghaṭotkaca** - Karṇa hoards the śakti for Arjuna. In the night battle of the [Droṇa Parva](mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md), with the Kauravas breaking before **Ghaṭotkaca**, he hurls it at the rākṣasa, and so secretly saves Arjuna's life (Mbh 7.154.51–58; 7.157.1–2).

## VI. Kṛṣṇa's offer and Kuntī's revelation (Mbh Udyoga 5.138–144; Ganguli Udyoga §§CXL–CXLVI)

- **Kṛṣṇa's secret offer** - On the eve of war, **Kṛṣṇa** takes Karṇa up into his chariot. He argues that a maiden's son (*kānīna*) is lawfully the son of the man who marries her, so Karṇa is **Pāṇḍu's son by dharma**, and offers him **the throne, the Pāṇḍavas' allegiance and Draupadī as wife "at the sixth turn"** (Mbh 5.138.1–27). Karṇa **refuses**. Adhiratha and Rādhā raised him, Rādhā's milk flowed for him, and Adhiratha performed his birth-rites, named him and married him to wives of the sūta community. He cannot betray Duryodhana (Mbh 5.139.1–17). He asks Kṛṣṇa to **keep the secret**: if Yudhiṣṭhira learned that Karṇa was Kuntī's firstborn, he would refuse the kingdom, and Karṇa would only hand it to Duryodhana (Mbh 5.139.19–23).
- **Kuntī's plea** - **Kuntī** goes to the bank of the **Gaṅgā**, where Karṇa recites prayers facing east, arms raised, until the sun warms his back. She reveals she is his mother and begs him to join his brothers (Mbh 5.142.24–5.143). A **voice from the Sun** confirms her words (Mbh 5.144.1–2).
- **The vow to spare four** - Karṇa reproaches her for abandoning him, but grants one mercy. He will **not kill Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma, Nakula or Sahadeva**, even when they are in his power, and will fight **Arjuna** alone, so that Kuntī will always have **five sons**: with Karṇa if Arjuna dies, or with Arjuna if Karṇa dies (Mbh 5.144.20–22). Kuntī accepts this as fate (Mbh 5.144.24–26). The vow shapes the battles that follow.

## VII. The war and death (Mbh — chiefly the [Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md))

- **Sidelined under Bhīṣma** - At the war council **Bhīṣma** ranks Karṇa only an **ardharatha ("half a chariot-warrior")**. Bhīṣma calls him harsh, boastful and base, and gives as reasons that he has **lost his natural armour and earrings**, is *ghṛṇī* (usually rendered "soft-hearted" or "compassionate"), and labours under **Rāma's curse and the Brāhmaṇa's words** (Mbh 5.165.4–6). **Droṇa** agrees, adding that Karṇa is arrogant, turns away in battle, and is **careless** (*pramādī*) (Mbh 5.165.7–8). Karṇa vows **not to fight while Bhīṣma lives** (Mbh 5.165.27), so he enters battle only after Bhīṣma falls ([06 — Bhīṣma Parva](mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md)). On the arrow-bed Bhīṣma tells him he has known from Nārada, Vyāsa and Keśava that Karṇa is **Kaunteya, not Rādheya**. He bears him no hatred: he spoke harshly to dampen his fire (*tejovadha*), because Karṇa hated the Pāṇḍavas without cause, and for fear of a split in the family (Mbh 6.117.9–13).
- **Sparing the lesser Pāṇḍavas** - True to his vow, Karṇa defeats each of the four brothers and lets him live, taunting him as he goes. In the [Droṇa Parva](mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md) he spares a disarmed **Bhīma**, mocking him as a "glutton" fit only for the forest (Mbh 7.114.67–79), and **Sahadeva** (Mbh 7.142.13–17). In the Karṇa Parva he spares **Nakula**, hanging his bow around his neck (Mbh 8.17.89–95), and **Yudhiṣṭhira**, whom he touches on the shoulder and tells to keep to Vedic study and sacrifice (Mbh 8.33.35–40). Three of the four passages explicitly cite "Kuntī's words" (7.114.67, 7.142.17, 8.17.95).
- **Commander-in-chief** - After Droṇa's death, **Duryodhana consecrates Karṇa as the third Kaurava senāpati** for days 16–17, with **Śalya** as his charioteer. Śalya has secretly promised Yudhiṣṭhira to sap Karṇa's spirit (*tejovadha*) from the driver's seat (Mbh 5.8.27–28; 12.5.12; [08 — Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md)).
- **The duel and the convergence of the two curses** - In the final duel with **Arjuna**, Karṇa's **chariot-wheel falls** into the ground "by the Brāhmaṇa's curse". At the same moment **the weapon he had received from Rāma does not come to mind** (Mbh 8.66.42; Ganguli Karṇa §90). Karṇa rails that dharma does not protect its devotees, and fights on. He forces out a brahmāstra, which Arjuna answers first with Indra's weapon and then, at Kṛṣṇa's urging, with a brahmāstra of his own (Mbh 8.66.43–52). Then **the earth swallows his wheel**. Karṇa begs Arjuna to hold off, by the warrior's code, while he lifts it (Mbh 8.66.59–65).
- **Kṛṣṇa's answer** - **Kṛṣṇa** retorts, "how fortunate that you now remember dharma". He lists the occasions when Karṇa's dharma was absent: Draupadī dragged in wearing a single garment, the rigged dice, Karṇa's laughter at her while she was in her season, and the second dice-challenge (Mbh 8.67.1–5).
  - *Variant (Vulgate; Mbh CE star passages \*1142–\*1144 after 8.67.3, \*1145 after 8.67.4, \*1146 after 8.67.5) [interpolation]:* the Vulgate lengthens the list with Bhīma's poisoning with snakes and poisoned food, the lac-house at Vāraṇāvata, the kingdom withheld after the thirteenth year, Karṇa's taunt that Draupadī should "choose another husband", and the **killing of the boy Abhimanyu by many warriors** (Ganguli Karṇa §91). The CE keeps all of these only in its apparatus.
- **The beheading** - Stung, Arjuna fights on. Karṇa showers another brahmāstra and **again struggles to free the chariot** (Mbh 8.67.8). Arjuna cuts down his banner with its elephant-rope device, then looses the ***Añjalika*** arrow with an oath of truth, and **strikes off Karṇa's head** in the afternoon (Mbh 8.67.14–24, 8.67.33). A radiance leaves Karṇa's body and enters the sky (Mbh 8.67.27). This is the war's true turning point, after which Kaurava defeat is certain ([Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md)).
- **Posthumous revelation** - Only at the water-offerings after the war does **Kuntī tell the Pāṇḍavas that Karṇa was their eldest brother**, born to her of the Sun. Yudhiṣṭhira grieves and has Karṇa's women brought to him (Mbh 11.27.11–23; [11 — Strī Parva](mahabharata/11-stri-parva.md)). In the [Śānti Parva](mahabharata/12-shanti-parva.md), Nārada tells him Karṇa's whole story (Mbh 12.1–5), and Yudhiṣṭhira **curses all women never again to keep a secret** (Mbh 12.6.10).
- **In heaven** - At the epic's end, Yudhiṣṭhira finds Karṇa in heaven, and Karṇa **enters his father, the Sun** (Mbh 18.4.4, 18.5.18; [18 — Svargārohaṇa Parva](mahabharata/18-svargarohana-parva.md)).

## VIII. Family — names, wives, and sons

- **Names/epithets** - **Vasuṣeṇa** ("born with wealth", i.e. the armour and earrings; Mbh 3.293.12) · **Vṛṣa** (Mbh 3.293.13; 1.126.37) · **Vaikartana**: the epic itself derives it from the **deed of cutting** (*vi-kṛt*) off his armour (*vaikartanaḥ karmaṇā tena*, Mbh 1.104.21; 3.294.38). The lexical alternative, "son of **Vikartana**, the Sun", rests on a name of the Sun found in the lexica; no CE parvan searched uses *vikartana* for the Sun [scholarly] · **Karṇa**: the epic says he "became Vaikartana Karṇa" through the same deed (Mbh 1.104.21), which Ganguli renders as "Karna (the cutter or peeler of his own cover)", a translator's gloss (Ganguli Ādi §CXI); "named for his ear-rings" is a conventional gloss [folk etymology] · **Rādheya** (son of Rādhā) · **Ādhirathi** (son of Adhiratha) · **Sūtaputra / Sūtaja** (charioteer's son) · **Aṅgarāja / Aṅgeśvara** (King of Aṅga; Mbh 1.126.36, 1.127.22).
- **Wives** - The **Critical Edition never names Karṇa's wives**. He says Adhiratha married him to wives of the **sūta** community, who bore him sons and grandsons (Mbh 5.139.10–14). The Strī Parva shows one of them, the **mother of Vṛṣasena** (and of Suṣeṇa), lamenting over his body (Mbh 11.21.10–12).
  - *Variant (Kaṭṭaikkūttu *Karṇa Mokṣam*, Tamil) [regional]:* his wife is **Ponnuruvi**, a Kaliṅga princess.
  - *Variant (Kāśīdāsī Mahābhārata, Bengali) [regional]:* his wife is **Padmāvatī**.
  - *Variant (Shivaji Sawant's Marathi novel **Mṛtyuñjaya**, 1967) [late text/modern]:* the widely known names **Vṛṣālī** (first wife) and **Supriyā** (second wife). They are **20th-century literary creations**, and "Vṛṣālī" appears to be coined to echo "Pāñcālī".
- **Sons** - The CE names **Vṛṣasena**, "the eldest son" (Mbh 8.32.41), slain by Arjuna before his father's eyes (Mbh 8.62.55–61, 8.63.1; 8.4.104 notes his other sons), and **Citrasena, Satyasena and Suṣeṇa**, slain by **Nakula** on the 18th day (Mbh 9.9.8–48). The accounts are not consistent even within the CE. At 8.32.49, on the 17th day, Bhīma already fells "Satyasena, Karṇa's son", where the Vulgate names him **Bhānusena** (Ganguli Karṇa §48). At 8.60.4–7 Karṇa's son **Suṣeṇa** falls to **Sātyaki**'s arrows, where the Vulgate names him **Prasena** (Ganguli Karṇa §82). So in the CE both Satyasena and Suṣeṇa die twice. When Janamejaya asks about the heroes in heaven, he names "Karṇa's valiant sons" among them (Mbh 18.5.3).
  - *Not sons:* **Śatruñjaya** and **Vipāṭa** (sometimes miswritten "Dvipata"), who appear in popular lists of Karṇa's sons, are his **younger (uterine) brothers**, sons of Adhiratha and Rādhā. Arjuna kills them on the twelfth day together with a third, unnamed younger brother, "three brothers of one womb" (*sodaryā ... trayaḥ*), before Karṇa's eyes (Mbh 7.31.58–60; Ganguli agrees).
  - *Variant (Jaimini Bhārata, Aśvamedha Parva) [late text/regional]:* **Vṛṣaketu**, Karṇa's son who survives the war, is taken under Arjuna's protection and rides with Yudhiṣṭhira's sacrificial horse, where he is **slain by Babhruvāhana** (Jaimini Aśv. ch. 30, per V. Mani, *Purāṇic Encyclopaedia*). He does **not appear in the Vyāsa Critical Edition** (none of the parvans searched names him), and the "sole surviving lineage-bearer" role belongs to this regional cycle.
  - *Variant [folk/late]:* a "**Sudāman**, son of Karṇa, slain at Draupadī's svayaṃvara" appears in some modern lists. It is **not in the CE svayaṃvara** (Mbh 1.177–1.181), where a star passage has Karṇa arrive with his son Vṛṣasena (\*1819 after 1.177.20); locus not verified. **Bāṇasena**, also in popular lists, likewise has no locus found in the CE.

## IX. Themes & afterlife

- **Caste and merit** - Karṇa is the epic's sharpest indictment of **birth-over-worth**: a king's son raised low, denied his due, and defined by the slur *sūtaputra*, yet the equal of any kṣatriya. Duryodhana's defence of him at the tournament ("the sources of heroes, like those of rivers, are hard to know"; Mbh 1.127.11) states the theme in the text itself.
- **Dāna (charity) as identity** - His refusal to refuse a petitioner, even giving away the armour that guards his life, makes him the archetypal **dānavīra**. The same virtue is the lever Indra uses to doom him (Mbh 3.284.12–13, 3.294).
- **Loyalty vs. dharma** - His fidelity to **Duryodhana** over his own brothers, and his choice of his adoptive mother **Rādhā** over his birth-mother Kuntī (Mbh 5.139.5–9), dramatise the conflict between gratitude and loyalty on one side and "rightful" dharma on the other.
- **Fate (daiva)** - More than any figure in the epic, Karṇa is **cursed from the womb**. Kuntī herself answers his vow with "fate is stronger" (Mbh 5.144.24), and the [Karṇa Parva](mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md) is the machinery of *daiva* closing on a great man.
- **Afterlife in reception** - He is hugely sympathetic in modern reception. Shivaji Sawant's ***Mṛtyuñjaya*** (Marathi, 1967) [late text], Ramdhari Singh 'Dinkar''s ***Rashmirathi*** (Hindi) [late text], the Tamil Kaṭṭaikkūttu ***Karṇa Mokṣam*** [regional], and countless TV serials and folk-theatre cycles recast him as the *real* hero of the Mahābhārata [folk].
