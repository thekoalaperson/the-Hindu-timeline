# Sudarśana, Śaṅkhacūḍa and Vyomāsura — the later Vraja slayings

> Cosmic address: three short post-*rāsa* episodes of Kṛṣṇa's Vraja boyhood, set in the closing **[Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** of the current 28th Mahāyuga (Śveta-Vārāha Kalpa › 7th [Vaivasvata] Manvantara) — the vidyādhara **Sudarśana** freed from a python's body, the guhyaka **Śaṅkhacūḍa** beheaded and his crest-jewel given to **Balarāma**, and **Vyomāsura** the sheep-thief crushed at a mountain cave. Principal source: **[Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md) 10.34** and **10.37.28–33**.
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Master index: [README](../README.md) · Up to: [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md) · Sits after: [Keśi & Ariṣṭa](keshi-arishta-keshava.md) · Roster: [Vraja Boyhood Demons](vraja-boyhood-demons.md)

This file gathers **three uncovered slayings** of the Vṛndāvana cycle that fall *after* the great *rāsa-līlā* and the demons cataloged elsewhere — episodes the corpus's Kṛṣṇa files pass over. They are minor in length but each turns on a distinctive point of doctrine or disambiguation: a **liberation-by-touch** (Sudarśana), a **name-collision** with the famous Tulasī-cycle demon (this Śaṅkhacūḍa is a *different being*), and a **māyā-duel** with a son of the architect-asura Maya (Vyomāsura). The **[Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md), Canto 10** is the sole continuous source for all three; the **Viṣṇu Purāṇa (Book 5)** and **Harivaṃśa (Viṣṇu Parva)** carry parallels for Sudarśana and Vyoma but treat them briefly.

---

## Where these sit — the two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic frame** — All three fall in the **[Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** of our 28th Mahāyuga, in Kṛṣṇa's Vraja youth *before* he leaves for Mathurā to slay [Kaṃsa](kamsa-vadha.md). On the internal chronology of Bhāgavata Canto 10 they come **after** the *rāsa-līlā* (10.29–33) and the horse-demon [Keśin](keshi-arishta-keshava.md) is not yet slain — Sudarśana and Śaṅkhacūḍa in **ch. 34**, Vyoma at the **close of ch. 37** (28–33), the same chapter that opens with Keśin's death and Nārada's *Keśava-stuti*. This is the last cluster of Vraja *līlās* before the Mathurā summons. Kali began at Kṛṣṇa's departure, **3102 BCE** by the traditional reckoning.
- **Academic / historical frame** — The **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** in its received form is dated **[scholarly] c. 9th–10th century CE** (a South-Indian composition, its Canto 10 the most-copied Kṛṣṇa biography); the **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** core is earlier, **[scholarly] c. 4th–5th century CE**, and the **Harivaṃśa** (a *khila* appendix to the Mahābhārata) earlier still, **[scholarly] c. 1st–4th century CE**. The episodes claim Dvāpara antiquity; the texts that preserve them are first-millennium CE. The two clocks are kept apart.

## Sudarśana — the vidyādhara freed from the python (BhP 10.34.1–17)

- **The pilgrimage to Ambikāvana** — On an auspicious day the cowherds of Vraja set out by bullock-cart for **Ambikāvana** — a forest on the bank of the **Sarasvatī** — to worship **Śiva (Paśupati)** and the goddess **Ambikā (Umā)**. They bathed in the river, worshipped the deities with gifts, fasted, and passed the night on the riverbank. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.34.1–3)
  - *Regional identification:* Ambikāvana is traditionally located in **Gujarat** (near modern Ambaji / the Sarasvatī's course); the text itself names only the forest and the river. [regional]
- **The python seizes Nanda** — In the night a huge, hungry **serpent (ajagara, python)** came and began to swallow the sleeping **[Nanda](krishna-lila.md)** from the feet upward. Nanda cried out to Kṛṣṇa in terror; the cowherds sprang up and beat the snake with **firebrands**, but the burning brands would not make it release its coils. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.34.4–6)
- **Liberation by the touch of Kṛṣṇa's foot** — **Kṛṣṇa** touched the serpent with his **foot**. At the touch of the Lord's foot the snake at once **shed its reptile body** and rose up as a **beautiful being — a vidyādhara**, radiant, adorned, its serpent-form dissolved. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.34.7–9)
- **His confession — pride punished by the Āṅgirasa sages** — The freed being told his story: he was a celebrated **vidyādhara named Sudarśana**, opulent and beautiful, who roamed the skies in his aerial car. Once, drunk on his own beauty, he **mocked and derided some homely, deformed-looking sages of the lineage of [Aṅgiras](angiras.md)**; for that insult they **cursed him into the body of a snake**. He had regarded the curse as a mercy, foreseeing that the Lord's touch would one day burn away all his sin. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.34.10–14)
  - *Reading:* the episode is a **liberation-by-contact** (*sparśa-mokṣa*) parable — the same touch that a lesser being would feel as violence (a foot on a snake) is, from the Lord, **redemptive**; the curse itself is reframed as grace. [scholarly]
- **Circumambulation and departure** — Sudarśana **circumambulated** Kṛṣṇa, bowed, obtained his leave, and returned to his own vidyādhara world, freed of the curse. The cowherds, having seen the wonder, completed their worship and returned to Vraja. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.34.15–17)
  - *Name-guard:* this **Sudarśana** is a *vidyādhara* and must not be confused with Viṣṇu's discus **Sudarśana-cakra** (see [Viṣṇu's lotus-eye & the Sudarśana](vishnu-lotus-eye-sudarshana.md)) — a pure homonym. [scholarly]

## Śaṅkhacūḍa — the guhyaka beheaded, the jewel to Balarāma (BhP 10.34.20–32)

- **The Yakṣa who scattered the gopīs** — Later, while **Kṛṣṇa** and **Balarāma** were wandering by night in the forest singing with the young women of Vraja, a wealthy **Yakṣa (guhyaka) named Śaṅkhacūḍa** — a **follower of Kubera** — came upon them. Proud and contemptuous, he began **driving the gopīs away northward** before the very eyes of the two Lords, as if seizing the wealth of another. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.34.20–24)
  - *Class-note:* a **guhyaka** ("hidden one") is a subclass of **[Yakṣa](../08-beings-and-bestiary/yakshas-and-guhyakas.md)**, treasure-attendants of **[Kubera](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/kubera.md)**; Śaṅkhacūḍa's raid is thus a Yakṣa's theft-by-nature turned against Kṛṣṇa's companions. [scholarly]
- **The pursuit and the fist-blow** — Hearing the women cry *"Kṛṣṇa! Rāma!"* like helpless folk calling their protectors, the two brothers snatched up **śāla trees** as clubs and gave chase, crying *"Do not fear!"* Kṛṣṇa swiftly overtook the fleeing Yakṣa, **struck off his head with a blow of his fist** (some renderings: with the tree), and killed him. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.34.25–29)
- **The crest-jewel given to Balarāma** — From the dead Yakṣa's head Kṛṣṇa took the shining **crest-jewel (cūḍāmaṇi / śiromaṇi)** and, returning, **gave it to his elder brother [Balarāma](characters/balarama.md)** in the sight of the women. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.34.30–32)
- **Disambiguation — NOT the Tulasī-cycle Śaṅkhacūḍa** — This guhyaka is a **different being** from the great daitya **Śaṅkhacūḍa** of the *Brahma-Vaivarta Purāṇa*'s Tulasī cycle, who is a former Kṛṣṇa-attendant reborn as an asura, husband of **Tulasī**, made invincible by Viṣṇu's *kavaca* and slain by **Śiva** (see [Śaṅkhacūḍa & Tulasī](shankhachuda-tulasi.md)). The two share only a **name** ("conch-crest"). The Bhāgavata's Śaṅkhacūḍa is a minor **forest Yakṣa** dispatched with a single blow; the Brahma-Vaivarta's is a cosmic asura with an elaborate prior-birth theology. The corpus keeps them strictly apart. [scholarly]

## Vyomāsura — the sheep-thief crushed at the cave (BhP 10.37.28–33)

- **The game of thieves and shepherds** — On another day the cowherd boys were playing at **"thieves and shepherds"** (*caura-pāla* / *steya*): some played the **sheep**, some the **keepers**, and some the **thieves** who tried to carry the sheep off. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.37.28–29)
- **Vyoma the son of Maya** — Into this game came a great **demon named Vyoma** ("Sky"), the **son of the asura Maya** (*Mayaputra*) — a powerful magician (*māyāvī*). Disguised as a cowherd boy, he took the part of a **thief**, and one by one carried off most of the boys who were playing sheep, **hiding them in a mountain cave** and sealing its mouth with a **boulder**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.37.30–31)
  - *Lineage-note:* his father **Maya (Mayāsura)** is the great **architect-magician of the asuras**; Vyoma inherits the illusionist gift, making this the one Vraja slaying that is essentially a **contest of māyā**. [scholarly]
- **Kṛṣṇa detects the thief** — When only **four or five boys** were left playing, Kṛṣṇa — the protector of the cowherds — **understood** what was happening. As Vyoma made to seize more, Kṛṣṇa **caught the demon** as a **lion seizes a jackal/wolf**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.37.32)
- **The crushing and the freeing of the boys** — Vyoma **swelled to the size of a mountain** and struggled, but Kṛṣṇa **dashed him to the ground and strangled/crushed him**, killing him as a **sacrificial animal is slain**. Kṛṣṇa then **broke open the cave**, released the trapped boys, and returned to Vraja **praised by the gods and his companions**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.37.33)
  - *Structural note:* Vyoma's death **closes Bhāgavata ch. 37** — the same chapter that opens with the slaying of the horse-demon [Keśin](keshi-arishta-keshava.md) and Nārada's prophetic *Keśava-stuti* — and is thus one of the **last Vraja līlās** before the demon-king's bow-sacrifice draws Kṛṣṇa to Mathurā. [scholarly]

## Why these three are grouped

- **All from one continuous source-run** — Sudarśana + Śaṅkhacūḍa (ch. 34) and Vyoma (ch. 37) are consecutive within a few chapters of the **Bhāgavata's** Vraja narrative, sharing its late-boyhood setting after the *rāsa-līlā*. Grouping them keeps the corpus's Kṛṣṇa coverage complete without inflating the main [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md) file. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.34, 10.37)
- **Three distinct modes of divine action** — **Sudarśana** shows Kṛṣṇa's touch as **liberating** (a curse dissolved); **Śaṅkhacūḍa** shows him as **martial protector** of the women (theft avenged, trophy to Balarāma); **Vyoma** shows him as **detector and destroyer of illusion** (the magician's own game turned on him). Together they round out the boyhood's repertoire beyond the famous serpent- and beast-demons. [scholarly]
- **Two name-guards in one file** — the file exists partly to **disambiguate**: *Sudarśana* the vidyādhara ≠ the *Sudarśana-cakra*; the *Yakṣa Śaṅkhacūḍa* ≠ the *daitya Śaṅkhacūḍa* of the Tulasī cycle. Both are recorded here explicitly so the collisions are not silently merged. [scholarly]

## Sources

**Primary scriptural texts**
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.34** — "The Deliverance of Sudarśana and Śaṅkhacūḍa": the Vraja pilgrimage to **Ambikāvana** on the **Sarasvatī** to worship Śiva; the **python** that swallows Nanda; **Kṛṣṇa's foot-touch** freeing the **vidyādhara Sudarśana**, cursed by the **Āṅgirasa sages** for mocking them in pride; then the **Yakṣa Śaṅkhacūḍa**, a follower of **Kubera**, who scatters the gopīs and is **beheaded by Kṛṣṇa**, his **crest-jewel given to Balarāma** (verses ~1–17 for Sudarśana, ~20–32 for Śaṅkhacūḍa).
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.37.28–33** — the closing of the Keśin chapter: the boys' game of **thieves and shepherds**; **Vyoma, son of Maya**, disguised as a boy, stealing the "sheep" boys into a **mountain cave**; **Kṛṣṇa** seizing and **crushing** him and **freeing the trapped boys**.
- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Book 5** — parallel notices of the Vraja demons (Sudarśana and Vyoma appear in compressed form); does not develop the guhyaka Śaṅkhacūḍa at the Bhāgavata's length. [scholarly]
- **Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva** — the earliest continuous Kṛṣṇa biography, with brief cognate accounts of the boyhood demon-slayings. [scholarly]
