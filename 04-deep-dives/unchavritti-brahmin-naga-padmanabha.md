# Uñchavṛtti-upākhyāna — the brahmin Dharmāraṇya and the Nāga Padmanābha

> *Cosmic address: the closing **upākhyāna** of the **Mokṣadharma** section of the **[Śānti Parva](mahabharata/12-shanti-parva.md)**, narrated by the dying **Bhīṣma** on his bed of arrows to **Yudhiṣṭhira** at the **Dvāpara–Kali junction** after Kurukṣetra. A doubt-ridden brāhmaṇa named **Dharmāraṇya** seeks out the virtuous **Nāga Padmanābha** in a serpent-city in the **Naimiṣa forest on the Gomatī**, hears the Nāga's eye-witness "wonder of Sūrya" gained from his turn dragging the Sun's one-wheeled car, and is converted to **uñchavṛtti** — the gleaner's life of picking up fallen, scattered grain — as the surest of all dharmas.*
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Master index: [README](../README.md)

A short but structurally deep **teaching-tale (upākhyāna)** that the *Mahābhārata* uses to **close its long liberation-doctrine (Mokṣadharma)**. Its whole burden is a single radical claim already dear to the epic: that the **non-acquisitive gleaner's life — uñchavṛtti** — the humblest and most self-denying of livelihoods, is a **higher road to release than sacrifice, wealth, or grand austerity**. This node is that tale's canonical home; the Śānti Parva index only thumbnails it, and the twin exaltations of the gleaner elsewhere in the epic (the mongoose's charter, the sage Mudgala) are cataloged separately and cross-linked below.

---

## Name-collisions to keep straight

- **This Padmanābha is a *Nāga*, not Viṣṇu** — the corpus is saturated with **Padmanābha as a Viṣṇu-epithet** ("lotus-navelled," from whose navel-lotus Brahmā springs) and as the reclining **Ananta-Padmanābha** enshrined at **Thiruvananthapuram / Śrī Padmanābhaswāmy** (a Divya Deśam; see [108 Divya Deśams](../06-sacred-geography/108-divya-desams.md), [Viṣṇu forms](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md)). **None of that is this figure.** Here Padmanābha (also called **Padma**) is a **righteous serpent-king (nāga)** of the Naimiṣa forest — a member of the [nāga race](../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md), one of the serpents who take monthly turns bearing the Sun's chariot. **This is the flagged false-positive set** — repo searches for "Padmanābha" return only Viṣṇu and the Kerala mūrti, never this Nāga. [scholarly]
- **This uñchavṛtti is not the mongoose's** — the same **uñcha / śila** gleaning-vow drives the *Mahābhārata*'s other great gift-parables: the **half-golden mongoose** and the Kurukṣetra gleaner-family who feed Dharma-in-disguise ([Āśvamedhika Parva; see the dāna diptych](dana-upakhyanas-shveta-and-the-mongoose.md)), and the sage **Mudgala** who lives by gleaning and declines heaven itself ([minor upākhyānas](minor-upakhyanas-catalog.md)). Those are the corpus's other uñchavṛtti hits; **this Mokṣadharma tale is a distinct, separately-narrated episode** with its own actors, thematically kin but not to be merged. [scholarly]
- **Dharmāraṇya the brāhmaṇa ≠ Dharmāraṇya the place** — the seeker's name means "forest of dharma"; it is also a famous **tīrtha/kṣetra** (the Dharmāraṇya of Gujarat, and a sacred wood named in tīrtha-lists). The person of this tale is not the geography. [disputed: shared name]
- **The "angry / discontented brāhmaṇa"** — some framings label Dharmāraṇya the *resentful* or *doubt-gnawed* brāhmaṇa. In the received Ganguli text he is presented as **righteous and self-restrained but troubled by a single doubt** — *which* dharma most surely wins liberation — rather than as literally wrathful; the discontent is intellectual, not a fit of anger. [disputed: characterization]

## Where and when it sits

- **The narration frame (Dvāpara–Kali cusp)** — Bhīṣma, lying on the [arrow-bed after the Kurukṣetra war](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md), instructs Yudhiṣṭhira; the upākhyāna comes near the **very end of the Mokṣadharma**, the long liberation-doctrine that closes the Śānti Parva (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva, Mokṣadharma). [scripture]
- **The tale's own time — the deep, undated past** — the encounter of Dharmāraṇya and Padmanābha floats in an unspecified antiquity of the current Mahāyuga; it is a **moral charter, not datable history**, and carries no yuga-anchor of its own. [scripture]
- **The setting — the Naimiṣa forest on the Gomatī** — Padmanābha dwells in a **city named after the Nāgas** "in that forest which is known by the name of **Naimiṣa**, and which is situate on the banks of the **Gomatī**" (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva ~CCCLV) — the same holy Naimiṣāraṇya of the Purāṇic recitations (see [Naimiṣāraṇya / Cakra-tīrtha](../06-sacred-geography/naimisharanya-chakra-tirtha.md)). [scripture]
- **Two-clock note (the text's age)** — on the **academic clock** the Mokṣadharma is one of the **latest, most heavily didactic strata** of the epic — an accretion of Sāṃkhya-Yoga and mokṣa material — **[scholarly] c. 200 BCE – 400 CE**, well after the war-narrative core; on the **scriptural clock** it is Vyāsa's single composition delivered through Bhīṣma at the Dvāpara–Kali turn. Never collapse the two. [late text]
- **Chapter numbering varies by recension** — the **Ganguli / Calcutta** line runs this arc as **Śānti Parva §§ CCCLV–CCCLXV (355–365)**; the **Critical Edition / Poona** gives it as **12.340–353**; the project's Śānti-Parva map cites the core at **XII.353–354** and the surrounding gleaner-praise at **XII.353–360**. The offsets are **edition artefacts, not different stories**. [disputed: chapter numbers vary by edition]

## The tale — Dharmāraṇya seeks the Nāga (Śānti Parva CCCLV–CCCLXI)

- **Padmanābha the virtuous Nāga** — in the serpent-city on the Gomatī lives **Padmanābha (Padma)**, a nāga famed less for venom than for **virtue**: he "gratifies all creatures in thought, word, and deed," protects the righteous and chastises the wicked, is truthful, free of malice, deeply learned in scripture, and — decisively for the tale — **fond of guests (atithi-priya)** (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva ~CCCLV). [scripture]
- **The Nāga's cosmic office — bearer of the Sun's car** — Padmanābha holds an extraordinary duty: **by monthly rotation he goes to drag the one-wheeled chariot (eka-cakra ratha) of Vivasvat (Sūrya)** across the sky, taking his turn among the beings who bear the Sun (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva ~CCCLXII). This office is what makes him an **eye-witness of the Sun's interior wonders**, and so the right teacher for a seeker's deepest question. [scripture]
- **The guest, the counsel on the road** — Dharmāraṇya, resolved on his purpose, journeys toward the Nāga's city; on the way a **host receives him for the night and instructs him on the duties of renunciation (sannyāsa)** — counsel that lifts a weight "like a heavy load off the shoulders" before he goes on (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva ~CCCLVI). [scripture]
- **Arriving to an absent host** — reaching the serpent-city, Dharmāraṇya finds **Padmanābha away on his turn at the Sun's car**; the **Nāga's wife** receives the brāhmaṇa, honours the guest, and, when her husband delays, is at length **persuaded to send for him** so the visitor's purpose can be met (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva ~CCCLVII–CCCLX). [the atithi-dharma frame: a guest must not be turned away] [scripture]
- **The question deferred** — when Padmanābha returns and the two converse, Dharmāraṇya frames his quest in Sāṃkhya-Yoga terms: "**relying on the Jīva-soul, I desire to attain to the Supreme Soul which is the end of the Jīva-soul**," professing himself "**neither attached to, nor dissociated from, the world**" — and, courteously, asks the Nāga to answer *his* question first before he discloses the object that has brought him (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva ~CCCLXI). His underlying doubt: **which dharma most surely wins liberation?** [scripture]

## The wonder of Sūrya, and the gleaner in the solar disc (CCCLXII–CCCLXIII)

- **What the Nāga saw on the Sun's car** — from his turns dragging Vivasvat's chariot Padmanābha recounts the **wonder of Sūrya**: that "**all the creatures that inhabit the three worlds have flowed from Sūrya**," that countless ascetics and deities **dwell within his rays**, that the Wind issues from him and by dividing it he makes the rains and turns the seasons, and that **the Supreme Soul, bathed in blazing effulgence, looks upon the universe from within the solar disc** (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva ~CCCLXII). See [Sūrya and his family](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/surya-and-his-family.md). [scripture]
- **The two effulgences that merged** — the Nāga's most astonishing sight: a **radiant Being of equal splendour approached Sūrya**, extended a hand in greeting, and the two energies **fused so wholly that "we could not any longer distinguish which was which"** — a vision of the identity of the individual and Supreme light (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva ~CCCLXII). [scripture]
- **Sūrya's own tale — the brāhmaṇa who reached the solar disc** — within the Nāga's account, **Sūrya himself** tells of a learned brāhmaṇa who, **devoted to the good of all creatures**, lived by the **Uñcha vow** — gleaning the grain left fallen and scattered after harvest — eking existence also on **fruits, roots, fallen leaves, water, and air alone**, ever reciting sacred texts and devoted to Mahādeva; by this austerity of *want*, not of wealth, **he won the end that crowns the perfected and to this day dwells, circling the Earth, within the disc of Sūrya** — a station **above** that of deities and celestials (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva ~CCCLXIII). [the tale's core exemplum] [scripture]
- **Why gleaning, of all vows** — the point is exact: the brāhmaṇa acquires **nothing, stores nothing, kills nothing, begs nothing** — he takes only grain already abandoned. Uñchavṛtti is thus the **least acquisitive, least injurious (ahiṃsā-tinged) livelihood conceivable**, and its very poverty of means is what makes it the **richest in merit** (cf. [ahiṃsā & Hindu ethics](../05-concepts-and-dharma/ahimsa-and-hindu-ethics.md)). [scripture]

## The conversion and the chain of narrators (CCCLXV)

- **The doubt dispelled** — Dharmāraṇya's intellectual torment resolves in a single line: "**That doubt has been dispelled by your discourse, for I have learnt the value of the Uñcha vow**" — the gleaner's life is answered to him as the surest dharma of all (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva ~CCCLXV). [scripture]
- **Formal initiation by Cyavana** — persuaded but seeking proper form, Dharmāraṇya **betakes himself to Cyavana of Bhṛgu's line** "from desire of being formally instructed and initiated in that vow"; the Bhārgava sage **performs the saṃskāra rite** and inducts him into the practice (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva ~CCCLXV). See [Cyavana](cyavana-sukanya.md); the initiation is a varṇāśrama-saṃskāra ([varṇa, āśrama & saṃskāras](../05-concepts-and-dharma/varna-ashrama-and-samskaras.md)). [scripture]
- **The gleaner departs** — "**firmly resolved**," the brāhmaṇa, instructed by the chief of Nāgas about his duty, **takes up the practice of Yama and Niyama**, subsists only on the food the Uñcha vow allows, and **withdraws into another forest** — a life, not a lecture, is the tale's end (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva ~CCCLXV). [scripture]
- **The nested chain of narrators** — the *Mahābhārata* wraps the episode in an unusually deep frame of transmission: **Cyavana → King Janaka → the ṛṣi Nārada → Indra → the assembled brāhmaṇas → Bhīṣma → Yudhiṣṭhira** — six removes, staging the tale as an authority handed down from creation-witnesses to the dying grandsire (*Mahābhārata*, Śānti Parva ~CCCLXV). [the frame's own point: this is settled, transmitted doctrine, not one man's opinion] [scholarly]

## What the upākhyāna is *for*

- **A liberation-charter, not a charity-charter** — unlike the mongoose tale (which exalts the *gift* of the gleaner's grain to a guest), this Mokṣadharma tale exalts the **gleaner's life itself as a path to mokṣa** — the non-acquisitive, harm-minimal existence as *the* dharma that carries a soul into the solar disc. Its telos is **release**, not almsgiving. [scholarly]
- **Nāga as guru, Sūrya as scripture** — the story quietly inverts hierarchy: a **serpent** teaches a **brāhmaṇa**, and the authority cited is not Veda-recitation but **direct sight of the Sun's interior** won by menial cosmic labour (dragging the car). Wisdom is located in **service and witness**, not in birth or rank — a recurring Mokṣadharma move. [scholarly]
- **The uñchavṛtti doctrine as an epic constant** — read against the mongoose, Mudgala, and the Anuśāsana dāna-teaching, the tale confirms **uñchavṛtti as a settled *Mahābhārata* ideal**: the minimal, gleaning, non-hoarding life is repeatedly named the epic's quiet summit of dharma, above sacrifice and above wealth. [scholarly]
- **Two-clock closing** — the **narration** is fixed to the epic's own chronology (Bhīṣma's post-war instruction, the Dvāpara–Kali junction); the **tale narrated** is timeless deep-past moral charter; the **text** is a late didactic stratum on the academic clock. All three frames are kept distinct, per the corpus rule. [scholarly]
