# The Kṣupa–Dadhīca dispute — brahma vs kṣatra and the charter of Sthāṇu-tīrtha

> Cosmic address: a primordial *Vedic / Vaivasvata-Manvantara* quarrel of the present **[7th — Vaivasvata — Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)**, set in the deep antiquity before the Vṛtra-war — the sage **Dadhīca** (Dadhīci), hardened by Śiva, out-endures King **Kṣupa's** *vajra* and **Viṣṇu's** *cakra*, then curses the gods to be burned at **Dakṣa's yajña**. It is the **Liṅga Purāṇa's** flagship Śaiva-supremacy proof-story and the **charter-myth of Sthāṇu-tīrtha** (Sthāneśvara, Thanesar) at Kurukṣetra.
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · See also: [Dadhīci — the sage who gave his bones](dadhichi.md) · [Sati, the Dakṣa-yajña & Pārvatī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md) · [The gurus Bṛhaspati & Śukra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md) · [Kurukṣetra — Dharmakṣetra](../06-sacred-geography/kurukshetra-dharmakshetra.md) · [Liṅga Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/linga-purana.md)

The **Kṣupa–Dadhīca dispute** (*Liṅga Purāṇa*, Pūrva-bhāga, chs. ~35–36) is the tradition's most explicit **Śaiva rewriting of the Dadhīci legend**: the same sage whose spine will one day be hammered into Indra's *vajra* here first proves *why* his bones are adamantine — because **Śiva** has made them so. A friendship between a Prajāpati-king and a Bhārgava ṛṣi curdles into the oldest argument in the *varṇa* order — **brahma** (priestly power) versus **kṣatra** (royal power). It escalates from words to a fist-blow to the hurling of Indra's thunderbolt; when Śiva's boon renders the *vajra* useless, **Viṣṇu Himself** enters the field on the king's side and is defeated. The episode ends with Dadhīca's **prophetic curse** — that the gods, and Viṣṇu with them, will one day be scattered and burned at the sacrifice of **Dakṣa** — and with the ground of the contest consecrated forever as **Sthāneśvara / Sthāṇu-tīrtha**. It is a sectarian polemic in narrative form: brahma over kṣatra, and Śiva over Viṣṇu.

---

## Placement on the two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic frame** - The quarrel sits in the deep-antiquity, "timeless" register of the present [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md) — the same Vedic stratum in which Dadhīci teaches the Madhu-vidyā and enables the Vṛtra-slaying. The Liṅga Purāṇa makes it *causally prior* to two other primordial events: the forging of the **[vajra from Dadhīci's spine](dadhichi.md)** (his backbone is hardened *here*, then donated *later*), and the destruction of **[Dakṣa's yajña](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)** (which his curse foretells). It thus belongs to the recurring deva-vs-sage / Śaiva-supremacy cycle rather than to a dated dynastic reign.
- **Academic / historical frame** - The narrative is a product of the **Liṅga Purāṇa's** Śaiva redaction, `[scholarly]` commonly placed **c. 5th–10th century CE**. As a story it is a **sectarian charter** — a Śaiva appropriation of the pan-Hindu Dadhīci material to assert Śiva's primacy over Viṣṇu and the *brāhmaṇa's* primacy over the *kṣatriya*, aimed at glorifying the Sthāneśvara *kṣetra*. `[scholarly]` The Purāṇa's own frame credits it to **Sūta** reciting at Naimiṣāraṇya.

## The disputants — who they are

- **Kṣupa (Kṣuva)** - A **king and Prajāpati**, named in the Liṅga Purāṇa as a **son of Brahmā**; he is *cakravartin*-figure who champions the sovereignty of the **kṣatra**. `[disputed]` His name appears in two spellings across the manuscripts and retellings — **Kṣupa** (the Liṅga Purāṇa reading) and **Kṣuva** (the form cited in the Skanda-cycle folk doublet). This node treats them as one figure; do not confuse this **Kṣupa** with the several minor **Kṣupa/Kṣupā** kings of the dynastic lists — the greps return no separate node, and the corpus's only prior mention is the "Kṣuva" doublet flagged in [Dadhīci](dadhichi.md).
- **Dadhīca / Dadhīci** - The Bhārgava ascetic. The Liṅga Purāṇa gives his father as **Cyavana** (the Bhṛgu-line reading), against the more common Ṛgvedic patronymic **Ātharvaṇa** ("son of Atharvan"). This is the *variant parentage* catalogued in the main [Dadhīci](dadhichi.md) node — see [Cyavana & Sukanyā](cyavana-sukanya.md) for the Bhārgava family. Here he is above all the **supreme devotee of Śiva** whose body has been made indestructible by a thousand years of *tapas*.
  - *Variant (parentage):* the Ṛgvedic and *Bhāgavata* tradition makes him son of **Atharvan** and **Citti/Śānti**; the Liṅga Purāṇa's **Cyavana**-parentage conflates the Atharvan and Bhārgava priestly houses. `[scholarly]` `[disputed]`

## The dispute — brahma vs kṣatra

- **The argument** - Kṣupa and Dadhīca are friends who fall into debate over the perennial question of *varṇa*-precedence: is the **kṣatriya** (the king, wielder of *daṇḍa* and lord of the eight directional guardians) supreme, or the **brāhmaṇa** (master of *tapas*, sacrifice, and the Veda)? Kṣupa presses the royal case — the king protects and governs all, therefore he is lord even of the priest; Dadhīca upholds the **primacy of brahma**, the *tapas*-power on which sovereignty itself depends. *(Liṅga Purāṇa, Pūrva-bhāga)*
- **From words to blows** - The debate degenerates. Dadhīca, in anger, strikes the king on the head **with his (left) fist**. Kṣupa, enraged at the insult from a subject, seizes the **vajra** — Indra's thunderbolt, in his possession as king / by Indra's grace — and hurls it at the sage. The bolt strikes Dadhīca full on the chest (or back). *(Liṅga Purāṇa, Pūrva-bhāga)*
- **The vajra fails** - Such is the power of a boon Dadhīca had earlier won from Śiva that **the vajra does him no harm at all** — it rebounds harmless, the thunderbolt of the king of gods defeated by a sage's body. This is the hinge of the whole story: the *kṣatra's* ultimate weapon is nullified by *brahma* armored in Śiva-bhakti. *(Liṅga Purāṇa, Pūrva-bhāga)*
  - *Variant (Skanda-cycle folk doublet):* a separate strand has Dadhīca **repeatedly killed and revived** across the quarrel, stressing his oft-restored, invulnerable body — the same motif as the horse-head in the Vedic layer. `[folk]` `[late text]` — see [Dadhīci](dadhichi.md) §"Conflict with Kṣuva."

## Śiva's boon and Śukra's Sañjīvanī — how the sage is made adamantine

- **The near-death and the revival** - In the fuller telling, an *earlier* blow of the vajra had **nearly killed** Dadhīca; he is restored by **Śukrācārya (Uśanas Kāvya)**, guru of the asuras, who enters the sage's body and revives him with the **Mṛta-sañjīvanī vidyā** — the death-defeating science that Śukra himself had won *from Śiva* through his own long penance. The Śaiva chain is deliberate: even the resurrection-power flows from Śiva. *(Liṅga Purāṇa, Pūrva-bhāga)* — see [The gurus Bṛhaspati & Śukra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md) §"Śukra, Śiva, and the origin of the Sañjīvanī."
- **The boon of the adamantine bones** - Dadhīca then performs relentless *tapas* to Śiva, who grants him boons of **fearlessness, indestructibility, and freedom from death except by his own will** — and makes his very **backbone harder, many times over, than the vajra** that had struck him. `[scripture]` This is the Liṅga Purāṇa's **etiology for the vajra-of-Vṛtra**: the spine is made diamond *here*, so that when — later — Dadhīci donates it, [Viśvakarman can forge from it the weapon that slays Vṛtra](dadhichi.md). The two legends are one continuous Śaiva arc.
  - *Note (why the bones can become a weapon):* the *Bhāgavata* explains the same "thunder in the bones" differently — the gods deposit their weapons with Dadhīci, who dissolves and drinks them. The Liṅga Purāṇa replaces that with **Śiva's direct boon**, subordinating the whole Dadhīci-vajra tradition to Śaiva agency. `[scholarly]`

## Viṣṇu enters — and the discus is defeated

- **Kṣupa appeals to Viṣṇu** - Unable to overcome the sage, Kṣupa turns to **Viṣṇu** in prayer and wins his aid — the point at which the story becomes an explicit **Viṣṇu-vs-Śiva** contest by proxy, the king backed by the Preserver against the sage backed by the Destroyer. *(Liṅga Purāṇa, Pūrva-bhāga)*
- **Viṣṇu in brāhmaṇa disguise** - Viṣṇu approaches Dadhīca **disguised as a brāhmaṇa** (a mendicant/ascetic), testing or seeking to trap him; Dadhīca, unafraid, offers a boon in return, and — in the sharpest irony of the tale — the disguised Viṣṇu is unmasked and outmatched. *(Liṅga Purāṇa, Pūrva-bhāga)*
- **The Sudarśana and the astras fail** - Viṣṇu deploys his full armory — the **Sudarśana-cakra** and his divine *astras* — against the sage. Every weapon is **turned back or rendered powerless**. `[scripture]` Where the [Ambarīṣa episode](ambarisha-durvasa.md) shows the Sudarśana as irresistible in defense of a Vaiṣṇava devotee, the Śaiva Liṅga Purāṇa deliberately **inverts** that: the same discus is nullified before a Śaiva devotee.
- **The blade of kuśa-grass** - Dadhīca meets Viṣṇu's weapons with nothing but **tips of kuśa-grass**, empowered by Śiva, which neutralize the divine *astras* — the starkest possible image of *tapas* over *śastra*, and of Śiva's grace making a straw stronger than the discus. *(Liṅga Purāṇa, Pūrva-bhāga)*
- **Viṣṇu's māyā, and Śiva seen in all** - Viṣṇu unleashes his **māyā**, multiplying himself into countless forms to bewilder the sage. Dadhīca, unshaken, perceives **Parama-Śiva pervading every form and every being** — the Śaiva *darśana* itself becomes his defense. The **Devas flee**, and Viṣṇu is left **motionless / stupefied**, defeated. `[scripture]`

## The curse and the consecration of Sthāṇu-tīrtha

- **Dadhīca curses the gods** - Victorious, Dadhīca **curses the Devas** for having taken the king's side against a brāhmaṇa and a devotee of Śiva. His curse is **prophetic**: he foretells that at the future **[yajña of Dakṣa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md)**, **Rudra** will teach all the gods — **Viṣṇu included** — a lesson, scattering and burning the sacrifice. `[scripture]` The Liṅga Purāṇa thus **weaves this dispute into the Dakṣa-yajña cycle** as its predictive charter: the humiliation of the gods at the sacrifice is here framed as the fulfillment of a Śaiva sage's word.
- **The place becomes Sthāneśvara** - The ground on which Dadhīca stood firm — where Śiva stood by him as the immovable **Sthāṇu** ("the pillar/post," Śiva as the fixed, unshakable one) — is consecrated as **Sthāneśvara** (Sthāṇu-īśvara), the **Sthāṇu-tīrtha** at **[Kurukṣetra](../06-sacred-geography/kurukshetra-dharmakshetra.md)**, modern **Thanesar** (Haryana). Those who attain this *kṣetra* are promised **Śiva-sāyujya** (union/absorption into Śiva). `[scripture]` The whole narrative is, structurally, a **sthala-purāṇa** — a tīrtha-glorification with a supremacy-myth attached. — cf. the trimūrti name **Sthāṇumālayan** at [Suchīndram](../06-sacred-geography/suchindram-sthanumalayan.md).

## Reading the polemic

- **Brahma over kṣatra** - The surface argument is settled by force of *tapas*: the king's *vajra* cannot touch the sage. The tale sits in the long Purāṇic literature of **brāhmaṇa-supremacy** contests (cf. Vasiṣṭha vs Viśvāmitra; the [Ambarīṣa–Durvāsā](ambarisha-durvasa.md) inversion where a king's *bhakti* humbles a sage). Here brahma wins **decisively and without qualification** — the Liṅga Purāṇa's priestly register. `[scholarly]`
- **Śiva over Viṣṇu** - The deeper polemic is sectarian. By making **Viṣṇu personally fight and lose** — his discus turned back, his *māyā* dissolved by the vision of Śiva-in-all — the text asserts **Śaiva primacy** over the Vaiṣṇava claim, and pointedly **inverts the Sudarśana's invincibility** celebrated in Vaiṣṇava sources. `[scholarly]` `[disputed]` The corresponding Vaiṣṇava texts never narrate this defeat; it is a Śaiva-Purāṇa episode.
- **Unifying the Dadhīci corpus** - Most elegantly, the story **retrofits the Vṛtra-vajra legend under Śiva**: Dadhīci's bones are adamantine not because he swallowed the gods' weapons (*Bhāgavata*) but because **Śiva hardened his spine** here — so the pan-Hindu tale of the sage who gives his bones becomes, in Śaiva hands, a downstream consequence of Śiva's boon. `[scholarly]` See [Dadhīci](dadhichi.md) for the vajra-donation proper.
- **Parallels** - The Liṅga Purāṇa version is echoed with variations in **Skanda-cycle** material (the "Kṣuva" doublet, with repeated deaths and revivals) and touched in the **Śiva Purāṇa's** broader Śaiva-supremacy corpus; these are variants of the same kernel, not independent tales. `[folk]` `[late text]`
