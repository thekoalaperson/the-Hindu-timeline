# Govardhana-dhāraṇa (the Lifting of Mount Govardhana)

> *Where this sits: the great Vṛndāvana līlā of [Kṛṣṇa](./krishna-lila.md) in which the boy-god lifts **Mount Govardhana** to shelter Vraja from Indra's deluge, set in the closing [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga — the charter myth of Govardhana Pūjā and the Annakūṭa.*
>
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Up to: [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md) · Companions: [Harivaṃśa](./harivamsha.md) · [Bhāgavata Tales Catalog](./bhagavata-tales-catalog.md)

The **Govardhana-dhāraṇa** ("the holding-up of Govardhana"; also **Govardhana-uddhāra**, "the raising of Govardhana") is the episode in which Kṛṣṇa, still a cowherd boy, persuades the people of Vraja to abandon their annual sacrifice to **Indra** in favour of worship of the hill **Govardhana**, the cows and the brāhmaṇas; when the slighted king of the gods retaliates with a world-ending storm, Kṛṣṇa **lifts the whole mountain on the little finger of his left hand** and holds it aloft as an umbrella for **seven days**, sheltering the cowherds and cattle until Indra submits. It is told most fully in the **[Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md) 10.24–27**, with the older **[Harivaṃśa](./harivamsha.md)** (Viṣṇu Parva) and **Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Book 5** as parallels. In the repo's [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md) biography it is a single bullet; this file expands it. For the wider tale-corpus see the [Bhāgavata Tales Catalog](./bhagavata-tales-catalog.md).

## The argument against Indra (Bhāgavata 10.24)

- **The interrupted sacrifice** — Kṛṣṇa finds **Nanda** and the elders of Vraja preparing the customary **Indra-yajña**, the yearly offering to the rain-god, lord of the clouds. He questions his father about its purpose, opening the debate that frames the whole episode (Bhāgavata X.24; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5; Harivaṃśa).
- **Kṛṣṇa's karma-doctrine** — Kṛṣṇa argues that beings reap the fruit of their own *karma* and *svabhāva* (nature); Indra cannot override destiny, and the cowherds, being a **forest-and-cattle people**, owe their living not to the distant sky-king but to their true sustainers — **Govardhana hill** (whose grass, water and caves feed the cows) and the cows and brāhmaṇas themselves. He urges the worship be redirected to these. [in the Bhāgavata this is voiced as a pointed critique of mechanical Vedic sacrifice in favour of svadharma and bhakti] [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa):* the older account makes the contrast sharper and more "pastoral-political" — Kṛṣṇa champions the *gopa* (cowherd) way of life and the cattle-and-mountain economy of Vraja against the claims of the Vedic Indra-cult; the elaborated karma-sermon is the Bhāgavata's later devotional overlay. [scholarly]
- **The new worship (the first Annakūṭa)** — Persuaded, the Vrajavāsīs divert the offerings to **Govardhana**: they circumambulate the hill (*parikramā*), bow to it, and heap before it a great mound of cooked food, milk-rice and sweets — the **annakūṭa** ("mountain of food"). Kṛṣṇa, to confirm the rite, **manifests a colossal form upon the summit** (the hill-deity, *Giri-rāja*) and with that mouth **eats the whole offering**, declaring "I am the hill" (*śailo 'smi*) — even as he stands among the cowherds in his own body (Bhāgavata X.24). This double presence — worshipper and worshipped at once — is the theological heart of the rite.

## Indra's deluge (Bhāgavata 10.25)

- **Indra's wrath** — Robbed of his sacrifice and his honour, Indra rages at the insolence of cowherds led by "an arrogant boy," and commands the **Sāṃvartaka clouds** (the clouds of universal dissolution) to destroy Vraja with a flood (Bhāgavata X.25; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5; Harivaṃśa).
- **The storm of dissolution** — The clouds loose a **pralaya-like deluge** — torrents thick as pillars, lightning, hail and gale — meaning to drown the land and its herds; the terrified cowherds, cattle and women run to Kṛṣṇa for refuge (Bhāgavata X.25).
- **The lifting of the hill** — Kṛṣṇa **uproots Mount Govardhana** and **holds it up on the little finger of his left hand** like a child holding a mushroom, raising it as a vast umbrella over the whole community (Bhāgavata X.25; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5; Harivaṃśa). The cowherds, cattle and gopīs, with their wagons and goods, shelter beneath it.
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata X.25):* the cowherds steady the hill with their **staves and goads** propped upward — in faith *helping* to hold it — a tender touch the tradition loves; their effort is of course needless before the Lord. [the standard iconographic detail]
  - *Variant (duration):* the canonical span is **seven days** (*sapta-rātram*, "seven nights") across the Bhāgavata, Viṣṇu Purāṇa and Harivaṃśa; throughout, Kṛṣṇa neither eats, sleeps nor lowers his arm, and yoga-māyā keeps those beneath untouched by hunger or thirst. [scholarly]
- **The breaking of Indra's pride** — After seven days the Sāṃvartaka clouds are spent and powerless; their fury exhausted against the unmoved hill, Indra's storm fails utterly. Kṛṣṇa sets Govardhana back **exactly in its place** and the people, unharmed, return to their homes (Bhāgavata X.25; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).

## Indra's submission and the Govinda-abhiṣeka (Bhāgavata 10.26–27)

- **The cowherds' wonder** — Vraja marvels at the boy who held a mountain for a week; Nanda recalls the prophecies of **Garga** about the child's divinity, and the elders begin to grasp who Kṛṣṇa is (Bhāgavata X.26).
- **Indra's surrender** — Humbled and ashamed, **Indra** descends, approaches Kṛṣṇa in secret, falls at his feet, praises him as the Supreme Lord (the **Indra-stuti** / *Govinda-praśasti*), and begs forgiveness for his pride (Bhāgavata X.27; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5; Harivaṃśa).
- **The consecration as Govinda** — Indra, together with the divine cow **Surabhi** (Kāmadhenu), **anoints Kṛṣṇa** with the milk of heaven's cows and the Gaṅgā water of Indra's elephant **Airāvata**, and confers on him the title **Govinda** ("finder/lord of cows") — hence the festival-name *Govinda-abhiṣeka* and the epithet **Govardhana-dhārī**, "bearer of Govardhana" (Bhāgavata X.27; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5; Harivaṃśa).
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa):* the consecration scene is older and is where the name **Govinda** and the *Upendra* relation to Indra are fixed; the Bhāgavata expands the hymn into a full bhakti-stotra. [scholarly]

## Festival & afterlife — Govardhana Pūjā and Annakūṭa

- **Govardhana Pūjā / Annakūṭa** — The līlā is the charter for the festival kept on **Kārttika Śukla Pratipadā**, the day after Dīpāvalī (the first day of the bright Kārttika fortnight): devotees build a **hill of food** (*annakūṭa*, "mound of grain") before the deity, often modelling Govardhana from cow-dung, food or flowers, and offer **fifty-six dishes** (*chappan bhog*). It commemorates the redirection of worship from Indra to Govardhana, the cow and Kṛṣṇa. [regional]
- **Govardhana parikramā** — The **21-km circumambulation** of Govardhana hill near Mathurā remains a major Braj pilgrimage; the hill itself is venerated as **Giri-rāja**, a form of Kṛṣṇa, and its stones (*śilā*) — especially **Govardhana-śilās** marked like a hill — are worshipped as the deity, particularly in **Gauḍīya** and **Puṣṭimārga** practice. [regional]
- **Puṣṭimārga centrality** — In **Vallabhācārya's Puṣṭimārga** the self-manifest deity **Śrī Nāthjī** (Govardhana-nātha) of Nathdwara *is* the Govardhana-dhārī Kṛṣṇa, his arm raised as if lifting the hill; the Annakūṭa is among the sect's foremost utsavas. [regional/sectarian]
- **Iconography** — The standard image (**Giridhārī / Govardhana-dhārī**) shows Kṛṣṇa with his **left arm raised** balancing the hill on one fingertip, cowherds and cattle gathered below — among the most reproduced subjects of Braj and Rajasthani painting and temple relief. [scholarly]

## Two clocks

- **Scriptural time:** the līlā falls in Kṛṣṇa's boyhood in the closing **[Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** of the current 28th Mahāyuga (traditionally before the onset of Kali Yuga, **3102 BCE**), in the same Vṛndāvana phase as the [Kāliya-mardana](./kaliya-mardana.md) and rāsa episodes.
- **Text-critical time:** the **oldest connected form is the [Harivaṃśa](./harivamsha.md)** (core c. 1st–2nd c. BCE, growing into the early CE centuries), restated in the **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** (~5th c. CE), and most elaborated and devotionalised in the **Bhāgavata** (~6th–10th c. CE) — which alone develops the karma-sermon, the *śailo 'smi* self-revelation and the full Indra-stuti. The skeleton — the diverted sacrifice, the diluvian wrath, the seven-day uplifted hill, and Indra's abhiṣeka of Govinda — is constant across all three. [scholarly]

## Sources

**Primary texts**
- *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam), **Canto X.24–27** — the argument against the Indra-yajña and the first Annakūṭa with the *śailo 'smi* self-manifestation (24), Indra's Sāṃvartaka deluge and the seven-day lifting of Govardhana (25), the cowherds' wonder (26), and Indra's submission with the Surabhi-and-Airāvata abhiṣeka conferring the name Govinda (27).

- *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*, **Book 5** — parallel narrative (the redirected worship, the storm, the seven-day umbrella-hill, Indra's homage).
