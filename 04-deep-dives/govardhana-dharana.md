# Govardhana-dhāraṇa (the Lifting of Mount Govardhana)

> *Where this sits: the great Vṛndāvana līlā of [Kṛṣṇa](./krishna-lila.md) in which the boy-god lifts **Mount Govardhana** to shelter Vraja from Indra's deluge, set in the closing [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga — the charter myth of Govardhana Pūjā and the Annakūṭa.*
>
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Up to: [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md) · Companions: [Harivaṃśa](./harivamsha.md) · [Bhāgavata Tales Catalog](./bhagavata-tales-catalog.md)

The **Govardhana-dhāraṇa** ("the holding-up of Govardhana"; also **Govardhana-uddhāra**, "the raising of Govardhana") is the episode in which Kṛṣṇa, still a cowherd boy (seven years old in the Bhāgavata, *sapta-hāyana*, 10.26.3, 10.26.14), persuades the people of Vraja to abandon their customary sacrifice to **Indra** in favour of worship of the hill **Govardhana**, the cows and the brāhmaṇas; when the slighted king of the gods retaliates with a storm of the clouds of dissolution, Kṛṣṇa **uproots the mountain and holds it aloft on one hand** as an umbrella for **seven days**, sheltering the cowherds and cattle until Indra calls off the storm and submits. It is told most fully in the **[Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md) 10.24–27**, with the older **[Harivaṃśa](./harivamsha.md)** (Viṣṇu Parva; critical edition chs. 59–62 and 63.1) and **Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.10–12** as parallels, and it is already alluded to in the **Mahābhārata** (Sabhā Parva 2.38.9–10, crit. ed.). In the repo's [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md) biography it is a single bullet; this file expands it. For the wider tale-corpus see the [Bhāgavata Tales Catalog](./bhagavata-tales-catalog.md).

## The argument against Indra (Bhāgavata 10.24)

- **The interrupted sacrifice** — Kṛṣṇa, staying "in that very place" (*tatraiva*) with **Baladeva**, finds **Nanda** and the elders preparing the customary **Indra-yāga**, the offering to the lord of the rain-clouds. Though he already knows its purpose, he humbly asks his father what it is for and whether it is scriptural or mere custom; Nanda answers that Indra (Parjanya) sends the rain on which all life depends, and that whoever abandons this inherited rite will not prosper (Bhāgavata 10.24.1–11; the warning, 10.24.11, is the Bhāgavata's alone).
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.10.16–24; Harivaṃśa 59.1–18 crit.):* after the rains, Kṛṣṇa asks "out of curiosity" what this *śakra-maha* (Indra's festival) is (VP 5.10.17–18; HV 59.2–3); the answer is that Indra's clouds bring the crops and grass, so "kings and all people worship Indra in the rains" (VP 5.10.24; HV 59.18). In the Viṣṇu Purāṇa the answer is Nanda's (5.10.18); in the Harivaṃśa it is given by "the eldest of the cowherds" (*vṛddhatamas tv eko gopaḥ*, 59.4), and Nanda is not named.
- **Kṛṣṇa's karma-doctrine** — Kṛṣṇa argues that beings are born and perish by their own *karma*, and that even a lord who dispenses fruits depends on the doer's acts; Indra cannot alter what each one's nature (*svabhāva*) ordains, the clouds rain by the guṇa of *rajas*, so "what can the great Indra do?" Karma itself is guru and lord, and "that by which one lives is one's deity" (Bhāgavata 10.24.13–23). Since the cowherds' living is cow-keeping (*go-vṛtti*, 10.24.21) and they are forest-and-hill dwellers, not town or village folk (10.24.24), he urges that the sacrifice, with all the materials gathered for Indra, be offered instead to **the cows, the brāhmaṇas and the hill** (10.24.25–30).
  - The Bhāgavata frames the speech as deliberate provocation: Kṛṣṇa speaks "to rouse Indra's anger" (*indrāya manyuṃ janayan*, 10.24.12) and to destroy his pride (10.24.31); the Viṣṇu Purāṇa says the same (*roṣāya tridaśendrasya*, 5.10.25). Śrīdhara Svāmī, as relayed in the BBT purport to 10.24.37, counts six theses in the speech (karma alone decides, nature rules, the guṇas rule, the Lord depends on karma, and so on) and says Kṛṣṇa "presented these arguments not because He believed them" but to divert the sacrifice and provoke Indra; the BBT purport to 10.25.5 likewise calls them Karma-mīmāṃsā and Sāṅkhya arguments that he "did not accept." [commentarial]
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa 59.19–29 crit.; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.10.25–41):* the older, near-identical speech has no karma-sermon. It is an argument from *vārtā* (livelihood): farmers live by tillage, traders by commerce, "we by cows"; each should worship what sustains him; the land ends in forest and the forest in hills, "and those are our refuge"; the mountains are shape-shifters (*kāma-rūpin*) who, as lions and tigers, kill forest-folk who wrong them; brāhmaṇas offer mantra-sacrifice, ploughmen furrow-sacrifice, "we cowherds, mountain-sacrifice" (*giri-yajña*); "cows and mountains are our gods — what have we to do with Indra?" (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.10.36). In the Harivaṃśa Kṛṣṇa even says he will carry out the cow-sacrifice "by force if need be" (59.60). Reading the Bhāgavata's karma-sermon as a later devotional-philosophical overlay on this pastoral argument is a modern scholarly view. [scholarly]
- **The new worship (the first Annakūṭa)** — Persuaded, the Vrajavāsīs do all he says: they have the brāhmaṇas recite blessings, offer the gathered materials to the hill and the brāhmaṇas, give grass to the cows, and with the herds in front **circumambulate the hill** (*pradakṣiṇa*), the gopīs riding in ox-carts singing Kṛṣṇa's deeds (Bhāgavata 10.24.32–34). The Bhāgavata's menu is all cooked and dairy food — sweet rice, soups, cakes, all the milk of the station (10.24.26). The word *annakūṭa* ("heap of food") is the later festival name, not the text's; the closest textual image is the Harivaṃśa's "mountain of rice" (*odana-parvata*, 60.16 crit.).
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa 59.29, 60.12–18 crit.; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.10.38, 5.10.44):* the older accounts include **animal sacrifice** — Kṛṣṇa tells them to "kill sacrificial beasts in due form" (*medhyān paśūn hatvā*); buffaloes and other animals are slaughtered, with vessels and heaps of meat beside the curds, milk-rice and rice, and the offering is "curds, milk-rice, meat and the rest" (*dadhi-pāyasa-māṃsādyaiḥ*, VP 5.10.44). The Bhāgavata's rite is bloodless.
- **"I am the hill"** — To give the cowherds faith, Kṛṣṇa takes on **another, huge form** and, declaring "I am the mountain" (*śailo 'smi*), **eats the abundant offering**; then, with the people of Vraja, he bows to that form — "himself to himself" (*ātmanātmane*) — saying "See, the hill has appeared in person and blessed us!" and warning that the hill kills forest-dwellers who neglect it (Bhāgavata 10.24.35–37). This double presence — worshipper and worshipped at once — is the theological heart of the rite. The Bhāgavata does not call the form *Giri-rāja*; that name is the later devotional one (e.g. the *Girirāja-khaṇḍa* of the Garga Saṃhitā). [late text]
  - The same scene is in the older texts, located **on the summit**: in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa Kṛṣṇa, embodied on the hilltop and saying "I am the mountain" (*śailo 'ham iti mūrtimān*), eats the food, while in his own form he climbs the hill with the cowherds and worships "his second body" (5.10.47–48); in the Harivaṃśa Kṛṣṇa "by māyā became the hill" and ate the food, milk, curds and meat, laughed in his divine form "I am satisfied," was seen in mountain shape on the hilltop, bowed to himself with the gopas, and promised as the hill to be their god from now on (60.18–28 crit.). (Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa)

## Indra's deluge (Bhāgavata 10.25)

- **Indra's wrath** — Learning that his worship has been set aside, Indra, "imagining himself lord" (*īśa-mānī*), rages at the forest-cowherds who have offended the gods by trusting "a mortal" Kṛṣṇa — "a prattling, childish, stubborn, ignorant boy who thinks himself learned" (*vācālaṃ bāliśaṃ stabdham ajñaṃ paṇḍita-māninam*) — and sends the **Sāṃvartaka** clouds, the clouds that bring the end of the world, to destroy the cattle, promising to follow on **Airāvata** with the Maruts (Bhāgavata 10.25.1–7; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.11.1–5; Harivaṃśa 61.1–7 crit.). The insults are the Bhāgavata's; in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa Indra blames "the witless cowherd Nanda," who stopped the rite relying on Kṛṣṇa (5.11.3). In the Harivaṃśa Indra orders the cows to be tormented with rain and wind "for seven nights" (*saptarātreṇa*, 61.4 crit.).
- **The storm of dissolution** — The clouds, "released from their bonds" (*nirmukta-bandhanāḥ*; BBT adds "untimely"), blaze with lightning and roar with thunder, hurling hail; rain falls in torrents thick as pillars until high ground cannot be told from low; the shivering cows, covering their heads and calves with their bodies, and the cowherd men and women, numb with cold, run to Govinda for refuge, crying "Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa, save us from the angry god!" (Bhāgavata 10.25.8–13). In the Harivaṃśa the gopas cry that "the end of the worlds has come, the earth is one ocean" (61.20 crit.); in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa and Harivaṃśa some cows die, others shield their calves, and the calves seem to beg Kṛṣṇa "save us" (VP 5.11.10–12; HV 61.21–24 crit.).
- **The lifting of the hill** — Seeing that this is angry Indra's work and resolving to break the pride of gods who think themselves lords of the world, Kṛṣṇa **picks up Govardhana with one hand** (*ekena hastena*) and holds it up as easily "as a child holds a mushroom" (Bhāgavata 10.25.14–19; cf. 10.26.3, "with one hand, like an elephant holding a lotus," and 10.26.25). He invites "Mother, father, people of Vraja" beneath it, assuring them it will not fall; they enter with their cows, wagons, goods, servants and priests (10.25.20–22, 10.25.27). The Viṣṇu Purāṇa likewise says he uprooted it and held it as if with one hand, in sport (*utpāṭyaika-kareṇeva … līlayā*, 5.11.16; Wilson: "with one hand in sport"), and Indra later says plainly that he held it "with one hand" (*kareṇaikena*, 5.12.10).
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa 61.29–30, 61.53–58 crit.):* Kṛṣṇa **uproots the hill with both arms** (*dorbhyām utpāṭayāmāsa*) and then **holds it on his left hand** (*savyena pāṇinā*), the hill becoming "a house" (61.30), which he calls a "hill-house" (*giri-gṛha*) for the herd (61.53); later he "held the hill with one hand like a dear guest" (61.58). The BBT purport to Bhāgavata 10.25.19 quotes this Harivaṃśa verse for the left hand.
  - *Variant (later devotional tradition):* Kṛṣṇa balances the hill on the **little finger of his left hand**. This is the standard form in Braj and Gauḍīya retellings and in painting, but none of the Bhāgavata, Viṣṇu Purāṇa or Harivaṃśa (critical text and appendix) says "finger". An early Gauḍīya locus is Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī's *Govardhanāśraya-daśaka* 1 (in his *Stavāvalī*, 16th c.), where the hill shines for seven days on "the little finger of Mura's foe's lotus-hand" (*saptāhaṃ murajit-karāmbuja-paribhrājat-kaniṣṭhāṅguli*); that verse does not say "left," and no earlier locus was found. [late text] [regional/sectarian]
  - *Variant (later devotional and iconographic tradition):* the cowherds prop the hill up with their **staves** — in faith *helping* to hold it, an effort needless before the Lord. This is a favourite detail of Braj retellings and of painting, but it is **not in Bhāgavata 10.25** or in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa or Harivaṃśa. [folk] [locus not verified]
- **Seven days** — The span is the same in every early source: Kṛṣṇa holds the hill **seven days** (*saptāham*, Bhāgavata 10.25.23); the clouds rain on Nanda's Gokula **seven nights** (*saptarātram*, Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.11.22; Harivaṃśa 61.61–62 crit.); the Mahābhārata's Śiśupāla also says *saptāham* (Sabhā Parva 2.38.9 crit.). The Bhāgavata verse says Kṛṣṇa, putting aside hunger, thirst and any thought of comfort, held the hill without moving from his place while the people of Vraja gazed at him (10.25.23); in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa the gopas and gopīs gaze at him wide-eyed and sing his praise (5.11.20–21). That he also forgot sleep, and that the Vrajavāsīs felt no hunger or thirst as they drank in his beauty, is the BBT purport's own gloss on 10.25.23, not the verse; the same purport credits Viśvanātha Cakravartī only with the point that Kṛṣṇa dried up the rain as it fell, so Mathurā was not flooded. [commentarial]
- **The breaking of Indra's pride** — Astonished at this display of Kṛṣṇa's yoga-power, his pride broken and his resolve thwarted, **Indra calls off his clouds** (*svān meghān sannyavārayat*, Bhāgavata 10.25.24); in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa and Harivaṃśa he does so as one whose vow has proved false (*mithyā-pratijña*, VP 5.11.23; HV 61.60 crit.). When the sky clears and the sun rises, "the lifter of Govardhana" (*govardhana-dharaḥ*) tells the cowherds to come out; they leave with their cows and loaded wagons, and only then does Kṛṣṇa set the hill back **in its original place, as before**, while all creatures watch (Bhāgavata 10.25.25–28; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.11.24–25; Harivaṃśa 61.62–64 crit.). Vraja embraces and blesses him, Yaśodā, Rohiṇī, Nanda and Balarāma among them, and the gods shower flowers (Bhāgavata 10.25.29–32).
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva 2.38.9–10, crit. ed.):* a hostile retelling. At Yudhiṣṭhira's consecration **Śiśupāla** mocks Bhīṣma's praise of Kṛṣṇa: if he held up for seven days a hill that was "a mere anthill" (*valmīka-mātraḥ*), that is no wonder; and "he ate a lot of food, playing on the mountain-top." It is the oldest allusion to the episode, and it already knows both the seven days and the eating on the summit.

## Indra's submission and the Govinda-abhiṣeka (Bhāgavata 10.26–27)

- **The cowherds' wonder** — The cowherds ask Nanda how a seven-year-old could hold up the great hill with one hand, and they list his earlier wonders (Pūtanā, the cart, Tṛṇāvarta, the arjuna trees, Baka, Vatsa, Dhenuka, Pralamba, the forest fire, Kāliya). Nanda answers by repeating **Garga's** prophecy at the naming: the child has taken white, red and yellow colours in past ages, is "equal to Nārāyaṇa," and will carry them through every danger. Their doubt goes and they honour Nanda and Kṛṣṇa (Bhāgavata 10.26.1–24).
- **Indra's surrender** — **Surabhi**, mother of the cows, comes from her own world (*go-loka*) with **Indra**. Ashamed, Indra approaches Kṛṣṇa **in a solitary place** (*vivikte*), touches his feet with his sun-bright crown, and praises him as the father, guru and lord of the worlds, who takes bodies at will to humble those who think themselves masters. He confesses that he tried to destroy Vraja out of wounded pride, and begs forgiveness (the **Indra-stuti**, Bhāgavata 10.27.1–13). Kṛṣṇa answers that he stopped the sacrifice out of mercy, so that Indra, drunk with power, would remember him, and sends him back to his post without pride (10.27.14–17).
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.12.1–12; Harivaṃśa 62.1–38 crit.):* there is no Surabhi. Indra rides down on **Airāvata** and finds Kṛṣṇa tending the cattle (VP) or seated on a rock of Govardhana (HV), with **Garuḍa**, invisible, shading his head with his wings (VP 5.12.4; HV 62.7 crit.). Indra dismounts and speaks to him privately (*ekānte*, VP 5.12.5; *vivikte*, HV 62.8 crit.). In the Viṣṇu Purāṇa his speech is less a confession than a congratulation: the gods' purpose is served, and he has come "urged by the cows" (5.12.6–11). In the Harivaṃśa it is a long cosmological eulogy that places the "world of cows" (*goloka*) above Brahmaloka and asks pardon (*kṣantum arhasi*); there he comes at the word of the cows and of Brahmā (62.11–38 crit.).
- **The consecration as Govinda** — Surabhi hails Kṛṣṇa as the cows' supreme deity and, "as directed by Brahmā," asks him to be **their Indra** (Bhāgavata 10.27.18–21). She bathes him with **her own milk**, and Indra, urged by the mothers of the gods (*deva-mātṛbhiḥ*; "Aditi and the others" is the BBT gloss), bathes him with **heavenly Gaṅgā water drawn up in Airāvata's trunk**, with the gods and sages, and names him **Govinda** (10.27.22–23). Gandharvas sing, the cows' milk wets the earth, rivers run with sweet juices, and even cruel creatures give up enmity (10.27.24–27). Indra then returns to heaven, having anointed "Govinda, lord of the cows and of Gokula" (10.27.28). Hence the episode's name, *Govinda-abhiṣeka*, and the epithet **Govardhana-dhara / -dhārī**, "bearer of Govardhana" (*govardhana-dharaḥ*, Bhāgavata 10.25.25; Harivaṃśa 63.1 crit.).
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.12.12–24; Harivaṃśa 62.41–99 crit.):* the older consecration names Kṛṣṇa **Upendra** as well as **Govinda**. Indra, speaking for the cows, says "you shall be Indra of the cows, Govinda" and installs him in Upendra-hood. In the Viṣṇu Purāṇa he anoints him with holy water from the **bell** (*ghaṇṭā*) of Airāvata (5.12.13; Wilson translates "ewer"); in the Harivaṃśa he uses **golden pots of divine milk**, and the cows add their own milk (62.42, 62.58–59 crit.); in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa too the cows' milk drenches the earth during the rite (5.12.14). The Harivaṃśa adds that the gods in heaven will hail him as Upendra (62.44 crit.) and that Indra yields him the latter half of the rains, the autumn, when both **Mahendra and Upendra** are to be honoured on flag-poles (the Indra-dhvaja festival, 62.45–57 crit.). So in this version Indra's cult is shared, not abolished. Both texts then add a **pact over Arjuna**: Indra commends his son, born of Kuntī, to Kṛṣṇa's protection, and Kṛṣṇa promises that no one will conquer Arjuna in battle as long as he himself remains on earth (VP 5.12.19–20), that no foe of Arjuna will prevail in his presence (*mamāgre*, VP 5.12.23; HV 62.96 crit.), and that he will restore all the Pāṇḍavas unharmed to Kuntī after the Bhārata war (VP 5.12.16–24; HV 62.68–98 crit.; in the Harivaṃśa Indra first urges him to kill Kaṃsa, Keśin and Ariṣṭa, 62.69). The Bhāgavata does not use the name Upendra here (Wilson's note to VP 5.12 makes the same observation, as a translator's comment), and its chapter 27 has no Arjuna pact.

## Festival & afterlife — Govardhana Pūjā and Annakūṭa

- **Govardhana Pūjā / Annakūṭa** — The līlā is the charter for the festival kept on **Kārttika Śukla Pratipadā**, the day after Dīpāvalī (the first day of the bright Kārttika fortnight). Devotees build a **hill of food** (*annakūṭa*, "heap of food") before the deity, often model Govardhana in cow-dung, food or flowers, and offer **fifty-six dishes** (*chappan bhog*). It commemorates the redirection of worship from Indra to Govardhana, the cows and Kṛṣṇa. [regional]
- **Govardhana parikramā** — The **21-km circumambulation** of Govardhana hill near Mathurā remains a major Braj pilgrimage; the hill itself is venerated as **Giri-rāja**, a form of Kṛṣṇa, and its stones (*śilā*), the **Govardhana-śilās**, are worshipped as the deity, particularly in **Gauḍīya** and **Puṣṭimārga** practice. [regional]
- **The hill's own legend** — The Garga Saṃhitā makes Govardhana the son of the mountain **Droṇa**. The sage **Pulastya**, who lived in Kāśī, asked Droṇa for his son so as to set him there for his austerities (2.2.20–26); Govardhana agreed on condition that he would stay wherever he was first put down (2.2.30–32), and rode in the sage's hand to Vraja (2.2.34–35). Foreseeing that Kṛṣṇa would play there, he made himself heavy (2.2.36–40); the weary sage put him down in Vraja, and he refused to rise again (2.2.41–46). Pulastya cursed him in anger to shrink "by a sesame seed" (*tila-mātram*) every day, and went on to Kāśī; the hill has been shrinking ever since (Garga Saṃhitā, Vṛndāvana-khaṇḍa 2.2.20–49). The same text devotes its *Girirāja-khaṇḍa* (canto 3) to the hill: its worship and festival (chs. 1–2), Kṛṣṇa's coronation-bathing (ch. 4) and the hill's holy places, birth and glory (chs. 7–10). [late text] [regional]
- **Puṣṭimārga centrality** — In **Vallabhācārya's Puṣṭimārga** the self-manifest deity **Śrī Nāthjī** (Govardhana-nātha) of Nathdwara *is* the Govardhana-dhārī Kṛṣṇa, his arm raised as if lifting the hill; the Annakūṭa is among the sect's foremost utsavas. [regional/sectarian]
- **Iconography** — The standard image (**Giridhārī / Govardhana-dhārī**) shows Kṛṣṇa with his **left arm raised**, balancing the hill on his hand or fingertip, with cowherds and cattle gathered below. The left hand matches the Harivaṃśa (*savyena pāṇinā*, 61.30 crit.), the verse the BBT purport to Bhāgavata 10.25.19 cites for it; the fingertip follows later devotional tradition. It is among the most reproduced subjects of Braj and Rajasthani painting and of temple relief; Wilson already noted "a remarkable representation" of it on the sculptured rocks of Mahabalipuram (note to VP 5.11, a translator's comment). [scholarly]

## Two clocks

- **Scriptural time:** the līlā falls in Kṛṣṇa's boyhood (his seventh year, Bhāgavata 10.26.3) in the closing **[Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** of the current 28th Mahāyuga (traditionally before the onset of Kali Yuga, **3102 BCE**), in the same Vṛndāvana phase as the [Kāliya-mardana](./kaliya-mardana.md) (Bhāgavata 10.16) and rāsa (10.29–33) episodes.
- **Text-critical time:** the oldest allusion is the Mahābhārata's (Sabhā Parva 2.38.9–10). The **oldest connected narrative is the [Harivaṃśa](./harivamsha.md)** (dates contested; see that file, which puts its oldest strata in the 1st–2nd c. BCE). It is restated, often verse for verse, in the **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** (~5th c. CE), and most elaborated and devotionalised in the **Bhāgavata** (~6th–10th c. CE). The Bhāgavata alone has the karma-sermon, the vegetarian rite, Surabhi's part in the coronation (her milk, beside Indra's Gaṅgā water from Airāvata's trunk), and the full devotional Indra-stuti; the Harivaṃśa and Viṣṇu Purāṇa alone have the animal sacrifice, the name **Upendra**, and Indra's pact over Arjuna. The skeleton is constant across all three: the diverted sacrifice, the hill-form eating the offering, the Sāṃvartaka storm, the hill held up for seven days, and Indra's abhiṣeka of Govinda. [scholarly]

## Sources

**Primary texts**
- *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam), **10.24–27**. Ch. 24: the argument against the Indra-yāga (13–30), the hill-worship (32–34) and the *śailo 'smi* form (35–37). Ch. 25: Indra's Sāṃvartaka deluge (1–13), the lifting with one hand (19) and the seven days (23), the recall of the clouds (24) and the hill replaced (28). Ch. 26: the cowherds' wonder, Kṛṣṇa's age of seven (3, 14), and Garga's prophecy retold (15–23). Ch. 27: Surabhi and Indra, the Indra-stuti (4–13), Surabhi's milk and the Ākāśa-Gaṅgā abhiṣeka conferring the name Govinda (22–23).
- *Harivaṃśa* (Viṣṇu Parva), **critical edition chs. 59–62 and 63.1** (GRETIL numbering; vulgate numbering differs) — the **oldest connected** account: the eldest gopa's account of the *śakra-maha* (59.1–18), the *vārtā* argument and the *giri-yajña* (59.19–61), the sacrifice with meat and the hill-form eating it on the summit (60.10–35), the Sāṃvartaka storm, both arms and left hand, seven nights (61), and Indra's visit, the Govinda/Upendra consecration, the shared Indra-dhvaja festival and the Arjuna pact (62), and *govardhana-dharaḥ* (63.1).
- *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*, **5.10–12** — the parallel narrative: the vārtā argument and the sacrifice with victims (5.10.25–44), *śailo 'ham* on the summit (5.10.47–48), the storm and the one-handed lifting for seven nights (5.11), and Indra on Airāvata, the Upendra/Govinda abhiṣeka and the Arjuna pact (5.12).
- *Mahābhārata*, **Sabhā Parva 2.38.9–10** (critical edition) — Śiśupāla's sneer: the "anthill" held for seven days, and the food eaten on the hilltop.
- *Garga Saṃhitā*, **Vṛndāvana-khaṇḍa 2.2** (Govardhana son of Droṇa, Pulastya of Kāśī, the hill set down in Vraja and Pulastya's curse, 2.2.20–49) and **Girirāja-khaṇḍa** (canto 3). [late text]
