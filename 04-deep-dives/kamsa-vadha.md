# Kaṃsa-vadha and the Mathurā Arena

> Where this sits: the climactic turning-point of [Kṛṣṇa](./krishna-lila.md)'s career — the day he leaves the cowherd world of Vraja, enters the city of **Mathurā**, and slays the tyrant **Kaṃsa**, fulfilling the birth-prophecy that framed the whole avatāra. This is the hinge from *cowherd to prince*.
>
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Up to: [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md) · Related: [Harivaṃśa](./harivamsha.md) · [Bhāgavata-tales catalog](./bhagavata-tales-catalog.md)

This file is a dedicated deep-dive on the single arc that ends Krishna's Vraja childhood: **Akrūra's summons, the breaking of Kaṃsa's bow, the killing of the rogue elephant Kuvalayāpīḍa, the wrestling deaths of Cāṇūra and Muṣṭika, and the slaying of Kaṃsa himself**, with the restoration of his deposed father Ugrasena. For the wider life see [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md); for the oldest version of these same events see the [Harivaṃśa](./harivamsha.md). The principal source is the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa X.36–44**, with the **Harivaṃśa** (Viṣṇu Parva) and **Viṣṇu Purāṇa Book 5** as parallels.

---

## The Frame: Prophecy and Tyranny

- **The ākāśavāṇī** — At the wedding-chariot of Devakī (Kaṃsa's sister) and Vasudeva, a disembodied voice warns Kaṃsa that Devakī's **eighth child** will be his death; the tyrant imprisons the couple and slays their first six infants (Bhāgavata X.1; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.1). The Mathurā arena is the *payoff* of this opening.
- **Kaṃsa as usurper** — Kaṃsa has deposed and imprisoned his own father **Ugrasena**, the rightful Bhoja/Yādava king of Mathurā; his death is therefore also a *restoration of legitimate kingship* (Bhāgavata X.44; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).
- **Keśin and the warning of Nārada** — Forewarned (in the Bhāgavata, by Nārada) that the boy raised in Vraja is his nemesis, Kaṃsa sends a last wave of demons — the horse-demon **Keśin** and the bull-demon **Ariṣṭa** — all slain. He then resolves to lure Krishna into the city (Bhāgavata X.36; Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva).
- **The Dhanur-yajña pretext** — Kaṃsa proclaims a **"bow-sacrifice" (Dhanur-yajña)** and a wrestling festival as cover, planning to kill the brothers under guise of invitation (Bhāgavata X.36).

## Akrūra's Mission

- **Akrūra summoned** — Kaṃsa dispatches the Yādava noble **Akrūra** (a secret devotee of Krishna) to Vraja to bring Krishna and Balarāma to Mathurā for the festival (Bhāgavata X.36–38; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).
- **Akrūra's joy and dread** — Akrūra rejoices to fetch the Lord he loves yet fears being Kaṃsa's instrument; his inner monologue is a celebrated bhakti set-piece (Bhāgavata X.38).
- **The vision in the Yamunā** — Bathing on the way, Akrūra sees Krishna and Balarāma **within the river's waters** as Viṣṇu reclining on Śeṣa — a theophany confirming the boy's divinity (Bhāgavata X.39).
- **Departure from Vraja** — Krishna and Balarāma mount Akrūra's chariot; the **gopīs' grief** (viraha) at the parting is left unresolved on earth (Bhāgavata X.39). [This separation is the emotional counterweight to the martial arc that follows.]
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa):* the summons and journey are present but the developed gopī-viraha and Akrūra's Yamunā theophany are less elaborated than in the Bhāgavata — the earlier text is more martial than devotional. [scholarly]

## Entering Mathurā — The Roadside Episodes

Before the arena, a cluster of gate-and-street encounters that establish Krishna's grace and defiance:

- **The washerman (rajaka)** — Krishna asks Kaṃsa's arrogant **washerman/dyer** for festive garments; refused and insulted, Krishna strikes off his head and the brothers dress in the fine cloth (Bhāgavata X.41; Harivaṃśa). [The first blood drawn against Kaṃsa's household.]
- **Blessing of the weaver/tailor** — A garment-maker who then fits them respectfully is blessed with prosperity (Bhāgavata X.41).
- **Sudāmā the garland-maker (mālākāra)** — The flower-seller **Sudāmā** (distinct from the boyhood Brahmin friend Sudāmā/Kucela) joyfully garlands the brothers and is blessed with wealth and devotion (Bhāgavata X.41). [late text note: some retellings conflate the two Sudāmās — they are separate.]
- **Trivakrā / Kubjā the hunchback** — The deformed maidservant **Trivakrā** ("bent in three places"), bearing sandal-paste for Kaṃsa, anoints Krishna; he straightens her crooked frame with a press of his foot and a tug under her chin, making her beautiful; she invites him and he later visits her (Bhāgavata X.42; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata X.48):* Krishna's actual visit to the now-beautiful Kubjā's house is narrated *later*, after Kaṃsa's death, as a brief, tender episode — the "boon" promised at the first meeting. [textual ordering]

## Breaking the Bow

- **The great bow of the Dhanur-yajña** — In the sacrificial hall Krishna seizes Kaṃsa's enormous ceremonial **bow**, strings it, and **snaps it in two** with a thunderous report heard across the city — a deliberate, public act of defiance (Bhāgavata X.42; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).
- **Slaying the guards** — Kaṃsa's keepers rush the brothers; Krishna and Balarāma beat them down with the broken halves of the bow, then withdraw to roam the city before nightfall (Bhāgavata X.42).
- **Kaṃsa's sleepless night** — Terrified by omens and dreams, the king passes the night in dread, ordering the arena readied (Bhāgavata X.42).

## The Arena Gate — Kuvalayāpīḍa

- **The rogue elephant** — At the entrance to the wrestling-arena Kaṃsa stations his war-elephant **Kuvalayāpīḍa**, goaded by its keeper to trample the brothers as they enter (Bhāgavata X.43; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).
- **The killing** — Krishna provokes, dodges, drags, and finally fells the beast, wrenching out a **tusk** with which he kills both the elephant and its mahout; he enters the arena tusk in hand, blood-spattered, "like death itself" to Kaṃsa and "the supreme being" to the devout (Bhāgavata X.43). The Bhāgavata famously notes the spectators perceive Krishna **differently according to their rasa** — lover, hero, terror, kin, God.

## The Wrestling Match — Cāṇūra and Muṣṭika

- **Kaṃsa's champions** — The royal wrestlers **Cāṇūra**, **Muṣṭika**, and (in the fuller lists) **Kūṭa, Śala, and Tośala** are set against the youths in an unequal, treacherous bout (Bhāgavata X.44; Harivaṃśa).
- **The crowd's protest** — The citizens cry out that pitting hardened champions against boys is *adharma*; the contest itself stages the injustice of Kaṃsa's rule (Bhāgavata X.44).
- **Cāṇūra slain by Krishna** — After a long grappling, Krishna whirls Cāṇūra overhead and dashes him to the ground, killing him (Bhāgavata X.44; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).
- **Muṣṭika slain by Balarāma** — Balarāma crushes **Muṣṭika** with blows; the remaining wrestlers (Kūṭa, Śala, Tośala) are dispatched by the brothers, and the rest flee (Bhāgavata X.44).

## The Vadha of Kaṃsa

- **Krishna leaps the dais** — As Kaṃsa orders the brothers, Nanda, Vasudeva, and Ugrasena seized and executed, Krishna **bounds onto the royal platform**, seizes the tyrant by the **hair**, knocks off his crown, and drags him down into the arena (Bhāgavata X.44; Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).
- **The killing** — Krishna falls upon him and slays him; the Bhāgavata stresses that Kaṃsa, by his constant *fear-filled meditation* on Krishna, attains a form of liberation through the very enmity (vairānubandha) that consumed him (Bhāgavata X.44).
- **Kaṃsa's eight brothers** — His brothers (Kaṅka, Nyagrodha, et al.) rush to avenge him and are killed by **Balarāma** (Bhāgavata X.44; Harivaṃśa).
- **Mourning of the queens** — Kaṃsa's widows (daughters of Jarāsandha) lament; Krishna consoles them and orders the funeral rites — a touch of dharma even toward the slain enemy (Bhāgavata X.44).

## Restoration and Aftermath

- **Freeing Devakī and Vasudeva** — Krishna and Balarāma release their imprisoned birth-parents and, in the Bhāgavata's tender scene, restrain themselves from full filial intimacy lest reverence overwhelm the reunion (Bhāgavata X.44–45).
- **Ugrasena reinstated** — Krishna recoronates his grandfather **Ugrasena** as king of the Yādavas, himself declining the throne (so the prophecy is *fulfilled without usurpation*) (Bhāgavata X.44–45; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).
- **The hinge of the avatāra** — With Kaṃsa dead, Krishna's life pivots from pastoral *līla* to dynastic and martial career — Sāndīpani's school, Jarāsandha's sieges, the founding of Dvārakā (see [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md)).
- **Jarāsandha's vendetta seeded** — Kaṃsa's father-in-law **Jarāsandha** of Magadha now vows revenge, launching the seventeen (in some counts) sieges that drive the Yādavas west — the death of Kaṃsa thus *opens* the next arc (Bhāgavata X.50; Harivaṃśa).

## Two Clocks

- **Scriptural time:** these events fall late in **[Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** of the current 28th Mahāyuga, in Krishna's youth, a generation before the Kurukṣetra war and his departure (traditionally **3102 BCE**, the onset of Kali Yuga).
- **Text-critical time:** the narrative's **oldest connected form is the Harivaṃśa** (core 1st–2nd c. BCE, with growth into the early CE centuries), refined in the **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** (~5th c. CE) and most elaborated, bhakti-saturated, in the **Bhāgavata** (~6th–10th c. CE). The arena's basic skeleton — Akrūra, bow, elephant, wrestlers, Kaṃsa — is constant across all three; the devotional framing (Akrūra's theophany, the rasa-perception of Krishna in the arena) is the Bhāgavata's later overlay. [scholarly] (See [Harivaṃśa](./harivamsha.md).)
