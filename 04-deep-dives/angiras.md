# Angiras — Mind-Born Prajāpati and Priestly Fountainhead of the Gods

> *Where this sits: a deep-dive into **Aṅgiras** (Aṅgirā / Aṅgiras Ṛṣi) — one of the mind-born Prajāpatis and Saptarṣi at the root of the timeline, co-revealer of the **Atharvaveda** (the *Atharvāṅgirasa*), eponym of the largest root gotra-cluster (the **Āṅgirasas**), and father of **Bṛhaspati**, guru of the gods. ⬆ Parent index: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Master index: [README](../README.md)*

**Aṅgiras** (*Aṅgiras*, connected by folk-etymology with *aṅgāra*, "glowing coal/ember," and with *aṅga*, "limb/member of the body") is the priestly fountainhead of the Vedic world. Where **[Bhṛgu](bhrigu.md)** heads the rival fire-priest line and **[Atri](atri-anasuya.md)** roots the Lunar dynasty, Aṅgiras stands as the archetypal *purohita* and *brahman*-priest: his hymns fill the **Ṛgveda**, his name brands half the **Atharvaveda**, his sons and pupils form the **eight Āṅgirasa gaṇas** (the single largest pravara-cluster of brahmin gotras — including the **[Bharadvāja](bharadvaja.md)** and **Gautama** lines), and his son **Bṛhaspati** becomes *Devaguru*, priest and counsellor of the gods themselves. He is a **[creation]**-period figure who recurs across manvantaras.

---

## Origin — mind-born Prajāpati

- **Mānasaputra of Brahmā** — Aṅgiras is one of the **mind-born sons** (*mānasaputras*) and Prajāpatis emitted by Brahmā at the dawn of creation, ranked among the seven primordial ṛṣis. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.7; Manusmṛti 1.35)
  - *Variant (born from the mouth/fire):* Several texts derive Aṅgiras from Brahmā's **mouth** or from the sacrificial **fire** — a play on *aṅgāra* ("ember") that grounds his identification with the priestly fire. (Bhāgavata 3.12) [folk]
  - *Variant (Atharvaveda self-account):* The **Atharvaveda** itself treats Aṅgiras as a primeval being coeval with Agni and the dawn of ritual, less a "son of Brahmā" than a personification of the priestly fire-power. (Atharvaveda) [scholarly]
- **Married to Smṛti / the daughters of Daksa** — In the Prajāpati genealogies Aṅgiras weds **Smṛti** (or, in the Dakṣa-marriage scheme, daughters of Dakṣa such as **Śraddhā** or **Svadhā**), through whom his ṛṣi-progeny descend. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.7; Bhāgavata 4.1)
- **Saptarṣi office** — Aṅgiras is counted among the **Saptarṣi** (Seven Sages); his membership shifts by manvantara and by Purāṇa, but he is a fixed fixture of the primordial seven — see [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md). (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.1) [scholarly]

## Vedic role — the priestly fountainhead

- **Ṛgvedic seer** — Aṅgiras and the **Āṅgirasas** are among the most invoked seer-families of the **Ṛgveda**; whole hymn-cycles are ascribed to his line, and the gods themselves are called *aṅgirastama* ("most Aṅgiras-like") when praised as ideal priests. (Ṛgveda)
- **The Aṅgirasa fire-priests** — In Ṛgvedic myth the Aṅgirasas are the primordial **singer-priests** who, with **Indra** and **Bṛhaspati**, split the rock **Vala** with their *bṛhat* chant and release the stolen **cows** (the dawns/waters) — the paradigmatic act establishing sacrifice and song as cosmic forces. (Ṛgveda, the Vala myth)
- **Co-revealer of the Atharvaveda** — The fourth Veda is canonically the **Atharvāṅgirasa**, named for its two founding priest-lines: the **Atharvans** (auspicious, *śānti*/healing rites) and the **Aṅgirasas** (the fierce *abhicāra*/sorcery and counter-sorcery formulae). Aṅgiras is thus one of the two eponymous revealers of the entire Veda. (Atharvaveda; Gopatha Brāhmaṇa) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (split function):* Later tradition reads "Atharvan" hymns as the white/protective magic and "Aṅgiras" hymns as the black/aggressive magic, the Veda holding both poles. [scholarly]
- **Fire and the ordering of sacrifice** — Aṅgiras is repeatedly linked to **Agni** (sometimes named Agni's first priest or even a form of fire), making him the mythic origin-point of the fire-cult and the office of *purohita*. (Ṛgveda; Atharvaveda)

## Family and progeny — the largest gotra-cluster

- **Father of Bṛhaspati, the Devaguru** — Aṅgiras's most celebrated son is **Bṛhaspati** (Bṛahaspati / Guru), lord of sacred speech (*brahmaṇaspati*), planet **Jupiter**, and **priest and preceptor of the gods** — the structural counterpart to **Śukra** (son of Bhṛgu), guru of the asuras. (Mahābhārata; Viṣṇu Purāṇa) — see [the gurus Bṛhaspati & Śukra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md).
- **Other sons and the Atharvan line** — Genealogies variously assign to Aṅgiras sons including **Utathya** (whose wife Mamatā bears Bharadvāja and Dīrghatamas), **Saṃvarta**, and a daughter or kindred figure tied to the **Atharvan** house; **Agni** is sometimes called his son or brother. (Mahābhārata, Ādi & Anuśāsana Parvans; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.7)
- **The "daughters" — sciences and stars** — In poetic genealogies Aṅgiras fathers personified **branches of knowledge** and several of the **lunar mansions/asterisms**, marking him as the source of learning itself. (Mahābhārata; Purāṇic lists) [late text]
- **The eight Āṅgirasa gaṇas** — The **Āṅgirasa** pravara-cluster is the single **largest** root-grouping of brahmin **gotras**, traditionally organized into **eight gaṇas** (sub-groups). Within it descend the **[Bharadvāja](bharadvaja.md)** gotra (via Utathya's son Bharadvāja) and the **Gautama** gotra (the line of the sage Gautama), among many others — so that a vast share of brahmin lineages trace their *pravara* back through Aṅgiras. (gotra–pravara tradition; Baudhāyana Śrautasūtra pravara lists) [scholarly]
  - *Note:* Exact membership and the count of gaṇas vary across pravara manuals; the **Āṅgirasa / Bhārgava / Ātreya / Kaśyapa / Vāsiṣṭha / Agastya** scheme is the standard frame, with Aṅgiras's branch the most populous. [disputed]

## Mahābhārata and Purāṇic episodes

- **The fire that quarrelled — Aṅgiras shames Agni** — In one *Vana/Anuśāsana* tradition, when **Agni** withdraws, **Aṅgiras** himself takes up the function of fire and blazes; Agni returns, and Aṅgiras is honoured as Agni's "father" or first kindler, sealing his identity with the sacrificial flame. (Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana / Vana Parvan)
- **Teacher across the corpus** — As an ageless ṛṣi Aṅgiras appears as instructor and dialogue-partner in didactic passages (e.g. discourses on *dharma* and cosmology), part of the standing council of sages around Brahmā and Indra. (Mahābhārata, Śānti Parvan)
- **Aṅgiras and the Upaniṣads** — In the **Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad** the knowledge passes Brahmā → Atharvan → Aṅgir(as) → Satyavāha Bhāradvāja → **Aṅgiras**, who teaches the householder **Śaunaka** the distinction of higher (*parā*) and lower (*aparā*) knowledge — locating Aṅgiras at the head of the **Atharvanic** wisdom-transmission. (Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 1.1) [scholarly]

## Manvantara recurrences — the "two clocks"

- **A recurring office, not a single life** — Aṅgiras, like the other primordial ṛṣis, is presented on the **scriptural clock** as **re-manifesting across manvantaras**: he is reckoned among the Saptarṣi or among the standing Prajāpatis of more than one world-age, his "name" naming an office that recurs each cosmic cycle. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.1–2) [scholarly]
- **Distinct from later "Aṅgirasas"** — On the **historical/text-transmission clock**, "Aṅgiras" also labels a whole *family* of later seers (the Āṅgirasa hymn-poets, the Atharvanic ritualists, the gotra-founders) who are downstream of the primordial Aṅgiras; the corpus rarely separates the eponymous ancestor from his namesake descendants. [scholarly]
- **Among the lunar-line genealogists** — Through Bṛhaspati and the Āṅgirasa priest-houses, Aṅgiras supplies the *purohitas* of the dynastic kings catalogued in the [Sūrya- & Candra-vaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md), tying the priestly fountainhead to the royal genealogies. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4)

## Sources

- **Ṛgveda** — Aṅgiras and the **Āṅgirasa** seer-family as principal hymn-poets; the **Vala** myth (Aṅgirasas with Indra and Bṛhaspati releasing the cows/dawns); gods praised as *aṅgirastama*; Aṅgiras linked to Agni and the origin of the fire-cult.
- **Atharvaveda** — the fourth Veda as the **Atharvāṅgirasa**, named for the **Atharvan** (auspicious/healing) and **Aṅgiras** (fierce/*abhicāra*) priest-lines; Aṅgiras as a primeval priestly-fire being.
- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** — **1.7** (Aṅgiras among the mind-born Prajāpatis; his marriage and ṛṣi-progeny); 3.1–2 (Saptarṣi and manvantara recurrence); 4 (Āṅgirasa purohitas of the dynasties).
- **Mahābhārata** — Ādi, Vana, Śānti & Anuśāsana Parvans: Aṅgiras's progeny (Bṛhaspati, Utathya, Saṃvarta), his identification with Agni/fire, and his appearances as teacher and sage.
- **Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 1.1** — the Atharvanic transmission Brahmā → Atharvan → Aṅgir → Bhāradvāja → Aṅgiras → Śaunaka, and the *parā/aparā* knowledge distinction.
- **Gopatha Brāhmaṇa** — Atharvanic framing of the Atharvan and Aṅgiras lines [scholarly].
- **Pravara/gotra tradition** (e.g. Baudhāyana Śrautasūtra pravara lists) — the **Āṅgirasa** gotra-cluster, its **eight gaṇas**, and the descent of the **Bharadvāja** and **Gautama** gotras; exact membership varies by manual [disputed].
- [Angiras — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiras) (mind-born origin, Atharvaveda eponymy, gotra-cluster, Bṛhaspati genealogy; variants).
