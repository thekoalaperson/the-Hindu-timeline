# Gautama — the Āṅgirasa Saptarṣi (and the Ahalyā Episode)

## Gautama the sage and his lineage

- **An Āṅgirasa Saptarṣi** - Gautama (Gotama) is a great seer of the **Aṅgiras line**, counted among the [Saptarṣi of the current Vaivasvata manvantara](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md) in the Bṛhadāraṇyaka/Purāṇic rosters (Atri, Bharadvāja, **Gautama**, Jamadagni, Kaśyapa, Vasiṣṭha, Viśvāmitra). A poet of parts of **Ṛgveda Maṇḍala I**. (*Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 2.2.4; *Ṛgveda*)
- **Founder of the Gautama gotra** - He is the eponymous ancestor of the **Gautama (Gotama) gotra**, one of the Āṅgirasa gaṇas — see [Ṛṣi Lineages & Gotras](../08-beings-and-bestiary/rishi-lineages-and-gotras.md). His āśrama lies near **Mithilā** (Videha), and the **Godāvarī** river is called **Gautamī** after him in the Deccan tradition. (*Mahābhārata*; Purāṇic geography)
- **Father of Śatānanda** - By Ahalyā, Gautama is the father of **Śatānanda**, who becomes the **purohita (family priest) of King Janaka** of Mithilā — so the family reconnects to Rāma's story at Janaka's court. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Bāla 66)
- **Name-association with Nyāya** - The author of the **Nyāya-sūtra**, the foundational text of the Nyāya school of logic, is also called **Gautama (Akṣapāda Gautama)** and is reckoned of this gotra; the two Gautamas are distinct figures linked only by name/clan, not the same person. [scholarly] (*Nyāya-sūtra* tradition)

## The Ahalyā episode — summary (full treatment: [Ahalyā](characters/ahalya.md))

- **Marriage** - **Ahalyā**, fashioned flawless by **Brahmā** (her name glossed *a-halyā*, "she with no blemish"), is given to Gautama — in the Purāṇic backstory after a **contest to circumambulate the three worlds**, which Gautama wins over Indra by a ritual technicality (circling a calving cow), seeding **Indra's grudge**. (*Brahma Purāṇa*; *Padma Purāṇa*) [late text]
- **The seduction** - Indra approaches Ahalyā **disguised in Gautama's exact form** while the sage is out for his dawn ablutions; the recensions split sharply on whether she is **deceived or knowingly complicit** — the variant-cluster is catalogued in [Ahalyā](characters/ahalya.md). (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Bāla 48)
- **The double curse** - Gautama curses **Indra** (loss of testicles / the thousand marks that become the **Sahasrākṣa** eyes) and **Ahalyā** (invisible ash-bed penance in Vālmīki; **stone** in the dominant Purāṇic-devotional strand), fixing her release on **Rāma's coming**; in one Purāṇic strand he also curses the **Moon (Candra)**, Indra's accomplice in the dawn-ruse, to wane / bear the lunar blemish. Full variant catalogue: [Ahalyā](characters/ahalya.md) and [Indra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/indra.md). (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Bāla 48–49; Purāṇas) [folk/disputed for the moon-strand]
- **The redemption** - Generations later **[Viśvāmitra](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md)** brings Rāma to the deserted āśrama; Ahalyā is freed by his **darśana** (Vālmīki) or the **touch of his foot** (popular tradition), Gautama is **reconciled with the purified Ahalyā**, and the party proceeds to Mithilā, where **Śatānanda** greets them — the **first public miracle of Rāma's redemptive divinity**. (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Bāla 49–50, 66)
- **Themes carried by the tale** - the curse-economy in which a **brāhmaṇa-ṛṣi's curse overpowers even Indra** (compare [Cyavana](cyavana-sukanya.md) and the [Viśvāmitra–Vasiṣṭha](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md) feud); the **etiological pay-offs** (Sahasrākṣa, the lunar blemish, the sanctity of the Gautamī/Godāvarī); and Ahalyā's canonization among the **pañca-kanyā** — all treated in the canonical file. [scholarly]
