# Khaṭvāṅga — liberation in a single muhūrta

> Cosmic address: an Ikṣvāku (Solar-dynasty) exemplum of *antakāla-smaraṇa* ("remembrance at the last hour"), set in the human world of the **[7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)** and narrated by **[Śuka](shuka-shukadeva.md)** to the dying **[Parīkṣit](../03-lineages-vamsha/parikshit.md)** in the **[Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md)**. It is the corpus's locus classicus for *kṣaṇa-mukti* — liberation won in one 48-minute muhūrta by total surrender.
> Parent: [Deep Dives](README.md)

King **Khaṭvāṅga** (Khaṭvāṅga Mahārāja) is a minor figure in the great Ikṣvāku genealogy of *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* Canto 9 who becomes, in Śuka's telling, the single most-cited proof that the **duration** of remaining life is irrelevant to salvation: told by the gods themselves that only **one muhūrta** of his life was left, he renounced everything and merged into the Absolute before the hour was out. Śuka invokes him twice — once as the doctrinal charter of *how to die* (BhP 2.1.13), once in the flow of the Solar-dynasty narrative (BhP 9.9.41–49) — and the two citations frame the whole seven-day recitation of the Bhāgavata itself, since Parīkṣit, cursed to die in **seven days**, has by Khaṭvāṅga's measure a near-eternity in hand. This file treats the **king**; for the skull-topped staff that shares his name, see the disambiguation below.

---

## I. The Frame — Śuka cites Khaṭvāṅga to a king with seven days to live

- **Parīkṣit's predicament** — Cursed by the brāhmaṇa boy Śṛṅgi to die of Takṣaka's venom within **seven days**, Parīkṣit renounces his throne, sits at the Ganges to fast unto death, and asks Śuka what a man about to die should hear, chant, do, and remember. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.19; 2.1) — see [Parīkṣit](../03-lineages-vamsha/parikshit.md) and [Śuka](shuka-shukadeva.md).
- **The charter verse — Khaṭvāṅga named (BhP 2.1.13)** — In answer, Śuka lays down the rule of *antakāla* and at once produces his precedent: **"Mahārāja Khaṭvāṅga, being informed that the duration of his life would last only a moment more (*muhūrta*), at once freed himself of all material activities and took shelter of the supreme safety, Lord Vāsudeva."** The example is offered *before* the story is told — Khaṭvāṅga is a name Parīkṣit is expected already to know. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 2.1.13)
- **The consolation implied** — If a king with one muhūrta could be liberated, then Parīkṣit's **seven days** are lavish; the whole Bhāgavata that follows is, structurally, an unhurried expansion of what Khaṭvāṅga did in under an hour. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 2.1.13, read against the seven-day frame of 1.19)

## II. Who Khaṭvāṅga was — the Ikṣvāku genealogy

- **Placement in the Solar line (BhP 9.9.40–41)** — In the recital of Ikṣvāku descendants after Bhagīratha and the bringing-down of Gaṅgā, Khaṭvāṅga appears as son of **Viśvasaha** (Viśvasaho ... Khaṭvāṅga), and father of **Dīrghabāhu**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.9.40–41) — see [Sūryavaṃśa & Candravaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
- **Grandfather of Raghu — great-line of Rāma** — Dīrghabāhu's son is **Raghu**, after whom the dynasty is styled *Raghuvaṃśa*; through Raghu → Aja → Daśaratha the line runs down to **Rāma**. Khaṭvāṅga is thus Rāma's paternal ancestor several generations up. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.9.41–44; 9.10) — see [Raghuvaṃśa kings](../03-lineages-vamsha/raghuvamsha-kalidasa-kings.md).
- **Name and its irony** — *Khaṭvāṅga* means "the limb (aṅga) of a bedstead (khaṭvā)," i.e. a bed-post or club shaped like one; the tradition offers no fixed etiology for why this king bore the name of a cudgel. The homonymy with Śiva's skull-staff (§VI) is coincidental and is a standing source of confusion in indexes. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.9; lexical) [scholarly]

## III. The war of the devas and the boon

- **Summoned by the gods (BhP 9.9.41–42)** — Khaṭvāṅga was **unconquerable in battle** (*ajita*). Requested by the **devas** to fight on their side against the **asuras**, he joined their war and secured their victory. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.9.41–42)
- **The offer of a benediction (BhP 9.9.42)** — Pleased, the demigods asked him to choose whatever boon he wished. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.9.42)
- **The one question that mattered (BhP 9.9.43)** — Instead of wealth, kingdom, or heaven, the king asked **how much of his own life remained**. The gods answered: **only a single muhūrta more**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.9.43)
  - *Point of the request:* the boon-scene is deliberately inverted — a man given a blank cheque by the gods spends it to learn the one fact (the hour of his death) that reduces every other boon to worthlessness. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.9.43) [scholarly]

## IV. The single muhūrta — renunciation and merging

- **Instant departure (BhP 9.9.44)** — On hearing the answer Khaṭvāṅga wasted no motion: by his mystic power he returned at once from the celestial region to his **own abode / earth**, fixing his intelligence directly on the Lord. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.9.44)
- **The discourse of detachment (BhP 9.9.45–48)** — In the hour he had, the king spoke (to himself / to the world) the resolution the tradition memorizes: nothing — not his **queens, kingdom, wealth, or even the demigods** who honoured him — is dearer to him than **Nārāyaṇa**; the brāhmaṇas and the Lord are his only refuge; the objects of the senses, entered only to satisfy a mind swayed by the *guṇas*, he now casts off as unreal. He resolves to take shelter of the Absolute Truth beyond name and form. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.9.45–48)
- **Merging in Brahman (BhP 9.9.49)** — His mind so cleansed of illusion (*māyā*), Khaṭvāṅga fixed himself in **Vāsudeva / the transcendental Brahman** and, within the muhūrta, attained the supreme abode — *brahma-nirvāṇa* / union with the Lord's own nature. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.9.49)
  - *What kind of liberation:* the wording (merging in the *brahman* that is the Lord's effulgence, taking shelter of Vāsudeva) is read as **sāyujya / brahma-sāyujya** by Advaita glossators and as **entry to the Lord's abode** by Vaiṣṇava commentators — the text is cited by both. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.9.49; commentarial) [disputed]

## V. How long is a muhūrta? — the two clocks of the story

- **Scriptural reckoning** — In the standard Purāṇic/Jyotiṣa division a **civil day (ahorātra) = 30 muhūrtas**, so **1 muhūrta = 48 minutes** (2 *ghaṭikās* / *nāḍikās*). By this measure Khaṭvāṅga had **48 minutes** to live. (*Sūrya-Siddhānta*; standard *kāla-vibhāga*) — see [time & cosmology](../00-time-and-cosmology/README.md) if consulting the calendar tables.
- **The looser popular gloss** — Because *muhūrta* also means simply "a moment / an instant," devotional retellings (and several English editions of the Bhāgavata) render the boon as "only a moment more," and popular summaries variously say **24 minutes** or even **12 minutes** — sometimes adding that Khaṭvāṅga was liberated in *half* his allotted time. These are homiletic figures, not a second scriptural measure. (Bhaktivedanta and later ISKCON glosses; oral retelling) [folk]
- **Why the number is doctrinally inert** — The teaching is precisely that the *quantum* of time is irrelevant: 48 minutes, a moment, or seven days are all "enough," because liberation turns on the **completeness of surrender**, not the clock. Collapsing the two readings would miss the point the episode exists to make. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 2.1.13; 9.9.49) [scholarly]

## VI. Disambiguation — the king vs. the *khaṭvāṅga* skull-staff

- **A pure homonym** — Quite separate from the king, **khaṭvāṅga** is a **divyāstra / ritual implement**: a **club or staff surmounted by a skull** (a bed-post fashioned into a mace with a skull finial), the attribute-weapon of **Śiva** (as **Bhairava / Aghora**), of **Kālī** and **Cāmuṇḍā**, and the signature emblem of **Kāpālika** and **Aghorī** ascetics, marking its bearer as the *conqueror of death* who haunts the cremation-ground. (Iconography; *Ayudhapuruṣa* tradition) — full entry: [divyāstras & divine weapons](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md).
- **Do not conflate** — The Ikṣvāku *king* Khaṭvāṅga has **no connection** to Śiva's staff beyond the shared word; he wields no such weapon in the Bhāgavata, and the skull-staff has no royal biography. Catalog indexes that list "Khaṭvāṅga" under *both* weapons and kings are recording two different referents. (Disambiguation note) [scholarly]

## VII. Variants, parallels, and disputed identity

- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa parallel** — The same one-muhūrta liberation of Khaṭvāṅga, returning from the gods' war to fix his mind on Brahman, is narrated in the Ikṣvāku genealogy of the **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** (Book IV), independently of the Bhāgavata's telling. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.4) [scholarly]
  - *Variant framing (Viṣṇu Purāṇa):* here the emphasis falls on his utterance of complete non-attachment and his realisation of the identity of self and Brahman, with less of the Bhāgavata's explicit *Vāsudeva*-bhakti colouring. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* IV.4)
- **Identification with Dilīpa (Mahābhārata / Purāṇic index)** — Several authorities identify Khaṭvāṅga with a king **Dilīpa**, "son of the woman **Ilibilā** (Iḍaviḍā)" — a name the Bhāgavata genealogy also preserves nearby (Aiḍaviḍi). By this reading Khaṭvāṅga = Dilīpa is one and the same Solar king under two names. (Mahābhārata references; Purāṇic Encyclopaedia s.v. *Khaṭvāṅga*) [disputed]
  - *Caution:* the Solar line contains **more than one Dilīpa** (notably Raghu's celebrated ancestor Dilīpa of Kālidāsa's *Raghuvaṃśa*, the Nandinī-serving king), and the corpus catalogs these under [Raghuvaṃśa kings](../03-lineages-vamsha/raghuvamsha-kalidasa-kings.md) without merging them; the Khaṭvāṅga = Dilīpa equation should not be read as identifying *that* Dilīpa. (Genealogical note) [disputed]
- **The sixteen great kings (Ṣoḍaśa-rājika)** — Some listings of the "sixteen exemplary kings" who died despite their greatness (Nārada's consolation of Sṛñjaya, Mahābhārata *Droṇa/Śānti Parva*) include a Khaṭvāṅga/Dilīpa; the *Ṣoḍaśa-rājika* is a floating set and its membership varies by recension, so his presence there is text-dependent. (*Mahābhārata*, *rāja-dharma* consolations) [disputed]

## VIII. Doctrinal significance — *antakāla-smaraṇa* and *kṣaṇa-mukti*

- **The charter of last-hour remembrance** — Khaṭvāṅga is the Purāṇic dramatisation of the **Gītā's** teaching that "whoever, at the end, quitting the body, remembers Me alone, attains My being" (*Bhagavad-gītā* 8.5–6): the state of mind at the **moment of death** (*antakāla*) fixes the soul's destination, and a lifetime's momentum can be overridden by a single fully-surrendered hour. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 2.1.13; *Bhagavad-gītā* 8.5–6) — see [puruṣārthas · karma · saṃsāra · mokṣa](../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md).
- **Complement to Ajāmila** — Where **[Ajāmila](ajamila.md)** is saved by an *accidental, ignorant* utterance of the Name (the doctrine of *nāmābhāsa*), Khaṭvāṅga is saved by a *deliberate, fully-conscious* renunciation — the two form the Bhāgavata's paired proofs that both the unintended and the intended turn to God at death suffice. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 6.1–3 vs. 9.9) [scholarly]
- **A king's mokṣa without renouncing the throne first** — Like **[Ambarīṣa](ambarisha-durvasa.md)** and **[Rantideva](rantideva.md)**, Khaṭvāṅga models the *rāja-ṛṣi* ideal: liberation is available to the active ruler, not reserved for the forest-dweller — here compressed into its most extreme form, a king who renounces and is freed in the same hour. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9) [scholarly]
- **Reception** — The episode is cited across bhakti and Vedāntic literature as the standing answer to "is there time to be saved?" and, in the Bhāgavata's own architecture, as the reason Parīkṣit's seven days are not a sentence but an opportunity. (post-medieval commentarial and bhakti reception) [tradition]
