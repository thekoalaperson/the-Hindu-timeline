# Bhṛgu's curse on Viṣṇu — Kāvyamātā's beheading, Śukra-Jayantī, and Bṛhaspati's impersonation

> *Cosmic address: a **Cross-Yuga deva–asura episode** whose curse ripens in the **[7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)**, giving the karmic charter for Viṣṇu's mortal avatāras — above all **Rāma's** birth and the loss of **Sītā** in the **Tretā Yuga**. One story-complex told across three canons: Matsya Purāṇa 47, Devī-Bhāgavata IV.10–15, and Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara-kāṇḍa 51.*
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Master index: [README](../README.md)

Three episodes that the tradition tells as one interlocked knot: (1) **Śukra's** great penance to Śiva for a mantra even the devas lack, and Indra's counter-move — planting his daughter **Jayantī** at the sage's side; (2) the deva assault on the leaderless asuras, their flight to **Śukra's mother** (**Kāvyamātā**, Bhṛgu's wife), her yogic paralysis of Indra, and **Viṣṇu's beheading of her** with the Sudarśana; and (3) **Bhṛgu's curse** dooming Viṣṇu to repeated human births and separation from his wife — the doctrinal seed of the avatāra-cycle and of Rāma's grief — closed by **Bṛhaspati's ten-year impersonation** of the asura-guru. This node gathers the three-way merge; the guru-pair and the Kaca theft live at [Bṛhaspati & Śukra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md), and the chest-kick / Śrīvatsa cycle at [Bhṛgu](bhrigu.md).

---

## Dramatis personae and the name-collision

- **Śukra (Uśanas Kāvya)** — son of **[Bhṛgu](bhrigu.md)**, guru of the Daityas, planet Venus, holder of the **Mṛtasañjīvanī** revival-lore; his patronymic **Kāvya** ("son of Kavi/Bhṛgu") names his mother in this tale. (*Matsya* 47; *Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.10)
- **Kāvyamātā** — literally *"mother of Kāvya (Śukra)"*, the wife of Bhṛgu who shelters the asuras and is beheaded. The *Rāmāyaṇa* calls her simply **Bhṛgu's wife (Bhārgavī / Bhṛgu-patnī)**; the *Devī-Bhāgavata* and later digests name her **Kāvyamātā / Kāvya-jananī**. [scholarly]
  - *Name-collision (honor the disambiguation):* [Bhṛgu](bhrigu.md) has two canonically-named wives — **Khyāti** (mother of Śrī/Lakṣmī's line and of Dhātṛ–Vidhātṛ) and **Pulomā** (mother of [Cyavana](cyavana-sukanya.md)). Śukra's mother in the Purāṇic genealogy is a **third, distinct** figure (Pauloma/Divyā in some lists), whom this cycle designates only functionally as *Kāvyamātā*. Do **not** identify the beheaded wife with Pulomā (Cyavana's mother) or with Khyāti; the texts keep her separate. [disputed]
- **Jayantī** — daughter of **[Indra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/indra.md)** and Śacī, sent to serve (and marry) Śukra; already named as one of Śukra's consorts in [the guru-pair file](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md).
- **Bṛhaspati** — **[deva-guru](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md)** (Āṅgirasa, planet Jupiter), Śukra's structural rival, who takes Śukra's very form to subvert the asuras.
- **[Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishnu-forms-and-vyuhas.md)** — the slayer of Kāvyamātā, and the one cursed; the curse becomes the charter of his **[daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)** descents.

## Two clocks — dating the three texts

- **Scriptural frame** — a timeless, recurring **deva–asura war** (one of the periodic Devāsura-saṃgrāmas, cf. [Asura wars & roster](../08-beings-and-bestiary/asura-wars-and-roster.md)); its curse ripens in the **Tretā Yuga** of the current 28th Mahāyuga as Rāma's incarnation, and again "again and again" across yugas as the avatāra-series.
- **Academic frame** — **Matsya Purāṇa**: among the older Mahāpurāṇas, narrative core [scholarly] c. 250–500 CE. **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara-kāṇḍa**: a later appendix to the epic, [scholarly] c. 200 BCE–200 CE, post-dating the Bāla and core kāṇḍas. **Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa**: a Śākta [upapurāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/devi-bhagavata-upapurana.md), [scholarly] c. 9th–12th CE, the latest and most elaborated of the three. [scholarly]

## Act I — Śukra's tapas and the counter-lore of the Sañjīvanī

- **The strategic deadlock** — Brahmā's boons to the asuras keep failing because **Viṣṇu finds loopholes** in them; Śukra concludes the asuras need a power *no deva possesses*, not even Bṛhaspati. He resolves on penance to **Śiva** to win a new mantra. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.10)
- **The severe vow** — Śukra performs Śiva's prescribed austerity: **head-down, inhaling the smoke of husk/chaff (dhūma-pāna)** for a thousand years, motionless. (*Matsya* 47; *Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.11)
- **Śiva's boon** — pleased, Śiva grants Śukra a **secret mantra invincible against any foe**, together with **Prajāpati-hood, mastery of wealth, and invincibility (avadhyatva)** — the counter-lore to break the devas' advantage, standing alongside the Mṛtasañjīvanī he already holds. (*Matsya* 47)
  - *Related acquisition-myth:* a parallel Śaiva tradition (Śiva Purāṇa) makes the same penance the origin of the **Mṛtasañjīvanī** itself, Śukra being swallowed and reborn from Śiva as *śukra* ("seed") — catalogued at [Bṛhaspati & Śukra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md). [late text]
- **The asuras left leaderless** — for the years of the vow the Daityas are **without their guru**, the opening Indra has been waiting for. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.11)

---

## Act II — Jayantī, Indra's planted daughter

- **Indra's insurance** — dreading that Śukra's completed tapas will make the asuras unbeatable, **Indra sends his daughter Jayantī** to attend the penancing sage, serving him with total devotion throughout the ordeal. (*Matsya* 47; *Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.11)
- **The boon of union** — when the vow is fulfilled and Śiva has granted the mantra, Śukra turns to reward Jayantī's service; she asks to live with him as his wife. He grants **ten years** together. (*Matsya* 47; *Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.12)
- **The veil of invisibility** — Śukra draws a **curtain of māyā** around his hermitage and dwells with Jayantī **unseen by any outsider** for the whole decade — the concealment that leaves the asuras, once again, effectively guru-less and makes Bṛhaspati's ruse possible. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.12)
  - *Variant (offspring):* the *Matsya* has Jayantī bear Śukra a daughter during these years and names her **Devayānī** before departing — a genealogical crossing with the [Kaca–Devayānī](characters/kacha-devayani.md) cycle, where Devayānī is Śukra's daughter; the two traditions differ on her mother. [disputed]

## Act III — Kāvyamātā shelters the asuras and is beheaded

- **The deva assault** — with Śukra hidden away, **Bṛhaspati (or Indra's host) falls on the leaderless asuras**, who are routed and flee for refuge. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.13)
- **Sanctuary with Bhṛgu's wife** — the Daityas take shelter at **Bhṛgu's āśrama** with **Kāvyamātā, Śukra's mother**; she grants them protection (*abhaya*). (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara 51; *Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.13)
- **Her yogic assault on Indra** — using her ascetic power she moves to **paralyze / stupefy Indra** — in the *Devī-Bhāgavata* she is about to **burn or freeze the king of the gods and put the devas to sleep**, threatening to end the war in the asuras' favor. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.13)
- **Viṣṇu beheads her** — Indra, undone, invokes **Viṣṇu**; to save him Viṣṇu looses the **[Sudarśana](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md) discus** and **severs Kāvyamātā's head**, though she is a woman and a brāhmaṇa's wife who should never be slain. (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara 51; *Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.13)
  - *Variant (the weapon/means):* the *Rāmāyaṇa* says only that Viṣṇu, "in fury," severed her head; the *Devī-Bhāgavata* and later digests specify the **Sudarśana Chakra**. [scholarly]

## Act IV — Bhṛgu's curse: the charter of the avatāras

- **The curse spoken** — returning to find his wife slain, **Bhṛgu curses Viṣṇu**: *"Because in your senseless fury you have slain my wife, who ought never to have been killed, you shall be **born again and again in the world of men**, and there you shall dwell **separated from your consort for many years**."* (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara 51.14–17; *Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.14)
  - *Devī-Bhāgavata amplification:* the curse specifies Viṣṇu will suffer the **misery of repeated birth and death** — imprisoned nine months in the womb each time — for the sin of *strī-vadha* (woman-slaughter). [late text]
- **Viṣṇu accepts** — far from resisting, Viṣṇu **willingly takes the curse on himself** "for the good of the worlds," converting a punishment into the **theological mechanism of his descents**. (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara 51)
- **The revival** — Bhṛgu then **sprinkles water from his kamaṇḍalu** and **restores Kāvyamātā to life**; she wakes "as though from deep sleep." The killing is thus undone in fact, but the curse stands. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.14)
  - *Variant (Rāmāyaṇa silent):* the Uttara-kāṇḍa records **no revival** — it needs only the curse, cited by Vasiṣṭha/the narrator to explain Rāma's mortal sorrow. The restoration is a Purāṇic addition. [scholarly]
- **Śiva's answering curse** — some Śākta tellings add that **Śiva**, whose devotee Kāvyamātā was, curses Viṣṇu in turn, deepening the load of mortal births Viṣṇu must bear. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.14) [disputed]
- **The Tretā fruition — Rāma and Sītā** — the *Rāmāyaṇa* makes this curse the **why** of the avatāra it belongs to: Viṣṇu is born as **[Rāma](ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md)**, and the "separation from his consort for many years" is fulfilled as the **abduction and loss of [Sītā](characters/sita.md)**. In the Uttara-kāṇḍa the frame runs on to **Durvāsa's prophecy** of Rāma's eleven-thousand-year reign, his two sons, and his eventual return to Brahmaloka — the curse's course run to its end. (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttara 51)

---

## Act V — Bṛhaspati's impersonation of the asura-guru

- **Taking Śukra's form** — during the hidden decade, **Bṛhaspati assumes the exact appearance of Śukra** and presents himself to the asuras as their returned guru, who accept him unquestioningly. (*Matsya* 47; *Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.13)
- **Subversion by false doctrine** — the counterfeit guru **leads the Daityas astray**, in the *Devī-Bhāgavata* preaching a **heterodox / Jaina-flavoured dharma** (ahiṃsā, renunciation of vengeance) that "disunites" them and saps their martial will — a Purāṇic charter for how the asuras were doctrinally disarmed. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.13) [scholarly]
- **The real Śukra rejected** — when the true Śukra completes his ten years with Jayantī and returns, the asuras — trusting the imposter — **reject the genuine guru as the fraud**, and the disguised Bṛhaspati brazenly denounces the newcomer as the counterfeit. (*Matsya* 47; *Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.13)
- **Śukra's curse on the asuras** — enraged at being spurned, **Śukra curses the Daityas** that they will be defeated and lose their power; only when Bṛhaspati's allotted term ends and he vanishes do they realize their blunder and beg forgiveness — too late, the war already lost. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* IV.15)
- **The recurring trope** — this is the sharpest instance of a standing pattern: the guru is each side's **irreplaceable strategic asset**, so removing or counterfeiting him decides the war — the same logic that drives the [Kaca–Devayānī](characters/kacha-devayani.md) theft of the Sañjīvanī. (*Matsya*; *Bhāgavata*)

## Why the three tellings differ

- **Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara 51** — keeps **only** the beheading and the curse, stripped of tapas, Jayantī and Bṛhaspati; it exists to **justify Rāma's mortality and Sītā's loss**, and pairs with the same kāṇḍa's use of the tale to license Rāma's killing of the yakṣiṇī **[Tāṭakā](ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md)** (a woman slain for the world's good). [scholarly]
- **Matsya 47** — centres the **Śaiva boon, Jayantī, and Bṛhaspati's ruse**; the beheading/curse is subordinate. It is the source for the Jayantī marriage and (variantly) her motherhood of Devayānī.
- **Devī-Bhāgavata IV.10–15** — the **fullest merge**, welding all five acts and adding the *strī-vadha* theology, the revival, Śiva's counter-curse, and the Jaina-dharma subversion; it reads the whole complex as a demonstration of **Śakti's** and karma's supremacy over even Viṣṇu. [late text]

## Sources

- **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara-kāṇḍa, sarga 51** ("Viṣṇu is cursed by Bhṛgu") — the deva–asura war; the Daityas sheltered by Bhṛgu's wife; Viṣṇu severs her head in fury; Bhṛgu's curse of repeated human birth and separation from his consort; Viṣṇu's willing acceptance "for the good of the worlds"; frame runs on to Durvāsa's prophecy of Rāma's reign. (No revival in this recension.)
- **Matsya Purāṇa 47** — Śukra's head-down husk-smoke tapas to Śiva; Śiva's boon (invincible mantra, Prajāpati-hood, avadhyatva); Indra's daughter **Jayantī** planted, the ten-year union under the veil of māyā (and, variantly, her bearing Devayānī); Bṛhaspati's impersonation of Śukra and the rejection of the true guru.
- **Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa IV.10–15** — the full merge: Śukra's motive (Viṣṇu voiding Brahmā's boons), the tapas, Jayantī, the assault on the leaderless asuras, **Kāvyamātā's** shelter of them and yogic paralysis of Indra, Viṣṇu's beheading of her with the **Sudarśana**, Bhṛgu's *strī-vadha* curse and her **revival** by kamaṇḍalu-water, Śiva's counter-curse, and Bṛhaspati's false-dharma subversion of the Daityas.
- **Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra-saṃhitā 2.5.50** — parallel origin of Śukra's Mṛtasañjīvanī via the swallowing-and-rebirth from Śiva (cross-reference). [late text]
