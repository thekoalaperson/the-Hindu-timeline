# The Pracetas, the Rudra-Gītā, and the marriage to Māriṣā

> Cosmic address: Shveta-Vārāha Kalpa → the primordial **Svāyambhuva-Manu / Dhruva line** (early dynastic prehistory), late in the [Pṛthu succession](prithu-prithvi.md) — Prācīnabarhi and his ten sons the **Pracetas**. The set-piece of the **Caturtha Skandha** (Fourth Canto) of the [Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md), narrated by Maitreya to Vidura, whose theological heart is the **Rudra-Gītā** — the hymn to Viṣṇu sung by **Śiva himself**.
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Up to [README](../README.md)

The **Pracetas** (Prācetasas, "the intelligent ones") are the ten sons of King **Prācīnabarhi**, standing near the end of the Svāyambhuva-line dynasty descended from Dhruva through [Pṛthu](prithu-prithvi.md). Commanded to beget progeny, they went to perform austerities and en route met **Śiva (Rudra)**, who out of causeless mercy taught them a stotra glorifying **Viṣṇu / Vāsudeva** — the **Rudra-Gītā** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.24.33–79). Chanting it for ten thousand years underwater, they won the Lord's grace; emerging, they found the earth choked with forest and burned it in wrath until **Soma** pacified them and gave them the tree-born maiden **Māriṣā** as their shared wife. From her was born the reborn **Dakṣa** — the "second Dakṣa" — closing the canto's arc from the Dakṣa who had reviled Śiva to the Pracetas whom Śiva blessed. The episode is the Bhāgavata's great **Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava bridge**.

---

## Where it sits — the two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic frame** — Deep prehistory of the present kalpa: the Svāyambhuva-Manu genealogy (Manu → Uttānapāda → [Dhruva](dhruva.md) → … → Aṅga → Vena → [Pṛthu](prithu-prithvi.md) → Antardhāna → Prācīnabarhi → the Pracetas). The corpus places this line at the very dawn of dynastic time; the reborn Dakṣa it produces re-seeds the population of the world. See [Svāyambhuva Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-01-svayambhuva.md). The action carries no dynastic "historical-clock" date.
- **Academic / historical frame** — [scholarly] The *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* is usually dated **c. 9th–10th century CE** (likely South Indian provenance); the parallel Pracetas / Māriṣā material in the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* is earlier, **c. 4th–5th century CE**. The Rudra-Gītā belongs to the Bhāgavata's programmatic **Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava synthesis**, characteristic of that milieu and of the emerging pan-sectarian bhakti settlement.
- **Function in the canto** — Canto 4 stages the Śiva–Viṣṇu tension in its first half (the [Dakṣa-yajña](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md), where Dakṣa reviles Śiva and loses his head) and **resolves** it in its last chapters: the same Śiva, now unwronged, personally consecrates the Pracetas in Viṣṇu-devotion, and their line brings the offending Dakṣa back reformed. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.2–7 → 4.24–31)

## I. The lineage — from Pṛthu to Prācīnabarhi to the ten Pracetas

- **Prācīnabarhi (Prācīnabarhis)** — Great-grandson (via Antardhāna/Havirdhāna in the Bhāgavata) of the first king [Pṛthu](prithu-prithvi.md); a Prajāpati-king so absorbed in Vedic ritual that the whole earth was blanketed with sacrificial *barhis* (kuśa-grass) whose tips all pointed east — whence his name, "he whose kuśa points east." (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.24.8–11)
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.13–15):* Pṛthu's line runs through his sons **Antardhāna** and **Pālin** to **Havirdhāna**, then **Prācīnabarhi**, then the ten Pracetas — the same skeleton with different intermediate names.
- **The ten Pracetas** — Prācīnabarhi's ten sons, of one mind and equal in vow (*eka-mati*, all sharing a single resolve). Before retiring to his own austerities, their father charged them to **increase the population** and continue the dynasty. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.24.9–14)
- **Nārada and the Purañjana allegory** — Interleaved with the sons' story, the sage [Nārada](narada.md) comes to Prācīnabarhi and, to wean the king off mere ritualism, narrates the **allegory of King Purañjana** (the soul as king of a nine-gated city) — see [The Purañjana Allegory](puranjana-allegory.md). Instructed, Prācīnabarhi departs for tapas at Kapila's āśrama. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.25–29)
- **The Pracetas set out** — Obeying their father, the ten brothers travel **west** to undertake austerity, and come upon a vast, pristine **lake** (the ocean-lake in some renderings) — the setting of the theophany that follows. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.24.14–17)

## II. The meeting with Śiva at the lake

- **Rudra emerges from the water** — As the Pracetas prepare to worship, **Śiva (Rudra)** himself rises up out of the lake, radiant, surrounded by his associates (the *pramathas* and attendant folk), to grace and encourage the young ascetics. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.24.18–24)
- **Śiva's benediction** — Pleased by their singleness of purpose, Śiva blesses them and declares the theological premise that frames the whole episode: **one who is dear to Kṛṣṇa / Vāsudeva is dear to Śiva**, and Viṣṇu's devotees are the most worshipful of beings — positioning himself not as Viṣṇu's rival but as his foremost worshipper. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.24.24–32)
- **The gift of a hymn** — Out of causeless mercy, Śiva announces he will teach them a **stava** (hymn) glorifying the Supreme Lord — a prayer whose recitation will accomplish their purpose and, beyond it, liberation. This hymn is the **Rudra-Gītā**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.24.32–33)

## III. The Rudra-Gītā (BhP 4.24.33–79) — the Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava bridge

- **What it is** — "The Song of Rudra": the stotra Śiva sings to **Bhagavān Vāsudeva / Saṅkarṣaṇa / Nārāyaṇa / Hari**, spanning verses **4.24.33–79** of the Fourth Canto. It is unusual precisely because its speaker is **Śiva** — the deity of the Śaivas offering worship to the deity of the Vaiṣṇavas. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.24.33–79)
- **Content of the hymn** — Śiva glorifies the Lord as the **Supersoul (Paramātman)** and pure *sattva*, the source and refuge of all; he praises the Lord's transcendental form, his lotus feet as the shelter of the surrendered, the potency of hearing and chanting the Name, and the supreme value of the **association of devotees (sat-saṅga)** and unmotivated *bhakti*. He closes by teaching the Pracetas that whoever recites this prayer with devotion, at dawn and dusk, is freed from all bondage and attains the Lord. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.24.33–79)
- **The bridge, made explicit** — In the Bhāgavata's Vaiṣṇava theology, Śiva is not a competitor but the **greatest of Vaiṣṇavas** (crystallized elsewhere as *vaiṣṇavānāṃ yathā śambhuḥ*, "as Śambhu is among Vaiṣṇavas," *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 12.13.16). The Rudra-Gītā **dramatizes** that doctrine: the very God whom one sect worships bows to and extols the God whom the other worships, so that the two great streams are reconciled rather than ranked. [scholarly]
  - *Thematic mirror:* the hymn deliberately answers the canto's opening catastrophe. At the [Dakṣa-yajña](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/sati-daksha-and-parvati.md) a Prajāpati had **insulted Śiva** and profaned his glory; here Śiva **repairs the breach from the other side**, elevating Viṣṇu and blessing the men whose progeny will be the reborn, chastened Dakṣa. See [Trimūrti & Tridevī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md). [scholarly]
- **Instruction to chant it** — Having taught the hymn, Śiva instructs the Pracetas to recite it steadily to obtain the Lord's favour, then vanishes. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.24.68–79)

## IV. Ten thousand years of tapas and Viṣṇu's boon

- **Austerity underwater** — The Pracetas entered the waters of the lake and, standing in meditation, chanted the Rudra-Gītā with fixed concentration for **ten thousand years**, until the Lord was pleased. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.30.1–4)
- **The darśana of Viṣṇu** — **Bhagavān Viṣṇu** appears before them — dark as a rain-cloud, four-armed, resplendent — and offers boons. The Pracetas ask for continued devotion, good company, and release; the Lord grants that they will father excellent progeny, rule and enjoy, and at the end **return to him**. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.30.5–34)
- **A time-dilation motif** — As with other Purāṇic penances, the ten millennia pass while, above, the surface of the earth changes utterly — the setup for what they find on emerging. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.30.1–4, 35–37) [scholarly]

## V. The burning of the tree-overgrown earth and Soma's intervention

- **The earth choked with forest** — Rising at last from the water after ten thousand years, the Pracetas find that in the absence of any caretaker the **trees had overrun the whole earth**, grown so tall and dense they blocked the very sky and left no room for the beings the brothers were charged to increase. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.30.35–41)
- **Fire and wind from their mouths** — Enraged that the forest had usurped the world, the Pracetas produced **fire and wind from their mouths** and began to **burn the trees to ashes**, threatening to annihilate all vegetation. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.30.42–45)
- **Soma pacifies them** — **Soma** — the moon-god and **king of the plants / lord of the vegetable kingdom (vanaspati)** — intervenes to save his subjects, calming the brothers' wrath: the trees should not be destroyed wholesale, for they are needed by all creatures, and their father's purpose is served not by burning but by begetting. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.30.44–46)
- **The peace-offering** — To seal the reconciliation, Soma presents to the Pracetas a **maiden** raised among the trees — **Māriṣā** — as their bride, so that the union of the ascetics with the daughter of the forest may repopulate the world their fire had almost emptied. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.30.46–48)

## VI. Māriṣā — the maiden of the trees — and the marriage

- **Māriṣā, the tree-born** — Called *vārkṣī* ("daughter of the trees"), Māriṣā is the girl nursed by the forest and by Soma; Soma delivers her to the ten Pracetas, who **marry her jointly** — one wife shared by the ten brothers. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.30.47–49)
  - *Backstory (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.15; and [Apsaras](../08-beings-and-bestiary/apsaras.md)):* Māriṣā was conceived when the apsarā **Pramlocā**, sent by Indra to break the terrible penance of the sage **Kaṇḍu (Kandu)**, lived with him for **907 years, 6 months and 3 days**; on being dismissed she wiped her sweat onto the treetops, and the foetus so left was **nourished by the trees and the moon** into the maiden Māriṣā. The Bhāgavata compresses this to Soma's gift; the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* and *Brahma Purāṇa* preserve the full Kaṇḍu–Pramlocā romance. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (polyandry motif):* the marriage of one bride to ten brothers is a recurring Purāṇic device (cf. Draupadī and the Pāṇḍavas); here it also economizes the genealogy, collapsing ten fathers into one begetter of the reborn Dakṣa. [scholarly]

## VII. The rebirth of Dakṣa (the "second Dakṣa")

- **Dakṣa reborn of the Pracetas and Māriṣā** — From Māriṣā and the ten Pracetas is born **Dakṣa** — the Prajāpati who, in the canto's first half, had been beheaded at his own sacrifice for reviling Śiva and restored with a **goat's head**, now **reborn** to resume his cosmic office of procreation. This "second Dakṣa" (Dakṣa Prācetasa) again fathers the daughters who become the mothers of gods, demons, and all creatures. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.30.48–49; → 6.4)
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.15):* Dakṣa is said to be reborn as the Pracetas' son **in the Cākṣuṣa (6th) manvantara**, having been the earlier Dakṣa of the Svāyambhuva age — the two Dakṣas explicitly distinguished. [late text] See [The Prajāpatis](../08-beings-and-bestiary/the-prajapatis.md).
  - *The closing arc:* the Śiva who was insulted by Dakṣa (Canto 4 opening) is the same Śiva who taught the Rudra-Gītā to the men who now father Dakṣa anew — the beheaded reviler of Śiva returns through a line **consecrated by Śiva to Viṣṇu**. The reconciliation is genealogical as well as doctrinal. [scholarly]

## VIII. Nārada's teaching and the Pracetas' liberation (BhP 4.31)

- **A life of rule, then renunciation** — Having fathered Dakṣa and governed as their father wished, the Pracetas at last entrust the earth and retire toward release. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.31.1–2)
- **Nārada's final discourse** — [Nārada](narada.md) comes to them and delivers the canto's summary teaching on **bhakti as the essence of all duty** — that devotion to the Lord fulfils and surpasses every ritual and austerity. Enlightened, the Pracetas fix their minds on the Lord and attain his abode. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.31.3–31)
- **Canto's close** — With the Pracetas' liberation the Fourth Canto ends, having carried its reader from the Dakṣa-yajña quarrel to a fully reconciled Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava devotion. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.31)

## Variants & significance

- **Bhāgavata vs. Viṣṇu Purāṇa** — The **Rudra-Gītā as a Śiva-taught hymn** is distinctively the *Bhāgavata's* contribution. The *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* (1.15) has the Pracetas practise austerity in the waters and be blessed by Viṣṇu, and gives the fullest **Kaṇḍu–Pramlocā–Māriṣā** backstory, but does not frame the theophany as Śiva singing to Viṣṇu. The two texts thus divide the material: VP owns Māriṣā's origin, the Bhāgavata owns the sectarian bridge. [scholarly]
- **Soma as moon vs. Soma as sap/plant-lord** — Soma's double identity — the **moon-god** and the **presiding lord of vegetation/Oṣadhi** — is exactly why he is the fitting intercessor for the burning trees; the ambiguity is intentional, not a confusion. [scholarly]
- **The "Rudra-Gītā" name-collision** — Note that other texts carry hymns also called *Rudra-Gītā* (e.g. within the *Varāha Purāṇa* and *Devī-Bhāgavata* traditions, where the sense is inverted — a song **about** or **to** Rudra). The present Rudra-Gītā is specifically **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 4.24.33–79**, a song **by** Rudra **to** Viṣṇu. Do not conflate them. [disputed]
- **Placement of Dakṣa's rebirth** — The Bhāgavata narrates Dakṣa's re-emergence directly from the Pracetas–Māriṣā union (4.30) and resumes his progeny at 6.4; the Viṣṇu Purāṇa spaces it across manvantaras. Both agree it is the **same soul reborn** — the corpus catalogs this as the "second Dakṣa." [disputed]
