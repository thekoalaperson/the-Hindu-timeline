# Kalki avatāra

> Cosmic address: the **tenth and final** of the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) and the **only avatāra still in the future** — prophesied at the close of the present **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** of the [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md). Has its own dedicated scripture, the **Kalki Purāṇa**.
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · See also: [The Cirañjīvis](./the-chiranjivis.md) · [Paraśurāma](./parashurama.md) · [Time & Cosmology](../00-time-and-cosmology/README.md)

**Kalki** (कल्कि, also *Kalkin*) is the apocalyptic horseman with whom Viṣṇu closes the **Daśāvatāra** cycle and the yuga-wheel itself. Where the previous nine avatāras are past events fixed in scriptural time, Kalki is **eschatology**: born in the village of **Śambhala** as son of the brahmin **Viṣṇuyaśas**, schooled by the deathless **Paraśurāma**, riding the white horse **Devadatta** and wielding a blazing sword, he annihilates the *mleccha* and adharmic kings, slays **Kali** personified (the demon-spirit of the age), and turns the spent **Kali Yuga** into a fresh **Satya Yuga**. The fullest narrative is the late **Kalki Purāṇa**; the seed-prophecies are in the **Bhāgavata** (1.3.25; 12.2), **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** (4.24), **Agni Purāṇa**, and a Mahābhārata passage on the destroyer-king **Pramiti**.

**Two clocks.** Scripturally Kalki belongs to the *end* of a 432,000-year Kali Yuga of which only ~5,000 years have elapsed — so his advent lies some **426,000+ years in the future**, not in any historical lifetime. Modern "Kalki has come / is coming soon" claims belong to a separate, non-canonical clock and are flagged as such below.

---

## I. The setting — Kali Yuga at its nadir

- **The world in ruin** — As Kali Yuga ends, *dharma* stands on one leg: kings become rapacious thieves, *mleccha* (barbarian, casteless, foreign) rulers dominate, the Vedas are forgotten, lifespans and stature shrink, famine and lawlessness reign. Viṣṇu descends to end the age. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 4.24; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 12.2)
- **Kali personified** — The age is embodied as the demon **Kali** (distinct from the goddess Kālī), born of Adharma and Mithyā ("Falsehood"), source of the era's vice. The Kalki Purāṇa makes his defeat the narrative spine. (*Kalki Purāṇa*; cf. *Bhāgavata* on the demon Kali subdued by Parīkṣit)

## II. Advent in Śambhala

- **Birth** — Viṣṇu is born as **Kalki**, son of the learned brahmin **Viṣṇuyaśas** (and mother **Sumati**), in the village of **Śambhala** (Sambhala / Shambhala). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 12.2.18; *Kalki Purāṇa* 1)
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata 1.3.25):* the avatāra-list verse simply names him *Kalki, son of Viṣṇuyaśas*, "when the kings have become thieves" — the earliest compact prophecy.
  - *Note (geography):* **Śambhala** is sometimes localized to Sambhal in Uttar Pradesh [regional]; it is unrelated to the later Tibetan Buddhist *Shambhala* paradise despite the shared name. [disputed]
- **Lineage of the avatāra** — Kalki is the climactic, *aṃśa*-complete descent that restores the *brahminical* order, born into a brahmin house rather than the kṣatriya houses of Rāma and Kṛṣṇa — fitting an age to be re-founded on the Veda. (*Kalki Purāṇa*)

## III. Tutelage under Paraśurāma

- **The deathless preceptor** — Kalki is taught by **[Paraśurāma](./parashurama.md)** (the 6th avatāra, a **cirañjīvi** surviving from Tretā into Kali), who initiates and arms him — uniquely, one avatāra serving as *guru* to another and closing the cosmic loop. (*Kalki Purāṇa*) — see [The Cirañjīvis](./the-chiranjivis.md).
- **Mahādeva's gifts** — In the Purāṇa, Kalki worships **Śiva** in the Himālaya and receives the swift white horse **Devadatta** ("God-given"), a parrot-sage (**Śuka**) who recounts past and future, and a blazing sword set with jewels. (*Kalki Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata 12.2.19–20):* Kalki rides "a swift horse, **Devadatta**," wielding a sword, coursing the earth eight-formed in *aiśvarya* (divine majesty), cutting down the *mleccha* kings — no Paraśurāma-tutelage; the school-of-arms detail is the Purāṇa's own expansion. [late text]

## IV. The campaign — destruction of the adharmic kings

- **The sword and the horse** — Mounted on **Devadatta**, sword flashing like a comet, Kalki sweeps the earth slaying thieves, *mleccha* warlords and the millions who have abandoned dharma. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 12.2.19–20; *Agni Purāṇa*)
- **War on the demon Kali** — Kalki and his allies wage cosmic war against **Kali** and his progeny (Krodha "Wrath," Hiṃsā "Violence," and their kin), the cities **Viśasana** and the like, finally vanquishing the age-demon and his army. (*Kalki Purāṇa*)
- **The cirañjīvis assist** — Tradition holds that the long-lived ones aid the turning of the age: **Paraśurāma** as guru, and others surviving to Kali's close. (*Kalki Purāṇa*) — see [The Cirañjīvis](./the-chiranjivis.md).

## V. The Mahābhārata Pramiti / Kalki passage

- **Pramiti the destroyer** — The **Mahābhārata** (Vana Parva, Mārkaṇḍeya's prophecy, 3.188–189) foretells a brahmin **Pramiti** of the Moon-line, called also **Kalki Viṣṇuyaśas**, arising at the yuga's end to slaughter the *mleccha* and barbarian kings and re-establish the Kṛta (Satya) age — the epic's seed of the later Kalki myth. (*Mahābhārata* 3.188–189) [scholarly]
  - *Note:* here the destroyer is described in *kṣatriya*-like militant terms born among brahmins; the full equine, sword-and-Śambhala iconography crystallizes only in the Purāṇas. [scholarly]

## VI. Marriages

- **Marriage to Padmā / Padmāvatī** — Kalki weds **Padmā** (Padmāvatī), a princess of **Siṃhala** (Sri Laṅka) — herself an incarnation of **Lakṣmī**, under a curse by which she destroys any other suitor, lifted only for Viṣṇu's avatāra. (*Kalki Purāṇa*)
- **Marriage to Ramā** — He also takes a second wife, **Ramā** (Rāmā), princess of the **Bṛhadratha** line. (*Kalki Purāṇa*) [late text]
  - *Variant:* tradition names his sons by these queens (e.g., **Jaya, Vijaya, Megha-māla, Bala** and others), who inherit the renewed earth as its first kings of the coming age. (*Kalki Purāṇa*) [regional]

## VII. The turning of the age

- **Adharma destroyed, dharma restored** — With the wicked annihilated and Kali subdued, the surviving righteous are purified; the **minds of men become as pristine as crystal**, and a new generation is begotten in *sattva*. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 4.24; *Bhāgavata* 12.2.23)
- **Dawn of the next Satya Yuga** — At Kalki's victory the **Kṛta / Satya Yuga** begins anew: dharma stands again on **all four legs**, the Veda and varṇāśrama are re-founded, and the Sun, Moon and Bṛhaspati (Jupiter) stand together in Puṣya — the astronomical sign of the age-change. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 4.24; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 12.2.24)
- **Kalki's return** — His mission done, Kalki ascends to his abode; the new line of kings begun by him continues the wheel into the next **[Mahāyuga](../00-time-and-cosmology/yugas-and-mahayuga.md)**. (*Kalki Purāṇa*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 4.24)

## VIII. Later & non-canonical reinterpretations

- *Variant (Sri Yukteswar school):* in *The Holy Science* (1894) and its 24,000-year cycle, Kali Yuga is said to have ended ~1700 CE (we are in **ascending Dvāpara**), recasting Kalki's "arrival" entirely. [non-canonical / modern variant] — see [Time & Cosmology](../00-time-and-cosmology/README.md).
- *Variant (claimant figures):* various historical and modern persons (e.g., the 19th-c. **Kalki Avatar** movements, and several living gurus) have been declared Kalki by their followers. These are devotional/sectarian claims, not scriptural, and collapse the "two clocks." [folk][disputed]
- *Comparative note:* the messianic horseman-at-the-end-of-time has invited comparison with apocalyptic riders of other traditions; such parallels are interpretive, not textual. [scholarly]

## Sources

- **Kalki Purāṇa** — the dedicated scripture: birth in Śambhala to Viṣṇuyaśas, tutelage under Paraśurāma, the horse Devadatta and the sword, the war on the demon Kali, the marriages to Padmā/Padmāvatī and Ramā, his sons, and the inauguration of the next Satya Yuga. [late text]
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.3.25** — the compact avatāra-list prophecy ("Kalki, son of Viṣṇuyaśas, when the kings turn thieves"); **12.2.18–24** — the fuller eschatological account (Devadatta, the sword, slaying of the *mleccha*, dawn of Kṛta Yuga).
- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.24** — the end-of-Kali prophecy: the brahmin of Śambhala who destroys the barbarian kings and restores the four-legged dharma of the new age.
- **Agni Purāṇa** — short Kalki-avatāra notice within its Daśāvatāra material.
- **Mahābhārata 3.188–189** (Vana Parva, Mārkaṇḍeya's prophecy) — the destroyer-brahmin **Pramiti / Kalki Viṣṇuyaśas** who ends the age; the epic seed of the Purāṇic myth. [scholarly]
