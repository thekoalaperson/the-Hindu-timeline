# The Syamantaka Jewel

> A standalone deep-dive on the **Syamantaka** (*Syamantaka-maṇi*), the sun-gem of Dwārakā — the one extended "detective" episode of Kṛṣṇa's career: the gem's gift to **Satrājit**, the death of **Prasena**, the false accusation against Kṛṣṇa, the 28-day cave-fight with the bear-king **Jāmbavān**, the marriages to **Jāmbavatī** and **Satyabhāmā**, the killing of **Śatadhanvā**, and the aetiology of the **Gaṇeśa-caturthī** moon-prohibition (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.56–57; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* Book 5; *Harivaṃśa*). Set in the **Dvāpara-yuga** of our [Mahāyuga 28](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md).
> Up one level: [Deep Dives README](README.md). Related: [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md) · [Rukmiṇī-haraṇa & the Aṣṭa-bhāryā](rukmini-harana.md) · [Bhāgavata Tales Catalog](bhagavata-tales-catalog.md).

The Syamantaka saga is unique in the Kṛṣṇa corpus: a self-contained whodunit in which Kṛṣṇa is himself the suspect and must clear his own name by tracking a stolen jewel. It braids together a chain of deaths, two of his eight chief marriages, a direct narrative bridge back to the **Rāmāyaṇa** (Jāmbavān, who served Rāma, now serves Rāma's later self), and a liturgical coda: the story is recited to dispel false accusation, and explains why one must not gaze at the moon on Gaṇeśa-caturthī. It is the marriage-source for both **Jāmbavatī** (mother of Sāmba) and **Satyabhāmā** (whose later demand drives the Narakāsura war and the Pārijāta theft — see [Rukmiṇī-haraṇa](rukmini-harana.md) and [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md)).

## The gem and its owner (Bhāgavata 10.56)

- **Satrājit's devotion** — **Satrājit**, a Yādava noble of Dwārakā and votary of the sun, propitiates **Sūrya**, who befriends him and gives him the **Syamantaka** jewel as a token (Bhāgavata 10.56; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13).
- **The sun-gem's powers** — Worn at the neck, the Syamantaka blazes like the sun and yields **eight bhāras of gold daily**; in a land where it is honoured it banishes drought, plague, snakebite and untimely death, but it destroys an impure or unworthy wearer (Bhāgavata 10.56; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13).
- **Satrājit's entry into Dwārakā** — He enters the city wearing the gem, so radiant that the citizens mistake him for **Sūrya himself** come to see Kṛṣṇa (Bhāgavata 10.56).
- **Kṛṣṇa's request refused** — Kṛṣṇa suggests the gem be given to King **Ugrasena** (the Yādava sovereign) for the common good; Satrājit, greedy, refuses — sowing the suspicion that follows (Bhāgavata 10.56; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13).

## The death of Prasena and the false accusation (Bhāgavata 10.56)

- **Prasena's hunt** — Satrājit's brother **Prasena** wears the jewel and rides into the forest to hunt; a **lion kills him and his horse** and carries off the gem in its mouth (Bhāgavata 10.56; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13).
- **Jāmbavān seizes the gem** — The bear-king **Jāmbavān** slays the lion, takes the Syamantaka, and gives it to his infant son to play with in his mountain **cave** (Bhāgavata 10.56).
- **The slander against Kṛṣṇa** — When Prasena fails to return, Satrājit and the city whisper that **Kṛṣṇa**, having been refused the gem, must have murdered Prasena for it; the unjust rumour spreads through Dwārakā (Bhāgavata 10.56; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13). [This is the slander the whole episode exists to clear.]
- **Kṛṣṇa resolves to clear his name** — To remove the stain, Kṛṣṇa sets out with citizens to trace Prasena's route; they find the bodies of Prasena and his horse, then the dead lion, then **bear-tracks** leading to a cave-mouth (Bhāgavata 10.56).

## The cave-fight with Jāmbavān (Bhāgavata 10.56)

- **Kṛṣṇa enters alone** — Leaving the others outside, Kṛṣṇa descends into the dark cave and hears a nurse soothing Jāmbavān's child, telling it not to cry over the jewel (Bhāgavata 10.56).
- **The 28-day duel** — Jāmbavān, not knowing him, fights Kṛṣṇa in single combat; the battle rages **twenty-eight days** without rest until the bear-king's strength fails (Bhāgavata 10.56; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13).
- **Recognition — the Rāmāyaṇa bridge** — As his might ebbs, Jāmbavān realises his opponent can be none but the **Supreme Lord** he once served as **Rāma**; he praises Kṛṣṇa as that same Viṣṇu and submits (Bhāgavata 10.56). [scholarly: this is the explicit cross-yuga link — Jāmbavān, Rāma's old ally from the Vānara host, recognises Rāma reborn as Kṛṣṇa; see the Rāmāyaṇa.]
- **Jāmbavatī given in marriage** — In atonement Jāmbavān returns the Syamantaka **and** gives his daughter **Jāmbavatī** to Kṛṣṇa as wife; she becomes one of the eight queens and mother of **Sāmba** (Bhāgavata 10.56; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13). See the [Aṣṭa-bhāryā list](rukmini-harana.md).

## Vindication and the marriage to Satyabhāmā (Bhāgavata 10.56)

- **The jewel restored publicly** — Kṛṣṇa emerges from the cave after his companions, having waited twelve days, had given him up; he returns to Dwārakā and **hands the Syamantaka back to Satrājit** in open assembly, clearing himself of the murder-charge (Bhāgavata 10.56; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13).
- **Satrājit's remorse** — Ashamed of his slander, Satrājit gives Kṛṣṇa both the **gem** and his daughter **Satyabhāmā** in marriage as amends (Bhāgavata 10.56).
- **Kṛṣṇa keeps the bride, not the gem** — Kṛṣṇa accepts **Satyabhāmā** but **returns the Syamantaka** to Satrājit, declining to keep the jewel for himself (Bhāgavata 10.56; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13). [scholarly: the refusal underscores that Kṛṣṇa's motive was honour, not wealth — the moral of the whole tale.]
  - *Variant (rival suitors):* in some tellings **Akrūra** and **Kṛtavarman** had earlier sought Satyabhāmā's hand and had been promised her, breeding the resentment that drives the next murder (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13; Bhāgavata 10.57). [regional emphasis]

## The murder of Satrājit and the theft of the gem (Bhāgavata 10.57)

- **The conspiracy** — While Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma are away (drawn to Hastināpura by news of the supposed burning of the **Pāṇḍavas** in the lac-house), **Akrūra** and **Kṛtavarman** incite **Śatadhanvā** to seize the coveted Syamantaka (Bhāgavata 10.57; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13).
- **Satrājit slain in sleep** — **Śatadhanvā kills Satrājit in his sleep** and carries off the jewel; **Satyabhāmā**, grieving for her murdered father, hurries to Hastināpura to tell Kṛṣṇa (Bhāgavata 10.57; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13).
- **The pursuit of Śatadhanvā** — Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma return and chase Śatadhanvā, who flees after entrusting the gem to **Akrūra**; abandoned by his fellow-plotters, his horse dies and he runs on foot (Bhāgavata 10.57).
- **Śatadhanvā beheaded** — Kṛṣṇa overtakes and **beheads Śatadhanvā with the Sudarśana cakra**, but finds the jewel is not on him (Bhāgavata 10.57; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13).
- **Balarāma's rift** — Believing Kṛṣṇa is concealing the recovered gem from him, **Balarāma** parts from him in anger and goes to **Videha (Mithilā)**, where he stays and teaches **Duryodhana** the mace (Bhāgavata 10.57; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13). [The quarrel is healed only later.]
- **Akrūra and the gem's resting-place** — Kṛṣṇa traces the jewel to **Akrūra**, who had fled with it; he has Akrūra produce it publicly to prove his own innocence again, then lets Akrūra **keep** the Syamantaka (for its prosperity-power is safe only in a man of righteous conduct), ending the saga (Bhāgavata 10.57; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13).
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa):* the **Harivaṃśa** (Viṣṇu-parva) preserves an older connected version of the gem's career, the cave-fight and the Yādava feud, broadly agreeing with the Purāṇic outline. [late text / variant recension]

## The Gaṇeśa-caturthī moon-curse aetiology

- **Why Kṛṣṇa was slandered at all** — A widely-attached frame explains the false accusation as the effect of Kṛṣṇa's having **looked at the moon on Bhādrapada Śukla Caturthī** (Gaṇeśa-caturthī); whoever sees the moon that night is fated to suffer a false charge or calumny (purāṇic/folk *vrata-kathā* attached to Bhāgavata 10.56–57). [folk / vrata-kathā]
- **The origin of the moon's curse** — The prohibition itself is traced to **Gaṇeśa**, who, mocked by the **moon (Candra)** for his broken tusk and pot-belly, cursed him so that any who gaze on him on that *caturthī* incur disgrace; Candra's plea won the mitigation that the *blame is dispelled by hearing the Syamantaka story* (Gaṇeśa-Purāṇa / regional *vrata* literature). [late text / regional]
- **The recitation-remedy** — Hence the Syamantaka narrative is **recited or heard on Gaṇeśa-caturthī**, and by anyone who has accidentally seen the moon that night, as a charm to **avert false accusation** — the story's enduring liturgical use (vrata-kathā tradition). [folk]
  - *Variant (the "doṣa" naming):* the inadvertent moon-sighting is called *mithyā-doṣa* or *kalaṅka*; some traditions prescribe specific verses or the throwing of stones at neighbours' houses to invite cleansing abuse. [regional / folk]
