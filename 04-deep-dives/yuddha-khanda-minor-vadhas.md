# The minor demon-slayings of the Yuddha-khaṇḍa — Dundubhinirhrāda, Vidala and Utpala

> Cosmic address: two short **Śaiva deva–asura vignettes** that close the *Śiva Purāṇa*'s war-book — the Daitya **Dundubhinirhrāda**, uncle of Prahlāda, who hunts and devours the brahmins of **Kāśī** until Śiva bursts from a Śivarātri liṅga in tiger-form to crush him; and the boon-proud infiltrators **Vidala** and **Utpala**, who steal into Kailāsa disguised as Śiva's gaṇas to abduct the Goddess and are killed in a single stroke of **Pārvatī's play-ball**. Both are localized at Kāśī and both leave a liṅga behind.
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · See also: [Śiva Myth-Cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md) · [Śiva's Avatars & Forms](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-avatars-and-forms.md) · [Asura Wars & Roster](../08-beings-and-bestiary/asura-wars-and-roster.md)

These two episodes are the **coda of the Yuddha-khaṇḍa** (Book of Warfare), the fifth and final section of the *Śiva Purāṇa*'s **Rudra-saṃhitā**. After the great set-pieces of that book — Tāraka, Śaṅkhacūḍa, Andhaka, Jalandhara, Tripura, the redemption of Bāṇa (ch. 56) and the flaying of Gajāsura (ch. 57) — the compiler tacks on two brief, self-contained demon-slayings (chs. 58–59) whose common thread is **Kāśī** and the **liṅga installed to memorialize each kill**. They are told by **Sanatkumāra to Vyāsa**. Neither demon appears in the better-known asura genealogies; both are effectively local Kāśī-māhātmya charters absorbed into the Rudra-saṃhitā. All variants and attributions are kept separate below, not merged.

---

## A. Dundubhinirhrāda — the brahmin-eating Daitya of Kāśī (ch. 58)

- **Who he is** — **Dundubhinirhrāda** ("[he whose roar is] the boom of the *dundubhi* war-drum") is a Daitya, named as an **uncle of Prahlāda** and **brother of Hiraṇyākṣa**. His campaign is a delayed vendetta: after Viṣṇu (as [Varāha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-avatars-and-forms.md)) slays Hiraṇyākṣa, Dundubhinirhrāda seeks a way to weaken the gods indirectly. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Rudra-saṃhitā, **Yuddha-khaṇḍa 58**).
- **The strategy — starve the gods by killing the brahmins** — He reasons that the strength of the devas is fed by the **Vedic sacrifices of the brahmins**; destroy the sacrificers and the gods wither at the root. He therefore turns his war not on heaven but on the priesthood. (*Yuddha-khaṇḍa 58*). [a rationalized "attack the yajña" motif, shared with Rāvaṇa's rākṣasas harassing ṛṣis]
- **He targets Kāśī** — Dundubhinirhrāda comes to **Kāśī (Vārāṇasī)**, the city of Śiva, to prey on its dense population of sacrificing brahmins. (*Yuddha-khaṇḍa 58*).
- **The shape-shifting ambushes** — He hunts by disguise, assuming a **succession of forms** to catch brahmins as they gather materials for sacrifice — appearing as a **forest-dweller, an aquatic creature, an invisible being, and a tiger** — and devours his prey, the text says, **"never leaving even a bone behind."** (*Yuddha-khaṇḍa 58*).

## B. Śiva as the tiger-lord — the Śivarātri kill

- **The Śivarātri worshipper** — On the night of **Śivarātri**, a certain **ascetic devotee**, unnamed in the text, sits absorbed in meditation on Śiva before a worshipped liṅga in his hut. (*Yuddha-khaṇḍa 58*).
- **The tiger-attack** — Dundubhinirhrāda, in **tiger form**, springs to seize and eat the meditating devotee. (*Yuddha-khaṇḍa 58*).
- **Śiva emerges from the liṅga** — In answer to the devotee's absorption, **Śiva bursts forth from the worshipped liṅga itself**, seizes the tiger-demon, **pins him under his armpit**, and kills him with a **fist-blow to the head**. (*Yuddha-khaṇḍa 58*). [the "god steps out of his own image to save the worshipper" motif, cf. Narasiṃha from the pillar]
- **The boon and the shrine** — Śiva promises: **"If anyone sees me here in this form with faith, I will undoubtedly remove his torments and mishaps"** (v. 39). A shrine is established at Kāśī **"in the name of the lord of the tiger"** to commemorate the deed. (*Yuddha-khaṇḍa 58*).
  - *On the shrine's name:* the *Śiva Purāṇa* does not assign a formal Sanskrit name; it says only "the lord of the tiger." Later Kāśī tradition attaches such a tiger-liṅga to the name **Vyāghreśvara** ("tiger-lord"), but the identification is inferential, not stated in the Yuddha-khaṇḍa. [disputed] [regional]

## C. Vidala and Utpala — the boon-proud infiltrators (ch. 59)

- **Who they are** — **Vidala** and **Utpala** are **two great Daitya heroes**, brothers, puffed up by a boon from **Brahmā** that they **cannot be slain by a man** (*na puruṣeṇa vadhyau*). Secure in it, they overrun and **defeat the gods** in battle. (*Śiva Purāṇa*, Rudra-saṃhitā, **Yuddha-khaṇḍa 59**).
- **The loophole** — The boon is the classic **gendered escape-clause**: immunity "from any man" leaves the demons open to death **at a woman's hand** — the same trap that dooms Mahiṣāsura (slain by Durgā) and, inversely, the "not by man or beast" boon of Hiraṇyakaśipu. Brahmā, petitioned by the defeated gods, reassures them: **"They will surely be slain by the Goddess."** (*Yuddha-khaṇḍa 59*). [scholarly: the boon-loophole is a structural device for asserting Śakti's supremacy]
- **Nārada baits the trap** — At **Śiva's direction**, the sage **[Nārada](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md)** visits the two Daityas and **extols the surpassing beauty of Pārvatī**, inflaming them with lust until they resolve to **abduct the Goddess**. (*Yuddha-khaṇḍa 59*). [Nārada as the deliberate provocateur — the same office he holds in the Śaṅkhacūḍa and many other Purāṇic wars]

## D. The infiltration of Kailāsa and the play-ball kill

- **Pārvatī at play with the ball** — While Śiva and Pārvatī are together, the Goddess is **playing with a ball (kanduka)** near him. The two Daityas, coursing the sky, spot her from above and are seized with desire. (*Yuddha-khaṇḍa 59*).
- **Descent by Śāmbarī magic** — Vidala and Utpala descend using **Śāmbarī māyā** — the deceptive shape-shifting illusion named for the sorcerer-asura **Śambara** — and **assume the guise of Śiva's own gaṇas (attendants)** to approach Pārvatī unsuspected. (*Yuddha-khaṇḍa 59*).
- **Śiva reads their eyes** — Śiva **detects the imposters by the excessive tremulousness (fickleness) of their eyes** — a lust and unsteadiness no true gaṇa would show — and, without a word, **shoots a meaningful glance at Pārvatī** to warn her that these are Daityas, not his servants. (*Yuddha-khaṇḍa 59*). [the tell is a moral one: the restless eye betrays the desirous heart]
- **The single stroke** — Understanding Śiva's silent signal, Pārvatī **strikes both demons simultaneously with the ball** she is holding. They **fall dead to the ground**, likened to **"two ripe fruits shaken from a palmyra (tāla) tree by the wind."** (*Yuddha-khaṇḍa 59*).
- **The ball becomes a liṅga** — The **kanduka (play-ball) itself is transformed into a liṅga at Kāśī**, called **Kandukeśvara** ("lord of the ball"), a sacred spot said to **remove all wickedness** and bless its devotees. (*Yuddha-khaṇḍa 59*).

## E. Reading the pair — motif, theology, and place

- **A matched diptych** — The two chapters are deliberately paired: in ch. 58 **Śiva** kills a demon who *invades a worshipper*; in ch. 59 **Pārvatī** kills demons who *invade the divine household* — masculine and feminine halves of the one deity each dispatching an intruder, and each act crystallizing into a Kāśī liṅga (the tiger-lord shrine; **Kandukeśvara**). [scholarly]
- **Śakti's precedence** — Ch. 59 is, in miniature, a **Śākta charter**: the invulnerable-to-man Daityas are killed not by Śiva's weapons but by the **Goddess at play**, with a toy, almost effortlessly — a compact statement that the ultimate slayer of asuras is the feminine power. It rhymes with the Andhaka war, where the Mātṛkās finish what Śiva begins. See [Andhakasura-vadha](andhakasura-vadha.md). [scholarly]
- **The disguised-devotee inversions** — Both demons weaponize **false form**: Dundubhinirhrāda wears animal and invisible shapes to eat the faithful; Vidala and Utpala wear **gaṇa-form** to reach the Goddess. In each case disguise is undone — by Śiva's leap from the liṅga, and by Śiva's eye for a false attendant. [scholarly]
- **Kāśī localization** — Neither figure is a pan-Indian myth; both function as **origin-legends for specific Kāśī liṅgas**, the kind of local *sthala* charter that Purāṇic redactors folded into a mahā-war anthology. This is why they sit at the very end of the Yuddha-khaṇḍa, after the cosmic set-pieces. See [Kāśī / Kīrti-mukha & minor liṅga legends](../06-sacred-geography/kotirudra-minor-linga-legends.md). [scholarly]
- **Two clocks** — *Scriptural / cyclic frame:* the episodes belong to the **timeless deva–asura war cycle** narrated by Sanatkumāra, with no yuga fixed — cosmological, not chronological; the internal marker (Dundubhinirhrāda avenging Hiraṇyākṣa, killed by Varāha) loosely sets him in the same primordial asura generations as Prahlāda and Andhaka. *Academic / historical frame:* the extant *Śiva Purāṇa*, and its Rudra-saṃhitā, are dated **[scholarly] c. 10th–14th century CE** (the Vidyeśvara- and Rudra-saṃhitās among the older strata, often placed **c. 1000–1200 CE**); the Kāśī liṅga-cults these chapters charter are **living, observable practice**, a different clock from the narrated myth.

## F. Disambiguation — name-collisions to avoid

- **Dundubhinirhrāda ≠ Dundubhi (the buffalo-demon).** The Rāmāyaṇa's **Dundubhi** is a buffalo-shaped asura, brother of Māyāvī, killed by **Vālī** (whose flung carcass falls near Ṛṣyamūka) — an entirely separate figure. The Yuddha-khaṇḍa's **Dundubhinirhrāda** is a Daitya kinsman of Prahlāda slain by Śiva at Kāśī. [do not conflate]
- **Vidala/Utpala ≠ Utpaladeva or the iconometric *utpala*.** **Utpaladeva** is the 10th-century Kashmiri Pratyabhijñā philosopher (author of the *Īśvara-pratyabhijñā-kārikā*); **utpala** ("blue lotus") and **vidala** are also ordinary Sanskrit nouns recurring in iconography and śilpa iconometry. The Daityas Vidala and Utpala of Yuddha-khaṇḍa 59 have no connection to any of these — text searches on the names return only these false positives. [flagged per brief]
- **Adjacent chapter, covered elsewhere.** The demon of the **preceding chapter (57), Gajāsura** the elephant-demon — whose flayed hide Śiva dances in as **Gajāntaka / Kṛttivāseśvara** — is treated under [Śiva's Avatars & Forms](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-avatars-and-forms.md), not here.
