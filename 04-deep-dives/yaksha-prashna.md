# The Yakṣa Praśna — Yudhiṣṭhira and the Riddles of Dharma

> Cosmic address: an episode of the present Kali Yuga's recension of the **Mahābhārata**, set at the very close of the Pāṇḍavas' twelve-year *vana-vāsa* (forest exile), immediately before their thirteenth year of *ajñāta-vāsa* (incognito living). A deep-dive episode-file. Up: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Master index: [../README.md](../README.md) · Parent corpus: [../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md)

The **Yakṣa Praśna** ("the Questions of the Yakṣa"), also called the **Āraṇeya-Parva** / **Āraṇeya-Upākhyāna** ("the Episode of the Firesticks," from *araṇi*, the fire-drill), the **Dharma-Baka-Upākhyāna** ("the Legend of the Crane that was Dharma"), and popularly the **Yakṣa-Yudhiṣṭhira-Saṃvāda** or **Dharma-Gītā / Yudhiṣṭhira-Gītā**, is the closing episode of the **Vana Parva (Āraṇyaka Parva)** of the Mahābhārata. In the Kisari Mohan Ganguli numbering it falls in sections **311–315 (CCCXI–CCCXV)**; the **BORI Critical Edition** places it as the Āraṇeya-parvan, the final sub-book of the Vana Parva (CE adhyāyas 295–299). It is at once a death-test, a riddle-contest, and a compressed *dharma-śāstra*: the disguised god **Dharma** kills four of the Pāṇḍavas and questions the fifth, **Yudhiṣṭhira**, whose answers double as a catechism of moral philosophy. It earns Yudhiṣṭhira his patronymic test and the boon that makes the coming year of concealment possible. Related death-dialogues: [./nachiketa-katha-upanishad.md](nachiketa-katha-upanishad.md), [./savitri-satyavan.md](savitri-satyavan.md), [./markandeya.md](markandeya.md) (the sage who narrates much of the surrounding Vana Parva).

## Setup — the lost firesticks and the chase

- **The Brāhmaṇa's stolen araṇi** - As the exile nears its end, a Brāhmaṇa comes to the Pāṇḍavas in distress: a deer, rubbing itself against his fire-drill, has caught the **araṇi** (the two churning-sticks used to kindle the sacred Vedic fire by friction) and a churning-staff in its antlers and bounded away, robbing him of the means to perform his daily *agni-hotra* (MBh Vana Parva 311).
- **The Pāṇḍavas give chase** - To recover the ritual implements, the five brothers — **Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma, Arjuna, Nakula, Sahadeva** — pursue the deer through the forest, tracking it by hoof-prints; the magical deer vanishes, leaving them lost, exhausted, and parched with thirst (MBh Vana Parva 311).
  - *Variant (reading of the deer):* later commentary treats the deer itself as a phantom sent by **Dharma**, the whole chase engineered to bring Yudhiṣṭhira to the lake for the test. [scholarly]

## The deadly lake and the four brothers

- **Nakula sent for water** - Sinking beneath a tree, the brothers send the youngest-but-one, **Nakula**, to climb up and find water; he spies a clear lake fringed with cranes and runs to drink (MBh Vana Parva 311).
- **The disembodied warning** - As Nakula stoops to drink, a voice (in many tellings the voice of a **crane / baka**, hence *Dharma-Baka*) warns: *this lake is mine; answer my questions first, then drink.* Heedless with thirst, Nakula drinks — and falls dead (MBh Vana Parva 311).
- **The brothers fall one by one** - Each brother in turn, sent to find the first, ignores the same warning, drinks, and dies: **Sahadeva**, then **Arjuna**, then **Bhīma** — until four of the five lie dead by the water's edge (MBh Vana Parva 311–312).
  - *Variant (order of deaths):* the standard sequence is Nakula → Sahadeva → Arjuna → Bhīma; some retellings of the *order in which Yudhiṣṭhira finds them* list the bodies differently, but the canonical drinking-order is the twins first, then Arjuna, then Bhīma. [scholarly]
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's grief and resolve** - Yudhiṣṭhira at last comes seeking his brothers, finds all four lifeless, and is overwhelmed; but seeing no wound and no spilled water sign of ordinary death, and hearing the same voice, he chooses to **answer rather than drink** (MBh Vana Parva 312).
- **The Yakṣa reveals its form** - The guardian declares itself a **Yakṣa** (a nature-spirit; here a towering, terrible apparition, in some descriptions tall as a palm-tree with eyes like the sun), saying it was *he* who felled the four — they drank against his prohibition. He invites Yudhiṣṭhira to answer his questions and so earn both water and his brothers (MBh Vana Parva 312).

## The Praśna — the Yakṣa's questions and Yudhiṣṭhira's answers

The dialogue is a rapid-fire series of riddling couplets; the catalog below gathers the most celebrated and representative pairs. The full sequence runs to scores of questions (traditional reckonings range widely — commonly cited as ~33, ~100, or ~125 depending on how multi-part verses are counted; the roster is **open-ended** and varies by recension and translation). [scholarly]

### The four "comparatives" (the most famous quartet)

- **What is heavier than the earth?** — *The mother* (*mātā gurutarā bhūmeḥ*); she who bears and sustains outweighs the earth itself (MBh Vana Parva 313).
- **What is higher than the heavens / the sky?** — *The father* (the father is higher than the heavens) (MBh Vana Parva 313).
- **What is swifter than the wind?** — *The mind* (*mano javyaḥ*); thought outruns the wind (MBh Vana Parva 313).
- **What is more numerous than the blades of grass?** — *Thoughts / cares* (anxieties; in some renderings, the dead — those gone before — are more numerous than grass) (MBh Vana Parva 313).
  - *Variant (Source: Ganguli):* the pair is sometimes rendered "more numerous than grass = the cares (cintā)"; other recensions answer "the dead." Both are attested. [scholarly]

### The two crown verses (most quoted in all of Indian literature)

- **What is the news? (kā vārtā?)** — Time the cosmic cook: *"This world, full of ignorance, is as a pan; the sun is the fire, the days and nights are the fuel, the months and the seasons constitute the wooden ladle; Time (Kāla) is the cook that cooks all creatures in that pan — this is the news."* (MBh Vana Parva 313). This is the Mahābhārata's great meditation on universal mortality.
- **What is the greatest wonder? (kim āścaryam?)** — *"Day after day countless creatures go to the abode of Yama (Death), yet those that remain behind believe themselves to be immortal (na-icchanti mantum) — what can be more wonderful than this?"* (MBh Vana Parva 313). The single most-cited line of the episode, the canonical Hindu statement on the denial of death.

### The "path" verse

- **What is the way / the path? (kaḥ panthāḥ?)** — *"Argument leads to no certain conclusion; the śruti (revealed texts) differ from one another; there is no single sage whose opinion is authority; the truth of dharma lies hidden in a cave (nihitaṃ guhāyām). Therefore, the path is that alone which the great ones (mahājanāḥ) have trodden."* (MBh Vana Parva 313). The classic charter of *mahājana-gata* (following the conduct of the noble) as a source of dharma.

### Renunciation pairs (abandoning what?)

- **Abandoning what does a man become dear / agreeable?** — Abandoning **pride (māna / darpa)** (MBh Vana Parva 313).
- **Abandoning what is one freed from grief?** — Abandoning **anger (krodha)** (MBh Vana Parva 313).
- **Abandoning what does one become wealthy / prosperous?** — Abandoning **desire (kāma / lobha)** (MBh Vana Parva 313).
- **Abandoning what does one become happy?** — Abandoning **greed / avarice (lobha)** (MBh Vana Parva 313).

### Dharma and the inner life

- **What is the highest dharma?** — **Ahiṃsā** (non-injury to living beings) (MBh Vana Parva 313).
  - *Variant (Source):* in some couplets the highest dharma / the surest road to heaven is given as **compassion (dayā / ānṛśaṃsya)** — "freedom from cruelty" — rather than ahiṃsā per se; both answers appear in the dialogue. [scholarly]
- **What is true ablution / bathing (snāna)?** — Washing the **mind** clean of impurities (not mere bathing of the body) (MBh Vana Parva 313).
- **What is true charity (dāna)?** — The **protection of all creatures** (MBh Vana Parva 313).
- **What is the supreme refuge / shelter of dharma?** — **Liberality / generosity (dākṣiṇya / dāna)** (MBh Vana Parva 313).
- **What is the highest happiness?** — **Contentment (santoṣa / tuṣṭi)** (MBh Vana Parva 313).
- **What, controlled, brings no regret?** — The **mind** (a controlled mind never causes sorrow) (MBh Vana Parva 313).

### Identity and worth

- **What makes a man a Brāhmaṇa — birth, study, learning, or conduct?** — Not birth, study, or learning, but **conduct / character (vṛtta / śīla)**: a man of bad conduct is no Brāhmaṇa however learned (MBh Vana Parva 313). A landmark anti-birth, pro-conduct verse on caste.
- **Who is truly happy (kaḥ sukhī)?** — The man who, free of debt and not living in exile/abroad, cooks even a few vegetables in his own home at the close of the fifth or sixth part of the day — *he* is happy (MBh Vana Parva 313).
- **What is the enemy hard to conquer? / What is the disease without end?** — **Anger** is the unconquerable enemy; **greed / desire** the incurable disease (MBh Vana Parva 313).
- **What, abandoned, makes one rich and free?** — Renunciation of attachment; the man who covets nothing is the wealthy man (MBh Vana Parva 313).
- **Who is the friend of one who stays at home? Of one who is dying?** — The **wife** is the friend of the householder; **charity (dāna)** is the friend of the dying; the **friend of the gods is the cloud / rain** (cross-paired triads in the dialogue (MBh Vana Parva 313)).
- **What is dead though seeming alive?** — A kingdom / household without scripture; a sacrifice without a *dakṣiṇā* (priestly fee); a man who has no learning — variously "more dead than the dead" in the dialogue's negative riddles (MBh Vana Parva 313). [scholarly — wording varies by recension]

## The choice of Nakula

- **The boon of one brother's life** - Satisfied that Yudhiṣṭhira has answered all, the Yakṣa offers to **restore to life one of the four dead brothers** — Yudhiṣṭhira may choose which (MBh Vana Parva 314).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira chooses Nakula** - Passing over the mighty **Bhīma** and the peerless archer **Arjuna** — his own full brothers, sons of his mother **Kuntī** — Yudhiṣṭhira asks instead for **Nakula**, son of his stepmother **Mādrī** (MBh Vana Parva 314).
- **The reasoning — equity between the two mothers** - Yudhiṣṭhira explains: his father **Pāṇḍu** had two wives, Kuntī and Mādrī; "Both my mothers must have a living son." Since he himself (Kuntī's son) survives, Kuntī is not sonless; but Mādrī would be left utterly bereft. By *justice (sama-dṛṣṭi)*, one of Mādrī's sons must live — hence Nakula. He prizes **impartiality over personal preference** even toward his own dearest brothers (MBh Vana Parva 314).
  - *Variant (the answer that wins all):* in the standard text it is precisely this selfless, equitable reasoning — choosing the weaker claimant's son over his own stronger brothers — that delights the Yakṣa and prompts the revelation and the restoration of *all four*. [scholarly]

## The revelation — the Yakṣa is Dharma

- **Dharma reveals himself** - Pleased beyond the bargain, the Yakṣa casts off his terrible form and stands revealed as **Dharma** (the god of righteousness; in the text identified with **Yama**, lord of death and justice), who is Yudhiṣṭhira's own **divine father** (Yudhiṣṭhira having been begotten by Dharma upon Kuntī) (MBh Vana Parva 314).
- **All four brothers revived** - Honouring the son's virtue, Dharma restores **all four** brothers to life, hale and unharmed, and returns the Brāhmaṇa's stolen **araṇi** and churning-staff so the fire-rite can resume (MBh Vana Parva 314).
- **The deer-and-firesticks frame closed** - The whole episode is thus disclosed as Dharma's contrivance: the deer that stole the araṇi was the lure that drew Yudhiṣṭhira to the test of his father's making (MBh Vana Parva 314).
  - *Variant (the earlier test):* Dharma reminds Yudhiṣṭhira this is not his first trial — he had once tested him as a *yakṣa* / once before in the matter of the dog and other proofs; the episode is read as one in a series of paternal tests culminating in the [./mahabharata-minor-episodes.md](mahabharata-minor-episodes.md) *mahāprasthāna* dog-test, where Dharma again accompanies Yudhiṣṭhira. [scholarly]

## The boon — concealment for the year of ajñāta-vāsa

- **Yudhiṣṭhira asks for success in disguise** - Granted boons, Yudhiṣṭhira asks first that the Brāhmaṇa recover his fire-sticks (done), and then — anticipating the hardest year ahead — that during the coming **thirteenth year of incognito living (ajñāta-vāsa)**, the Pāṇḍavas may pass **unrecognized** by anyone, wherever they go (MBh Vana Parva 315).
- **Dharma's grant of non-recognition** - Dharma grants it: even should the brothers roam the three worlds, none shall know them; they will dwell that year in **Virāṭa's kingdom (Matsya)** undiscovered, defeating **Duryodhana's** condition that detection within the year would restart the exile (MBh Vana Parva 315).
- **A further boon — victory over the six foes** - Dharma adds a boon of inner conquest: that Yudhiṣṭhira's mind shall ever delight in **truth, self-restraint, asceticism, and charity**, and that he overcome avarice, folly, and anger — the moral armour for the war to come (MBh Vana Parva 315).
- **Transition to the Virāṭa Parva** - With the boon secured, the Vana Parva ends; the Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī proceed to assume disguises and enter the court of King Virāṭa — the action of the **Virāṭa Parva** (see [../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md)) (MBh Vana Parva 315 → Virāṭa Parva 1).

## Significance and connections

- **A pocket Dharma-śāstra** - The Q&A functions as a condensed ethical handbook; its verses on the supremacy of conduct over birth, contentment over wealth, and non-cruelty as the highest law are quoted across later Hindu moral literature.
- **The "greatest wonder" verse** - The line on men believing themselves immortal while death harvests daily is among the most quoted in all Sanskrit literature and a touchstone of Hindu reflection on mortality — thematically twinned with the death-dialogues of [./nachiketa-katha-upanishad.md](nachiketa-katha-upanishad.md) (Naciketas and Yama) and [./savitri-satyavan.md](savitri-satyavan.md) (Sāvitrī out-arguing Yama).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's defining virtue** - The episode crystallizes Yudhiṣṭhira as **Dharma-rāja**, the son of Dharma whose impartiality (choosing Nakula) and command of moral knowledge mark him as the rightful, righteous king. See his fuller arc and the closing dog-test in [./mahabharata-minor-episodes.md](mahabharata-minor-episodes.md).
- **Two-clock note** - *Scripture clock:* the event sits at the hinge of the Pāṇḍavas' 12+1-year exile in the Dvāpara-Yuga, just before the Kurukṣetra war that opens the Kali Yuga (traditionally 3102 BCE). *Academic clock:* the Āraṇeya-parvan is generally regarded as belonging to the older narrative core of the Vana Parva, though the long didactic question-strings show the same dharma-encyclopaedic accretion seen elsewhere in the epic's growth (c. 400 BCE – 400 CE range for the epic's redaction). [scholarly]
