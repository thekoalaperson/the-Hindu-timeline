# Hayagrīva

> Where this sits: a deep-dive on the **horse-necked avatāra of Viṣṇu** (हयग्रीव, *haya* "horse" + *grīva* "neck") — slayer of the demons Madhu and Kaiṭabha, recoverer of the stolen Vedas, and an independently worshipped deity of knowledge invoked by students. A named member of the longer avatāra rosters whose own narrative the corpus repeatedly cites but rarely tells.
>

**Hayagrīva** (also **Hayaśiras** / **Hayaśīrṣa**, "horse-headed") is a form of [Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) with a human body and a horse's head/neck, white as the autumn moon (*śveta*), whose two great functions are **recovering the Vedas** seized by demonic powers and **embodying knowledge** (*vidyā*, *jñāna*) itself. He stands among the earliest avatāra-forms named in the [Nārāyaṇīya](./narayaniya.md) and in the Bhāgavata's extended **24-avatāra** roster, yet his episode is told fully only in scattered Purāṇic and Tantric sources. Theologically he is the *jñāna*-avatāra par excellence — the Lord who guards and restores revealed scripture — and over time became an autonomous cult-deity worshipped especially in **Śrī-Vaiṣṇava** and **Tantric** circles by those seeking learning, memory, and eloquence. [scholarly]

> **Disambiguation — two Hayagrīvas.** The name attaches to *two opposed figures*: (1) **Hayagrīva the avatāra**, the horse-headed Viṣṇu treated here; and (2) **Hayagrīva the asura**, a horse-necked demon who steals the Vedas from a sleeping Brahmā and is slain by [Matsya](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) (Matsya & Bhāgavata Purāṇas). The two must never be merged: in one strand the horse-headed being is the rescuer of the Vedas, in another the horse-necked being is the thief. Catalog both. [scholarly]

## The Madhu–Kaiṭabha episode and the rescue of the Vedas

- **The demons born of Viṣṇu's body** — at the start of a cosmic cycle, while Viṣṇu sleeps on the cosmic waters and Brahmā is born upon the lotus from his navel, two *asuras*, **Madhu** ("honey/intoxication," embodying *tamas*) and **Kaiṭabha**, arise — from Viṣṇu's ear-wax in the common telling — and threaten the newborn Brahmā. (Source: Mahābhārata; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*; cf. [Cosmogony — Creation Accounts](../00-time-and-cosmology/cosmogony-creation-accounts.md))
- **The Vedas stolen** — in the strand that links the episode to Hayagrīva, the two demons **seize the Vedas** (or scatter/carry them off into the cosmic waters) at the moment of creation, leaving Brahmā unable to begin the world's ordering without scripture. (Source: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 2.7; Purāṇic tradition)
- **Viṣṇu assumes the horse-headed form** — to slay the demons and recover the lost Vedas, Viṣṇu takes the **Hayagrīva / Hayaśiras** form; the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*'s avatāra-hymn (SB 2.7) explicitly names Hayaśiras/Hayagrīva as the descent connected with the recovery of the Veda and the destruction of the Madhu-class demons. (Source: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 2.7)
- **The slaying of Madhu and Kaiṭabha** — the demons are destroyed and the Vedas restored to Brahmā so that creation may proceed; Viṣṇu thereby earns the epithet **Madhusūdana** ("slayer of Madhu"). (Source: Mahābhārata; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*)
  - *Variant (Devī Māhātmya 1, Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa):* in the [Śākta] telling, it is the **Goddess (Yoganidrā / Mahāmāyā)** who is the decisive agent — she withdraws from Viṣṇu's eyes so he wakes, and she so deludes Madhu and Kaiṭabha that they boastfully grant the boon by which Viṣṇu slays them on his own thigh; the horse-head form is not foregrounded. Do not collapse this into the Hayagrīva strand. (Source: *Devī Māhātmya* 1 / *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa*) [regional]
  - *Variant (Matsya recovery):* in a separate and opposed tradition the Vedas are stolen not *by* Madhu–Kaiṭabha but by **the asura Hayagrīva** (or Śaṅkhāsura), and recovered by **Matsya**, not by the avatāra Hayagrīva — see the disambiguation above and [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md). (Source: *Matsya Purāṇa*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 8.24) [disputed]

## The Devī Bhāgavata account — Viṣṇu's own head replaced

- **A distinct origin-of-the-form myth** — the [Śākta] **Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa** gives an *etiology of the horse head itself*: Viṣṇu, exhausted after long battle, rests with his chin on the end of his strung **bow**; ants (sent by the gods, or by the Goddess's design) gnaw the bowstring, which snaps and **decapitates Viṣṇu**, his head flying off and lost. (Source: *Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.5) [regional]
- **The horse-head affixed by the Goddess's will** — at the gods' appeal the **Great Goddess** decrees the remedy: a **horse's head** is severed and joined to Viṣṇu's headless body, producing the **Hayagrīva** form, in which the Lord then slays the *demon* **Hayagrīva** who could be killed only by a horse-headed being (the demon's own boon). (Source: *Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.5) [regional] [late text]
- **Note on framing** — this is a genuinely different account from the SB 2.7 Madhu–Kaiṭabha strand: there the horse-head is a *deliberate avatāra* assumed to rescue the Vedas; in the Devī Bhāgavata it is a *contingency* worked by the Goddess after Viṣṇu is beheaded. Both belong to the corpus; keep them separate. [scholarly]

## Place in the avatāra rosters — the "two clocks"

- **Earliest list — the Nārāyaṇīya** — among the oldest avatāra rosters, the [Nārāyaṇīya](./narayaniya.md) of the Śānti Parva names **Hayaśiras / Hayagrīva** (the horse-headed recoverer of the Vedas) alongside Haṃsa, Varāha, Narasiṃha, and Vāmana — a proto-list in which Hayagrīva is prominent though absent from the later canonical ten. (Source: Mahābhārata, Nārāyaṇīya / Śānti Parva; cf. [Nārāyaṇīya](./narayaniya.md))
- **The Bhāgavata 24-avatāra list** — the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*'s enumeration of *līlā-avatāras* is commonly extended to **twenty-four** by adding **Haṃsa** (the swan, expounder of knowledge) and **Hayagrīva** (the horse-headed restorer of the Vedas) to the core descents; in this scheme Hayagrīva is one of the named twenty-four. (Source: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.3 with traditional commentary; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 2.7)
  - *Variant (SB 1.3 vs. 2.7):* the famous SB 1.3 list of twenty-two does not always spell out Hayagrīva by name; the **second-canto** avatāra-hymn (SB 2.7) is the surer locus for the horse-headed Veda-recovering form. Catalog both references. [scholarly]
- **Not in the fixed Daśāvatāra** — Hayagrīva belongs to the *wider open roster* of *līlā* / *vibhava* descents (with Nara-Nārāyaṇa, Kapila, Dattātreya, Haṃsa, etc.), **not** the standardized ten of the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md); do not retroject him into the canonical ten. (Source: Bhāgavata / Pāñcarātra tradition) [scholarly]
- **Scriptural vs. historical clocks** — on the *scriptural/cyclic* clock the Hayagrīva descent is a **primordial creation-event**, recurring at the dawn of each cycle when the Vedas must be restored to Brahmā before world-ordering can begin; on the *historical* clock there is no datable episode — Hayagrīva is encountered only through **temple cult and devotional practice** in later centuries. The two registers must be kept distinct. [scholarly]

## Independent cult — the deity of knowledge

- **Patron of learning** — distinct from his role in the avatāra-lists, Hayagrīva is invoked as the **bestower of vidyā** — knowledge, memory, eloquence, and success in study — and is the tutelary deity of **students, scholars, and reciters**; his worship is prominent in **Śrī-Vaiṣṇava** and **Tantric** traditions. (Source: devotional / Tantric tradition) [scholarly]
- **Hayagrīva Upaniṣad** — a minor (Vaiṣṇava) Upaniṣad of the [Atharva] tradition supplies the **mantras and meditation (*dhyāna*)** on Hayagrīva as the supreme imparter of *brahmavidyā*, recited for the attainment of learning and liberation. (Source: *Hayagrīva Upaniṣad*) [late text]
- **Tirumala Hayagrīva** — a celebrated shrine of Hayagrīva is associated with **Tirumala / Tirupati** (Andhra), where the deity is venerated as the source of sacred knowledge. (Source: regional temple tradition) [regional]
- **Chettipunyam (Tamil Nadu)** — the **Hayagrīva temple at Chettipunyam** (near Chengalpattu) is a noted Śrī-Vaiṣṇava centre where students seek the deity's grace before examinations; the cult is linked to the Vedānta Deśika tradition. (Source: regional temple tradition) [regional]
- **Vedānta Deśika's Hayagrīva Stotra** — the 14th-century Śrī-Vaiṣṇava ācārya **Vedānta Deśika** composed a famous **Hayagrīva Stotra** invoking the horse-faced Lord as the embodiment of all knowledge; it is central to the deity's devotional cult and to his association with scholarship. (Source: Śrī-Vaiṣṇava tradition; Vedānta Deśika) [regional]
- **Iconography** — depicted with a **white horse's head** on a human body, four-armed bearing Viṣṇu's emblems — **śaṅkha** (conch) and **cakra** (discus) — and characteristically the **book / scripture** and the gesture of teaching (*jñāna-mudrā*), seated in lotus posture; the white colour signifies pure *sattva* and knowledge. (Source: iconographic tradition) [scholarly]

## Sources

- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.3** — the master avatāra enumeration extended in commentary to twenty-four, the roster in which Hayagrīva is counted. **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 2.7** — the second-canto avatāra-hymn naming Hayaśiras/Hayagrīva as the Veda-recovering, Madhu-class-slaying descent. **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.24** — the Matsya episode where the *demon* Hayagrīva (or Śaṅkhāsura) steals the Vedas (the opposed strand). See [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.5** — the [Śākta] etiology of the horse-head: Viṣṇu beheaded by the snapped bowstring and given a horse's head by the Goddess to slay the demon Hayagrīva. [regional]
- **Mahābhārata — the Nārāyaṇīya / Mokṣadharma of the Śānti Parva (Book 12)** — the early avatāra roster naming Hayaśiras/Hayagrīva; the Madhu–Kaiṭabha frame. See [The Nārāyaṇīya](./narayaniya.md).
- **Devī Māhātmya 1 (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa)** — the Madhu–Kaiṭabha slaying as the Goddess's act (variant of the same primordial episode). [regional]
- **Hayagrīva Upaniṣad** — minor Vaiṣṇava Upaniṣad with the *dhyāna* and mantras of Hayagrīva as imparter of *brahmavidyā*. [late text]
