# Vāc and the purchase of Soma — why women love song and dance

> *Where this sits: a deep-dive into the **somakrayaṇa** — the Brāhmaṇa etiology of the "buying of Soma" in the śrauta ritual, in which the gods fashion **Vāc (Speech)** into a woman to purchase the Soma-stalks from the woman-coveting **Gandharvas**, then win her back when she chooses song and dance over Veda-recitation. Primordial deva–gandharva contest; canonical home for the myth that [gandharvas-and-kinnaras](../08-beings-and-bestiary/gandharvas-and-kinnaras.md) treats only generically and [saraswati](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saraswati.md) treats only theologically. ⬆ Parent index: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Master index: [README](../README.md)*

The **somakrayaṇa** ("Soma-purchase") is one of the oddest moments in the classical Soma sacrifice: before it can be pressed, the King Soma must be *bought* — haggled over in a fixed dialogue, paid for with a tawny one-year-old cow (the *somakrayaṇī*), gold, cloth and a she-goat, and then, the price partly snatched back and the seller driven off with blows. The Brāhmaṇas explain this ritual anomaly with a myth that is at once a **charter for the buying-rite**, a **statement about where Soma came from** (it was with the Gandharvas, who hoard it and covet women), and a **gnomic etiology of female nature** — the story of how Vāc, Speech herself, was turned into a woman, sold, and re-won by the lute and the dance. The three principal recensions — [Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa](../90-literature-corpus/vedas/brahmanas/shatapatha-brahmana.md) 3.2.4, the [Taittirīya Saṃhitā](../90-literature-corpus/vedas/yajurveda.md) 6.1.6, and the [Aitareya Brāhmaṇa](../90-literature-corpus/vedas/brahmanas/aitareya-brahmana.md) 1.27 — agree on the skeleton and diverge in the details, and this file catalogs both.

---

## Dramatis personae

- **Vāc (Speech)** — the feminine deity of speech, already a great cosmogonic power in the Ṛgveda (RV 10.125, the *Devī-sūkta*, "I move with the Rudras and Vasus…"), and in the Brāhmaṇas progressively identified with **[Sarasvatī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saraswati.md)**. Here she is the *price* (*krayaṇa*) paid for Soma — the gods "make her a woman" and hand her over. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 3.2.4; Taittirīya Saṃhitā 6.1.6)
- **The Gandharvas** — the celestial musicians who in Vedic thought are the **guardians of Soma** and are proverbially *strī-kāma*, "lovers of women"; it is precisely this weakness the gods exploit. Their standing Soma-knower is **Viśvāvasu**, the gandharva of the marriage-hymn who is the bride's prior claimant. (see [Gandharvas & Kinnaras](../08-beings-and-bestiary/gandharvas-and-kinnaras.md); Ṛgveda 10.85; Atharvaveda)
- **The gods (devas)** — on earth, sacrificing; they lack Soma, which is "in the sky" / with the Gandharvas, and must contrive to fetch it and buy it. (see [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md))
- **Soma** — the pressed plant / entheogenic draught and its personified king, guarded aloft before the sacrifice can begin; this "purchase" is the ritual's way of bringing him down and into the sacrificer's possession. (see [Chandra (Soma)](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/chandra-soma.md))
- **The vīṇā (lute)** — created by the gods on the spot as the instrument of seduction; the myth is the Veda's own account of *why music wins*. (see [Vādya — sacred instruments](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/vadya-sacred-musical-instruments.md))

## 1. The ritual problem the myth explains

- **Soma must be bought, not simply taken** — in the Agniṣṭoma and all *soma-yāga*, the Soma-stalks are formally purchased from a **Soma-seller (soma-vikrayin)** in a scripted bargaining, the chief price being a **one-year-old tawny cow with reddish eyes**, the *somakrayaṇī*, supplemented by gold, a cloth, a she-goat and other goods. (Aitareya Brāhmaṇa 1.12–13; Taittirīya Saṃhitā 6.1.6; śrauta-sūtra practice)
- **The price is reclaimed and the seller struck** — after the sale the buyer **takes back part of the price** and the seller is struck / driven off — a scandalous-looking act the ritualists must justify. The myths below supply the reason: the Soma was never really the seller's, and the "woman" given for it was meant to be won back all along. (Aitareya Brāhmaṇa 1.12–13 for the reclaiming of the price; the **striking/driving-off of the seller** is a śrauta-sūtra feature — Kātyāyana Śrauta Sūtra 7.8.25 — not stated in the Brāhmaṇa itself)
- **Two etiological knots tied together** — the Brāhmaṇas fuse (a) *how Soma came down at all* — the metres, led by **Gāyatrī**, fly to heaven and fetch it — with (b) *why buying it involves a woman and a swindle* — the Vāc-for-Soma exchange with the Gandharvas. This file treats the second; the metre-flight is its immediate frame in TS 6.1.6. (Taittirīya Saṃhitā 6.1.6; Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 3.2.4 [note the same numbering also carries the Gāyatrī-Soma theft]) [scholarly]

## 2. The myth in the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa (3.2.4.1–7)

- **Vāc is the buying-price** — the Brāhmaṇa states flatly that **Speech was that with which the Soma was bought**; the whole rite is read back onto her. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 3.2.4.1–2)
- **The Gandharvas hold the Soma** — the Gandharvas guard the Soma, and the gods deliberate how to get it from them. Knowing the Gandharvas are **fond of women**, they resolve to use Vāc as bait. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 3.2.4.3)
- **Speech made a woman, sent, and returned with Soma** — the gods **fashion Vāc into a woman** and send her to the Gandharvas; she goes, and comes back to the gods **bringing the Soma with her** — the purchase accomplished, Speech exchanged for the draught. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 3.2.4.4)
- **The Gandharvas come to claim her** — the Gandharvas follow after and demand, "**The Soma is yours; let Vāc be ours.**" The gods reply: so be it — but if she would rather return to us, **do not carry her off by force; let us woo her for her.** The Gandharvas agree to a wooing-contest. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 3.2.4.5)
- **The contest — Veda versus the lute** — the **Gandharvas wooed her with the Veda**, reciting sacred lore: "Thus much we know, thus we recite." But the **gods created the lute (vīṇā)** and sat **singing and playing** before her: "Thus we will sing to you, thus we will delight you." (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 3.2.4.6)
- **She turns to song and dance — the moral** — **Vāc turned to the gods**, i.e. to the music; and, says the Brāhmaṇa, she thereby turned to the *frivolous* — "turning away from those reciting praise, toward dance and song." **Therefore even now women are given to vanity** (*strī-lampaṭa* things): for it was thus that Vāc first turned, and other women do as she did — and hence it is **the man who dances and sings** to whom women most readily take a fancy. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 3.2.4.6)
- **Application to the rite** — because Vāc was given for Soma and won back, the sacrificer likewise **hands over the price and takes it back**; the buying-rite re-enacts the gods' recovery of Speech. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 3.2.4.7)

## 3. The Taittirīya Saṃhitā variant (6.1.6.5–6)

- **Framed by the metre-flight** — in the Kṛṣṇa-Yajurvedic account the section opens with the metres fetching Soma from the far heaven (Gāyatrī succeeding where Jagatī and Triṣṭubh fail), and *then* turns to the purchase and the Gandharvas — so the Vāc-episode sits inside the larger "descent of Soma." (Taittirīya Saṃhitā 6.1.6.1–6) [scholarly]
- **Vāc given to the Gandharvas and won back by dance** — the TS agrees that the gods **gave Vāc** (as a woman) to the woman-loving Gandharvas in exchange for Soma and then **won her back with dance and song**; hence, in this recension too, **women are fond of the dancer and the singer**, and the buyer of Soma reclaims what he paid. (Taittirīya Saṃhitā 6.1.6.5–6)
  - *Variant (emphasis):* where the Śatapatha stresses the **lute** and the *contrast of Veda-recitation with music*, the Taittirīya foregrounds **dance** and the reclaiming of the price as the ritual point; the traditions are recorded side by side, not merged. (Taittirīya Saṃhitā 6.1.6; cf. Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 3.2.4)
- **Charter for reclaiming the somakrayaṇī** — the TS uses the tale to license the ritual **taking-back of the cow/price** from the Soma-seller: since the woman given for Soma was always to be recovered, so is the purchase-price. (Taittirīya Saṃhitā 6.1.6.6)

## 4. The Aitareya Brāhmaṇa — the buying-rite (1.12–13) and the unfit seller (1.27 for the Vāc-myth)

- **The technical somakrayaṇa** — Aitareya Brāhmaṇa 1.12–13 (in the Agniṣṭoma section) prescribes the **purchase of King Soma**: the graded price (gold, the she-goat, cloth, and above all the **cow**), the bargaining formulae, and the physical handling of the Soma-bundle. Haug's own cross-reference for the buying of the Soma points here ("see 1, 12–13"). (Aitareya Brāhmaṇa 1.12–13)
- **Why the seller goes empty-handed** — because the Soma **truly belongs to the gods** and cannot really be sold, the buyer **takes back the price** and the seller is treated as **unfit** — in Haug's note, not admitted to the sacrificial compound — the swindle sanctioned by the divine precedent that the price given (Vāc) was meant to return to the giver. (Aitareya Brāhmaṇa 1.12–13; in the Vāc-myth of 1.27 it is the **cow**, the price, that is driven away and re-bought) [the prominent **beating/driving-off of the Soma-seller** is *not* the Aitareya's but a śrauta-sūtra feature — Kātyāyana Śrauta Sūtra 7.8.25, in the Śukla-Yajurveda tradition]
  - *Variant (the seller's identity):* later śrauta practice variously casts the Soma-seller as a **śūdra** or an outsider figure, expressly so that the cheating/beating falls on one outside the sacrificial community — an application the Brāhmaṇa myth underwrites but does not itself spell out. [scholarly] [regional/ritual-school variation]
- **Vāc behind the price** — the Aitareya's treatment stands in the same complex: the exchange-and-recovery it ritualizes is the one the Śatapatha and Taittirīya narrate mythically as the giving and winning-back of Speech. (Aitareya Brāhmaṇa 1.27; cf. Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 3.2.4)

## 5. Meaning, afterlife, and the theology of Speech

- **A charter myth, three ways at once** — the tale simultaneously justifies (a) *buying* Soma, (b) *taking back the price / beating the seller*, and (c) the gnomic wisdom that **women love song, dance, and the frivolous** and may be won by a singer — one of the Veda's clearest "just-so" etiologies of a social attitude. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 3.2.4; Taittirīya Saṃhitā 6.1.6)
- **Speech is worth a god's draught** — read theologically, the myth prices **Vāc equal to Soma itself**: Speech is the very medium of the sacrifice, so the gods can offer nothing less for the immortal draught, and cannot finally do without her — hence the insistence on winning her back. This coheres with the Brāhmaṇas' larger elevation of Vāc as a cosmic first principle. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa; cf. RV 10.125) — see [Sarasvatī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saraswati.md).
- **Vāc → Sarasvatī** — the same Śatapatha layer that tells this story elsewhere **equates Vāc with Sarasvatī**; the goddess of speech who later presides over the vīṇā, music and learning is here the very stake fought over by reciters and musicians — a resonance the tradition itself would develop. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa; later Purāṇic Sarasvatī) [scholarly bridge]
- **Gandharvas, Soma, and women** — the myth crystallizes the Vedic bundle in which the **gandharva is Soma-guardian, secret-knower, and the bride's supernatural first claimant** (Viśvāvasu of RV 10.85, dismissed so the human groom may take the bride): the Gandharvas' claim on Vāc-the-woman is the same claim they press on every bride. (Ṛgveda 10.85; Atharvaveda) — see [Gandharvas & Kinnaras](../08-beings-and-bestiary/gandharvas-and-kinnaras.md) and [Apsarās](../08-beings-and-bestiary/apsaras.md); cf. [saṃvāda hymns](../90-literature-corpus/vedas/rigveda-samvada-and-secular-hymns.md).
- **The invention of the lute** — the passage is also a small **origin-of-music** notice: the gods *make* the vīṇā expressly to seduce Speech, so classical Hindu music theory can trace the instrument's dignity to a divine, sacrificial first use. (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 3.2.4.6) — see [Vādya — sacred musical instruments](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/vadya-sacred-musical-instruments.md).

## 6. The two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic frame** — the episode belongs to the **primordial establishment of the yajña**, the timeless deva–gandharva stratum "before" the ordered world of men, and is *re-enacted in every Soma sacrifice* — a recurring, not a one-time, event: each somakrayaṇa performs again the giving and recovery of Vāc. There is no yuga-date; on the cosmic clock it sits with the founding of the sacrifice. (Śatapatha, Taittirīya, Aitareya Brāhmaṇas)
- **Academic / historical frame** — [scholarly] these are **Brāhmaṇa-prose** compositions of the Vedic ritual period: the Taittirīya Saṃhitā's Brāhmaṇa portions are conventionally placed **c. 1000–800 BCE**, the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa **c. 800–600 BCE**, and the Śatapatha (Mādhyandina) among the **latest**, **c. 700–600 BCE** or later — so the three retell an inherited ritual myth across the mature Vedic corpus. The "Soma" being bought is the still-unresolved botanical/entheogenic Soma of the Ṛgveda, not the later Purāṇic moon-god. Never collapse the ritual-eternal frame into the text-historical one.
