# Gautama's curse on the ungrateful brahmins

> *Cosmic address: a **curse-etiology of the current [7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)** whose fruit ripens forward into the **[Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md)** — the **[Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/devi-bhagavata-upapurana.md)'s** Śākta explanation for why brāhmaṇas of the Kali age abandon **Gāyatrī / Devī** worship. The Sage **Gautama** feeds the starving brāhmaṇas through a famine by the Devī's grace, is framed with an illusory dying cow, and curses his ingrates to become Devī-averse and heterodox. Source: Devī-Bhāgavata XII.8–9.*
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Master index: [README](../README.md)

A short, sharp **polemical legend** told in the Devī-Bhāgavata's Gāyatrī-glorification (Book XII, the Devī-Māhātmya matter that follows the [Devī Gītā](devi-gita.md)). Its purpose is not narrative but **etiological**: the Śākta author needs to answer a hostile question — *if Gāyatrī/Devī worship is the highest, why do so many brāhmaṇas of the present age ignore it and drift into other cults?* — and answers with a **curse**. The Sage **Gautama** (the Āṅgirasa Saptarṣi) keeps ten thousand brāhmaṇas alive through a long famine by the boon of **Gāyatrī Devī**; the moment the rains return, the fed brāhmaṇas repay him with **jealousy and a fabricated cow-slaughter charge**, and the outraged sage curses them to become **averse to the Devī, to Gāyatrī, and to the whole Vedic order** — the doctrinal charter for Kali-age brahmanical decline. This node is the canonical home of that Śākta version; it is **carefully distinct** from the identically-motifed **[Godāvarī / Gautamī descent legend](../06-sacred-geography/godavari-gautami-dakshina-ganga.md)** (the *Brahma Purāṇa's* māyā-cow that ends in a river, not a curse) and from the **[Ahalyā episode](gautama-ahalya.md)** (which carries no famine matter at all).

---

## The frame — why the tale exists

- **A Śākta apologetic, not a hero-tale** — the legend sits inside the **Gāyatrī-māhātmya** of Devī-Bhāgavata Book XII, whose whole burden is to enthrone **Gāyatrī = Mūla-Prakṛti = Śrī Devī** as the supreme reality. The Gautama story is deployed as the *pratyavāya*-answer: neglect of the Devī in the present age is not the Devī's weakness but the working-out of an ancient **brahmin curse**. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.8–9) [scholarly]
- **The Nārada frame** — the tale is set within Nārada's visit: having heard **Gautama's** fame praised by **Indra** in the divine assembly, **Nārada** enters the **temple of Devī Gāyatrī**, beholds the Goddess, and the deeds of Gautama are recounted in her praise. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.8)
- **The polemical target** — the curse is aimed squarely at brāhmaṇas who follow **Kāpālikas, Bauddha śāstras, and pāṣaṇḍa (heretical) doctrines**, or who worship "other" deities in place of Gāyatrī — i.e. the sectarian rivals of the text's own Śākta milieu. The story converts a live sectarian complaint into sacred history. [scholarly]
- **A standing genre** — the "ungrateful-beneficiary cursed by his benefactor" pattern recurs across the Purāṇas; compare **[Śukra's curse on the ungrateful Daityas](bhrigus-curse-on-vishnu.md)** and the brahmin-legend catalogues ([Brāhmaṇa legends](brahmana-legends-catalog.md), [Pitṛ-kalpa seven brahmins](pitrikalpa-seven-brahmins-brahmadatta.md)) — but here the ingratitude is weaponized specifically to explain **Devī-aversion**. [scholarly]

## Two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic frame** — the events belong to the deep *ṛṣi*-past of the present **[Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)**; the curse is explicitly *forward-dated*: the guilty brāhmaṇas suffer in **Kumbhīpāka hell until Śrī Kṛṣṇa's incarnation** (the [Dvāpara–Kali junction](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md), **Kali beginning 3102 BCE**), then are reborn **in the Kali age** stripped of the three sandhyās and of devotion to Gāyatrī — so the tale's "present" is the reader's own degraded age.
- **Academic / historical frame** — the **Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa** is a **Śākta [upapurāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/devi-bhagavata-upapurana.md)**, dated **[scholarly] c. 9th–12th CE** (Bengal/eastern-India milieu), later than the Vaiṣṇava *Bhāgavata* it answers; this Gāyatrī-māhātmya matter is among its characteristically **late, sectarian-polemical** strata. [late text]

## Gautama and the name-collisions to keep straight

- **Who this Gautama is** — the sage of the legend is **Gautama (Gotama) Maharṣi**, the **Āṅgirasa [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md)** of the current manvantara — the same seer treated on his ṛṣi-side node with the **[Ahalyā episode](gautama-ahalya.md)**. The Devī-Bhāgavata makes him a great **Gāyatrī-devotee**, which the Ahalyā material does not foreground. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.8)
- **Not the Ahalyā story** — the [Ahalyā deep-dive](gautama-ahalya.md) (Indra's seduction, the double curse, Rāma's redemption) contains **no famine, no feeding, and no illusory cow of this kind**; the two Gautama-tales share only the sage. (Do not import Ahalyā's "circling-a-calving-cow" ritual technicality here — that is a *different* cow-motif in the Ahalyā backstory.) [scholarly]
- **Not the Godāvarī descent** — the **māyā-cow that frames Gautama for go-hatyā** is *also* the engine of the **[Godāvarī / Gautamī river-descent](../06-sacred-geography/godavari-gautami-dakshina-ganga.md)** in the *Brahma Purāṇa's* **Gautamī-māhātmya** — but that version resolves into **Śiva releasing the Gaṅgā from his locks** as the Dakṣiṇa Gaṅgā, a *tīrtha*-etiology with **no anti-brahmin curse**. The Devī-Bhāgavata borrows the same framing-device and steers it to the **opposite payoff** — not a holy river but a curse of Devī-aversion. The differentiation is set out in full in §"The two māyā-cow legends" below. (*Brahma Purāṇa* vs *Devī-Bhāgavata*) [scholarly]

## The tale — Devī-Bhāgavata XII.8–9

- **The great famine** — a catastrophic **drought (anāvṛṣṭi)** — a **twelve-year** famine in the telling (a **fifteen-year** span in some recensions) — strikes the land, and starving **brāhmaṇas** stream toward the hermitage of **Gautama**, whose āśrama alone remains unblighted. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.8) [disputed — famine-length varies by recension]
- **Gāyatrī's boon of the inexhaustible vessel** — Gautama worships **Devī Gāyatrī** in intense devotional trance; pleased, she grants him a **vessel (pātra) full to the brim that yields whatever he wishes** — an Akṣaya-pātra-like cornucopia by which **all comers can be fed without limit**. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.8)
- **Ten thousand brāhmaṇas fed through the famine** — by this grace Gautama **feeds the assembled brāhmaṇas and munis — traditionally ten thousand — throughout the twelve famine-years**, so that his āśrama swells into a refuge whose **boundary is said to extend a hundred yojanas**, and his fame reaches Indra's court. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.8)
- **The honour that breeds envy** — sustained and sheltered for years, the fed brāhmaṇas nonetheless grow **jealous of the honour** heaped on their host, and — when good rains at last return and harvests are abundant everywhere — they **resolve to leave** rather than continue under his roof. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.9)
- **Gautama's forbearance** — in one strand, when the brāhmaṇas first move to depart in resentment, the Muni receives them **graciously**, telling them not to fear and thanking them for **blessing his house with the dust of their feet** — his generosity redoubling their spite rather than shaming it. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.9)
- **The illusory dying cow (māyā-dhenu)** — conspiring, the brāhmaṇas **fashion by māyā an aged, dying cow** and drive her into Gautama's **sacrificial hall (homa/yajña-śālā)** during his fire-rite; when Gautama **cries out and drives her off**, the phantom cow **collapses dead**, and the brāhmaṇas at once **charge him with go-hatyā** — cow-slaughter, a cardinal sin — the very men he had fed now turning his ritual purity into a weapon against him. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.9)
- **The betrayal named** — the *point* the text hammers is the **ingratitude**: the beneficiaries repay twelve years of famine-relief with a **fabricated capital charge**, making the story an indictment of a brahminhood that has forgotten *dharma* even while professing it. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.9)

## The curse itself

- **The sage's fury** — recognizing both the fraud and the ingratitude, **Gautama curses** the assembled brāhmaṇas with an elaborate, cascading anathema — the theological centrepiece of the whole legend. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.9)
- **Averse to the Devī and Gāyatrī** — they and their line shall become **incapable of meditating on or reciting Gāyatrī**, and **averse to Mūla-Prakṛti Śrī Devī** — to her **dhyāna, mantra, and pūjā** and to her temple-worship. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.9)
- **Averse to the Vedic order** — they shall grow **indifferent to Vedic study, to the three sandhyās, to the agnihotra and Vedic sacrifice**, to **sense-restraint** and the **pitṛ (ancestral) rites** — the whole apparatus of orthodox brahmanical duty falling away. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.9)
- **Turned to heterodoxy** — they shall instead **follow the Kāpālikas, the Bauddha śāstras, and other pāṣaṇḍa (heretical) doctrines**, and **worship other deities**, losing faith in Gāyatrī altogether. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.9) [the sectarian sting of the curse]
- **Moral degradation** — they shall sink to **licentiousness (consorting with others' wives)** and to **selling their families, the Vedas, and dharma itself** — the outward Veda retained as a trade while its inward force is lost. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.9)
- **The karmic sentence and forward-dating** — the cursed brāhmaṇas shall fall into **[Kumbhīpāka hell](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/narakas-and-afterlife.md)** and remain there **until Śrī Kṛṣṇa takes his incarnation**, then be **reborn on earth in the Kali age, devoid of the three sandhyās and of devotion to Gāyatrī** — precisely the class of nominal brāhmaṇa the text observes in its own day. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* XII.9)
- **The etiological payoff** — the curse thus *becomes* the answer to the framing question: the Devī-neglecting brāhmaṇa of the Kali age is the **reborn ingrate of Gautama's famine**, his aversion a curse working itself out, not a verdict on the Goddess. (compare the *prāyaścitta* / sin-and-fruit logic in [Pāpa, Puṇya & Prāyaścitta](../05-concepts-and-dharma/papa-punya-and-prayaschitta.md)) [scholarly]

## The two māyā-cow legends — a required disambiguation

- **Shared device, opposite ends** — both the *Brahma Purāṇa's* **[Gautamī-māhātmya](../06-sacred-geography/godavari-gautami-dakshina-ganga.md)** and this *Devī-Bhāgavata* legend open the same way: a **famine at Gautama's āśrama**, the sage **feeding the starving sages** by a divine boon, the sages' **jealousy** when the rains return, and their **illusory dying cow** driving a **false go-hatyā charge**. The traditions diverge decisively on the resolution. [scholarly]
  - *Brahma Purāṇa (Gautamī-māhātmya):* the grain-boon is from **Varuṇa**; the framing drives Gautama to **fierce tapas to Śiva at Brahmagiri**, and Śiva **looses the Gaṅgā from his matted locks** — the **Godāvarī / Gautamī Gaṅgā**, the **Dakṣiṇa Gaṅgā** — clearing his sin and blessing the Deccan. The jealous sages' malice becomes a **tīrtha**; there is **no curse on them**. (Told in full at [Godāvarī — the Gautamī & the Dakṣiṇa Gaṅgā](../06-sacred-geography/godavari-gautami-dakshina-ganga.md) §II; catalogue at [Gautamī-māhātmya tales](gautami-mahatmya-tales-catalog.md).)
  - *Devī-Bhāgavata XII.8–9 (this file):* the grain-boon is from **Gāyatrī Devī** (the inexhaustible vessel); the framing drives Gautama to **curse the brāhmaṇas** into Devī-aversion, heterodoxy, hell, and Kali-age rebirth. The payoff is **not a river but a polemic** — a Śākta charter for brahmanical decline. **No Godāvarī, no Śiva, no jaṭā-Gaṅgā.**
- **Why they must not be merged** — the corpus rule is that **variants are catalogued, never resolved**: these are two *different traditions* (a Smārta/tīrtha *sthala-purāṇa* vs a Śākta sectarian apologetic) that have **repurposed one folk-motif** — the framing of a righteous sage with a phantom cow — toward incompatible doctrinal ends. Reading either into the other collapses a real sectarian disagreement. [scholarly]
