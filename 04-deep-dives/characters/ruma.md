# Rumā — Sugrīva's Queen, and the Charge on Which Vālī Died

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Rumā** — wife of **Sugrīva**, queen of Kiṣkindhā, and the woman whose seizure by **Vālī** is the single specific offence [Rāma](./rama.md) names when he answers for killing him from concealment. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). Her whole textual life is four passages of the [Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md); the debate they sit inside is treated in full at [Sugrīva & Vālī §IX](./sugriva-and-vali.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* her life falls in the **Tretā Yuga** of the present 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara; the scholarly baseline is Vālmīki (vulgate/Southern sarga), the Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa belonging to the epic's core stratum. [scholarly]

## Name and identity

- **Rumā (रुमा)** — wife of **Sugrīva**, son of Sūrya, and queen of **Kiṣkindhā** after his enthronement; a vānarī of the Kiṣkindhā line (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa*). See the [vānaras & ṛkṣas roster](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/vanaras-and-rikshas.md) and [Kiṣkindhā](../../06-sacred-geography/kishkindha-hampi-anegundi.md).
- **No parentage, no speech, no scene** — Vālmīki names no father and no mother for her, gives her not one line of dialogue, and never once shows her alone. She is present in the epic four times and on every occasion as an object of the sentence: taken, cited, regained, embraced. Against **[Tārā](./tara-vanara-queen.md)** — who argues Vālī out of the first duel, negotiates with Lakṣmaṇa, and is counted among the **Pañcakanyā** — the contrast is stark and deliberate, and it is why she has been so easy to leave out. [scholarly]
- **Why she is load-bearing anyway** — the killing of Vālī is the *Rāmāyaṇa*'s most contested act, and Rāma's answer to it turns on one named woman. Remove Rumā and the defence has no specific charge left in it, only the general claim that a king may punish. She is silent and structural at once. [scholarly]

## The seizure (Kiṣkindhā 9–10)

- **The feud's origin** — Sugrīva tells Rāma the story himself: the asura **Māyāvī** (brother of Dundubhi, both sons of the dānava Maya) challenges Vālī by night; Vālī pursues him into a cavern and orders Sugrīva to wait at the mouth. After a year, blood foams out of the hole and only the demon's cries are heard; Sugrīva seals the opening with a boulder, performs the water-offering for his brother, and returns to Kiṣkindhā, where the ministers crown him (*Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa* 9).
- **Vālī's return and the double dispossession** — Vālī emerges alive, reads the crowning as usurpation, refuses every conciliation, and drives Sugrīva out with a single garment. Sugrīva's own summary of what followed is the epic's plainest statement of it: *"thus he cast me out and **stole my wife** as well, O Rāma; and for fear of him alone I roamed the whole earth with its forests and its seas"* (*Kiṣkindhā* **10.27**, *hṛta-dāraḥ ca*).
- **The one refuge** — only **Ṛṣyamūka** is closed to Vālī, by **Mataṅga's** curse that he will die on the spot if he comes within a *yojana* of the sage's grove; the exile lives there while his wife is held in his brother's house (*Kiṣkindhā* 9–11).

## The legal argument (Kiṣkindhā 18)

Vālī, shot from cover while duelling Sugrīva, delivers a formal indictment (*Kiṣkindhā* 17). **He never mentions Rumā** — the charge that will be laid against him is one he does not attempt to answer. Rāma's reply is *Kiṣkindhā* 18, and Rumā is at its centre.

- **The kinship rule (18.13–14)** — a man has **three fathers**: his own father, his elder brother, and the one who teaches him; correspondingly a **younger brother, a son, and a worthy disciple are to be reckoned one's own sons**. From this the offence follows arithmetically: the younger brother's wife stands to the elder as a son's wife stands to him.
- **The charge (18.18–19)** — *"you approached your brother's wife, forsaking the eternal way"*; and, exactly: *"while the great-souled Sugrīva yet lived, you, of sinful habit, lustily misbehaved with Sugrīva's wife **Rumā**, who is to be counted your **snuṣā** — your daughter-in-law."* The word is the whole argument: what Vālī has done is not adultery but incest by the rule of the household.
- **The sentence (18.21–23)** — Rāma holds that no control other than **punishment** exists for one who conducts himself against dharma, and that the śāstra's penalty for this transgression is death — the class of grave aggressor (*ātatāyin*) whom a king strikes on sight, without trial. The other three arms of the defence — Bharata's writ over the earth, the hunting of beasts by ambush, the promise given to Sugrīva — are treated at [Sugrīva & Vālī §IX](./sugriva-and-vali.md).
- **Vālī concedes (18.66)** — at the end of the exchange Vālī withdraws his own accusation and asks Rāma's pardon for having blamed him unthinkingly. The concession is on the record and is what makes the debate a debate rather than a verdict — later tradition has argued the ethics of the ambush ever since, but not the fact of the offence against Rumā. [disputed within tradition]

## Restored, and then the reason for the delay (Kiṣkindhā 26, 33)

- **The restoration (26.42)** — with Vālī dead, Sugrīva is consecrated at Kiṣkindhā and Aṅgada made *yuvarāja*; **Rumā is restored to him**, and the sarga closes on the pairing — *regaining Rumā, he regained the kingdom, like Indra chief of the gods*. Note that the rite itself is performed by the vānara chiefs **inside** the city: Rāma's exile-vow bars him and Lakṣmaṇa alike from entering any town, and both wait outside (*Kiṣkindhā* **26**). See [Aṅgada](./angada.md).
- **The forgetting (29–33)** — the monsoon sets in and Sugrīva, sunk in drink and music and the company of his two queens, forgets the vow to search for [Sītā](./sita.md); **Hanumān**'s counsel first rouses him (*Kiṣkindhā* 29), and when Lakṣmaṇa comes into the city in fury he finds the king seated in his inner apartments **embracing Rumā** amid his attendants (*Kiṣkindhā* **33.66**), Tārā having gone out to pacify the guest.
- **The symmetry the epic builds and does not comment on** — the woman whose recovery justified the killing becomes, in the very next movement, the reason the promise bought with that killing goes unkept. Vālmīki lets the two scenes stand thirty sargas apart and draws no moral from the pairing. [scholarly]

## Later tradition

- **Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa** — the devotional recension keeps Rumā in exactly the two positions Vālmīki gives her: named in Tārā's speech as part of what Sugrīva has to enjoy, and present when Lakṣmaṇa finds him. The bhakti layer expands Tārā and leaves Rumā where she was. See [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md). [late text]
  - *Variant (a courtship supplied):* a retelling strand gives her a father who refused the match and has Sugrīva carry her off with **Hanumān's** help. Vālmīki gives Rumā neither father nor courtship, and no Sanskrit recension carries the episode; it circulates chiefly in modern summary retellings. [folk] [disputed]
- **The modern recovery** — where Tārā is recovered as a stateswoman, Rumā is recovered precisely as the epic's clearest case of a woman who is all consequence and no voice: the *casus belli* of the Kiṣkindhā war who is never asked what she wants, either when she is taken or when she is given back. [scholarly]
