# Yuyutsu

> Where this sits: a **Dvāpara-era life** on the [Dvāpara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)→[Kali](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) cusp — the lone [Kaurava](./duryodhana.md) prince who crossed to the Pāṇḍavas before the [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/README.md) war, the only son of Dhṛtarāṣṭra to survive Kurukṣetra, and afterward steward-regent of Hastināpura. Two clocks: the epic narrates *itihāsa* (war trad. ~3138 BCE; Kali begins 3102 BCE); academic dating of the text differs.
> Up: [Epic Characters](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md).

**Yuyutsu** (Sanskrit *Yuyutsu*, "one who desires to fight / eager for battle," a desiderative of *yudh-*) is the Mahābhārata's great study in conscience over blood. He is a son of the blind king **Dhṛtarāṣṭra** — but not by Queen **Gāndhārī**; his mother is a **vaiśya serving-woman**, named **Sughadā** or **Sauvalī** in the tradition, who attended the queen during her ill-omened two-year pregnancy. Born the same season as the hundred Kauravas yet set apart by birth, Yuyutsu is the **only Kaurava prince who answers Yudhiṣṭhira's pre-war call for defectors and walks across to the Pāṇḍava side**, the **sole son of Dhṛtarāṣṭra to live through the eighteen-day war**, and the man Kṛṣṇa and Yudhiṣṭhira afterward leave in charge of **Hastināpura** as protector of the realm and, ultimately, of the infant heir **[Parikṣit](../mahabharata/README.md)**. His arc is the epic's quiet counter-melody to [Duryodhana](./duryodhana.md): the brother who chose dharma.

---

## I. Birth — the vaiśya maid's son (Mbh Ādi Parva ~115)

- **Born during Gāndhārī's failed pregnancy** — While Gāndhārī carries her hard ball of flesh (the *māṃsapeśī* that Vyāsa will split into the hundred Kauravas), Dhṛtarāṣṭra is attended in that interval by a **vaiśya (merchant-caste) serving-woman**; of this union is born **Yuyutsu**, of an age with the Kaurava brothers but of mixed birth. (Mbh Ādi Parva, Sambhava-parva ~115)
- **His mother's name** — The maid is generally named **Sughadā** in the tradition; some recensions and retellings give **Sauvalī** (also rendered Saubali), a name elsewhere attached to the same servant. (Mbh Ādi Parva)
  - *Variant (Sughadā vs Sauvalī):* the catalog gives both forms for the same vaiśya woman; "Sauvalī"/"Saubali" recurs in Purāṇic genealogies of the Kuru line, "Sughadā" in epic-summary tradition — never two distinct mothers, only two names for one. [disputed]
- **A son but not a Kaurava heir** — Because his mother is a śūdra-status/vaiśya serving-woman, Yuyutsu is counted a son of Dhṛtarāṣṭra but **stands outside the hundred Kauravas** and outside the throne-claim — structurally the same throne-barring-by-birth that defines [Vidura](./vidura.md), one generation earlier in the very same house. (Mbh Ādi Parva)
  - *Note (the recurring motif):* the Kuru line repeatedly produces its **wisest/most-righteous males from serving-women** — Vidura (incarnate Dharma) from a maid in the niyoga, Yuyutsu the lone just Kaurava from a vaiśya maid — a deliberate epic pattern of merit divorced from birth-rank.

## II. The boy who tried to save the Pāṇḍavas (Mbh Ādi Parva)

- **An ally inside the hostile court** — Raised alongside the hundred sons of Gāndhārī, Yuyutsu is the one brother **not party to Duryodhana's malice**; the epic notes him as righteous and friendly to the Pāṇḍavas from boyhood. (Mbh Ādi Parva)
- **Warner against the poisoning of Bhīma** — In several tellings Yuyutsu is the Kaurava insider who **alerts the Pāṇḍavas (or Vidura) to Duryodhana's plots** against the young Bhīma — the poisoned food and the binding-and-drowning at Pramāṇakoṭi — quietly working against his half-brothers' schemes. (Mbh Ādi Parva)
  - *Variant [folk]:* the explicit "Yuyutsu tipped off the Pāṇḍavas about the poison" detail is strong in retellings and summaries but lightly drawn in the critical text, where his decisive moral act is reserved for the eve of war; treat the boyhood warnings as the tradition's foreshadowing of his great defection. [folk]

## III. The defection — crossing to the Pāṇḍavas (Mbh Bhīṣma Parva ~1, before the battle)

- **Yudhiṣṭhira's open call for defectors** — On the first day, with both armies arrayed at Kurukṣetra, **Yudhiṣṭhira lays down his weapons, crosses the field on foot to seek the blessings of Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Kṛpa, and Śalya**, and then **publicly invites anyone on the Kaurava side who wishes to join him to come over** — declaring he will accept such a one as his own. (Mbh [Bhīṣma Parva](../mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md) ~43; Jambūkhaṇḍa/Bhūmi section preceding the battle proper)
- **Yuyutsu alone answers** — Of the entire Kaurava host, **only Yuyutsu responds.** He renounces Duryodhana's cause aloud and **walks across to the Pāṇḍava army**, the single defector of the war; Yudhiṣṭhira welcomes him with joy, and Yuyutsu fights the eighteen days **on the Pāṇḍava side**. (Mbh [Bhīṣma Parva](../mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md) ~43)
- **The dharma-choice that defines him** — His crossing is the epic's purest dramatization of **conscience over clan**: blood, court, and father all pull one way, righteousness the other, and Yuyutsu — uniquely among a hundred-and-one brothers — chooses dharma. (Mbh Bhīṣma Parva)
  - *Variant (the trigger):* some retellings frame his defection as the culmination of his lifelong disgust at Duryodhana's adharma (lac-house, dice, Draupadī's disrobing); the critical text simply records that, at Yudhiṣṭhira's invitation, Yuyutsu alone came over. [folk]

## IV. The war and the sole survivor (Mbh Bhīṣma → Śalya → Sauptika Parvas)

- **Fights for the Pāṇḍavas** — Yuyutsu serves through the battle on Yudhiṣṭhira's side, a competent but not front-rank warrior; the epic keeps him alive while it kills, one by one, **all hundred sons of Gāndhārī** (Bhīma's vow). (Mbh war parvas)
- **The only son of Dhṛtarāṣṭra to survive** — When the dust settles, **every Kaurava is dead** — Duryodhana, Duḥśāsana, and the ninety-eight others by Bhīma — and **Yuyutsu alone of Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sons lives**, precisely because he had crossed sides. He is spared even the night-massacre of the [Sauptika](../mahabharata/README.md) raid, sleeping outside the doomed camp. (Mbh Strī/Śalya Parvas)
  - *Note (a grim symmetry):* the Pāṇḍavas keep only **five sons** plus a few; the Kauravas keep only **one** — and that one is the brother who left them. The survival is the moral verdict made flesh.

## V. Steward of Hastināpura & guardian of Parikṣit (Mbh Strī → Śānti → Āśramavāsika → Mausala Parvas)

- **Entrusted with the city in mourning** — In the war's aftermath, with the realm gutted of its men, **Yuyutsu is given charge of the surviving women, the treasury, and the day-to-day governance of Hastināpura** as Yudhiṣṭhira assumes the throne; he becomes the practical administrator of a depopulated kingdom. (Mbh Strī/Śānti Parvas)
- **Regent during the forest-retreat** — When the aged **Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Gāndhārī, Kuntī, and [Vidura](./vidura.md)** withdraw to the forest (*vānaprastha*) and later the Pāṇḍavas themselves travel to visit them, **Yuyutsu is repeatedly left to look after the city and the people** — Yudhiṣṭhira's trusted caretaker-regent. (Mbh [Āśramavāsika Parva](../mahabharata/README.md))
  - *Variant:* he is also depicted ministering to the old blind king's needs in the forest-retreat period, the dutiful surviving son to a father who had lost all hundred others. [regional]
- **Caretaker of Parikṣit's realm** — At the very end, when the Pāṇḍavas renounce the world and set out on the **Great Departure (Mahāprasthāna)**, they install **Parikṣit** (Abhimanyu's posthumous son) on the throne — and **Yuyutsu, the senior survivor of the elder Kuru house, is left as protector/steward of the young king and his kingdom** alongside Kṛpa's tutelage. (Mbh [Mausala](../mahabharata/README.md)/Mahāprasthānika Parva)
- **Last of the elder generation** — Yuyutsu thus outlives the entire Kaurava–Pāṇḍava war-generation in office, the bridge between the war that ends the Dvāpara and the reign of [Parikṣit](../mahabharata/README.md) that opens the Kali age. (Mbh, closing parvas)
  - *Variant [late text]:* a few Purāṇic notices extend his role further into the early Kali transition; the epic's own last word leaves him guarding Parikṣit's Hastināpura. [late text]

## VI. Family & names

- **Father:** Dhṛtarāṣṭra. **Mother:** the vaiśya serving-woman **Sughadā / Sauvalī**. **Half-brothers:** Duryodhana and the ninety-nine [Kauravas](./duryodhana.md) (by Gāndhārī); the sole Kaurava sister **Duḥśalā**. (Mbh Ādi Parva)
- **Epithets** — Called **Dhārtarāṣṭra** (son of Dhṛtarāṣṭra) like his brothers, and in the Purāṇic genealogies **Sauvalāyana / Saubalya** after his mother — marking exactly the birth that set him apart. (Mbh; Purāṇic vaṃśa lists)
- **Place in the vaṃśa** — Through Yuyutsu the Kuru elder-line technically survives the war in a male body, though sovereignty passes through the Pāṇḍava–Abhimanyu–Parikṣit line; he is a survivor, not a successor. (Mbh; cf. [lineages](../../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md))

## VII. Themes & afterlife

- **Conscience over clan** — Yuyutsu is the canonical answer to "could even one Kaurava have chosen rightly?" — the single defection that proves the war was a moral and not merely a dynastic choice. (Mbh Bhīṣma Parva)
- **Survival as vindication** — In an epic where death is the wage of adharma, the lone Kaurava who lives is precisely the one who refused adharma; his survival is itself the verdict. [scholarly]
- **The serving-woman's righteous son** — He completes a deliberate pattern with [Vidura](./vidura.md): the Kuru house's most just males are born of maids and barred from the throne, a standing rebuke to birth-pride in the very dynasty obsessed with it. [scholarly]
- **Cross-links** — His brother and foil [Duryodhana](./duryodhana.md); the elder serving-woman's son [Vidura](./vidura.md); the king who welcomed him [Yudhiṣṭhira](./yudhishthira.md); and the war-book of his defection, [06 — Bhīṣma Parva](../mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md).
