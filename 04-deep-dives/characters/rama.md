# Rāma

> Where this sits: canonical character hub for **Rāma** (Rāmacandra, Rāghava, Maryādā Puruṣottama) — prince of Ayodhyā, seventh avatāra of Viṣṇu, and protagonist of the *Rāmāyaṇa*. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md). This page covers his names, ethics, recensions, and worship-afterlife; the **narrative** lives in the seven kāṇḍa files of the [Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md) — [Bāla](../ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md), [Ayodhyā](../ramayana/2-ayodhya-kanda.md), [Araṇya](../ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md), [Kiṣkindhā](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md), [Sundara](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md), [Yuddha](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md), [Uttara](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* Rāma's life falls in the **[Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the present 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara — tradition places him near the Tretā's end. The academic frame instead dates the Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa*'s core (Kāṇḍas 2–6) to roughly the 7th–4th centuries BCE, with the Bāla and Uttara Kāṇḍas as later strata. [scholarly]

## Names, epithets, and titles
- **Rāma** — "the pleasing / delightful one," from the root *ram* (to delight); the name is itself treated as a mantra (see Rāma-nāma below) and appears among the thousand names of Viṣṇu (*Viṣṇu Sahasranāma* — see [the stotra file](../stotras/vishnu-sahasranama.md)).
- **Rāmacandra** — "Rāma the moon(-like)," the common devotional form of the name; largely a Purāṇic and medieval usage rather than Vālmīki's own idiom. [late text]
- **Rāghava / Rāghavendra** — "descendant of [Raghu](../raghu.md)," after the great ancestor of the Solar line; the dynasty itself becomes the *Raghuvaṃśa* (see [Kālidāsa's Raghuvaṃśa kings](../../03-lineages-vamsha/raghuvamsha-kalidasa-kings.md)) (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*).
- **Dāśarathi / Kausalyā-nandana** — "son of [Daśaratha](./dasharatha.md)," "joy of [Kausalyā](./kausalya.md)" (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*).
- **Kākutstha / Aikṣvāka** — after the ancestors Kakutstha and Ikṣvāku of the [Sūryavaṃśa (Solar dynasty)](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*).
- **Sītāpati / Jānakī-vallabha** — "lord/beloved of [Sītā](./sita.md)" — the pair is worshipped as a unit, Sītā-Rāma (*devotional usage*).
- **Kodaṇḍa-Rāma / Kodaṇḍa-pāṇi** — "Rāma with the bow (kodaṇḍa)," his defining iconographic attribute (see Iconography below).
- **Raghunātha / Raghupati / Rājā-Rāma** — "lord of the Raghus," the royal titles central to North Indian bhakti and to the *Rāmcaritmānas* (*Tulsīdās and bhakti usage*). [late text]
- **Maryādā Puruṣottama** — "the supreme person of maryādā (bounded propriety/decorum)": the epithet crystallizing his character as the man who never oversteps dharma's limits even at devastating personal cost; the phrase as a fixed title belongs to later devotional tradition rather than Vālmīki's text. [late text]

## Identity on the cosmic clock
- **Seventh avatāra of Viṣṇu** — in the standard *daśāvatāra* list Rāma is the seventh descent, following Vāmana and Paraśurāma and preceding Kṛṣṇa; the thumbnail sits in the [Daśāvatāra roster](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) (*Purāṇic daśāvatāra lists; Bhāgavata Purāṇa*).
- **Purpose of the descent** — Rāvaṇa's boon from Brahmā made him invulnerable to devas, dānavas, and other celestial beings — but he disdained to name humans; Viṣṇu therefore takes **human** birth to slay him, the gods petitioning Viṣṇu while Daśaratha's *putrakāmeṣṭi* sacrifice (conducted through [Ṛśyaśṛṅga](../rishyashringa.md)) yields the divine pāyasa that Daśaratha's queens consume (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa*). For the deeper curse-history of the line see [Anaraṇya's curse on Rāvaṇa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/anaranya-ravana-curse.md).
- **Birth** — born at [Ayodhyā](../../06-sacred-geography/ayodhya.md) to Daśaratha and Kausalyā on **Caitra śukla navamī** (celebrated as Rāma Navamī), with brothers [Lakṣmaṇa](./lakshmana.md), [Bharata, and Śatrughna](./bharata-shatrughna-and-their-wives.md) born of [Sumitrā](./sumitra.md) and [Kaikeyī](./kaikeyi.md) (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa*).
- **Tretā Yuga placement** — tradition sets his career in the later Tretā Yuga; Purāṇic framing treats the avatāra as recurring in the Tretā of successive cycles. Attempts to fix a calendar year by archaeoastronomy circulate widely but have no scholarly consensus. [disputed]
- **Academic clock** — historical-critical scholarship treats Rāma as an epic hero whose story was composed and expanded c. 7th c. BCE – 3rd c. CE (core books first; Bāla and Uttara Kāṇḍas, where his divinity is most explicit, judged later strata in the Baroda Critical Edition tradition). [scholarly]

## Life in one glance (narrative lives elsewhere)
- **[Bāla Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md)** — birth; tutelage under Viśvāmitra; slaying of [Tāṭakā](./tataka.md); redemption of [Ahalyā](./ahalya.md); breaking Śiva's bow at Mithilā and wedding Sītā (see also [Janaka's Videha line](../../03-lineages-vamsha/videha-mithila-line-janaka.md)).
- **[Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/2-ayodhya-kanda.md)** — Kaikeyī's two boons; Rāma accepts 14 years' exile without protest; Bharata's regency with Rāma's sandals; [Guha](./guha.md) ferries the exiles; Citrakūṭa sojourn ([the hill itself](../../06-sacred-geography/chitrakoot-kamadgiri.md)).
- **[Araṇya Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md)** — Daṇḍaka forest years ([the terrain](../../06-sacred-geography/dandakaranya-rama-forest.md)); [Śūrpaṇakhā](./shurpanakha.md); the golden deer [Mārīca](./maricha.md); Sītā's abduction by [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md); [Jaṭāyu's](./jatayu-and-sampati.md) fall; [Śabarī's](./shabari.md) hospitality.
- **[Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md)** — alliance with [Sugrīva; the slaying of Vālī](./sugriva-and-vali.md) (ethics below); the search mustered.
- **[Sundara](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md) & [Yuddha Kāṇḍas](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md)** — [Hanumān's](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md) leap; the bridge at [Rāmeśvaram / Setubandha](../../06-sacred-geography/rameswaram-setu-bandha.md); [Vibhīṣaṇa's](./vibhishana.md) defection; the fall of [Indrajit](./indrajit-meghnad.md), Kumbhakarṇa, and Rāvaṇa; the agni-parīkṣā; return to Ayodhyā and coronation (Rāma-paṭṭābhiṣeka).
- **[Uttara Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md)** — Rāma-rājya; Sītā's banishment; [Lava & Kuśa](../uttara-ramayana-lava-kusha.md); Sītā's return to the Earth; Rāma's own departure into the [Sarayū](../../06-sacred-geography/sarayu-ayodhya-river.md), resuming his Viṣṇu-form. [late text]

## Maryādā Puruṣottama — the ethics debates
The tradition itself, not just modern critics, interrogates Rāma's hardest choices; the epic stages the objections in the mouths of his victims and family.

- **"I think myself a man"** — before the agni-parīkṣā the assembled gods hail Rāma as Viṣṇu, and he answers *ātmānaṁ mānuṣaṁ manye* — "I hold myself a man, Rāma son of Daśaratha" (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa*). Scholars read this as the hinge between the older humanized hero and the later full divinization. [scholarly]
- **The Vālī-vadha** — Rāma shoots [Vālī](./sugriva-and-vali.md) from concealment while Vālī duels Sugrīva; the dying Vālī delivers a formal indictment (you struck one who was not fighting you; I could have retrieved Sītā myself). Rāma's defense: the earth is Bharata's realm and Rāma its enforcer; Vālī violated dharma by taking his brother's wife Rumā; and kings hunt beasts by ambush — monkeys being fair game (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa*). Vālī accepts the judgment and dies reconciled — but the exchange remains the classic locus of "can the ends of dharma use unfair means." [disputed within tradition]
- **The agni-parīkṣā and repudiation of Sītā** — after Rāvaṇa's fall Rāma publicly disclaims Sītā, saying he fought for honour, not for her; she enters the fire and Agni returns her unharmed, whereupon Rāma states he never doubted her but required proof for the world (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa*). Full treatment and the Māyā-Sītā resolutions are cataloged on the [Sītā page](./sita.md).
- **The banishment of Sītā** — hearing a washerman's slander, Rāma sends the pregnant, already-vindicated Sītā to the forest; the epic frames it as the king sacrificing the man — the rājadharma of answering public opinion crushing the husband's dharma (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa*). [late text] Later poets (Bhavabhūti's *Uttararāmacarita*) dwell on Rāma's anguish; devotional recensions often soften or omit the episode. [scholarly]
- **The Śambūka episode** — a brahmin's son dies untimely; sages ascribe it to adharma in the realm; Rāma finds the śūdra ascetic Śambūka performing tapas forbidden to his varṇa in that yuga, and beheads him, whereupon the boy revives and the gods applaud (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa*). [late text] The episode sits in the Uttara Kāṇḍa, which critical scholarship holds to be a later addition; it is the sharpest modern flashpoint — anti-caste readings (Ambedkar, Periyar and after) center it, while several devotional recensions (e.g., the *Rāmcaritmānas*) simply omit it. [scholarly][disputed]
- **The obedient son and the one-wife vow** — the counterweights the tradition celebrates: Rāma forfeits the throne instantly to keep Daśaratha's word to Kaikeyī, without resentment (*Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa*), and holds the *eka-patnī-vrata* (one wife only) — at his aśvamedha a golden image of Sītā sits in the wife's place rather than a new queen (*Uttara Kāṇḍa*). [late text]
- **Rāma-rājya** — his reign becomes the proverbial ideal polity: no untimely death, no want, dharma on four feet (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha/Uttara Kāṇḍa*); the term's modern political afterlife runs through Gandhi's usage. [scholarly]

## Portrayal across recensions
Full profiles live in the [recensions folder](../ramayana/recensions/README.md) and the [Adhyātma & variant Rāmāyaṇas overview](../ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md); this is the Rāma-specific delta.

- *Variant ([Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md)):* Rāma is unambiguously **nirguṇa Brahman appearing saguṇa** — never deluded, never truly suffering; grief and doubt are līlā, and the Māyā-Sītā device protects the ordeals' appearances. Frame for the [Rāma Gītā](../rama-gita.md). [late text]
- *Variant ([Rāmcaritmānas of Tulsīdās](../ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md), 1574 CE):* Rāma as the accessible saguṇa Lord of bhakti; Śiva himself narrates and adores him; the harsh edges (Śambūka; the banishment) are dropped or softened. See [Tulsīdās](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/tulsidas.md). [late text]
- *Variant ([Kamban's Irāmāvatāram](../ramayana/recensions/kamban-iramavataram.md), Tamil, c. 12th c.):* Rāma divine from the outset in a Tamil poetic register; famously, Rāvaṇa never physically touches Sītā — he lifts the earth beneath her hut. [regional]
- *Variant ([Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇ](../ramayana/recensions/krittivasi-ramayan.md), Bengali):* devotional Rāma; the celebrated interpolated episode of Rāma's **autumnal Durgā-pūjā** (akāla-bodhana) before the war ties him into Bengal's Śākta calendar. [regional][late text]
- *Variant ([Jain Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/recensions/jain-and-buddhist-ramayanas.md) — Vimalasūri's Paumacariya etc.):* Rāma (Padma) is a non-violent śalākā-puruṣa who never kills; **Lakṣmaṇa slays Rāvaṇa** and bears the karmic cost; Rāma attains Jain liberation. [disputed]
- *Variant (Buddhist Daśaratha Jātaka):* Rāma-paṇḍita is the Bodhisattva; the exile story stands but there is no abduction and no Rāvaṇa, and (in this telling) Sītā is his sister, later consecrated queen — a moral tale of equanimity at a father's death. [disputed]
- *Variant ([Southeast Asian Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/recensions/southeast-asian-ramayanas.md) — Ramakien, Kakawin Rāmāyaṇa, Hikayat Seri Rama, etc.):* Rāma (Phra Ram etc.) as royal exemplar across Thai, Javanese, Khmer, Malay, Lao and Burmese courts, with local theologies (in the Thai Ramakien he is an avatar of Phra Narai/Viṣṇu within a Buddhist frame). [regional]

## Rāma beyond the Rāmāyaṇa
- **[Rāmopākhyāna](../ramopakhyana.md)** — the *Mahābhārata* carries its own compact Rāma-story, told by Mārkaṇḍeya to console Yudhiṣṭhira in exile — notable for lacking several famous late motifs (*Mahābhārata, Vana/Āraṇyaka Parva*).
- **[Rāma Gītā](../rama-gita.md)** — Rāma as Advaitin teacher, instructing Lakṣmaṇa on the Self (*Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa*). [late text]
- **[Āditya-Hṛdayam](../stotras/aditya-hridayam.md)** — the Sun-hymn Agastya teaches the battle-weary Rāma before the final duel with Rāvaṇa (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa*; placement varies by recension). 
- **Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha** — the young Rāma's world-weariness and Vasiṣṭha's vast reply frame the entire [Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha](../../90-literature-corpus/yoga-vasishtha.md), making Rāma the archetypal seeker as well as the archetypal king. [late text]
- **Purāṇic summaries** — the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (Skandha 9) and *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* (Book 4) carry the Solar-line digest of his career; Kālidāsa's *Raghuvaṃśa* (cantos 10–15) gives the classical kāvya treatment (see [Raghuvaṃśa kings](../../03-lineages-vamsha/raghuvamsha-kalidasa-kings.md)). [scholarly for dating]

## Rāma-nāma and the worship afterlife
- **The name as tāraka-mantra** — "Rāma" is held to be the *tāraka* ("carrying-across") mantra; in Śaiva-Vaiṣṇava synthesis Śiva whispers it to the dying at Kāśī, and Śiva tells Pārvatī that saying "Rāma" thrice equals the whole *Viṣṇu Sahasranāma* (verse cited in [the Sahasranāma's](../stotras/vishnu-sahasranama.md) appended dialogue). [late text]
- **Vālmīki and the inverted name** — the robber Ratnākara, unable to utter "Rāma," repeats "marā" until it becomes the name and he becomes [Vālmīki](./valmiki-ratnakara-conversion.md) — the charter myth of the name's power exceeding the intention of its speaker. [late text][folk]
- **Kali-yuga soteriology** — the *Kali-Santaraṇa Upaniṣad* embeds "Rāma" in the mahā-mantra (Hare Rāma…) as the Kali-age remedy; nāma-japa traditions (Rāmnām notebooks, continuous likhita-japa) build on this. [late text]
- **Bhakti lineages** — Rāmānanda (c. 14th–15th c.) opens Rāma-bhakti to all castes at Vārāṇasī; the **Rāmānandī sampradāya** descending from him becomes among the largest Vaiṣṇava monastic orders. [Kabīr](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/kabir.md) and the [nirguṇa sants](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/north-bhakti-sants.md) take "Rām" as the name of the formless Absolute — pointedly not Daśaratha's son — while [Tulsīdās](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/tulsidas.md) anchors the saguṇa stream. [scholarly]
- **Protective liturgy** — the [Rāma-rakṣā Stotra](../stotras/ramaraksha-stotra.md) (attributed to Budha-Kauśika) armours the reciter limb-by-limb with Rāma's names; the [Hanumān Cālīsā](../stotras/hanuman-chalisa.md) approaches Rāma through his servant. [late text]
- **Festivals** — **Rāma Navamī** (Caitra śukla 9) for the birth; **Vijayadaśamī/Daśaharā** commemorating Rāvaṇa's defeat (Rāmlīlā performance cycles across North India, effigy-burning); **Dīpāvalī** linked in North Indian tradition to the exiles' homecoming to Ayodhyā. [regional] (attributions vary by region — Daśaharā and Dīpāvalī carry other charter myths elsewhere).
- **Sacred geography** — [Ayodhyā](../../06-sacred-geography/ayodhya.md) as janmabhūmi and one of the seven mokṣa-giving cities; [Rāmeśvaram](../../06-sacred-geography/rameswaram-setu-bandha.md), where tradition says Rāma worshipped Śiva at the bridgehead; [Citrakūṭa](../../06-sacred-geography/chitrakoot-kamadgiri.md), [Pañcavaṭī](../../06-sacred-geography/panchavati-nashik-godavari.md), and the whole exile trail as a living pilgrimage map.
- **Documented-history clock** — Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa composed/expanded c. 7th c. BCE–3rd c. CE; Kamban c. 12th c. CE; Rāmānanda c. 1400 CE; *Rāmcaritmānas* begun 1574 CE; Bhadrācalam and other great Rāma temples early-modern; the Ayodhyā Rāma Janmabhūmi temple consecrated 2024 CE after a long-contested history. [scholarly]

## Iconography
- **The bowman-king** — standard form: standing, two-armed (the human avatāra keeps human arms, unlike four-armed Viṣṇu), dark (blue/green-black) complexion, holding the bow **Kodaṇḍa** and an arrow, wearing kirīṭa-mukuṭa as royal heir (*Vaiṣṇava iconographic tradition; āgamic prescriptions*).
- **The family group** — Rāma flanked by Sītā (his right in most conventions), Lakṣmaṇa with bow, and Hanumān kneeling with folded hands — the composite icon of most Rāma temples (*temple convention*).
- **Paṭṭābhiṣeka-Rāma** — the enthroned coronation tableau (Rāma and Sītā crowned, brothers and Hanumān attending), the standard "darbar" image and the closing image of recitation traditions (*temple and manuscript-painting convention*). [regional]
- **Emblems** — the Kodaṇḍa bow, the single unfailing arrow, and the sandals (pādukā) that ruled from the throne during Bharata's regency — the pādukā itself an object of veneration (Rāmānandī and other orders). [folk][regional]
