# Rāma

> Where this sits: canonical character hub for **Rāma** (Rāmacandra, Rāghava, Maryādā Puruṣottama) — prince of Ayodhyā, seventh avatāra of Viṣṇu, and protagonist of the *Rāmāyaṇa*. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md). This page covers his names, ethics, recensions, and worship-afterlife; the **narrative** lives in the seven kāṇḍa files of the [Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md) — [Bāla](../ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md), [Ayodhyā](../ramayana/2-ayodhya-kanda.md), [Araṇya](../ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md), [Kiṣkindhā](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md), [Sundara](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md), [Yuddha](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md), [Uttara](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* Rāma's life falls in the **[Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the present 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara — tradition places him near the Tretā's end. The academic frame instead dates the Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa*'s core (Kāṇḍas 2–6) to roughly the 7th–4th centuries BCE, with the Bāla and Uttara Kāṇḍas as later strata. [scholarly]
> Verse numbers: **CE** = the Baroda Critical Edition (as digitized on GRETIL); **vulgate** = the Southern text as printed on valmikiramayan.net (Yuddha and earlier) or in Shastri's translation (Uttara). The two numberings differ, so both are given where both were checked.

## Names, epithets, and titles
- **Rāma** — "the pleasing / delightful one," from the root *ram* (to delight). Kālidāsa makes the gloss explicit: the family guru named the child "Rāma" for his delightfulness (*abhirāma*), "the first auspicious word of the world" (*Raghuvaṃśa* 10.68). The name is itself treated as a mantra (see Rāma-nāma below) and appears among the thousand names of Viṣṇu (*Viṣṇu Sahasranāma* — see [the stotra file](../stotras/vishnu-sahasranama.md)).
- **Rāmacandra** — "Rāma the moon(-like)," the common devotional form of the name; largely a Purāṇic and medieval usage rather than Vālmīki's own idiom. The Critical Edition has it only once, and then as a metaphor: the monkeys despair to see "the Rāma-moon swallowed by the Rāhu that is Rāvaṇa" (*rāmacandramasaṃ … grastaṃ rāvaṇarāhuṇā*, CE 6.90.26). [late text]
- **Rāghava / Raghunandana / Rāghavendra** — "descendant / joy of [Raghu](../raghu.md)," after the great ancestor of the Solar line; the dynasty itself becomes the *Raghuvaṃśa* (see [Kālidāsa's Raghuvaṃśa kings](../../03-lineages-vamsha/raghuvamsha-kalidasa-kings.md)). *Rāghava* (over a thousand times) and *Raghunandana* (over a hundred) are Vālmīki's own; *Rāghavendra* does not occur in the Critical Edition and is later usage.
- **Dāśarathi / Kausalyā-nandana** — "son of [Daśaratha](./dasharatha.md)," "joy of [Kausalyā](./kausalya.md)." Vālmīki's own form of the second is *kausalyānandavardhana*, "who increases Kausalyā's joy," used from Nārada's opening sketch onward (CE 1.1.16).
- **Kākutstha / Aikṣvāka** — after the ancestors Kakutstha and Ikṣvāku of the [Sūryavaṃśa (Solar dynasty)](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*; *Kākutstha* alone occurs over two hundred times in the CE).
- **Sītāpati / Jānakī-vallabha** — "lord/beloved of [Sītā](./sita.md)" — the pair is worshipped as a unit, Sītā-Rāma (*devotional usage*; neither compound occurs in the CE).
- **Kodaṇḍa-Rāma / Kodaṇḍa-pāṇi** — "Rāma with the bow (kodaṇḍa)," his defining iconographic attribute (see Iconography below). *Kodaṇḍa* as the name of his bow is later devotional and temple usage; the word does not occur in the Critical Edition, where his great bow is the "Vaiṣṇava" bow given by Agastya (see Iconography).
- **Raghunātha / Raghupati / Rājā-Rāma** — "lord of the Raghus," the royal titles central to North Indian bhakti and to the *Rāmcaritmānas* (*Tulsīdās and bhakti usage*; neither *raghunātha* nor *raghupati* occurs in the CE). [late text]
- **Maryādā Puruṣottama** — "the supreme person of maryādā (bounded propriety/decorum)": the epithet crystallizing his character as the man who never oversteps dharma's limits even at devastating personal cost; the phrase as a fixed title belongs to later devotional tradition rather than Vālmīki's text. Vālmīki does call Rāma *puruṣottama* ("best of men"), but the word is a common one in the epic: it defines the best kind of man in maxims (e.g. CE 4.29.39, 6.1.7) and names Viṣṇu in Brahmā's hymn (CE 6.105.14), and it is never paired with *maryādā*. [late text]

## Identity on the cosmic clock
- **Seventh avatāra of Viṣṇu** — in the standard *daśāvatāra* list Rāma is the seventh descent, following Vāmana and Paraśurāma and preceding Kṛṣṇa, or Balarāma where Balarāma is counted (as in Jayadeva's *Gītagovinda*, *keśava dhṛta-rāma-śarīra*, 1.11, between Bhṛgupati 1.10 and Haladhara 1.12; see [Jayadeva](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/jayadeva-and-the-gita-govinda.md)). The thumbnail sits in the [Daśāvatāra roster](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) (*Purāṇic daśāvatāra lists*).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.3):* the *Bhāgavata*'s own list counts twenty-two descents, and Rāma, "having become a king" (*naradevatvam āpannaḥ*, literally "a god among men") to subdue the ocean, is the eighteenth, after Vyāsa (1.3.21–22).
- **Purpose of the descent** — Rāvaṇa's boon from Brahmā made him invulnerable to devas, dānavas, and other celestial beings — but he disdained to name humans; Viṣṇu therefore takes **human** birth to slay him, the gods petitioning Viṣṇu while Daśaratha's *putrakāmeṣṭi* sacrifice (conducted through [Ṛśyaśṛṅga](../rishyashringa.md)) yields the divine pāyasa that Daśaratha's queens consume (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa*). Kausalyā's son is called "half of Viṣṇu" (*viṣṇor ardham*, CE 1.17.6). For the deeper curse-history of the line see [Anaraṇya's curse on Rāvaṇa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/anaranya-ravana-curse.md).
- **Birth** — born at [Ayodhyā](../../06-sacred-geography/ayodhya.md) to Daśaratha and Kausalyā, with brothers [Lakṣmaṇa](./lakshmana.md), [Bharata, and Śatrughna](./bharata-shatrughna-and-their-wives.md) born of [Sumitrā](./sumitra.md) and [Kaikeyī](./kaikeyi.md) (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa*). The date celebrated as Rāma Navamī — Caitra, the ninth tithi, under Punarvasu, with Cancer rising — is given in the vulgate ("in the twelfth month, Caitra, on the ninth tithi," vulgate 1.18.8–9); the Critical Edition's birth sarga gives no date, asterism or horoscope (CE 1.17.1–12). The "śukla" (bright fortnight) of the festival date is calendar tradition, not stated in that verse.
- **Age** — Daśaratha protests that Rāma is "not yet sixteen" when Viśvāmitra asks for him (CE 1.19.2). At the exile Sītā tells the disguised Rāvaṇa that her husband was twenty-five (CE 3.45.10 / vulgate 3.47.10).
  - *Variant (recensions):* how long the couple lived in Ayodhyā before the exile: "one year" in the Critical Edition (*saṃvatsaraṃ cādhyuṣitā*, CE 3.45.4), "twelve years" in the vulgate (*uṣitvā dvādaśa samāḥ*, vulgate 3.47.4).
- **Tretā Yuga placement** — tradition sets his career in the later Tretā Yuga; Purāṇic framing treats the avatāra as recurring in the Tretā of successive cycles. Attempts to fix a calendar year by archaeoastronomy circulate widely but have no scholarly consensus. [disputed]
- **Academic clock** — historical-critical scholarship treats Rāma as an epic hero whose story was composed and expanded c. 7th c. BCE – 3rd c. CE (core books first; Bāla and Uttara Kāṇḍas, where his divinity is most explicit, judged later strata in the Baroda Critical Edition tradition). [scholarly]

## Life in one glance (narrative lives elsewhere)
- **[Bāla Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md)** — birth; tutelage under Viśvāmitra; slaying of [Tāṭakā](./tataka.md); redemption of [Ahalyā](./ahalya.md); breaking Śiva's bow at Mithilā and wedding Sītā (see also [Janaka's Videha line](../../03-lineages-vamsha/videha-mithila-line-janaka.md)); on the road home [Paraśurāma](../parashurama.md) challenges him with the Vaiṣṇava bow (CE 1.74.27); Rāma strings it, Paraśurāma, drained of power, hails him as the imperishable slayer of Madhu, lord of the gods, and at his own request Rāma's arrow destroys the worlds Paraśurāma had won by tapas (CE 1.75.4–23); Rāma then gives that bow to Varuṇa (CE 1.76.1).
- **[Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/2-ayodhya-kanda.md)** — Kaikeyī's two boons; Rāma accepts 14 years' exile without protest, promising her with the line that became proverbial, "Rāma does not speak twice" (*rāmo dvir nābhibhāṣate*, CE 2.16.19 / vulgate 2.18.30); Bharata's regency with Rāma's sandals; [Guha](./guha.md) ferries the exiles; Citrakūṭa sojourn ([the hill itself](../../06-sacred-geography/chitrakoot-kamadgiri.md)).
- **[Araṇya Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md)** — Daṇḍaka forest years ([the terrain](../../06-sacred-geography/dandakaranya-rama-forest.md)); [Agastya](../agastya.md) arms him with Viṣṇu's bow, Brahmā's unfailing arrow, two inexhaustible quivers and a sword (CE 3.11.29–33 / vulgate 3.12.32–36); [Śūrpaṇakhā](./shurpanakha.md); the golden deer [Mārīca](./maricha.md), who warns Rāvaṇa that "Rāma is dharma embodied" (*rāmo vigrahavān dharmaḥ*, CE 3.35.13 / vulgate 3.37.13); Sītā's abduction by [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md); [Jaṭāyu's](./jatayu-and-sampati.md) fall; [Śabarī's](./shabari.md) hospitality.
- **[Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md)** — alliance with [Sugrīva; the slaying of Vālī](./sugriva-and-vali.md) (ethics below); the search mustered.
- **[Sundara](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md) & [Yuddha Kāṇḍas](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md)** — [Hanumān's](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md) leap; the bridge at [Rāmeśvaram / Setubandha](../../06-sacred-geography/rameswaram-setu-bandha.md); [Vibhīṣaṇa's](./vibhishana.md) defection; the fall of [Indrajit](./indrajit-meghnad.md), Kumbhakarṇa, and Rāvaṇa; the agni-parīkṣā; return to Ayodhyā and coronation (Rāma-paṭṭābhiṣeka).
  - **The refuge vow** — when the monkey council suspects Vibhīṣaṇa, Rāma overrules them: one who comes in friendship he will never abandon, even if at fault (CE 6.12.3 / vulgate 6.18.3), and "to one who comes for refuge even once, saying 'I am yours,' I grant safety from all beings: this is my vow" (*sakṛd eva prapannāya tavāsmīti ca yācate / abhayaṃ sarvabhūtebhyo dadāmy etad vrataṃ mama*, CE 6.12.20 / vulgate 6.18.33), "whether it is Vibhīṣaṇa or Rāvaṇa himself" (6.12.21 / 6.18.34). Later Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition treats this verse as a scriptural charter of *śaraṇāgati* (surrender). [scholarly for the later reception]
  - **The Brahmā-arrow** — Rāvaṇa falls to the great Brahmā-made arrow that Agastya had first given Rāma, loosed when the charioteer Mātali reminds him of it (CE 6.97.1–4).
- **[Uttara Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md)** — Rāma-rājya; Sītā's banishment; [Lava & Kuśa](../uttara-ramayana-lava-kusha.md); Sītā's return to the Earth; Rāma's own departure into the [Sarayū](../../06-sacred-geography/sarayu-ayodhya-river.md), resuming his Viṣṇu-form: he walks into the river, Brahmā calls from the sky "Come, Viṣṇu," and he enters the Vaiṣṇava radiance in his own body with his brothers (CE 7.100.1–10). [late text]
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.11):* after Sītā enters the earth, Rāma keeps celibacy and an unbroken agnihotra for thirteen thousand years, then "goes to his own light" (*ātmajyotir agāt*, 9.11.15–19).

## Maryādā Puruṣottama — the ethics debates
The tradition itself, not just modern critics, interrogates Rāma's hardest choices; the epic stages the objections in the mouths of his victims and family.

- **"I think myself a man"** — while Sītā is in the fire, the assembled gods arrive and ask Rāma, "creator of all the world," how he can watch Sītā fall into the fire as though he were an ordinary man (CE 6.105.5–8 / vulgate 6.117). He answers *ātmānaṃ mānuṣaṃ manye rāmaṃ daśarathātmajam* — "I hold myself a man, Rāma son of Daśaratha; let the Lord tell me who and whose I am" (CE 6.105.10 / vulgate 6.117.11), and Brahmā replies with a hymn naming him Nārāyaṇa (CE 6.105.11ff.). Agni returns Sītā only after this (CE 6.106.1–9 / vulgate 6.118.1–10). Scholars read this as the hinge between the older humanized hero and the later full divinization. [scholarly]
- **The Vālī-vadha** — Rāma shoots [Vālī](./sugriva-and-vali.md) from concealment while Vālī duels Sugrīva; the dying Vālī delivers a formal indictment: what merit is there in killing one whose face was turned away (*parāṅmukhavadham*, CE 4.17.13 / vulgate 4.17.16), one who was fighting someone else; he is a forest animal and Rāma a lord of men (CE 4.17.26); and had Rāma asked, he would have brought Rāvaṇa bound by the neck and fetched Sītā back even from the ocean or the underworld (CE 4.17.41–42; the vulgate adds "in a single day," vulgate 4.17.49–50). Rāma's defense: the earth with its hills and forests belongs to the Ikṣvākus and Bharata rules it, with Rāma as its enforcer (CE 4.18.6–11 / vulgate 4.18.6); Vālī violated dharma by living with his brother's wife Rumā, who stood to him as a daughter-in-law (*snuṣā*, CE 4.18.19 / vulgate 4.18.19); and men take beasts by snares and ambush, hidden or seen, and royal sages hunt — monkeys being "tree-animals" (*śākhāmṛga*), fair game whether fighting back or not (CE 4.18.34–36 / vulgate 4.18.37–40). Vālī asks Rāma's pardon and dies reconciled, commending Aṅgada to him (CE 4.18.46–57 / vulgate 4.18.66) — but the exchange remains the classic locus of "can the ends of dharma use unfair means." [disputed within tradition]
- **The agni-parīkṣā and repudiation of Sītā** — after Rāvaṇa's fall Rāma publicly disclaims Sītā: the war was fought to wipe out the stain on his line, "not for your sake" (CE 6.103.15–16 / vulgate 6.115.15), and she may go where she likes — to Lakṣmaṇa, Bharata, Sugrīva or Vibhīṣaṇa (CE 6.103.22–23; vulgate 6.115.22–23 adds Śatrughna). Sītā herself asks Lakṣmaṇa for a pyre; he reads assent in Rāma's face and builds it (CE 6.104.18–21 / vulgate 6.116.18–21), and she enters the fire calling on Agni as the world's witness (CE 6.104.24–25 / vulgate 6.116.25–29). Agni returns her unharmed, declaring that she never strayed "in word, mind or eye" (CE 6.106.5 / vulgate 6.118.6), whereupon Rāma states he knew her to be wholly devoted but required proof "for the conviction of the three worlds" (CE 6.106.13–14 / vulgate 6.118.15–17) (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa*). Full treatment and the Māyā-Sītā resolutions are cataloged on the [Sītā page](./sita.md).
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata, Rāmopākhyāna):* there is no fire. Rāma rejects her, Sītā swears by the wind, fire, water, sky and earth to take her life if she has sinned, and Vāyu, Agni, Varuṇa and Brahmā speak for her, Brahmā adding that Nalakūbara's curse had kept Rāvaṇa from her; Daśaratha appears from heaven to bless his son (MBh BORI 3.275.10–35). A fire-ordeal on the Vālmīki model appears only in an appendix passage of some manuscripts, not in the constituted text.
- **The banishment of Sītā** — the pregnant Sītā has just asked to spend a night among the forest hermitages (CE 7.41.22–26) when Rāma asks his companion **Bhadra** what the city says. Bhadra, pressed to tell the bad with the good, reports the citizens' talk: after taking back a wife Rāvaṇa held on his lap, "we too shall have to put up with it from our wives, for as the king does, the people follow" (CE 7.42.4–19 / vulgate Uttara 43); his other friends confirm it (CE 7.42.22). Rāma sends the already-vindicated Sītā to the forest under cover of her own wish; the epic frames it as the king sacrificing the man — the rājadharma of answering public opinion crushing the husband's dharma (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa*). [late text] Later poets (Bhavabhūti's *Uttararāmacarita*) dwell on Rāma's anguish; devotional recensions often soften or omit the episode. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.11.8–10):* Rāma himself, walking the city at night in disguise, overhears an unnamed man refuse his wife: "I will not keep you, who stayed in another's house; let a woman-ruled Rāma keep Sītā — not I." Fearing such talk, he abandons her and she goes to Vālmīki's hermitage.
  - *Variant ([Padma Purāṇa](../../90-literature-corpus/puranas/padma-purana.md), Pātāla-khaṇḍa 55–56):* Rāma's spies go through the city; five hear only praise, but the sixth hears a **washerman** beat and turn out his wife for staying a day in another man's house, shouting that he is "not King Rāma" to take such a wife back. The spies report it, and Rāma, fainting with grief, consults Bharata. This is a Purāṇic source of the popular "washerman" telling, which is not in Vālmīki. [late text]
- **The Śambūka episode** — a brahmin's son dies untimely; Nārada explains that tapas belonged to brahmins alone in the Kṛta age, to kṣatriyas as well in the Tretā, to vaiśyas in the Dvāpara, and to śūdras only in the Kali, so that a śūdra practicing severe tapas now is a grave adharma and the cause of the death (CE 7.65.9–23). Rāma searches the realm, finds the ascetic hanging head-down (CE 7.66.10–13), and learns that he is the śūdra **Śambūka**, seeking to reach the gods' world in his own body (CE 7.67.2–3); he beheads him, and "at that moment" the boy returns to life (CE 7.67.4–5). Agastya then tells him that the gods are speaking of him as the slayer of the śūdra (CE 7.67.10). [late text]
  - *Variant (vulgate Uttara 76, Shastri tr.):* adds that the gods cry "Well done!", rain flowers, and offer Rāma a boon, saying the śūdra will not now reach heaven; Rāma asks for the boy's life, and they tell him the boy revived when the head fell. The Critical Edition has none of this.
  - The episode sits in the Uttara Kāṇḍa, which critical scholarship holds to be a later addition; it is the sharpest modern flashpoint — anti-caste readings (Ambedkar, Periyar and after) center it, while several devotional recensions (e.g., the *Rāmcaritmānas*) simply omit it. [scholarly][disputed]
- **The obedient son and the one-wife vow** — the counterweights the tradition celebrates: Rāma forfeits the throne instantly to keep Daśaratha's word to Kaikeyī, without resentment (*Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa*), and holds the *eka-patnī-vrata* (one wife only) — at his aśvamedha a golden image of Sītā sits in the wife's place rather than a new queen (*kāñcanīṃ mama patnīm*, CE 7.82.19), and he chooses no other wife after her (CE 7.89.4) (*Uttara Kāṇḍa*). [late text] The phrase *eka-patnī-vrata* is the tradition's label, not Vālmīki's.
- **Rāma-rājya** — his reign becomes the proverbial ideal polity. Vālmīki describes it twice: in Nārada's opening preview, where people are content and righteous, no man sees a son die, widows are none, and there is no fear of wind, water or fire, "as in the Kṛta age," for 11,000 years (CE 1.1.71–76 / vulgate 1.1.90–97); and at the close of the Yuddha Kāṇḍa, where no widow laments, there is no fear of snakes or sickness, no robbers, no old man performs a child's funeral, trees always bear, rain comes in season, and the reign lasts 10,000 years (CE 6.116.84–90 / vulgate 6.128.99–106). The image of dharma standing "on all four feet" in Rāma's reign is Tulsīdās's (*cāriu carana dharma jaga māhīṃ*, *Rāmcaritmānas*, Uttar Kāṇḍ 21.2). The term's modern political afterlife runs through Gandhi's usage. [scholarly]

## Portrayal across recensions
Full profiles live in the [recensions folder](../ramayana/recensions/README.md) and the [Adhyātma & variant Rāmāyaṇas overview](../ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md); this is the Rāma-specific delta.

- *Variant ([Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md)):* Rāma is unambiguously **nirguṇa Brahman appearing saguṇa** — never deluded, never truly suffering; grief and doubt are līlā, and the Māyā-Sītā device protects the ordeals' appearances. Frame for the [Rāma Gītā](../rama-gita.md). [late text]
- *Variant ([Rāmcaritmānas of Tulsīdās](../ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md), 1574 CE):* Rāma as the accessible saguṇa Lord of bhakti; Śiva himself narrates and adores him; the harsh edges (Śambūka; the banishment) are dropped or softened — the Uttar Kāṇḍ moves from the description of Rāma-rājya to the simple statement that Sītā bore two sons, Lava and Kuśa (Uttar 25), without a banishment. See [Tulsīdās](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/tulsidas.md). [late text]
- *Variant ([Kamban's Irāmāvatāram](../ramayana/recensions/kamban-iramavataram.md), Tamil, c. 12th c.):* Rāma divine from the outset in a Tamil poetic register; famously, Rāvaṇa never physically touches Sītā — mindful of a word Brahmā once spoke against him, he digs up the ground beneath her a yojana deep and lifts it away with her on it (Kamban, Āraṇiya kāṇṭam, Irāvaṇaṉ cūḻccip paṭalam, v. 72). [regional]
- *Variant ([Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇ](../ramayana/recensions/krittivasi-ramayan.md), Bengali):* devotional Rāma; the celebrated interpolated episode of Rāma's **autumnal Durgā-pūjā** (akāla-bodhana) before the war ties him into Bengal's Śākta calendar. [regional][late text]
- *Variant ([Jain Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/recensions/jain-and-buddhist-ramayanas.md) — Vimalasūri's Paumacariya etc.):* Rāma (Padma) is a non-violent śalākā-puruṣa who never kills; **Lakṣmaṇa**, the Vāsudeva of the pair, **slays Rāvaṇa** and goes to hell for it, while Rāma, destined for liberation in that very life, must not kill; Rāma attains Jain liberation. (Studies in Jainism, Ramakrishna Mission Institute of Culture, p. 166.) [regional]
- *Variant (Buddhist [Daśaratha Jātaka](../ramayana/recensions/jain-and-buddhist-ramayanas.md), Jātaka no. 461):* Rāma-paṇḍita ("Rāma the Wise") is the Bodhisatta. King Daśaratha of Benares himself sends his two elder sons away for twelve years, to keep them safe from his second queen, who wants the throne for her son Bharata; their sister Sītā chooses to go with them. They live in the Himālaya; the king dies of grief in the ninth year; Bharata comes to bring Rāma back and is amazed that Rāma alone does not weep, and Rāma explains why the wise do not grieve. Rāma's straw slippers rule for the remaining three years; then Sītā is made his queen-consort and he reigns 16,000 years. There is no abduction and no Rāvaṇa. In the closing identifications Daśaratha is Suddhodana, Sītā is Rāhula's mother, Bharata is Ānanda, and Rāma is the Buddha himself (Rouse tr., 1901). The relative date of the Jātaka and the epic is debated. [scholarly]
- *Variant ([Southeast Asian Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/recensions/southeast-asian-ramayanas.md) — Ramakien, Kakawin Rāmāyaṇa, Hikayat Seri Rama, etc.):* Rāma (Phra Ram etc.) as royal exemplar across Thai, Javanese, Khmer, Malay, Lao and Burmese courts, with local theologies (in the Thai Ramakien he is an avatar of Phra Narai/Viṣṇu within a Buddhist frame). [regional]

## Rāma beyond the Rāmāyaṇa
- **[Rāmopākhyāna](../ramopakhyana.md)** — the *Mahābhārata* carries its own compact Rāma-story, told by Mārkaṇḍeya to console Yudhiṣṭhira after Jayadratha's attempt to carry off Draupadī (*Mahābhārata*, Vana/Āraṇyaka Parva, BORI 3.257–275). It lacks several famous motifs: Rāma's birth and marriage take two verses, Sītā simply being Janaka's daughter whom Tvaṣṭṛ himself made to be Rāma's queen (3.258.8–10), with no Viśvāmitra, Tāṭakā, Ahalyā or bow-contest; there is no fire-ordeal (see above); and it ends with the coronation and ten horse-sacrifices on the Gomatī (3.275.65–69) — no Uttara Kāṇḍa events. A reign of 11,000 years followed by Rāma's going to heaven is added only in a starred passage of some manuscripts (*1307), not the constituted text.
- **[Rāma Gītā](../rama-gita.md)** — Rāma as Advaitin teacher, instructing Lakṣmaṇa on the Self (*Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa*). [late text]
- **[Āditya-Hṛdayam](../stotras/aditya-hridayam.md)** — the Sun-hymn Agastya teaches the battle-weary Rāma before the final duel with Rāvaṇa (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, vulgate Yuddha 105). It is not in the Critical Edition's constituted text: there the night-long duel (CE 6.96.30–31) runs straight on to Mātali reminding Rāma of the Brahmā-arrow (CE 6.97.1–4). [interpolation]
- **Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha** — the young Rāma's world-weariness and Vasiṣṭha's vast reply frame the entire [Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha](../../90-literature-corpus/yoga-vasishtha.md), making Rāma the archetypal seeker as well as the archetypal king. [late text]
- **Purāṇic summaries** — the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (Skandha 9, chs. 10–11; see the [Bhāgavata skandha map](../../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md)) and *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* (Book 4) carry the Solar-line digest of his career; Kālidāsa's *Raghuvaṃśa* (cantos 10–15) gives the classical kāvya treatment (see [Raghuvaṃśa kings](../../03-lineages-vamsha/raghuvamsha-kalidasa-kings.md)). [scholarly for dating]

## Rāma-nāma and the worship afterlife
- **The name as tāraka-mantra** — "Rāma" is held to be the *tāraka* ("carrying-across") mantra; in Śaiva-Vaiṣṇava synthesis Śiva whispers it to the dying at Kāśī. The *Muktikā Upaniṣad* puts this in Rāma's own mouth, telling Hanumān that one who dies at the Brahmanāla in Kāśī receives the tāraka-mantra and liberation, and that anywhere else in Kāśī Maheśvara utters the tāraka-mantra into the dying person's right ear (*Muktikā Upaniṣad* 1.1.18–23). Śiva also tells Pārvatī that saying "Rāma" thrice equals the whole *Viṣṇu Sahasranāma* (verse cited in [the Sahasranāma's](../stotras/vishnu-sahasranama.md) appended dialogue). [late text]
- **Vālmīki and the inverted name** — the robber Ratnākara, unable to utter "Rāma," repeats "marā" until it becomes the name and he becomes [Vālmīki](./valmiki-ratnakara-conversion.md) — the charter myth of the name's power exceeding the intention of its speaker. [late text][folk]
- **Kali-yuga soteriology** — the *Kali-Santaraṇa Upaniṣad* embeds "Rāma" in the mahā-mantra (Hare Rāma…) as the Kali-age remedy; nāma-japa traditions (Rāmnām notebooks, continuous likhita-japa) build on this. [late text]
- **Bhakti lineages** — Rāmānanda (c. 14th–15th c.) opens Rāma-bhakti to all castes at Vārāṇasī; the **Rāmānandī sampradāya** descending from him becomes among the largest Vaiṣṇava monastic orders. [Kabīr](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/kabir.md) and the [nirguṇa sants](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/north-bhakti-sants.md) take "Rām" as the name of the formless Absolute — pointedly not Daśaratha's son — while [Tulsīdās](../../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/tulsidas.md) anchors the saguṇa stream. [scholarly]
- **Protective liturgy** — the [Rāma-rakṣā Stotra](../stotras/ramaraksha-stotra.md) (attributed to Budha-Kauśika) armours the reciter limb-by-limb with Rāma's names; the [Hanumān Cālīsā](../stotras/hanuman-chalisa.md) approaches Rāma through his servant. [late text]
- **Festivals** — **Rāma Navamī** (Caitra śukla 9) for the birth; **Vijayadaśamī/Daśaharā** commemorating Rāvaṇa's defeat (Rāmlīlā performance cycles across North India, effigy-burning); **Dīpāvalī** linked in North Indian tradition to the exiles' homecoming to Ayodhyā. [regional] (attributions vary by region — Daśaharā and Dīpāvalī carry other charter myths elsewhere).
- **Sacred geography** — [Ayodhyā](../../06-sacred-geography/ayodhya.md) as janmabhūmi and one of the seven mokṣa-giving cities; [Rāmeśvaram](../../06-sacred-geography/rameswaram-setu-bandha.md), where tradition says Rāma worshipped Śiva at the bridgehead; [Citrakūṭa](../../06-sacred-geography/chitrakoot-kamadgiri.md), [Pañcavaṭī](../../06-sacred-geography/panchavati-nashik-godavari.md), and the whole exile trail as a living pilgrimage map.
- **Documented-history clock** — Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa composed/expanded c. 7th c. BCE–3rd c. CE; Kamban c. 12th c. CE; Rāmānanda c. 1400 CE; *Rāmcaritmānas* begun 1574 CE; Bhadrācalam and other great Rāma temples early-modern; the Ayodhyā Rāma Janmabhūmi temple consecrated 2024 CE after a long-contested history. [scholarly]

## Iconography
- **The bowman-king** — standard form: standing, two-armed (the human avatāra keeps human arms, unlike four-armed Viṣṇu), dark (blue/green-black) complexion, holding the bow **Kodaṇḍa** and an arrow, wearing kirīṭa-mukuṭa as royal heir (*Vaiṣṇava iconographic tradition; āgamic prescriptions*).
- **The family group** — Rāma flanked by Sītā and by Lakṣmaṇa with his bow, with Hanumān kneeling with folded hands — the composite icon of most Rāma temples (*temple convention*). Which side Sītā stands on varies: her place is more commonly at Rāma's left, and some temples reverse it — the Kodaṇḍarāma temple at Hiremagalur places her at his right, which its local legend explains as the wedding scene shown to Paraśurāma (Wikipedia, single source). [regional]
- **Paṭṭābhiṣeka-Rāma** — the enthroned coronation tableau (Rāma and Sītā crowned, brothers and Hanumān attending), the standard "darbar" image and the closing image of recitation traditions (*temple and manuscript-painting convention*). [regional]
- **Emblems** — the Kodaṇḍa bow, the single unfailing arrow, and the sandals (pādukā) that ruled from the throne during Bharata's regency — the pādukā itself an object of veneration (Rāmānandī and other orders). [folk][regional] In Vālmīki the bow and the unfailing Brahmā-made arrow are Agastya's gifts (CE 3.11.29–33; the arrow again at CE 6.97.4).
