# Babhruvāhana — the Son Who Slew Arjuna

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Babhruvāhana**, Arjuna's son by **Citrāṅgadā** of Maṇipura — the matrilineal heir who, goaded by the Nāga princess **Ulūpī**, kills his own father in single combat during the imperial horse-sacrifice and revives him with the serpent-realm gem, his deed expiating the Vasus' curse for **Bhīṣma's** death. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). The episode is the climax of the [Āśvamedhika Parva](../mahabharata/14-ashvamedhika-parva.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* this falls in the present 28th Mahāyuga of the 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara, in late Dvāpara Yuga, the year after the Mahābhārata war (Kali Yuga begins 3102 BCE at the war's close); the Critical Edition of the *Mahābhārata* (BORI) is the scholarly textual baseline. [scholarly]

## Birth, name, and the Maṇipura succession

- **Son of Arjuna and Citrāṅgadā** — born to **Citrāṅgadā**, warrior-princess of **Maṇipura (Maṇalūra)**, whom Arjuna married during his twelve-year *tīrtha*-exile; he is Arjuna's son by his third marriage and half-brother to Abhimanyu (Subhadrā's), Irāvān (Ulūpī's), and Śrutakarman (Draupadī's) (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~207–208; Āśvamedhika Parva ~79*). See [Arjuna](./arjuna.md), [Citrāṅgadā](./chitrangada.md), and [Abhimanyu](./abhimanyu.md).
- **The matrilineal marriage-pact** — Citrāṅgadā's father, King **Citravāhana**, gave her only on the condition that **her son remain in Maṇipura as its heir**, the royal line being one of single-child succession; so Babhruvāhana grew up a king in his mother's realm and **never joined the Pāṇḍava household** at Hastināpura/Indraprastha (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~207*). See [Citrāṅgadā](./chitrangada.md).
- **The name** — glossed as "bearing a tawny/brown (*babhru*) mount or standard (*vāhana*)"; he is also transliterated **Babruvāhana** and titled **Maṇipura-rāja** (king of Maṇipura) (*Mahābhārata*).
- **Reared and crowned by his grandfather** — in the epic he is raised and consecrated at Maṇipura by **Citravāhana**, succeeding to his mother's throne rather than to any Kuru inheritance (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~79*). [scholarly]

## The horse reaches Maṇipura

- **Yudhiṣṭhira's Aśvamedha** — after the war, Yudhiṣṭhira performs the imperial **horse-sacrifice** as atonement and seal of sovereignty; the consecrated stallion roams free for a year with **Arjuna** as its protector, pacifying every realm it enters (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~71–73*). See [Āśvamedhika Parva](../mahabharata/14-ashvamedhika-parva.md).
- **Babhruvāhana's peaceful welcome** — when the wandering horse reaches Maṇipura, the young king **comes out in homage** — humbly, with priestly gifts and tribute — to greet his father, not in arms (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~79*).
- **Arjuna rebukes him** — bound by *kṣatriya* code to give battle to any kingdom the horse enters, Arjuna is **displeased** and **reproaches his son as unmanly and unwarriorlike** for offering submission instead of a fight, demanding combat (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~79–80*). See [Arjuna](./arjuna.md).

## Ulūpī's goading and the slaying

- **Ulūpī rises from the earth** — **Ulūpī**, the Nāga princess (Arjuna's serpent-wife and Babhruvāhana's step-mother), emerges from the ground and **incites Babhruvāhana to take up arms against his father**, overriding his reluctance (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~80*). See [Ulūpī](./ulupi.md).
- **The duel** — father and son fight a fierce archery duel before Maṇipura, and the son proves a match for the otherwise unconquerable Arjuna (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~80–81*).
- **Babhruvāhana kills Arjuna** — his arrows strike Arjuna down, and the great Pāṇḍava archer **falls dead** on the field — a near-unique instance of Arjuna being slain in the epic; Babhruvāhana too swoons, wounded, beside him (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~81*).
  - *Variant (Critical / Vyāsa core):* Arjuna is felled by arrows piercing his chest; the death comes by ordinary (if mortal) wounds, with no decapitation (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~81*). [scholarly]
  - *Variant (South-Indian / Yakṣagāna *Babruvāhana* cycle):* the popular drama has Babhruvāhana **behead** Arjuna outright, heightening the grief-and-revival for the stage — beyond Vyāsa's terser narrative (*regional folk tradition*). [folk] [regional]
- **The double grief** — recovering and finding he has killed his father, Babhruvāhana resolves to **die beside him**; his mother **Citrāṅgadā** rushes out, laments, and **reproaches Ulūpī** for engineering a son's slaying of his father (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~81*). See [Citrāṅgadā](./chitrangada.md).

## Revival by the Nāga gem and the hidden purpose

- **The Saṃjīvana / Nāgamaṇi** — Ulūpī summons the serpent-realm jewel of resurrection — the **Mṛtasaṃjīvanī (Saṃjīvana-maṇi / Nāgamaṇi)**, supplied through the Nāga realm — and it is placed on Arjuna's chest; **Arjuna returns to life**, the death undone (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~81*). See [Ulūpī](./ulupi.md).
- **Expiation of Bhīṣma's death — the Vasus' curse** — Ulūpī reveals she **orchestrated the whole episode** for Arjuna's good: the **Eight Vasus** (of whom Bhīṣma was the incarnation Dyaus/Prabhāsa) had cursed Arjuna for slaying Bhīṣma **unfairly — from behind Śikhaṇḍī** — in the war; the curse, and Gaṅgā's grief, could be discharged only if Arjuna **fell by his own son's hand**. Babhruvāhana's deed thus **expiated the sin** of Bhīṣma's killing, and the gem reversed the death (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~81–82*). See [Bhīṣma](./bhishma.md).
- **Reconciliation** — Arjuna, restored, is reconciled with his son; Babhruvāhana, Citrāṅgadā, and Ulūpī are later invited to attend the sacrifice at Hastināpura, and the horse continues its circuit (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~82*).

## Matrilineal succession and the Manipuri tradition

- **He stays to inherit his mother's throne** — by the original marriage-pact Babhruvāhana does **not** carry the Kuru/Pāṇḍava throne — that line passes through Abhimanyu's son **Parīkṣit** — but remains **king of Maṇipura**, the matrilineal heir of Citrāṅgadā's house (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~207; Āśvamedhika Parva ~79–82*). See [Abhimanyu](./abhimanyu.md).
- **A study in *kṣatriya-dharma*** — the episode is read as a lesson that even the invincible Arjuna must bow to **karmic justice**: the dutiful son becomes, blamelessly, the instrument of fate (*scholarly note*). [scholarly]
- *Variant (Manipuri regional tradition) [regional]:* the kingdom of **Maṇipura** in the far north-east is widely identified with the epic's Maṇipura, and Babhruvāhana is honoured there as a local ancestral king; the identification with present-day Manipur is **contested by scholars**, who note the epic's Maṇalūra may instead lie in the Kaliṅga/Odisha or southern coastal region. [disputed] [regional]
- *Variant (Jaiminīya Aśvamedha) [late text] [folk]:* the independent **Jaiminīya Aśvamedhaparvan** (attributed to Jaimini; c. 12th c.) hugely expands the tale — after killing Arjuna, Babhruvāhana **descends into Pātāla / Nāgaloka** and wages war on the serpents to seize the *amṛta* or the Saṃjīvanī jewel to revive his father, a multi-chapter quest absent from the critical epic; for many regional audiences this *is* the Babhruvāhana story (*Jaiminīya Aśvamedha Parva*).
- *Variant (Telugu / Harikathā) [folk]:* devotional recitations frame Babhruvāhana as the perfect son whose terrible act is divinely scripted to free his father from the Vasus' curse — fate, not parricide.
