# Tārā — Queen of Kiṣkindhā, the Vānara Pañcakanyā

> The **vānara queen** of the [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md) — daughter of the chief **Suṣeṇa**, wife of **Vālī** and afterwards of **Sugrīva**, mother of **[Aṅgada](./angada.md)**, and the shrewdest political head in the [Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md). Three scenes are hers alone: the **intelligence warning** that would have saved Vālī's life, the **lament** over his body, and the **interception of Lakṣmaṇa** at the palace gate that saves Sugrīva's throne and the war with it. She is the fourth name in the **Pañcakanyā** verse.

> **This file is about the vānara queen and no other Tārā.** She is **not** [Tārā the wife of Bṛhaspati](../soma-tara-budha.md), carried off by Soma in the Tārakāmaya war and mother of Budha; she is **not** [Tārā the Mahāvidyā goddess of Tārāpīṭha](../../06-sacred-geography/tarapith-tara-vashishtha-bamakhepa.md); and she is not the **male** vānara chief **Tāra**, Sugrīva's minister, begotten by **Bṛhaspati** in the Bāla 17 deva-father roster, who proposes the cave-secession at Kiṣkindhā 54. Four unrelated figures, one name — do not merge them; note in particular that it is the *male* Tāra, and not this queen, whom the epic attaches to Bṛhaspati.

**Tārā** (Sanskrit *Tārā*, "star") is the wife of **Vālī**, king of Kiṣkindhā, and after his death the wife of his brother **Sugrīva** — a levirate the text states plainly and never apologises for. Vālmīki gives her the sharpest political mind in the vānara kingdom and stages her three times at the exact moment when an argument, rather than a blow, decides the plot: she reads Sugrīva's second challenge correctly and is overruled; she mourns a husband she told not to go; and she talks an armed and furious **[Lakṣmaṇa](./lakshmana.md)** down from destroying the alliance on which Rāma's whole campaign depends. Her counsel is twice explicitly commended inside the epic — once by the dying Vālī, who instructs Sugrīva to be guided by her in everything, and once by Rāma, who accepts her plea and restores the peace. She is the vānara member of the **Pañcakanyā**, the five women whose daily remembrance is said to destroy great sin.

## I. Name, house, and marriages (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa)

- **Daughter of Suṣeṇa** - Tārā is the daughter of the vānara chief **Suṣeṇa**, whom the corpus elsewhere identifies with the army's physician and with Varuṇa's son of the same name. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa.) See [Nala, Nīla & Suṣeṇa](./nala-nila-and-sushena.md).
  - *Note (the three Suṣeṇas):* the merge of Tārā's father, Varuṇa's son in the Bāla 17 roster, and the Yuddha-kāṇḍa physician into one vānara is a **harmonisation the text never performs**. Her parentage stands as the epic gives it — daughter of a chief named Suṣeṇa — with the wider identification flagged wherever it bears weight. [disputed]
- **Queen of Vālī, mother of Aṅgada** - She is Vālī's chief queen and the mother of **Aṅgada**, the crown prince; the boy's scouts are the source of her intelligence in §II. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 15, 22.)
- **Afterwards queen of Sugrīva** - After Vālī's death and Sugrīva's consecration Tārā becomes Sugrīva's wife alongside **Rumā**; Vālmīki states it without comment, and Tārā herself names it as one of the goods Rāma's arrow restored to Sugrīva — "everlasting kingship of the monkeys, and Rumā, and myself." (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 35**; cf. 26, 33.)
  - *Note:* the Pañcakanyā tradition reads this second marriage, like **[Mandodarī's](./mandodari.md)**, as precisely the reason she is honoured — virtue measured by inner fidelity to dharma rather than by the simple norm of one husband. [scholarly]
- **Among the Pañcakanyā** - She is the fourth name of the daily-remembrance verse *Ahalyā Draupadī Kuntī Tārā Mandodarī tathā…*, the "five maidens" whose recollection is said to destroy great sin (*later subhāṣita / smṛti tradition*). [late text] See [Ahalyā](./ahalya.md) for the full verse and its logic.
  - *Variant (which Tārā?):* commentators occasionally read the verse's Tārā as Bṛhaspati's wife rather than Vālī's; the dominant tradition, and the corpus's reading, is the **vānara queen**. [disputed] See [Soma, Tārā & the Tārakāmaya War](../soma-tara-budha.md).

## II. The warning before the second duel (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 15)

- **She catches him at the gate** - When Sugrīva returns and roars his second challenge, Tārā takes hold of Vālī as he goes out and **begs him not to fight**. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 15**.)
- **The argument is intelligence, not sentiment** - Her case is analytical and it is right: a brother beaten to the ground days ago does not come back **so soon, and so confident**, unless something has changed — and what has changed, as **Aṅgada's scouts report**, is that Sugrīva has made an ally of **Rāma**, whose prowess she then recounts. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 15.)
- **The policy she proposes** - Her conclusion is statecraft: with a power like Rāma's in the field the sound course is **alliance, not war** — make Sugrīva crown prince, receive Rāma as a friend, and lose nothing. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 15.)
- **Dismissed** - Vālī answers with pride — that he cannot refuse a challenge, that Rāma is righteous and will not strike him — and goes out to the duel in which Rāma's concealed arrow kills him. Vālmīki has built the scene so that the king dies of ignoring the only person who understood the situation. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 15–16.) See [Sugrīva & Vālī](./sugriva-and-vali.md).
  - *Note (sarga number):* the counsel is **Kiṣkindhā 15**, immediately after the second challenge at 14 and immediately before the fatal arrow at 16; rosters that place it at 16 have slipped a sarga.

## III. The battlefield, the lament, and the arrow she asks for (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 19–24)

- **She refuses to flee** - Hearing that Vālī has fallen, Tārā runs from Kiṣkindhā toward the battlefield with Aṅgada. The vānara generals, fleeing the other way, urge her to **crown Aṅgada at once or evacuate the city** before Sugrīva's people take it. She rejects the counsel outright: *"Of what avail is a son, or a kingdom, or I to myself, when my husband, that lion among monkeys, has perished?"* — and walks on to where he lies. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 19**.)
- **The lament** - Her grief-speech over Vālī's body is among the Rāmāyaṇa's celebrated ***vilāpa*** passages, and it is not only grief: she arraigns her own unheeded warning, and names herself the widow of a king who would not listen. **Hanumān** consoles her; she laments again. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 20, 21, 23**.)
- **"Kill me with the same arrow"** - She asks **Rāma** to kill her with the shaft that killed Vālī, arguing that a wife is her husband's own self and that the killing would therefore be no sin, so that she may follow him. Rāma refuses, consoles her, tells her Aṅgada will be crown prince and her honour secure, and she abandons the resolve. In the same sarga Sugrīva, stricken with remorse, resolves to enter his brother's pyre and is likewise dissuaded. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 24**.)
- **Vālī's last instruction about her** - Before dying, Vālī tells Sugrīva to **be guided by Tārā's counsel in everything** — "she never errs in judging what is to be done" — in the same breath in which he hands over Indra's golden pendant and commends Aṅgada to his brother's care. The epic thus certifies her judgement from the mouth of the man who ignored it. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 22**.)
- **The cremation** - She is present at Vālī's funeral rites on the mountain stream, and passes with the household into Sugrīva's reign. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 25–26.)
  - *Variant (Tārā's curse on Rāma):* in several later tellings Tārā, by the power of her chastity, **curses Rāma** that he too will lose his wife soon after regaining her; the Bengali [Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/recensions/krittivasi-ramayan.md) adds that **in his next birth Rāma will be killed by Vālī** — the Kṛṣṇa-and-the-hunter link. There is no curse in Vālmīki, where she asks for death rather than pronouncing one. [variant] [folk]

## IV. The interception of Lakṣmaṇa (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 33–35)

- **The crisis** - The rains end, Sugrīva has done nothing, and **Lakṣmaṇa** comes into Kiṣkindhā armed and shaking with rage, twanging his bowstring at the palace until the sound goes through the city. Sugrīva, terrified, jerks up from his seat — and **sends Tārā out to meet him**, because no one else can. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 33**.)
- **The approach** - She comes out to Lakṣmaṇa unarmed and unhurried, acknowledges at once that **Rāma's anger is just** and that the time allowed has run out — conceding the whole charge before answering it — and reminds him that **great souls are not violent with women**. He hears her; she brings him inside. Disarming him by admitting he is right is the move the scene turns on. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 33**.)
- **The discourse** - Once Lakṣmaṇa has delivered his reproach to Sugrīva (34), Tārā answers it at length (35), and the speech is her masterpiece:
  - **Character** — Sugrīva is not ungrateful, not treacherous, not false; he has never forgotten who removed Vālī from his path.
  - **Human nature** — a man newly restored to wife, throne and kingdom loses his hold on time; she cites **Viśvāmitra**, who lost ten years with the apsaras **Ghṛtācī** and did not notice them passing. The precedent is chosen to be unanswerable by a brahmanically-minded prince.
  - **Scale** — Rāvaṇa's rākṣasas are numbered in crores; this is not a war two men and a monkey-king can walk into, but one that requires a **mustered host**.
  - **Fact** — and the host is *already coming*: the summons has gone out, millions of monkeys and bears are on the roads, and they will arrive today. Sugrīva is not idle; he is waiting for an army.
  (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 35**.)
- **The outcome** - Lakṣmaṇa relents. Sugrīva apologises, disclaims all merit — saying it was Rāma's arrow and not his own strength that won him the kingdom — and the muster proceeds into the four-quarter search. Without this speech the alliance breaks in the one season when Sītā's whereabouts are still unknown. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 35–36.) See [Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md).
  - *Note (where the scene sits):* Tārā's approach to Lakṣmaṇa is **sarga 33** and her discourse **sarga 35**; sarga 34 is Lakṣmaṇa's reproach to Sugrīva, in which Tārā does not speak. Citations that compress the whole episode into a single sarga, or shift it earlier, blur three distinct movements.

## V. Character and reception

- **The epic's political intelligence** - Of the three great counsel-scenes in the Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa — Tārā to Vālī, Hanumān to Aṅgada, Jāmbavān to the party — hers is the only one that is **right and ignored**, and the only one delivered by a woman to two different kings and a prince of Ayodhyā. Vālmīki uses her to argue that the vānara court has statecraft, not merely strength. [scholarly]
- **Grief given an argument** - Her lament is unusual among the epic's *vilāpa* passages in that it reasons: she does not only mourn, she indicts a policy. It is quoted in the rhetorical tradition as a model of *karuṇa-rasa* with forensic content. [scholarly]
- **Pañcakanyā and its logic** - Her place in the five-maidens verse rests on a life that broke the ordinary norm — widowed, remarried to her husband's killer's ally and brother — and is honoured for fidelity of judgement and of dharma rather than of circumstance. See [Ahalyā](./ahalya.md), [Mandodarī](./mandodari.md). [late text]
- **In the retellings** - Vernacular and performance traditions expand her enormously: the curse on Rāma (§III), extended debates with Sugrīva, and in some Rāmlīlā and Yakṣagāna repertoires a full scene of confrontation with Rāma over the manner of Vālī's killing. [folk] [regional]
  - *Variant (churning-born apsaras):* a strand of later retelling makes Tārā an **apsaras born of the [churning of the ocean](../samudra-manthana.md)**, taken by Vālī while he assisted at the churning. **No named text carries it**, and it is recorded here as unsourced tradition. [folk] [open-ended]
