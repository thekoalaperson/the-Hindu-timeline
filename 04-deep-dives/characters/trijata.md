# Trijaṭā — the Compassionate Rākṣasī of the Aśoka-vana

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Trijaṭā** (Trijaṭa) — the lone kindly rākṣasī of Laṅkā who guards and consoles the captive [Sītā](./sita.md) in the Aśoka grove, restrains the other demonesses from harming her, and dreams the prophetic dream that foretells [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md)'s ruin and Rāma's victory. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). Her whole role unfolds in the [Sundara Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md) (the prophetic dream) and the Yuddha Kāṇḍa; later tradition makes her the daughter of [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* her scenes fall in the Tretā Yuga of the present 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara; the scholarly baseline is the Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa* (cited here by vulgate/Southern sarga), with the *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa* and Tulsīdās's *Rāmcaritmānas* layering a bhakti reading above it (c. 1st millennium CE onward). [scholarly]

## Name and identity

- **Trijaṭā (त्रिजटा)** - "she of the three braids / three twisted locks" (*tri* "three" + *jaṭā* "matted lock"), an aged rākṣasī set among the demonesses guarding Sītā in Rāvaṇa's Aśoka grove; she alone among them is moved by compassion (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍa*).
- **The old / venerable rākṣasī** - Vālmīki describes her as aged (*vṛddhā*) and wise, which lends authority to her dream and her rebuke of the younger demonesses (*Sundara Kāṇḍa 27*).
- **No parentage in Vālmīki** - the epic never names her father or mother; the identification of Trijaṭā as Vibhīṣaṇa's daughter is later, widely-repeated tradition (see below). [late text]
- **Note on namesakes** - "Trijaṭā" is also borne by a male rākṣasa elsewhere and by a brahmin in the *Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa*; the Aśoka-vana figure is distinct from these unrelated namesakes. [scholarly]

## The guard of the Aśoka-vana

- **One demoness among many** - after Sītā's abduction, Rāvaṇa lodges her in the Aśoka grove under a watch of fierce rākṣasīs charged with breaking her resistance by turns of threat and persuasion; Trijaṭā is counted among this guard (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍa* / *Araṇya–Sundara* transition).
- **The threats of the other rākṣasīs** - the demonesses alternately cajole Sītā to accept Rāvaṇa and, when she refuses, threaten to tear her limb from limb and devour her; Sītā, terrified, longs for death (*Sundara Kāṇḍa 23–26*).
- **Trijaṭā's restraint of the demonesses** - at the height of their cruelty Trijaṭā wakes and rebukes the others, forbidding them to harm Sītā — declaring that she has just dreamt a terrible dream foretelling the destruction of Rāvaṇa and the rākṣasas and the triumph of Rāma (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍa 27*).
- **The compassionate exception** - she stands as the single sympathetic figure within the demon-women's circle, the human face of mercy inside Laṅkā's hostility, anticipating the kindness Sītā elsewhere meets only from Saramā and Hanumān (*Sundara Kāṇḍa*). [scholarly]

## The prophetic dream (Svapna of Sundara Kāṇḍa 27)

- **The frame** - Trijaṭā narrates her dream precisely to silence and frighten the other rākṣasīs into sparing Sītā; the dream functions in the epic as a divine omen confirming, from within the enemy camp, that Rāma will prevail (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍa 27*).
- **Rāma victorious on the ivory elephant / aerial car** - she sees Rāma, accompanied by Lakṣmaṇa, mounted on a great white elephant (or a celestial aerial chariot drawn by a thousand swans/horses in variant readings), reunited with Sītā — a vision of his coming victory and the recovery of his queen (*Sundara Kāṇḍa 27*).
- **Sītā clad in white, reunited** - she sees Sītā robed in white, seated with Rāma upon a white mountain or a heap of bones (Laṅkā's ruin), and washing the limbs of her lord — signs of restoration and honour (*Sundara Kāṇḍa 27*).
- **Rāvaṇa's doom foreseen** - she sees Rāvaṇa fallen, shaven-headed, smeared with oil, clad in red, drunk, dragged southward (the direction of Yama and death) in a chariot drawn by asses, sinking into a pit of mire — the classic dream-omens of impending death (*Sundara Kāṇḍa 27*).
- **Laṅkā in flames; the demon-line undone** - she sees Kumbhakarṇa and the sons of Rāvaṇa likewise fallen, the city of Laṅkā with its gates and ramparts burning and cast into the sea — while [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md) alone is seen exalted, clad in white, surviving the ruin (*Sundara Kāṇḍa 27*). [scholarly]
- **The counsel that follows** - on the strength of the dream Trijaṭā urges the demonesses to seek Sītā's favour rather than her harm, since she who will be queen of the victors should now be propitiated; this stays their hands (*Sundara Kāṇḍa 27*).

## Comfort to Sītā and the Yuddha Kāṇḍa role

- **Consoling Sītā in despair** - Trijaṭā's reassurance steadies Sītā at her lowest, when she has resolved to end her life; the dream is offered as a promise that rescue is near (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara/Yuddha Kāṇḍa*).
- **The false head is exposed by Saramā, not by her** - When Rāvaṇa, with the conjurer **Vidyujjihva**, sets an illusory severed head of Rāma and a phantom bow before Sītā to break her (Yuddha 31–32), the woman who comes to her, tells her it is *māyā*, and assures her that Rāma lives is **Saramā** — Vibhīṣaṇa's wife — who then goes and eavesdrops on Rāvaṇa's council for her (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa* **33–34**). The vulgate assigns this scene to Saramā throughout; retellings that give it to Trijaṭā are merging the epic's two kind women of Laṅkā. See [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md). [disputed]
- **The aerial view of the battle** - In a celebrated scene Trijaṭā takes the grieving Sītā up in the **Puṣpaka** to look over the corpse-field where Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa lie bound by Indrajit's arrows, naming the warriors and showing her that her husband and his brother live — a device that lets the poet survey the whole host through Sītā's eyes (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa* **47–48**).
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas):* Tulsīdās expands this into a tender exchange in which Trijaṭā repeatedly comforts Sītā, denounces Rāvaṇa's wickedness, and bears devout witness to Rāma's divinity (*Rāmcaritmānas, Sundara/Laṅkā Kāṇḍa*). [late text]

## Later tradition: daughter of Vibhīṣaṇa

- **Daughter of Vibhīṣaṇa and Saramā — not in Vālmīki** - **Vālmīki gives Trijaṭā no parentage at all.** The identification of her as the daughter of the righteous [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md) and his wife Saramā is **later tradition** — near-universal, and attractive because it neatly explains her sympathy for Sītā and her foreknowledge of the rākṣasa line's fall, but supplied rather than found. State it that way and not more strongly. [late text]
  - *Variant:* some recensions instead pair Trijaṭā with the informant-daughter of Saramā named Kalā / Nandā / Analā, blurring the two kindly-rākṣasī figures who console Sītā. [disputed]
- **The bhakti reframing (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa & Rāmcaritmānas)** - in the *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa* and Tulsīdās's *Rāmcaritmānas* Trijaṭā becomes an avowed devotee of Rāma; her dream and consolations are recast as the testimony of a soul who has recognised Rāma's supreme Godhead from within demon-ruled Laṅkā (*Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa*; *Rāmcaritmānas*). [late text]
- **A model of compassion and devotion** - in devotional retellings Trijaṭā is honoured as proof that grace can flower even in a rākṣasa heart, the demoness who chose dharma and Rāma over the malice of her kin — a feminine counterpart to her (traditional) father Vibhīṣaṇa's *śaraṇāgati*. [late text]
- **Regional and folk afterlife** - she recurs across vernacular Rāmāyaṇas and Rāmlīlā performance as the gentle confidante of the captive Sītā, sometimes given expanded dialogue and her own short hymns of praise to Rāma. [folk]/[regional]
