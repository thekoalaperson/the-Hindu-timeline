# Trijaṭā — the Compassionate Rākṣasī of the Aśoka-vana

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Trijaṭā** (Trijaṭa), the one kindly rākṣasī of Laṅkā. She stops the other demonesses from harming the captive [Sītā](./sita.md) in the Aśoka grove with a prophetic dream of [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md)'s ruin and Rāma's victory, and later reassures Sītā on the battlefield. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). In Vālmīki she has exactly two scenes: the dream in the [Sundara Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md) (sarga 27) and the flight over the battlefield in the [Yuddha Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md) (sargas 47–48). Later tradition makes her the daughter of [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md), and some texts make her his wife or sister instead.
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* her scenes fall in the Tretā Yuga of the present 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara. The scholarly baseline is the Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa*, cited here by vulgate sarga.verse with the Baroda Critical Edition (CE) given where checked. The Mahābhārata's *Rāmopākhyāna* gives a second, independent early portrait, and the *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa* and Tulsīdās's *Rāmcaritmānas* layer a bhakti reading above both (c. 1st millennium CE onward). [scholarly]

## Name and identity

- **Trijaṭā (त्रिजटा)** - "she of the three braids / three twisted locks" (*tri* "three" + *jaṭā* "matted lock"). She is one of the rākṣasīs guarding Sītā in Rāvaṇa's Aśoka grove, and alone among them she takes Sītā's side (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍa* 27).
- **The old rākṣasī** - Vālmīki introduces her as aged and as having been asleep until the threats woke her: *rākṣasī trijaṭā vṛddhā śayānā vākyam abravīt* (*Sundara* 27.4; CE 5.25.4, printed there as *trijaṭāvṛddhā*). Her age lends authority to her dream and to her rebuke of the others.
- **No parentage in Vālmīki** - the epic never names her father or mother. Vālmīki does name a daughter of Vibhīṣaṇa, **Analā** (or Nalā), but she is a different woman (see below). Every identification of Trijaṭā with Vibhīṣaṇa's family is later. [late text]
- **Namesake** - a poor brahmin of the Garga clan called **Trijaṭa** (masculine) appears in the *Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa*. Rāma, giving away his wealth before the exile, tells him in jest that he can have as many cows as his thrown staff can reach (*Ayodhyā* 32.29 ff.; CE 2.29.22–27). He is unrelated to the rākṣasī.

## The guard of the Aśoka-vana

- **One demoness among many** - Rāvaṇa lodges Sītā in the Aśoka grove under a watch of fierce rākṣasīs, who are told to break her resistance by threats and persuasion. Trijaṭā is one of this guard (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍa* 23–27).
- **The threats of the other rākṣasīs** - after Rāvaṇa's visit, the demonesses first urge Sītā to accept him and then, when she refuses, threaten to cut her up and eat her. Several of them are named (*Sundara* 23–24). Sītā laments that she would welcome death (*Sundara* 25–26). As sarga 27 opens, some go off to report her defiance to Rāvaṇa, and the rest tell her that they will now eat her flesh (*Sundara* 27.1–3; CE 5.25.1–3).
- **Trijaṭā's rebuke** - Trijaṭā wakes and turns on them: "eat yourselves; you will not eat Sītā," the dear daughter of Janaka and daughter-in-law of Daśaratha (*ātmānaṃ khādatānāryā na sītāṃ bhakṣayiṣyatha*, 27.5; CE 5.25.5). She has just seen a terrible dream, one that means the rākṣasas' destruction and her husband's rise (27.6). The frightened demonesses ask her to tell it (27.7–8) (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍa* 27).
- **She speaks to the guards, not to Sītā** - throughout sarga 27 Trijaṭā addresses the rākṣasīs. Sītā hears her, and so does Hanumān, hidden in the tree: "Hanumān heard everything, Sītā's words, Trijaṭā's, and the threats of the rākṣasīs" (*Sundara* 30.1; CE 5.28.1). In the vulgate Hanumān later repeats her rebuke and the gist of the dream to the vānaras, together with Sītā's reply (*Sundara* 58.84–89). The CE's text of that report leaves the whole episode out: the guards fall asleep and Sītā laments (CE 5.56.73–75).
- **The compassionate exception** - she is the one sympathetic figure among the guards. The only other kindness Sītā meets in Laṅkā comes from **Saramā** (*Yuddha* 33–34), from Vibhīṣaṇa's daughter and the mother who sent her (*Sundara* 37.11), and from Hanumān. [scholarly]

## The prophetic dream (Svapna of Sundara Kāṇḍa 27)

- **The frame** - Trijaṭā tells her dream to frighten the other rākṣasīs into sparing Sītā. In the epic the dream works as an omen, from inside the enemy camp, that Rāma will win (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍa* 27).
- **Rāma's vehicles** - the dream gives Rāma several vehicles in turn:
  - **an ivory palanquin moving through the sky** (*gajadantamayīṃ divyāṃ śibikām antarikṣagām*), which he rides with Lakṣmaṇa, both wearing white garlands and white clothes (27.9–10). The vulgate text yokes it to a thousand swans (*haṃsasahasreṇa*). The CE reads a thousand horses (*vājisahasreṇa*, CE 5.25.10).
  - **a great four-tusked elephant, as large as a mountain** (*caturdantaṃ mahāgajam … śailasaṃkāśam*), on which Rāma moves about with Lakṣmaṇa (27.12; CE 5.25.12). Vālmīki does not name the elephant.
  - **a chariot drawn by eight white bulls** (27.17; CE 5.25.17).
  - **the Puṣpaka**, in which he flies north with Lakṣmaṇa and Sītā (27.18–19).
- **Sītā restored** - Sītā sits robed in white on a white mountain ringed by the ocean, united with Rāma "as light with the sun" (27.11–12; CE 5.25.11). She sits on the back of the elephant standing in the sky, rises from her husband's lap and wipes the sun and the moon with her hands (27.14–15; CE 5.25.14–15), and the elephant stands over Laṅkā (27.16).
- **Rāvaṇa's doom** - the dream gives Rāvaṇa the standard omens of approaching death, one after another:
  - on the ground, smeared with oil, dressed in red, wearing oleander garlands, drinking and drunk (27.22).
  - fallen from the Puṣpaka, shaven-headed, in black clothes, and dragged by a woman (27.23; CE 5.25.18).
  - riding south in a chariot drawn by donkeys, in red garlands and unguents, drinking oil, laughing and dancing (27.24–25; CE 5.25.19). He falls head-first from the donkey (27.25–26).
  - sinking, naked and raving, into a stinking pit of mire (27.27; CE 5.25.19 *praviṣṭaḥ kardamaṃ hradam*).
  - tied by the throat and hauled south, the direction of Yama, by a black woman in red with mud-smeared limbs (27.28; CE 5.25.20).
- **His house goes the same way** - Kumbhakarṇa and all Rāvaṇa's sons are smeared with oil (27.29). Rāvaṇa rides south on a boar, Indrajit on a porpoise (*śiṃśumāra*), and Kumbhakarṇa on a camel (27.30; CE 5.25.21). The rākṣasa chiefs put on red cloth and enter a pool of cow-dung (27.38; CE 5.25.25). A great drinking party of rākṣasas in red sings and plays (27.34; CE 5.25.22), and rākṣasa women drink oil, laugh and dance in a Laṅkā "rough with ash" (27.37; CE 5.25.24).
- **Vibhīṣaṇa apart; Laṅkā in the sea** - Vibhīṣaṇa alone appears under a white umbrella, in white garlands, clothes and unguents. To the sound of conches and drums he mounts a four-tusked elephant and rises into the sky with his four ministers (27.31–33). Laṅkā falls into the ocean with its gates and arches broken (27.35; CE 5.25.23). It has been burned by "a swift vānara, Rāma's messenger" (*dagdhā rāmasya dūtena vānareṇa tarasvinā*, 27.36), which points ahead to Hanumān.
  - *Text note (CE):* the CE's text of this sarga (5.25, 38 verses against the vulgate's 51) lacks several vulgate verses. Among them are the Vibhīṣaṇa verses (27.31–33), the burning by Rāma's messenger (27.36), the flight north in the Puṣpaka (27.18–19), Rāma's being "of Viṣṇu's prowess" and unconquerable (27.20–21), the first oiled-Rāvaṇa verse (27.22), and the oiling of Kumbhakarṇa and the sons (27.29). The fall from the donkey and the sinking in the mire (27.24–27) are compressed into one verse (CE 5.25.19). Read as the CE prints it, the dream says nothing of any rākṣasa surviving the fall. [scholarly]
- **The counsel that follows** - Trijaṭā tells the guards to go: Rāma will win Sītā back and will not tolerate threats against his wife (27.39–40). "Enough of cruel words; let us speak gently and beg Vaidehī's favour" (*abhiyācāma vaidehīm etad dhi mama rocate*, 27.41; CE 5.25.28). A woman in sorrow who has such a dream is soon freed from her sorrows (27.42). Sītā, appeased by prostration, can protect them from the great danger coming from Rāma (27.43–45; CE 5.25.30–31). (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍa* 27)
- **Reading Sītā's body** - Trijaṭā ends by reading the signs on Sītā's body. She finds no inauspicious mark, only a dimming of complexion from grief (27.45–46). She notes the throbbing of Sītā's eye, left arm and left thigh, and a bird calling consolingly from a branch, all omens that Rāma is near (27.47–51; CE 5.25.34–38). Sarga 29 repeats the same body-omens in the narrator's voice.
  - *Variant (Sītā's promise):* Wikipedia's summary of C. Bulcke adds that Sītā promises to protect the guards if the dream comes true. The vulgate has this promise, but only in Hanumān's later report of the scene: "if it is true, I will be your refuge" (*avocad yadi tat tathyaṃ bhaveyaṃ śaraṇaṃ hi vaḥ*, *Sundara* 58.89). It is not in the valmikiramayan.net text of sarga 27, in CE 5.25, or in the CE's version of the report. [vulgate only]

## Comfort to Sītā and the Yuddha Kāṇḍa role

- **The dream does not end Sītā's despair** - in Vālmīki the dream does not steady Sītā. In the very next sarga she still decides to die and reaches for her own braid to hang herself (*Sundara* 28.16–18). The good omens that appear as she holds the branch (28.19–20, sarga 29) turn her back, and then Hanumān speaks (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍa* 28–29). In later retellings Trijaṭā is the one who talks her out of suicide (see the *Rāmcaritmānas* below).
- **The false head is exposed by Saramā, not by her** - Rāvaṇa, with the conjurer **Vidyujjihva**, sets an illusory severed head of Rāma and a phantom bow before Sītā to break her (*Yuddha* 31–32). The woman who comes to her, tells her it is *māyā*, and assures her that Rāma lives is **Saramā** (*saramā nāma rākṣasī*). Saramā then goes and eavesdrops on Rāvaṇa's council for her (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa* **33–34**; CE 6.24–25). The vulgate gives this scene to Saramā throughout. Retellings that give it to Trijaṭā have merged the epic's two kind women of Laṅkā. The earliest such text found here is Kālidāsa's *Raghuvaṃśa*, where Trijaṭā revives Sītā, dazed by the sight of Rāma's severed head, by telling her it is illusion (*sītāṃ māyeti śaṃsantī trijaṭā samajīvayat*, *Raghuvaṃśa* 12.74). See [Saramā](./sarama.md) and [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md). [late text]
- **The aerial view of the battle** - Indrajit's arrows have bound Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa and left them senseless. **Rāvaṇa** summons the guards and Trijaṭā (*rākṣasyas trijaṭā cāpi*, *Yuddha* 47.6; CE 6.37.6) and orders them to fly Sītā over the field in the **Puṣpaka**, so that, seeing her husband dead, she will give up hope and submit to him (47.7–10). Sītā rides out "with Trijaṭā" (*sītāṃ trijaṭayā saha*, 47.14, 47.17; CE 6.37.14, 6.37.16). She sees the brothers lying on a bed of arrows and breaks into lament (47.19–24; 48.1–21) (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa* **47–48**).
- **Trijaṭā's reasons** - this is the only place in Vālmīki where Trijaṭā speaks to Sītā directly. "Do not despair, lady; your husband lives" (48.22; CE 6.38.22). She offers evidence rather than comfort (48.23–33; CE 6.38.23–33):
  - an army whose leader has fallen shows neither anger nor eagerness in its faces, and drifts "like a ship that has lost its rudder". This army is calm and is guarding the two princes (48.24, 48.26–27).
  - the celestial Puṣpaka would not be carrying her if the two were dead (*divyaṃ tvāṃ dhārayen nedaṃ yady etau gatajīvitau*, 48.25; CE 6.38.25; both texts print *gaja-* for *gata-*). The usual reading is that the vehicle would not bear a widow, though the verse itself does not say so. [scholarly]
  - though senseless, the brothers' faces have not lost their radiance, while the faces of the dead change terribly (48.31–33).
- **Her pledge of friendship** - "I have never spoken a falsehood and never will. By your conduct and your gentle nature you have entered my heart" (*anṛtaṃ noktapūrvaṃ me na ca vakṣye kadā cana / cāritrasukhaśīlatvāt praviṣṭāsi mano mama*, 48.29; CE 6.38.29). Sītā answers with joined palms, "may it be so" (48.34). Trijaṭā brings her back to Laṅkā, and the two come down from the Puṣpaka into the Aśoka grove (48.35–36; CE 6.38.35–36).
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas):* Tulsīdās drops this flight but makes Trijaṭā Sītā's regular source of news and comfort throughout the war (see the *Rāmcaritmānas* below). [late text]
- **Vālmīki's last word on her** - after *Yuddha* 48 (CE 6.38) the Critical Edition does not name Trijaṭā again. She is absent from the reunion and from the *Uttara Kāṇḍa*, and no verse rewards her. After Rāvaṇa's death, when Hanumān offers to kill the guards who tormented Sītā, Sītā refuses. She says they were servants acting on their king's orders. Trijaṭā is not singled out in that scene (*Yuddha* 113.30–45). [scholarly]

## Trijaṭā in the Mahābhārata (Rāmopākhyāna)

The Mahābhārata's retelling of the Rāma story, which the sage Mārkaṇḍeya narrates to Yudhiṣṭhira (*Vana/Āraṇyaka Parva*, CE 3.257–276, with Mārkaṇḍeya's closing consolation at 3.276), gives Trijaṭā a larger and somewhat different part. See [Āraṇyaka Parva](../mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md) and [the Rāmopākhyāna among the variant Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md).

- **In the guard-list** - Rāvaṇa's grotesque guards are listed by their deformities: two-eyed, three-eyed, eye-in-forehead, long-tongued, tongueless, three-breasted, one-footed, and "*trijaṭā*, one-eyed" (*tristanīm ekapādāṃ ca trijaṭām ekalocanām*, MBh 3.264.44). Here the word may simply describe a type, "the three-braided one".
- **She speaks to Sītā herself, as a friend** - once the others have gone to report to Rāvaṇa, "a rākṣasī named Trijaṭā, knowing dharma and kind of speech" (*dharmajñā priyavādinī*) comforts Sītā in private and calls her *sakhi*, "friend" (MBh 3.264.53–54). In Vālmīki her dream is aimed at the guards. Here it is told to Sītā.
- **The messenger of Avindhya** - she brings word from **Avindhya**, a wise old rākṣasa who wishes Rāma well: Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa are safe, and Rāma has made an alliance with the vānara king (Sugrīva). Rāvaṇa cannot force her, because **Nalakūbara's curse**, laid on him when he violated Rambhā, stops him from taking an unwilling woman (MBh 3.264.55–60). In Vālmīki's CE, Avindhya only warns that Rāma will destroy the rākṣasas, and Rāvaṇa does not listen. Sītā mentions his counsel to Hanumān right after Analā's report (CE 5.35.11–13). The Avindhya verses are not in the valmikiramayan.net text of vulgate *Sundara* 37.
- **A different dream** - her dreams (*svapnāḥ*, plural) also show signs of Rāvaṇa's end (MBh 3.264.61–71), but the details differ from Vālmīki's:
  - Rāvaṇa, oiled and *vikaca* (shorn, or with hair dishevelled), sinks into mud and stands, as if dancing, in a donkey-chariot (64).
  - Kumbhakarṇa and the rest, naked, in red garlands, are dragged south (65).
  - Vibhīṣaṇa alone, under a white umbrella with a turban and white garlands, stands on a **white mountain** with his four ministers (66–67).
  - Rāma's weapon encircles the earth (68).
  - **Lakṣmaṇa** sits on a **heap of bones** eating honeyed milk-rice and looking in every direction (*asthisaṃcayam ārūḍho bhuñjāno madhupāyasam*, 69).
  - **Sītā**, weeping and wet with blood, guarded by a tiger, goes north (70).
  - Trijaṭā promises that Sītā will soon be reunited with her husband (71).
- **Afterwards** - Sītā takes hope (MBh 3.264.72). She lives on in the grove "attended by Trijaṭā" (*sevyamānā trijaṭayā*, 3.265.30). When Hanumān comes, Sītā says she recognises him because of Avindhya's report (3.266.64–65).
- **Rewarded by Rāma** - after the victory Rāma gives Avindhya a boon and "joined the rākṣasī Trijaṭā with wealth and honour" (*trijaṭāṃ cārthamānābhyāṃ yojayām āsa rākṣasīm*, MBh 3.275.39). Vālmīki has no such scene.

## The Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa and the Rāmcaritmānas

- **Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa** - as the guards threaten Sītā with swords and gaping mouths, "the aged rākṣasī Trijaṭā" (*trijaṭā vṛddhā rākṣasī*) stops them and tells them to bow to Jānakī (*Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍa* 2.47–49). Her dream (2.49–54) differs from Vālmīki's:
  - Rāma comes with Lakṣmaṇa on the white **Airāvata**.
  - Rāma himself burns all Laṅkā and kills Rāvaṇa in battle, then sits on a mountain-top with Jānakī on his lap.
  - Rāvaṇa, oiled and naked, plunges into a pool of cow-dung with his sons and grandsons.
  - Vibhīṣaṇa, glad at heart, serves Rāma's feet with devotion (*bhaktisaṃyutaḥ*) and receives the lordship of Laṅkā.

  The frightened guards fall silent and go to sleep (2.55), and Sītā, still terrified, looks for a way to die (2.56–57). The Adhyātma passage gives the devotion to **Vibhīṣaṇa**, not to Trijaṭā, and Trijaṭā does not appear in its *Yuddha Kāṇḍa*. See [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md). [late text]
- **Rāmcaritmānas, the dream** - Tulsīdās introduces her as "a rākṣasī named Trijaṭā, devoted to Rāma's feet and skilled in discernment" (*trijaṭā nāma rācchasī ekā / rāma carana rati nipuna bibekā*, *Sundara Kāṇḍa* 11.1). She tells the guards to serve Sītā for their own good, and her dream has its own content: a monkey burns Laṅkā and kills the rākṣasa army; Rāvaṇa rides a donkey, naked, shaven, his twenty arms cut off, heading south; Laṅkā passes to Vibhīṣaṇa; Rāma's proclamation goes through the city, and he sends for Sītā. She says it will come true within a few days (*gaeṃ dina cārī*, literally "four days", an idiom for a short while), and the guards fall at Sītā's feet (11.2–4). In the Mānas Rāvaṇa's limit is **one month** (*māsa divasa bīteṃ*, dohā 11), against Vālmīki's two (*Sundara* 22.8).
- **Rāmcaritmānas, the pyre** - Sītā asks Trijaṭā with joined palms, "mother, you are my companion in misfortune" (*mātu bipati saṃgini taiṃ morī*), to build her a pyre and light it. Trijaṭā holds her feet, consoles her with the greatness of Rāma's power and fame, says "no fire is to be had at night", and goes home (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* 12.1–3). Hanumān then drops the ring (sorṭhā 12). [late text]
- **Rāmcaritmānas, the war** - in the *Laṅkā Kāṇḍa* Trijaṭā brings Sītā news of the war, including Indrajit's death. On the eve of the final duel she reassures her that Rāma will strike Rāvaṇa through the heart (S. L. Nagar p. 364 for Indrajit's death and C. Bulcke p. 109 for the duel, both as cited in Wikipedia). See [Rāmcaritmānas](../ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md). [late text]

## Later tradition: daughter of Vibhīṣaṇa

- **Daughter of Vibhīṣaṇa and Saramā — not in Vālmīki** - **Vālmīki gives Trijaṭā no parentage at all.** The identification of her as the daughter of the righteous [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md) and his wife Saramā is **later tradition**. It is attractive because it explains her sympathy for Sītā and her foreknowledge of the rākṣasa line's fall, but it is supplied rather than found in the text. Bulcke's survey lists the texts that make her **Vibhīṣaṇa's daughter**: Kamban's Tamil *Irāmāvatāram*, Govindarāja's *Bhūṣaṇa* commentary on Vālmīki, Balarāmadāsa's Oḍia *Rāmāyaṇa*, the Javanese *Kakawin Rāmāyaṇa* and the Malay *Hikayat Seri Rama*. Most later literature follows them. Goldman calls Govindarāja's reading "strange" given her advanced age in the epic. (C. Bulcke, "Sita's Friend Trijata," pp. 107–8, and R. P. Goldman and S. J. Sutherland Goldman, *Sundarakāṇḍa*, p. 422, both as cited in Wikipedia, "Trijata"). See [Kamban](../ramayana/recensions/kamban-iramavataram.md). [late text]
  - *Variant (other kinship):* the *Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa* makes her Vibhīṣaṇa's **wife**, and Eknāth's Marathi *Bhāvārtha Rāmāyaṇa* makes her his **sister**. The Jain *Vasudevahiṇḍī* of Saṃghadāsa makes her a sister of Rāvaṇa, Vibhīṣaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa and Śūrpaṇakhā, and Jayamaṅgala's commentary on the *Bhaṭṭikāvya* also calls her Rāvaṇa's sister (Bulcke pp. 107–8; U. P. Shah; G. G. Leonardi, as cited in Wikipedia). [disputed]
  - *Not to be merged:* the daughter of Vibhīṣaṇa whom Vālmīki does name is **Analā**. Sītā tells Hanumān that "Analā, Vibhīṣaṇa's eldest daughter, sent by her mother herself," told her that Rāvaṇa would not give her back (*jyeṣṭhā kanyānalā nāma vibhīṣaṇasutā kape / tayā mamaitad ākhyātam mātrā prahitayā svayam*, *Sundara* 37.11; CE 5.35.11). The sandhi can also be read *kanyā Nalā*, and some recensions call her **Kalā** or **Nandā** (Bulcke, via Wikipedia). Her informant role is the one the Mahābhārata gives to Trijaṭā (above). [disputed]
- **Roles taken over from others** - Bulcke observes that later literature hands Trijaṭā the roles Vālmīki gives to Vibhīṣaṇa's daughter, to Saramā and to Mandodarī (Bulcke p. 105, via Wikipedia):
  - besides the *Raghuvaṃśa* (above), the *Setubandha*, Balarāmadāsa's *Rāmāyaṇa*, the *Kakawin* and the *Seri Rama* give her Saramā's unmasking of the illusory head.
  - the *Prasannarāghava* has her tell Sītā about the burning of Laṅkā, and also has Sītā ask her for a pyre, which she refuses for want of firewood.
  - Balarāmadāsa has her stop Rāvaṇa from killing Sītā, Mandodarī's role elsewhere.
  - in Rājaśekhara's *Bālarāmāyaṇa* she sends two rākṣasas to bring her battle news.

  See [Saramā](./sarama.md) and [Mandodarī](./mandodari.md). [late text]
- **Rāvaṇa's agent instead** - a few texts reverse her. In the Jain versions of Svayambhūdeva (*Paümacariu*) and Hemacandra, Trijaṭā reports Sītā's joy at Hanumān's ring to Rāvaṇa, and Hemacandra says her task was to tempt Sītā on his behalf. The Bengali *Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇ* has her urge Sītā to marry Rāvaṇa, and makes Saramā the friend instead (Bulcke p. 110, via Wikipedia). See [Jain and Buddhist Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/recensions/jain-and-buddhist-ramayanas.md) and [Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇ](../ramayana/recensions/krittivasi-ramayan.md). [disputed]
- **After the war** - many retellings reward her, as the Mahābhārata already does.
  - the *Bālarāmāyaṇa* and the *Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa* bring her to Ayodhyā in the Puṣpaka with Sītā; the *Ānanda* brings Saramā too.
  - the *Kakawin* has Sītā honour her with gifts as the friend who twice saved her life.
  - the *Paümacariu* has her summoned from Laṅkā to vouch for Sītā's chastity before the final ordeal (Bulcke pp. 109–12, via Wikipedia). [late text]
- **The bhakti reframing (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa & Rāmcaritmānas)** - the devotee Trijaṭā belongs to the *Rāmcaritmānas* (*Sundara* 11.1). The *Adhyātma* keeps her as the "aged rākṣasī" and gives the explicit devotion to Vibhīṣaṇa. See the section above. [late text]
- **A model of compassion and devotion** - in devotional retellings Trijaṭā is honoured as proof that grace can flower even in a rākṣasa heart. She is the demoness who chose dharma and Rāma over the malice of her kin, a feminine counterpart to the *śaraṇāgati* of Vibhīṣaṇa, her father in the traditions that make her his daughter. [late text]
- **Southeast Asia** - she has a larger part in Southeast Asian tellings (Wikipedia, "Trijata," citing Bulcke and Kam):
  - in the *Kakawin Rāmāyaṇa* she alone comforts Sītā among three hundred guards and plays games with her.
  - in the Malay *Hikayat Seri Rama* (where she is Dewi Seri Jati / Srijati) she has charge of Sītā. She exposes Rāvaṇa's trick of the two false heads, carries Sītā's dagger to Rāma, and returns with his token. After the war she marries Hanumān and bears him a son.
  - Wikipedia (citing Bose, *The Ramayana Revisited*, p. 359) equates her with **Benyakai** of the Thai *Ramakien*, who also marries Hanumān, and with Punukay of the Lao *Phra Lak Phra Lam*. That name-equation is Wikipedia's, not checked here.

  See [Southeast Asian Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/recensions/southeast-asian-ramayanas.md). [regional] [disputed]
- **Regional and folk afterlife** - she recurs across vernacular Rāmāyaṇas and Rāmlīlā performance as the gentle confidante of the captive Sītā, sometimes with expanded dialogue and short hymns of praise to Rāma of her own. In the Mānas tradition Sītā addresses her as "mother" (*Sundara* 12.1). Local cults worship her as a goddess:
  - **Varanasi:** a shrine near the Kāśī Viśvanātha temple, crowded on Kārttika Pūrṇimā and the day after. Local lore says Sītā, unable to take a rākṣasī to Ayodhyā, sent her to Kāśī for liberation and blessed her to be worshipped.
  - **Ujjain:** a shrine in the Balvīr Hanumān temple complex, with a three-day pūjā from Kārttika Pūrṇimā.

  The cult details come from Hindi press accounts (Aaj Tak, News18, Dainik Bhaskar) cited at that passage in Wikipedia, "Trijata". The article also cites T. Pintchman, *Guests at God's Wedding* (2005), pp. 41–42, for a related Kāśī custom: those who keep the Kārttika vow end it with a Gaṅgā bath at Daśāśvamedha Ghāṭ in Trijaṭā's name. None of this was checked first-hand here. [folk] [regional]
