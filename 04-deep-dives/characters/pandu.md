# Pāṇḍu — the Pale King and the Kindama Curse

> Where this sits: a dedicated deep-dive on **Pāṇḍu ("the pale")** — *niyoga*-born son of Vyāsa and **Ambālikā**, king of Hāstinapura between his blind elder brother and his own abdication, husband of **Kuntī** and **Mādrī**, the conqueror whom the deer-sage **Kindama's curse** barred from fatherhood, nominal father of the five Pāṇḍavas by divine agency, and the king whose death in Mādrī's embrace opens the epic's succession crisis. Up: [Epic Characters](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md).
> Cross-links: his mother [Ambālikā](./ambika-ambalika.md); his brothers [Dhṛtarāṣṭra](./dhritarashtra.md) and [Vidura](./vidura.md); his queens [Kuntī](./kunti.md) and [Mādrī](./madri.md); the parva that carries his whole life, the [Ādi Parva walkthrough](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md).
> Two clocks: Pāṇḍu's life belongs to the **Dvāpara Yuga** of this 28th Mahāyuga of the Vaivasvata Manvantara as *itihāsa*; academic dating puts the Mahābhārata's composition and redaction roughly between c. 400 BCE and 400 CE, in layers. [scholarly]

## Identity & names

- **Pāṇḍu (पाण्डु)** — literally "the pale / whitish one"; the name records his complexion at birth, which the epic ties directly to the manner of his conception (below) (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Kaurava by line, father of the Pāṇḍavas by name** — Pāṇḍu is a scion of the Kuru house of [Śāntanu](../../03-lineages-vamsha/shantanu.md), yet his name attaches to the *anti*-Kaurava party: the five **Pāṇḍavas** ("sons of Pāṇḍu") carry his name although none is his son by body — the epic's central irony of paternity (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **The middle brother** — of Vyāsa's three sons in the Kuru palace, [Dhṛtarāṣṭra](./dhritarashtra.md) is the eldest (born blind), Pāṇḍu the second, and [Vidura](./vidura.md) the youngest (born of a śūdra maid); each defect or disqualification of birth shapes the succession (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).

## Birth by niyoga — why the king was born pale

- **The crisis of the empty line** — [Vicitravīrya](./vichitravirya-and-chitrangada.md) dies childless of consumption, leaving his two widows [Ambikā and Ambālikā](./ambika-ambalika.md) and no heir; the dowager [Satyavatī](./satyavati.md) first asks [Bhīṣma](./bhishma.md) to beget sons on the widows, and when he refuses by his vow she summons her premarital son, the sage **Vyāsa**, for *niyoga* (levirate begetting) (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Ambālikā's pallor** — warned by her sister's ordeal, **Ambālikā** keeps her eyes open before the grim-visaged sage but **turns pale with fright**; Vyāsa declares that her son will therefore be born **pale (pāṇḍu)** — and so he is named (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). The elder Ambikā, who shut her eyes, bears the **blind** Dhṛtarāṣṭra; the fearless maidservant bears the wise Vidura. See [Ambikā & Ambālikā](./ambika-ambalika.md) for the full niyoga night.
- **Field and seed** — by the epic's own law of the *kṣetra* (field), the boys are Vicitravīrya's sons in name and Vyāsa's by seed; Pāṇḍu is thus, like his brothers, a *niyoga* child — the very institution he will later invoke for his own sons (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).

## Accession — king past an elder brother

- **Dhṛtarāṣṭra passed over** — though eldest, Dhṛtarāṣṭra is set aside from the throne because of his **blindness** (a king must be whole-bodied); Vidura is barred by his mother's śūdra status; so **Pāṇḍu is consecrated king of Hāstinapura**, with Bhīṣma as guardian-regent and Vidura as counsellor (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). This inversion — younger crowned over elder — is the seed of the Kaurava grievance; see [Dhṛtarāṣṭra](./dhritarashtra.md).
- **The able young king** — the Ādi Parva praises Pāṇḍu's skill with the bow and his energetic rule; under him and Bhīṣma's stewardship the Kuru realm prospers (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).

## Marriages — Kuntī and Mādrī

- **Kuntī by svayaṃvara** — **Pṛthā (Kuntī)**, daughter of Śūra of the Yādava line and adoptive daughter of Kuntibhoja, chooses Pāṇḍu at her *svayaṃvara*; she becomes his senior queen (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). Her premarital son by Sūrya — Karṇa — is her sealed secret; see [Kuntī](./kunti.md).
- **Mādrī by bride-price** — Bhīṣma journeys to **Madra** and obtains its princess **Mādrī**, sister of [Śalya](./shalya.md), as Pāṇḍu's junior queen by paying the Madra **śulka** (bride-price) in gold, gems, elephants and horses, per Madra custom (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). See [Mādrī](./madri.md).

## The digvijaya — conquests of the pale king

- **The campaign of the four quarters** — soon after his consecration Pāṇḍu rides out on a war of conquest to restore Kuru supremacy diminished since [Śāntanu's](../../03-lineages-vamsha/shantanu.md) day: the Ādi Parva has him defeat the **Daśārṇas**, slay **Dīrgha (Dārva), the overweening king of Magadha**, at his own capital, and reduce **Mithilā (the Videhas)**, **Kāśī**, **Suhma** and **Puṇḍra** to tribute (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **The treasure brought home** — Pāṇḍu returns to Hāstinapura with vast spoils — gems, gold, cattle, horses, elephants — which he lays before Bhīṣma, Satyavatī and the elders; the epic marks this as the height of Kuru glory in his generation (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sacrifices funded** — with the wealth of Pāṇḍu's conquests Dhṛtarāṣṭra later performs great sacrifices with lavish gifts; the blind brother rules on the pale brother's treasure (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).

## The curse of Kindama

- **The fatal hunt** — fond of the chase, Pāṇḍu retires with his queens to the forested foothills and lives as a royal hunter; one day he shoots **a mating pair of deer** with five arrows — and the stag is in truth the sage **Kindama**, coupling with his mate in deer form (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Why a deer** — the dying sage explains that, out of shame at human congress in the open forest, he had taken **deer shape with his wife** to mate; Pāṇḍu's true offence is not the killing (a king may hunt deer) but killing a creature **in the act of union**, before its desire was fulfilled — a cruelty no righteous king should commit (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **The curse** — Kindama curses Pāṇḍu with the mirror of the wrong: **when Pāṇḍu next approaches a wife in desire, in that very act he shall die**, and the wife shall follow him in death; then the sage dies (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
  - *Variant (the sage's name and framing):* the Critical Edition and Ganguli translation carry the episode with the sage named **Kindama**; some retellings and summaries transmit the episode namelessly ("a certain ṛṣi in deer form") or soften the wife's inclusion in the curse. The dual-death clause is what Mādrī's end fulfils (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva; popular retellings*). [disputed]

## Abdication — the king becomes an ascetic

- **Renunciation of the throne** — stricken with remorse and despair ("I, begotten to continue the line, cannot beget"), Pāṇḍu resolves to renounce kingship and live as a forest ascetic; he sends his ornaments and wealth back to Hāstinapura, distributes gifts to brāhmaṇas, and **the rule passes in practice to Dhṛtarāṣṭra** (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **The queens refuse to leave** — Pāṇḍu bids Kuntī and Mādrī return to the capital; both **refuse absolutely**, declaring they will share his vows and his forest life; he yields, and the three live in austerity together (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **The mountain hermitages** — the epic tracks their wanderings through **Nāgaśata/Nāgasabha, Caitraratha, the Himālaya and Gandhamādana**, protected by great beings, until they settle at **Śataśṛṅga**, "the mountain of a hundred peaks," among the ascetics there (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Barred even from heaven** — the Śataśṛṅga sages tell Pāṇḍu that the sonless (*aputra*) man has no good worlds hereafter; the debt to the ancestors is paid only through offspring — which the curse forbids him to beget (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).

## The divine-father decision — how the Pāṇḍavas came to be

- **Pāṇḍu's plea for niyoga** — anguished, Pāṇḍu asks Kuntī to raise up offspring for him by another, invoking the old law: he recites the kinds of sons a man may own and the ancient liberty of women, citing how the sage **Śvetaketu**, son of Uddālaka, first fixed the strict marriage rule that had not always bound wives (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Kuntī's counter-story** — Kuntī resists, telling Pāṇḍu the tale of queen **Bhadrā and Vyuṣitāśva**, whose devotion raised sons from her dead husband himself; she will imagine no man in her arms but Pāṇḍu (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **The Durvāsā mantra revealed** — pressed, Kuntī reveals her secret: the irascible sage **Durvāsā**, pleased by her service in her father's house, gave her a **mantra of invocation** by which any deva she summons must beget a child upon her; Pāṇḍu, overjoyed, directs its use (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). The mantra's history — including Karṇa — is treated at [Kuntī](./kunti.md).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira (Dharma)** — Pāṇḍu chooses **Dharma** first, "for he is incapable of error"; a bodiless voice at the boy's birth proclaims him the best of the virtuous, **[Yudhiṣṭhira](./yudhishthira.md)** (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Bhīma (Vāyu)** — next Pāṇḍu asks for a son of matchless strength; Kuntī summons **Vāyu** and bears **[Bhīma](./bhima.md)**, born (the epic notes) on the same day as [Duryodhana](./duryodhana.md) (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Arjuna (Indra)** — for the third son Pāṇḍu performs a **year of austerities to propitiate Indra**, standing on one leg; Indra promises a son who will be world-famed; Kuntī summons him and bears **[Arjuna](./arjuna.md)**, at whose birth a divine voice prophesies his victories (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **The mantra lent, the count fixed at five** — at Pāṇḍu's asking Kuntī grants **Mādrī one use** of the invocation; Mādrī summons the **twin Aśvins** and bears the twins **[Nakula and Sahadeva](./nakula-sahadeva.md)**; when Pāṇḍu asks for yet more, Kuntī refuses — the epic (voiced through Pāṇḍu and Kuntī) holds that a woman who goes to a fourth is wanton — and the Pāṇḍavas number **five** (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). See [Mādrī](./madri.md).
  - *Variant (Kuntī's motive):* popular retellings colour the refusal as pique at being outdone by Mādrī's twin-trick; the critical text grounds it in the stated limit on lawful unions. [folk]

## Death in the spring, and after

- **The fatal embrace** — years later, in a flowering spring on Śataśṛṅga, Pāṇḍu, walking alone with **Mādrī**, is overwhelmed by desire; though she resists and warns him, he embraces her — and **Kindama's curse kills him in the act** (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Mādrī's sati** — after a contest of grief in which each queen claims the right to follow him, **Mādrī ascends Pāṇḍu's pyre**, arguing that Kuntī is better fitted to raise all five sons without favour; she entrusts the twins to Kuntī (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). The full argument is at [Mādrī](./madri.md).
- **The bodies brought to Hāstinapura** — the Śataśṛṅga ascetics escort **Kuntī and the five boys** to Hāstinapura and deliver the remains of Pāṇḍu and Mādrī; Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Bhīṣma and [Vidura](./vidura.md) perform the **funeral rites and śrāddha** with royal honours, and the city mourns its pale king (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **A father seen once more** — decades later, in the forest-retreat of the elders, **Vyāsa by his power raises the dead to sight for one night on the Gaṅgā**, and Pāṇḍu appears among the slain and departed, seen by his sons and the widows (*Mahābhārata, Āśramavāsika Parva*).
- **Pāṇḍu in heaven** — the epic's frame assigns Pāṇḍu after death to the **abode of Indra** among the meritorious kings; at the epic's end the Svargārohaṇa vision gathers the Kuru dead into their divine originals (*Mahābhārata, Svargārohaṇa Parva*).
  - *Variant (Purāṇic afterframe):* devotional summaries sometimes state the Pāṇḍavas' fathers reabsorbed into the gods who begot them, folding Pāṇḍu himself into the frame loosely; the epic keeps Pāṇḍu's own station as a virtuous king in svarga, distinct from the five sons' divine paternities. [disputed]

## Why Pāṇḍu matters

- **The engine of the succession war** — every axis of the Kurukṣetra conflict runs through Pāṇḍu: crowned past a blind elder brother (the Kaurava grievance), dead before his sons could inherit (the regency question), father in name only (the legitimacy taunts flung at the Pāṇḍavas) (*Mahābhārata, Ādi & Sabhā Parvas*).
- **Niyoga twice over** — Pāṇḍu is himself niyoga-born and secures his own sons by a still higher form of it (divine invocation); the epic uses his house to display the full range of lawful fatherhood beyond the body (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **The hunter hunted by dharma** — the Kindama episode is a set-piece on the limits of royal privilege: the hunt is lawful, cruelty within it is not, and the punishment fits the offence with exact irony (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). [scholarly]

## Roster note

- The sequencing of Pāṇḍu's digvijaya relative to his two marriages, the naming of Kindama, and the framing of Kuntī's refusal of a fourth invocation vary in emphasis across recensions and retellings; the BORI Critical Edition is the baseline here, with devotional and popular layers cataloged as variants, not corrections. [scholarly]
