# Virata, Uttara and Uttaraa

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **King Virāṭa of Matsya** and his two famous children — **Prince Uttara** (the boastful boy of the cattle-raid) and **Princess Uttarā** (Abhimanyu's wife, mother of Parīkṣit). The family frames the [Virāṭa Parva](../mahabharata/04-virata-parva.md), the Pāṇḍavas' thirteenth incognito year; through Uttarā the Kuru line survives into the Kali Yuga. ## Virāṭa and the kingdom of Matsya
- **King of Matsya** - **Virāṭa** (Virata) is the aged, prosperous, just king of the **Matsya** ("Fish") janapada — placed in the region of modern eastern Rajasthan / Mewat, with a capital usually called **Upaplavya / Virāṭanagara** (modern Bairat is often identified with it) (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva*). The whole fourth parva of the epic is named for him.
- **Why the Pāṇḍavas chose his realm** - Bhīṣma later reasons that the Pāṇḍavas must be wherever the land is most prosperous and dharmic; the brothers had settled on Matsya for exactly that reason — a wealthy, cattle-rich, virtuous court in which to vanish for the thirteenth year (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva* 1, 25–30). See [Virāṭa Parva](../mahabharata/04-virata-parva.md).
- **His court and household** - queen **Sudeṣṇā**; her brother **Kīcaka**, the over-mighty commander-in-chief; sons including the prince **Uttara**; daughter **Uttarā**. Kīcaka is effectively the power behind the throne until Bhīma kills him (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva*).
- **The five hidden servants** - Virāṭa unknowingly hosts all five Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī in disguise: Yudhiṣṭhira as the dicing-courtier **Kaṅka**, Bhīma as the cook-wrestler **Vallabha**, Arjuna as the eunuch dance-master **Bṛhannalā**, Nakula as horse-keeper **Granthika**, Sahadeva as cowherd **Tantipāla**, and Draupadī as the maid **Sairandhrī** (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva* 1–5).
- **The double cattle-raid (Go-grahaṇa)** - with the exile nearly expired, Virāṭa's herds are targeted in a coordinated plan: **Suśarmā of Trigarta** strikes from the south and *captures Virāṭa himself*, while the **Kauravas** raid from the north (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva* 30–32).
  - **Virāṭa rescued by Bhīma** - the four Pāṇḍava servants ride out with the king against Trigarta; **Bhīma routs the Trigartas, frees the captured Virāṭa, and takes Suśarmā prisoner**, whom Yudhiṣṭhira mercifully releases (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva* 32–33).
- **The dice-blow blunder** - hearing his own son credited with the northern victory, Virāṭa rejoices; when Kaṅka (Yudhiṣṭhira) corrects him — it was Bṛhannalā's doing — the king in anger **hurls a die and strikes Yudhiṣṭhira's face**, drawing blood that Draupadī catches in a vessel lest it touch the earth (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva* 63).
- **The reveal and the alliance** - when the disguises fall, Virāṭa is mortified to have set the world's greatest warriors to menial work; he offers his daughter and becomes the Pāṇḍavas' first great royal ally, hosting the war-council at Upaplavya (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva* 67; *Udyoga Parva*).
- **Virāṭa at Kurukṣetra and his death** - he fights for the Pāṇḍavas as a senior ally; on the **17th day** Virāṭa, along with his surviving sons, is **slain by Droṇa** (some passages name Droṇa's onslaught generally) (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*). His death, with that of his children, leaves the Matsya royal house all but extinguished.

## Prince Uttara (Uttara Kumāra / Bhūmiñjaya)
- **The untried prince** - **Uttara**, also **Uttara Kumāra** or **Bhūmiñjaya**, is Virāṭa's young son — brave in word, untested in war (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva*).
- **The boast** - with the main army gone south against Trigarta, only Uttara is left in the city; he boasts before the harem women that he would scatter the whole Kuru host single-handed *if only he had a charioteer* (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva* 35).
- **Bṛhannalā as charioteer** - at the prompting of his sister Uttarā and of Sairandhrī (Draupadī), the "eunuch" dance-master **Bṛhannalā (Arjuna)** is offered as driver; the women laughingly send the prince out with the dance-teacher at the reins (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva* 35–36). See [Arjuna](./arjuna.md).
- **The panic and flight** - the boy sees the vast army under Bhīṣma's and Droṇa's banners, loses all nerve, and **leaps from the chariot to run away**; Bṛhannalā chases him, drags him back by the hair, and forces him into the chariot — the famous comic-terrifying reversal (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva* 36–37).
- **Frame for the Brihannala reveal** - it is Uttara's panic that forces Arjuna to drop the disguise: Bṛhannalā drives to the **śamī tree**, has the trembling prince climb up and fetch the hidden celestial arms, recites the lineage of each great weapon, strings the **Gāṇḍīva**, and blows the conch **Devadatta**. The thirteenth year is over; Uttara now serves as *Arjuna's* charioteer while the god-archer single-handedly routs the Kuru army with the **Sammohana (sleep) astra** (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva* 38–62). See [Arjuna](./arjuna.md).
- **The trophy garments** - at Arjuna's order Uttara strips the upper garments from the sleeping Kuru lords as a teasing trophy for the princesses' dolls (colours vary by recension) (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva* 61).
- **Uttara's honesty** - returning, the prince refuses his father's praise and the credit: it was the mighty Bṛhannalā, "a god or gandharva," who won — though he loyally conceals the warrior's true name until the agreed day (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva* 64). The arc from boaster to honest witness is the point of the character.
- **Death in the war** - Uttara fights for the Pāṇḍavas. On the **first day** of Kurukṣetra he is **killed by Śalya, king of Madra** (his brother **Śveta** falls to Bhīṣma the same day in many tellings) (*Mahābhārata, Bhīṣma Parva*). The Matsya princes thus die early and unremarked, contrasting with their sister's pivotal survival.
  - *Variant (the brothers Śveta and Śaṅkha):* several passages and retellings give Virāṭa additional sons — **Śveta** and **Śaṅkha** — as great warriors slain in the opening days (Śveta by Bhīṣma); rosters and which son dies to whom vary by recension. [disputed]

## Princess Uttarā — mother of the surviving line
- **The dance-pupil** - **Uttarā**, Virāṭa's daughter, is the young princess to whom Bṛhannalā teaches **song and dance** during the incognito year; she and Sairandhrī are the ones who send the disguised Arjuna out as her brother's charioteer (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva* 2, 35). She is *not* the heroine of the dance-hall — that is Draupadī/Sairandhrī — but the king's literal daughter.
- **Declined by Arjuna, given to Abhimanyu** - when Virāṭa offers Uttarā to Arjuna, he **declines her as a wife** — having been her dance-teacher for a year, he treats her as a daughter and so guards her honour against gossip — and takes her instead **as daughter-in-law**, wedding her to his son **Abhimanyu**, who arrives with Kṛṣṇa and the Yādavas; the wedding at Upaplavya seals the Matsya–Pāṇḍava–Yādava alliance for the coming war (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva* 67). See [Abhimanyu](./abhimanyu.md).
- **Widowed pregnant** - on the **13th day** of the war Abhimanyu is killed in the *cakravyūha*; Uttarā, then carrying his only child, is widowed — her lament is among the epic's most-quoted (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*; *Strī Parva*). See [Abhimanyu](./abhimanyu.md).
- **The Brahmāstra in the womb** - after the war, **Aśvatthāman**, to extinguish the Kuru line, looses the **Brahmāstra (Aiṣīka weapon)**; redirected by Vyāsa/Kṛṣṇa, its lethal force is aimed at **Uttarā's womb** and the unborn heir (*Mahābhārata, Sauptika Parva*; *Aiṣīka Parva* coda). See [Sauptika Parva](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md).
- **Kṛṣṇa's rescue and the dead-born child** - the child is struck/stillborn within the womb; **Kṛṣṇa revives him** by his power (in the *Bhāgavata*, by the truth of his own perfect dharma), and curses Aśvatthāman to deathless, festering wandering (*Mahābhārata, Sauptika Parva*; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.7–1.8). See [Sauptika Parva](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md).
  - *Variant (placement of the revival):* the *Mahābhārata* most fully narrates the revived child's birth and naming in the **Āśvamedhika Parva** (the post-war horse-sacrifice cycle), where the dead-born infant is restored and named; the *Bhāgavata* folds the rescue into the immediate post-raid scene. [scholarly]
- **Mother of Parīkṣit** - the saved child is **Parīkṣit** ("he who was tested" / born when the race was all but extinguished), sole surviving Kuru heir, who succeeds to Hastināpura after the Pāṇḍavas' final journey; through him the dynasty **continues into the Kali Yuga**, and his son **Janamejaya**'s snake-sacrifice is the very frame at which the whole *Mahābhārata* is recited (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika & Ādi Parva* frame; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.8–1.12). Uttarā is thus the hinge on which the surviving lineage of the epic turns.
- **Uttarā's later life** - widowed young, she lives on as queen-mother of Parīkṣit's line; some Purāṇic and devotional retellings dwell on her grief and her devotion to Kṛṣṇa as her child's saviour (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa*). [late text]

## Regional and devotional variants
- *Variant (Javanese wayang — "Dewi Utari"):* in Indonesian shadow-puppet tradition Uttarā appears as **Dewi Utari**, wife of **Abimanyu** and mother of **Parikesit**, a major figure in the local Bhāratayuddha cycle. [folk]
- *Variant (name confusion Uttara / Uttarā):* the brother **Uttara** (Kumāra) and the sister **Uttarā** are routinely confused in summaries; the epic keeps them distinct — the boy is the cattle-raid charioteer-prince, the girl is Abhimanyu's wife. [disputed]
- *Variant (Bairat / Virāṭanagara identification):* the Matsya capital is popularly identified with **Bairat** in Rajasthan, linking the epic court to a real archaeological site; this is a folk/regional geographic claim, not a textual one. [regional]
- *Variant (Madhva/Dvaita emphasis):* the Virāṭa Parva, and Bhīma's protection of Draupadī within Virāṭa's court, carry special devotional weight in the Madhva tradition, recited for protection. [folk]
