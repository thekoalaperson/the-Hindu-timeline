# Kṛtavarma — Yādava Warrior and Sauptika Raider

> Where this sits: a **Dvāpara-era life** straddling two camps — the Vṛṣṇi (Yādava) chief **Kṛtavarma** (Kṛtavarman), kinsman of [Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md), who fights *for the Kauravas* at [Kurukṣetra](../mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md), is one of only **three Kaurava survivors** of the war, the **third raider** of the night-massacre in the [Sauptika Parva](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md), and is finally beheaded by Sātyaki in the Yādava self-slaughter of the [Mausala Parva](../mahabharata/16-mausala-parva.md). Two clocks: the epic narrates *itihāsa* (war trad. ~3138 BCE; Kali begins 3102 BCE); academic dating of the text differs.
> Up: [Epic Characters](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md).

**Kṛtavarma** (Sanskrit *Kṛtavarman*, "one whose armour is made/whose protection is accomplished") is the Mahābhārata's rare **cross-faction Yādava**: a leader of the **Bhoja** branch of the Vṛṣṇi clan, a blood-kinsman of [Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md) and Balarāma, yet the one major Yādava commander who marches **against** the Pāṇḍavas. Where Sātyaki (Yuyudhāna) leads a Vṛṣṇi contingent on Yudhiṣṭhira's side, Kṛtavarma leads one on Duryodhana's — the clan split across the war. He outlives almost every named warrior of either army, only to die not on the field but in a drunken kinsmen's brawl at Prabhāsa years later, killed by the very Sātyaki who fought beside Kṛṣṇa. His arc is a study in the long reach of the **Sauptika sin**: the night-raid he abetted is the wound Sātyaki reopens to end him.

## I. Lineage — the Bhoja of the Vṛṣṇis

- **Son of Hṛdika** — Kṛtavarma is the son of **Hṛdika**, a Yādava chief; he is thus styled **Hārdikya** ("son of Hṛdika") in the war-books, and reckoned among the foremost of the **Bhoja-Andhaka-Vṛṣṇi** confederacy of Dvārakā (Mbh, throughout; Bhāgavata IX.24).
- **Kinsman of Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma** — As a Vṛṣṇi/Bhoja noble he belongs to the same Yādava house as [Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md); he is a chief of the **Bhojas**, one of the clan-branches whose intermarriage and rivalry shape the Dvārakā cycle (Mbh; Purāṇic vaṃśa lists).
- **A foremost mahāratha** — He is counted a great car-warrior (*mahāratha*), repeatedly listed among the senior commanders of the Kaurava host alongside Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Kṛpa and Śalya (Mbh Udyoga/Bhīṣma P.).
- **Note (which Yādavas fought where)** — The Yādava clan divides for the war: **Kṛṣṇa** drives Arjuna's chariot (non-combatant), **Balarāma** abstains and goes on pilgrimage, **Sātyaki** leads a Vṛṣṇi force for the Pāṇḍavas, and **Kṛtavarma** leads one **for the Kauravas** — the same split that will curdle into the Mausala fratricide. [scholarly]

## II. The Syamantaka intrigue — a stain before the war (Bhāgavata / Viṣṇu Purāṇa)

- **Rival suitor of Satyabhāmā** — In the Dvārakā cycle, Kṛtavarma (with **Akrūra**) had sought the hand of **Satyabhāmā**, daughter of Satrājit, and resented her marriage to Kṛṣṇa — the grudge that drives the next crime (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13; Bhāgavata 10.57). See [The Syamantaka Jewel](../syamantaka-jewel.md).
- **The conspiracy against Satrājit** — While Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma are drawn away to Hastināpura (by the false news of the Pāṇḍavas' burning in the lac-house), **Akrūra and Kṛtavarma incite Śatadhanvā** to seize the coveted **Syamantaka** jewel; Śatadhanvā murders Satrājit in his sleep and takes the gem (Bhāgavata 10.57; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13).
  - *Variant (degree of guilt):* the Purāṇas make Kṛtavarma an **instigator** rather than the killer; when Kṛṣṇa returns and hunts down Śatadhanvā, Kṛtavarma and Akrūra refuse Śatadhanvā aid, and the plot unravels — Kṛtavarma escaping direct punishment. [regional emphasis]
- *Thematic note:* the Syamantaka affair pre-figures the Sauptika — in both Kṛtavarma is **complicit in a killing of the unguarded** (Satrājit asleep; the Pāṇḍava camp asleep), a pattern Sātyaki will later cite to justify killing him. [scholarly]

## III. The Kurukṣetra war — fighting for the Kauravas (Mbh Bhīṣma → Śalya Parvas)

- **Commander of a Kaurava akṣauhiṇī** — Kṛtavarma brings a Vṛṣṇi-Bhoja force (a *nārāyaṇī* / Yādava contingent in some tellings) to Duryodhana's side and is enrolled among the principal Kaurava generals from the first day (Mbh Udyoga/Bhīṣma P.).
- **Throughout the eighteen days** — He fights across the whole war as a steady second-rank champion under each of the four commanders (Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Karṇa, Śalya), engaging the Pāṇḍava chiefs without ever being the decisive duellist (Mbh, war parvas).
- **Among the six who fell Abhimanyu** — Kṛtavarma is named among the **six warriors who jointly broke and killed the lone [Abhimanyu](./abhimanyu.md)** inside the Cakravyūha — with Droṇa, Karṇa, Kṛpa, Aśvatthāman and Bṛhadbala — one of the war's blackest collective deeds (Mbh 7.48; see [Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md)).
- **Role in Bhūriśravas's death** — In the Sātyaki–Bhūriśravas combat, Kṛtavarma is associated with the fighting around Bhūriśravas; later, in the Mausala quarrel, his taunt and Sātyaki's are both anchored in these battlefield grievances (Mbh Droṇa P.; recalled at Mausala P. 3).
- **One of the surviving four** — After Day 18, of the eleven akṣauhiṇīs Duryodhana once led, only a handful live: **Duryodhana** (mortally wounded by the lake), **Aśvatthāman**, **Kṛpa**, and **Kṛtavarma** (Mbh IX, Hrada-praveśa P.; see [Śalya Parva](../mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md)).

## IV. The night-raid — the third Sauptika raider (Mbh Sauptika Parva)

- **The three reach the dying Duryodhana** — Aśvatthāman, Kṛpa and Kṛtavarma find Duryodhana broken-thighed by the lake; Duryodhana, with his last breath, consecrates **Aśvatthāman as the final senāpati** of the Kaurava army (Mbh X, adhy. 1).
- **Rest under the banyan** — The three lie down under a great nyagrodha; Kṛpa and Kṛtavarma sleep while the sleepless Aśvatthāman watches an **owl slaughter roosting crows** and conceives the night-massacre (Mbh X, adhy. 1).
- **Kṛpa argues against it; Kṛtavarma assents** — When Aśvatthāman proposes the raid, **Kṛpa pleads against** the sin of killing sleepers; the text's principal dissent is Kṛpa's, and the two — Kṛpa and Kṛtavarma — finally **agree to accompany Aśvatthāman**, taking station at the camp gates while he enters (Mbh X, adhy. 1–2).
  - *Variant (Kṛtavarma's reluctance):* unlike Kṛpa, Kṛtavarma is given little independent protest in the critical text — he is the silent third, complicit by accompaniment; some retellings extend Kṛpa's moral hesitation to him as well. [folk]
- **The gate-guards who let none escape** — While Aśvatthāman (in many recensions filled with **Śiva/Rudra's** destructive energy) goes through the sleeping Pāñcāla–Pāṇḍava camp killing **Dhṛṣṭadyumna, the five Upapāṇḍavas (Draupadeyas), Śikhaṇḍin** and the host, **Kṛpa and Kṛtavarma hold the three gates** and **set the camp afire in three places**, cutting down any who flee the blades back into the flames (Mbh X, adhy. 8; see [Sauptika Parva](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md)).
- **The departure at dawn** — Their work done, the three ride back to report the destruction to the dying Duryodhana, who dies content believing the Pāṇḍava line extinguished (Mbh X, adhy. 9).
- **Spared the Brahmaśiras reckoning** — When Bhīma, Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa pursue Aśvatthāman and the curse of Aśvatthāman/Vyāsa falls on Droṇa's son alone, **Kṛpa and Kṛtavarma are not pursued** — they slip back to their own lands, Kṛtavarma returning to Dvārakā among the Yādavas (Mbh X, adhy. 13–16).
  - *Note (the gate-keeping vs. the killing):* the epic distinguishes Aśvatthāman, who does the slaughter, from Kṛpa and Kṛtavarma, who only **guard and burn** — yet Sātyaki later treats Kṛtavarma's gate-role as fully damning, "a slayer of sleeping men." (Mausala P. 3)

## V. Death at Prabhāsa — the Mausala fratricide (Mbh Mausala Parva)

- **The cursed iron club** — Thirty-six years after the war, the Yādavas, doomed by the **curse of the sages** (and Gāndhārī's curse on Kṛṣṇa's clan), gather at **Prabhāsa** and, maddened by wine, fall to quarrelling (Mbh XVI; Bhāgavata XI.30). See [Mausala Parva](../mahabharata/16-mausala-parva.md).
- **Sātyaki's accusation** — Inflamed by drink, **Sātyaki (Yuyudhāna)** publicly taunts **Kṛtavarma** for the **night-massacre of the sleeping camp** and for his hand in **Bhūriśravas's death**, declaring that no warrior who slew sleeping men deserves to live (Mausala P. 3).
- **The counter-taunt** — Kṛtavarma retorts by mocking **Sātyaki** for cutting down the **unarmed, fasting Bhūriśravas** (who had withdrawn into yoga on the field); **Pradyumna**, Kṛṣṇa's son, backs Sātyaki, and the quarrel turns lethal (Mausala P. 3).
- **Beheaded by Sātyaki** — Sātyaki springs up and **cuts off Kṛtavarma's head**; the **Bhojas and Andhakas** then turn on Sātyaki en masse, and the brawl explodes into the general self-slaughter of the Yādava clan (Mausala P. 3; Bhāgavata XI.30).
- **The clan consumes itself** — In the melee the reeds-turned-clubs of the curse become weapons, and **Balarāma, Sātyaki, Pradyumna, Aniruddha and almost the whole Yādava race** kill one another, ending the Vṛṣṇi line (Mbh XVI; see [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](../krishna-lila.md)).
  - *Variant (poetic justice):* the tradition reads Kṛtavarma's death as **karmic closure** — the Sauptika gate-keeper, who once let none escape the burning camp, is himself given no escape; killed by a fellow Yādava over the very crime, his end mirrors his sin. [scholarly]

## VI. Family & names

- **Father:** **Hṛdika** (whence the patronymic **Hārdikya**). **Clan:** Bhoja branch of the Vṛṣṇi/Yādava confederacy of Dvārakā. **Kinsmen:** [Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md), Balarāma, and the wider Yādava house (Mbh; Bhāgavata IX.24).
- **Son:** Purāṇic genealogies give Kṛtavarma a son (named in some lists), continuing the Hārdikya line in the post-Mausala Yādava remnant [late text].
- **Epithets** — **Hārdikya** (son of Hṛdika); **Bhojādhipa / Bhoja-rāja** (Bhoja chief) in the war-rosters (Mbh).

## VII. Themes & significance

- **The Yādava on the wrong side** — Kṛtavarma is the structural counterweight to Sātyaki: the same clan, the same Kṛṣṇa-kinship, opposite camps. The epic uses the pair to show that **blood did not dictate allegiance** in the great war — and that the division would outlast it, into the clan's own destruction.
- **Complicity vs. commission** — Across both the Syamantaka plot and the Sauptika raid, Kṛtavarma is the **abettor**, not the prime hand — yet the epic refuses to let abettance off the hook: Sātyaki's verdict treats the gate-keeper as guilty as the killer.
- **Survival deferred, not escaped** — One of only three Kaurava warriors to live through Kurukṣetra, Kṛtavarma's survival is provisional; the Sauptika sin he shared follows him thirty-six years to Prabhāsa and there collects its due. [scholarly]
- **Threshold note (two clocks)** — Scripturally the war sits at the **Dvāpara–Kali junction** (~3138 BCE; Kali from 3102 BCE) within the [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md), and the Mausala destruction marks Kṛṣṇa's departure and the Kali age's full onset. Academically, the Vṛṣṇi confederacy and its Bhoja-Andhaka branches reflect a real west-Indian clan-polity whose internal feuds the Mausala episode mythologizes [scholarly].
