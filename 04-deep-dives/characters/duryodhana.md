# Duryodhana — the Eldest Kaurava

> Where this sits: a **Dvāpara-era life** at the very junction of [Dvāpara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and the [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) — the war whose end roughly coincides with **Kali Yuga's onset (3102 BCE** in the scriptural clock; academic dating differs sharply — two clocks). Duryodhana is the prime mover of the [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/README.md) conflict.
> Up: [Epic Characters](README.md) · Parent epic: [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/README.md) · His death-book: [09 — Śalya Parva](../mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md) · His heaven: [18 — Svargārohaṇa Parva](../mahabharata/18-svargarohana-parva.md). Back to the [master index](../../README.md).

**Duryodhana** (Sanskrit *Duryodhana*, "hard to fight"; the epic also calls him **Suyodhana**, "good fighter", some two hundred times — see §XII) is the eldest of the **hundred [Kauravas](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md)**, sons of the blind king **[Dhṛtarāṣṭra](dhritarashtra.md)** and **[Gāndhārī](gandhari.md)** of the Kuru line. He is the great human antagonist of the [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/README.md): the cousin and lifelong enemy of the **[Pāṇḍavas](../mahabharata/README.md)**, the patron and friend of **[Karṇa](../karna.md)**, the player for whom his uncle **[Śakuni](shakuni.md)** throws the dice, and — in the epic's own theology — a **portion of Kali** (the personified spirit of the Kali age and of strife, *not* the goddess Kālī). The text is unusually even-handed with him: a generous lord, a skilled mace-fighter, and a man who, having "offered his body in battle," is shown seated in **heaven** before the righteous Yudhiṣṭhira arrives.

> **Citation key.** "CE" = the BORI Critical Edition, read in the GRETIL e-text (`book.chapter.verse`). "Starred" (`*nnnn`) and "App. I" passages are lines the CE editors found in only part of the manuscripts and printed in the apparatus, not in the constituted text; most of them appear in the **vulgate** that Ganguli translated. The "~" numbers kept in the section headings below are approximate, mostly Ganguli/vulgate-style; the exact CE loci are given in the bullets.

## I. Cosmic identity & previous birth (Mbh Ādi Parva, Aṃśāvataraṇa)

- **An aṃśa of Kali (the asura of strife)** - In the Aṃśāvataraṇa list Vaiśaṃpāyana says King Duryodhana was born on earth "from a portion of Kali" (*kaler aṃśāt*), calling him the *kalipūruṣa*, hated by the whole world, "who caused the whole earth to be slaughtered"; his brothers, the hundred headed by Duḥśāsana, were all **Paulastyas** (rākṣasas of Pulastya's line) (CE 1.61.80–83). In the same list his uncle Śakuni is an aṃśa of **Dvāpara** (CE 1.61.72). This frames the war as a clash of incarnate cosmic forces. [scriptural]
  - *Purāṇic echo:* the Bhāgavata calls him "Duryodhana the sinner, **Kali**, the disease of the Kuru line" (*kaliṃ kuru-kulāmayam*), the one guest who could not bear the Pāṇḍavas' prosperity at the Rājasūya (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.74.53).
- **A body made by Śiva and the Goddess (Mbh Vana Parva)** - When he is fasting to death after the gandharva episode (§VI), the **Dānavas** of Pātāla tell him that he was obtained long ago from **Maheśvara (Śiva)** by their own austerities; the **upper part of his body was built of masses of adamant (*vajra*)**, unpierceable by weapons, and the **lower part was made "of flowers" by the Goddess (*devyā*)**, beautiful to women — "so you are divine, not human" (CE 3.240.5–8). This is constituted CE text, not a late addition. [scriptural]
  - *Variant [folk]:* a widely retold popular story makes the weakness come from **Gāndhārī**: she unbinds her eyes once and her gaze turns her son's body to adamant, except the loins or thighs, which he had covered (in many tellings at Kṛṣṇa's prompting). This is not in the CE (no such passage in the Śalya or Strī Parvas), and it should not be merged with the Dānavas' account above. [folk] [late text]
  - *Note:* the "Vaikuṇṭha gatekeeper" frame proper to **[Jaya & Vijaya](../jaya-vijaya.md)** belongs to Śiśupāla/Rāvaṇa/Hiraṇyakaśipu, *not* canonically to Duryodhana; some retellings loosely borrow the cursed-doorkeeper motif for him [folk] [disputed].

## II. The flesh-ball birth & ill omens (Mbh Ādi Parva ~107–108)

- **Gāndhārī's two-year pregnancy** - **[Vyāsa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/README.md)**, pleased by her hospitality, grants **Gāndhārī** a hundred sons; she conceives but carries the embryo **two years** (*saṃvatsaradvayam*) without delivery. Hearing that **Kuntī** has borne a son "bright as the morning sun," she secretly, "with great effort," makes her own womb miscarry (*sodaraṃ pātayām āsa*) (CE 1.107.7–11). Later translations and retellings describe her striking her belly.
- **The mass of flesh (*māṃsapeśī*)** - What comes out is a lump of flesh "hard as an iron ball." Vyāsa stops her from throwing it away, orders **a hundred pots filled with ghee** (*ghṛtapūrṇaṃ kuṇḍaśatam*) and has the lump sprinkled with cold water; it divides into **a hundred and one** embryos "the size of a thumb-joint," which are placed in the pots under guard (CE 1.107.12–21).
  - *Variant:* that the **101st piece** became the daughter **Duḥśalā** because Gāndhārī privately wished for a girl, and that Vyāsa fetched an extra ghee-pot for her, is told only in **CE App. I No. 63**. The constituted text simply says a daughter was born beyond the hundred and names her Duḥśalā (CE 1.107.34, 1.107.37).
  - *Variant on timing:* Vyāsa tells Gāndhārī to open the pots "after the same length of time again" (*kālenaitāvatā punaḥ*, CE 1.107.22), which readers take as another two years; the constituted text then says the hundred sons and the daughter were born "within a month" (*māsamātreṇa*, CE 1.107.34). A starred line adds that they came out a day apart (`*1139`); Ganguli's vulgate translation does not have it, and gives Vyāsa's interval as "full two years."
- **Duryodhana emerges first** - "In this order King Duryodhana was born first of them, but by date of birth **King Yudhiṣṭhira was the eldest**" (CE 1.107.24) — the seniority question that underlies the succession quarrel. In the same chapter Dhṛtarāṣṭra himself concedes that Yudhiṣṭhira, "the eldest of our line," has earned the kingdom, and asks only whether Duryodhana will rule after him (CE 1.107.26–27). Yuyutsu, his half-brother by a vaiśya woman, is born in the same year (CE 1.107.35–36; see [Yuyutsu](yuyutsu.md)).
  - *Variant:* that Duryodhana and **[Bhīma](bhima.md)** were born on the same day is a starred line (`*1142`), not constituted text.
- **The omens at his birth** - In the constituted text, as Dhṛtarāṣṭra finishes asking his question, **flesh-eating creatures and jackals howl** ominously in every direction (CE 1.107.28). The fuller list — the newborn braying like an ass, asses, vultures, jackals and crows answering, winds and burning horizons, a rain of blood — belongs to starred passages (`*1142`, `*1143`).
- **The unheeded warning** - **[Vidura](vidura.md)** and the Brāhmaṇas tell Dhṛtarāṣṭra that this son will clearly be the **destroyer of the family**: peace lies in abandoning him; "let you have a hundred less one"; "one should abandon one for the sake of the family, a family for the village, a village for the country, and the earth for the self." Out of love for his son, Dhṛtarāṣṭra does not do it — the epic's first fatal indulgence (CE 1.107.29–33).

## III. Childhood rivalry & early plots (Mbh Ādi Parva)

- **The plots against Bhīma at Pramāṇakoṭi** - Resenting **[Bhīma](bhima.md)**, who bullied the Kaurava boys, Duryodhana has pleasure-tents built for water-sports at **Pramāṇakoṭi** on the Gaṅgā. When Bhīma, exhausted, falls asleep there, Duryodhana **binds him with creepers and drops him into deep water**; Bhīma wakes, breaks his bonds and climbs out. Snakes are then set on him in his sleep, but their fangs cannot pierce his skin. Duryodhana next puts fresh **kālakūṭa** poison into his food; Yuyutsu warns the Pāṇḍavas, and Bhīma digests it without harm. Duryodhana, Karṇa and Śakuni try many such means (CE 1.119.24–43). Bhīma recites the same list — Pramāṇakoṭi, kālakūṭa, the snakes, the lac house — as he drinks Duḥśāsana's blood (CE 8.61.12).
  - *Variant [vulgate]:* the familiar version — poison first fed to him by Duryodhana's own hand, Bhīma sinking unconscious to the **Nāga realm**, his kinsman **Āryaka** and the king **Vāsuki** welcoming him, and Bhīma draining **eight bowls of elixir, each holding "the strength of a thousand nāgas"** — is in **CE App. I Nos. 71–72**, not in the constituted text. Ganguli's translation follows this vulgate.
- **Tutelage under Droṇa** - Raised alongside the Pāṇḍavas, Duryodhana and Bhīma become **[Droṇa's](drona.md)** two pupils pre-eminent in the **mace** (CE 1.123.40). At the graduation display they fight a mace bout so heated that the crowd splits into partisans and Droṇa has Aśvatthāman stop it (CE 1.124.30–1.125.4).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata):* Duryodhana **learned the mace from Balarāma** while Balarāma stayed some years at **Mithilā** during the Syamantaka affair, after Śatadhanvan's death (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.57.24–26). In the epic, Balarāma too calls both men his pupils at the final duel (CE 9.33.5, 9.54.23).
- **Jealousy hardens at the graduation tournament** - When the unknown **[Karṇa](../karna.md)** challenges Arjuna and is mocked for his birth, Duryodhana on the spot has him **consecrated king in Aṅga** (CE 1.126.35–36); Karṇa asks only for his **friendship** (*atyantaṃ sakhyam*), sealing the bond that defines both their lives (CE 1.126.37–38). Duryodhana defends Karṇa's worth against the taunts about his birth (CE 1.127).

## IV. The lacquer house & the kingdom split (Mbh Ādi/Sabhā Parva)

- **The Lākṣāgṛha (house of lac)** - After persuading Dhṛtarāṣṭra to send the Pāṇḍavas away (CE 1.129–130), Duryodhana orders his minister **Purocana** to build at **Vāraṇāvata** a house of hemp, resin, ghee, oil and **lac** (CE 1.132.9–11), to **burn the Pāṇḍavas and Kuntī alive**. Warned by **Vidura's** coded message and given a tunnel dug by Vidura's miner, they set the fire themselves and escape; a **Niṣāda woman and her five sons**, asleep drunk inside, are taken for their corpses (CE 1.133–136; Niṣādī at 1.136.7–8).
- **Indraprastha and renewed envy** - After the Pāṇḍavas resurface (post-Draupadī's svayaṃvara), Dhṛtarāṣṭra grants them **Khāṇḍavaprastha**, which they raise into **Indraprastha**. After Yudhiṣṭhira's **Rājasūya**, Duryodhana stays on with Śakuni to view **Maya's hall** (CE 2.43.1): he lifts his robe at a crystal floor he takes for water, then falls fully clothed into a pool he takes for floor; **the servants laugh**, and so do **Bhīma, Arjuna and the twins** (CE 2.43.3–9). Telling his father of it later, Duryodhana says Bhīma laughed, that **Kṛṣṇa laughed with Arjuna**, and that **Draupadī with the women** "pained my mind" (CE 2.46.27–30). The humiliation feeds his request for the dice game, which Śakuni proposes (CE 2.44.18–19).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata):* Bhīma laughs, and so do the women and the kings, with Kṛṣṇa's approval, though Yudhiṣṭhira tries to stop them (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.75.37–38).
  - *Variant [folk]:* the famous line **"andhasya putra andhaḥ"** ("the blind man's son is blind") attributed to Draupadī during this fall is **not in the Critical Edition** (it does not occur in the CE Sabhā Parva, apparatus included) — a popular and televised addition. [late text]

## V. The dice-plot (Dyūta) & Draupadī's outrage (Mbh Sabhā Parva)

- **Śakuni's dice** - Unable to defeat the Pāṇḍavas openly, Duryodhana takes up **[Śakuni's](shakuni.md)** plan: Śakuni, a master dicer, will play on his behalf, and Dhṛtarāṣṭra is persuaded to invite Yudhiṣṭhira to Hastināpura (CE 2.44–2.53).
- **The total loss** - Yudhiṣṭhira loses wealth, kingdom, his brothers, himself, and finally **[Draupadī](draupadi.md)**, who is in her period and wearing a single garment (*ekavastrā … rajasvalā*, CE 2.60.15) when she is summoned (CE 2.58–2.60).
- **The dragging and the disrobing (vastraharaṇa)** - Duryodhana first sends the usher (*prātikāmin*); when she sends back her question, he orders **[Duḥśāsana](dushasana.md)** to fetch her, and Duḥśāsana **drags her by the hair** into the assembly (CE 2.60.1–22). After Vikarṇa protests, it is **Karṇa** who tells Duḥśāsana to strip the Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī of their clothes (CE 2.61.26–38). As Duḥśāsana pulls at her garment, **another garment of the same kind appears, again and again** (CE 2.61.40–41). Later Duryodhana, smiling at Karṇa and taunting Bhīma, **shows Draupadī his left thigh** (*savyam ūrum adarśayat*, CE 2.63.10–12) — the sexual insult that **Bhīma vows to avenge** by breaking that thigh with his mace (CE 2.63.14).
  - *Variant [vulgate]:* Draupadī's **prayer to Kṛṣṇa** ("Govinda, dweller in Dvārakā…") and Kṛṣṇa's coming to her aid are **starred passages** (`*0542`, `*0543`). The CE keeps the miracle of the endless cloth but does not say in the constituted text who causes it; later, in the Udyoga Parva, Kṛṣṇa recalls as a debt that Draupadī "cried out 'Govinda!'" to him when he was far away (CE 5.58.21), but the explicit Kṛṣṇa-miracle belongs to the vulgate and to devotional retellings.
  - *Note:* that Duryodhana invited her to "sit on" his thigh is how later tellings spell out the gesture; the CE says only that he showed it to her.
- **The boons and the second game** - A **jackal howls in Dhṛtarāṣṭra's agnihotra-house** and asses answer; **Gāndhārī and Vidura** report the omen, and the frightened king rebukes Duryodhana and offers Draupadī boons. She asks for Yudhiṣṭhira's freedom, then for the other four with their chariots and bows, and declines a third (CE 2.63.22–35). Duryodhana then persuades his father to allow a **second game**, whose stake is **twelve years in the forest and a thirteenth year unrecognised among people, with another twelve years if discovered** (CE 2.67.9–12). The Pāṇḍavas lose and go.

## VI. Exile-period episodes (Mbh Vana / Virāṭa Parva)

- **Maitreya's curse** - When the sage **Maitreya** urges peace with the Pāṇḍavas, Duryodhana, smiling, **slaps his own thigh**, shaped "like an elephant's trunk," and scratches the ground with his foot; Maitreya curses him that in the coming war **Bhīma will break his thigh with blows of the mace**, the curse to lapse only if he makes peace (CE 3.11.28–36). Kṛṣṇa cites this curse when he defends Bhīma's blow (CE 9.59.15).
- **The Ghoṣa-yātrā (cattle-expedition) & the gandharva capture** - On Karṇa's and Śakuni's advice Duryodhana goes to the forest on the pretext of inspecting the royal herds, to flaunt his prosperity before the exiles (CE 3.226–229). He clashes with the **gandharva Citrasena**, is **taken prisoner** after Karṇa is routed, and is **rescued by the Pāṇḍavas** — Bhīma, Arjuna and the twins — at Yudhiṣṭhira's order (CE 3.230–235). Shamed, he resolves to **fast unto death (prāyopaveśa)**; Karṇa, Śakuni and Duḥśāsana fail to move him (CE 3.236–239). The **Dānavas** then send a sorceress (*kṛtyā*) who carries him to **Rasātala**, where they reveal his made body (§I), promise that demons will possess Bhīṣma, Droṇa and Kṛpa, and that the soul of the slain **Naraka** has entered Karṇa (CE 3.239.18–3.240.19). Returned to his place, he takes it for a dream, but resolves to beat the Pāṇḍavas, and when Karṇa vows to kill Arjuna he rises and abandons the fast (CE 3.240.27–40).
- **The Vaiṣṇava-yajña** - His priests tell him the **Rājasūya** is closed to him while **Yudhiṣṭhira lives and while his own father Dhṛtarāṣṭra lives**; instead they offer the **Vaiṣṇava** sacrifice, "equal to the Rājasūya," which "no one but the ancient Viṣṇu has performed," its ground ploughed with a golden plough made from tributary kings' gold (CE 3.241.25–33).
- **Durvāsā's test** - *Variant [vulgate]:* Duryodhana hosts the irascible sage **Durvāsā** and asks him, as a boon, to visit the forest-Pāṇḍavas with his ten thousand disciples after Draupadī has eaten, hoping she cannot feed them; **Kṛṣṇa** eats a last particle of food from her vessel and the sages feel full. This episode is **CE App. I No. 25** (inserted after CE 3.247.47), not constituted text.
- **The Virāṭa cattle-raid (Go-grahaṇa)** - In the 13th (incognito) year, Duryodhana leads the Kaurava raid on **Matsya's** cattle (CE 4.24–62). **Arjuna (as Bṛhannalā)**, with Prince Uttara as charioteer, routs the host; he stupefies the Kauravas with the **saṃmohana** weapon and sends Uttara to take **Droṇa's and Kṛpa's white garments, Karṇa's yellow one, and the blue garments of Aśvatthāman and the king** (Duryodhana) (CE 4.61.8–15), which are later given to Uttarā (CE 4.64.34).

## VII. The peace embassy & refusal of five villages (Mbh Udyoga Parva)

- **The choice of armies** - Duryodhana and Arjuna come to Dvārakā on the same day; Duryodhana, entering first, sits at the sleeping **Kṛṣṇa's head**, Arjuna stands at his feet. Kṛṣṇa, waking, sees Arjuna first and gives him first choice: Kṛṣṇa himself, **unarmed and not fighting**, or an *arbuda* of his cowherd warriors, the **Nārāyaṇas**. Arjuna chooses Kṛṣṇa; Duryodhana gladly takes the army (CE 5.7.5–21). **[Balarāma](balarama.md)** tells him he will help neither side (CE 5.7.21–28), and **Kṛtavarmā** gives him an akṣauhiṇī (CE 5.7.29).
- **Śalya won by hospitality** - Duryodhana has rest-houses built along the road of the Madra king **[Śalya](shalya.md)**, who is marching to join the Pāṇḍavas; delighted, Śalya offers a boon to whoever built them, and Duryodhana steps out and asks him to lead his army (CE 5.8.6–12).
- **Kṛṣṇa's peace mission and the five villages** - Yudhiṣṭhira, through Sañjaya and again through Kṛṣṇa, reduces his claim to **five villages**: **Kuśasthala, Vṛkasthala, Āsandī, Vāraṇāvata, and "any one more" as a fifth** — one for each brother (CE 5.31.19–20; 5.70.14–16; repeated 5.80.7).
  - *Variant [vulgate]:* Ganguli's text reads **Kuśasthala, Vṛkasthala, Mākandī, Vāraṇāvata** and one more in the Sañjaya message, and **Aviṣṭhala, Vṛkasthala, Mākandī, Vāraṇāvata** and one more when the list is repeated to Kṛṣṇa; the names Mākandī and Aviṣṭhala do not occur in the CE Udyoga Parva.
  - *Variant [folk / modern identification]:* the list **Indraprastha, Svarṇaprastha (Sonipat), Pāṇiprastha (Panipat), Vyāghraprastha (Baghpat), Tilaprastha (Tilpat)** is a modern popular identification, not found in the epic. The constant across all versions is the number **five**.
- **"Not a needle-point of land"** - Duryodhana refuses to yield, without war, **as much land as the sharp point of a needle can pierce** (*yāvad dhi sūcyās tīkṣṇāyā vidhyed agreṇa*). He says it twice: first to his father in the Kuru assembly (CE 5.57.18), then to Kṛṣṇa's face (CE 5.125.26) — the single most quoted line of his intransigence.
- **The failed arrest & the Viśvarūpa** - Duryodhana, Karṇa, Śakuni and Duḥśāsana plot to **seize Kṛṣṇa** in the court (CE 5.128.2–3); Sātyaki and Vidura expose the plan, and Kṛṣṇa, laughing, shows his cosmic form before the assembly, then departs — peace is dead and war is certain (CE 5.129).
- **The reconciliation rejected** - Kṛṣṇa's appeals, and those of Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Vidura, Gāndhārī and Dhṛtarāṣṭra, all fail. Duryodhana answers that he sees in himself **no transgression, "not even the subtlest"**: the Pāṇḍavas lost the dice game they had gladly accepted, and their goods were returned to them (CE 5.125.2–9).

## VIII. The war: commander and survivor (Mbh Bhīṣma → Śalya Parvas)

- **Generalissimo politics** - Duryodhana is lord of the **eleven akṣauhiṇīs** of the Kaurava host (*ekādaśacamūbhartā*, CE 9.28.25) and appoints, in turn, **Bhīṣma → Droṇa → Karṇa → Śalya** as supreme commander (and at the last, Aśvatthāman; §IX). He repeatedly reproaches **Bhīṣma** for going easy on the Pāṇḍavas; in the night council after the eighth day's fighting, after Karṇa tells him Bhīṣma always shows them compassion, he asks Bhīṣma to stand aside and let Karṇa fight (CE 6.92.77–6.94.20).
- **Personal valor** - Though cast as villain, the text shows him a **formidable mace-fighter and archer** who fights through every major engagement. He is not shown as never retreating — at the end, his army destroyed, he flees on foot, mace in hand, to the lake (CE 9.28.24–25) — but Balarāma praises him as a fair fighter who "offered himself into the fire of his enemies" (CE 9.59.24–25), and Nārada says he won the heroes' world by offering his body in battle, having been fearless in great danger (CE 18.1.14–15).
- **Loss of his brothers** - Bhīma, who vowed in the dice hall to kill the Dhārtarāṣṭras (CE 2.68.22–28), kills **[Duḥśāsana](dushasana.md)** most gruesomely, tearing open his chest and drinking his blood in revenge for Draupadī (CE 8.61.3–11). By the last day Arjuna can say that "all Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sons have been slain by Bhīma" except the two still standing, Duryodhana and Sudarśana (CE 9.26.13); Bhīma then beheads Sudarśana (CE 9.26.47–48). Karṇa's death on the seventeenth day leaves Duryodhana nearly alone.

## IX. The mace-duel & the thigh-blow (Mbh Śalya Parva ~28–60)

- **Hiding in the lake (Hrada-praveśa)** - After Śalya's fall and his army's annihilation, Duryodhana, his horse killed, flees east on foot with his mace (CE 9.28.24–25), enters the lake **Dvaipāyana-hrada** and **solidifies its waters by illusion** (*astambhayata toyaṃ ca māyayā*, CE 9.28.52). Hunters who supply Bhīma with meat overhear him and report him; the Pāṇḍavas surround the lake and **taunt him out** (CE 9.29–9.31).
- **The single-combat offer** - Yudhiṣṭhira offers him combat **one against one, with any weapon he likes**: kill one of them and be king, or die and gain heaven (CE 9.31.24–25). Duryodhana chooses the **mace** and calls on whichever brother thinks himself his match (CE 9.31.26–30). Kṛṣṇa rebukes Yudhiṣṭhira for staking everything on a duel no Pāṇḍava but Bhīma could survive; Bhīma steps forward (CE 9.32).
- **Balarāma arrives as umpire** - **[Balarāma](balarama.md)** returns from his pilgrimage (whose story fills CE 9.34–53) to watch "the mace-fight of my two pupils" (CE 9.33.5; 9.53.31–37), and at his word the duel moves to the holy field of **Samantapañcaka / Kurukṣetra** (CE 9.54).
- **Duryodhana's superior technique** - Asked by Arjuna which is better, Kṛṣṇa says both had the same teaching; Bhīma is stronger, but **Duryodhana is the more practised**, having trained with the mace for thirteen years to kill Bhīma, and Bhīma "fighting by the rules will not win" (CE 9.57.3–17). In the fight Duryodhana's blow leaves Bhīma bleeding and close to fainting (CE 9.57.35–38).
- **Arjuna's signal & the foul blow** - Hearing Kṛṣṇa, **Arjuna slaps his own thigh** where Bhīma can see (*hastenorum atāḍayat*, CE 9.57.18); Bhīma takes the sign. As Duryodhana leaps up to dodge, Bhīma hurls his mace at his **thighs and shatters both (*ūru-bhaṅga*)** (CE 9.57.41–45); winds, dust-rain, an earthquake and a falling meteor follow (CE 9.57.46–47). Bhīma then **puts his left foot on the fallen king's head**; the Somakas disapprove, and Yudhiṣṭhira rebukes Bhīma and weeps over Duryodhana (CE 9.58.5–22).
  - *Note:* the foulness is acknowledged in the text itself. Kṛṣṇa's case rests on **Bhīma's dice-hall vow** (CE 2.63.14), **Maitreya's curse** (CE 3.11.34; §VI), the arrival of the **Kali age** (*prāptaṃ kaliyugaṃ viddhi*, CE 9.59.21), and stratagem as the gods' way against the asuras (CE 9.57.5; 9.60.57–62).
- **Balarāma's fury, Kṛṣṇa's defense** - Seeing the king's head struck, Balarāma cries out that a blow **below the navel** is forbidden by the rules of mace-fighting (*adho nābhyā na hantavyam*), raises his **plough** and rushes at Bhīma; Kṛṣṇa holds him back with both arms and pleads the vow and the curse (CE 9.59.3–16, 9.59.20–21). Balarāma, unappeased, declares that Bhīma will be known as an **unfair fighter** (*jihmayodhin*) and Duryodhana as a **fair fighter** (*ṛjuyodhin*) who goes to eternal reward, having "offered himself into the fire of his enemies," and leaves for Dvārakā (CE 9.59.22–26).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata):* Balarāma comes to the duel, tells the two that they are evenly matched — one, he thinks, has more vitality, the other more training — and should stop, and when they ignore him leaves for Dvārakā; there is no plough-raising (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.79.23–29).
- **Duryodhana's dying speech** - Felled and broken, Duryodhana reviles Kṛṣṇa as "heir of Kaṃsa's slave," saying Kṛṣṇa prompted the false "break his thighs" signal through Arjuna and brought down Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Karṇa and Bhūriśravas by trickery (CE 9.60.26–38). Kṛṣṇa answers by listing Duryodhana's crimes — the poison, the lac house, the dragging of Draupadī, the cheating at dice, Jayadratha's assault on Draupadī, and the killing of Abhimanyu (CE 9.60.39–46). Duryodhana replies that he studied, gave gifts, ruled the sea-girt earth, stood on his enemies' heads and now dies a kṣatriya's death — "who has had a better end than I?" — and will go to heaven with his friends while they live on in grief (CE 9.60.47–50). **Flowers rain on him**, gandharvas play and siddhas cry "well done"; the Pāṇḍavas are ashamed, and Kṛṣṇa admits that he used stratagems because these warriors could not be killed fairly (CE 9.60.51–61).
- **The night-massacre as his last command** - The dying Duryodhana has **Kṛpa** consecrate **[Aśvatthāman](ashvatthama.md)** commander with a pot of water (CE 9.64.36–40); that night Aśvatthāman, Kṛpa and Kṛtavarmā slaughter the sleeping Pāṇḍava camp (the **[Sauptika Parva](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md)** raid). Told of it at dawn, Duryodhana says Aśvatthāman has done for him what Bhīṣma, Karṇa and Droṇa did not, that he now feels equal to Indra, bids them farewell — "we shall meet again in heaven" — and dies (CE 10.9.47–55).

## X. Duryodhana in heaven (Mbh Svargārohaṇa Parva ~1)

- **Seen enthroned in Svarga** - When **[Yudhiṣṭhira](yudhishthira.md)** reaches heaven, he sees **Duryodhana** seated in splendour, **shining like the sun**, attended by gods and Sādhyas (CE 18.1.4–5).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's revulsion** - Yudhiṣṭhira turns away: he will not share worlds with the greedy, short-sighted man "for whose sake the whole earth" and their friends were slain, and who had Draupadī abused in the assembly; he asks to go where his brothers are (CE 18.1.6–10).
- **The kṣatriya-dharma explanation** - **Nārada** tells him, smiling, that Duryodhana has reached the **heroes' world "by offering his body in battle"** (*yuddhe hutvātmanas tanum*), by kṣatriya dharma, having been **fearless in great danger**; in heaven enmities do not continue, and Yudhiṣṭhira should forget the dice game and Draupadī's suffering (CE 18.1.11–18). Yudhiṣṭhira still demands to see his brothers and Karṇa (CE 18.1.19–26).
  - *The text's own qualification:* when the illusion of hell is lifted, Indra explains that every king must see hell, that good and bad deeds form **two heaps**, and that "**one whose deeds are mostly sinful enjoys heaven first**" and hell afterwards (CE 18.3.11–13). The epic does not apply this rule to Duryodhana by name; reading his heaven as temporary on its basis is an interpretation. In the final reckoning, "all Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sons, yātudhānas (rākṣasas), purified by weapons, went to heaven" (*śastrapūtā divaṃ gatāḥ*), while Śakuni enters Dvāpara (CE 18.5.18–19). [interpretation]

## XI. Cremation, family & afterlife in tradition

- **Cremation by his enemy's order** - After the war Yudhiṣṭhira charges **Sudharman, Dhaumya, Sañjaya, Vidura, Yuyutsu**, Indrasena and the servants to carry out the funeral rites so that no body perishes unclaimed; Duryodhana and his brothers are the first named among those burned (CE 11.26.24–31). The water-offerings follow (CE 11.27).
- **Wives and son** - The CE does not name Duryodhana's queen. In the Śānti Parva, Nārada tells how Duryodhana, with **Karṇa's** help, **carried off the daughter of King Citrāṅgada of Kaliṅga** from her svayaṃvara at Rājapura after she passed him by (CE 12.4.2–21). In the Strī Parva, Gāndhārī points out the grieving "**mother of Lakṣmaṇa**" (CE 11.17.23). His son **Lakṣmaṇa** is slain by **[Abhimanyu](abhimanyu.md)** on the thirteenth day (CE 7.45.11–17). His daughter **Lakṣmaṇā** is carried off from her svayaṃvara by Kṛṣṇa's son **Sāmba** (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.68.1).
  - *Variant [later literature]:* the name **Bhānumatī** for his queen comes from later Sanskrit drama, notably Bhaṭṭa Nārāyaṇa's *Veṇīsaṃhāra* (whose second act turns on her ill-omened dream); it does not occur as his wife's name in the CE books checked (1–12, 18). Bhāsa's *Ūrubhaṅga* gives him two queens, **Mālavī** and **Pauravī**, and a young son **Durjaya**. Popular retellings often merge these with the unnamed Kaliṅga princess. [late text] [disputed]
- **Later drama** - Bhāsa's one-act *Ūrubhaṅga* ("The Breaking of the Thighs") dramatises his last hours with marked sympathy: he faces his parents, wives and son, renounces revenge and dies a hero. In the *Veṇīsaṃhāra* he remains the proud antagonist. [late text]
- **Folk reverence** - Duryodhana is **worshipped as a deity** in a few places. In the **Tons valley of Uttarkashi district, Uttarakhand** (Jakhol, Saur and nearby villages), villagers once said the wooden temples were dedicated to him, the local deity **Samsu** is said to have originally been Duryodhana as king of the area, and the cult is now largely regarded as that of **Someshwar** (a form of Śiva). In **Kerala**, at the **Poruvazhy Peruviruthy Malanada** shrine in Kollam district, a shrine with no idol or sanctum, only a raised platform, the deity (*Mala Appooppan*) is identified with Duryodhana, Kurava-community pūjārīs serve the related local shrines, and the local legend says an elderly Kurava woman gave him toddy when he asked for water. [folk] [regional]

## XII. Character & themes (epic & scholarly)

- **The generous, brave villain** - The Mbh is deliberately **ambivalent** about him: loyal to Karṇa across caste, personally courageous, openly stating his grievances (CE 5.125.2–9), praised as a fair fighter even by Balarāma (CE 9.59.24) and honoured by flowers from the sky at his fall (CE 9.60.51) — a foil to Yudhiṣṭhira's compromised righteousness.
- **The aṃśa-of-Kali frame** - His birth as a portion of Kali (CE 1.61.80) and Kṛṣṇa's appeal to the arrival of the Kali age at his fall (CE 9.59.21) make him a **structural necessity** for the age-transition, complicating simple blame; the epic both condemns and theologically excuses him.
- **Suyodhana vs Duryodhana** - Both names are used throughout the CE, by narrators and characters on both sides: Yudhiṣṭhira (CE 1.134.21–25; 9.31.22), the Dānavas (CE 3.240.1), Kṛṣṇa (CE 5.129.2) and the Svargārohaṇa narrator (CE 18.1.6). "Duryodhana" is far more frequent, and it is the name in the list of the hundred (CE 1.108.2). The popular claim that **Suyodhana was his original birth-name** and "Duryodhana" a **victors' renaming** is not stated anywhere in the text; it is a modern reading, sometimes put forward as evidence of redactional layering. The parallel "Suśāsana" for Duḥśāsana does not occur in the CE books checked. [disputed]
- **Cross-links** - See his champion [Karṇa](../karna.md); the dice-master [Śakuni](shakuni.md); his brother [Duḥśāsana](dushasana.md); his rival [Bhīma](bhima.md); his parents [Dhṛtarāṣṭra](dhritarashtra.md) and [Gāndhārī](gandhari.md); the cosmic-rebirth motif in [Jaya & Vijaya](../jaya-vijaya.md); and the war-books [Sabhā](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md) → [Śalya](../mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md) → [Sauptika](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md) → [Svargārohaṇa](../mahabharata/18-svargarohana-parva.md).
