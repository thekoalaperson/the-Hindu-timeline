# Arjuna

> Where this sits: a **Dvāpara-era life** spanning the whole [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/README.md) — from a divine birth in Śataśṛṅga to a death on the Himālayan ascent at the cusp of the [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md). He belongs to the [Mahābhārata deep-dives](../README.md) and to the [28th Mahāyuga, Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md).
> Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md) · Parent epic: [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/README.md) · His rival: [Karṇa](../karna.md) · His teacher's reject: [Ekalavya](../ekalavya.md)

**Arjuna** (Sanskrit *Arjuna*, "bright/silver, spotless") is the third [Pāṇḍava](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md), son of the rain-king **[Indra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md)** by **Kuntī**, and the central human hero of the [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/README.md): the peerless archer who wields the celestial bow **Gāṇḍīva**, the chosen friend and brother-in-law of **[Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md)**, and the warrior to whom the [**Bhagavad Gītā**](../bhagavad-gita.md) and its sequel the [**Anugītā**](../anugita.md) are spoken. His many epithets — **Dhanañjaya, Pārtha, Phālguna, Savyasācī, Bībhatsu, Jiṣṇu, Vijaya, Kirīṭin, Guḍākeśa, Śvetavāhana, Kaunteya** — each fix a deed or a trait.

---

## I. Birth and the divine sonship of Indra (Mbh Ādi Parva ~114–123)

- **Conceived by Indra** - Pāṇḍu, cursed to die if he touches a wife, has Kuntī use the **Durvāsa boon-mantra** to summon gods; after Dharma (→ Yudhiṣṭhira) and Vāyu (→ Bhīma), she invokes **Indra**, who begets **Arjuna** — making him *Aindri*, "son of Indra" (Mbh Ādi Parva ~123).
- **Born in Śataśṛṅga** - Arjuna is born in the **Śataśṛṅga ("hundred-peaked") mountain hermitage** where Pāṇḍu lives in exile; his birth is greeted by more sages, **ādityas, rudras, gandharvas and apsaras** than any of his brothers, with a **disembodied voice (ākāśavāṇī)** prophesying his glory across the three worlds (Mbh Ādi Parva ~123).
- **The name Arjuna** - The celestial voice names him for his **bright, spotless (arjuna) deeds** and "white" purity of fame; born under the star **Uttarā Phālgunī**, he is also called **Phālguna** (Mbh Ādi Parva ~123).
- **Roster of epithets** - **Pārtha / Kaunteya** (son of Pṛthā/Kuntī) · **Dhanañjaya** (wealth-winner, for the tribute he gathers) · **Savyasācī** (ambidextrous — draws the bow with either hand) · **Bībhatsu** (one who recoils from a foul deed) · **Jiṣṇu** (ever-victorious) · **Vijaya** · **Kirīṭin** (diadem-wearer, for Indra's crown) · **Guḍākeśa** ("sleep-conqueror," master of *nidrā*) · **Śvetavāhana** (white-horsed) · **Kapidhvaja / Vānaradhvaja** (his banner bears Hanumān) (Mbh Virāṭa Parva ~44 lists the ten chief names).

---

## II. Pupil of Droṇa — the peerless archer (Mbh Ādi Parva ~123–138)

- **Droṇa's favourite** - Among the Kuru princes, **[Droṇa](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md)** marks Arjuna as his finest pupil and secretly vows to make **no archer his equal** (Mbh Ādi Parva ~123).
- **The eye of the bird** - In the famous test, only Arjuna, asked what he sees, answers **"the eye of the wooden bird — and nothing else"**; Droṇa declares his absolute one-pointedness the mark of a master (Mbh Ādi Parva ~123).
- **The crocodile and night-practice** - Arjuna frees Droṇa from a river-crocodile, earning the **brahmāstra** with a charge never to use it against a weak foe; he trains in the dark until he can shoot by sound alone — the root of his name **Guḍākeśa** (Mbh Ādi Parva ~123–124).
- **Ekalavya's thumb** - When the Niṣāda **[Ekalavya](../ekalavya.md)** surpasses Arjuna by self-teaching before a clay Droṇa, Droṇa — to keep his vow to Arjuna — demands Ekalavya's **right thumb** as *guru-dakṣiṇā*, crippling the rival [disputed: framed as Droṇa's injustice].
- **The graduation tournament** - At the Kuru display Arjuna dazzles with fire-, water- and vanishing-arrows; the duel he is challenged to by an unknown **[Karṇa](../karna.md)** opens their lifelong rivalry (Mbh Ādi Parva ~134–136).
- **The guru-fee: capturing Drupada** - As Droṇa's fee, Arjuna defeats and binds King **Drupada** of Pāñcāla — sowing the enmity that later produces **Draupadī** and **Dhṛṣṭadyumna** (Mbh Ādi Parva ~137–138).

---

## III. Gāṇḍīva and the divine arsenal

- **The bow Gāṇḍīva** - Arjuna's signature weapon is the celestial bow **Gāṇḍīva**, made by **Brahmā/Prajāpati**, passed Soma → Varuṇa, and given to Arjuna with **two inexhaustible quivers**, a **divine chariot bearing the Hanumān-banner**, and **white celestial steeds** by **[Agni](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md)** (via Varuṇa) as the price of the Khāṇḍava burning (Mbh Ādi Parva ~225). *See §IV.*
  - *Variant:* the bow's lineage is told as **Brahmā 1000 yrs → Prajāpati → Indra → Soma → Varuṇa → Arjuna**; counts of prior owners vary by recension.
- **The Devadatta conch** - With the chariot Arjuna receives (from Indra) the great war-conch **Devadatta**, whose blast terrifies enemy hosts (Mbh; sounded at the [Gītā](../bhagavad-gita.md)'s opening).
- **Returning Gāṇḍīva** - After the war and Kṛṣṇa's death, at Agni's behest Arjuna **casts Gāṇḍīva back into the waters of Varuṇa** during the final journey — its loan ended (Mbh Mahāprasthānika Parva).

---

## IV. The burning of Khāṇḍava (Mbh Ādi Parva ~214–225)

- **Agni's hunger** - **Agni**, sick from over-sacrifice, must consume the **Khāṇḍava forest** to be cured but is foiled by **Indra** (who protects his friend the Nāga **Takṣaka** dwelling there); disguised as a brāhmaṇa, Agni asks **Arjuna and [Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md)** to help (Mbh Ādi Parva ~214–216).
- **The reward of weapons** - To enable them, Agni procures **Gāṇḍīva, the quivers, chariot and steeds** for Arjuna, and the **Sudarśana discus + Kaumodakī mace** for Kṛṣṇa (Mbh Ādi Parva ~216–225).
- **Defeating Indra** - The two **ring the forest with arrows and fire**, slaughtering its creatures and **beating back Indra himself** with all the gods — the feat for which Indra later pledges Arjuna his own weapons (Mbh Ādi Parva ~225).
- **Sparing Maya** - They spare the **asura-architect Maya**, who in gratitude builds the Pāṇḍavas' wondrous illusion-hall at Indraprastha — the very hall of the dice-game (Mbh Ādi/Sabhā Parva). The Nāga **Aśvasena** (Takṣaka's son) escapes and bears a grudge that resurfaces in the [Karṇa Parva](../mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md).

---

## V. Draupadī's svayaṃvara (Mbh Ādi Parva ~176–191)

- **The bow-and-target test** - Drupada sets a test: **string a mighty bow and pierce a revolving fish-target's eye through its reflection in water below**; the assembled kings, including **[Karṇa](../karna.md)**, fail or are barred (Mbh Ādi Parva ~178–179).
- **The brāhmaṇa archer** - Arjuna, disguised as a **brāhmaṇa** (the Pāṇḍavas living incognito after the lac-house escape), strings the bow and **splits the target with five arrows**, winning **[Draupadī](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md)** (Mbh Ādi Parva ~179).
- **The shared bride** - Returning, the brothers tell Kuntī "see what we have brought"; she, unseeing, says **"share it among you"** — and to honour the word, Draupadī becomes wife to **all five Pāṇḍavas** (Mbh Ādi Parva ~182–191). With Arjuna she bears **Śrutakarman/Śrutakīrti**.
  - *Variant [scholarly]:* the BORI **Critical Edition** treats Draupadī's explicit on-stage refusal of Karṇa as *Sūtaputra* as a **later interpolation**; the oldest layer has him simply fail.
  - *Variant:* polyandry is rationalised by **Draupadī's past-life boon** (she asked Śiva five times for a husband) and by Vyāsa's revelation that the five are aṃśas of Indra(s) — explanations the epic adds to soften an awkward custom.

---

## VI. Exile, tīrtha-yātrā, and three more wives (Mbh Ādi Parva ~206–225)

- **The twelve-year vow** - For breaking the brothers' privacy-pact with Draupadī (entering Yudhiṣṭhira's chamber to fetch arms), Arjuna imposes on himself a **twelve-year pilgrimage-exile** (Mbh Ādi Parva ~206).
- **Ulūpī the Nāga** - In the Gaṅgā Arjuna is drawn under by **Ulūpī**, daughter of the Nāga king Kauravya; their union (a *gāndharva* match) gives the son **Irāvān/Aravān**, who dies heroically on the war's first day (Mbh Ādi Parva ~206; [Bhīṣma Parva](../mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md)).
- **Citrāṅgadā of Maṇipura** - Arjuna weds **Citrāṅgadā**, warrior-princess of **Maṇipura**, on terms that **their son stays to inherit her matrilineal throne** — that son is **Babhruvāhana** (Mbh Ādi Parva ~207–208). *See §X.*
- **Subhadrā of Dvārakā** - On Kṛṣṇa's counsel Arjuna **abducts Subhadrā**, Kṛṣṇa's sister, from the Raivataka festival road; **Balarāma** rages but Kṛṣṇa reconciles all, and Subhadrā bears **[Abhimanyu](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md)** — through whose son **Parīkṣit** the Kuru line survives (Mbh Ādi Parva ~218–223).
  - *Variant:* some tellings have Subhadrā herself drive the chariot in the "abduction," underscoring her consent.

---

## VII. The divine astras and the Pāśupata (Mbh Vana Parva ~38–45, ~163–172)

- **Tapas on Indrakīla** - During the *thirteen-year* exile (after the dice-game), on Kṛṣṇa's advice Arjuna performs fierce **tapas** on **Indrakīla mountain** to win celestial weapons for the coming war (Mbh Vana Parva ~38–39).
- **Śiva as the Kirāta** - **[Śiva](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md)** comes disguised as a **mountain hunter (Kirāta)**; both shoot the boar-demon **Mūka**, and a quarrel over the kill becomes a wrestling/archery duel in which Arjuna is overpowered — recognising the god, he worships him (Mbh Vana Parva ~39–40). [The episode is the seed of Bhāravi's classic poem *Kirātārjunīya*.]
- **The Pāśupata-astra** - Pleased, Śiva grants Arjuna the supreme **Pāśupata** (also *Brahmaśiras*-class weapon), discharged by mind, eye, word, or bow, **never to be used against a weak foe** lest it destroy creation (Mbh Vana Parva ~40–41).
- **The Lokapālas' gifts** - The four world-guardians then appear: **Varuṇa** gives nooses, **Yama** the *Daṇḍa/Antaka* mace, **Kubera** the *Antardhāna*, and Indra summons him to heaven (Mbh Vana Parva ~41–42).
- **Five years in Indra's heaven** - Arjuna ascends to **Amarāvatī**, learns the full divine arsenal and Indra's **Vajra**-class astras, and studies **music and dance** under the gandharva **Citrasena** — skills that later cloak him as Bṛhannalā (Mbh Vana Parva ~43–45).
- **Slaying the Nivātakavacas & Kālakeyas** - As Indra's champion Arjuna destroys the ocean-demon clans the **Nivātakavacas** and the city **Hiraṇyapura**, earning the diadem (→ **Kirīṭin**) and the conch's fame (Mbh Vana Parva ~168–172).
- **The Urvaśī curse** - The apsarā **[Urvaśī](../pururavas-urvashi.md)** desires Arjuna; he refuses, calling her **"mother"** (she having been wife to his ancestor **[Purūravas](../pururavas-urvashi.md)**); insulted, she curses him to become a **eunuch/dancer**. Indra softens it: the curse will bind **only one year, of Arjuna's choosing** (Mbh Vana Parva ~45–46). *This sets up §VIII.*

---

## VIII. Bṛhannalā — the eunuch dance-master (Mbh Virāṭa Parva ~1–69)

- **The thirteenth year incognito** - For the mandatory year of undetected hiding, Arjuna uses the Urvaśī-curse and lives at **King Virāṭa's Matsya court** as **Bṛhannalā** ("big-reed"), a **eunuch teacher of song and dance** to Princess **Uttarā** (Mbh Virāṭa Parva ~2, ~10–11).
  - *Variant [disputed]:* tellings differ on whether the disguise *requires* the curse or merely uses it; the curse-link is the standard explanation for how a peerless warrior passes as a *ṣaṇḍha*.
- **The Gograhaṇa (cattle-raid)** - When the Kauravas raid Virāṭa's cattle, Prince **Uttara** panics; Bṛhannalā becomes his **charioteer**, retrieves the Pāṇḍava arms from the *śamī* tree, and **single-handedly routs Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Karṇa, Kṛpa, Aśvatthāman and Duryodhana**, lulling them with the **Sammohana (sleep) astra** (Mbh Virāṭa Parva ~32–67).
- **Revealed and rewarded** - The feat ends the exile; Arjuna declines Uttarā for himself (he was her teacher) and weds her to his son **Abhimanyu** instead (Mbh Virāṭa Parva ~71–72).

---

## IX. The Bhagavad Gītā and the Kurukṣetra war

- **The despair (viṣāda)** - On the war's eve Arjuna, seeing kin arrayed to be killed, **drops Gāṇḍīva in grief**; Kṛṣṇa, his charioteer, answers with the [**Bhagavad Gītā**](../bhagavad-gita.md) — 18 chapters of karma-, jñāna- and bhakti-yoga ([Gītā ch.1, Arjuna-viṣāda-yoga](../bhagavad-gita/01-arjuna-vishada-yoga.md)).
- **The Viśvarūpa** - Granted **divine sight (divya-cakṣus)**, Arjuna beholds Kṛṣṇa's **cosmic Universal Form** in [Gītā ch.11](../bhagavad-gita/13-to-18-jnana-shatka.md), and takes up his bow with doubt resolved.
- **War deeds** - Across the 18-day war Arjuna fells **Bhīṣma** (via Śikhaṇḍin's screen, day 10), **Bhagadatta**, **Jayadratha** (avenging Abhimanyu, day 14, racing a sunset vow), and finally **[Karṇa](../karna.md)** with the **Añjalika** arrow when Karṇa's wheel sinks (day 17) ([Bhīṣma](../mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md)/[Droṇa](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md)/[Karṇa Parva](../mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md)).
- **Hanumān on the banner** - Arjuna's chariot-flag bears **[Hanumān](../ramayana/README.md)**, who had earlier humbled his pride over Rāma's bridge; the chariot, scorched by divine weapons, survives only because Hanumān and Kṛṣṇa hold it — exploding to ash once Kṛṣṇa dismounts after the war (Mbh).

---

## X. Babhruvāhana and the death-and-revival (Mbh Āśvamedhika Parva ~78–81)

- **The horse reaches Maṇipura** - In Yudhiṣṭhira's post-war **Aśvamedha**, Arjuna follows the sacrificial horse and reaches **Maṇipura**, where his son **Babhruvāhana** greets him peaceably; Arjuna, faulting him as unwarriorlike, **provokes a fight** (Mbh Āśvamedhika Parva ~78–79).
- **The slaying** - Goaded (in many tellings by his Nāga step-mother **Ulūpī**), Babhruvāhana looses an arrow that **pierces Arjuna's chest and kills him** (Mbh Āśvamedhika Parva ~80).
- **The Vasus' curse fulfilled** - The death enacts a hidden curse of the **eight Vasus**, who had doomed Arjuna to **fall by his own son** as punishment for slaying **Bhīṣma** (a Vasu incarnate) unfairly behind Śikhaṇḍin (Mbh; Ulūpī's explanation).
- **Revival by the Nāgamaṇi** - Ulūpī fetches the Nāga **Saṃjīvana gem (Nāgamaṇi/Mṛtasaṃjīvanī)** and lays it on Arjuna's breast; he **rises as from sleep**, reconciled with his son (Mbh Āśvamedhika Parva ~81).
  - *Variant [folk/regional]:* a popular South-Indian/Yakṣagāna cycle (*Babruvāhana*) elaborates Babhruvāhana **beheading** Arjuna, with Ulūpī and Citrāṅgadā's grief and the gem-revival as high drama — beyond Vyāsa's terser core [late text].

---

## XI. The Anugītā — the forgotten Gītā (Mbh Āśvamedhika Parva ~16–51)

- **"Teach me again"** - Back at Indraprastha, Arjuna confesses the **Gītā has slipped from his memory** and asks Kṛṣṇa to repeat it; Kṛṣṇa replies he **cannot recite it verbatim** — it was uttered in a yoga-charged state — and instead retells its essence through **ancient narratives** (Mbh Āśvamedhika Parva ~16). See the dedicated [**Anugītā**](../anugita.md).
- **Content** - The ~36-chapter discourse covers the **Brāhmaṇa-and-his-wife dialogue, the Guru-and-disciple teaching, prāṇa/indriya allegories, the cosmic Tree, the three guṇas and mokṣa** — a drier, more scholastic recap than the original Gītā [the "second Gītā," sometimes judged a [late text] addition].

---

## XII. End of life (Mbh Mausala → Svargārohaṇa Parvas)

- **Dvārakā's fall** - After the **Yādava massacre** at Prabhāsa and **Kṛṣṇa's death**, Arjuna comes to bring the surviving Yādava women and children to safety — but, his **powers and astras gone with Kṛṣṇa**, he is humiliatingly **unable to lift Gāṇḍīva** or recall his weapons, and the refugees are looted by mere **Ābhīra herdsmen** (Mbh Mausala Parva).
- **The great departure** - The five Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī, with a **dog**, renounce all and walk north toward **Mount Meru/Svarga**; on the climb Arjuna falls — his sin, says Yudhiṣṭhira, was **pride in his archery and a vow (to burn all foes in a day) he failed to keep** (Mbh Mahāprasthānika Parva).
- **Heaven** - He attains the heavenly worlds; the kingdom passes to his great-grandson's father **Parīkṣit** (son of Abhimanyu), whose son **Janamejaya** becomes the Mahābhārata's hearer ([Svargārohaṇa Parva](../mahabharata/18-svargarohana-parva.md)).

---

## XIII. Family — at a glance

- **Father:** Indra (divine); Pāṇḍu (mortal/legal). **Mother:** Kuntī.
- **Brothers:** Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma (elder); Nakula, Sahadeva (twins, by Mādrī).
- **Wives & sons:** **Draupadī** → Śrutakarman; **Ulūpī** (Nāga) → Irāvān/Aravān; **Citrāṅgadā** (Maṇipura) → Babhruvāhana; **Subhadrā** (Yādava) → Abhimanyu (father of Parīkṣit).
- **Grandson-line:** Abhimanyu → **Parīkṣit** → **Janamejaya** — the surviving Kuru dynasty.

---

## XIV. Themes

- **The ideal kṣatriya** - Skill, discipline and devotion fused; the chosen vessel for the Gītā precisely because he is the dutiful doer who must be taught *why* to act.
- **Friendship with the divine** - The **Nara-Nārāyaṇa** pairing: Arjuna is **Nara**, the eternal sage, to Kṛṣṇa's **Nārāyaṇa** — companions across yugas (Mbh; the two are aṃśas of the primeval ṛṣis).
- **Pride and its fall** - From Ekalavya's thumb to the failed final vow, the epic repeatedly checks Arjuna's pride in his bow — completed only when Gāṇḍīva returns to the waters.
