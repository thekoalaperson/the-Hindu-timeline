# Arjuna

> Where this sits: a **Dvāpara-era life** spanning the whole [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/README.md) — from a divine birth in Śataśṛṅga to a death on the Himālayan ascent at the cusp of the [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md). He belongs to the [Mahābhārata deep-dives](../README.md) and to the [28th Mahāyuga, Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md).
> Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md) · Parent epic: [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/README.md) · His rival: [Karṇa](../karna.md) · His teacher's reject: [Ekalavya](../ekalavya.md)

**Arjuna** (Sanskrit *Arjuna*, "bright/silver, spotless") is the third [Pāṇḍava](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md), son of the rain-king **[Indra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md)** by **Kuntī**, and the central human hero of the [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/README.md): the peerless archer who wields the celestial bow **Gāṇḍīva**, the chosen friend and brother-in-law of **[Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md)**, and the warrior to whom the [**Bhagavad Gītā**](../bhagavad-gita.md) and its sequel the [**Anugītā**](../anugita.md) are spoken. His many epithets — **Dhanañjaya, Pārtha, Phālguna, Savyasācī, Bībhatsu, Jiṣṇu, Vijaya, Kirīṭin, Kṛṣṇa, Guḍākeśa, Śvetavāhana, Kaunteya** — each fix a deed or a trait.

**How to read the citations.** "CE" is the BORI Critical Edition, cited book.adhyāya.verse (e.g. CE 1.123.64). "G" is Ganguli's translation, cited by book and section; Ganguli follows the older vulgate numbering, which runs ahead of the CE in the Ādi Parva by roughly 9–12 sections. The parva ranges in the section headings below are rough and mix the two systems; the exact references are in the bullets. A "starred passage" (`*nnnn`) or an "Appendix I" passage is text that the CE editors found in only part of the manuscripts and printed outside the constituted text. The page keeps its original section order, so §IV (Khāṇḍava) comes after §V–VI in the story.

## I. Birth and the divine sonship of Indra (Mbh Ādi Parva ~114–123)

- **Conceived by Indra** - Pāṇḍu, cursed to die if he unites with a wife, has Kuntī use the **boon-mantra Durvāsas gave her** to summon gods. After Dharma (→ Yudhiṣṭhira) and Vāyu (→ Bhīma), Pāṇḍu sets Kuntī a year-long vow and himself performs fierce austerity standing on one foot to win Indra over. Indra promises a son "famed in the three worlds", and Kuntī then invokes him and bears **Arjuna** (CE 1.114.20–27; G Ādi 123).
- **Born in Śataśṛṅga** - Arjuna is born in the **Śataśṛṅga ("hundred-peaked") mountain hermitage** where Pāṇḍu lives in exile. A **disembodied voice (vāg aśarīriṇī)** proclaims him equal to Kārtavīrya and to Śibi in prowess, invincible like Śakra, a Viṣṇu to Kuntī as Viṣṇu was to Aditi. It foretells his conquests, the Khāṇḍava burning and three great sacrifices (CE 1.114.28–35). Only for Arjuna's birth does the text then bring the seven ṛṣis, the gandharvas, the apsarases, the ādityas, the eleven rudras, the Vasus and the nāgas to celebrate (CE 1.114.37–62; G Ādi 123; Yudhiṣṭhira and Bhīma get only the voice, CE 1.114.5–10).
  - *Variant (vulgate/starred):* the voice's further prophecies that he will win the **Pāśupata** from Śiva and destroy the **Nivātakavacas** and **Hiraṇyapura** are starred passages (CE *1221, *1222). Ganguli prints the Pāśupata and Nivātakavaca lines (*1222) but not the Hiraṇyapura lines (*1221) (G Ādi 123). Where the CE has *śibitulyaparākramaḥ* ("equal to Śibi in prowess", 1.114.29), Ganguli reads "Siva".
- **The name Arjuna** - Arjuna himself explains his names to Prince Uttara: he is **Arjuna** because his complexion is rare on earth and his deeds are "white" (*śukla*). He is **Phālguna** because he was born by day on the slopes of Himavat, under the Pūrva and Uttarā **Phalgunī** stars (CE 4.39.14, 18; G Virāṭa 44, where Ganguli names only Uttarā Phalgunī).
  - *Variant (starred):* a starred passage in the birth chapter repeats the two Phalgunīs and adds that he was born in the month Phālguna, deriving the name from the month (CE 1.114.27 *1217).
- **Roster of epithets** - The epic's own list of **ten names** is Arjuna, Phalguna, Jiṣṇu, Kirīṭin, Śvetavāhana, Bībhatsu, Vijaya, **Kṛṣṇa**, Savyasācin and Dhanañjaya (CE 4.39.8; G Virāṭa 44). Arjuna glosses each one (CE 4.39.11–20):
  - **Dhanañjaya** — he conquered every land and stood amid the wealth he took.
  - **Vijaya** — he never returns from battle unvictorious.
  - **Śvetavāhana** — white horses draw his car.
  - **Kirīṭin** — Śakra set a sun-bright diadem on his head when he fought the Dānavas.
  - **Bībhatsu** — he never does a loathsome deed in battle.
  - **Savyasācin** — both his hands are "right hands" in drawing Gāṇḍīva.
  - **Jiṣṇu** — he is hard to approach and a subduer.
  - **Kṛṣṇa** — the "tenth name", which his father gave out of love for the dark-skinned, pure-hued child.

  Other epithets lie outside the ten:
  - **Pārtha / Kaunteya** — son of Pṛthā (Kuntī). In the Virāṭa name passage the explicit gloss "my mother is Pṛthā" comes only in CE App. I No. 37.
  - **Kapidhvaja** — the ape-bannered one (BhG 1.20 = CE 6.23.20).
  - **Guḍākeśa** — used in the Gītā (BhG 1.24, 2.9, 10.20, 11.7). The epic does not explain it. Śaṅkara glosses it two ways: "lord of sleep" (*guḍākā* = *nidrā*, hence *jita-nidra*, "one who has conquered sleep"), "or else thick-haired" (*ghana-keśa*) (Śaṅkara, *Gītābhāṣya* on BhG 10.20).

## II. Pupil of Droṇa — the peerless archer (Mbh Ādi Parva ~123–138)

- **Droṇa's favourite** - The text tells these episodes in this order:
  - Droṇa secretly orders the cook never to serve Arjuna food in the dark.
  - One night a wind blows out the lamp while Arjuna is eating. His hand still finds his mouth, and he realises that practice works without light, so he begins shooting at night.
  - Hearing his bowstring in the dark, Droṇa embraces him and promises: "I will strive so that no other archer in the world is your equal" (CE 1.123.1–6; G Ādi 134).
  - *Note:* the epic does not connect this night practice to the name Guḍākeśa. That link is a modern popular gloss.
- **The eye of the bird** - Droṇa has craftsmen set an **artificial *bhāsa* bird (a vulture or kite) on a treetop**. He asks each pupil what he sees. Yudhiṣṭhira and the rest see the tree, the teacher, their brothers and the bird, and are sent away. Arjuna sees only the bird, and then, asked again, says: **"I see the head of the bird, not its body."** On Droṇa's word he shears the head off with a razor-headed arrow (CE 1.123.46–67; G Ādi 135).
  - *Variant (later retelling):* the popular version, in which Arjuna sees only **"the eye of the bird"**, is not what the CE or Ganguli says. Both texts have the *head*.
- **The crocodile and night-practice** - While Droṇa bathes in the Gaṅgā, a crocodile seizes his shank. The other pupils freeze. Arjuna kills it with five arrows. Droṇa rewards him with the **Brahmaśiras** weapon, with the methods for loosing and recalling it. Droṇa warns that it must **never be used against human beings**, since loosed on a foe of little power it could burn up the world; it is to be used only against a superhuman enemy (CE 1.123.68–78; G Ādi 135). On the night-practice, see the first bullet.
- **Ekalavya's thumb** - The Niṣāda prince **[Ekalavya](../ekalavya.md)**, refused by Droṇa, teaches himself before a clay image of Droṇa and surpasses the Kuru pupils. Arjuna reminds Droṇa of his promise that no pupil would excel him. As fee (*vetana*), Droṇa asks for Ekalavya's **right thumb** (*aṅguṣṭho dakṣiṇaḥ*), and Ekalavya cuts it off without hesitation. "Arjuna was glad, his fever gone, and Droṇa's word held true" (CE 1.123.10–39; G Ādi 134). The narrator calls Droṇa's demand "harsh" (*dāruṇa*, 1.123.36) and praises Ekalavya's steadfastness, but passes no further judgment. Reading the episode as an injustice by Droṇa is a modern interpretation that the epic itself does not voice.
- **The graduation tournament** - At the Kuru display, Arjuna makes fire with the **āgneya** weapon and water with the **vāruṇa**, and vanishes with the **antardhāna** (CE 1.125.18–20). The unknown **[Karṇa](../karna.md)** then enters, matches his feats and challenges him to a duel, which opens their lifelong rivalry (CE 1.126; G Ādi 136–139).
- **The guru-fee: capturing Drupada** - Droṇa asks his pupils for King **Drupada** of Pāñcāla as his fee. In the constituted text the pupils capture Drupada together and the victory is credited to Pārtha. Droṇa gives back half the kingdom and keeps the northern half (CE 1.128, esp. 1.128.5, 18; G Ādi 140).
  - *Variant (CE App. I No. 78):* the fuller account in which the Kauravas attack first and are beaten off, and Arjuna then takes Drupada, is relegated to Appendix I. Ganguli prints it (G Ādi 140). The humiliation leads Drupada to the sacrifice that produces **Dhṛṣṭadyumna** and **Draupadī** (CE 1.155).

## III. Gāṇḍīva and the divine arsenal

- **The bow Gāṇḍīva** - At the Khāṇḍava forest, **[Agni](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md)** summons **Varuṇa** and asks him for "the bow and quivers that King Soma gave you, and the ape-bannered chariot". Varuṇa gives Arjuna:
  - **Gāṇḍīva** itself, "equal to a hundred thousand bows".
  - **two inexhaustible quivers**.
  - a divine chariot with a **foremost-of-apes banner**, which Prajāpati had made and Soma had ridden. Silver-white gandharva horses draw it.

  It is all the price of the Khāṇḍava burning (CE 1.216.1–20; G Ādi 227). *See §IV.*
  - *Variant / second account:* in the Virāṭa Parva, Arjuna gives the bow's full history with years of ownership: **Brahmā 1,000 years → Prajāpati 503 → Śakra 85 → Soma 500 → Varuṇa 100 → Pārtha 65** (CE 4.38.39–41; G Virāṭa 43). This lineage is in the constituted text, not a manuscript variant. The Ādi Parva says only that Brahmā made the bow and that Soma gave it to Varuṇa (CE 1.216.3, 17).
- **The Devadatta conch** - The great conch **Devadatta** has two acquisitions in the epic, and both stand in the CE:
  - (a) After the Khāṇḍava, the asura **Maya** fetches Varuṇa's conch Devadatta from Lake **Bindusaras**, together with a mace for Bhīma, and gives it to Arjuna (CE 2.3.5–18; G Sabhā 3).
  - (b) Before Arjuna's war on the Nivātakavacas, Indra ties the diadem on his head, and the gods present him with "Devadatta, born of the waters" (CE 3.165.13, 22).

  He sounds it at the Gītā's opening (BhG 1.15 = CE 6.23.15).
- **Returning Gāṇḍīva** - On the final journey the Pāṇḍavas reach the **Lauhitya** sea, with Arjuna still carrying Gāṇḍīva. **Agni**, standing before them like a mountain, says he fetched the bow from Varuṇa for Pārtha's sake and it must now go back to Varuṇa. At his brothers' urging, Arjuna throws the bow and the two quivers into the water (CE 17.1.31–40; G Mahāprasthānika 1).

## IV. The burning of Khāṇḍava (Mbh Ādi Parva ~214–225)

- **Agni's hunger** - A huge brāhmaṇa approaches **Arjuna and [Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md)** near Khāṇḍava and begs one meal. He reveals himself as **Agni**: he wants to eat the forest, but **Indra** keeps putting the fire out to protect his friend the Nāga **Takṣaka**, who lives there (CE 1.215.1–7; G Ādi 225).
  - *Variant (vulgate / CE App. I No. 118):* the backstory of Agni's sickness is the endless sacrifices of King **Śvetaki**, whose surfeit of ghee leaves Agni ill and needing the forest's fat. It is printed by Ganguli (G Ādi 225) but relegated to CE App. I No. 118 (after 1.215.11).
- **The reward of weapons** - Agni procures from Varuṇa **Gāṇḍīva, the two quivers, the chariot and horses** for Arjuna (§III). He himself gives Kṛṣṇa the **discus with a thunderbolt hub (*vajranābha cakra*)**, and Varuṇa adds the mace **Kaumodakī** (CE 1.216.21–25; G Ādi 227).
- **Defeating Indra** - The two heroes guard the burning forest from either side and kill the creatures that flee. They beat back **Indra** and the gods. Then a bodiless voice tells Indra that Takṣaka is away at Kurukṣetra and that these two are the **ancient gods Nara and Nārāyaṇa**, and Indra withdraws (CE 1.217–219, esp. 1.218.4, 1.219.13–15; G Ādi 228–230). The fire burns for six days (CE 1.225.15). Afterwards Indra offers a boon. Arjuna asks for all of Indra's weapons, and Indra fixes the time: "when Mahādeva is pleased with you" (CE 1.225.7–12; G Ādi 236, where Ganguli's text reads "Madhava"). *See §VII.*
- **Sparing Maya** - Only six beings survive the burning:
  - the **asura-architect Maya**, who runs from Takṣaka's dwelling crying to Arjuna for help and is granted safety.
  - the Nāga **Aśvasena** (Takṣaka's son), whom Indra saves by confusing Arjuna.
  - the four **Śārṅgaka** birds (CE 1.218.5–9, 1.219.35–40).

  In gratitude, Maya builds the Pāṇḍavas' wondrous hall at Indraprastha (CE 2.1–3). That hall is where Duryodhana mistakes floor for water and water for floor, the humiliation that feeds his envy (CE 2.43.1–9). The dice game itself is played at Hastināpura, in a new thousand-pillared hall that Dhṛtarāṣṭra has built for it (CE 2.51.17–21, 2.52.7, 22).
  - In the [Karṇa Parva](../mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md), Karṇa fires a serpent-mouthed arrow that carries a nāga. It strikes off Arjuna's diadem, and Kṛṣṇa identifies it as the snake whose mother died in Khāṇḍava. Arjuna then cuts it down (CE 8.66.5–24). The CE does not name this serpent. The name Aśvasena comes from the vulgate tradition.

## V. Draupadī's svayaṃvara (Mbh Ādi Parva ~176–191)

- **The bow-and-target test** - Drupada has a very stiff bow made and sets up a **golden target (*lakṣya*) behind an aerial machine (*yantra vaihāyasa*)**. The suitor must string the bow and send **five arrows through the hole in the machine** into the target (CE 1.176.10–11, 34; G Ādi 187). The assembled kings cannot even string it: they are thrown to the ground, their armlets and earrings crushed (CE 1.178.15–17; G Ādi 189).
  - *Variant (later retellings):* the familiar **revolving fish (*matsya-yantra*) whose eye is shot by looking at its reflection in water** is not in the CE or in Ganguli. Both have a target set behind a machine with a hole in it.
- **The brāhmaṇa archer** - Arjuna stands among the brāhmaṇas in disguise, the Pāṇḍavas living incognito after the lac-house escape. He circles the bow, strings it in the blink of an eye, and with five arrows **brings the target down through the hole**. Flowers fall from the sky, and **[Draupadī](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md)** comes to him with a white garland (CE 1.179.15–22; Ganguli has "a white robe and a garland of flowers", G Ādi 190).
- **The shared bride** - The brothers bring Draupadī home and announce her as "alms" (*bhikṣā*). Kuntī, not looking, says **"enjoy it all together"** (*bhuṅkteti sametya sarve*), and then sees the girl and is dismayed (CE 1.182.1–2; G Ādi 193). Draupadī becomes the wife of **all five Pāṇḍavas**.
  - *Her son by Arjuna:* **Śrutakarman** in the narrative of the five sons' births, so called because he was born when Kirīṭin returned from a famous feat (CE 1.213.72, 76). The prose genealogy instead names Arjuna's son **Śrutakīrti**, and gives Śrutakarman to Sahadeva (CE 1.90.82). Both are CE readings and are kept here unmerged.
  - *Variant [CE vs vulgate]:* in the vulgate, **Karṇa** strings the bow and is about to shoot when Draupadī cries **"I will not choose a Sūta"** (G Ādi 189). The CE moves this whole passage to a starred passage (*1827, after 1.178.17). In the constituted text Karṇa never takes the bow. He is simply listed later among the kings who "could not bend it" (CE 1.179.4).
  - *Variant (the epic's own justifications):* the polyandry is explained in two ways. Vyāsa tells the Pāṇḍavas, and later Drupada, that in a former life Draupadī asked Śiva **five times** for a husband and was granted five (to the Pāṇḍavas: CE 1.157.6–14; to Drupada: 1.189.41–47). He also tells the **story of the five Indras**, who were reborn as the five Pāṇḍavas (CE 1.189.1–33; G Ādi 199). *[interpretive]* A common modern reading takes these as explanations the epic offers for a custom unusual in its own society. That is an interpretation, not a statement in the text.

## VI. Exile, tīrtha-yātrā, and three more wives (Mbh Ādi Parva ~206–225)

- **The twelve-year vow** - On Nārada's advice, the brothers have agreed that whoever intrudes on another brother alone with Draupadī will spend twelve years in the forest. A brāhmaṇa's cattle are stolen, and the Pāṇḍavas' weapons are in the room where Yudhiṣṭhira sits with her. Arjuna goes in to fetch them, recovers the cattle, and then insists on keeping the rule, leaving for **twelve years of brahmacarya-exile** (CE 1.205, esp. 1.205.30; G Ādi 215).
- **Ulūpī the Nāga** - At **Gaṅgādvāra** (Haridvāra), the Nāga princess **Ulūpī**, daughter of **Kauravya** of Airāvata's line, draws Arjuna under the water to her father's palace. He objects that he is under a vow of celibacy. She argues that the vow concerns Draupadī only, and that saving her life is itself dharma. He stays the night with her (CE 1.206.6–34; G Ādi 216). The constituted Ādi text does not mention a son; a starred line has Irāvat begotten that night (CE *2025, after 1.206.34).
  - *The son Irāvat (Irāvān; Tamil Aravān):* in the constituted text he fights only in the Bhīṣma Parva, where his parentage is told: he is Arjuna's son by the Nāga king's daughter (CE 6.86.6–8). *Variant:* there she is said to have been given by Airāvata, **childless, after Garuḍa had killed her husband** (CE 6.86.6–7; G Bhīṣma 91). He is killed by the rākṣasa **Alambuṣa (Ārśyaśṛṅgi)** on the **eighth day** of the war, not the first (CE 6.86.70; the day closes at 6.92.79; [Bhīṣma Parva](../mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md)).
- **Citrāṅgadā of Maṇipura** - Travelling east through Aṅga, Vaṅga and Kaliṅga and then along the seashore past Mount Mahendra, Arjuna comes to King **Citravāhana** and asks for his daughter **Citrāṅgadā**. The king explains that Śiva granted his ancestor Prabhaṃkara only one child per generation. So Citrāṅgadā has been designated a ***putrikā***, a daughter whose son will be counted as her father's heir. Her son must stay to continue the line, and that is the bride-price. Arjuna agrees and lives there **three years** (CE 1.207.13–23; G Ādi 217). On a second visit their son is born, **Babhruvāhana**, the future king (CE 1.209.23–24). *See §X.*
  - *Name variant:* the CE Ādi Parva calls the city **Maṇalūra**. Ganguli has **Manipura**, and the CE Āśvamedhika Parva has **Maṇipūra** (CE 14.77.46).
  - *Missing episode — the five crocodile-apsarases:* at the southern tīrthas, where ascetics fear to bathe, Arjuna drags out a crocodile that seizes him. It turns into the apsaras **Vargā**. She and four companions had been cursed by a brāhmaṇa to be crocodiles for a hundred years until a man pulled them from the water, and Arjuna frees all five (CE 1.208–209; G Ādi 218–219).
- **Subhadrā of Dvārakā** - At **Prabhāsa** Arjuna meets Kṛṣṇa, who takes him to the **Raivataka** festival (CE 1.210–211). There Arjuna falls for **Subhadrā**, Kṛṣṇa's sister. Kṛṣṇa advises that seizing a bride is approved for a kṣatriya. With Yudhiṣṭhira's consent sent by messenger, Arjuna, armed "on the pretext of hunting", **lifts her onto his chariot** as she leaves Raivataka and drives off (CE 1.212.1–8; G Ādi 222). **Balarāma** and the Vṛṣṇis rage, but Kṛṣṇa calms them, and Arjuna is brought back and married there (CE 1.213.1–12; G Ādi 223). Subhadrā comes to Indraprastha dressed as a cowherd woman and wins Draupadī's favour (CE 1.213.17–20; G Ādi 223). She bears **[Abhimanyu](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md)**, whom Arjuna trains himself (CE 1.213.59–70). Through Abhimanyu's son **Parīkṣit** the Kuru line survives.
  - *Variant (CE App. I Nos. 114–115):* manuscript passages that the CE relegates to Appendix I have **Subhadrā take the reins herself**: no one on earth handles reins as well as she does (App. I 114, ll. 354–356; 115, l. 20). The same appendix block has Arjuna living in Dvārakā disguised as a **yati** (ascetic), to whom Subhadrā is told to bring food (App. I 114, ll. 13–74; *2053).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.86.2–9):* Arjuna hears that Balarāma means to give Subhadrā to **Duryodhana**. He goes to Dvārakā as a **tridaṇḍī ascetic** and stays through the rainy months, honoured by Balarāma, who does not recognise him. Then he carries her off from a temple festival "with the consent of her parents and of Kṛṣṇa".

## VII. The divine astras and the Pāśupata (Mbh Vana Parva ~38–45, ~163–172)

- **Tapas on Indrakīla** - During the forest exile after the dice game, it is **Vyāsa**, not Kṛṣṇa, who comes to the Pāṇḍavas. He teaches Yudhiṣṭhira the **Pratismṛti** spell for Arjuna to win celestial weapons, and Yudhiṣṭhira passes it on (CE 3.37.20–37; G Vana 36–37). At **Indrakīla**, Indra in the guise of a gaunt, matted ascetic tells Arjuna to win over Śiva first (CE 3.38.30–43; G Vana 37–38). Arjuna's austerity escalates: a meal every third night, then every sixth, then every fortnight of fallen leaves, then air alone, arms raised, standing on his toes (CE 3.39.21–23).
- **Śiva as the Kirāta** - **[Śiva](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md)** comes disguised as a **Kirāta hunter**, with Umā and her women. Both shoot the boar-shaped Daitya **Mūka** at the same moment, and the quarrel over the kill becomes a fight. Śiva swallows Arjuna's arrows and then the bow itself. Arjuna fights on with his sword and then his fists. In the wrestling that follows he crushes Arjuna until he falls senseless (CE 3.40.4–49, esp. 3.40.37–40; G Vana 39). Arjuna recognises the god and worships him. Śiva tells him: **"You were Nara in a former body, companion of Nārāyaṇa"** (CE 3.41.1). [The episode is the source of Bhāravi's classical poem *Kirātārjunīya* (c. 6th century CE).]
- **The Pāśupata-astra** - Asked for a boon, Arjuna requests "that dreadful weapon **Brahmaśiras**, the Raudra, your **Pāśupata**" (CE 3.41.7–8). Śiva grants it with these terms (CE 3.41.13–16; G Vana 40):
  - not even Indra, Yama, Kubera, Varuṇa or Vāyu knows it.
  - it must not be loosed rashly on any man, for "loosed on one of little power it would burn the whole world".
  - it can be discharged by **mind, eye, word or bow**.
- **The Lokapālas' gifts** - The world-guardians appear (CE 3.42; G Vana 41):
  - **Yama** gives his irresistible **daṇḍa** (3.42.23).
  - **Varuṇa** gives his **nooses** (*pāśa*) (3.42.27).
  - **Kubera** gives the **antardhāna** weapon (3.42.33).
  - **Indra** promises to take him to heaven.
- **Five years in Indra's heaven** - Mātali brings Arjuna to Indra's city (CE 3.43–44; G Vana 42–44). There he learns the great weapons with their withdrawals, receives from Indra's hand the **vajra** weapon and the thundering **aśani** bolts, and stays **five years** (CE 3.45.4–5). On Indra's instruction he learns **song, dance and instrumental music** from the gandharva **Citrasena**, "which do not exist in the world of men" — the skills that later cloak him as Bṛhannaḍā (CE 3.45.6–8; G Vana 44).
- **Slaying the Nivātakavacas & Kālakeyas** - Arjuna himself tells this story to Yudhiṣṭhira (CE 3.164–171; G Vana 167–173):
  - As his fee to his divine teacher, he goes to war on the Dānava **Nivātakavacas**, who live in a fortress in the belly of the ocean. Indra lends him **Mātali** and the divine chariot, binds the **diadem** on his head (→ **Kirīṭin**), and the gods give him the conch **Devadatta** (CE 3.165.10–22). He destroys the Nivātakavacas (CE 3.166–168).
  - On the way back he wrecks **Hiraṇyapura**, the flying city that Brahmā granted the **Paulomas and Kālakeyas**, and kills its demons (CE 3.170.1–63).
  - Śiva had promised that his **Raudra** weapon would come to Arjuna's aid (CE 3.163.48).
- **The Urvaśī curse** - *[Not in the CE constituted text: the whole episode is CE App. I No. 6, after 3.45.8. Ganguli prints it as G Vana 45–46.]*
  - The apsaras **[Urvaśī](../pururavas-urvashi.md)** comes to Arjuna by night. He refuses her and honours her **"as a mother"**, because she is the ancestress of the Puru line (through **[Purūravas](../pururavas-urvashi.md)**), revered like Kuntī, Mādrī and Śacī.
  - Spurned, she curses him to live **among women as a dancer, without honour, known as one who is no man (*apumān*), like a eunuch**.
  - Indra consoles him: the curse will serve him. He is to pass it in the **thirteenth year of exile, the year of hiding**, and after **one year** as a dancer he will regain his manhood. Arjuna does not choose the year; the curse is tied to the year of hiding.
  - *This sets up §VIII.*
- **Gandharva Citrasena at Dvaitavana** *(Ghoṣayātrā)* - Later in the exile, gandharvas capture Duryodhana, who has come to gloat over the Pāṇḍavas. On Yudhiṣṭhira's order Arjuna fights them until their leader reveals himself as his friend **Citrasena**, and Duryodhana is freed to his shame (CE 3.229–235, esp. 3.234).

## VIII. Bṛhannalā — the eunuch dance-master (Mbh Virāṭa Parva ~1–69)

- **The thirteenth year incognito** - Arjuna's own plan is to declare himself a ***ṣaṇḍhaka*** (one of the third sex). The great bowstring scars on his arms are hard to hide (the bangles that cover them come in starred lines and in his court costume, CE *0058, 4.10.1). He will wear earrings and a braid, take the name **Bṛhannaḍā** (Ganguli "Vrihannala"/"Brihannala"), and teach song, dance and music to the women of **King Virāṭa's** Matsya court (CE 4.2.21–27; G Virāṭa 2). At court he is given to Princess **Uttarā** as her dance teacher, after Virāṭa has satisfied himself that his "unmanliness is lasting" (CE 4.10.1–12, esp. 4.10.11; G Virāṭa 11).
  - *Variant [CE vs vulgate]:* in the CE constituted text, Arjuna's plan in Virāṭa 4.2 says nothing about **Urvaśī**. The explicit link — "I was cursed by Urvaśī", "by Urvaśī's curse I have reached eunuchhood" — appears only in starred passages (CE *0053 after 4.2.21, *0062 after 4.2.26, *0758 after 4.40.13), and the curse story itself is App. I No. 6 (§VII). The curse-link is the standard explanation in the vulgate and in popular retellings.
- **The Gograhaṇa (cattle-raid)** - While Virāṭa is away fighting the Trigartas, the Kauravas raid his northern herds. Prince **Uttara** boasts and then panics. Bṛhannaḍā becomes his **charioteer** and takes the Pāṇḍava weapons down from the ***śamī* tree** (CE 4.33–39; G Virāṭa 35–44). He tells Gāṇḍīva's history (§III) and reveals himself with the ten names (§I). Arjuna then routs **Karṇa, Kṛpa, Droṇa, Aśvatthāman, Duryodhana and Bhīṣma** (CE 4.40–62; G Virāṭa 45–67). At the climax he looses the **sammohana** (stupefying) weapon, and the Kurus fall senseless at his conch-blast. Arjuna sends Uttara to take **Droṇa's and Kṛpa's white cloths, Karṇa's yellow, and Aśvatthāman's and Duryodhana's blue** for Uttarā's dolls. He warns Uttara to avoid **Bhīṣma, who "knows how to counter my weapon"** and is not stupefied (CE 4.61.8–18; G Virāṭa 65).
- **Revealed and rewarded** - Once the Pāṇḍavas reveal themselves, Virāṭa offers Uttarā to Arjuna. Arjuna declines: he has lived a year in the inner apartments, she trusted him "as a father", and accepting her would invite suspicion. He takes her instead as **daughter-in-law**, the bride of **Abhimanyu** (CE 4.67.1–9; G Virāṭa 71–72).

## IX. The Bhagavad Gītā and the Kurukṣetra war

- **Choosing Kṛṣṇa** - Before the war, Arjuna and Duryodhana both come to Dvārakā to seek Kṛṣṇa's help. Kṛṣṇa offers one side his army of Nārāyaṇa warriors and the other side himself, unarmed and not fighting. Duryodhana arrived first, but Kṛṣṇa saw Arjuna first on waking, so he helps both. Because the younger is entitled to choose first, Arjuna chooses first. Arjuna chooses **Kṛṣṇa unarmed** (CE 5.7.13–19; G Udyoga 7).
- **The despair (viṣāda)** - On the field, Arjuna asks Kṛṣṇa, his charioteer, to halt between the armies. He sees his kin arrayed, and **"Gāṇḍīva slips from my hand"** (BhG 1.30). He sits down in the chariot, casting aside bow and arrows (BhG 1.47). Kṛṣṇa answers with the [**Bhagavad Gītā**](../bhagavad-gita.md), eighteen chapters of karma-, jñāna- and bhakti-yoga (= CE 6.23–40; G Bhīṣma 25–42; [Gītā ch.1, Arjuna-viṣāda-yoga](../bhagavad-gita/01-arjuna-vishada-yoga.md)).
- **The Viśvarūpa** - Granted **divine sight (divya-cakṣus)** (BhG 11.8), Arjuna beholds Kṛṣṇa's **cosmic Universal Form** in [Gītā ch.11](../bhagavad-gita/07-to-12-bhakti-shatka.md). By the end (BhG 18.73) he says his delusion is destroyed and he will do as Kṛṣṇa says.
- **War deeds** - Across the 18-day war, Arjuna:
  - fells **Bhīṣma** from behind **Śikhaṇḍin**, on the tenth day ([Bhīṣma Parva](../mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md)).
  - kills **Bhagadatta** after Kṛṣṇa takes the old king's **Vaiṣṇava** weapon on his own chest, where it becomes a garland. Arjuna then pierces Bhagadatta's heart (CE 7.28.17–43).
  - cuts off the sword-arm of **Bhūriśravas** while he is fighting Sātyaki, a blow from outside the duel that Bhūriśravas protests (CE 7.117.57–7.118.2).
  - beheads **Jayadratha**, fulfilling his vow to avenge **Abhimanyu** before sunset on the fourteenth day. On Kṛṣṇa's advice, he sends the head into the lap of Jayadratha's father **Vṛddhakṣatra**, whose own pronouncement splits the head of whoever drops his son's head to the ground (CE 7.121.15–36).
  - kills **[Karṇa](../karna.md)** with the **añjalika** arrow on the seventeenth day, after the earth swallows Karṇa's chariot wheel (wheel: CE 8.66.59–64; añjalika: 8.67.16–33).

  See the [Droṇa](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md) and [Karṇa Parva](../mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md) pages.
- **The Gāṇḍīva vow and the quarrel with Yudhiṣṭhira** - Arjuna has vowed to kill anyone who tells him to give Gāṇḍīva to another. On the seventeenth day Karṇa wounds Yudhiṣṭhira and drives him from the field. Arjuna comes to the camp to see him, and Yudhiṣṭhira, who had hoped to hear that Karṇa was dead, finds Karṇa still alive and taunts Arjuna to give his bow to someone better (CE 8.46.1–43, 8.48, 8.49.61). Arjuna draws his sword on his brother. Kṛṣṇa resolves the clash between the vow and the sin by having Arjuna address his elder brother as ***tvam***, the familiar "you", because insulting an elder that way counts as killing him (CE 8.49.9–11, 61–70).
- **Hanumān on the banner** - Arjuna's chariot-flag bears an ape. In the Vana Parva, **[Hanumān](../ramayana/README.md)** promises his brother Bhīma that he will sit on **Vijaya's banner** and roar to terrify their enemies (CE 3.150.15; G Vana 150). After the last battle, Kṛṣṇa makes Arjuna get down first. The divine ape vanishes from the flagstaff, and the chariot **falls to ash**. Kṛṣṇa explains that Droṇa's and Karṇa's divine weapons, among them the brahmāstra, had burned it long before, and that only his own presence had held it together (CE 9.61.7–27, Śalya Parva).
  - *Variant [folk/regional]:* in a popular modern tale, Hanumān humbles Arjuna's pride over **Rāma's bridge** at Rāmeśvaram, which leads to Hanumān's place on the banner. It is not in the Mahābhārata (it does not appear in the CE), and this page has not identified its textual source.
- **Recalling the Brahmaśiras** - After the night massacre, **Aśvatthāman** looses the **Brahmaśiras** against the Pāṇḍavas. Arjuna answers with his own, and Nārada and Vyāsa stand between the two weapons. Arjuna, who has kept *brahmacarya* and obeyed his teacher, **withdraws his weapon**. Aśvatthāman cannot withdraw his, and turns it on the wombs of the Pāṇḍava women (CE 10.14–15, esp. 10.15.1–10; [Sauptika Parva](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md)).

## X. Babhruvāhana and the death-and-revival (Mbh Āśvamedhika Parva ~78–81)

- **The horse reaches Maṇipura** - Following Yudhiṣṭhira's post-war **Aśvamedha** horse, Arjuna reaches **Maṇipūra**. His son **Babhruvāhana** comes out humbly with brāhmaṇas and gifts. Arjuna denounces him — "shame on you, ignorant of kṣatriya dharma" — for meeting an armed intruder with peace (CE 14.78.1–5; G Āśvamedhika 79).
- **The slaying** - **Ulūpī** rises through the earth, names herself his "mother", and urges Babhruvāhana to fight. In the battle, Babhruvāhana **pierces Arjuna in the heart** with an arrow, and Arjuna falls dead (CE 14.78.8–33). Citrāṅgadā laments and accuses Ulūpī, and Babhruvāhana sits down to fast to death (CE 14.78.39–14.80).
- **The Vasus' curse fulfilled** - Ulūpī explains (CE 14.82.9–19; G Āśvamedhika 81):
  - Arjuna killed **Bhīṣma** while Bhīṣma was engaged with Śikhaṇḍin and using him as cover.
  - The **Vasus**, with **Gaṅgā**, cursed Arjuna for it at the Gaṅgā's bank.
  - Ulūpī's father pleaded with them, and they fixed the release: when his own son Babhruvāhana felled him, the curse would end.
- **Revival by the Nāgamaṇi** - Ulūpī calls to mind the **saṃjīvana maṇi** ("reviving gem"), and Babhruvāhana lays it on his father's breast. Arjuna **rises as if from sleep** and is reconciled with his son (CE 14.81.2–21; G Āśvamedhika 80). The epic calls it simply the *saṃjīvana maṇi*. "Mṛtasaṃjīvanī" is a name from later retellings.
  - *Variant [late text]:* in the **Jaiminīya Aśvamedha** (the Aśvamedhaparvan of the *Jaiminibhārata*, a separate medieval Sanskrit retelling of uncertain date), Babhruvāhana **beheads** Arjuna, and the revival becomes a larger adventure. Secondary summaries describe a war in the Nāga world for the reviving gem; this page has not checked the Sanskrit text for the details. The Mughal Persian *Razmnāma* of 1616–17 illustrates "Babhruvāhana severs Arjuna's head, which then disappears into the sky". These versions go beyond the epic's terser core, in which Arjuna dies of a single arrow to the heart.

## XI. The Anugītā — the forgotten Gītā (Mbh Āśvamedhika Parva ~16–51)

- **"Teach me again"** - After the war, Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna stay in the **assembly hall at Indraprastha** (CE 14.15.5, 14.16.1–3), presumably the hall Maya built, though the text does not name him here. Arjuna confesses that what Kṛṣṇa told him on the battlefield "is all lost to me, my mind having strayed", and asks to hear it again. Kṛṣṇa is displeased — "surely you lack faith and are dull-witted" (CE 14.16.10). He says he **cannot repeat it in full**, because he spoke the supreme Brahman then "while joined in yoga". Instead he tells an **ancient narrative** on the same theme (CE 14.16.5–13; G Āśvamedhika 16). See the dedicated [**Anugītā**](../anugita.md).
- **Content** - The discourse runs **35 adhyāyas in the CE (14.16–50)**, or 36 sections in Ganguli (16–51). It nests several teachings:
  - Kāśyapa and a siddha.
  - a **brāhmaṇa and his wife** (the "Brāhmaṇa-gītā").
  - a **teacher and pupil**, which reports Brahmā's teaching to the ṛṣis.

  Its topics include the vital breaths and senses, the three guṇas, the "Brahma-tree", and mokṣa. It is a drier, more scholastic recap than the original Gītā.
  - *[scholarly]* **K. T. Telang** (SBE 8, 1882, introduction to the Anugītā, p. 227) judged it very old — known to Śaṅkara, probably older than Āpastamba's Dharmasūtra. He nonetheless concluded that "the Anugītā stands at a very considerable chronological distance from the Bhagavadgītā", that is, it is considerably later.

## XII. End of life (Mbh Mausala → Svargārohaṇa Parvas)

- **Dvārakā's fall** - After the **Yādava massacre** and **Kṛṣṇa's death**, Arjuna leads the surviving Vṛṣṇi women, children and old men out of Dvārakā, which the sea then swallows. In the land of the five rivers, **Ābhīra** robbers armed with sticks attack the column (CE 16.8.43–47). Arjuna's failure is described precisely (CE 16.8.52–66; G Mausala 7–8):
  - he **strings Gāṇḍīva only with great difficulty**.
  - he **cannot recall his divine weapons**.
  - his once-inexhaustible arrows run out, so he strikes at the robbers with the bow's tip.
  - the robbers carry off many of the women before his eyes.

  The epic does not say he was unable to lift the bow. He settles the survivors at Mārtikāvata, by the Sarasvatī, and at **Indraprastha**, where Kṛṣṇa's great-grandson **Vajra** is made king (CE 16.8.67–73). Then Vyāsa consoles him: his task is done and his time is past (CE 16.9).
- **The great departure** - Yudhiṣṭhira crowns **Parīkṣit** at Hastināpura. The five Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī, followed by a **dog**, renounce everything and walk east, south and west, and then north past the Himālaya towards **Meru** (CE 17.1). They fall one by one: Draupadī, Sahadeva, Nakula, then Arjuna, then Bhīma. Of Arjuna, Yudhiṣṭhira says: **"He said 'I will burn up our enemies in a single day' and did not do it. Proud of his valour, he fell. He also despised all other bowmen"** (CE 17.2.21–22; G Mahāprasthānika 2).
- **Heaven** - When Yudhiṣṭhira reaches heaven, he sees Kṛṣṇa in his divine form, **attended by the radiant Phalguna** (CE 18.4.3; [Svargārohaṇa Parva](../mahabharata/18-svargarohana-parva.md)). Arjuna's line continues on earth through his **grandson Parīkṣit** (son of Abhimanyu), whose son **Janamejaya** is the king who hears the Mahābhārata recited.

---

## XIII. Family — at a glance

- **Father:** Indra (divine); Pāṇḍu (mortal/legal). **Mother:** Kuntī (Pṛthā).
- **Brothers:** Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma (elder, also by Kuntī); Nakula and Sahadeva (the twins, by Mādrī). Karṇa was Kuntī's first son, born before her marriage.
- **Wives & sons:**
  - **Draupadī** → Śrutakarman (CE 1.213.72) / Śrutakīrti (CE 1.90.82).
  - **Ulūpī** (Nāga) → Irāvat / Irāvān (CE 6.86.6).
  - **Citrāṅgadā** (Maṇalūra / Maṇipura) → Babhruvāhana, heir to her father's line (CE 1.207.22, 1.209.24).
  - **Subhadrā** (Yādava) → Abhimanyu, husband of Uttarā and father of Parīkṣit.
- **Grandson-line:** Abhimanyu → **Parīkṣit** (Arjuna's grandson) → **Janamejaya** (his great-grandson) — the surviving Kuru dynasty.

## XIV. Themes

- **The ideal kṣatriya** - Skill, discipline and devotion fused; the chosen vessel for the Gītā precisely because he is the dutiful doer who must be taught *why* to act.
- **Friendship with the divine** - The **Nara-Nārāyaṇa** pairing recurs throughout the epic: Arjuna is **Nara** and Kṛṣṇa **Nārāyaṇa**, ancient ṛṣi-gods and companions across ages. The heavenly voice at Khāṇḍava says it (CE 1.219.15), so does Śiva at the Kirāta encounter (CE 3.41.1), and so does Bhīṣma's speech in the Kuru council, where they are "one being divided in two" (CE 5.48.1–20). See also [Dambhodbhava and Nara-Nārāyaṇa](../dambhodbhava-nara-narayana.md).
- **Pride and its fall** - *[interpretive]* The epic repeatedly checks Arjuna's pride in his bow. He asks Droṇa to keep his promise against Ekalavya. He is humbled by the Kirāta. His weapons fail him against the Ābhīras. And Yudhiṣṭhira names his boasting and his contempt for other archers as the cause of his fall. The arc closes when Gāṇḍīva goes back to Varuṇa's waters.
