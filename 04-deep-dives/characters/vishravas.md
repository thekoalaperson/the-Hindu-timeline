# Viśravas — the Sage Who Fathered Both Houses

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Viśravas (Viśrava)** — son of the Prajāpati [Pulastya](./pulastya.md), father of **[Kubera](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/kubera.md)** by his brahmin wife and of **[Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md), [Kumbhakarṇa](./kumbhakarna.md), [Śūrpaṇakhā](./shurpanakha.md)** and **[Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md)** by the rākṣasī **[Kaikasī](./kaikasi.md)** — the single hinge on which the *Rāmāyaṇa*'s two opposed dynasties turn. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). His narrative belongs entirely to the [Uttara Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md); the dynastic frame is [Paulastya / rākṣasa vaṃśa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/paulastya-rakshasa-vamsha.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* he belongs to the deep prehistory of the **[Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the present 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara — a generation above Rāvaṇa and two below Brahmā. On the academic clock he exists only in the **Uttara Kāṇḍa** and its Purāṇic parallels, with a divergent Mahābhārata account (§Other traditions). [scholarly]

## Name, birth and the sentence that made him

- **Born of an overheard declaration (Uttara Kāṇḍa 2)** — **Pulastya**, mind-born son of Brahmā, undertakes austerities at the hermitage of **Tṛṇabindu** on the slopes of Meru. The daughters of sages, nāgas and rājarṣis come there to sing and play and break his concentration, and the sage declares aloud that **whichever maiden next falls under his gaze shall conceive**. One girl did not hear him — **Tṛṇabindu's own daughter** — and she walks in on him reciting the Veda and shows the signs at once. Tṛṇabindu brings her back and gives her to Pulastya in marriage as alms; pleased with her, the sage promises her **a son "like unto myself"** (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa* 2).
- **The name is the mechanism** — the boy is called **Viśravas** because the declaration was *viśruta*, "heard abroad" — and because the girl was hearing the **Veda recited** when she conceived. He is also **Paulastya**, "son of Pulastya," the patronymic Rāvaṇa inherits (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 2). The whole Laṅkā line therefore descends from a single sentence spoken out of irritation, and the corpus flags that this passage is **core Uttara text, not a regional embellishment** (see [The Mind-Born Seers](../the-mind-born-seers.md)).
- **A sage in his own right** — Vālmīki presents Viśravas as a **brahmarṣi** of the first rank, "renowned throughout the three worlds for his wisdom and piety," austere, truthful, and the equal of his father. He is never drawn as a weak or corrupted man; the epic's problem with him is not his character but his consequences (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 2–3).
- **Viśravas is not Vaiśravaṇa** — the commonest confusion in the genealogy. **Viśravas** is the father; **Vaiśravaṇa** is the patronymic borne by his eldest son, Kubera. Translations that render both as "Vaisravana" collapse a generation (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 3).

## The first house — Ilavidā and Kubera (Uttara Kāṇḍa 3)

- **The brahmin marriage** — Viśravas marries **Ilavidā** (also **Ilavilā / Iḍaviḍā / Devavarṇinī**), daughter of the sage **[Bharadvāja](../bharadvaja.md)**, who gives her to him. Their son is a child "full of vigour, endowed with all the brahmic qualities," whom Pulastya himself names **Vaiśravaṇa** — because, the text says, the child resembles Viśravas (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 3). Bharadvāja is thereby Kubera's maternal grandfather, and stands one marriage away from the house that produces Rāvaṇa.
- **The son who out-ascends the father** — Vaiśravaṇa leaves for the forest and performs penance for a thousand years, living on water, then air, then nothing at all. **Brahmā** grants him the **lordship of wealth**, station as the **fourth Lokapāla** beside Indra, Yama and Varuṇa, the flying chariot **Puṣpaka**, and equality with the gods (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 3).
- **The father chooses his son's capital** — Brahmā gives Kubera **no city**. It is Vaiśravaṇa who asks his father where he should dwell, and **Viśravas** who names the golden **Laṅkā on Mount Trikūṭa** — built by Viśvakarmā, standing empty since Viṣṇu drove the rākṣasas down to Rasātala. Kubera takes up residence there on his father's word (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 3). The corpus is precise about this because the popular account, in which Brahmā bestows Laṅkā, gets both the giver and the sarga wrong (see [Uttara Kāṇḍa §II](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md) and [Kubera](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/kubera.md)).

## The second house — Kaikasī and the twilight ruling (Uttara Kāṇḍa 9)

- **The rākṣasī at dusk** — **Kaikasī**, daughter of the exiled rākṣasa lord **[Sumālī](./sumali-and-malyavan.md)**, is sent by her father to win Viśravas expressly so that she may bear sons the equal of Kubera. She arrives at **dusk**, interrupting his evening devotions, and stands silent with her eyes on her feet, scratching the earth with her toe (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9).
- **He does not refuse; he rules on the hour** — Viśravas accepts her, and pronounces on the moment rather than the woman: because she has come at that hour she will bear **offspring of dark aspect, delighting in the company of doers of evil deeds**. The taint is attached to the timing of the union, not to her blood or to any fault of hers (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9).
- **The exception he grants** — Kaikasī objects — *"I do not desire such sons whose nature is depraved; be gracious unto me"* — and Viśravas concedes exactly one: **the last-born shall be like myself, and virtuous**. That single sentence is the textual origin of **Vibhīṣaṇa**, the brother who defects to Rāma and survives to rule Laṅkā (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9).
- **Four children** — **Rāvaṇa**, **Kumbhakarṇa**, **Śūrpaṇakhā**, **Vibhīṣaṇa**, in that order (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9). Vālmīki assigns all four to Kaikasī alone; the schemes that spread them across several rākṣasī mothers belong to other texts (§Other traditions).
- **What the epic does not say** — Vālmīki nowhere condemns the marriage, nowhere calls it a seduction, and nowhere has Viśravas repent of it. The judgment supplied by retellings — that the sage was tricked, or fell — is not in the sarga. [scholarly]

## The two rulings on Laṅkā — the sage who decides the city twice

The one thing Viśravas does in the epic besides father children is to say who shall hold Laṅkā. He says it twice, in opposite directions, and both times he is obeyed.

- **First ruling: Kubera in (Uttara 3)** — he names the empty golden city to his elder son and installs the wealth-god there.
- **Second ruling: Kubera out (Uttara 11)** — when Sumālī comes up from Rasātala and **[Prahasta](./prahasta.md)** argues the claim, Rāvaṇa sends Prahasta as envoy demanding Laṅkā. Kubera will not answer alone: he goes to **his father** for counsel. Viśravas tells him plainly that Rāvaṇa's boons have unhinged his judgment — that his brother **can no longer discriminate between what is just and what is not** — and that rather than see brothers fight, Kubera should **give up the city and withdraw**. Kubera obeys, leaves with his household and treasure, and founds his new capital on **Mount Kailāsa**; Rāvaṇa enters Laṅkā and is consecrated in it (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 11).
- **Why the pair matters** — Laṅkā changes hands **without a battle**, on a father's advice, and the corpus keeps this beat strictly separate from the later **yakṣa war** (Uttara 13–15), in which Rāvaṇa murders Kubera's envoy, marches on Kailāsa, strikes his brother down with a mace and carries off the **Puṣpaka**. The peaceful cession and the war are two events, two sargas apart, and retellings routinely fuse them (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 11 vs. 13–15; see [Rāvaṇa §IV](./ravana.md)).
- **Then silence** — after Uttara 11 Viśravas leaves the narrative. He does not appear at the war, is not among the elders who counsel Rāvaṇa in the Yuddha Kāṇḍa, and Vālmīki records no death for him. [open-ended]

## Other traditions

- *Variant (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva — the anger-birth):* the epic of the Bhāratas reverses the generations. There **Kubera is Pulastya's own son**, and Viśravas is produced afterwards: slighted when Kubera attends Brahmā over his father, Pulastya brings forth **Viśravas out of half of his own being**, and his wrath passes into the new sage. To appease that anger Kubera sends Viśravas three rākṣasīs as wives, and the demon brood springs from them. Vālmīki's Viśravas is simply Pulastya's son and Kubera's father; the two schemes are not reconcilable and the corpus keeps them apart (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva*; canonical home: [Rāmopākhyāna](../ramopakhyana.md), and see [Kubera §Parentage](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/kubera.md)). [disputed]
- *Variant (Purāṇic genealogies):* the Purāṇas follow Vālmīki's generation-count — Pulastya → Viśravas → Kubera — while distributing the rākṣasa children among several of Sumālī's daughters rather than to Kaikasī alone (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 1.8, 1.21; Purāṇic genealogical tradition). [disputed]
- *Variant (mother's name):* Kubera's mother appears as **Ilavidā, Ilavilā, Iḍaviḍā** and **Devavarṇinī** across recensions, and her father as **Bharadvāja** or, in scattered lists, **Tṛṇabindu** — which would make Kubera's mother his father's own half-sister. The corpus records the spread and adopts none of it as settled (*recensional variance*; see [Bharadvāja](../bharadvaja.md)). [disputed]
- *Gotra tradition:* Viśravas transmits the **Paulastya** gotra, and the patronymic **Paulastya** is used of Rāvaṇa throughout the epic — the brahmin descent the antagonist never loses, and the reason his killing raises the brahmin-slaying question at all (*gotra-pravara tradition*; see [Ṛṣi lineages & gotras](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rishi-lineages-and-gotras.md)). [scholarly]

## Why he matters

- **The join** — every hybrid quality the *Rāmāyaṇa* gives Rāvaṇa comes through Viśravas: the Vedic mastery, the sāma-chanting, the *tapas* that wins Brahmā's boon, the brahmin patronymic. The epic's antagonist is not an outsider to the sacred order but a descendant of it in the third generation from Brahmā (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 2–10). [scholarly]
- **Two sons, two cities, one father** — Kubera in Alakā and Rāvaṇa in Laṅkā are the epic's paired images of what wealth and power do in the hands of the dharmic and the boon-drunk; both hold their capital on Viśravas's word.
- **The silent standard** — the corpus notes that Vālmīki uses Viśravas as a measure rather than an actor: he is invoked to explain what Rāvaṇa should have been, and the concession he grants Kaikasī is the only reason Laṅkā has a righteous heir left to crown at the end of the war.
