# Kṛpa (Kṛpācārya) — Royal Preceptor and Cirañjīvi

> Where this sits: the **Dvāpara-into-Kali life** of the first royal weapons-teacher at Hastināpura — the *ācārya* who outlived the war and is counted among the deathless. **Kṛpa** (Sanskrit *Kṛpa*, "pity / grace"; hence **Kṛpācārya**, "teacher Kṛpa") is the brahmin-born master of arms who is the **first** weapons-instructor of the Kuru princes — preceptor to both the Kauravas and the [Pāṇḍavas](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md) before [Droṇa](drona.md) ever reaches Hastināpura. Son of the sage **Śaradvat** (Śaradvān, of the Gautama–Aṅgirasa line), twin brother of **Kṛpī**, brother-in-law of **Droṇa** and uncle of **[Aśvatthāman](../the-chiranjivis.md)**, he is one of the very few warriors to **survive** the Kurukṣetra war, and one of the eight **[cirañjīvīs](../the-chiranjivis.md)** (deathless ones). His arc is the epic's image of the *perennial teacher*: present at the dice-hall, on the battlefield as a *mahāratha*, in the dishonour of the night-massacre, and finally surviving as a tutor to the next generation — the line of teaching that does not die when the warriors do.

## I. Birth from the śara-grass (Mbh Ādi Parva)

- **Conception of Śaradvat's twins** - The sage **Śaradvat** (son of Gautama) is born already holding arrows and is devoted to *dhanurveda* rather than the Veda. The apsarā **Jānapadī** (sent by Indra to break his austerities) appears before him; at the sight his seed (*retas*) escapes and falls upon a clump of **śara-grass** (reed), where it splits into two — a **boy and a girl** (Mbh Ādi Parva ~129–130). Like Droṇa, the twins are *ayonija* (not womb-born).
- **Found and named for pity** - King **Śantanu** (some tellings: his men, while hunting) discovers the abandoned twins in the forest and, **moved to pity** (*kṛpā*), takes them to his palace and rears them — naming the boy **Kṛpa** and the girl **Kṛpī** for the compassion that saved them (Mbh Ādi Parva).
- **Śaradvat returns to teach his son** - Learning by his powers that the children are his own, **Śaradvat** comes to Śantanu's court and imparts to **Kṛpa** the full science of arms — the four divisions of *dhanurveda*, the secrets of weapons and their recall — making him a complete *ācārya* (Mbh Ādi Parva ~130).
- **The two ayonija houses joined** - Kṛpī is given in marriage to **Droṇa**; from that union comes **Aśvatthāman**. Kṛpa is thus uncle to Droṇa's son, and the Śaradvat and Bharadvāja lines are bound together at the Kuru court (Mbh Ādi Parva). → cross-reference: [Droṇa](drona.md), [the Cirañjīvīs](../the-chiranjivis.md).

## II. First preceptor of the Kuru princes (Mbh Ādi Parva)

- **The earliest teacher at Hastināpura** - **Bhīṣma** appoints **Kṛpa** to instruct the young Kuru and Pāṇḍava boys in arms — he is the **first formal weapons-master** of the princes, *before* Droṇa arrives (whom Bhīṣma later adds when Kṛpa's brother-in-law demonstrates the grass-arrow feat) (Mbh Ādi Parva ~130–131).
- **A standing court figure** - Thereafter Kṛpa is a permanent fixture of the Hastināpura court — a *rāja-purohita*-like elder and *ācārya*, ranked with Bhīṣma, Droṇa and Vidura among the venerable counsellors (Mbh Ādi Parva onward).
- **At the graduation tournament** - Kṛpa is present at the public exhibition of arms; in some tellings it is **Kṛpa who challenges Karṇa** to declare his lineage (since combat with Arjuna requires equal royal birth), exposing Karṇa's unknown parentage and prompting Duryodhana to crown him king of Aṅga (Mbh Ādi Parva ~136).
  - *Variant (tellings):* the lineage-challenge to Karṇa is variously assigned to Kṛpa or to Bhīṣma in different recensions; the Critical Edition gives the formal challenge to Kṛpa.

## III. Before and through the war (Mbh Sabhā–Śalya Parvas)

- **At the dice-hall** - Kṛpa is among the elders present at the dice game and the disrobing of Draupadī; like Bhīṣma and Droṇa he does not prevent it, bound to the Kaurava bread (Mbh Sabhā Parva).
- **Bound to the Kauravas** - In the war Kṛpa fights for **Duryodhana**, whose sustenance he and his nephew have eaten, though (like his brother-in-law) he holds no enmity for the Pāṇḍavas (Mbh Udyoga Parva).
- **A mahāratha of the Kaurava host** - Kṛpa is reckoned a **mahāratha** (master car-warrior) and fights through all eighteen days; he is repeatedly named among the screen of great warriors around Droṇa and Duryodhana (Mbh Bhīṣma–Karṇa Parvas).
- **Among the killers of Abhimanyu** - On day 13, Kṛpa is one of the warriors — with **Droṇa, Karṇa, Aśvatthāman, Duryodhana, [Kṛtavarman](kritavarma.md)** and Bṛhadbala — who surround and kill the disarmed teenage **Abhimanyu** many-against-one inside the Cakravyūha, against the rules of single combat (Mbh Droṇa Parva ~48). → [Droṇa](drona.md).
- **Grief at Droṇa's fall** - When his brother-in-law Droṇa is beheaded by Dhṛṣṭadyumna while seated in yoga (day 15), Kṛpa shares in the family's grief — a wound that returns at the war's end (Mbh Droṇa Parva).
- **Counsel to the dying Duryodhana** - In the final days Kṛpa repeatedly counsels peace; in one episode he urges Duryodhana to make terms with the Pāṇḍavas, who cannot be beaten — advice the king rejects (Mbh Śalya Parva).

## IV. The Sauptika night-massacre (Mbh Sauptika Parva)

- **One of the last three Kaurava survivors** - After Duryodhana is struck down at the lake (close of [Śalya Parva](../mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md)), the **only Kaurava warriors left alive** are **Aśvatthāman**, **Kṛpa** and **[Kṛtavarman](kritavarma.md)** (Mbh X, Sauptika P.). → full narrative: [10 — Sauptika Parva](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md).
- **Under the banyan** - The three reach the dying **Duryodhana**, who consecrates **Aśvatthāman** as final commander-in-chief; at nightfall they lie down under a great banyan. Kṛpa and Kṛtavarman sleep; only the rage-sick Aśvatthāman cannot (Mbh X, adhy. 1).
- **Kṛpa argues against the night-raid** - Woken and told Aśvatthāman's plan to massacre the sleeping Pāñcālas, **Kṛpa speaks for dharma** — urging that they rest, consult **Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Gāndhārī**, and resume open battle by day, warning that to kill the sleeping and unarmed is sinful and will damn them (Mbh X, adhy. 1–2). This is Kṛpa's moral high-water mark: he is the voice of restraint.
- **He yields and stands guard** - Unable to dissuade his nephew (and unwilling to abandon him), Kṛpa relents. He and Kṛtavarman **guard the three gates** of the Pāṇḍava camp and **set fire to it in three places**, cutting down any who flee the blades, while Aśvatthāman does the killing within (Mbh X, adhy. 2, 8).
  - *Reading [scholarly]:* the epic carefully distinguishes Kṛpa's culpability — he opposes the deed in word, yet his hand is on the gate and the torch. The parva makes him neither instigator nor innocent: the elder who knew better and complied. The night-raid is technically a *senāpati's* act (Aśvatthāman's), but Kṛpa's assent stains him.
- **Return at dawn** - Their work done, the three ride back to the dying Duryodhana and report the destruction of the Pāñcālas and the Draupadeyas; Duryodhana dies content (Mbh X, adhy. 9). Kṛpa is not pursued or punished by the Pāṇḍavas — only the gem-bearing Aśvatthāman is hunted.

## V. Survival, the cirañjīvi tradition, and the post-war teacher (Mbh Strī–Mahāprasthānika; Purāṇas)

- **One of the war's handful of survivors** - At the close of Sauptika Parva, of the entire Kaurava host only the three night-raiders live; Kṛpa, alone of them, ends the war **without curse or open wound** — his nephew is cursed to wander, Kṛtavarman returns to a doomed Yādava fate, but Kṛpa simply endures (Mbh X–XI).
- **Reconciled to the victors** - In the aftermath Kṛpa is **re-accepted at Hastināpura**; the Pāṇḍavas bear him no grudge, honouring the *ācārya* who first taught them arms (Mbh Strī / Śānti Parvas).
- **Preceptor of Parīkṣit** - With the Kuru line nearly extinct, Kṛpa becomes the **weapons-teacher of Parīkṣit** — the revived grandson of Arjuna who inherits the throne — carrying the office of *ācārya* into the new generation of the Kali age (Mbh; Purāṇic tradition).
  - *Variant (tellings):* some traditions also make Kṛpa a teacher of **Janamejaya** (Parīkṣit's son), extending his tutorship across two further Kuru generations [late text / regional].
- **One of the eight Cirañjīvīs** - Kṛpa is named in the standard **cirañjīvi-smaraṇa** verse (*Aśvatthāmā Balir Vyāso Hanumāṁś ca Vibhīṣaṇaḥ / Kṛpaḥ Paraśurāmaś ca…*) as one of the deathless ones who endure through the whole Kalpa (traditional *stotra*; numerous Purāṇic compilations). → [the Cirañjīvīs](../the-chiranjivis.md).
  - *Note [scholarly]:* unlike Aśvatthāman, whose deathlessness is a **curse**, Kṛpa's is grace — he embodies the **continuity of right teaching** that outlives every war.
- **Future Saptarṣi** - Purāṇic tradition holds that Kṛpa will become one of the **Saptarṣis (Seven Sages) of the coming eighth (Sāvarṇi) Manvantara** — his immortality bridging this world-cycle to the next (Purāṇas; see the [manvantara system](../../00-time-and-cosmology/manvantara-system.md)).
  - *Reading [scholarly]:* among the cirañjīvīs, three look forward to the next age — **Bali** (future Indra), **Kṛpa** (future Saptarṣi), and **Paraśurāma** (Kalki's guru). Kṛpa's promotion from royal tutor to cosmic sage seals his attribute: *guidance that does not die*.

## Sources

- *Mahābhārata*, **Ādi Parva** — the birth of Kṛpa and Kṛpī from Śaradvat's seed on the śara-grass, their rearing by Śantanu, Śaradvat's teaching of arms, Kṛpa's appointment as first preceptor of the princes, and the tournament challenge to Karṇa (chs. ~129–136).
- *Mahābhārata*, **Sabhā / Udyoga Parvas** — Kṛpa at the dice-hall and his bound loyalty to the Kauravas.
- *Mahābhārata*, **Droṇa / Karṇa / Śalya Parvas** — Kṛpa as *mahāratha*, his part in the killing of Abhimanyu, and his counsel of peace to Duryodhana.
- *Mahābhārata*, **Sauptika Parva (Book 10)** — Kṛpa as one of the three survivors, his argument against the night-raid, his guarding of the gate and firing of the camp (Critical Edition / Ganguli, adhy. 1–9).
- *Mahābhārata*, **Strī / Śānti / Āśvamedhika Parvas** — his survival, reconciliation, and tutorship of Parīkṣit.
- **Cirañjīvi tradition** — the *cirañjīvi-smaraṇa* couplet (daily *stotra*) naming Kṛpa among the eight immortals; Purāṇic notice of Kṛpa as a future Saptarṣi of the Sāvarṇi Manvantara.
