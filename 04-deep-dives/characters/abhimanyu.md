# Abhimanyu

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Abhimanyu** (Saubhadra), son of Arjuna and Subhadrā, the sixteen-year-old prodigy who broke into the *cakravyūha* he could enter but not exit, was killed by a ring of Kaurava champions on the 13th day, and whose posthumous son Parīkṣit carried the Kuru line. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). His war and death sit inside the [Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md), in the embedded *Abhimanyu-vadha Parva*.
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* this falls in the present 28th Mahāyuga of the 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara, on the cusp of Dvāpara/Kali (Kali Yuga begins 3102 BCE at the war's close); the Critical Edition of the *Mahābhārata* (BORI) is the scholarly textual baseline. [scholarly]

## Birth, divine origin, and names
- **Son of Arjuna and Subhadrā** - born at Indraprastha after Arjuna abducted-and-wed Subhadrā (Kṛṣṇa's and Balarāma's sister) at the close of his twelve-year tīrtha-exile; thus a Pāṇḍava prince and a Yādava on his mother's side (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva, Subhadrā-haraṇa & Harāṇa-hārika Parvas*). See [Ādi Parva](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md).
- **Incarnation of Varcas, son of Soma (Candra)** - in the gods' descent-plan, the moon-god agreed to let his son **Varcas** be born on earth as Arjuna's son to aid the burden-lifting of the earth, but on the strict condition that he return to Candra's abode after only sixteen years (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva, Sambhava Parva*). This celestial pact is the scriptural reason for his early death.
- **The name "Abhimanyu"** - glossed as "fearless / of unbroken fierce temper" (*abhi-* + *manyu*, "wrath, spirit"); the epithet **Janmavīra**, "brave from birth," carries the same sense (*Mahābhārata*).
- **Other names / epithets** - **Saubhadra** ("son of Subhadrā"), **Ārjuni / Arjunātmaja** ("son of Arjuna"), **Kārṣṇi** (through the Yādava line) (*Mahābhārata*).
- **Reared partly at Dvārakā** - in many retellings the boy spends his early years among the Vṛṣṇis at Dvārakā during the Pāṇḍavas' troubles, trained there before rejoining his father. [folk]

## Teachers and the half-learned cakravyūha
- **Trained by Arjuna, Kṛṣṇa, Balarāma, and Pradyumna** - schooled in arms by his father and his Yādava uncles/cousins; counted a first-rank archer while still a boy (*Mahābhārata*).
- **The womb-lesson** - the celebrated *Mahābhārata*-tradition tale: while Abhimanyu was in Subhadrā's womb, Arjuna narrated the technique of penetrating the spiral *cakravyūha*; Subhadrā fell asleep before the exit was described, so the unborn child learned only how to break **in**, never how to break **out** (*Mahābhārata*-tradition; popularly cited as the seed of his doom).
  - *Variant (Critical Edition emphasis):* the explicit "learned-in-the-womb, only-half" narration is treated as later folklore overlaid on the epic; the Critical text simply has Abhimanyu know how to pierce the formation but not how to escape it. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Krishna as narrator):* some regional retellings make Kṛṣṇa, not Arjuna, the one whose narration Subhadrā dozed through. [folk]
- **Knowledge gap, stated plainly** - on the 13th day Abhimanyu himself tells Yudhiṣṭhira he can break in but "does not know how to come out if danger arises within" (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).

## Marriage to Uttarā
- **Wed to Uttarā of Matsya** - married the princess **Uttarā**, daughter of King Virāṭa, at the end of the Pāṇḍavas' thirteenth (incognito) year; Arjuna, who had taught Uttarā dance/music as the eunuch **Bṛhannalā**, declined her for himself and took her as daughter-in-law instead, joining the houses of Matsya and the Pāṇḍavas (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva*). See [Virāṭa Parva](../mahabharata/04-virata-parva.md).
- **The alliance** - the wedding seals Matsya's, Pāñcāla's, and Yādava support for the coming war (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva*).

## War service before the 13th day
- **Ranked Atiratha** - Bhīṣma in his survey of warriors counts the boy among the *atirathas* / *rathas*, an extraordinary rating for his youth (*Mahābhārata, Udyoga/Bhīṣma Parvas*).
- **Early-day exploits** - over the first days he duels and overcomes many Kaurava notables — among them **Bṛhadbala** (king of Kosala), **Vikarṇa**, **Lakṣmaṇa** (Duryodhana's son), and Gāndhāra troops — and at one point **breaks Bhīṣma's flagstaff** (*Mahābhārata, Bhīṣma Parva*).
- **A standing terror** - repeatedly forces back front-rank Kauravas, marking him as the Pāṇḍava side's rising champion behind Arjuna (*Mahābhārata, Bhīṣma/Droṇa Parvas*).

## The 13th day — into the cakravyūha
- **Droṇa's wheel-array** - now commander, Droṇa arranges the army as a *cakravyūha* (discus/spiral formation) and lures Arjuna far away with the **Saṃśaptaka** (oath-bound) suicide-warriors, since only Arjuna can break the formation (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva, Abhimanyu-vadha Parva*).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's charge** - with Arjuna gone, Yudhiṣṭhira asks Abhimanyu to pierce the array; the boy agrees, warning he knows the entry but not the exit, on the understanding that the other Pāṇḍavas will follow close behind him into the breach (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).
- **The breach** - Abhimanyu shatters the formation's mouth and storms in, his chariot cutting deep into the Kaurava center (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).
- **Jayadratha seals the gap** - **Jayadratha**, king of Sindhu, holds the entry-point and turns back Bhīma, Yudhiṣṭhira and the rest, stranding Abhimanyu alone inside; he can do this because of a boon from **Śiva** (won by austerity) that he could check all the Pāṇḍavas in battle on a single day — except Arjuna (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*). This stranding is the act for which Arjuna later vows to kill Jayadratha by sunset on the 14th day; see [Karṇa](../karna.md) and [Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md).
- **Lone havoc** - trapped but undaunted, Abhimanyu kills warriors by the thousands and individually routs or wounds great names — in various passages **Karṇa, Śalya, Duḥśāsana, Kṛpa**, and others — and slays **Lakṣmaṇa**, Duryodhana's son, enraging the king (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).

## The death — the ring of warriors
- **Droṇa's counsel of foul means** - unable to stop him fairly, Droṇa advises that the only way is to break his bow, kill his horses, slay his wheel-guards and charioteer — i.e., disarm him by gang-assault, against the *kṣatriya-dharma* of single combat (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).
- **Systematic disarming** - the boy is stripped of his means of fight step by step:
  - **Karṇa cuts his bowstring** (and shatters the bow) from behind (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).
  - **Kṛtavarman kills his horses**; **Kṛpa slays the two wheel-guards** (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).
  - his charioteer is killed and his chariot smashed; his sword and shield are broken (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).
- **The chariot-wheel last stand** - weaponless, Abhimanyu lifts a chariot **wheel** as a shield-and-club and fights on, then a mace, killing more attackers even so — the iconic image of his death (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).
- **The fatal blow** - finally **the son of Duḥśāsana** (Duḥsāsana) strikes Abhimanyu on the head with a mace, shattering his crown and killing him at the age of sixteen (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).
- **A murder, not a battle-death** - the simultaneous assault by many great car-warriors on one disarmed boy is presented as the gravest breach of the war's code, the moral nadir of the Kaurava side and the trigger for the 14th day's vengeance (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).

### Variant rosters of the attackers (catalogued)
- *Variant A (Droṇa Parva narrative — the gang of six maharathis):* **Droṇa, Karṇa, Kṛpa, Kṛtavarman, Aśvatthāman, and Śakuni** coordinate the unfair assault (per the death-sequence chapter).
- *Variant B (Wikipedia/Hindupedia summary list):* the six are **Duryodhana, Duḥśāsana, Droṇa, Kṛpa, Karṇa, and Śakuni**.
- *Variant C (popular "seven mahārathis"):* the encircling ring named as **Droṇa, Karṇa, Kṛpa, Aśvatthāman, Bṛhadbala / Bhūriśravas, Kṛtavarman, and Śakuni** (rosters vary by retelling). [folk]
- *Variant D (the formation-makers vs. the killers):* some retellings distinguish the warriors who **built/held** the *cakravyūha* (adding **Śalya, Jayadratha, Bhūriśravas**) from the smaller group that actually **disarmed and killed** him. [folk]
- *Variant (final-blow agent):* near-universally **Duḥśāsana's son** (unnamed in many texts; called **Durmāsana / Durmukha** in some regional retellings) deals the killing mace-blow; a minority popular telling credits the mob collectively. [folk]
- Note: rosters differ in which great names "fought" Abhimanyu versus which delivered the disarming strokes; the disarming triad (bow / horses / wheel-guards) is the stable core, the surrounding cast is fluid. [disputed]

## Aftermath and legacy
- **Arjuna's oath** - returning to find his son dead, Arjuna swears to kill **Jayadratha** before sunset the next day or enter fire; Kṛṣṇa engineers the feat on the 14th day (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*). See [Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md).
- **Bhīma's vengeance** - Bhīma's later slaughter of Duḥśāsana and the hundred Kaurava brothers is partly framed as retribution for Abhimanyu (*Mahābhārata, Karṇa/Śalya Parvas*).
- **Widowed Uttarā** - left pregnant; her grief is among the epic's most-quoted laments (*Mahābhārata, Strī Parva*).

## Father of Parīkṣit — the line preserved
- **Posthumous son** - Uttarā, pregnant at Abhimanyu's death, bears his only child after the war: **Parīkṣit**, sole surviving heir of the Kuru/Pāṇḍava line (*Mahābhārata, Aśvamedhika/Mausala framing; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.8–1.12*).
- **Aśvatthāman's Brahmāstra and Kṛṣṇa's rescue** - after the war Aśvatthāman, to end the lineage, directs the **Brahmāstra (/ Aiṣīka weapon)** at Uttarā's womb; the child is stillborn (or struck within the womb), and **Kṛṣṇa revives him** by his power/truth, sparing the heir (*Mahābhārata, Sauptika Parva; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.8*). See related immortality-curse material in [The Chiranjivis](../the-chiranjivis.md).
- **The name "Parīkṣit"** - glossed "he who has been tested" (and, in a brahmin's prophecy, "born when the race was all but extinguished, who will go searching/*parīkṣ-* for the deliverer he saw in the womb"); he becomes king of Hastināpura after the Pāṇḍavas' final journey, and his death by the snake Takṣaka frames the entire *Mahābhārata*'s recitation at Janamejaya's snake-sacrifice (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva* frame; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*). 
  - *Variant (name-etymology):* *Bhāgavata* derives the name from his later wandering (*parīkṣamāṇa*) over the earth seeking the person he had glimpsed saving him in the womb (Kṛṣṇa). [late text]

## Regional and devotional variants
- *Variant (Javanese wayang — "Abimanyu"):* in Indonesian shadow-puppet tradition he is a central hero with wives **Dewi Siti Sundari** (daughter of Kṛṣṇa) and **Dewi Utari** (Uttarā), holds sacred revelations (*Wahyu Hidayat*, *Wahyu Makutharama*), and is father of **Parikesit**; his death likewise comes by encirclement. [folk]
- *Variant (folk "Vatsala/Śaśirekhā-haraṇa" cycle):* Telugu/Andhra and other folk dramas add a first marriage of Abhimanyu to **Śaśirekhā / Vatsalā** (Balarāma's daughter), with Ghaṭotkaca's help thwarting her betrothal to Duryodhana's son — an episode absent from the Critical Edition. [folk] [late text] See [Ghaṭotkaca](./ghatotkacha.md).
- *Variant (devotional emphasis):* bhakti retellings stress the Varcas/Soma pact so that Abhimanyu's death is fated and serene rather than merely tragic. [late text]
