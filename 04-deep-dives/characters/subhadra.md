# Subhadrā — Yādava Princess, Wife of Arjuna

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Subhadrā** — younger sister of [Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md) and Balarāma, abducted-and-wed by [Arjuna](./arjuna.md) at Kṛṣṇa's own counsel, mother of [Abhimanyu](./abhimanyu.md) and so great-grandmother of the surviving Kuru heir Parīkṣit; later deified as **Subhadrā** of the Jagannātha Purī triad. > Two clocks: as *itihāsa* this falls in the present 28th Mahāyuga of the 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara, on the Dvāpara/Kali cusp (Kali Yuga begins 3102 BCE at the war's close); the BORI Critical Edition of the *Mahābhārata* is the scholarly textual baseline. [scholarly]

## Identity, names, and lineage
- **Daughter of Vasudeva** — born to the Yādava chief **Vasudeva** (of the Vṛṣṇi line), making her full or half sister of **Kṛṣṇa** and **Balarāma**; her mother is named **Rohiṇī** in some tellings and **Devakī** in others (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva; Harivaṃśa; Bhāgavata Purāṇa*).
  - *Variant (Rohiṇī's daughter):* the *Harivaṃśa* and *Bhāgavata* commonly make her Rohiṇī's child, hence Balarāma's uterine sister — which sharpens his preference for Duryodhana as her groom. [scholarly]
- **Subhadrā ("the very auspicious / blessed")** — her standing name; the matronymic **Saubhadra** ("son/child of Subhadrā") attaches to her son Abhimanyu (*Mahābhārata*).
- **Bhadrā / Citrā** — alternative names given in some Purāṇic genealogies for Vasudeva's daughter (*Purāṇic genealogies*). [disputed]
- **Kṛṣṇa's and Pāṇḍu's kin both ways** — as Vasudeva's daughter she is Kṛṣṇa's sister; as wife of Arjuna she enters Kuntī's house, and Kuntī is herself a Yādava (sister of Vasudeva), so the marriage doubles an already-close Vṛṣṇi-Pāṇḍava tie (*Mahābhārata*). See [Kuntī](./kunti.md).

## The abduction-and-marriage (Subhadrā-haraṇa)
- **Setting at Dvārakā** — during the final stretch of his twelve-year *tīrtha*-pilgrimage (the penance for breaching the brothers' compact over Draupadī), Arjuna comes to **Prabhāsa** and then to **Dvārakā / Raivataka hill** at Kṛṣṇa's invitation, where the Vṛṣṇis are holding a festival and Arjuna first sees Subhadrā (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva, Subhadrā-haraṇa Parva*). See [Ādi Parva](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md).
- **Kṛṣṇa's counsel to seize her** — Arjuna, smitten, fears that ordinary suit will fail; **Kṛṣṇa advises abduction (rākṣasa-vivāha by capture)**, noting that *svayaṃvara* is uncertain for a kṣatriya and that forcible seizure of a bride is sanctioned and even praised for warriors (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva, Subhadrā-haraṇa Parva*).
- **Why abduction, not betrothal** — **Balarāma favours Duryodhana** as the match for Subhadrā; Kṛṣṇa wants Arjuna. Seizing her with Kṛṣṇa's quiet blessing pre-empts Balarāma's plan while letting Kṛṣṇa appear blameless (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **The seizure** — with Kṛṣṇa's leave (and that of the Yādava elders for Arjuna's pilgrim presence), Arjuna carries Subhadrā off in his chariot as she returns from worship at Raivataka; the Yādava guards are scattered, Subhadrā herself driving the team in some tellings (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva, Subhadrā-haraṇa Parva*).
- **Balarāma's fury, Kṛṣṇa's mediation** — Balarāma rages at the affront and would pursue and punish Arjuna; **Kṛṣṇa pacifies him**, arguing that Arjuna is the best of grooms, of equal Yādava blood through Kuntī, and that an abducted bride honours rather than shames the house. The Yādavas relent and the marriage is celebrated openly (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva, Harāṇa-hārika Parva*).
  - *Variant (Subhadrā's consent):* many retellings stress that Subhadrā **loved Arjuna and connived in her own "abduction,"** so that the rākṣasa-form is nominal — a face-saving device, not a true capture. [folk]
- **Dowry and return** — the Vṛṣṇis send rich gifts — chariots, horses, elephants, cattle, and servants — after the couple; Arjuna takes Subhadrā to **Indraprastha**, where Draupadī, initially displeased, is won over (Subhadrā approaches her as a servant/younger sister) (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Chronology note** — this falls within Arjuna's exile-pilgrimage, before the burning of Khāṇḍava and well before the dice-game and the great war; it precedes the births of Draupadī's sons in the timeline of Indraprastha (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). [scholarly]

## Mother of Abhimanyu — the womb-lesson
- **Birth of Abhimanyu** — Subhadrā bears **Abhimanyu** (Saubhadra) at Indraprastha, the prodigy who will die at sixteen breaking the *cakravyūha* (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva, Sambhava/Subhadrā-haraṇa Parvas*). See [Abhimanyu](./abhimanyu.md).
- **The half-heard cakravyūha (folk motif)** — in the celebrated *Mahābhārata*-tradition tale, while Abhimanyu was in Subhadrā's womb Arjuna narrated the technique of piercing the spiral *cakravyūha*; **Subhadrā fell asleep before the exit was described**, so the unborn child learned only how to break **in**, never how to break **out** — the seed of his doom (*Mahābhārata*-tradition; popularly cited). [folk]
  - *Variant (Critical Edition):* the explicit "learned-in-the-womb, only half" narration is later folklore overlaid on the epic; the Critical text simply has Abhimanyu know the entry but not the escape. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Kṛṣṇa as narrator):* some regional retellings make **Kṛṣṇa**, not Arjuna, the one whose narration Subhadrā dozed through. [folk]
- **The mother's role in the motif** — note that the doom is laid at Subhadrā's drowsiness, not the boy's inattention; the motif thus makes her an unwitting agent of Abhimanyu's fate. [folk]

## Widowhood, grief, and the line preserved
- **Loss of Abhimanyu** — Abhimanyu is killed on the war's 13th day by a ring of Kaurava champions; Subhadrā's lament (with the widowed Uttarā) is among the epic's most-quoted (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa/Strī Parvas*).
- **Genealogical keystone** — Abhimanyu's posthumous son **Parīkṣit** — saved in Uttarā's womb from Aśvatthāman's Brahmāstra by Kṛṣṇa — is the **sole surviving heir of the Kuru/Pāṇḍava line**. Subhadrā is thus Parīkṣit's grandmother and the Yādava-Pāṇḍava bridge through whom the dynasty (and the very frame of the *Mahābhārata*'s recitation at Janamejaya's sacrifice) continues (*Mahābhārata; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.8–1.12*). See [Abhimanyu](./abhimanyu.md).
- **Survives the Yādava end** — unlike most of her kin she outlives the Mausala destruction of the Vṛṣṇis and Kṛṣṇa's departure; she is among those Arjuna escorts from Dvārakā, and she ends her days with the Pāṇḍavas at Hastināpura (*Mahābhārata, Mausala/Mahāprasthānika framing*). [scholarly]
- **Guardian of the heir** — in many retellings the aged Subhadrā helps rear the orphaned Parīkṣit alongside Uttarā, closing her arc as protector of the line she founded by marriage. [folk]

## Subhadrā as deity — the Jagannātha triad
- **The Purī triad** — at the **Jagannātha temple, Purī (Odisha)**, the central image is flanked by two others: **Jagannātha** (Kṛṣṇa), **Balabhadra** (Balarāma), and **Subhadrā** between them, with the disc **Sudarśana** as a fourth element; here Subhadrā is worshipped as a **goddess**, the divine sister, not merely a heroine (*Sthala-purāṇa / temple tradition*). [regional]
- **Rath Yātrā (Car Festival)** — in the annual chariot festival each of the three rides a separate *ratha*; Subhadrā's car is named **Darpadalana** (or **Devadalana / Padmadhvaja** in variant lists), smaller than her brothers', drawn in procession to the Guṇḍicā temple (*temple tradition*). [regional]
- **Iconography** — the Purī images are aniconic wooden forms with large round eyes and stump-like arms; Subhadrā's image is the smallest of the three and lacks the arm-stumps of her brothers, often read as the feminine/Śakti pole of the triad (*temple tradition*). [regional]
- **Identifications layered on her** — devotional and Tantric strands variously identify the Purī Subhadrā with **Ekānaṃśā / Yoga-māyā** (Kṛṣṇa's sister-goddess who escaped Kaṃsa), with **Bhuvaneśvarī**, or with **Lakṣmī/Durgā** — collapsing the epic princess into a pan-Indian goddess (*Purāṇic & Tantric tradition*). [regional] [late text]
  - *Variant (Ekānaṃśā equation):* the *Harivaṃśa*'s **Ekānaṃśā** — the girl-child of Yaśodā substituted for the infant Kṛṣṇa and dashed by Kaṃsa, who rises as a goddess — is in several traditions equated with Subhadrā, giving the Yādava princess an independent divine birth-story. [disputed]
