# Sumitrā — Junior Queen, Mother of Lakṣmaṇa and Śatrughna

> The wise **third queen of Daśaratha** of Ayodhyā — mother of the twins **[Lakṣmaṇa](./lakshmana.md)** and **Śatrughna** (see [Bharata, Śatrughna & their wives](./bharata-shatrughna-and-their-wives.md)) — born when she shares both co-queens' portions of the divine pāyasa. The least-spoken but most clear-sighted of the three queens: her name (*su-mitrā*, "good friend / well-disposed") is borne out in conduct, and tradition crowns her with the celebrated **farewell counsel** that sends Lakṣmaṇa to serve Rāma "as a god" in the forest.
> Sits in the **[Tretā-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md). Her arc runs through the [Bāla-](../ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md) and [Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/2-ayodhya-kanda.md).
> Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Section index: [Epic Characters](./README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md) · Co-queens: [Kausalyā](./kausalya.md).

**Sumitrā** (Sanskrit *Sumitrā*, "good friend, kindly disposed") is the **third and junior-most queen** of King **Daśaratha** of Ayodhyā, after **[Kausalyā](./kausalya.md)** (the eldest, mother of Rāma) and **Kaikeyī** (the favourite, mother of Bharata). She is the mother of **two** of Daśaratha's four sons — the twins **[Lakṣmaṇa](./lakshmana.md)** and **Śatrughna** — because at the distribution of the sacrificial pāyasa she receives a half-share from each of her two co-wives. In the epic she is given little direct speech, yet what she is given is decisive: the tradition makes her the **voice of clear dharmic counsel**, the queen who, unlike Kaikeyī, harbours no ambition for her sons, and who reframes Rāma's exile not as loss but as the highest opportunity for service.

---

## I. Birth of her sons — the division of the pāyasa

- **The putrakāmeṣṭi** — Childless Daśaratha performs an **aśvamedha** followed by a **putrakāmeṣṭi (son-seeking) sacrifice**, conducted through the sage **[Ṛṣyaśṛṅga](../rishyashringa.md)**. A radiant being (the **Yajña-puruṣa / Prajāpati's messenger**) rises from the fire bearing a vessel of divine **pāyasa (sweet milk-rice porridge)** and instructs Daśaratha to share it among his queens (Rām. Bāla-kāṇḍa 15–16).
- **The distribution** — Daśaratha gives **half** the pāyasa to **Kausalyā** and **half of the remainder** to **Kaikeyī**; he then gives **half of what is left to Sumitrā**, deliberates, and gives the **final remaining portion also to Sumitrā** (Rām. Bāla-kāṇḍa 16). Because she alone consumes **two portions** (one drawn from each co-queen's share), Sumitrā bears **two sons**, and they are reckoned **twins**.
  - *Variant (sequence of giving):* recensions differ on the exact order — whether Sumitrā's two parts are given consecutively or whether her second part is what Kausalyā and Kaikeyī each set aside; the constant is that **her sons arise from a doubled / co-mingled share**. [variant]
- **Why this matters theologically** — In the pan-tradition reading, Rāma is **Viṣṇu**, and the brothers are portions of Viṣṇu's essence; Sumitrā's drawing from **both** Kausalyā's and Kaikeyī's pāyasa is taken to explain why her twins **attach severally** to the two elder brothers: **Lakṣmaṇa to Rāma** (son of Kausalyā) and **Śatrughna to Bharata** (son of Kaikeyī) (Purāṇic/devotional gloss on Rām. Bāla-kāṇḍa 18).
  - *Variant (Vaiṣṇava):* with Lakṣmaṇa as **Śeṣa/Ananta**, her two sons embody Viṣṇu's serpent-couch and the **Sudarśana cakra** (Śatrughna); see [Lakṣmaṇa](./lakshmana.md) and [Bharata & Śatrughna](./bharata-shatrughna-and-their-wives.md). [devotional]
- **Birth and naming** — The four princes are born; Sumitrā's elder twin is named **Lakṣmaṇa** ("of auspicious marks") and the younger **Śatrughna** ("slayer of foes"). The naming and horoscopes are set by **Vasiṣṭha** (Rām. Bāla-kāṇḍa 18).

## II. The mother who gives her sons away

- **No claim on the throne** — Unlike Kaikeyī (who demands Bharata's coronation and Rāma's exile) and unlike the grieving Kausalyā, Sumitrā is portrayed as **wholly without dynastic ambition**. Her sons are, by the pāyasa's own logic, **born to serve** the elder two rather than to rule — and the tradition treats her acceptance of this as her defining virtue.
- **Lakṣmaṇa's attachment to Rāma** — From childhood Lakṣmaṇa cannot be parted from Rāma; he eats and sleeps only where Rāma is, and Śatrughna likewise shadows Bharata (Rām. Bāla-kāṇḍa 18). Sumitrā, far from resenting this, is shown to **encourage** it.

## III. Sumitrā's farewell counsel — sending Lakṣmaṇa to the forest (Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa)

This is the episode for which Sumitrā is best remembered, and it is the centrepiece of her characterization. See the [Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/2-ayodhya-kanda.md) for the full sequence.

- **The crisis** — When Kaikeyī's two boons force **Rāma into fourteen years' exile** and Rāma resolves to go, **Lakṣmaṇa insists on accompanying** him; before departing he comes to his mother **Sumitrā** for leave.
- **Vālmīki's counsel** — In the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sumitrā does **not weep or restrain him**. She blesses his resolve, telling him in effect: **"Rāma is your dharma; the forest is your Ayodhyā; Sītā is your Sumitrā (your mother); go and serve, and never falter."** Her speech reframes the exile as an honour, not a calamity (Rām. Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa, Sumitrā's blessing).
  - The widely-quoted formulation — **"rāmaṃ daśarathaṃ viddhi māṃ viddhi janakātmajām / ayodhyām aṭavīṃ viddhi gaccha tāta yathāsukham"** — "Know Rāma to be Daśaratha (your father), know Sītā to be myself (your mother), know the forest to be Ayodhyā; go, my son, in happiness" — is the most cited line attributed to Sumitrā, condensing her counsel into a single śloka. [scholarly] (Its exact placement varies by recension; it circulates as the emblem of her wisdom.)
- **Tulsīdās's farewell (Rāmcaritmānas)** — In the **[Rāmcaritmānas](../ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md)** of Tulsīdās the counsel is expanded into its most famous devotional form: Sumitrā tells Lakṣmaṇa that the one supremely fortunate is he who finds the chance to serve Rāma and Sītā, **"prabhu (Rāma) is your father and mother and all; serve them with single-minded love, count their forest-dwelling your great good fortune, and let no thought of home or comfort enter your heart."** She sends her son away **rejoicing** that he has been chosen for such service (Rāmcaritmānas, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa). [late text]
  - *Variant (the "god to be served" framing):* a strand of the devotional tradition has Sumitrā explicitly tell Lakṣmaṇa to treat **Rāma as God incarnate** and the forest-service as worship — making her the first to articulate, within the family, Rāma's divinity and the exile as **tapas/bhakti** rather than punishment. [devotional]
- **Contrast with the other queens** — The Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa deliberately juxtaposes the three mothers: **Kaikeyī** the cause of the sorrow, **Kausalyā** prostrate in grief at losing Rāma, and **Sumitrā** alone composed — losing **both** her sons to the exile (Lakṣmaṇa to the forest, and Śatrughna soon away with Bharata) yet meeting it with serenity. This contrast is the chief literary purpose of her brief role. [scholarly]

## IV. During the exile and after

- **The queen who waits** — Through the fourteen years Sumitrā remains in Ayodhyā among the bereaved royal household; she shares **Kausalyā's vigil** and the kingdom's mourning, but the epic keeps her largely silent.
- **Daśaratha's death** — Daśaratha dies of grief soon after Rāma's departure, crying out for Rāma and recalling the curse of the blind sage's son (the **Śravaṇa-kumāra** episode). Sumitrā is among the widowed queens; in the funerary and dynastic aftermath she defers, as ever, to the elder Kausalyā (Rām. Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa).
- **The reunion** — On Rāma's triumphant return and coronation (the **Rāma-rājya**), the three queens are reunited with their sons. Sumitrā, who gave away both children to the service of the elder princes, recovers them honoured — Lakṣmaṇa as Rāma's right hand, Śatrughna (slayer of **Lavaṇāsura** and ruler of **Madhurā/Mathurā**) as a king in his own right (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa tradition; see [Bharata, Śatrughna & their wives](./bharata-shatrughna-and-their-wives.md)).

## V. Character and themes

- **The ideal of detached motherhood** — Sumitrā is held up as the mother who loves without clinging: she gives her sons to a cause higher than her own household and finds joy in their service. Against Kaikeyī's possessive ambition she is the dharmic counter-image.
- **"Good friend" to all three** — Her name *su-mitrā* is read as her conduct toward her co-queens: she never schemes, never competes, and is on good terms with both Kausalyā and (even) Kaikeyī — a stabilizing presence in the harem politics that drive the tragedy. [scholarly]
- **Voice of bhakti** — In the devotional stream (Tulsīdās especially), Sumitrā's farewell makes her the household's **first teacher of Rāma-bhakti**, the one who names service to Rāma as the soul's true fortune.
- **Worship and memory** — Sumitrā has no independent cult, but she is honoured within the Rāma-katha as the mother of Lakṣmaṇa and Śatrughna and as the speaker of the exemplary farewell; her counsel is a staple of Rāmāyaṇa recitation and discourse (*kathā/pravacana*). [scholarly]

## Sources

- **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa** — Bāla-kāṇḍa 15–18 (the putrakāmeṣṭi, the **division of the pāyasa**, the birth and naming of the four sons, the twins' attachments) and Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa (Lakṣmaṇa's leave-taking and **Sumitrā's blessing**; the queens' grief at Rāma's exile and Daśaratha's death). The base text — terse on Sumitrā but the source of her composure and her counsel.
- **Tulsīdās, Rāmcaritmānas** — Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa: the expanded **farewell of Sumitrā to Lakṣmaṇa**, framing forest-service to Rāma and Sītā as the supreme good fortune; the most beloved devotional form of her counsel. [late text] See [Rāmcaritmānas & variant Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md).
- **Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa & Purāṇic theology** — the brothers as portions of Viṣṇu's essence (Lakṣmaṇa as **Śeṣa/Ananta**, Śatrughna as the **Sudarśana cakra**), grounding the doubled-pāyasa logic. [devotional]
