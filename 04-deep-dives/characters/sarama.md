# Saramā — Vibhīṣaṇa's Wife, and Sītā's One Friend in Laṅkā

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Saramā** — gandharva princess, wife of [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md), and the woman who tells the captive [Sītā](./sita.md) that the severed head of Rāma set before her is an illusion, then goes and eavesdrops on [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md)'s war-council for her. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). Her two scenes are **Yuddha Kāṇḍa 33–34**; her parentage and her name-story are **Uttara Kāṇḍa 12**. Retellings that give her scenes to [Trijaṭā](./trijata.md) are merging the epic's two kind women of Laṅkā.
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* her scenes fall in the **Tretā Yuga** of the present 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara; the scholarly baseline is Vālmīki (vulgate/Southern sarga), with the Uttara Kāṇḍa a later stratum than the war-book that carries her main role. [scholarly]

## Name and identity

- **Saramā (सरमा)** — the epic supplies its own etymology: as an infant she played at the **Mānasa lake** and its waters rose to drown her, and her mother cried out ***"sara, mā!"*** — "O lake, do not overflow!" — and the words became the child's name (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa* 12).
- **A gandharva princess, not a rākṣasī by birth** — she is the **daughter of Śailūṣa, king of the gandharvas**, given by Rāvaṇa in marriage to his youngest brother Vibhīṣaṇa (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 12). The war-book, which meets her only inside Laṅkā and among Sītā's guard, counts her with the rākṣasīs; the birth is the Uttara's correction. See the [gandharvas & kinnaras roster](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/gandharvas-and-kinnaras.md).
- **Not the Saramā of the Ṛgveda** — the name is shared with **Saramā the divine bitch**, Indra's messenger who tracks the stolen cows to the Paṇis' cave (*Ṛgveda* 10.108) and whose sons are the Sārameyas, Yama's two hounds. The two are unconnected; that Saramā's entry sits in [mythic beasts & composites](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/mythic-beasts-and-composites.md). [scholarly]
- **Her father's name-collision** — **Śailūṣa** is also the name the *Agni Purāṇa* supplies for the gandharva king Bharata destroys at Takṣaśilā; Vālmīki never connects the two, and never names Bharata's foe at all. The consequence of merging them is set out in [Bharata's gandharva conquest](../bharata-gandharva-conquest-takshashila.md). [disputed]

## Marriage into the house of Laṅkā

- **One of four matches (Uttara 12)** — in the round of dynastic marriages Rāvaṇa arranges after taking Laṅkā, he weds **Mandodarī**, daughter of Maya (with the śakti as dowry); gives **Vajrajvālā**, daughter of Virocana, to Kumbhakarṇa; **Saramā**, daughter of Śailūṣa, to [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md); and his sister [Śūrpaṇakhā](./shurpanakha.md) to the dānava Vidyujjihva (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 12). Every one of the four brides is brought in from outside the rākṣasa line — Saramā from the gandharvas, Vajrajvālā and Mandodarī from the dānavas.
- **The pattern the marriage sets** — Vibhīṣaṇa is the brother who keeps dharma inside an adharmic house, and the wife he is given keeps it with him: both of Saramā's scenes have her working, at personal risk, for the prisoner her brother-in-law is holding. [scholarly]
- **Her daughter** — Vālmīki gives [Trijaṭā](./trijata.md) **no parentage at all**; the near-universal later identification of Trijaṭā as the daughter of Vibhīṣaṇa and Saramā is supplied tradition, not text. A separate strand names Saramā's informant-daughter **Kalā**, **Nandā** or **Analā**. Both belong to the later strata and are recorded as such. [late text] [disputed]

## The false head — Yuddha Kāṇḍa 33

- **How the epic introduces her** — after Rāvaṇa has shown Sītā the conjured head and bow and left her rolling in the dust of the aśoka grove, Saramā comes to her: an **affectionate companion** among the guard set over Sītā, **kind-hearted and firm in austerity**, who has watched over her out of love and not out of office (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 33.3–8). She tells Sītā that she had been hidden nearby and heard every word of the exchange with Rāvaṇa, and that she has **no fear of Rāvaṇa, because of her love for Sītā**.
- **The illusion named as illusion** — "a trickery has been hurled on you here by the cruel Rāvaṇa, a conjurer" — the head and the bow are the *māyā* of **Vidyujjihva**, and **Rāma is not dead** (*Yuddha* 33.14). This is the hinge: Sītā has been persuaded she is a widow, and Saramā's word alone undoes it.
  - *Name-collision, flagged:* this war-book **Vidyujjihva**, a māyā-adept in Rāvaṇa's service, is not the Kālakeya dānava **Vidyujjihva** of the Uttara 12 marriage-round above — Śūrpaṇakhā's husband, whom Rāvaṇa kills many sargas earlier (*Uttara* 23). Tradition keeps the illusionist and the husband distinct; the shared name invites conflation and the corpus refuses it. Canonical home of the distinction: [Śūrpaṇakhā](./shurpanakha.md). [disputed]
- **The news from the shore** — she then tells Sītā what no one in the grove has told her: **Rāma has crossed the ocean**, reached the southern shore, and is encamped there with the vānara host, guarded on every side (*Yuddha* 33.16); and she describes the army — the rutting elephants, the chariot-horses, the thousands of dart-bearing riders, the shields and mail and the noise of bells and war-trumpets (*Yuddha* 33.23–29).
- **The scene is Saramā's, not Trijaṭā's** — the vulgate assigns the whole false-head consolation to Saramā, at 33 and 34; Trijaṭā's own great scenes are the prophetic dream (*Sundara* 27) and the flight over the corpse-field in the Puṣpaka (*Yuddha* 47–48). Retellings routinely transfer this one to Trijaṭā. See [Trijaṭā](./trijata.md). [disputed]

## The embassy to the council — Yuddha Kāṇḍa 34

- **The offer** — Saramā offers to carry Sītā's word to Rāma herself and return in secret, boasting a speed the **wind-god could not follow** (*Yuddha* 34.3–4).
- **What Sītā asks for instead** — Sītā refuses the message and asks the harder thing: **go and find out what Rāvaṇa has decided** — whether he will give her back or hold her (*Yuddha* 34.7, 11). It is a choice of intelligence over comfort, and it is Sītā's.
- **What she overhears** — Saramā goes to the council and returns with the deliberation entire (*Yuddha* 34.14ff.). **Rāvaṇa's mother Kaikasī** and the **aged counsellor Aviddha** (Avindhya in other printings, and the form under which the *Rāmopākhyāna* develops him into a full protector of Sītā) both urge him to restore Sītā honourably to Rāma, arguing that **Janasthāna alone should have been warning enough**, and that no mortal crosses the ocean and cuts down rākṣasas as this one has (*Yuddha* 34.20–24). Rāvaṇa refuses: he **will not give her up but by dying in battle**.
- **The assurance and the drums** — Saramā closes by telling Sītā that Rāma will kill Rāvaṇa and take her home to Ayodhyā by every means (*Yuddha* 34.26); and while they speak the sound of the vānara host mixed with kettledrums reaches the grove (*Yuddha* 34.27). The siege has begun, and Sītā has been told the truth about it by a rākṣasa king's sister-in-law.
- **She is inside Laṅkā after her husband has left it** — Vibhīṣaṇa defects to Rāma at *Yuddha* 17; Saramā is still in the aśoka grove, and free enough to stand at Rāvaṇa's council door, at *Yuddha* 33–34. The war-book never remarks on it and never says what became of her. [scholarly]

## Where the identification comes from

- **The war-book does not name her husband** — in *Yuddha* 33–34 Saramā is simply one of the women set over Sītā, and Vibhīṣaṇa is not mentioned in either sarga. The identification of this Saramā as **Vibhīṣaṇa's wife** is made by the **commentators** on the war-book and stated outright only in **Uttara Kāṇḍa 12**, a later stratum of the epic. Tradition is unanimous on it; the text arrives at it in two stages, and the file records both. [scholarly]
- **What that leaves unsettled** — everything read into her position (that she risks a defector's wife's exposure, that she is Trijaṭā's mother, that she was left behind deliberately) rests on joining the Uttara's parentage to the war-book's scenes. The joining is traditional and reasonable; it is not Vālmīki stating it in one place. [scholarly]
