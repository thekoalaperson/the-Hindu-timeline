# Kuntī

## Identity & names
- **Pṛthā (पृथा)** - her birth-name in the Yādava house; means "broad/the earth-like," and the patronymic-style epithet **Pārtha** ("son/child of Pṛthā") attaches to her sons, most famously Arjuna (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Kuntī** - the name she takes from her adoptive father **Kuntibhoja**; the matronymic **Kaunteya** ("son of Kuntī") covers Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma, and Arjuna alike (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Mātṛ of five, mother of four** - reckoned mother of five Pāṇḍavas by custom (she raised Mādrī's twins after Mādrī's death) but bore only three of them; Karṇa is her concealed first child, making four sons of her body (*Mahābhārata*).

## Lineage & adoption
- **Daughter of Śūrasena** - born eldest child of the Yādava chief **Śūrasena** of the Vṛṣṇi line; sister (in most tellings) of **Vasudeva**, father of Kṛṣṇa — so Kuntī is Kṛṣṇa's paternal aunt (*Mahābhārata; Bhāgavata Purāṇa*). This kinship grounds Kṛṣṇa's lifelong partisanship for the Pāṇḍavas.
- **Given in adoption to Kuntibhoja** - Śūrasena had pledged his firstborn to his childless cousin/friend **Kuntibhoja**; Pṛthā is handed over and renamed Kuntī, raised in his court and trained in royal duties (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
  - *Variant (later/devotional gloss):* Kuntibhoja is sometimes described as son of Śūrasena's paternal aunt, framing the transfer as keeping the child within an extended kin-circle rather than a stranger's house (*Purāṇic/commentarial*). [late text]

## The Durvāsa mantra
- **Service to Durvāsa** - the volatile sage Durvāsa lodges at Kuntibhoja's court under harsh conditions of hospitality; the young Kuntī attends him with flawless patience for the full duration of his stay (a year, in the common telling) (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **The boon as a counter-mantra** - pleased, Durvāsa grants her an *ātharvaṇa* invocation (often a five-part *mantra*) by which she may summon any deity she names; the god so summoned must appear and beget a child upon her, willing or not (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
  - *Variant (motive of the gift):* some retellings present the boon as a foresighted gift anticipating Pāṇḍu's future inability to father children; others present it simply as reward for service, with the prophetic value emerging only later (*Mahābhārata; popular retellings*). [folk]
  - *Variant (number of invocations):* the mantra is usually treated as repeatable per-deity but limited in total uses — the arithmetic (Karṇa + three Pāṇḍavas + one shared with Mādrī = five uses) is reconstructed from the narrative rather than stated as a fixed cap in one verse. [disputed]

## Sūrya and the secret birth of Karṇa
- **Testing the mantra** - shortly after reaching womanhood, out of curiosity (and, in some readings, disbelief), Kuntī recites the mantra at dawn invoking **Sūrya**, the sun-god (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva; Vana Parva*).
- **Sūrya's compulsion** - bound by the mantra, Sūrya appears; Kuntī, alarmed at the consequence, tries to retract, but the invocation cannot be unsaid. He begets a son and (to preserve her maidenhood) restores her virginity afterward (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva*).
- **Karṇa born with kavaca-kuṇḍala** - the child is born already wearing natural golden armor (*kavaca*) and earrings (*kuṇḍala*) fused to his body — Sūrya's protective gift (*Mahābhārata, Ādi/Vana Parva*).
  - *Variant (birth from the ear → "Karṇa"):* a widespread tradition holds the child issued from Kuntī's ear (*karṇa*), giving his name and explaining her intact virginity (*later/popular tradition; Sūrya Purāṇic gloss*). [folk] The BORI critical text emphasizes Sūrya restoring her maidenhood rather than an ear-birth; the ear-etymology is a secondary explanation. [disputed]
- **Abandonment on the river** - unable to face disgrace as an unwed mother, Kuntī seals the infant in a basket (with the armor) and sets it adrift — on the **Aśvanadī / Carmaṇvatī (Chambal)** in the epic, popularly recalled as the **Gaṅgā** (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva*). He is found and raised by the sūta charioteer **Adhiratha** and his wife **Rādhā**, hence **Rādheya / Sūta-putra**. See [Karṇa](../karna.md).

## Marriage to Pāṇḍu & the svayaṃvara
- **Kuntī's svayaṃvara** - in the standard sequence she chooses **Pāṇḍu**, the Kuru king, by garlanding him at her self-choice ceremony; she becomes his first/senior queen, **Mādrī** of Madra being the second (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Pāṇḍu's curse (Kindama)** - Pāṇḍu kills the sage **Kindama** while he is mating in deer form; the dying sage curses Pāṇḍu to die the instant he approaches a wife in desire — making natural fatherhood impossible (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). This curse is the hinge that activates Kuntī's mantra.

## Mother of the three elder Pāṇḍavas (niyoga by the mantra)
- **Pāṇḍu's request** - barred from begetting heirs, Pāṇḍu (informed of the boon) asks Kuntī to summon gods and bear sons under the sanction of *niyoga* / *āpaddharma* (dharma in extremity) (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira by Dharma** - Kuntī invokes **Dharma** (Yama-Dharmarāja); the eldest, embodiment of righteousness and truth, is born (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Bhīma by Vāyu** - she invokes **Vāyu**, the wind-god; the immensely strong second son is born — making Bhīma a brother of Hanumān (also Vāyu's son) (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Arjuna by Indra** - she invokes **Indra**, king of the devas; the supreme archer is born, the central Pārtha of the [Bhagavad Gītā](../bhagavad-gita.md) (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Sharing the mantra with Mādrī** - at Pāṇḍu's wish Kuntī teaches the invocation (one use) to Mādrī, who cannily summons the twin **Aśvins** and bears **Nakula and Sahadeva** in a single act — quietly outstripping Kuntī's per-deity allotment (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Raising the five** - after Pāṇḍu dies attempting to embrace Mādrī (the curse fulfilled) and Mādrī follows him on the pyre, Kuntī alone returns to Hastināpura and rears all five boys (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).

## Kuntī's dharma-counsel & the years of exile
- **House of lac (Vāraṇāvata)** - Kuntī escapes the burning lacquer-house (*Jatugṛha*) with her sons through Vidura's tunnel; en route Bhīma slays the rākṣasa **Hiḍimba**, and Kuntī sanctions his union with **Hiḍimbā** (mother of Ghaṭotkaca) (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **The unwitting command that makes Draupadī common wife** - at Draupadī's svayaṃvara won by Arjuna, the sons call out "we have brought alms"; Kuntī, unseeing, says "share it among yourselves" — and her word, being a mother's command, becomes binding, the chief scriptural sanction for Draupadī's polyandry (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
  - *Variant (Draupadī's prior karma):* the polyandry is elsewhere justified by Draupadī's boon (asking five times for a husband) and past-life karma, with Kuntī's slip the proximate trigger rather than the cause (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva; later glosses*). [late text]
- **Brāhmaṇa hospitality at Ekacakrā** - in disguise the family shelters with a brāhmaṇa family threatened by the rākṣasa **Baka**; Kuntī volunteers Bhīma to take the household's place and end the demon — a model of guest-dharma and quiet courage (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Message through Kṛṣṇa (Udyoga Parva)** - when Kṛṣṇa's peace-embassy to Hastināpura fails, he asks Kuntī what to tell her sons; she sends a fierce exhortation to fight for their right rather than live on charity (*Mahābhārata, Udyoga Parva*).
- **The Vidulā-ākhyāna** - her chosen message is the inset tale of **Vidulā**, a Kṣatriya mother who shames her despondent son into reclaiming his kingdom — Kuntī uses it to goad Yudhiṣṭhira toward war as a warrior's duty (*Mahābhārata, Udyoga Parva*). Thematically adjacent to the statecraft of [Vidura-nīti](../vidura-niti.md).

## The meeting with Karṇa
- **Kuntī's appeal before the war** - learning war is certain and dreading fratricide, Kuntī goes to **Karṇa** at his riverside worship of Sūrya and reveals the secret: "You are my son, the son of Kuntī, not of Rādhā; Adhiratha is not your father" — and begs him to join the Pāṇḍavas, where as eldest he would be king (*Mahābhārata, Udyoga Parva*).
- **Sūrya's corroboration** - in the epic Sūrya himself speaks to confirm Kuntī's words and urge Karṇa to heed her, yet Karṇa holds to his loyalty (*Mahābhārata, Udyoga Parva*).
- **Karṇa's refusal and his promise** - Karṇa reproaches her for abandoning him and ruining his standing as a Kṣatriya, refuses to betray Duryodhana, but grants two things: he will fight only **Arjuna**, and either way she will still have five living sons after the war — for either he or Arjuna will die (*Mahābhārata, Udyoga Parva*). This vow is why he spares Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma, Nakula, Sahadeva in battle. See [Karṇa](../karna.md).

## Aftermath, revelation & death
- **The post-war disclosure** - after the Pāṇḍavas' victory, during the funeral rites Kuntī (with Kṛṣṇa) reveals that Karṇa was her firstborn and their eldest brother; the brothers, horrified at fratricide, perform his obsequies as for kin (*Mahābhārata, Strī Parva / Śānti Parva*).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's grief and reproach** - grief-stricken Yudhiṣṭhira laments that the secret was kept; in anguish he upbraids his mother for the concealment (*Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva*).
  - *Variant (the "curse on womankind"):* a popular tradition has Yudhiṣṭhira **curse all women** to be henceforth unable to keep a secret. This curse is **absent from the Sanskrit critical text** (neither Strī nor Śānti Parva records it) and is best treated as a later folk accretion. [folk] [disputed]
- **Retirement to the forest** - in old age Kuntī leaves Hastināpura with **Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Gāndhārī, and Vidura** to live as a forest ascetic (*Mahābhārata, Āśramavāsika Parva*).
- **Death in the forest fire** - the elders perish together in a wildfire in the forest, Kuntī among them; Nārada later brings the news to the Pāṇḍavas (*Mahābhārata, Āśramavāsika Parva*).

## Devotional & theological afterlife
- **Kuntī's prayer (Kunti-stuti)** - the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (1.8) gives her famous hymn to Kṛṣṇa as he departs Hastināpura, in which she paradoxically asks for *more* calamities, since adversity keeps her mind fixed on him — a touchstone of bhakti theology (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.8*).
- **Aṃśa / incarnation glosses** - some Purāṇic and Vaiṣṇava traditions read Kuntī as a partial manifestation of the Lord's "success potency" (*siddhi*); such identifications are devotional overlays on the epic figure (*Purāṇic/commentarial*). [late text]

## Roster note
- The exact count and arithmetic of mantra-uses, the river of Karṇa's abandonment, the ear-birth etymology, and the "curse on women" are **points where traditions diverge**; each is flagged above. Treat the BORI critical text as the baseline and popular/Purāṇic layers as cataloged variants, not corrections. [scholarly] [open-ended]
