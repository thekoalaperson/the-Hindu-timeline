## Identity & names
- **Pṛthā (पृथा)** - her birth-name in the Yādava house (*Mahābhārata* 1.104.1). The word is usually connected with *pṛthu*, "broad" (etymology, not stated in the epic). The matronymic **Pārtha** ("son of Pṛthā") belongs to all three of her Pāṇḍava sons, though Arjuna is the one most often called by it; Kuntī also tells Karṇa "you are a Pārtha" (*Mahābhārata* 5.143.3, 5.143.12).
- **Kuntī** - the name she takes from her adoptive father **Kuntibhoja**; the matronymic **Kaunteya** ("son of Kuntī") covers Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma, and Arjuna alike, and Kuntī applies it to Karṇa too: "kaunteyas tvaṃ na rādheyaḥ" (*Mahābhārata* 5.143.2). The epic also calls her **Vārṣṇeyī**, a woman of the Vṛṣṇi clan (5.142.29).
- **Mother of five, of four by birth** - she is the mother of five Pāṇḍavas by custom, because the dying Mādrī handed her twins to Kuntī to raise "as your own sons" (*Mahābhārata* 1.116.28). She bore only three of the five. Karṇa is her hidden first child, which makes four sons of her own body.
- **Divine portion** - in the Ādi Parva's list of the gods and powers who took human birth (*aṃśāvataraṇa*), Kuntī and Mādrī are the goddesses **Siddhi** ("accomplishment") and **Dhṛti** ("steadfastness"), and Gāndhārī is **Mati** ("intelligence") (*Mahābhārata* 1.61.98). See also the devotional glosses below.

## Lineage & adoption
- **Daughter of Śūra** - her father is **Śūra**, "best of the Yadus" and father of **Vasudeva** (*Mahābhārata* 1.104.1; 3.287.23-24, "vasudevasya bhaginī"). This makes Kuntī Kṛṣṇa's paternal aunt, and the kinship underlies Kṛṣṇa's lifelong partisanship for the Pāṇḍavas. Later retellings commonly call the father **Śūrasena**.
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.24.30-31):* the Purāṇa lists Pṛthā as one of Vasudeva's five sisters: Pṛthā, Śrutadevā, Śrutakīrti, Śrutaśravā and Rājādhidevī. It too names her father Śūra, who gave her to his childless friend Kunti (9.24.31).
- **Given in adoption to Kuntibhoja** - Śūra had promised his firstborn child to **Kuntibhoja**, who was childless and was both his friend and the son of Śūra's paternal aunt (*paitṛṣvaseya*). Pṛthā was his eldest-born (*agrajā*), so she was handed over (*Mahābhārata* 1.104.2-3; Kuntibhoja himself recalls the promise at 3.287.24). She is renamed Kuntī and set to honour the household's gods and guests (1.104.4).
  - *Kuntī's own view (Udyoga Parva):* when she meets Kṛṣṇa at Hastināpura she blames her father above anyone: he gave her away as a little girl playing with a ball, "like wealth handed over by gamblers" (*dhanaṃ dhūrtair ivārpitā*). She says she has been wronged by her father and by her in-laws alike (*Mahābhārata* 5.88.61-63).

## The Durvāsa mantra
- **Service to Durvāsa** - the Ādi Parva tells this briefly. In her father's house Kuntī served the fierce brāhmaṇa "whom they know as Durvāsas" and satisfied him with every effort (*Mahābhārata* 1.104.4-5). The Vana Parva tells it at length and does not name the guest there. A tall, bearded ascetic sets terms: he will come and go as he pleases, and no one may offend him over his bed or seat. Kuntibhoja puts his daughter in charge of the guest, warning her that an angered brāhmaṇa could burn up the whole family. The guest arrives at odd hours or not at all and demands food that is hard to get, and Kuntī never fails him. When a full year (*saṃvatsare pūrṇe*) has passed he is satisfied (*Mahābhārata* 3.287-289; the year at 3.289.12).
  - *Text note:* in the Ādi version the "year" appears only in a passage the CE places in its apparatus (1.104.5 *1114). Within the CE text it rests on the Vana account.
- **The boon** - in the Vana version Kuntī first declines a boon. Because she fears his curse, she cannot refuse a second time, and he teaches her a *mantragrāma* (body of mantras) "heard in the Atharvaśiras". Any god she calls with it must come under her power, "willing or unwilling" (*akāmo vā sakāmo vā*) (*Mahābhārata* 3.289.15-20). In the Ādi version she will have a son by whichever god she summons (1.104.6-7). Kuntī repeats the formula to Pāṇḍu at 1.113.34-35.
  - *Motive of the gift:* the Ādi Parva itself says Durvāsas gave the mantra "looking ahead to *āpaddharma*", that is, to a time of distress (*āpaddharmānvavekṣayā*, 1.104.6). The foresight reading is therefore in the text, not only in later retellings. In her late confession Kuntī adds that Durvāsas told her "you will be the mother of Dharma" (15.38.6).
  - *Limit on its use:* the epic gives no fixed count of invocations. The limit it states is a social rule, which Kuntī cites to Pāṇḍu: even in distress the wise do not approve a fourth birth by such means, since a woman who goes further is called *cāriṇī* ("wanton"), and after a fifth *bandhakī* (*Mahābhārata* 1.114.65). Popular retellings that turn this into a "five-use" quota are reconstructions.

## Sūrya and the secret birth of Karṇa
- **Testing the mantra** - out of curiosity (*kautūhalāt*), while still a maiden, Kuntī tries the mantra on **Sūrya** (*Mahābhārata* 1.104.8). In the Vana telling she first wonders what the strength of the mantra is. Her period comes while she is still a maiden; she sees the rising sun and, given divine sight, sees the god in his armour and earrings, and out of curiosity she calls him (3.290.1-7).
- **Sūrya's compulsion** - the Vana account is detailed. Sūrya splits himself by yoga, keeping one form in the sky and coming to her in the other (3.290.8-9). She begs him to leave. He refuses to be summoned in vain, and he threatens to curse her and to burn her father and the brāhmaṇa who gave the mantra (3.290.12-17). He shows her the gods watching from the sky (3.290.18-20). He argues that a maiden (*kanyā*) is free to choose, deriving the word from the root *kan* (glossed "desires all", *sarvān kāmayate*), and he promises she will become a maiden again (3.291.13-16). Kuntī yields on condition that the son is born with his father's armour and with Aditi's nectar-made earrings (3.291.17-21). Sūrya enters her by yoga, touching her navel, "and did not defile her" (*na caivaināṃ dūṣayām āsa*) (3.291.23-28). The Ādi Parva gives the same outline in a few lines: Sūrya begets the child and then restores her maidenhood (1.104.10-12).
- **Karṇa born with kavaca-kuṇḍala** - the child is born already wearing natural armour (*sahajaṃ kavacam*) and earrings (*kuṇḍala*) (*Mahābhārata* 1.104.10-11; 3.292.4-5). He is named **Vasuṣeṇa**, by his foster-parents in the Ādi Parva (1.104.15) and by brāhmaṇas in the Vana Parva (3.293.12-13). The Ādi Parva explains his other name, **Vaikartana**, by his later "cutting off" (*ut-kṛt*) of the armour for Indra (1.104.18-21).
  - *Variant (birth from the ear → "Karṇa"):* a popular modern tradition says the child came out of Kuntī's ear (*karṇa*), which explains both his name and her intact maidenhood. No Sanskrit source for this was found. The CE says she conceived, carried the child in secret and bore it, remaining a maiden by Sūrya's grace (3.292.1-4). The ear-birth appears in 20th-century devotional teaching (for example a 1968 letter of A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda), and Devdutt Pattanaik's *Jaya* reports it for an Indonesian telling. [folk] [regional]
- **Concealment and abandonment** - Kuntī hides the pregnancy, and no woman except her nurse (*dhātreyikā*) knows of it (*Mahābhārata* 3.292.1-3). At night, with the nurse, she seals the newborn in a wax-lined basket and sets it on the river **Aśvanadī** (3.292.6-7, 22), with a long lament blessing him to the gods (3.292.9-21). The basket drifts from the Aśvanadī into the **Carmaṇvatī** (Chambal), then into the **Yamunā** and the **Gaṅgā**, reaching Campā in the Sūta country (3.292.25-26). There **Rādhā**, wife of the sūta **Adhiratha**, sees it floating on the Gaṅgā (Jāhnavī) and the couple adopt the child (3.293.1-11). This is the origin of the names **Rādheya** and **Sūtaputra**. The Ādi Parva says only that she "let him go in the water" (1.104.13). Kuntī learns through a spy where he is growing up (3.293.14). See [Karṇa](../karna.md).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.24.32-36):* she receives from Durvāsas a *devahūti* ("god-summoning") lore and calls the Sun to test it. Sūrya promises to keep her womb undefiled, and the boy is born at once (*sadyaḥ*). Fearing the world, she abandons him in a river.
- **The tournament** - at the princes' display of arms Karṇa challenges Arjuna. Kuntī recognises him and faints. Vidura revives her by sprinkling sandal-water, and she looks on her two armoured sons in anguish, at a loss what to do (*Mahābhārata* 1.126.27-29). Later in the same scene she recognises him by his divine marks and feels a hidden joy (*channā prītiḥ*) when Duryodhana makes him king of Aṅga (1.127.22).

## Marriage to Pāṇḍu & the svayaṃvara
- **Kuntī's svayaṃvara** - Kuntibhoja holds a self-choice ceremony for her, and among thousands of kings she chooses **Pāṇḍu** (*Mahābhārata* 1.105.1-3). Bhīṣma then buys **Mādrī** of Madra as Pāṇḍu's second wife for a large bride-price (1.105.4-5). Kuntī calls herself the senior lawful wife (*jyeṣṭhā dharmapatnī*) at 1.116.23. See [Pāṇḍu](./pandu.md).
- **Pāṇḍu's curse (Kiṃdama)** - while hunting, Pāṇḍu shoots a stag mating with a doe. The stag is the sage **Kiṃdama**, who had taken deer form. Dying, he curses Pāṇḍu to die when he unites with a beloved wife, and foretells that the same wife will follow him in death (*Mahābhārata* 1.109.5-30, the name at 1.109.26). The curse makes natural fatherhood impossible, and so it is what brings Kuntī's mantra into play.

## Mother of the three elder Pāṇḍavas (niyoga by the mantra)
- **Pāṇḍu's request and Kuntī's resistance** - Pāṇḍu, now an ascetic on Śataśṛṅga, argues that a man without offspring wins no heaven, and lists the kinds of son the law recognises, six of whom count as heirs (*Mahābhārata* 1.111.11-31). He cites Śāradaṇḍāyanī, who, appointed by her elders (*gurubhir niyuktā*), bore sons to a brāhmaṇa, and asks Kuntī to conceive by "a brāhmaṇa of superior ascetic power" (1.111.33-36; 1.113.30). Kuntī refuses: he should not speak this way to his devoted wife (1.112.2-3). She counters with the story of **Vyuṣitāśva**, whose widow **Bhadrā** bore sons by his corpse (1.112.7-33). Pāṇḍu replies with the "ancient law" of free unions among women, which **Śvetaketu** son of **Uddālaka** ended (1.113.1-20), and then cites a wife's duty to obey her husband's command (1.113.25-27). Only then does Kuntī reveal Durvāsas's boon and ask which god she should call (1.113.31-38). Pāṇḍu directs each birth: he names Dharma, then asks for a son "first in strength" (Kuntī calls Vāyu), then wins Indra's promise by tapas and tells her to call him (1.113.39-42; 1.114.8-9; 1.114.15-27).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira by Dharma** - she offers *bali* and recites the japa Durvāsas gave her. She unites with Dharma in a yogic form, and the eldest is born at noon on the eighth (*abhijit*) muhūrta. A bodiless voice proclaims him the best of the righteous (*Mahābhārata* 1.114.1-7).
- **Bhīma by Vāyu** - she invokes **Vāyu** (*Mahābhārata* 1.114.9-10). As an infant Bhīma falls from her lap when she starts up in fear of a tiger, and his body shatters the rock he lands on (1.114.11-13). He is born on the same day as Duryodhana (1.114.14). The epic itself makes him Hanumān's brother, both being sons of Vāyu (*Mahābhārata* 3.147.3-4, 3.147.11-13).
- **Arjuna by Indra** - after a year-long vow for Kuntī, with Pāṇḍu standing on one foot in tapas, Indra promises a peerless son (*Mahābhārata* 1.114.19-23). Arjuna is born, the central Pārtha of the [Bhagavad Gītā](../bhagavad-gita.md). Kuntī later recalls the voice at his birth, which promised that he would conquer the Kurus and perform three horse-sacrifices (5.88.64-65; 5.135.1-6).
- **The limit invoked** - Pāṇḍu asks for still more sons. Kuntī refuses, citing the rule against a fourth such birth (*Mahābhārata* 1.114.64-66; see *Limit on its use* above).
- **Sharing the mantra with Mādrī** - Mādrī is too shy to ask her co-wife herself and asks Pāṇḍu to intercede (*Mahābhārata* 1.115.1-8). Kuntī tells Mādrī to "think of a deity once" (*sakṛt*). Mādrī thinks of the twin **Aśvins** and bears **Nakula and Sahadeva** (1.115.15-17). When Pāṇḍu asks again on Mādrī's behalf, Kuntī refuses: "Told to use it once, she got a pair; I have been cheated (*vañcitā*)", and she fears being outdone by her (1.115.22-24). See [Mādrī](./madri.md).
- **Pāṇḍu's death and the pyre dispute** - in spring Pāṇḍu forces himself on Mādrī despite her resistance and dies (*Mahābhārata* 1.116.2-12). Kuntī rebukes Mādrī for failing to guard him (1.116.17-21) and claims, as senior wife, the right to follow him in death (1.116.23-24). Mādrī insists on going herself, asks Kuntī to raise her sons as Kuntī's own, and mounts the pyre (1.116.25-31). The sages then escort Kuntī, the five boys and the remains of Pāṇḍu and Mādrī to Hastināpura (1.117.1-30).

## Kuntī's dharma-counsel & the years of exile
- **House of lac (Vāraṇāvata)** - warned by Vidura, the family escapes through a tunnel dug by a miner Vidura sends (*Mahābhārata* 1.135). On the last night Kuntī gives a feast for brāhmaṇas as a pretext. A Niṣāda woman with five sons comes for food, gets drunk and sleeps in the house. Bhīma sets the fire, and the burnt bodies of the woman and her sons are taken for Kuntī and the Pāṇḍavas (1.136.5-9; 1.137.7). Bhīma carries his mother and brothers to safety (1.137.23).
- **Hiḍimbā** - after Bhīma kills the rākṣasa **Hiḍimba**, his sister **Hiḍimbā** bows to Kuntī and Yudhiṣṭhira and appeals to Kuntī as a woman who knows the pain of desire. She asks to be joined with Kuntī's son (*Mahābhārata* 1.143.4-15). Yudhiṣṭhira sets the terms: Bhīma may be with her by day but must be brought back every night (1.143.16-19). The son born of the union is **Ghaṭotkaca**. See [Hiḍimba & Hiḍimbā](./hidimba-hidimbi.md).
- **Brāhmaṇa hospitality at Ekacakrā** - the family lodges in disguise with a brāhmaṇa household whose turn has come to send a victim to the rākṣasa **Baka** (*Mahābhārata* 1.145-148). Kuntī offers one of her five sons in place of the brāhmaṇa's only son and daughter (1.149.1-3). Yudhiṣṭhira protests that she is giving up her own son for another's (1.150.5-9). Kuntī defends the choice as dharma toward a host and confident in Bhīma's strength (1.150), and Bhīma kills Baka.
- **The unwitting command that makes Draupadī common wife** - Arjuna and Bhīma bring Draupadī home from the svayaṃvara and announce "alms (*bhikṣā*)!" From inside the hut, without looking, Kuntī says, "enjoy it, all of you together" (*bhuṅkteti sametya sarve*). Seeing the bride, she exclaims "what have I said!" and asks Yudhiṣṭhira how her words can be kept from being false without bringing an unheard-of adharma on the princess (*Mahābhārata* 1.182.1-5). Yudhiṣṭhira first tells Arjuna to marry her (1.182.7) and then decides that all five will. Before Drupada and Vyāsa, Kuntī says she is gravely afraid of having spoken an untruth, and Vyāsa assures her she will be freed from it (1.188.17-18). See [Draupadī](./draupadi.md).
  - *The epic's other justifications:* the polyandry has other grounds in the CE itself, not only in later glosses. In a former life Draupadī asked Śiva five times for a husband (Vyāsa to the Pāṇḍavas, *Mahābhārata* 1.157.7-13; repeated to Drupada at 1.189.41-46). The five brothers were former Indras, and she was destined for them (the *Pañcendra-upākhyāna*, 1.189.1-33). Kuntī's slip is the immediate trigger and these stories are the deeper cause; both are in the text.
- **During the forest exile** - when the Pāṇḍavas lose the second dicing and leave for twelve years, Vidura says that the noble Pṛthā should not go to the forest, and she stays in his house (*Mahābhārata* 2.69.5). She laments as Draupadī and her sons depart and is led away by Vidura and others (2.70.1-22).
- **Message through Kṛṣṇa (Udyoga Parva)** - on reaching Hastināpura, Kṛṣṇa calls on Dhṛtarāṣṭra and then visits Kuntī at Vidura's house, where she weeps over her sons (*Mahābhārata* 5.88.1-3). After his peace embassy fails, he asks her what to tell her sons (5.130.1-4). She sends Yudhiṣṭhira a fierce exhortation. She tells him his dharma is wasting away and that a kṣatriya is made to live by the strength of his arms. She cites King Mucukunda, who refused the earth as a gift and ruled what he had won himself (5.130.5-10). To Arjuna and Bhīma she sends the line "the time has come for which a kṣatriya woman bears a son" (*yadarthaṃ kṣatriyā sūte tasya kālo 'yam āgataḥ*, 5.135.9). The twins are told to prize wealth won by valour above life (5.135.13). She praises Draupadī, and says the loss of the kingdom and the exile grieve her less than Draupadī weeping in the assembly hall under abuse (5.135.11-18).
- **The Vidulā-ākhyāna** - at the heart of her message is the inset tale of a kṣatriya mother who shames her son **Saṃjaya**, defeated by the king of Sindhu and lying despondent, into rising to reclaim his kingdom (*Mahābhārata* 5.131-134; the Sindhu king at 5.131.4, the son addressed as Saṃjaya from 5.131.27). The CE spells her name **Vidurā** (5.131.1-3); Ganguli's translation reads "Vidula". Kuntī later says she told it to rouse her sons (15.23.18). The tale is thematically close to the statecraft of [Vidura-nīti](../vidura-niti.md).

## The meeting with Karṇa
- **Why she goes** - Kṛṣṇa has already told Karṇa the secret of his birth (*Mahābhārata* 5.138-141). Vidura comes to Kuntī afraid of the coming kin-slaughter (5.142.1-9). She thinks: "Shame on wealth, for whose sake this slaughter of kin will come", fears Karṇa above all, and resolves to win him over by telling him the truth (5.142.10-25).
- **Kuntī's appeal before the war** - she finds Karṇa on the bank of the Gaṅgā, facing east with arms raised, reciting. She waits behind him in the shade of his upper garment, withering "like a lotus garland" in the sun, until he finishes at the hour when the sun heats his back (*Mahābhārata* 5.142.26-30). He greets her, naming himself "Rādheya, son of Adhiratha". She answers: "You are Kaunteya, not Rādheya; Adhiratha is not your father … you are a Pārtha", and urges him to join his brothers. Karṇa and Arjuna together would be like Balarāma and Kṛṣṇa, and he would shine surrounded by his five brothers (5.143.1-12).
- **Sūrya's corroboration** - Karṇa then hears a voice issue from the sun, speaking like a father: "Pṛthā speaks the truth; do as your mother says." Even so, his mind does not waver (*Mahābhārata* 5.144.1-3).
- **Karṇa's refusal and his promise** - Karṇa accuses her of a great wrong. She cast him off, so that he was denied the rites due to a kṣatriya, and she never acted as a mother until now, when she seeks only her own interest (*Mahābhārata* 5.144.4-8). He will not desert Duryodhana, who has always honoured him (5.144.9-19). But her effort will not be fruitless: he will not kill Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma or the twins even when they are in his power, and he will fight Arjuna alone. "Your five sons will never perish: either without Arjuna and with Karṇa, or with Arjuna when I am slain" (5.144.20-22). Trembling, Kuntī embraces him. She says "fate is stronger" and asks him to remember his pledge of safety for the four, and they part (5.144.23-26). The pledge is why he spares the other four brothers in battle. See [Karṇa](../karna.md).

## Aftermath, revelation & death
- **The post-war disclosure** - at the water-offerings for the dead on the Gaṅgā, Kuntī alone, weeping, tells her sons to offer water to Karṇa too, "your elder brother, born to me from the Sun, with earrings and armour" (*Mahābhārata* 11.27.6-11). Yudhiṣṭhira asks how she could hide him "like fire under a cloth" and says her keeping the secret has destroyed them (11.27.13-20). He makes the offering and sends for Karṇa's women to perform the rites (11.27.21-24).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's grief and reproach** - in the Śānti Parva Yudhiṣṭhira tells Nārada he is tormented as a brother-killer. In the dice hall his anger would fade when he looked at Karṇa's feet, which resembled Kuntī's, though he could never guess why (*Mahābhārata* 12.1.18-42). Nārada tells the story of Karṇa's curses (12.2-5). Kuntī then consoles Yudhiṣṭhira. She tried to make Karṇa know his brothers, and so did Sūrya, who urged him in a dream and again in her presence, but neither could win him over (12.6.3-8).
  - *The curse on womankind (in the CE):* Yudhiṣṭhira tells his mother "I am crushed by your keeping of the secret". Then, in great grief, he **curses all women in every world: "they shall not keep a secret"** (*bhavatyā gūḍhamantratvāt pīḍito 'smi … śaśāpa ca … sarvalokeṣu ca striyaḥ / na guhyaṃ dhārayiṣyanti*, *Mahābhārata* 12.6.10). The curse is in the critical text of the Śānti Parva, not a folk accretion. Popular retellings often move it to the Strī Parva water-rite scene, where the CE does not have it.
- **Retirement to the forest** - after fifteen years of Yudhiṣṭhira's reign (*Mahābhārata* 15.1.6; 15.4.12), Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Gāndhārī leave Hastināpura for the forest, and Kuntī goes with them. She leads the blindfolded Gāndhārī, whose hand rests on her shoulder (*Mahābhārata* 15.21.9). Vidura and Saṃjaya go with them (15.22.4). Yudhiṣṭhira and Bhīma beg her to stay. She answers that she roused them to war to save Pāṇḍu's line and their honour, not to enjoy a kingdom won by her sons. She has already enjoyed royal fortune with her husband, and now seeks her husband's worlds through tapas and service to her parents-in-law (15.22-23; 15.23.1-20).
- **Confession to Vyāsa and the night of the dead** - in the hermitage Kuntī tells Vyāsa, her father-in-law, the whole story of Durvāsas and Sūrya. She confesses that she ignored the son she knew to be hers, and that this burns her (*Mahābhārata* 15.38.1-17). Vyāsa absolves her: she committed no fault, since she regained her maidenhood, and gods can beget by thought, word, sight, touch or union, so human law does not bind them (15.38.19-23). He promises that Kuntī will see Karṇa (15.39.2). That night the fallen warriors rise from the Gaṅgā and meet their kin (15.39-41).
- **Death in the forest fire** - Vidura has died earlier in the forest, passing by yoga into Yudhiṣṭhira's body (*Mahābhārata* 15.33.25-27). Some time later Nārada reports to Yudhiṣṭhira what happened. Kuntī fasted a month at a time and served as Gāndhārī's eyes (15.45.14, 17). A wind-driven forest fire caught the elders near the Gaṅgā, too weak from fasting to flee. Dhṛtarāṣṭra sent Saṃjaya away and sat facing east in yoga with Gāndhārī and Kuntī, and the three perished; Saṃjaya escaped and went to the Himālaya (15.45.18-34). Nārada adds that the fire came from Dhṛtarāṣṭra's own sacred fires, which his priests had abandoned in the forest, so he was not burned "by a common fire" (15.47.1-7). Yudhiṣṭhira weeps for his mother (15.45.41-42).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa):* in the Purāṇa's version Dhṛtarāṣṭra leaves with Vidura, and only Gāndhārī follows him (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.13.29-30). Nārada foretells that Dhṛtarāṣṭra's body will burn and Gāndhārī will enter the fire (1.13.57-58). Kuntī is not with them. She dies later in Hastināpura: on hearing from Arjuna of the destruction of the Yādavas and Kṛṣṇa's departure, Pṛthā "fixed her self on the Lord with single-minded devotion and withdrew from worldly existence" (1.15.33). The two endings are recorded side by side, not reconciled.

## Devotional & theological afterlife
- **Kuntī's prayer (Kuntī-stuti)** - as Kṛṣṇa is about to leave Hastināpura after the war, the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* gives Kuntī a hymn of twenty-six verses (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.8.18-43). In its best-known verse she asks that calamities come to her again and again, since in them she sees Kṛṣṇa, and seeing him ends rebirth (*vipadaḥ santu tāḥ śaśvat*, 1.8.25). The hymn is a touchstone of bhakti theology. Kṛṣṇa smiles, assents and re-enters Hastināpura, where Yudhiṣṭhira lovingly holds him back from leaving (1.8.44-45).
- **Lament to Akrūra** - in Book 10 of the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*, when Akrūra visits Hastināpura, Pṛthā asks after her parents and brothers. She calls herself a doe among wolves and takes refuge in Kṛṣṇa in a short prayer (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.49.7-14).
- **Aṃśa / incarnation glosses** - the epic's own identification of Kuntī with the goddess **Siddhi** (*Mahābhārata* 1.61.98; see *Divine portion* above) is in the CE. Later Vaiṣṇava devotional commentary reads this identification theologically. That reading is an overlay on the epic figure (*commentarial*; no specific commentary verified). [late text]

## Roster note
- Traditions diverge on the father's name (Śūra in the CE, Śūrasena in later tellings), on the ear-birth story (no Sanskrit source located), and on Kuntī's end: the Mahābhārata has her die in the forest fire, the Bhāgavata has her pass away on hearing of Kṛṣṇa's departure. Each is flagged above. Points sometimes treated as late or folk are in the CE text itself: Kuntibhoja as Śūra's cousin, the boon given in foresight of distress, the four-birth limit, Draupadī's prior-life boon, the Siddhi incarnation, and Yudhiṣṭhira's curse on women. Treat the BORI critical text as the baseline and the Purāṇic and popular layers as cataloged variants, not corrections. [scholarly] [open-ended]
