# Svayamprabhā & Hemā — the Ṛkṣabila Cave and the Lost Month

> The two women of the **Ṛkṣabila**, the jewelled subterranean world in the Vindhyas that swallows a month of the southern search in the [Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md). **Hemā** is the **apsaras** for whom **[Maya](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/maya-danava.md)** the dānava built it and over whom Indra killed him; **Svayamprabhā**, daughter of **Merusāvarṇi** and Hemā's dearest friend, is the ascetic left to keep it — who feeds the starving vānaras and then, because no one who enters may leave alive, sets them outside by her own power.
> Sits in **[Tretā-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md). Parent: [Characters](README.md) · Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md)
> **Two things the retellings routinely reverse.** **Hemā is not "the divine architect's daughter"** — Maya *is* the architect, and she is the apsaras he built the cave for. And the **Ṛkṣabila is not the cave of the Māyāvī pursuit** at Kiṣkindhā where Vālī shut Sugrīva out behind a boulder; that one is at Kiṣkindhā and starts the brothers' feud, this one is hundreds of yojanas away in the Vindhyas. See [Kiṣkindhā — Hampi & Anegundi](../../06-sacred-geography/kishkindha-hampi-anegundi.md).

**Svayamprabhā** (*Svayamprabhā*, "self-luminous") and **Hemā** (*Hemā*, "the golden one") own between them the strangest interlude in the Rāmāyaṇa: an underground paradise of golden mansions, orchards and inexhaustible food, kept by a single woman in black deerskin, into which the search-party for **Sītā** blunders while dying of thirst and out of which it emerges to find its deadline gone and its lives forfeit. Neither woman is a combatant, a queen or a devotee; both are custodians of somebody else's ruined household. What they contribute to the plot is precise and irreversible — the cave costs the vānaras the month Sugrīva gave them, and the loss of that month is exactly what strands them on the beach where the vulture **Sampāti** finds them and names Laṅkā.

## I. Whose cave it is (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 51)

- **Maya built it** - The dānava **Maya**, architect of the asuras, practised ascesis in the forest for a thousand years and was granted by **Brahmā** the wealth of **Śukra** and mastery of craft; with that mastery he raised this golden mansion and its magical woodland, and lived there at his ease. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 51.12–13**.) See [Maya Dānava](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/maya-danava.md).
- **Hemā, and why Indra killed him** - Maya took as his beloved the **apsaras Hemā**, a virtuoso of dance and music. For that union **Indra struck him down with the vajra**; the commentarial tradition reads the apsaras as the bait by which the architect who had usurped Śukra's knowledge was drawn out and destroyed. **Brahmā then bestowed the woodland and the golden mansion on Hemā** in compensation. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 51.14–15**.)
  - *Note (not the architect's daughter):* Hemā is **not Maya's daughter** and not the "architect's daughter." She is the apsaras the architect built for. The daughter is **[Mandodarī](./mandodari.md)** — Maya's child by Hemā, later chief queen of Rāvaṇa (*Uttara-kāṇḍa 12*), which makes this cave the childhood house of Laṅkā's queen and Maya's sons **Māyāvī** and **Dundubhi** the brothers-in-law of Rāvaṇa and the two great victims of Vālī. The epic never remarks the linkage and the retellings never notice it. See [Sugrīva & Vālī](./sugriva-and-vali.md).
- **Svayamprabhā, the keeper** - She identifies herself to the vānaras in her own words: *"I am the daughter of Merusāvarṇi, named Svayamprabhā… Hemā is a virtuoso in dance and music and my dearest friend, and she bestowed a boon upon me."* Hemā, withdrawn to **Brahmā's world** after Maya's death, left the mansion in her friend's keeping. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 51.16–17**.)
  - *Note (friend, not daughter):* Svayamprabhā is **Merusāvarṇi's** daughter and **Hemā's friend** — a distinction the trail-literature and several rosters collapse. Merusāvarṇi is the same guardian sage the western search-party is told to look for at the sunset-Meru (Kiṣkindhā 42). [scholarly]
  - *Name-collision note:* a **Svayamprabhā** also appears among the apsaras rosters of the Mahābhārata (Ādi Parva); the cave-ascetic of the Rāmāyaṇa is a different figure of the same name. See [Apsarās](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/apsaras.md). [scholarly]

## II. The Ṛkṣabila (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 50–51)

- **How they find it** - The southern party under **[Aṅgada](./angada.md)** — with **Hanumān**, **[Jāmbavān](./jambavan.md)**, Nīla and the rest — is tormented by thirst in the Vindhyas when it notices water-birds, wet-winged, flying out of a hole in the rock, and follows them in hoping for water. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 50**.)
- **What is inside** - A vast cavern of golden mansions, jewelled halls, flowering orchards and inexhaustible food and drink — an entire built world underground, empty of anyone but a single **ascetic woman clad in black deerskin**, absorbed in austerity. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 50–51**.)
- **Not the other cave** - The cavern into which **Māyāvī** flees, and at whose mouth Vālī rolls the boulder that seals Sugrīva out and begins the feud, is at **Kiṣkindhā** and belongs to sargas 9–11. The Ṛkṣabila is a different place in a different range, reached only at 50. Conflating them is the single commonest error in the Kiṣkindhā material. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 9–11 vs. 50–52.)

## III. The month lost, and the deliverance (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 52–53)

- **Hanumān tells her everything** - Questioned, **Hanumān** gives her the whole story: Rāma, Lakṣmaṇa and Sītā in the Daṇḍaka; Rāvaṇa's theft from Janasthāna; Sugrīva's dispatch of the parties; the exhausted search of the south; the thirst that brought them into her cavern. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 52**.)
- **A month, not a year** - The time the cave costs them is the **month Sugrīva allowed**, and Vālmīki does not number the days spent inside: Aṅgada says only that *"the month fixed by him is zeroed out while we were in the Black Cavity"* and that the term set for the vānaras passed while they searched the mountains. The commentators reckon the total overstay at about two months. Retellings that make it **a year in the cave** are elaborating; the text gives a lapsed month. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 52; 53.2, 53.8**.)
- **No one leaves alive** - Svayamprabhā feeds the famished host from the cave's own abundance — and then tells them the condition of the place: whoever enters the Ṛkṣabila does not come out of it living. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 52**.)
- **Eyes shut** - She exempts them by her ascetic power. *"All of you best of vānaras shall close your eyelids, for it is indeed impossible to attempt an exit with unclosed eyes."* They cover their faces with their hands, and she carries the whole party out through the rock and sets them down **barely within a minute** — on the **southern shore**, with the deadline spent and death for failure waiting at home. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 52**.)

## IV. What the cave does to the plot

- **It puts them on the beach** - The deliverance lands the party exactly where **Sampāti** is, and Sampāti is the only being alive who can tell them Sītā is a hundred yojanas out to sea in Laṅkā. The cave that looks like a detour is the mechanism that finds her. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 52–58.) See [Jaṭāyu & Sampāti](./jatayu-and-sampati.md).
- **It creates the fast** - The lost month is what makes return to Sugrīva a death sentence, which is why Aṅgada proposes the **prāyopaveśa**, the fast unto death, on the shore. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 53, 55.)
- **It is proposed as a fortress** - In the statecraft duel that follows, the **male** chief **Tāra** — Sugrīva's minister, not [Tārā](./tara-vanara-queen.md) the queen — urges the party to secede and hole up **in Svayamprabhā's cave**, where Sugrīva cannot reach them, with Aṅgada as king there. Hanumān's *daṇḍa* argument closes it off: **Lakṣmaṇa alone** could break open any cave they hid in. The Ṛkṣabila is thus the only place in the epic seriously proposed as a refuge from Rāma's alliance. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 54**.) See [Aṅgada](./angada.md).

## V. Reception and geography

- **The trail folds the two caves into one** - The **Anegundi/Hampi** Rāmāyaṇa trail shows a single cave and attaches both stories to it, so that the Ṛkṣabila is pointed out at Kiṣkindhā itself. It is a sthala-tradition, not the text's geography. [regional] See [Kiṣkindhā — Hampi & Anegundi](../../06-sacred-geography/kishkindha-hampi-anegundi.md).
- **The ascetic in the ruined pleasure-house** - Vālmīki's staging is deliberate and unsentimental: a woman in deerskin doing austerities in the middle of a dead dānava's palace of pleasures, feeding strangers out of a wealth that killed its owner. She asks nothing, converts no one, and is never mentioned again. [scholarly]
- **Hemā's afterlife in the epic** - Hemā's own line runs on without her: through Mandodarī into the house of Laṅkā, and through Māyāvī and Dundubhi into the Vālī–Sugrīva feud that opens the kāṇḍa in which her cave appears. (Vālmīki, *Uttara-kāṇḍa* 12; *Kiṣkindhā* 9–11.)
  - *Variant (Mandodarī's mother):* regional tellings substitute a frog-maiden **Madhurā** for Hemā as Mandodarī's mother. [folk] [regional] See [Mandodarī](./mandodari.md).
