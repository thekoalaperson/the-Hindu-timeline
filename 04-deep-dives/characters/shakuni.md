# Śakuni — the Master of the Loaded Dice

> Where this sits: a **Dvāpara-era life** at the Dvāpara–Kali junction — the Gandhāra prince whose rigged dice-game topples the Pāṇḍavas and lights the fuse of [Kurukṣetra](../mahabharata/README.md). Up: [Epic Characters](README.md) · Parent epic: [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/README.md) · His scheme-book: [02 — Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md) · His death-book: [09 — Śalya Parva](../mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md). Back to the [master index](../../README.md).

**Śakuni** (Sanskrit *Śakuni*, "bird / [bird of] omen," patronymically **Saubala**, "son of Subala") is a prince — and in later life king — of **Gandhāra** (the north-west realm around modern Kandahar), son of King **Subala**, and the elder brother of **[Gāndhārī](gandhari.md)**, queen of the blind Kuru king Dhṛtarāṣṭra. He is therefore the **maternal uncle (mātula)** of the hundred Kauravas, and the constant counsellor, gambling-proxy, and goad of his nephew **[Duryodhana](duryodhana.md)**. The Mahābhārata gives him a single defining gift — an uncanny, dishonest mastery of dice — and makes that gift the hinge of the entire epic: it is Śakuni's loaded play in the [Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md) that strips Yudhiṣṭhira of kingdom, brothers, self, and wife, converting a dynastic rivalry into an unhealable blood-feud. The text casts him as the war's chief human architect after Duryodhana himself, and theologically as an incarnation of **Dvāpara**, the very age-spirit of the dice.

---

## I. Cosmic identity & lineage (Mbh Ādi Parva, Aṃśāvataraṇa)

- **An aṃśa of Dvāpara** — the **Aṃśāvataraṇa** roll-call (Ādi Parva ~1.61) lists Śakuni as **born of a portion of Dvāpara**, the personified spirit of the Dvāpara age and of the gambling-die — a fitting cosmic identity for the man whose dice end the age (Mbh Ādi Parva). [scriptural]
- **Son of Subala, brother of Gāndhārī** — prince of the **Saubala/Gāndhāra** house; his sister's marriage into the Kuru line makes him uncle to the Kauravas and ties his fortunes to Hāstinapura (Mbh Ādi Parva).
- **Sons of Śakuni** — he has sons of his own, chief among them **Ulūka**, his messenger and lieutenant in the war (Mbh Udyoga/Śalya Parvas).

## II. Variant backstory — the Gandhāra grievance (late/folk)

- **The critical text gives no grudge** — in the **BORI Critical Edition** Śakuni is simply Duryodhana's scheming, dice-skilled uncle; the epic supplies **no origin-story of revenge**, and his malice is treated as of a piece with his nephew's. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (the imprisoned Subalas — late/folk):* a widely-told regional and televised backstory holds that **Bhīṣma**, having forced the marriage of the blind Dhṛtarāṣṭra to Gāndhārī, drew the wrath of the Gandhāra house; in retaliation (or on a later pretext) the Kurus **imprison Subala and his hundred sons and starve them**, feeding the family only a single handful of rice a day. The family resolves to keep **one son alive to avenge them**, and pool their rations into Śakuni, the cleverest. As they die one by one, the dying Subala asks that Śakuni's **anklebones be fashioned into dice** after his death, so that the bones of the murdered family will forever obey Śakuni's will — explaining the magically loaded dice. Śakuni emerges as the sole survivor, vowing to **destroy the Kuru line from within** by befriending and ruining it through Duryodhana. Absent from the critical text. [folk] [late text] [disputed]
  - *Variant (the grudge over Gāndhārī's blind husband):* a milder version (also told of Gāndhārī's own page) has Śakuni merely **resent that his sister was married off to a congenitally blind king** without disclosure, nursing this as a lifelong motive for his intrigues — again a retelling-layer motive, not fixed in the critical recension. [folk] [late text]
  - *Note:* these backstories are theologically redundant with the epic's own **Dvāpara-aṃśa** frame; scholars read them as later narrative attempts to humanize and motivate a figure the critical text leaves flatly villainous. [scholarly]

## III. The lacquer-house plot (Mbh Ādi Parva ~141–151)

- **Co-architect of the Lākṣāgṛha** — with **[Duryodhana](duryodhana.md)** and the agent **Purocana**, Śakuni helps devise the **house of lac, resin, and ghee** at **Vāraṇāvata**, built to burn the Pāṇḍavas and Kuntī alive in their sleep; the plot is foiled by Vidura's coded warning and the tunnel-escape (Mbh Ādi Parva). This is the first of the great Kaurava conspiracies in which Śakuni's hand is shown.

## IV. The dice-game (Dyūta) — Śakuni's masterpiece (Mbh Sabhā Parva ~58–65)

- **The plan after the Rājasūya** — when Duryodhana returns from Yudhiṣṭhira's **Rājasūya** consumed by envy (and humiliated by his fall in Maya's illusory palace), it is **Śakuni who proposes the dice-game** as the means to win by fraud what cannot be won by arms, offering to play in Duryodhana's stead because *"I know the secret of the dice"* (Mbh Sabhā Parva). [scriptural]
- **Persuading Dhṛtarāṣṭra** — Śakuni and Duryodhana work on the blind king until he sanctions inviting Yudhiṣṭhira to a "friendly" match at Hāstinapura; Vidura's protests are overruled (Mbh Sabhā Parva ~51–58).
- **The rigged play** — Yudhiṣṭhira, bound by kṣatriya custom not to refuse a challenge, gambles against **Śakuni as Duryodhana's proxy**; Śakuni's dice **never fail**, and Yudhiṣṭhira loses in sequence his wealth, his kingdom, his brothers, himself, and finally **Draupadī** — staked and lost while she is in menstrual seclusion (Mbh Sabhā Parva ~58–65). Śakuni's taunting refrain — *"jitam"* ("won!") — punctuates each loss.
- **The disrobing** — Śakuni's victory directly triggers **Draupadī's vastraharaṇa**: Duryodhana orders Duḥśāsana to drag and strip her, and bares his thigh — the outrage that seeds the war-vows. (For the disrobing proper and the cloth-miracle variants, see the [Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md) and [Draupadī's](draupadi.md) treatments.)
- **The second game and the exile-stake** — after Dhṛtarāṣṭra's boons free the Pāṇḍavas, Śakuni and Duryodhana force a **second match** whose single stake is **thirteen years' exile** (twelve forest + one incognito); again Śakuni wins, and the Pāṇḍavas depart (Mbh Sabhā Parva ~66–72). Bhīma here vows to one day kill Duḥśāsana and Duryodhana; **Sahadeva vows to kill Śakuni** — the vow that fixes his slayer.

## V. The relentless goad (Mbh Udyoga Parva)

- **Against every peace** — through the embassy of [Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md) and the elders' pleas, Śakuni stands with Duryodhana, Karṇa, and Duḥśāsana in the war-party, **encouraging the refusal of even five villages** and hardening his nephew against reconciliation (Mbh Udyoga Parva).
- **The plot to seize Kṛṣṇa** — Śakuni is named among those who plot to **arrest Kṛṣṇa** in open court during the peace mission, the scheme that ends with Kṛṣṇa's Viśvarūpa and the collapse of peace (Mbh Udyoga Parva ~129–130).
- **Ulūka's insulting embassy** — on the eve of battle Śakuni's son **Ulūka** carries Duryodhana's deliberately provocative message to the Pāṇḍava camp, stoking both sides to war (Mbh Udyoga Parva).

## VI. The war and his death (Mbh Bhīṣma → Śalya Parvas)

- **A commander of the Gandhāra contingent** — Śakuni leads his Gandhāra cavalry through the eighteen days, fighting in the major engagements and continuing as Duryodhana's closest strategist (Mbh Bhīṣma–Karṇa Parvas).
- **Death at Sahadeva's hands** — on **Day 18**, in the **Śalya-vadha sub-parva**, **Sahadeva** confronts Śakuni Saubala, the dice-master and architect of the war; after an arrow-duel Sahadeva **severs Śakuni's head** (some passages: cleaves his body), fulfilling his own dice-hall vow (Mbh [Śalya Parva](../mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md), Śalya-vadha P. ~27). [scriptural]
  - *Variant (agent):* the slaying of Śakuni is **universally Sahadeva's** across recensions — one of the few epic vows whose assigned slayer never wavers.
  - *Detail:* his son **Ulūka** is slain in the same engagement, also by Sahadeva (Mbh Śalya Parva).

---

## VII. Character & themes (epic & scholarly)

- **The archetypal schemer** — Śakuni is Indian literature's enduring emblem of the **treacherous gambler and poisonous counsellor**; unlike the ambivalently-drawn [Duryodhana](duryodhana.md), the critical text grants him little nobility, making him the war's purest agent of *adharma*. [scholarly]
- **Dvāpara incarnate** — his aṃśa-of-Dvāpara identity makes him a **structural instrument** of the age-transition: the dice that end the Dvāpara are wielded by Dvāpara's own embodiment, so that the fall is fated as much as plotted.
- **The grievance debate** — the popular revenge-backstory (imprisoned Subalas, bone-dice) supplies a motive the epic withholds; whether to read Śakuni as a wronged avenger or a motiveless schemer is the central interpretive fork in his reception. [folk] [disputed]
- **Cross-links** — see his nephew and instrument [Duryodhana](duryodhana.md); his sister [Gāndhārī](gandhari.md); the dice-game in the [Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md); and his death in the [Śalya Parva](../mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md).
