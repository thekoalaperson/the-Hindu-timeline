# Subāhu — Tāṭakā's Son, Demon at Viśvāmitra's Sacrifice

> The **rākṣasa son of [Tāṭakā](./tataka.md)** and brother of [Mārīca](./maricha.md) who, with his brother, **rains flesh and blood on [Viśvāmitra](../vishwamitra-vasishtha.md)'s Siddhāśrama yajña** in the [Bāla Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md) — and is burned dead by [Rāma](../ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md)'s **Āgneyāstra** while Mārīca is merely flung a hundred yojanas by the Mānavāstra. The **second being Rāma kills**, he completes the canonical *Tāṭakā → astra-gift → Mārīca-Subāhu* apprenticeship trio.
> Sits in **[Tretā-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md) (*itihāsa*-time); academic dating of the Rāmāyaṇa differs sharply — two clocks throughout. [scholarly]
> Up: [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md) · Mother: [Tāṭakā](./tataka.md) · Brother: [Mārīca](./maricha.md).

**Subāhu** (Sanskrit *Subāhu*, "of beautiful/strong arms") is a **rākṣasa**, son of the yakṣiṇī-turned-rākṣasī **[Tāṭakā](./tataka.md)** by her husband **Sunda**, and younger pairing of his brother **[Mārīca](./maricha.md)**. Unlike his brother, Subāhu has no afterlife in the epic: he exists almost entirely as **one of the two demons who defile Viśvāmitra's sacrifice** and as the **target of Rāma's fire-weapon**. His death is brief but structurally pointed — where Mārīca is spared (and returns as the golden deer of the Araṇya Kāṇḍa), Subāhu is killed outright, making him **Rāma's second slaying** after Tāṭakā and the culminating "application" of Rāma's new weapon-craft. [scriptural]

---

## I. Parentage and the line of Tāṭakā–Sunda (Rām. Bāla 25)

- **Son of Tāṭakā** - Subāhu is born of the yakṣiṇī **[Tāṭakā](./tataka.md)** (Tāḍakā), daughter of the yakṣa **Suketu**, who was cursed by sage **[Agastya](../agastya.md)** into a man-eating rākṣasī (Vālmīki, Bāla 25). Through his mother he is of **yakṣa stock**, not of Rāvaṇa's Paulastya line.
- **Father Sunda** - His father is the yakṣa (in some readings asura) **Sunda**, son of Jharjha, destroyed by Agastya's power after he troubled the ṛṣi — the death that maddens Tāṭakā and triggers the curse (Vālmīki, Bāla 25).
- **Brother of Mārīca** - Subāhu and **[Mārīca](./maricha.md)** are named together as Tāṭakā's sons; the two are always paired as the demons who pollute Viśvāmitra's yajña (Vālmīki, Bāla 25–26). Mārīca turns rākṣasa by Agastya's curse alongside his mother; Subāhu shares the demon-nature of the line.
  - *Variant (catalog kinship):* later demon-catalogs and regional tellings loosely fold Subāhu and Mārīca among **vassals/kin of the Janasthāna rākṣasas** drawn into Rāvaṇa's orbit; Vālmīki keeps them simply as Tāṭakā's sons. [folk]

---

## II. The defiling of Siddhāśrama (Rām. Bāla 19–20, 29)

- **Viśvāmitra's vow** - [Viśvāmitra](../vishwamitra-vasishtha.md) seeks the boy-princes **Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa** from Daśaratha precisely because two rākṣasas — **Mārīca and Subāhu**, servants of **Rāvaṇa** — repeatedly wreck the sage's sacrifices, and as one bound by ascetic vow he may not curse them mid-rite (Vālmīki, Bāla 19–20).
- **The threatened yajña at Siddhāśrama** - At Viśvāmitra's hermitage **Siddhāśrama** (the "perfected āśrama," anciently Viṣṇu's Vāmana-tapas site), the sage consecrates a six-day/six-night sacrificial session (*dīkṣā*) and posts the brothers as guards (Vālmīki, Bāla 29).
- **Raining flesh and blood** - At the climax of the rite the demons swoop from the sky; **Mārīca and Subāhu pour streams of blood and gushes of flesh upon the altar**, defiling the consecrated fire and ground — the standard rākṣasa sabotage of brahmanical sacrifice (Vālmīki, Bāla 29–30). This pollution is what Rāma is stationed to repel.

## III. The slaying — Rāma's second kill by the Āgneyāstra (Rām. Bāla 30)

- **The demons descend** - As the altar is fouled, **Rāma sees Mārīca and Subāhu rushing down** with their host and resolves to act without killing the sacrifice-guarding sages or breaking the rite (Vālmīki, Bāla 30).
- **Mārīca hurled, not killed** - Rāma first looses the **Mānavāstra** ("weapon of Manu") at **[Mārīca](./maricha.md)**, which strikes him without killing and **flings him a full hundred yojanas into the ocean** — Vālmīki dwells on the distance to mark Rāma's restraint and mercy (Vālmīki, Bāla 30). [scriptural]
- **Subāhu burned dead** - Rāma then takes up the **Āgneyāstra** (the fire-weapon of Agni) and looses it at **Subāhu**, who is **struck in the chest and falls dead** to the earth — killed outright, with no reprieve (Vālmīki, Bāla 30). The contrast is deliberate: **Mārīca spared and flung away alive, Subāhu slain.**
- **The remaining host scattered** - Rāma then dispatches the lesser **night-rangers (rākṣasa rabble)** with the **Vāyavyāstra** (wind-weapon), clearing the sky so the sacrifice may be completed unpolluted; Viśvāmitra's rite reaches its successful end (Vālmīki, Bāla 30).
  - *Variant (which weapon kills Subāhu):* the standard Vālmīki text names the **Āgneya** for Subāhu and the **Mānava** for Mārīca; a few retellings blur or swap the weapon-names, but the killed-vs-spared structure is constant. [disputed]
  - *Variant (number of demons):* some tellings have only the two brothers; others add a swarm of attending rākṣasas slain with the wind-weapon, as in Vālmīki. [regional]

## IV. The completed apprenticeship trio

- **Second being Rāma kills** - Subāhu is **Rāma's second slaying**, after his mother **[Tāṭakā](./tataka.md)** (the first). The two killings frame Rāma's debut: a woman slain reluctantly on dharma's authority, then a male demon slain in defence of sacrifice. [scriptural]
- **The structural trio** - The episode completes a deliberately built sequence — a **test** (Tāṭakā's slaying), an **empowerment** (Viśvāmitra's gift of the full astra-arsenal with their *saṃhāra*/recall mantras, Bāla 27–28), and an **application** (the defence of Siddhāśrama against Mārīca and Subāhu). Subāhu's death is where the gifted **Āgneyāstra is first used in earnest** — Rāma's military and moral apprenticeship in miniature. See [Bāla Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md).
- **Foil to his brother** - Subāhu's narrative function is to *die* so that Mārīca may *live*: the surviving brother becomes the epic's living witness to Rāma's power and, later, the golden deer of [Mārīca](./maricha.md)'s tragic arc. Subāhu is the sacrificed half of the pair, narratively closed at Bāla 30. [scholarly]

## V. Purāṇic and later retellings

- **Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa** - The devotional **Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa** (Bāla Kāṇḍa) keeps the episode but tilts it toward bhakti: the demons' defilement and Subāhu's death by Rāma's weapon are folded into the Lord's *līlā* of clearing the earth, with the killing read as grace. See [Adhyātma & variant Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md). [late text]
- **Rāmcaritmānas** - Tulsīdās' **Rāmcaritmānas** (Bāla Kāṇḍa) tells the Siddhāśrama defence briefly: Rāma **burns Subāhu with a fiery shaft and flings Mārīca far** with another, the act framed as the divine child beginning his demon-clearing work rather than as a martial test. [late text]
  - *Variant (death-as-liberation):* consistent with bhakti theology, devotional strands count Subāhu — slain by the Supreme — among the demons **liberated by death at Rāma's hand**, a redemptive reading paralleling the Tāṭakā and Mārīca cases. [folk/devotional]
- **Purāṇic summaries** - Purāṇic and *Rāmopākhyāna*-type compressions of the Rāma story (e.g. in the Mahābhārata's [Rāmopākhyāna](../ramopakhyana.md) and Purāṇic Rāma-carita sections) typically **abbreviate or omit Subāhu by name**, mentioning only that Rāma guarded Viśvāmitra's sacrifice and destroyed the demons — Subāhu survives chiefly in the full Vālmīki and Vālmīki-derived recensions. [scholarly]

---

## VI. Significance and motifs

- **The expendable demon** - Subāhu embodies the **sacrificed pair-member**: a figure whose entire purpose is to fall, throwing his spared brother's later prominence into relief. He has no speeches, no character study, no afterlife — a pure plot-function antagonist. [scholarly]
- **Sacrifice-defilement topos** - The flesh-and-blood rain on the altar is the archetypal **rākṣasa assault on Vedic ritual** — the demon as anti-sacrificer, the inversion of brahmanical order that the kṣatriya-avatāra is summoned to defend. [scholarly]
- **Mercy and lethality paired** - In a single moment Rāma displays both restraint (Mārīca flung, not killed) and lethal resolve (Subāhu burned dead) — the avatāra's discrimination, sparing where mercy serves and striking where it does not. [scriptural]
