# Drupada — the Pāñcāla King Whose Vengeance Lit the War-Altar

> Where this sits: the **Dvāpara-era life** of **Drupada** (Yajñasena), king of Pāñcāla, the founding antagonist whose feud with [Droṇa](./drona.md) drives him to a fire-sacrifice that yields the very children destined to undo the Kuru world. He belongs to the [epic-character deep-dives](README.md) within the [04-deep-dives](../README.md) layer, and to the [28th Mahāyuga, Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md), at the cusp of the [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).
> Up: [Characters index](README.md) · Parent epic: [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/) · Origin-book: [01 — Ādi Parva](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md) · Death-book: [07 — Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md) · master [index](../../README.md)

**Drupada** (Sanskrit *Drupada*, "firm-footed / pillar," also titled **Yajñasena**, "he whose army is sacrifice," and **Pārṣata**, son of **Pṛṣata**) is the king of **Pāñcāla** (north-central India, capital variously **Kāmpilya** / **Ahicchatra**) and the structural hinge of the Mahābhārata's machinery of doom. His boyhood friendship with the brahmin [Droṇa](./drona.md), his haughty repudiation of that friendship once crowned, and the half-kingdom humiliation Droṇa exacts in return, drive him to a *putrakāmeṣṭi* (son-desiring) sacrifice that produces **[Dhṛṣṭadyumna](./dhrishtadyumna.md)** — born to kill Droṇa — and **[Draupadī](./draupadi.md)** — born to marry the Pāṇḍavas. Through his transgender son **[Śikhaṇḍin](./amba-shikhandi.md)** he also supplies the instrument of Bhīṣma's fall. Few figures so concentrate the epic's logic that private grievance, pursued through ritual, ripens into world-catastrophe.

---

## I. Lineage and kingship (before the Pāṇḍava generation; Mbh Ādi Parva)

- **Son of Pṛṣata** — Drupada is the son of King **Pṛṣata** of the **Pāñcāla** country, of the Bharata-descended Sṛñjaya/North-Pāñcāla line; hence his patronymic **Pārṣata** (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~128–129*).
- **The throne-name Yajñasena** — He is widely called **Yajñasena**; his children are accordingly styled **Yājñaseni** (Draupadī) and the like (*Mbh, Ādi Parva*).
- **A great kṣatriya king** — Drupada rules a powerful Pāñcāla realm and is reckoned among the foremost monarchs of the age, a fact that sharpens the coming clash with a penniless brahmin (*Mbh, Ādi Parva*).

## II. Boyhood with Droṇa and the broken vow (Mbh Ādi Parva ~129)

- **Schooled together at Bharadvāja's āśrama** — As a boy Drupada is reared and educated alongside **[Droṇa](./drona.md)**, son of the sage **Bharadvāja**, in whose hermitage the two become inseparable friends (*Mbh, Ādi Parva ~129*).
- **The boyish promise** — In the affection of equals-in-childhood, Drupada vows that when he becomes king he will **share half his kingdom** with Droṇa — a promise made innocently, in the manner boys make oaths (*Mbh, Ādi Parva ~129*).
- **The crown changes the man** — Drupada succeeds Pṛṣata and grows into a proud, prosperous king; the schoolfellow's vow recedes into a forgotten boyhood trifle (*Mbh, Ādi Parva*).
- **Droṇa comes to claim the promise** — Sunk in poverty, unable even to give his son Aśvatthāman milk, Droṇa journeys to Pāñcāla and presents himself as Drupada's friend, expecting the half-kingdom (*Mbh, Ādi Parva ~129*). → see the approach from Droṇa's side in [Droṇa §III](./drona.md).
- **The humiliation** — Drupada rebuffs and mocks him: *"friendship subsists only between equals — a beggar cannot be the friend of a king; what friendship was there long ago has perished with time."* He offers, at most, alms, not partnership — and turns Droṇa away insulted (*Mbh, Ādi Parva ~129*).
  - *Reading [scholarly]:* the scene is the epic's clearest fable of how status corrodes loyalty — and it is Drupada, not Droṇa, who first breaks faith. The whole revenge-economy that follows is set in motion by this single act of pride.

## III. Captured as guru-dakṣiṇā — half the kingdom lost (Mbh Ādi Parva ~137)

- **Droṇa's fee from the princes** — Now preceptor of the Kuru and Pāṇḍava princes at Hastināpura, Droṇa demands as his teaching-fee that they **defeat Drupada and bring him bound** (*Mbh, Ādi Parva ~137*).
- **The Kaurava failure and Arjuna's victory** — The Kauravas (led by Duryodhana) attack Pāñcāla and are routed by Drupada; then **Arjuna** storms the capital, defeats Drupada, takes him captive, and drags him before Droṇa (*Mbh, Ādi Parva ~137*).
- **Half of Pāñcāla seized** — Droṇa spares Drupada's life but takes **half his kingdom** — the lands north of the **Gaṅgā** (around **Ahicchatra**) — leaving Drupada the southern half about **Kāmpilya** along the Carmaṇvatī. Droṇa then mockingly offers renewed "friendship between equals," now that both are kings of half-realms (*Mbh, Ādi Parva ~137*).
  - *Note [scholarly]:* the partition is poetic justice turned inside-out — the half-kingdom Drupada refused to *give* is the half he is forced to *lose*. The two humiliations (Droṇa's, then Drupada's) mirror each other and lock the feud.

## IV. The fire-altar of vengeance — the putrakāmeṣṭi (Mbh Ādi Parva ~155–169)

- **A king consumed by revenge** — Burning to avenge the humiliation, and knowing no warrior of his own could overcome the astra-master Droṇa, Drupada wanders the brahmin settlements of the Gaṅgā–Yamunā seeking a priest who can grant him **a son fated to kill Droṇa** (*Mbh, Ādi Parva ~155*).
- **Yāja and Upayāja** — He finds the brahmin brothers **Upayāja** and his elder **Yāja**; after long entreaty and the gift of cattle, Yāja agrees to perform the **putrakāmeṣṭi** (son-desiring sacrifice) for him (*Mbh, Ādi Parva ~166–167*).
- **The fire-born twins** — From the sacrificial fire rises a youth, fully armed, blazing like fire — **[Dhṛṣṭadyumna](./dhrishtadyumna.md)** ("bold-splendour"), proclaimed by a voice as **born to slay Droṇa**. Then, unasked, from the altar rises a dark, beautiful maiden — **[Draupadī](./draupadi.md)** (**Kṛṣṇā**, "the dark one"; **Yājñasenī**, **Pāñcālī**), destined to be the cause of the kṣatriyas' destruction (*Mbh, Ādi Parva ~167–169*).
  - *Variant (Mbh, Ādi Parva tellings):* in some recensions the offering of *havis* to Drupada's queen is mistimed or refused, and the children spring directly from the flames rather than from the womb — underscoring their **ayonija** (not-womb-born) nature.
- **The bitter irony** — Drupada, who sought only an instrument against Droṇa, is given a daughter whose marriage will bind him to the **Pāṇḍavas** and a son who will indeed behead Droṇa — but at the cost of his own house's annihilation in the [Sauptika](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md) night-raid (*Mbh, Ādi Parva*).

## V. Father of Draupadī — the svayaṃvara and the Pāṇḍava alliance (Mbh Ādi Parva ~176–191)

- **The bow-and-target test** — Drupada announces a **svayaṃvara** for Draupadī, setting an all-but-impossible feat: stringing a mighty bow and piercing a revolving target through a wheel-aperture. Secretly he hopes only **Arjuna** can win her, longing for alliance with the Pāṇḍavas he believes burnt at Vāraṇāvata (*Mbh, Ādi Parva ~176–179*).
- **Arjuna wins Kṛṣṇā** — A brahmin-disguised **Arjuna** (the Pāṇḍavas living incognito after the lac-house) accomplishes the feat and wins Draupadī, fighting off the affronted kings (*Mbh, Ādi Parva ~179–185*).
- **The shared-wife marriage** — When the five brothers bring her home and Kuntī's unwitting word bids them "share" the gift, Draupadī becomes the **common wife of all five Pāṇḍavas**. Drupada is scandalized until **Vyāsa** explains the dharmic precedent and her past-life boon; he then consents (*Mbh, Ādi Parva ~187–191*). → full arc in [Draupadī](./draupadi.md).
- **The alliance sealed** — The marriage forges the **Pāñcāla–Pāṇḍava** alliance that becomes the backbone of the war coalition — exactly the bond Drupada's fire-rite, aimed at Droṇa, had unwittingly engineered (*Mbh, Ādi Parva*).

## VI. Father of Śikhaṇḍin — the instrument of Bhīṣma's fall (Mbh Udyoga Parva ~188–193)

- **The child raised as a son** — Drupada is also father of **[Śikhaṇḍin](./amba-shikhandi.md)**, born (in the dominant telling) a daughter, **Śikhaṇḍinī**, but raised as a boy on the strength of a divine promise; she is the rebirth of **Ambā**, sworn across lifetimes to cause **Bhīṣma's** death (*Mbh, Udyoga Parva ~188–192*).
- **The exchange of sex** — When the deception threatens to be exposed at Śikhaṇḍin's marriage, a **yakṣa (Sthūṇākarṇa)** exchanges sexes with the child, and Śikhaṇḍin becomes male — the loophole by which Bhīṣma, who will not strike one "formerly a woman," is felled on day 10 of the war (*Mbh, Udyoga Parva ~193*). → full arc in [Ambā → Śikhaṇḍin](./amba-shikhandi.md).
- **One house, two doom-instruments** — Thus Drupada fathers both the slayer-enabler of **Bhīṣma** (Śikhaṇḍin) and the slayer of **Droṇa** (Dhṛṣṭadyumna) — the two grandsires of Kuru arms whose falls hinge the war (*Mbh, Udyoga/Bhīṣma/Droṇa Parvas*).

## VII. The war and his death (Mbh Udyoga–Droṇa Parvas)

- **Counsel before war** — Drupada urges early diplomacy, dispatching his own **family priest (purohita)** as envoy to Hastināpura, and counsels mustering allies in case the embassy fails (*Mbh, Udyoga Parva ~4–5*).
- **A Pāṇḍava commander-candidate** — When the Pāṇḍavas choose a generalissimo, Drupada is named among the worthies; the command goes to his son **Dhṛṣṭadyumna**, but the aged king fights in the front ranks as a *mahāratha* (*Mbh, Udyoga Parva ~151–155*).
- **His death at Droṇa's hands** — On the fifteenth day, in the climactic rampage of the [Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md), the aged Drupada confronts his lifelong enemy **Droṇa** and is **slain by him** — the feud's penultimate beat, just before Droṇa is himself beheaded by Drupada's fire-born son Dhṛṣṭadyumna (*Mbh, Droṇa Parva ~186*).
  - *Note [scholarly]:* the sequence is deliberate symmetry — Droṇa kills the father (Drupada) and the grandfather-king **Virāṭa** on the same day, and is in turn killed by the son (Dhṛṣṭadyumna) within hours. The two old enemies fall almost together, the original quarrel consuming both.
- **The end of his line** — In the [Sauptika Parva](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md) night-massacre, **Aśvatthāman** (Droṇa's son) slaughters the sleeping Pāñcālas — including Dhṛṣṭadyumna and Śikhaṇḍin — so that the entire fire-born harvest of Drupada's vengeance, and his vengeance itself, is annihilated (*Mbh, Sauptika Parva ~8*).

## VIII. Reading the figure [scholarly]

- **The first mover of the catastrophe** — More than Droṇa, Drupada is the *originating* cause: it is his broken boyhood vow that opens the feud, and his ritual hunger for revenge that births the children through whom the war is won and the kṣatriya order destroyed.
- **Vengeance that devours itself** — Every fruit of Drupada's revenge turns double-edged: the daughter binds him to a war that kills his sons; the son kills Droṇa but is killed in turn; the king himself dies by the very enemy his life was bent on destroying. The Mahābhārata uses him as its parable of how *putra-kāma* (desire-for-progeny) weaponized by hatred becomes self-annihilating.
- **Name and place traditions [folk/regional]** — Pāñcāla sites in modern Uttar Pradesh (around **Kāmpil**, Farrukhabad district, and **Ahicchatra**, Bareilly) are locally identified with Drupada's two half-kingdoms; these are post-epic regional attachments, not textual.
