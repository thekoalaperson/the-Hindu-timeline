# Prahasta — Rāvaṇa's Uncle, Minister and Commander-in-Chief

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Prahasta** — eldest-known son of **[Sumālī](./sumali-and-malyavan.md)**, maternal uncle of **[Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md)**, chief minister and *senāpati* of Laṅkā; the counsellor who argues Rāvaṇa into claiming the city from **[Kubera](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/kubera.md)**, and the first of the king's own house to fall in the war, killed by **Nīla**. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). His embassy is in the [Uttara Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md); his sons die in the [Sundara Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md); his command and death are in the [Yuddha Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* his life falls in the **[Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the present 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara. On the academic clock his war-book career sits in the oldest surviving stratum of the Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa*, while his genealogy and his embassy belong to the later **Uttara Kāṇḍa**. [scholarly]

## Name, birth and office

- **Prahasta (प्रहस्त)** — the name is built on *pra-* + *hasta*, "hand," and is conventionally rendered "**long-armed**" or "he of the outstretched hand." Vālmīki offers no gloss; the epithets attached to him instead are of function — *senāpati* (army-chief), *mantrin* (counsellor), and the plain "chief of Rāvaṇa's ministers" (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 57; *Sundara Kāṇḍa* 45).
- **Son of Sumālī and Ketumatī** — the first-named of Sumālī's **ten sons** — Prahasta, Akampana, Vikaṭa, Kālakāmukha, Dhūmrākṣa, Daṇḍa, Suparśva, Saṃhrādi, Praghasa, Bhāsakarṇa — born of the fallen Laṅkā dynasty in Pātāla. He is therefore brother to **[Kaikasī](./kaikasi.md)** and **maternal uncle** of Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa, Śūrpaṇakhā and Vibhīṣaṇa; the office he holds in Laṅkā is a family office (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5). Full roster: [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md).
  - *Note (two of his brothers' names recur in the war-books):* **Praghasa** and **Bhāsakarṇa** are also names among the five grove-*senāpatis* of *Sundara* 46 and among rākṣasas killed at *Yuddha* 43; the text identifies neither set with this one. [disputed] — see [Paulastya / rākṣasa vaṃśa §7](../../03-lineages-vamsha/paulastya-rakshasa-vamsha.md).
- **The uncle-generation in command** — three of Sumālī's sons hold field commands in the war and die in it: **Dhūmrākṣa** and **Akampana** are killed by Hanumān, and Prahasta by Nīla. The corpus notes that the Akampana of the war-council and the Akampana who brings the news of Janasthāna to Laṅkā (*Araṇya* 31) bear the same name and are generally read as one rākṣasa (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 51–58; see [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md)). [disputed]
- **His household** — his son **Jambumālī** is the first named champion Hanumān kills in the Aśoka-grove fighting, and **seven more sons** die at the grove's gateway in the sarga that follows (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* 44–45). His mansion stands among the great houses of Laṅkā that Hanumān's burning tail runs through — the burn-roll opens with it (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* 54).

## The embassy to Kubera — the founding crime (Uttara Kāṇḍa 11)

Rāvaṇa's first act as a boon-holder is to take his brother's city, and it is Prahasta who talks him into it and Prahasta who goes to say so.

- **The proposal, and the king's hesitation** — **Sumālī** comes up out of Rasātala with his companions and urges his grandson to reclaim Laṅkā from **Dhanada (Kubera)**. Rāvaṇa balks: Kubera is his elder brother, and the claim smells of impiety (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 11).
- **Prahasta's argument** — the minister answers with precedent rather than sentiment. The earth with its mountains, girdled by the ocean, **formerly belonged to the Daityas**, until the gods took it from them; possession has always followed strength, and among brothers born of one father there is no senior title to a city. The argument is that dominion is not owned but held — and Rāvaṇa is persuaded (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 11).
- **The envoy** — Rāvaṇa sends Prahasta himself to Kubera with the demand. Kubera at first offers to **share the dominion and its riches** rather than surrender the city; then, unwilling to answer alone, he goes to their common father **[Viśravas](./vishravas.md)**, who tells him that Rāvaṇa's boons have unhinged his judgment — that he "can no longer discriminate between what is just" — and that he should yield rather than fight a brother. Kubera departs with his household for **Mount Kailāsa**, and Rāvaṇa is consecrated in Laṅkā (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 11).
- **Why it is the founding crime** — no blow is struck and no law is broken on the face of it, and that is the point: the dynasty's charter act is an *argument*, won by a minister, that turns a boon into a title. Everything the house afterwards holds — the city, the fleet, the war — rests on Prahasta's speech. The corpus keeps this bloodless cession strictly apart from the **yakṣa war** two sargas later, in which Rāvaṇa murders Kubera's envoy, storms Kailāsa and takes the **Puṣpaka** by force (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 13–15; see [Rāvaṇa §IV](./ravana.md)).

## In the war-council

- **Among the boasters (Yuddha 9)** — when Rāvaṇa asks his court how to answer Rāma's approach, Prahasta rises with **Nikumbha, Rabhasa, Sūryaśatru, Suptaghna, Yajñakopa, Mahāpārśva, Mahodara, Agniketu, Raśmiketu, Indrajit, Virūpākṣa, Vajradaṃṣṭra, Dhūmrākṣa, Atikāya** and **Durmukha** and joins the collective vaunt — *now, at once, we shall kill Rāma with Lakṣmaṇa and Sugrīva and the wretched Hanumān who broke into Laṅkā*. Vālmīki gives him no individual speech in the sarga; the boast is the court's, and he is named in it (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 9).
- **What he says he advised** — the epic's one statement of Prahasta's private counsel comes from his own mouth, at the moment he is given the army: he tells Rāvaṇa that he **had advised returning Sītā** and avoiding the war, and that having been overruled he will now spend his life in it (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 57). On Vālmīki's own reckoning the abduction is not his policy: the proposal to seize Sītā comes to Rāvaṇa from **Akampana**, the survivor of Janasthāna (*Araṇya Kāṇḍa* 31), and is pressed by **Śūrpaṇakhā** (*Araṇya* 33–34). Prahasta belongs to Laṅkā's standing chorus of unheeded counsel — with **Vibhīṣaṇa**, **Mālyavān**, **Kumbhakarṇa** on policy, **Mandodarī**, and **Kaikasī** with the aged minister Aviddha (*Yuddha* 34) — and not to the party that made the war.
- **The measure of the man** — the same minister argues his king into taking a city and argues him out of keeping a woman, and loses the second argument. The corpus reads him as the epic's portrait of the competent servant of an incompetent policy: he is never wrong about the state's interest and never able to move his king twice.

## Commander-in-chief — the march from the eastern gate (Yuddha 57)

- **Appointed in extremity** — after **Dhūmrākṣa**, **Vajradaṃṣṭra** and **Akampana** have all been killed in successive sorties, Rāvaṇa summons Prahasta and gives him the army: *take a company of warriors, place yourself in their midst, and go out to conquer all those monkeys* (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 51–57).
- **His answer** — he accepts without illusion. Having recorded that he had counselled otherwise, he pledges the whole of himself: **"neither wives nor sons nor riches are going to be spared by me"** — the vow of a man discharging a debt to a house rather than a general expecting a victory (*Yuddha* 57).
- **The chariot** — gold-worked, drawn by very swift horses under a skilled charioteer, and flying a **serpent standard** (*Yuddha* 57).
- **The omens, itemized** — Vālmīki loads the march with portents and the sarga is one of the epic's set-pieces of doom: **vultures wheel over the chariot**; the **clouds rain blood** on it; **meteors fall**; the **goad drops repeatedly from the charioteer's hand**; the horses **stumble on level ground**; and the splendour that had always shone from Prahasta simply goes out. He reads none of them aloud and marches anyway, out of the **eastern gate** of Laṅkā (*Yuddha* 57).
- **The armies close** — the vānaras take up trees and rocks at the sight of him, and the two hosts meet with an uproar; the general engagement of sarga 57 runs straight into the duel of 58.

## The death — Nīla and the rock (Yuddha 58)

- **The duel** — Prahasta drives at **Nīla**, son of Agni and leader of the vānara van, pouring arrows into him from the chariot. Nīla answers by hurling a **huge sāla tree** that kills the chariot-horses, and breaks the bow (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 58).
- **On foot** — Prahasta takes up a **formidable mace**, leaps down from the wrecked chariot, and closes hand-to-hand; he strikes Nīla on the **forehead** and draws blood, and for a moment has the better of it (*Yuddha* 58).
- **The killing stroke** — Nīla lifts **a huge rock** and hurls it down on Prahasta's head, and the head breaks into a thousand pieces; the commander dies where he stands (*Yuddha* 58).
- **The consequence** — leaderless, the rākṣasa host loses heart and **withdraws into Laṅkā** without holding the field. It is the first defeat of the war that takes a member of Rāvaṇa's own house, and it forces the king out: Rāvaṇa takes the field in person in the next sarga, is disarmed and humiliated by Rāma, and is sent home to rest — after which he wakes **Kumbhakarṇa** (*Yuddha* 58–60).
- **Structural note** — the war-book's ladder of champions runs *commanders → uncle → brother → son → king*, and Prahasta is the rung on which it turns from expendable officers to family. His death is the point at which Laṅkā stops sending others (*Yuddha* 51–60). [scholarly]

## Variants and afterlife

- *Variant (roster assignment):* recensions distribute the minor rākṣasa kills of Yuddha 51–58 differently among Hanumān, Aṅgada, Nīla and Dvivida; the assignment of **Prahasta to Nīla** is stable across the mainstream Vālmīki tradition and is the corpus's reading (*Vālmīki recensions*; see [Aṅgada](./angada.md)). [variant]
- *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas and the vernacular retellings):* Tulsīdās and the regional Rāmāyaṇas compress the war-book's minor champions heavily, and Prahasta typically survives as a name in the muster and a single fall, without the embassy, the omens or the counsel; the Uttara-kāṇḍa material that makes him Rāvaṇa's uncle is absent from retellings that stop at the coronation (*Tulsīdās, Rāmcaritmānas*, Laṅkākāṇḍa; cf. [Variant & Regional Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md)). [late text]
- *Iconography and performance:* in Rāmlīlā and in the Southeast Asian dance-drama traditions Prahasta is a standing role — the veteran retainer who marches out under bad omens — and the omen-laden departure of Yuddha 57 is the scene the stage keeps. [folk] [regional]
