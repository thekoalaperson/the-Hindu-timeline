# Vikarna

> Where this sits: a **Dvāpara-era life** on the [Dvāpara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)→[Kali](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) cusp — the **one righteous Kaurava**, the lone son of Dhṛtarāṣṭra to rise in the dice-hall and argue on dharmic grounds that [Draupadī](../draupadi.md) was never lawfully won, and yet the brother who stays with [Duryodhana](duryodhana.md) and dies among the hundred at [Bhīma](../bhima.md)'s hands — mourned even by his killer. Two clocks: the epic narrates *itihāsa* (war trad. ~3138 BCE; Kali begins 3102 BCE in the scriptural reckoning); academic dating of the text differs sharply.
> Up: [Epic Characters](README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md).

**Vikarṇa** (Sanskrit *Vikarṇa*, conventionally glossed "large-eared" or "ear-less / mis-eared," from *vi-* + *karṇa*) is the **third son of the blind king Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Gāndhārī** and the *Mahābhārata*'s great moral anomaly: the **single brother among the hundred Kauravas who possesses a conscience**. With his ninety-nine brothers he is, in the epic's incarnation-scheme, an aṃśa of the **Paulastya rākṣasas** (the demon-line of Pulastya), while [Duryodhana](duryodhana.md) alone is the aṃśa of **Kali** — yet from this collectively demonic brood Vikarṇa breaks ranks exactly once, and unforgettably. In the [Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md), as [Draupadī](../draupadi.md) is dragged before the assembly and her unanswerable *praśna* paralyses the elders, it is **Vikarṇa who rises and declares that she was not lawfully staked** — because [Yudhiṣṭhira](yudhishthira.md) had already gambled away himself before he wagered her, and a man who is no longer his own master cannot lawfully wager another. It is the **one voice of dharma from the Kaurava benches**, and it is shouted down. Thereafter Vikarṇa's tragedy is that conscience never becomes defection: unlike his half-brother [Yuyutsu](yuyutsu.md), he stays, fights for Duryodhana, and falls — and [Bhīma](../bhima.md), who kills him, openly grieves the killing.

## I. Birth & cosmic identity (Mbh Ādi Parva)

- **Third of the hundred from the flesh-ball** — Vikarṇa is among the hundred sons ripened from Gāndhārī's *māṃsapeśī* (the ball of flesh divided by **[Vyāsa](../vyasa.md)** into a hundred-and-one thumb-sized parts, each sealed in a ghee-pot); the standard lists name him the **third-born**, after Duryodhana and [Duḥśāsana](dushasana.md). (Mbh Ādi Parva ~107, *Sambhava*) [scriptural]
- **Aṃśa of the Paulastya rākṣasas** — The **Aṃśāvataraṇa Parva** assigns the ninety-nine brothers other than Duryodhana to portions of the **rākṣasas of Pulastya's line**, framing the brood collectively as incarnate demonic force (Mbh Ādi Parva ~1.61) — which makes Vikarṇa's lone moment of righteousness all the more striking against his own cosmic grain. [scriptural]
  - *Variant (birth-order lists):* the standard enumeration of the hundred Kauravas in the Ādi Parva ranks Vikarṇa **third**; some retellings and regional lists shuffle the lower ranks, but his place near the head of the hundred is stable. [disputed]

## II. The dice-hall — the one righteous voice (Mbh Sabhā Parva ~61)

- **The setting of his great moment** — After Yudhiṣṭhira loses everything and then Draupadī at the loaded dice, [Duḥśāsana](dushasana.md) drags her into the men's assembly; Draupadī puts the *praśna* that the elders cannot answer — *did Yudhiṣṭhira lose himself first, and could a man who had already lost himself lawfully stake her?* — and a paralysed silence falls over [Bhīṣma](bhishma.md), Droṇa, and the court. (Mbh [Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md) ~60–61) [scriptural]
- **Vikarṇa rises — *"she is not won"*** — Into that silence **Vikarṇa alone stands up** and argues the case on dharma: hunting, drinking, dicing and womanizing are kings' besetting vices; Yudhiṣṭhira was **incited into the game and staked Draupadī after he had already lost himself**, and a slave (he who has lost his own freedom) has **no property left to wager** — therefore **Draupadī was never lawfully won**. He calls on the assembly to weigh the question and appeals to the elders to speak. (Mbh Sabhā Parva ~61) [scriptural]
  - *Detail:* the text frames it as the youthful prince shaming his silent seniors — Vikarṇa repeatedly slaps his arms and calls for an answer, and **no elder responds**, so the boy answers for them. The dharmic point he makes is precisely the one Draupadī herself raised, now spoken from the Kaurava side. [scriptural]
- **Karṇa shouts him down** — **Karṇa** angrily rebukes Vikarṇa for a child presuming to teach his elders, ruling that Draupadī, lawfully won, is a slave; he declares a woman with five husbands no chaste woman and orders her stripped — over Vikarṇa's objection. Vikarṇa's verdict is overruled, but it has been **spoken aloud in open court**, and the assembly knows it. (Mbh Sabhā Parva ~61) [scriptural]
- **The lone conscience of the hundred** — Vikarṇa's intervention is the epic's proof that **even on the Kaurava benches the injustice was visible**; it isolates Duryodhana's faction morally, and tradition remembers Vikarṇa almost solely for this single act of courage. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (devotional / Vulgate retellings):* in many popular tellings Vikarṇa's protest is expanded and applauded, sometimes paired with the *akṣaya-vastra* miracle that follows ([Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md) supplying endless cloth); the **Critical Edition keeps his speech brief and has it simply overruled**, the garments renewing impersonally as dharma's own answer. [late text]
  - *Variant [disputed]:* a minority of scholars read the whole disrobing sequence as a late layer; the mainstream retains Vikarṇa's protest as integral — it is structurally needed to make the Kaurava court's complicity a *choice* rather than mere ignorance.

> For the full dyūta sequence and the recension debate over the disrobing miracle, see the [Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md) walkthrough, the [Draupadī](../draupadi.md) deep-dive, and the parallel death-loop in [Duḥśāsana](dushasana.md).

## III. Conscience without defection (Mbh Vana → Udyoga Parvas)

- **He stays a Kaurava** — Crucially, Vikarṇa's righteousness in the sabhā **never ripens into the crossing that defines [Yuyutsu](yuyutsu.md)**. When war comes he remains loyal to his brothers and fights under Duryodhana — making him the *Mahābhārata*'s study of conscience that speaks but does not act, the tragic counter-case to the brother who walked across the field. (Mbh war parvas) [scholarly]
  - *Note (the deliberate contrast):* the epic gives the Kaurava house **two righteous sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra** — Vikarṇa, who saw the wrong and said so but stayed; and Yuyutsu, who saw it and left. Only the one who acted on conscience survives; the one who merely voiced it dies with the hundred. The pairing is the epic's quiet verdict on speech versus deed. [scholarly]

## IV. The war and his death (Mbh Bhīṣma → Karṇa Parvas)

- **A frontline Kaurava warrior** — Vikarṇa fights through the eighteen-day war as one of Duryodhana's brothers, named among the Kaurava champions in the daily engagements as Bhīma hunts the hundred sons of Gāndhārī in fulfilment of his dice-hall vow. (Mbh Bhīṣma / Droṇa Parvas) [scriptural]
- **Slain by Bhīma — the killer who grieves (Mbh Karṇa Parva)** — Bhīma corners and **kills Vikarṇa in the [Karṇa Parva](../mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md)**, but the text marks this death apart from the slaughter of his brothers: **Bhīma laments the man he has just killed**, recalling Vikarṇa's lone stand for Draupadī in the dice-hall and praising him as a warrior who knew dharma, before lamenting that the duty of war (his vow to slay all Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sons) has forced him to fell so righteous a foe. (Mbh Karṇa Parva) [scriptural]
  - *Detail:* this open mourning by his slayer is **unique among the hundred Kaurava deaths** — Bhīma drinks [Duḥśāsana](dushasana.md)'s blood and exults, but over Vikarṇa he sorrows. The contrast is the epic's way of certifying Vikarṇa's righteousness from the mouth of his enemy. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (the slayer & the war-book):* the great majority of tradition has **Bhīma** kill Vikarṇa in the Karṇa Parva with the explicit lament; some retellings place the death earlier or hedge the precise day, but the **Bhīma-laments-Vikarṇa** scene is the fixed and most-cited form. [regional]
- **Falls among the hundred** — With his death Vikarṇa is counted among the **ninety-nine brothers Bhīma slays** (Duryodhana falling last, by the foul mace-blow of the [Śalya Parva](../mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md)); only [Yuyutsu](yuyutsu.md), the defector, survives. (Mbh war parvas) [scriptural]

## V. Family & afterlife in tradition

- **Father:** Dhṛtarāṣṭra. **Mother:** Gāndhārī. **Brothers:** Duryodhana, [Duḥśāsana](dushasana.md) and the rest of the hundred [Kauravas](duryodhana.md); the half-brother [Yuyutsu](yuyutsu.md) (of the vaiśya maid) and the sister **Duḥśalā**. (Mbh Ādi Parva)
  - *Variant [disputed]:* genealogies of the hundred Kauravas differ on which sons and wives attach to which brother; details specific to Vikarṇa beyond his parentage and birth-rank are not stable across recensions.
- **The righteous Kaurava** — Tradition remembers Vikarṇa almost solely as **the conscience of the dice-hall**: the one Dhārtarāṣṭra who dared name the wager unlawful when Bhīṣma and Droṇa sat mute. He is invoked in later moral and legal discussion as the voice that stated the dharmic flaw at the heart of the dyūta — that a man who has lost himself cannot stake another. [scholarly]
- **Speech versus deed** — His afterlife in the tradition is bound to the comparison with [Yuyutsu](yuyutsu.md) and with the blood-drinking horror of [Duḥśāsana](dushasana.md): three sons of one father standing for conscience-in-word, conscience-in-deed, and pure cruelty — and the epic's grief, voiced through [Bhīma](../bhima.md) at the kill, is reserved for Vikarṇa alone. [scholarly]
