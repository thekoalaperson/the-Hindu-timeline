# Pulastya — the Prajāpati at the Root of Laṅkā

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Pulastya** — mind-born son of **Brahmā**, Prajāpati and [Saptarṣi](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md), father of **[Viśravas](./vishravas.md)** and so grandfather of both **[Kubera](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/kubera.md)** and **[Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md)** (in the Mahābhārata, Kubera's father); also one of the canon's principal **transmitters of Purāṇa and tīrtha lore**. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). For the four seers as a group see [The Mind-Born Seers](../the-mind-born-seers.md); for the dynasty he roots, [Paulastya / rākṣasa vaṃśa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/paulastya-rakshasa-vamsha.md).
> Two clocks: on the **scriptural clock** Pulastya belongs to the first emission of the present *kalpa*. Vālmīki sets his fathering of Viśravas "formerly, in the Kṛta Yuga" (*purā kṛtayuge*, CE 7.2.4), and he is still alive and acting before Rāma's time in the [Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) of the current Mahāyuga, as the seers of the first manvantara are. Nothing here is datable on the historical clock; he functions as a genealogical **origin** and a narrative **frame-voice**, not as a figure of recorded chronology. [scholarly]

## Name and station

- **Pulastya (पुलस्त्य)** — one of Brahmā's **mānasaputras**, emitted without union at the dawn of the *kalpa*, and titled **Prajāpati** ("lord of progeny") and **Brahmarṣi**. The counts differ by text, and the corpus keeps them apart:
  - *Mahābhārata:* six great mind-born sons — Marīci, Aṅgiras, Atri, **Pulastya**, Pulaha, Kratu (*MBh* 1.60.4, BORI).
  - *Vālmīki, Sundara Kāṇḍa:* the rākṣasī Ekajaṭā, pressing Sītā to accept Rāvaṇa, calls Pulastya "the fourth of the six Prajāpatis, the mind-born son of Brahmā" (CE 5.21.6 = vulgate 5.23.6). She names no list; it is presumably the Mahābhārata's six, in which he also stands fourth.
  - *Vālmīki, Araṇya Kāṇḍa:* in Jaṭāyu's longer roster of Prajāpatis (Kardama first, Kaśyapa last) Pulastya is one of seventeen (CE 3.13.7–9 = vulgate 3.14.7–9).
  - *Viṣṇu Purāṇa:* nine mind-born sons, the "nine Brahmās" — Bhṛgu, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu, Aṅgiras, Marīci, Dakṣa, Atri, Vasiṣṭha (*VP* 1.7.5–6).
  - *Bhāgavata Purāṇa:* ten, with Nārada as the tenth (*BhP* 3.12.22).
  - For the full mānasaputra roster see [The Prajāpatis](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/the-prajapatis.md).
- **Saptarṣi of the first manvantara, not of the present one** — Pulastya is placed in the first, **Svāyambhuva** manvantara; the Purāṇa Index cites the Vāyu for this (Vāyu 9.102, 28.22, via Wisdomlib; not checked against the text) [secondary]. The Viṣṇu Purāṇa says only that it has already described that manvantara's seven seers (*VP* 3.1.8), presumably meaning the mind-born sons of 1.7, who there number nine; it does not list the seven again. Its seven for the **present, Vaivasvata** manvantara are Vasiṣṭha, Kāśyapa, Atri, Jamadagni, Gautama, Viśvāmitra and Bharadvāja, and Pulastya is not among them (*VP* 3.1.32). For the star-mapping of the Great Bear and the roster variants, see [Saptarṣi](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md).
  - *Variant (birth-organ):* the Bhāgavata gives each mind-son a part of Brahmā's body to issue from, and Pulastya comes from the **ears** (*pulastyaḥ karṇayor ṛṣiḥ*, *BhP* 3.12.24). Other Purāṇas derive him instead from Brahmā's vital breath **udāna**: the Śiva Purāṇa 2.1.16 does so, and the Purāṇa Index cites the Brahmāṇḍa and Vāyu for the same derivation. These are given here as collected on Wisdomlib and are not checked against the texts. [disputed]
- **Paulastya, the gotra and the patronymic** — he heads the brahmin **Paulastya gotra** carried in *pravara* recitation (*gotra-pravara tradition*; see [Ṛṣi lineages & gotras](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rishi-lineages-and-gotras.md)). In the Rāmāyaṇa the patronymic falls on the whole house:
  - Pulastya himself coins it: he promises Tṛṇabindu's daughter a son "who will carry on both lines, known as Paulastya" (*ubhayor vaṃśakartāraṃ paulastya iti viśrutam*, CE 7.2.26).
  - **Rāvaṇa** — called Paulastya most often.
  - **Indrajit** — "Paulastya" in the war (CE 6.68.3, 6.74.9 = vulgate 6.87.9).
  - **The rākṣasa host** — called "the Paulastyas" in the plural (CE 3.21.12, 3.22.28).
  - **Vibhīṣaṇa** — in the Mahābhārata (*MBh* 3.275.66).
  - **The poem itself** — Vālmīki gives the Rāmāyaṇa a third title, ***Paulastya-vadha***, "the slaying of Pulastya's descendant" (CE 1.4.6 = vulgate 1.4.7).
- **Wife and household** — the texts give him different wives and the corpus merges none of them [disputed]:
  - **Vālmīki:** the unnamed daughter of the rājarṣi **Tṛṇabindu** (CE 7.2; below).
  - **Viṣṇu Purāṇa:** **Prīti**, one of **Dakṣa's** daughters given to the sages (*VP* 1.7.25–27). Their son is **Dattoli** (*VP* 1.10.9).
  - **Bhāgavata:** **Havirbhū**, daughter of **Kardama** (*BhP* 3.24.22, 4.1.36).
  - *Variant (Agastya as his son — reconciled by rebirth):* the Bhāgavata makes **Agastya** and Viśravas sons of Pulastya by Havirbhū, and adds that this Agastya was in another birth the digestive fire Dahrāgni (*so 'nya-janmani dahrāgniḥ*, *BhP* 4.1.36). The Viṣṇu Purāṇa makes the same link in the other direction: Pulastya's son Dattoli "was Agastya in a former birth, in the Svāyambhuva manvantara" (*VP* 1.10.9). Both texts thus place the Pulastya-born figure in another birth (the VP also in another manvantara). Neither passage mentions the older pot-birth from Mitra-Varuṇa at the sight of Urvaśī; the rebirth framing leaves room for it but does not address it. The pot-birth is the account the corpus carries at [Agastya](../agastya.md). [disputed]
  - **Other sons:** the Viṣṇu Purāṇa also names **Nidāgha**, "son of Pulastya," the pupil to whom the sage Ṛbhu teaches non-duality. Nidāgha lives at **Vīranagara** on the Devikā river, a city "founded by Pulastya" (*VP* 2.15.4–7).

## The declaration on Tṛṇabindu's mountain (Uttara Kāṇḍa 2)

- **Core Vālmīki, not a regional tale** — the story is told to Rāma by **Agastya**, the Uttara Kāṇḍa's narrator of the rākṣasa prehistory (CE 7.2.1–3). Pulastya goes to the hermitage of the rājarṣi **Tṛṇabindu** on the slope of Meru and practises austerities and Vedic recitation there (CE 7.2.6–7). The maidens who come to the lovely grove break his concentration.
  - *CE:* they are daughters of devas, pannagas and rājarṣis, with apsarases, who come there to play (CE 7.2.8–9).
  - *Vulgate:* they are daughters of ṛṣis, pannagas and rājarṣis, with apsarases, and they sing, play instruments and dance (Shastri, Uttara ch. 2).
- **The declaration and the conception** — provoked, he declares aloud that **whichever maiden comes into his sight shall conceive** (*yā me darśanam āgacchet sā garbhaṃ dhārayiṣyati*, CE 7.2.10). The others stay away. **Tṛṇabindu's own daughter** had not heard him, and she wanders into the hermitage looking for her friends. She hears the sound of the Veda, sees the ascetic, and turns pale, with the signs of pregnancy showing on her body (CE 7.2.12–19).
- **The marriage** — her father learns the cause by meditation and offers her to Pulastya "as alms" (*bhikṣām*, CE 7.2.20–24). Pulastya, pleased with her service, promises her **a son "equal to myself in virtues,"** who will carry on both their lines (CE 7.2.25–26). This sits in the Uttara Kāṇḍa's own opening genealogy, the same stratum as Kubera's boons and Rāvaṇa's birth, and not in any later or regional layer. [core text]
- **The name carries the mechanism** — the son is **Viśravas** because "the Veda was heard (*viśruta*) by you while I was reciting it here" (CE 7.2.27). The name records what his mother heard, not the declaration. Full treatment: [Viśravas](./vishravas.md).
  - *Variant (Vālmīki's own Sundara Kāṇḍa — Viśravas mind-born):* Ekajaṭā's pedigree speech calls Viśravas Pulastya's ***mānasaḥ sutaḥ***, a "mind-born son" (CE 5.21.7 = vulgate 5.23.7). That sits against the Uttara's birth from Tṛṇabindu's daughter, and the epic does not reconcile the two. [disputed]
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata — Tṛṇabindu's daughter one generation down):* in the Bhāgavata, Tṛṇabindu's daughter by the apsaras Alambuṣā is **Iḍaviḍā/Ilavilā**, and she is the wife of **Viśravas** and the mother of Kubera, not Pulastya's wife (*BhP* 9.2.31–32). This is a likely source of the "Tṛṇabindu" entry in the spread of names for Kubera's mother recorded at [Viśravas](./vishravas.md). [disputed]
- **What it costs** — the corpus's shortest statement of the epic's causal chain: an ascetic loses his temper at some noise, and two generations later a war destroys Laṅkā. Vālmīki never draws the moral, and never has Pulastya reflect on it. [scholarly]
- **Grandfather at Kubera's naming** — when Viśravas's first son is born to **Devavarṇinī**, daughter of [Bharadvāja](../bharadvaja.md), the "grandfather," most naturally Pulastya, names him **Vaiśravaṇa**, together with the devarṣis, "because he resembles Viśravas" (CE 7.3.3–7). [disputed] The CE calls the namer only *pitāmaha*, "the grandfather," which is also Vālmīki's usual name for Brahmā. Shastri's vulgate translation says outright that it is the "paternal grandfather … Paulastya" (Uttara ch. 3).

## Root of two dynasties

- **Through Viśravas, both houses (Vālmīki)** — Viśravas has two wives:
  - **Devavarṇinī**, a brahmin and Bharadvāja's daughter (CE 7.3.3). Their son is **Kubera**, the yakṣa wealth-god and first tenant of Laṅkā. The Purāṇic name for Kubera's mother is **Iḍaviḍā** (*BhP* 4.1.37).
  - The rākṣasī **[Kaikasī](./kaikasi.md)**. Their children are **Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa, Śūrpaṇakhā** and **Vibhīṣaṇa** (CE 7.9.21–27).

  Pulastya is therefore the common grandfather of the *Rāmāyaṇa*'s wealth-god and its demon-emperor alike, and Rāvaṇa is a **great-grandson of Brahmā** (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa* 2–9; *BhP* 4.1.36–37). See [Yakṣas & Guhyakas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/yakshas-and-guhyakas.md) and [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata — Rāvaṇa's mother):* in the Jaya–Vijaya frame the Bhāgavata names Rāvaṇa's and Kumbhakarṇa's mother **Keśinī**, not Kaikasī (*BhP* 7.1.43). [disputed]
- **The "Pulastya line" is one of four rākṣasa origins, not the only one** — the corpus keeps four incompatible answers apart, because they answer different questions. Merging them loses all four. [disputed]
  - **Race (Vālmīki, Uttara 4):** Brahmā's water-guardians divide themselves by their cry, *rakṣāmaḥ* ("we shall protect") against *yakṣāmaḥ*. It is a guardianship etymology with no Pulastya in it. Vālmīki stages the clash: Rāma says he had heard that "the rākṣasas sprang from Pulastya's line" (*pulastyavaṃśād udbhūtā rākṣasā iti naḥ śrutam*), and asks Agastya about this other origin (CE 7.4.3–4).
  - **Progeny (Mahābhārata):** "the rākṣasas are Pulastya's, and the vānaras and kiṃnaras" (*rākṣasās tu pulastyasya vānarāḥ kiṃnarās tathā*, *MBh* 1.60.7). A star passage adds the yakṣas (*MBh* 1.60.7 \*541). Here a whole class of beings is his offspring, not one royal house.
  - **Descent through Kaśyapa (Viṣṇu Purāṇa):** the line runs from **Khasā**, daughter of Dakṣa, through Kaśyapa (*VP* 1.21.25).
  - **Dynasty:** the **named royal house of Laṅkā**, which is Pulastya's and no one else's.

  See [Kaśyapa](../kashyapa.md) and [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md).
- **Not the ancestor of Sukeśa's line** — a distinction the retellings routinely blur. **Heti, Praheti, Sukeśa, Mālyavān, Sumālī and Mālī** — the *pre-Rāvaṇa* rākṣasa kings and the original tenants of Laṅkā — descend from Brahmā's water-guardians, **not** from Pulastya. Pulastya's blood enters the demon house only when Kaikasī marries Viśravas, and it enters from the father's side (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 4–5, 9; see [Sumālī & Mālyavān](./sumali-and-malyavan.md)).
  - *Note on Uttara 8:* after Viṣṇu drives them to Pātāla, Agastya says that Sumālī's people belong to "the Sālakaṭaṅkaṭa line" (CE 7.8.22). The next verse reads *ye tvayā nihatās te vai paulastyā nāma rākṣasāḥ / sumālī mālyavān mālī … rāvaṇād balavattarāḥ* (CE 7.8.23). This most naturally contrasts the two groups: "the rākṣasas you slew were the Paulastyas; Sumālī, Mālyavān, Mālī … were mightier than Rāvaṇa." Shastri's vulgate translation instead renders the verse as "warriors of the family of Paulastya named Sumali, Malyavan, Mali" (Uttara ch. 8). The genealogy of Uttara 4–5 settles the descent either way. [disputed]

## The Mahābhārata's genealogy — Viśravas as Pulastya's half-self

- **A different family tree, not a variant spelling** — in the *Rāmopākhyāna*, Mārkaṇḍeya tells Yudhiṣṭhira a different genealogy, and the corpus records it beside Vālmīki's rather than merging the two (*MBh* 3.258.11–3.259.9, BORI). [disputed]
  - **Kubera is Pulastya's own son**, "born of a cow" (*tasya vaiśravaṇo nāma gavi putro 'bhavat*, 3.258.12).
  - **Viśravas is Pulastya's half-self.** Kubera leaves his father to attend on Brahmā. In anger Pulastya "created himself by himself": **Viśravas** is born "from half of his own self," to retaliate against Vaiśravaṇa (3.258.13–14; *pulastyasya tu yaḥ krodhād ardhadeho 'bhavan muniḥ*, 3.259.1).
  - **Brahmā rewards Kubera** with immortality, lordship of wealth, Lokapāla rank, Śiva's friendship, a son Nalakūbara, and Laṅkā as his capital (3.258.15–16).
  - **Rāvaṇa's mothers.** Kubera sees that his "father" Viśravas is angry with him and tries to win his favour (3.259.2). To that end he sends him three rākṣasī attendants, **Puṣpotkaṭā, Rākā and Mālinī**. Puṣpotkaṭā bears **Rāvaṇa** and **Kumbhakarṇa**, Mālinī bears **Vibhīṣaṇa**, and Rākā bears the twins **Khara** and **Śūrpaṇakhā** (3.259.3–8). There is no Kaikasī.
  - **Brahmā as *pitāmaha*.** The Mahābhārata opens Rāvaṇa's birth by calling Brahmā his *pitāmaha*, "grandfather" (3.258.11). That is also Brahmā's standing title, "the Grandsire," so it need not count generations. Read literally, it fits Viśravas being Pulastya himself in a second body. The [Kubera](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/kubera.md) page records this as the "epic version."

## The intercession — Pulastya frees Rāvaṇa (Uttara Kāṇḍa 32–33)

- **Rāvaṇa taken alive** — in the course of his world-conquest Rāvaṇa attacks **Arjuna Kārtavīrya (Sahasrārjuna)**, the thousand-armed Haihaya emperor of Māhiṣmatī. He is beaten, **bound and carried off as a prisoner** to Arjuna's own city (CE 7.32.62–72). It is one of the defeats the Uttara concedes its antagonist.
- **The grandfather comes for him** — the gods report the capture in heaven, and Pulastya hears of it (CE 7.33.1). Moved by "affection for his son's son" (*putrasutasnehāt*), he flies to Māhiṣmatī by the path of the wind (CE 7.33.2–3).
  - **The welcome.** Arjuna receives him with arghya and madhuparka and offers kingdom, sons and wives at his command (CE 7.33.6–12).
  - **The request.** Pulastya praises the king's strength, which has bound "this grandson of mine, so hard to conquer" (*pautro me 'tīvadurjayaḥ*), and asks for his release (CE 7.33.14–16).
  - **The pact.** Arjuna frees Rāvaṇa without demur and makes with him a "non-violent friendship witnessed by fire" (*ahiṃsākaṃ sakhyam … sāgnikam*, CE 7.33.17–18). Rāvaṇa is embraced by Pulastya and sent off "ashamed" (*lajjamānaḥ*), and Pulastya returns to Brahmaloka (CE 7.33.19–20).
  - **Agastya's moral.** Here Agastya does draw one for Rāma: "there are always those stronger than the strong; no one who seeks his own good should despise another" (CE 7.33.22). Rāvaṇa promptly resumes his slaughter of men across the earth (CE 7.33.23).
  - **The only rescue.** In Vālmīki this is the only occasion on which Pulastya intervenes in the affairs of his descendants, and he intervenes on behalf of the one who will destroy the line.
  - *Note on the parallel:* the same defeat-then-friendship shape recurs immediately afterwards. **Vālī** humbles Rāvaṇa and then takes him into a fire-witnessed pact of his own (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 34). Twice in three sargas the epic shows its boon-protected king beaten by beings his boon never covered, a man and a vānara. See [Rāvaṇa §IV](./ravana.md) and [Sugrīva & Vālī](./sugriva-and-vali.md).
  - *Variant (Purāṇic):* the Purāṇa Index lists Purāṇic tellings of Pulastya's appeal to Kārtavīrya (*Brahmāṇḍa* 3.69.36; *Matsya* 43.38). They are not checked here against the texts. [secondary]
- **A later Uttara appearance (the Ila episode)** — Pulastya comes with Kardama, Kratu, Vaṣaṭkāra and Oṃkāra to the hermitage where Budha is sheltering **Ilā**, the king Ila turned woman. The assembled sages approve Kardama's remedy of a horse-sacrifice to Rudra (CE 7.81.8–13 = vulgate 7.90; Shastri ch. 90). The sages are said to speak in turn, but none of his words are given, and the episode has nothing to do with his descendants. See [Ila / Sudyumna](../../03-lineages-vamsha/ila-sudyumna.md).

## The transmitter — Pulastya as a voice of the tradition

Outside his own genealogy, Pulastya's chief role in the canon is that of **narrator**: the sage from whom several of the tradition's largest catalogues are said to descend.

- **The rākṣasa-sattra of Parāśara — why Pulastya grants the Purāṇa boon** — **Parāśara** (Vyāsa's father) holds a sacrificial session to burn all rākṣasas in vengeance for his father Śakti, who was devoured by one. Pulastya intercedes for his own progeny, and the two great texts tell the scene differently:
  - *Mahābhārata:* Atri, **Pulastya**, Pulaha and Kratu come "wishing to save the rākṣasas' lives" (*rakṣasāṃ jīvitepsayā*). Pulastya rebukes Parāśara for "this extermination of my offspring" (*prajocchedam imaṃ mahyam*) of innocent rākṣasas. He and Vasiṣṭha persuade him to end the rite, and the gathered fire is cast away on the northern flank of the Himālaya (*MBh* 1.172.9–17, BORI). The rebuke is in the Gandharva's narration in the Caitraratha section.
  - *Viṣṇu Purāṇa:* Vasiṣṭha talks Parāśara down. Pulastya then arrives and rewards him for "not cutting off my line even in anger" (*saṃtater na mamocchedaḥ kruddhenāpi yataḥ kṛtaḥ*). The boons are mastery of all śāstras, **authorship of a Purāṇa-saṃhitā**, and true knowledge of the gods (*VP* 1.1.19–28). Parāśara's recitation of the Viṣṇu Purāṇa is framed as this boon's fruit, and he says so again at its close: "by Pulastya's boon this came to my memory" (*VP* 1.1.29–30, 6.8.50).
  - *Bhāgavata echo:* Maitreya says Parāśara taught him "the first Purāṇa" at Pulastya's bidding (*ukto … pulastyena*, *BhP* 3.8.9).
- **The tīrtha catalogue to Bhīṣma** — in the Mahābhārata's **Tīrtha-yātrā Parva**, **Nārada** tells Yudhiṣṭhira how **Bhīṣma** once heard the fruits of pilgrimage "from Pulastya" (*MBh* 3.80–83 BORI = Ganguli Vana 82–85).
  - **The setting.** Bhīṣma was keeping the vow to the ancestors (*pitryaṃ vratam*) at **Gaṅgādvāra** on the Bhāgīrathī when the seer appeared (3.80.11–15).
  - **The catalogue.** Pulastya then recites the fords of the whole subcontinent.
  - **The precondition.** The tradition's restriction on pilgrimage is stated here: the fruit belongs only to one whose hands, feet and mind are controlled and who has learning, tapas and good name (*yasya hastau ca pādau ca manaś caiva susaṃyatam / vidyā tapaś ca kīrtiś ca sa tīrthaphalam aśnute*, 3.80.30). The verses after it add refusal of gifts, freedom from ego and anger, and treating all beings as oneself (3.80.31–33).
  - **Better than sacrifice.** The rationale is social: costly sacrifices are out of reach of the poor, while tīrtha-going is "the seers' supreme secret," superior even to sacrifice (3.80.35–38).
  - **The close.** Pulastya vanishes, and Bhīṣma tours the earth "at Pulastya's word" (3.83.96–97).
  - Canonical home: [Mahābhārata tīrtha-yātrā fords](../../06-sacred-geography/mahabharata-tirtha-yatra-fords.md) and [Tīrtha & tīrtha-yātrā](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/tirtha-and-tirtha-yatra.md). See also [Bhīṣma](./bhishma.md).
- **The Padma Purāṇa frame** — the second-largest Mahāpurāṇa is relayed by the Sūta **Ugraśravas**, son of Lomaharṣaṇa, to the sages at Naimiṣa (*Padma Purāṇa*, Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa 1). Its content was "narrated to Pulastya by Brahmā," and Brahmā sends Pulastya to **Bhīṣma**, who is meditating at **Gaṅgādvāra** (Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa 2.46–47, 60–70; Deshpande tr.). The Padma thus reuses the Mahābhārata's setting, Bhīṣma devoted to the ancestors at Gaṅgādvāra, as the occasion for a whole Purāṇa, and later Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa chapters keep the Bhīṣma-asks, Pulastya-answers form (e.g. ch. 9 on śrāddha). See [Padma Purāṇa](../../90-literature-corpus/puranas/padma-purana.md).
- **The Vāmana Purāṇa frame** — that text opens with **Nārada asking Pulastya**, who is seated in his hermitage. Nārada asks how Viṣṇu took the dwarf form, how Prahlāda fought the gods, why Satī was reborn as Himavat's daughter, and about tīrthas, gifts and vows. Pulastya answers with the whole Purāṇa (*Vāmana Purāṇa* 1.2–10; see [Vāmana Purāṇa](../../90-literature-corpus/puranas/vamana-purana.md)).
- **Counsel to Dhruva** — Pulastya is one of the seven seers whom the boy [Dhruva](../dhruva.md) meets. Each tells him to worship Viṣṇu, and Pulastya's line is that by worshipping Hari, "the supreme Brahman, the supreme abode," one attains even hard-won liberation (*VP* 1.11.46).
- **Other stations** — the Viṣṇu Purāṇa and the Bhāgavata place Pulastya among the sages who ride the Sun's chariot in the month of Madhu, that is Caitra (*VP* 2.10.3; *BhP* 12.11.33). The Bhāgavata also lists him among the seers who come to see Kṛṣṇa at Kurukṣetra during the solar eclipse (*BhP* 10.84.4).
- **The pattern** — the same sage whose one moment of irritation seeds the epic's antagonist is, elsewhere in the canon, the standing voice of cosmology, dharma and pilgrimage. It is also the same sage who, in the Parāśara story, speaks up for the rākṣasas as his own children. The corpus notes the juxtaposition without harmonizing it: the tradition does not treat the Tṛṇabindu declaration as a fall. [scholarly]

## Why he matters

- **The genealogical floor of the antagonist** — Rāvaṇa's brahmin descent traces to Pulastya in two steps, and so does the pedigree his own side boasts of. Ekajaṭā presses it on Sītā as a reason to accept him (CE 5.21.6–8). The *Rāmāyaṇa* is unusual among epics in giving its villain a pedigree from the creator himself, and this is that pedigree (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 2–10). [scholarly]
  - *Later tradition:* in the Skanda Purāṇa's *Setu-māhātmya*, Rāma asks the sages how to expiate "the sin arising from killing the descendant of Pulastya," and they tell him to install the Rāmanātha liṅga on Gandhamādana (*Skanda Purāṇa*, Brahma-khaṇḍa, Setu-māhātmya 44, tr. on Wisdomlib). The popular Rāmeśvaram legend tells this as expiation for *brahmahatyā*, since Rāvaṇa was a brahmin by descent. None of it is in Vālmīki. [late text]
- **The two-sided grandfather** — Kubera the Lokapāla and Rāvaṇa the world-conqueror are the same seed two generations down, differing only in which mother. The corpus treats the pairing as the epic's own thesis about descent: lineage supplies power and settles nothing about its use. In the Mahābhārata the pairing is even tighter, since Viśravas is Pulastya's own half-self, made in anger at Kubera.
- **The unmoved narrator** — Pulastya's appearances in the Uttara's rākṣasa narrative bracket the prehistory. He opens it by fathering Viśravas (and, if the *pitāmaha* of CE 7.3.6 is he, by naming Kubera), and closes his part in it by freeing Rāvaṇa from Māhiṣmatī. His only later Uttara appearance is as one of the visiting sages in the Ila episode, with no words of his own (CE 7.81.9). He never comes to Laṅkā, never counsels his descendants, and takes no part in the war.
