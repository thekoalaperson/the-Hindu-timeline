# Pulastya — the Prajāpati at the Root of Laṅkā

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Pulastya** — mind-born son of **Brahmā**, Prajāpati and [Saptarṣi](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md), father of **[Viśravas](./vishravas.md)** and so great-grandfather of both **[Kubera](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/kubera.md)** and **[Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md)**; also one of the canon's principal **transmitters of Purāṇa and tīrtha lore**. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). For the four seers as a group see [The Mind-Born Seers](../the-mind-born-seers.md); for the dynasty he roots, [Paulastya / rākṣasa vaṃśa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/paulastya-rakshasa-vamsha.md).
> Two clocks: on the **scriptural clock** Pulastya belongs to the first emission of the present *kalpa* — billions of years before the Rāmāyaṇa, though he is still alive and acting in the [Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) of the current Mahāyuga, as the seers of the first manvantara are. Nothing here is datable on the historical clock; he functions as a genealogical **origin** and a narrative **frame-voice**, not as a figure of recorded chronology. [scholarly]

## Name and station

- **Pulastya (पुलस्त्य)** — one of Brahmā's **mānasaputras**, emitted without union at the dawn of the *kalpa*, and simultaneously titled **Prajāpati** ("lord of progeny"), **Brahmarṣi**, and one of the original **Saptarṣi** mapped onto the stars of the Great Bear (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 1.7, 3.1; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.12). For the full mānasaputra roster and the 7/10/21 count-variants, see [The Prajāpatis](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/the-prajapatis.md).
  - *Variant (birth-organ):* the Purāṇas assign each mind-son a faculty of Brahmā to issue from; Pulastya is most often given the **ear** — a fitting organ for the seer whose son is named for hearing. The assignments differ by text (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 3.12; *Matsya Purāṇa*). [disputed]
- **Paulastya, the gotra and the patronymic** — he heads the brahmin **Paulastya gotra** carried in *pravara* recitation, and the patronymic **Paulastya** is used of Rāvaṇa throughout the Rāmāyaṇa. The antagonist of the epic is never allowed to forget, or the reader to forget, that he is a Prajāpati's great-grandson (*gotra-pravara tradition*; *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* passim; see [Ṛṣi lineages & gotras](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rishi-lineages-and-gotras.md)).
- **Wife and household** — the Purāṇic marriage-tables give Pulastya one of **Dakṣa's** daughters, **Prīti** (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 1.10), while the Bhāgavata's parallel scheme marries him to **Havirbhū**, daughter of **Kardama** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.1). The corpus records both and merges neither. [disputed]
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata 4.1 — Agastya as his son):* the same Bhāgavata passage makes **Agastya** a son of Pulastya by Havirbhū, alongside Viśravas. This sits against the far older and far better-attested **pot-birth** of Agastya from Mitra-Varuṇa at the sight of Urvaśī, which is the account the corpus carries at [Agastya](../agastya.md). The two parentages are not reconciled anywhere in the tradition. [disputed] [late text]

## The declaration on Tṛṇabindu's mountain (Uttara Kāṇḍa 2)

- **Core Vālmīki, not a regional tale** — Pulastya takes up austerities at the hermitage of **Tṛṇabindu** on the slopes of Meru. The daughters of sages, nāgas and rājarṣis come there to play, sing and dance, and break his concentration; provoked, he declares aloud that **the next maiden who falls under his gaze shall conceive**. The one girl who had not heard him — **Tṛṇabindu's own daughter** — walks in on him reciting the Veda and shows the signs at once. Her father brings her back and gives her to the sage in marriage as alms, and Pulastya, pleased with her service, promises her **a son "like unto myself"** (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa* 2). This sits in the Uttara Kāṇḍa's own opening genealogy — the same stratum as Kubera's boons and Rāvaṇa's birth — and not in any later or regional layer. [core text]
- **The name carries the mechanism** — the son is **Viśravas**, "because it was *heard*" (*viśruta*), and because his mother conceived while hearing the Veda. Full treatment: [Viśravas](./vishravas.md).
- **What it costs** — the corpus's shortest statement of the epic's causal chain: an ascetic loses his temper at some noise, and four generations later a war destroys Laṅkā. Vālmīki never draws the moral, and never has Pulastya reflect on it. [scholarly]

## Root of two dynasties

- **Through Viśravas, both houses** — by his brahmin wife Ilavidā, Viśravas fathers **Kubera**, the yakṣa wealth-god and first tenant of Laṅkā; by the rākṣasī **[Kaikasī](./kaikasi.md)**, he fathers **Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa, Śūrpaṇakhā** and **Vibhīṣaṇa**. Pulastya is therefore the common ancestor of the *Rāmāyaṇa*'s wealth-god and its demon-emperor alike, and Rāvaṇa is a **great-grandson of Brahmā** (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa* 2–9; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 1.8) — see [Yakṣas & Guhyakas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/yakshas-and-guhyakas.md) and [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md).
- **The "Pulastya line" is one of three rākṣasa origins, not the only one** — the corpus keeps three incompatible answers apart, because they answer different questions. **Race:** Vālmīki's own Uttara 4 account, in which Brahmā's water-guardians divide themselves by their cry, *rakṣāmaḥ* ("we shall protect") against *yakṣāmaḥ* — a guardianship etymology with no Pulastya in it. **Descent:** the Purāṇic line from **Khasā**, daughter of Dakṣa, through Kaśyapa. **Dynasty:** the **named royal house of Laṅkā**, which is Pulastya's and no one else's. Merging them loses all three (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 4; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 1.21; see [Kaśyapa](../kashyapa.md) and [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md)). [disputed]
- **Not the ancestor of Sukeśa's line** — a distinction the retellings routinely blur. **Heti, Praheti, Sukeśa, Mālyavān, Sumālī and Mālī** — the *pre-Rāvaṇa* rākṣasa kings and the original tenants of Laṅkā — descend from Brahmā's water-guardians, **not** from Pulastya. Pulastya's blood enters the demon house only when Kaikasī marries Viśravas, and it enters from the father's side (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 4–5, 9; see [Sumālī & Mālyavān](./sumali-and-malyavan.md)).

## The intercession — Pulastya frees Rāvaṇa (Uttara Kāṇḍa 32–33)

- **Rāvaṇa taken alive** — in the course of his world-conquest Rāvaṇa attacks **Arjuna Kārtavīrya (Sahasrārjuna)**, the thousand-armed Haihaya emperor of Māhiṣmatī, and is beaten, **bound and carried off as a prisoner** to Arjuna's own city — one of the two humiliations the epic concedes its antagonist (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa* 32).
- **The great-grandfather comes for him** — the sarga that follows is titled for the act: **Arjuna releases Rāvaṇa at the request of Paulastya**. Pulastya travels to Māhiṣmatī and asks; the emperor honours the seer, frees his prisoner, and the two make a **fire-pact of friendship** (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 33). It is the only occasion in the epic on which Pulastya intervenes in the affairs of his descendants, and he intervenes on behalf of the one who will destroy the line.
  - *Note on the parallel:* the same ancestor-rescue shape recurs immediately afterwards when **Vālī** humbles Rāvaṇa and lets him go into a fire-pact of his own (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 34) — the epic establishing, twice in three sargas, that its unkillable king has been beaten by beings a boon never covered. See [Rāvaṇa §IV](./ravana.md) and [Sugrīva & Vālī](./sugriva-and-vali.md).

## The transmitter — Pulastya as a voice of the tradition

Outside his own genealogy, Pulastya's chief role in the canon is that of **narrator**: the sage from whom several of the tradition's largest catalogues are said to descend.

- **Pulastya → Parāśara → the Viṣṇu Purāṇa** — Pulastya grants **Parāśara** (Vyāsa's father) the boon of becoming a complete narrator of Purāṇa-lore; the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*'s own chain of transmission runs through him, making him a custodian of the genre as such (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 1.1). [scholarly]
- **The tīrtha catalogue to Bhīṣma** — in the Mahābhārata's **Tīrtha-yātrā Parva**, Pulastya recites the fords of the whole subcontinent to a young **Bhīṣma** practising austerities on the Gaṅgā — the prototype *anukramaṇī* of tīrthas that every later Purāṇic māhātmya expands. It is also where the tradition's own restriction on pilgrimage is stated: the fruit belongs only to one whose hands, feet, mind, learning and tapas are controlled (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva* 82–85; canonical home: [Mahābhārata tīrtha-yātrā fords](../../06-sacred-geography/mahabharata-tirtha-yatra-fords.md) and [Tīrtha & tīrtha-yātrā](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/tirtha-and-tirtha-yatra.md)).
- **The Padma Purāṇa frame** — the second-largest Mahāpurāṇa is traditionally narrated by **Pulastya to Bhīṣma** at Gaṅgādvāra, and relayed onward by Sūta to the sages of Naimiṣa (*Padma Purāṇa*, Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa 1; see [Padma Purāṇa](../../90-literature-corpus/puranas/padma-purana.md)).
- **The Vāmana Purāṇa frame** — that text opens with **Nārada asking Pulastya** how and why Viṣṇu took the dwarf form, and Pulastya answering (*Vāmana Purāṇa* 1; see [Vāmana Purāṇa](../../90-literature-corpus/puranas/vamana-purana.md)).
- **The pattern** — the same sage whose one moment of irritation seeds the epic's antagonist is, elsewhere in the canon, the standing voice of cosmology, dharma and pilgrimage. The corpus notes the juxtaposition without harmonizing it: the tradition does not treat the Tṛṇabindu declaration as a fall. [scholarly]

## Why he matters

- **The genealogical floor of the antagonist** — Rāvaṇa's Vedic learning, his sāma-chanting, his capacity for a ten-thousand-year *tapas* and the brahmin descent that complicates his killing all trace to Pulastya in three steps. The *Rāmāyaṇa* is unusual among epics in giving its villain a pedigree from the creator himself, and this is that pedigree (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 2–10). [scholarly]
- **The two-sided grandfather** — Kubera the Lokapāla and Rāvaṇa the world-conqueror are the same seed two generations down, differing only in which mother. The corpus treats the pairing as the epic's own thesis about descent: lineage supplies power and settles nothing about its use.
- **The unmoved narrator** — Pulastya's Uttara appearances bracket the whole rākṣasa prehistory: he opens it by fathering Viśravas and closes his own part in it by freeing Rāvaṇa from Māhiṣmatī. He never comes to Laṅkā, never counsels his descendants, and takes no part in the war.
