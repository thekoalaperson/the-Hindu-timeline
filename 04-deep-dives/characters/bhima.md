# Bhīma

> Where this sits: a **Dvāpara-era life**, second of the five [Pāṇḍava](../mahabharata/) brothers, spanning the whole [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/) from a strength-marked infancy to the Kurukṣetra War and the final mountain-march. Bhīma belongs to the [Mahābhārata deep-dives](../mahabharata/) and to the [28th Mahāyuga, Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md), at the Dvāpara–[Kali](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) junction.
> Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Parent epic: [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/) · Master index: [the-Hindu-timeline](../../README.md)

**Bhīma** (Sanskrit *Bhīma*, "the terrible / the formidable") is the second-born [Pāṇḍava](../mahabharata/), the **son of [Kuntī](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md) by Vāyu**, the wind-god, and therefore the **half-brother of [Hanumān](../ramayana/)** (also a son of Vāyu). He is the epic's incarnation of **raw physical power, appetite, and ferocious loyalty**: the strongest of the brothers, the family's mace-and-wrestling champion, the slayer of rākṣasas, and the avenger who personally fulfils the **two great oaths of the dice-hall** — to drink **Duḥśāsana's** blood and to break **Duryodhana's** thigh. His names mark facets of that force: **Bhīmasena**; **Vṛkodara** ("wolf-belly", the name the birth-narrative itself uses, CE 1.114.11); **Vāyuputra** ("son of the wind", the narrator's epithet for him as Hanumān answers him, CE 3.147.4; he calls himself *vāyutanaya*, 3.147.3); **Ballava**, his cook-name at Virāṭa's court (CE 4.2.1, 4.7.5).

**How to read the citations.** **CE** = the BORI Critical Edition, cited by book.chapter.verse from the GRETIL e-text. In that edition a **starred passage** (*nnnn) or an **Appendix I** passage (App. I No. n) is material found in some manuscripts but left out of the constituted text. **G** = Ganguli's translation, which follows the vulgate. Its section numbers do not match CE chapter numbers. The "~" figures in the section headings below are rough Ganguli section numbers; the bullets give the exact references. Part VI (the dice game) comes before Part V (the exile) in the story's order.

## I. Birth and the wind-god's strength (Mbh Ādi Parva)

- **Son of Vāyu via the Durvāsa-mantra** - Pāṇḍu cannot father children because of the deer-sage Kindama's curse: he will die if he lies with a woman (CE 1.109). So **Kuntī** uses the **boon-mantra of sage Durvāsas**. After Yudhiṣṭhira's birth, Pāṇḍu says that a kṣatriya must be "foremost in strength" and asks for such a son. Kuntī then summons **Vāyu**, who begets **Bhīma**: the second Pāṇḍava, born after **Yudhiṣṭhira** (by Dharma) and before **Arjuna** (by Indra) (CE 1.114.8–9; G Ādi CXXIII).
  - *Starred passages:* Vāyu arrives "riding a deer", and Kuntī asks for a son "great-bodied, the breaker of every pride" (CE 1.114 *1205, which Ganguli renders).
- **"Foremost of the strong"** - At his birth a bodiless voice proclaims: "this one is born the best of all the strong" (CE 1.114.10). Ganguli has "the foremost of all endued with strength."
- **The shattered rock** - Kuntī is frightened by a tiger and jumps up, forgetting the infant asleep in her lap. He falls onto the mountain rock and **shatters it "into a hundred pieces"** with his body, and is not hurt. Pāṇḍu marvels at it (CE 1.114.11–13; G Ādi CXXIII). The tiger is in the constituted text itself, not a later variant.
  - *Starred passage:* a fuller version places this on the tenth day after the birth. Kuntī is going to worship, Pāṇḍu shoots the tiger with three arrows, and the baby falls from the slope (CE 1.114 *1209).
- **Born on Duryodhana's birthday** - Bhīma is born on the same day as **Duryodhana** (CE 1.114.14; G Ādi CXXIII). This links the two rivals from birth.
- **Half-brother of Hanumān** - Both are **sons of Vāyu**, so Bhīma and **[Hanumān](../ramayana/)** are brothers. The kinship becomes explicit in the Saugandhika episode (see §V).

## II. Childhood, the poisoning, and the Nāga-nectar (Mbh Ādi Parva ~127–128)

- **Boyhood rivalry with the Kauravas** - In games Bhīma overpowers all of Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sons. He drags them by the feet, holds ten of them under water until they are half-drowned, and kicks the trees they climb for fruit so that they fall out. He does this out of boyish rivalry, "not from malice". Duryodhana nevertheless resolves to kill him and then imprison Yudhiṣṭhira and Arjuna (CE 1.119.14–28; G Ādi CXXVIII).
- **Poisoned and thrown into the Gaṅgā** - **Duryodhana** builds pleasure-pavilions for a water-sports outing at **Pramāṇakoṭi** on the Gaṅgā. There Bhīma, exhausted and drowsy, falls asleep. Duryodhana **binds him with creepers and throws him into the deep water**. Bhīma wakes, breaks every bond, and climbs out. Another time snakes are set on him as he sleeps, but their fangs cannot pierce his skin. Duryodhana later puts fresh **kālakūṭa** poison in his food; **Yuyutsu**, Dhṛtarāṣṭra's son by a vaiśya woman, warns the Pāṇḍavas, and Bhīma digests it without harm. Duryodhana, Karṇa and Śakuni keep plotting. The Pāṇḍavas know of it all but stay silent on **Vidura's** advice (CE 1.119.29–43).
  - *Appendix passage:* the version in which Duryodhana feeds Bhīma the kālakūṭa with his own hand at the picnic is CE App. I No. 71. Ganguli's text follows it (G Ādi CXXVIII).
- **The Nāga realm and the strength-nectar** *(CE App. I No. 72; G Ādi CXXVIII–CXXIX)* - In the longer vulgate version the unconscious Bhīma sinks into the **world of the Nāgas**. The serpents' bites **neutralise the plant poison: "the immobile poison was killed by the mobile one"** (CE 1.119 *1312). The Nāga **Āryaka**, Kuntī's father's grandfather, recognises him. The Nāga king **Vāsuki** offers wealth, but Āryaka asks instead that the boy drink the **rasa**, which holds "the strength of a thousand nāgas" in each vessel. Bhīma drains **eight vessels (kuṇḍas)**, each in a single breath, and sleeps. He wakes **on the eighth day**, and the Nāgas tell him he now has **the strength of ten thousand nāgas** ("elephants" in Ganguli). Meanwhile Kuntī, missing him, has turned to Vidura, who tells her to keep silent. Bhīma returns and tells everything, and Yudhiṣṭhira orders him to say nothing.
  - *Note:* the Critical Edition puts this whole episode in an appendix, so it is a widespread **vulgate** tradition rather than constituted text. The constituted CE never takes Bhīma to Nāgaloka.
- **Drona's pupil; the tournament** - Bhīma and Duryodhana become Droṇa's pupils, above all in the mace (CE 1.123.40). At the graduation tournament the two face each other with maces "like rutting elephants". The crowd splits into factions, and Droṇa sends Aśvatthāman to part them (CE 1.124.30–1.125.4; G Ādi CXXXIV). When Karṇa is crowned king of Aṅga, Bhīma mocks him as a charioteer's son who should "take up the whip" (CE 1.127.5–7; G Ādi CXXXIX).
- **The lacquer-house (Jatugṛha/Lākṣāgṛha)** - At **Vāraṇāvata**, Bhīma himself **sets fire to the lac house** at the spot where Purocana sleeps. A Niṣāda woman and her five sons, who had come to a feast there by chance, also die in it (CE 1.136.7–9). The family leaves through the tunnel but is too drowsy and frightened to hurry, so Bhīma **carries all of them**. He takes Kuntī on his shoulder, the twins on his hips and Yudhiṣṭhira and Arjuna by the hands, breaking trees as he goes (CE 1.136.15–19; G Ādi CL).

## III. The forest demons: Hiḍimba, Bakāsura, Kirmīra, Jaṭāsura (Mbh Ādi & Vana Parvas)

- **Hiḍimba slain; marriage to Hiḍimbā** - Bhīma keeps watch in the forest while the others sleep. The man-eating **rākṣasa Hiḍimba** sends his sister **Hiḍimbā** to lure them, but she falls in love with Bhīma. Hiḍimba attacks and they wrestle. Arjuna tells the tired Bhīma to rest and offers to take over. Stung, Bhīma crushes Hiḍimba to the ground, **kills him "like a beast" and breaks him in the middle** (CE 1.142.26–30; G Ādi CLIV–CLVI).
- **Ghaṭotkaca born** - Hiḍimbā pleads her case to Kuntī and Yudhiṣṭhira. Yudhiṣṭhira sets the terms: she may have Bhīma **by day**, but must bring him back **every night** (CE 1.143.16–18). Bhīma agrees to stay with her until she has a son (G Ādi CLVII). Their son **[Ghaṭotkaca](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md)** is born and, "though a child, reached youth" at once, for rākṣasīs conceive and give birth the same day (CE 1.143.31–32). He promises to come whenever his fathers need him (CE 1.143.37). Ghaṭotkaca later dies by **Karṇa's** infallible **Śakti** spear in the night battle of the [Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md). The text adds that Indra created him as Karṇa's foil for this purpose (CE 1.143.38; G Ādi CLVII).
- **Bakāsura of Ekacakrā** - The Pāṇḍavas live disguised as Brāhmaṇas in **Ekacakrā**. The man-eating rākṣasa **Baka** takes, from each household in turn, a cartload of food and the man who brings it. At Kuntī's direction Bhīma takes his host's turn. He **eats the food himself in front of the demon** and ignores Baka's blows to his back while he eats. Then they fight with uprooted trees. Bhīma **presses his knee into Baka's back**, seizes his neck and his waist-cloth, and **bends him double** until he vomits blood and dies (CE 1.151.1–24; G Ādi CLXIV–CLXV).
- **Kirmīra** - On the way into exile the rākṣasa **Kirmīra**, **Baka's brother** and **Hiḍimba's friend**, blocks the Pāṇḍavas' path and vows to avenge both (CE 3.12.22, 3.12.32). He and Bhīma fight with uprooted trees. Bhīma then pins him with a knee to the waist, strangles him, and **kills him "like a beast"** (CE 3.12.42–69). Vidura tells the story to Dhṛtarāṣṭra (CE 3.11–12; G Vana X–XI).
- **Jaṭāsura** - While Bhīma is away hunting, the rākṣasa **Jaṭāsura**, posing as a Brāhmaṇa, seizes the brothers' weapons and carries off **Draupadī and three Pāṇḍavas**: Yudhiṣṭhira and the twins (CE 3.154.4–6). **Sahadeva** wrenches himself free and challenges him (CE 3.154.7, 3.154.27). Bhīma arrives and kills Jaṭāsura in single combat (CE 3.154; G Vana CLVI).
- **Maṇimān and the yakṣas** - This is a **separate and later** episode from the lotus quest (§V). At Draupadī's urging, Bhīma climbs the Gandhamādana peak to Kubera's realm. There he kills the rākṣasa **Maṇimān**, Kubera's friend, and many of his followers (CE 3.157.52–70; G Vana CLIX–CLX).

## IV. Jarāsandha — the wrestling-duel and the "split body" (Mbh Sabhā Parva ~20–24)

- **The tyrant of Magadha** - **Jarāsandha**, king of Magadha, is the father of **Kaṃsa's** wives Asti and Prāpti (CE 2.13.30). He has imprisoned **eighty-six kings**, "fourteen short" of a hundred, to offer to Mahādeva (Śiva) (CE 2.14.19; G Sabhā XIV–XV). He is the chief obstacle to Yudhiṣṭhira's **Rājasūya**.
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa):* the kings he holds number **twenty thousand** (*ayute dve*, BhP 10.70.24).
- **The brāhmaṇa disguise** - **Kṛṣṇa, Bhīma, and Arjuna** enter Magadha unarmed, **in the guise of snātaka Brāhmaṇas** (CE 2.19.21). They reveal themselves and challenge him to single combat, and Jarāsandha **chooses Bhīma** (CE 2.21.3; G Sabhā XXIII). He first has his son Sahadeva consecrated as king (CE 2.20.29).
- **The marathon wrestling-match** - The fight begins on the **first day of Kārttika** and goes on **day and night without rest**. On the **thirteenth** day the two are still locked together, and on the **night of the fourteenth** Jarāsandha stops from exhaustion (CE 2.21.17–18; G Sabhā XXIII).
  - *Starred passage:* some manuscripts make it **fifteen** days (CE 2.21 *0249–*0250). The **Bhāgavata** gives no count: they fight first with maces, then with fists, "without decision" (BhP 10.72.33–39).
- **How Jarāsandha dies — and the "split body"** - In the **constituted CE** the killing is short. Kṛṣṇa remarks that a tired enemy should not be pressed too hard (CE 2.21.19–21). Then he urges Bhīma to show "the strength of Mātariśvan (Vāyu)" (CE 2.22.4). Bhīma **lifts Jarāsandha, whirls him a hundred times, and breaks his back**, crushing him (*babhañja pṛṣṭhe*, CE 2.22.5–6). Ganguli: "pressed his knee against Jarāsandha's backbone and broke his body in twain" (G Sabhā XXIV).
  - *Starred and appendix passages (CE):* in some manuscripts Kṛṣṇa **signals by splitting a twig** (*viṭapaṃ pāṭayann iva*, *0253). Bhīma **tears Jarāsandha in two**, treading on one leg and pulling the other, so that people see two halves with one eye, one ear and one leg each (*0256–*0258). In CE App. I No. 8 the halves **rejoin** and the fight resumes, until Kṛṣṇa splits a **reed** (*naḷa*) and throws the two pieces apart, and Bhīma copies him.
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa):* the twig signal and the tearing apart "from the anus upward", one leg pinned under his foot, are the main account in BhP 10.72.40–44, in verses that match the CE starred lines above (*0253, *0258). This is the account behind the well-known version. Jarāsandha's birth in two halves, joined by the rākṣasī **Jarā**, is told in the Mahābhārata (CE 2.16–17).
- **Result** - The **captive kings are freed** and **Sahadeva**, Jarāsandha's son, is accepted as king of Magadha (CE 2.22.39–45; BhP 10.72.46). The way is clear for the **Rājasūya** ([02 — Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md)).
- **Conquest of the East** - For the Rājasūya, Bhīma marches east. **Śiśupāla** of Cedi receives him as a friend, he defeats Kāśirāja Subandhu, and he **beats Karṇa** in Aṅga before reaching the Lauhitya (Brahmaputra) country (CE 2.26–27).

## V. Exile: the lotus-quest and the meeting with Hanumān (Mbh Vana Parva ~146–157)

- **The Saugandhika flower** - In the Himālaya the wind blows a **thousand-petalled, sun-bright lotus** to Draupadī (CE 3.146.6). She asks for more, and Bhīma sets off to find them, crashing through the forests (CE 3.146; G Vana CXLV–CXLVI).
- **Hanumān blocks the path** - In a vast **plantain grove (kadalīṣaṇḍa)** Bhīma finds an aged monkey lying across the path (CE 3.146.42–73). The monkey says he is too sick to rise and tells Bhīma to step over him. Bhīma refuses out of reverence for the Supreme that dwells in every body, "or I would leap over you as Hanumān leapt the ocean" (CE 3.147.4–9). He then tries to **lift the tail aside and cannot**. Ashamed, he bows and asks who this is. The monkey says he is **Hanumān**, son of Vāyu, Bhīma's brother (CE 3.147.17–24; G Vana CXLVI).
- **The blessing** - At Bhīma's request Hanumān shows **the form in which he crossed the ocean**, towering over the plantain grove "like a second mountain" (CE 3.149.2–5; Ganguli: "the height reached by the Vindhya", G Vana CXLIX). He then embraces Bhīma, restoring his strength (CE 3.150.1–2), and promises to **roar from the flagstaff of Arjuna's chariot** in the coming war (CE 3.150.15; G Vana CL). Finally he points the way to the Saugandhika forest (CE 3.149.22).
- **Kubera's lake** - The lotus pool is guarded by **Krodhavaśa rākṣasas** under Kubera's orders. Bhīma routs them and gathers the lotuses. The survivors report to Kubera, who lets him take them. Yudhiṣṭhira and the others then follow him there (CE 3.151–153; G Vana CLII–CLIII).
  - *Correction:* Maṇimān's death and **Kubera's release from Agastya's curse** belong to the later Gandhamādana episode, not to this lake (§III). Kubera explains the curse. Flying overhead, his friend Maṇimān spat on the head of the sage Agastya. Agastya cursed that Maṇimān and his troops would be killed by a man, and that Kubera would be freed when he saw that man. Bhīma is that man (CE 3.158.47–59; G Vana CLX–CLXI).

## VI. The dice-game and the great vows (Mbh Sabhā Parva ~58–72)

- **The rigged game** - In Hastināpura **Śakuni** plays dice on Duryodhana's behalf. **Yudhiṣṭhira loses kingdom, brothers, himself, and finally Draupadī**. **Duḥśāsana** drags her into the assembly **by the hair** (CE 2.60.22–28) and tries to **strip her** ([02 — Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md)).
- **Bhīma against Yudhiṣṭhira** - Bhīma's first outburst is aimed at his brother: "Sahadeva, bring fire, I will burn your arms" (CE 2.61.6). **Arjuna** rebukes him, and Bhīma relents (CE 2.61.7–10; G Sabhā LXVI).
- **Vow #1 — Duḥśāsana's blood** - During the disrobing Bhīma swears before the assembled kṣatriyas never to reach his ancestors' world **unless he rips open Duḥśāsana's chest in battle and drinks his blood** (CE 2.61.44–46; G Sabhā LXVI).
- **Duryodhana's insult — the bared thigh** - Smiling at Karṇa, **Duryodhana** pulls aside his garment and shows Draupadī **his left thigh** (*savyam ūrum*) (CE 2.63.10–12; G Sabhā LXX).
- **Vow #2 — Duryodhana's thigh** - With reddened eyes Bhīma swears never to reach his ancestors' world **if he does not break that thigh with his mace in the great battle** (CE 2.63.14; G Sabhā LXX).
- **The vows renewed at the departure** - As the Pāṇḍavas leave for exile, Duḥśāsana dances round them crying "Cow! Cow!" (CE 2.68.19). Bhīma **repeats the blood vow** and vows to **kill the Dhārtarāṣṭras in battle**. He then divides the killing: "I will kill Duryodhana, Arjuna Karṇa, Sahadeva Śakuni". He adds that he will kill Duryodhana with his mace and **stand with his foot on his head** (CE 2.68.20–29; G Sabhā LXXVI). This last vow foreshadows the kick in §IX.
  - *Not in the CE vows:* a separate vow **to tear off the arm that seized Draupadī's hair** does not appear among Bhīma's dice-hall oaths. It surfaces only in starred passages at Duḥśāsana's death (§VIII).
- **Into exile** - The brothers leave for **twelve years in the forest and a thirteenth year in hiding**, carrying these oaths. Bhīma walks out flexing his great arms, as a sign of what he will do (CE 2.71.3).

## VII. The Kīcaka episode (Mbh Virāṭa Parva ~14–24)

- **Disguised as the cook Ballava** - In the **thirteenth year**, spent in hiding at King **Virāṭa's** Matsya court, Bhīma serves as the royal cook **Ballava** (CE 4.2.1, 4.7.1–8; Ganguli writes "Vallabha", G Virāṭa II, and "Vallava", G Virāṭa VIII). He also kills the famous wrestler **Jīmūta** in the arena (CE 4.12.16–25; G Virāṭa XIII).
- **Kīcaka's assault on Draupadī** - **Kīcaka**, Queen Sudeṣṇā's brother and the commander of Virāṭa's army, lusts after Draupadī, who is disguised as the maid **Sairandhrī**. When she flees to the court, he **throws her down and kicks her in front of the king** (CE 4.15.7; G Virāṭa XVI). At night she goes to Bhīma in the kitchen and begs for vengeance (CE 4.16–20).
- **The night ambush** - Draupadī sets a meeting with Kīcaka in the empty **dancing hall (nartanāgāra)**. Bhīma waits there in her place, wrestles Kīcaka, and pounds him into **"a lump of flesh"** (*māṃsapiṇḍa*, CE 4.21.60), with his limbs driven into his body **"like a tortoise"** (CE 4.22.2; G Virāṭa XXII).
- **The Upakīcakas** - Kīcaka's kinsmen try to **burn Sairandhrī on his pyre**. Bhīma, in disguise, uproots a tree and kills all **105 of them** (*śataṃ pañcādhikam*, CE 4.22.25–29; G Virāṭa XXIII, printed in Ganguli as a second "XXII").

## VIII. The Kurukṣetra War — slaying the hundred Kauravas (Mbh Bhīṣma–Śalya Parvas)

- **The unexpected peace speech** - Before Kṛṣṇa's embassy, Bhīma surprises everyone by asking him to **speak only of peace**: "we will follow Duryodhana humbly, lest the Bharatas perish." Kṛṣṇa, amused at this mildness, "as unheard-of as lightness in a mountain", taunts him back into his warrior temper (CE 5.72–73; [Udyoga Parva](../mahabharata/05-udyoga-parva.md)).
- **The mace champion** - Bhīma and Duryodhana are **both mace pupils of Balarāma** (CE 9.54.23), and both earlier trained under Droṇa (CE 1.123.40). Bhīma is the Pāṇḍavas' shock-fighter, though he also fights as an archer and against elephants. Early in the war he destroys the **Kaliṅga** division. Among the dead are the Kaliṅga prince Śakradeva, whom his mace kills, and the Kaliṅga king (Śrutāyus) and Ketumat (CE 6.50.21–22, 6.50.112).
- **He kills the hundred sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra** - The epic credits Bhīma with killing Dhṛtarāṣṭra's hundred sons. Dhṛtarāṣṭra fears it early on: "Bhīma will kill all my sons, no doubt" (CE 6.61.9). Gāndhārī says it afterwards: "killing the hundred sons of this old man, why did you not spare even one?" (CE 11.14.20). Many fall to him in the duels of the fourteenth day (CE 7.106–114) and the last ones in the Śalya Parva. The son Dhṛtarāṣṭra had by a vaiśya woman, **Yuyutsu**, crosses to the Pāṇḍava side before the fighting begins (CE 6.41.90–95) and survives the war (CE 9.28.75–92).
- **Karṇa spares him** - On the fourteenth day Karṇa leaves Bhīma weaponless. Remembering his promise to Kuntī, he **does not kill him**. Instead he pokes him with the tip of his bow and taunts him: "beardless", "fool", "glutton" (*tūbaraka … audarika*) (CE 7.114.67–69; G Droṇa CXXXVIII).
- **Other kills** - Bhīma's mace takes off the head of the old Kuru **Bāhlīka** (CE 7.132.15). On the fifteenth day he kills an elephant named **Aśvatthāman** and, ashamed, shouts to Droṇa that "Aśvatthāman is slain". This sets up Droṇa's death (CE 7.164.71–73; [Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md)).
- **The death of Duḥśāsana — the blood-oath fulfilled** - On the **seventeenth day** Bhīma's mace wrecks Duḥśāsana's chariot and fells him. Bhīma leaps down, plants his foot on Duḥśāsana's throat, draws his sword, **rips open his chest and drinks his warm blood**. He declares it sweeter than "mother's milk, honey, ghee, wine… milk and curds" (CE 8.61.3–7; G Karṇa 83). He then recites the old wrongs, from the poisoning at Pramāṇakoṭi to the dice. He turns to Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna: one vow is paid, and Duryodhana, "the second victim", is next (CE 8.61.11–16). Onlookers flee, crying "this is no man" (CE 8.61.10). ([08 — Karṇa Parva](../mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md)).
  - *Starred and appendix passages:* the scene where Bhīma **tears off the arm "that seized Draupadī's hair"** is in CE 8.61 *0935 and App. I No. 32, not in the constituted text. It is absent from Ganguli's section 83 as well.
  - *Bhīma's own account (Strī Parva):* Gāndhārī reproaches him for drinking a kinsman's blood. Bhīma answers that **the blood did not pass his lips and teeth** — "only my hands were red with it, as Yama knows". He did it to terrify the enemy when Vṛṣasena had just killed Nakula's horses, and to keep his dice-hall vow (CE 11.14.12–18; G Strī 15).
  - *Later literature:* the image of Draupadī's hair bound up again with Kaurava blood is the theme of **Bhaṭṭa Nārāyaṇa's** Sanskrit drama ***Veṇīsaṃhāra*** ("The Binding of the Braid"; date disputed, often placed in the 7th–8th c. CE). There the vow is mainly **Bhīma's**, and in the last act he ties her hair with a hand still wet with **Duryodhana's** blood [late text] (Hiltebeitel). The widespread version in which **Draupadī washes her hair in Duḥśāsana's blood** belongs to folk theatre and modern retellings. It is **not in the critical text** [folk].

## IX. The mace-duel with Duryodhana and the thigh-blow (Mbh Śalya Parva ~31–60)

- **Duryodhana's last stand** - Once his army is destroyed, **Duryodhana hides in Lake Dvaipāyana**, holding the waters still by magic (CE 9.28.52, 9.29.61). The Pāṇḍavas taunt him out. Yudhiṣṭhira rashly offers to let him fight **any one** of them with any weapon and keep the kingdom if he wins. Duryodhana chooses the mace and says he will fight any one of them: "Sahadeva, Bhīma or Nakula", or Arjuna, or Yudhiṣṭhira himself (CE 9.31.24–58; G Śalya 32). Kṛṣṇa rebukes Yudhiṣṭhira, since only Bhīma could match Duryodhana and even that is uncertain. **Bhīma steps forward** (CE 9.32.1–29; G Śalya 33).
- **Balarāma presides** - **Balarāma**, teacher of both fighters, returns from his pilgrimage to watch (CE 9.33–34; G Śalya 34). The duel then moves to **Samantapañcaka** (CE 9.54.4–7). In the vulgate it is Balarāma who proposes the move (CE 9.54 *0313; G Śalya 55).
- **Duryodhana the better technician** - Arjuna asks Kṛṣṇa which of the two is better. Kṛṣṇa answers that **their training is equal and Bhīma is stronger, but Duryodhana has practised harder**: "fighting fairly, Bhīma will not win; fighting unfairly, he may kill him" (CE 9.57.3–4; G Śalya 58). Duryodhana has practised the mace for thirteen years (CE 9.57.16), against an iron figure of Bhīma (CE 9.32.4).
- **The thigh-signal — given by Arjuna** - Kṛṣṇa reminds Arjuna of Bhīma's dice-hall vow. **Arjuna then slaps his own thigh** where Bhīma can see it, and Bhīma takes the hint (*hastenorum atāḍayat*, CE 9.57.18; G Śalya 58: "Dhananjaya struck his own left thigh"). Later the dying Duryodhana accuses Kṛṣṇa of prompting the hint through Arjuna (G Śalya 61).
  - *Popular retellings* often have **Kṛṣṇa himself** slap his thigh. In the text the signal comes from Arjuna, on Kṛṣṇa's advice.
- **The thigh-blow below the navel** - Duryodhana leaps up to dodge, and Bhīma **hurls his mace at his thighs**, breaking both (CE 9.57.41–44; G Śalya 58). This fulfils Vow #2. Balarāma protests that "**one must not strike below the navel** — that is the rule of the science" (*adho nābhyā na hantavyam*, CE 9.59.5–6).
- **The kick and Balarāma's fury** - Bhīma **puts his left foot on the fallen king's head**, as he had vowed (CE 9.58.5, 9.58.12; cf. 2.68.28). The Somaka leaders do not applaud this (CE 9.58.13), and **Yudhiṣṭhira** rebukes him: "do not crush his head with your foot" (CE 9.58.15). **Balarāma raises his plough** to attack Bhīma. **Kṛṣṇa** holds him back and defends the blow: Bhīma had to keep his vow, the sage **Maitreya** had once cursed Duryodhana to have his thighs broken by Bhīma, and "know that the **Kali age** has come" (CE 9.59.7–21; G Śalya 60). Unconvinced, Balarāma says that Bhīma will be known as a "crooked fighter" and goes back to Dvārakā (CE 9.59.22–26).
  - *The text's own verdict:* the Pāṇḍavas are troubled that Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Karṇa, Bhūriśravas and now Duryodhana have all fallen by unfair means. Kṛṣṇa admits that **none of them could have been killed in a fair fight** and that he devised these stratagems himself (CE 9.60.55–61; G Śalya 61).
- **Aftermath** - Before he dies, Duryodhana makes **Aśvatthāman** commander (CE 9.64.38–40), and Aśvatthāman's night massacre of the Pāṇḍava camp follows ([10 — Sauptika Parva](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md)). Draupadī demands the gem on Aśvatthāman's head. Bhīma alone sets out after him, with Nakula as his charioteer (CE 10.11.20–28). Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna follow, and when the gem is surrendered **Bhīma gives it to Draupadī** (CE 10.16.25–26).

## X. Later life, the great departure, and themes

- **The iron statue** - When the Pāṇḍavas meet the blind **Dhṛtarāṣṭra** after the war, he embraces Yudhiṣṭhira and then reaches for Bhīma, meaning to crush him. **Kṛṣṇa** has foreseen this and puts an **iron statue of Bhīma** in his arms instead, and the old king crushes it (CE 11.11.13–18; G Strī 12). Kṛṣṇa then reproves him (CE 11.12).
- **Crown prince** - At his coronation Yudhiṣṭhira names Bhīma **yuvarāja**, the heir-apparent (CE 12.41.8; G Śānti XLII).
- **Harshness toward Dhṛtarāṣṭra** - During the old king's years at court, Bhīma never forgives him; the twins, knowing his mind, quietly approve (CE 15.4.14). Behind Yudhiṣṭhira's back he does things to hurt him and **slaps his arms** in front of friends while boasting of the Kauravas he has killed. This helps drive Dhṛtarāṣṭra to leave for the forest (CE 15.4.2–14). Bhīma also objects to giving him treasury wealth for the Kauravas' funeral rites. Yudhiṣṭhira tells him to be quiet, and Arjuna offers the wealth from his own treasury (CE 15.17.7–15.18.4). ([Āśramavāsika Parva](../mahabharata/15-ashramavasika-parva.md)).
- **The great departure (Mahāprasthāna)** - On the final Himālayan march the falls come in a fixed order: **Draupadī, Sahadeva, Nakula, Arjuna, and then Bhīma**, the **last of the four brothers to fall**, leaving Yudhiṣṭhira alone with the dog. Bhīma asks why he has fallen. Yudhiṣṭhira answers: "**you ate too much, and you boasted of your strength**, without regard for others" (*anavekṣya param*). Ganguli renders the last clause as "never attended to the wants of others while eating" (CE 17.2.23–25; G Mahāprasthānika 2; [17 — Mahāprasthānika Parva](../mahabharata/17-mahaprasthanika-parva.md)).
- **Wives and sons** - **Hiḍimbā** (son **Ghaṭotkaca**); **Draupadī**, shared with his brothers (son **Sutasoma**, CE 1.57.102, 1.90.82); and **Baladharā** of Kāśī, won with a bride-price of valour (*vīryaśulkā*; Ganguli "Valandhara") (son **Sarvaga**, CE 1.90.84; G Ādi XCV).
- **Themes** - Bhīma embodies **bala (might), bhakṣaṇa (appetite), and pratijñā (the binding vow)**. He is the brother who **acts** while the others deliberate. His most terrible deeds, the blood-drinking and the thigh-blow, are framed by the text as vows kept (CE 9.59.14, 11.14.17–18) and are still judged by Balarāma, Yudhiṣṭhira and Gāndhārī. That makes them the epic's hardest tests of when force becomes dharma.
- **Afterlife in tradition** - Bhīma remains a favourite strongman of folk theatre and of regional and Himālayan cults, where he is honoured as a patron of wrestlers [folk].
