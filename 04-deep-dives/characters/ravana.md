# Rāvaṇa

> The ten-headed **rākṣasa** emperor of **Laṅkā** — supreme antagonist of the [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md), abductor of **Sītā**, conqueror of the three worlds, and yet a peerless **Śiva-devotee**, Vedic scholar, vīṇā-player, and Sāma-veda chanter. A study in *tapas* misused: brilliance, learning, and devotion yoked to lust and pride.
> Sits in **[Tretā-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md). His life is narrated across the [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md) and his ancestry/back-story in the [Uttara-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md).
> Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md) · The curse-frame that includes him: [Jaya & Vijaya](../jaya-vijaya.md).

**Rāvaṇa** (Sanskrit *Rāvaṇa*, "He Who Makes (the Worlds) Roar / Scream" — caus. of √*ru*) is the ten-headed (**Daśagrīva, Daśānana**), twenty-armed king of the rākṣasas, ruler of golden **Laṅkā**. Born of a **brāhmaṇa father and a rākṣasī mother**, he is a hybrid: heir to the *tapas*-power and Vedic mastery of the sage-line of **Pulastya**, and to the demonic ferocity of his mother's people. By terrific austerity he won near-invincibility from **Brahmā**, seized the three worlds, drove out his half-brother **Kubera** and took the flying chariot **Puṣpaka**, and humbled gods, gandharvas, and kings — until his abduction of **Sītā** brought down upon him the avatāra **[Rāma](../ramayana/README.md)**. His many names — **Daśagrīva, Daśānana, Rāvaṇa, Laṅkeśvara, Paulastya, Daśakaṇṭha** — each mark a face of that doom. The Rāmāyaṇa is unusual in dignifying its villain: Vālmīki grants him grandeur, scholarship, and even Rāma's posthumous respect.

## I. Ancestry and the Pulastya–Viśravas line (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa)

- **Pulastya** - One of the ten **Prajāpatis** / mind-born sons (*mānasa-putra*) of **Brahmā**, and one of the **[Saptarṣi](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md)** — Rāvaṇa's paternal great-grandfather, the fount of the rākṣasa-brāhmaṇa line (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 2–9).
- **Viśravas (Viśrava)** - Son of Pulastya by the daughter of **Tṛṇabindu**, conceived when the sage — disturbed at his austerities by the hermitage maidens — declared that whichever of them heard him would conceive; the boy is named "Viśravas" *because he was heard* (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 2). A great sage in his own right, he is father of both **Kubera** (by his first wife **Ilavidā/Devavarṇinī**, Bharadvāja's daughter) and of Rāvaṇa's brood (by Kaikasī). The shared father makes **Kubera Rāvaṇa's elder half-brother** (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 3–9).
- **Kaikasī** - Daughter of the rākṣasa king **Sumālī** (son of **Sukeśa**); sent by her father to Viśravas to bear powerful sons who would restore rākṣasa fortunes. She approaches the sage at an inauspicious twilight hour — a *doṣa* that Viśravas warns will taint her firstborn with cruelty (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 9).
- **The four children of Viśravas & Kaikasī**, in the order Uttara-kāṇḍa 9 gives them -
  - **Rāvaṇa (Daśagrīva)** — born with **ten heads and twenty arms**; the sky reportedly rained blood and ill omens marked his birth.
  - **Kumbhakarṇa** — the giant brother, whose asked-for sleep belongs to Uttara 10 and whose six-month cycle belongs to Yuddha 61 (§III).
  - **Śūrpaṇakhā** — third-born, of hideous aspect; the sister whose mutilation by Lakṣmaṇa ignites the war ([Araṇya-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md)).
  - **Vibhīṣaṇa** — last-born, and the only one whose birth brings a rain of flowers and celestial drums: the righteous, dharma-loving brother who defects to Rāma.
- **Maternal rākṣasa line** - Through Sumālī and Sukeśa, Rāvaṇa inherits the older rākṣasa dynasty once led by **Mālyavān, Sumālī, Mālī** — the original lords of Laṅkā driven out by Viṣṇu before Kubera held it (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 5–8). See [Beings & Bestiary — rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/README.md).

## II. The ten heads — meanings and variants

- **Daśagrīva / Daśānana** - "Ten-Necks / Ten-Faces." The default Rāmāyaṇa portrayal: ten heads and twenty arms, a monstrous birth-form (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 9).
- *Variant (allegorical):* the ten heads represent **mastery of the four Vedas and six Śāstras** (Vedāṅgas) — i.e. Rāvaṇa as the supreme scholar. [folk/devotional]
- *Variant (allegorical):* the ten heads symbolize ten vices — **kāma, krodha, moha, lobha, mada, mātsarya** (lust, anger, delusion, greed, pride, envy) plus manas, buddhi, citta, ahaṅkāra — the inner enemies a devotee must conquer at **Daśaharā / Vijayadaśamī**. [folk]
- *Variant (nine heads):* he is sometimes shown with **nine** heads, having **cut one off** during his austerities (see §III). [variant]
- *Variant (self-multiplied):* some Śaiva tellings make the heads a yogic feat — heads he could regrow, tied to the boon (see §VI on his death). [late text]

## III. The tapas and Brahmā's boon (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 10)

- **Eviction by Viśravas; the brothers' austerities** - Counselled (in some tellings by their grandfather) to win power the lawful way, the three brothers undertake fierce **tapas**. Rāvaṇa performs penance for a vast span (commonly **10,000 years**) (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 10).
- **The self-decapitation offering** - To force Brahmā's hand, Rāvaṇa **severs his own heads one by one** and offers them into the fire; as he is about to offer the tenth (last) head, **Brahmā appears** and stops him, restoring the offered heads (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 10; widely retold).
- **The boon — invincibility with a fatal omission** - Rāvaṇa asks for **immortality**; refused, he instead names the classes he is to be unslayable by: **suparṇas, nāgas, yakṣas, daityas, dānavas, rākṣasas, and devas** — the seven-fold roster of Uttara-kāṇḍa 10 — but, in his contempt, **omits men and animals**, thinking such lowly creatures could never threaten him. This omission is the hinge of the whole epic: only a **human (Rāma)** aided by **vānaras** can kill him.
  - **The rest of the grant** - Brahmā **restores the ten heads** Rāvaṇa had offered into the fire, and adds the power to **assume any form at will** (Uttara 10). The heads that regrow as fast as Rāma cuts them in the final duel are the working-out of this on the battlefield (Yuddha 107), not a separate boon.
  - *Roster note:* later retellings expand or contract the list (adding gandharvas, piśācas, or "all celestial and demonic beings"); Uttara 10's own seven classes are the text's, and neither gandharvas nor piśācas is among them.
- **Kumbhakarṇa's boon goes wrong** - At the gods' plea **Sarasvatī enters Kumbhakarṇa's mouth** and speaks in his place, so that when Brahmā offers the boon he asks for *nidrā* — **sleep** — and Brahmā grants it as asked. Vālmīki's Uttara 10 states no intent behind the request and attaches **no waking clause**: it is a plain slip of the tongue, and it is the older layer of the story. The gloss that he *meant* to ask *nirdevatvam* ("a world without gods"), and the commuted term of **six months' sleep to one day's waking**, belong to a separate and later passage, Vibhīṣaṇa's account at **Yuddha-kāṇḍa 61**. Vibhīṣaṇa asks only for steadfast dharma. Full treatment: [Kumbhakarṇa](./kumbhakarna.md).

## IV. Conquest of the three worlds (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 11–34)

- **Taking Laṅkā — cession, then war** - Two distinct beats the retellings usually fuse. First, **Kubera cedes Laṅkā without a fight**: warned by Sumālī's embassy and counselled by their common father **Viśravas** to yield to his brother rather than provoke him, Kubera withdraws to Mount Kailāsa and Rāvaṇa enters the golden city as its lord (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 11). Only later, when Kubera sends an envoy urging him to abandon adharma, does Rāvaṇa **kill the envoy** and march on Kailāsa: the **yakṣa war** follows, Kubera is defeated and struck down by Rāvaṇa's mace, and only then is the **Puṣpaka** taken as spoil (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 13–15).
- **The Puṣpaka Vimāna** - Kubera's **flying chariot** — a self-moving aerial car that travels at the speed of thought, expands to any size, and obeys its rider; seized in the yakṣa war, it becomes Rāvaṇa's signature vehicle. Returned to Kubera after the war, it is **re-gifted by Kubera to Rāma** with the clause that it will come whenever he thinks of it (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 41). See [Artifacts — vimānas](../../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/README.md).
- **Digvijaya — conquest of the directions** - Rāvaṇa subdues yakṣas, gandharvas, nāgas, and kings across the worlds, and binds up the **Lokapālas** (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 11–34). His son **Meghanāda** defeats **Indra** and earns the name **Indrajit** ("Conqueror of Indra").
- **Marutta's sacrifice** - Rāvaṇa descends on the sacrifice of King **Marutta**, and the attending gods, rather than face him, hide themselves in animal shapes — **Indra a peacock, Dharmarāja (Yama) a crow, Kubera a chameleon, Varuṇa a swan**. Marutta would fight, but his officiant **Saṃvarta** forbids it lest the rite be broken, and Rāvaṇa leaves claiming the victory. Afterwards each god blesses the creature whose form sheltered him: the peacock's eyed tail, the crow's long life, the swan's whiteness, the chameleon's gold (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 18).
- **The Yamaloka war** - He storms the realm of **Yama** and fights him for seven days; when Yama at last raises the **kāla-daṇḍa** (the rod of Death, which cannot fail), **Brahmā appears and forbids the stroke** — the boon he himself gave would be falsified, and the Grandsire made a liar. Yama withholds the weapon and vanishes from the field, leaving Rāvaṇa to claim the victory and depart in the Puṣpaka. His "defeat of Death" is thus a stopped fight, not a kill (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 21–22).
- **The nāgas and the Nivātakavacas** - He descends into Rasātala and subdues the nāga city **Bhogavatī**; against the **Nivātakavacas** he is fought to a **year-long stalemate**, at the end of which **Brahmā brokers a pact of friendship** — and Rāvaṇa learns from them the **hundred māyās** (illusion-arts) his house deploys in the war. The same campaign carries him on against **Varuṇa's** sons and grandsons (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 23).
- **The Kālakeya slaughter** - In the same sweep he destroys the **Kālakeya** host, among them his sister **Śūrpaṇakhā's** husband **Vidyujjihva** — the widowing (by her own brother's hand) that leads to her consolation and her posting to Daṇḍaka with Khara (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 23–24).
- **Madhu, and the death of Sumālī** - Learning that the rākṣasa **Madhu** of Madhuvana has carried off **Kumbhīnasī** — Mālyavān's granddaughter, reckoned Rāvaṇa's own sister — he marches on Madhupura, but spares Madhu at her plea and takes him as an ally instead (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 25). That forbearance leaves Madhu's son **Lavaṇa** alive for Śatrughna to kill generations later. His maternal grandfather **Sumālī**, who set the whole dynastic project in motion, is crushed by the Vasu **Savitṛ** in the war on Indraloka (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 27).
- **Lifting Mount Kailāsa** - The Puṣpaka is halted over **Mount Kailāsa** and **Nandī** bars the way; Rāvaṇa mocks his monkey-face, and Nandī curses him that **monkeys of his own shape, of prodigious strength, will be born to destroy Rāvaṇa and his race** — the epic's earliest sentence of the vānara host upon him. Rāvaṇa then tries to **uproot the mountain**; **Śiva** presses it down with a toe, crushing his arms beneath it. Trapped, he **cries out so that the three worlds tremble**, and laments a thousand years, until Śiva releases him, names him **Rāvaṇa** ("he who makes the worlds cry out"), and grants him the sword **Candrahāsa** ("moon-laugh"). This is a full, developed episode of the core text, not an allusion (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 16).
  - *Variant:* the **Rāvaṇānugraha** ("Śiva's grace to Rāvaṇa") iconography, and the elaboration of the thousand-year lament into the singing of the *Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra*, are Purāṇic and Tamil Śaiva developments on the Uttara 16 base. [late text/regional]
- **Curbed by mightier kings** - The Rāmāyaṇa concedes Rāvaṇa was not unbeaten: he was **captured by Kārtavīrya Arjuna (Sahasrārjuna)**, the thousand-armed Haihaya king, and bound until released; and was **humbled by the vānara king Vālī**, who tucked him under his arm during his sandhyā worship (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 32–34). These foreshadow that a non-divine being can subdue him.
- **Wives and sons** - Chief queen **Mandodarī** (daughter of the asura-architect **Maya** and the apsarā Hemā) — virtuous and loyal to the end. Sons include **Meghanāda/Indrajit**, **Akṣayakumāra**, **Atikāya**, **Narāntaka**, **Devāntaka**, **Triśira** — most slain in the [Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md). The *Sundara-kāṇḍa* describes a vast harem.

## V. The curses that doomed him — Vedavatī, Rambhā/Nalakūbara, Puñjikasthalā, Jaya-Vijaya

- **Vedavatī → Sītā** - A chaste ascetic woman (daughter of the sage **Kuśadhvaja**), doing *tapas* to win **Viṣṇu** as husband, is accosted by Rāvaṇa, who seizes her by the hair. She **cuts off the defiled hair, enters the fire**, and curses that she will be **reborn to cause his destruction** — reborn as **Sītā** (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 17; elaborated in later texts). Full treatment of the sarga and the rebirth-identification: [Vedavatī](./vedavati.md).
  - *Variant:* a strand of tradition makes **Sītā literally Vedavatī reborn** and even identifies her as Rāvaṇa's own daughter (abandoned), heightening the tragic irony. [folk/regional]
- **Rambhā & Nalakūbara's curse** - Rāvaṇa **rapes the apsarā Rambhā**, who was betrothed to **Nalakūbara** (Kubera's son). Nalakūbara curses that **if Rāvaṇa ever again forces himself on an unwilling woman, his head will shatter into seven pieces** (*saptadhā*) (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 26).
- **Brahmā's curse over Puñjikasthalā** - A **second and separate** curse to the same effect, and the one Rāvaṇa himself invokes. He had assaulted the apsarā **Puñjikasthalā** on her way to Brahmā's court; Brahmā cursed that if he ever again forced a woman unwilling, **his head would burst into a hundred pieces** (*śatadhā*). Rāvaṇa confesses this in his own war-council, in his own voice, as the reason he cannot compel Sītā to his bed (Rām. Yuddha-kāṇḍa 13.11–15). This — not the Nalakūbara curse — is the *war-book* answer to why the captive is never touched, and the two are distinct curses with distinct terms, not two names for one.
- **Nandī's curse** - At Kailāsa, mocked for his monkey-face, Nandī curses that **monkeys of prodigious strength will be born to destroy Rāvaṇa and his race** (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 16; §IV above).
- **Anaraṇya's curse** - The Ikṣvāku king **Anaraṇya**, dying under Rāvaṇa's hand, curses that **a son born in his own line will kill him** — the Solar-dynasty warrant for Rāma. Full treatment: [Anaraṇya & Rāvaṇa's curse](../../03-lineages-vamsha/anaranya-ravana-curse.md) (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 19).
- **The abducted women's collective curse** - The chaste wives Rāvaṇa carried off and confined in Laṅkā curse him together: **a woman shall be the cause of his destruction** (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 24). Four independent curses, each naming a different instrument — a woman, a king's descendant, monkeys, his own lust — converge on the war.
  - *Variant (cow/sage curses):* regional tellings multiply further minor curses earned during his conquests. [folk]
- **The Jaya–Vijaya frame** - The *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* overlays a theological cause: Rāvaṇa and Kumbhakarṇa are **Jaya and Vijaya**, Viṣṇu's Vaikuṇṭha gatekeepers, in the **2nd of three demonic births** (Hiraṇyakaśipu/Hiraṇyākṣa → Rāvaṇa/Kumbhakarṇa → Śiśupāla/Dantavakra), so that Rāvaṇa's death is a cursed soul's return to God by *dveṣa-bhakti* (devotion-through-hatred) (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 7.1, 7.10). Full treatment: [Jaya & Vijaya](../jaya-vijaya.md).
  - *Variant:* the Rāmāyaṇa itself does **not** know this frame — it gives Rāvaṇa a self-contained Pulastya ancestry; the gatekeeper-overlay is retrofitted by the Purāṇas. [late text]
  - *Pairing note:* usually **Jaya → Rāvaṇa, Vijaya → Kumbhakarṇa**.

## VI. The war and death (Rām. Yuddha-kāṇḍa)

- **The abduction** - Provoked by Śūrpaṇakhā's mutilation and inflamed by her account of Sītā's beauty, Rāvaṇa has **Mārīca** take the golden-deer form to lure Rāma away, then **abducts Sītā**. The vehicle is a **golden chariot yoked to mules**, never named Puṣpaka; **Jaṭāyu** shatters it and Rāvaṇa completes the flight carrying her bodily (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 35, 49, 51–52). See [Araṇya-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md).
- **Sītā in Aśoka-vana** - Held captive but unviolated — by his own account it is **Brahmā's Puñjikasthalā curse** that stays his hand (§V) — Sītā refuses him under a **twelve-month ultimatum** (Araṇya 56); **Hanumān** finds her in the [Sundara-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md) and burns Laṅkā.
- **Vibhīṣaṇa's defection** - Counselling Rāvaṇa to return Sītā and avoid ruin, the righteous brother **Vibhīṣaṇa** is spurned and exiled; he joins Rāma and reveals Rāvaṇa's secrets ([Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md)).
- **The fall of his champions** - Kumbhakarṇa is roused and slain; **Indrajit**, master of the **Brahmāstra** and invisibility (and the Nāgāstra that fells Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa), is finally killed by **Lakṣmaṇa** — breaking Rāvaṇa's last great hope ([Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md)).
- **Rāma vs. Rāvaṇa — the final duel** - In a cosmic battle Rāma severs Rāvaṇa's heads, but **they regrow** as fast as cut (Yuddha 107). **Agastya** teaches Rāma the **Āditya-hṛdaya** hymn for strength. At last **Mātali**, Indra's charioteer, reminds Rāma of the arrow he is carrying — the shaft **made by Brahmā and given to Rāma on the field by Agastya**, wind in its feathers, sun and fire in its head, the weight of Meru in its body (Yuddha 108.1–4). Rāma charges it with the Brahmā-mantra and looses it: it **strikes Rāvaṇa in the chest and tears out his heart**, and, its work done, **returns silently to Rāma's quiver** (Yuddha 108.17–20). There is no amṛta and no navel anywhere in the sarga.
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas and regional Rāmāyaṇas):* the nectar of immortality is lodged in **Rāvaṇa's navel**, and Vibhīṣaṇa's disclosure of it sends the arrow there to dry it up. This is the famous popular image, and it is **not Vālmīki's** — his weapon takes the heart. [variant]
- **A dignified end** - Dying, Rāvaṇa is honored: **Rāma sends Lakṣmaṇa to Rāvaṇa's deathbed to learn statecraft and wisdom** from the fallen sage-king (a beloved folk episode), and orders him **cremated with full rites**, telling Vibhīṣaṇa that "enmity ends with death." [folk/regional for the Lakṣmaṇa-lesson]
- **Aftermath** - Vibhīṣaṇa is crowned king of Laṅkā; Sītā's chastity is affirmed (the **Agni-parīkṣā**); Rāma flies home in the recovered **Puṣpaka** for the coronation that founds **Rāma-rājya** (return narrated in [Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md)).

## VII. The other Rāvaṇa — devotee, scholar, artist

- **Parama-Śiva-bhakta** - Rāvaṇa is among the greatest devotees of **[Śiva](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md)**: the Kailāsa episode (§IV), his lifelong worship, and his composition of the **Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra** define him as much as his villainy. The Śaiva *Rāvaṇānugraha* ("Śiva's grace to Rāvaṇa") is a major iconographic theme.
- **Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra** - The thunderous Sanskrit hymn (in *pañcacāmara* metre) praising Śiva's cosmic dance — traditionally attributed to Rāvaṇa, sung (in one tradition) while pinned beneath Kailāsa, or built from a **vīṇā fashioned from one of his severed heads strung with his own sinews** (the **Rāvaṇahatthā / "Rāvaṇa-hasta vīṇā"**, a bowed instrument named for him). [devotional; Tamil Śaiva *Tēvāram* tradition]
- **The Ātma-liṅga** - Pleased, Śiva grants Rāvaṇa the **Ātma-liṅga** to carry to Laṅkā, on condition it never touch the ground; **Gaṇeśa** (as a boy) tricks Rāvaṇa into setting it down at **Gokarṇa** (Karnataka), where it roots forever as **Mahābaleśvara** (Sthala-purāṇa of Gokarṇa). [folk/regional Sthala-purāṇa]
- **Master of the Vedas** - A learned brāhmaṇa by his father's line: reputed master of the **four Vedas and six Vedāṅgas**, a great **Sāma-veda chanter**, and (in later tradition) author of works on **Ayurveda (Arka-prakāśa), astrology, and the Śaiva āgamas** — even **Rāvaṇa-saṃhitā** astrological texts bear his name. [late attributions]
- **Yajña-officiant for Rāma** - In a striking irony of some retellings, Rāma asks the dying or living Rāvaṇa (the supreme brāhmaṇa-scholar) to **officiate the pre-war yajña** to consecrate the bridge / worship Śiva at Rāmeśvaram. [folk]
- **Regional veneration** - Rāvaṇa is honored, not burned, in pockets of India and Sri Lanka — e.g. among some communities in Madhya Pradesh (Bisrakh, claimed birthplace), Karnataka, and as a king/ancestor figure in Sri Lankan tradition. [regional]
