# Rāvaṇa

> The ten-headed **rākṣasa** emperor of **Laṅkā** — supreme antagonist of the [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md), abductor of **Sītā**, conqueror of the three worlds, and yet a peerless **Śiva-devotee**, Vedic scholar, vīṇā-player, and Sāma-veda chanter. A study in *tapas* misused: brilliance, learning, and devotion yoked to lust and pride.
> Sits in **[Tretā-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md). His life is narrated across the [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md) and his ancestry/back-story in the [Uttara-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md).
> Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md) · The curse-frame that includes him: [Jaya & Vijaya](../jaya-vijaya.md).

**Rāvaṇa** (Sanskrit *Rāvaṇa*, "He Who Makes (the Worlds) Roar / Scream" — caus. of √*ru*) is the ten-headed (**Daśagrīva, Daśānana**), twenty-armed king of the rākṣasas, ruler of golden **Laṅkā**. Born of a **brāhmaṇa father and a rākṣasī mother**, he is a hybrid: heir to the *tapas*-power and Vedic mastery of the sage-line of **Pulastya**, and to the demonic ferocity of his mother's people. By terrific austerity he won near-invincibility from **Brahmā**, seized the three worlds, drove out his half-brother **Kubera** and took the flying chariot **Puṣpaka**, and humbled gods, gandharvas, and kings — until his abduction of **Sītā** brought down upon him the avatāra **[Rāma](../ramayana/README.md)**. His many names — **Daśagrīva, Daśānana, Rāvaṇa, Laṅkeśvara, Paulastya, Daśakaṇṭha** — each mark a face of that doom. The Rāmāyaṇa is unusual in dignifying its villain: Vālmīki grants him grandeur, scholarship, and even Rāma's posthumous respect.

## I. Ancestry and the Pulastya–Viśravas line (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa)

- **Pulastya** - One of the ten **Prajāpatis** / mind-born sons (*mānasa-putra*) of **Brahmā**, and one of the **[Saptarṣi](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md)** — Rāvaṇa's paternal great-grandfather, the fount of the rākṣasa-brāhmaṇa line (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 2–9).
- **Viśravas (Viśrava)** - Son of Pulastya (in some tellings of Pulastya's son **Agastya**-contemporary lineage), a great sage; father of both **Kubera** (by his first wife Ilavilā/Devavarṇinī) and of Rāvaṇa's brood (by Kaikasī). The shared father makes **Kubera Rāvaṇa's elder half-brother** (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 3–9).
- **Kaikasī** - Daughter of the rākṣasa king **Sumālī** (son of **Sukeśa**); sent by her father to Viśravas to bear powerful sons who would restore rākṣasa fortunes. She approaches the sage at an inauspicious twilight hour — a *doṣa* that Viśravas warns will taint her firstborn with cruelty (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 9).
- **The four children of Viśravas & Kaikasī** -
  - **Rāvaṇa (Daśagrīva)** — born with **ten heads and twenty arms**; the sky reportedly rained blood and ill omens marked his birth.
  - **Kumbhakarṇa** — the giant brother, cursed to sleep six months at a stretch.
  - **Vibhīṣaṇa** — the righteous, dharma-loving brother who defects to Rāma.
  - **Śūrpaṇakhā** — the sister whose mutilation by Lakṣmaṇa ignites the war ([Araṇya-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md)).
- **Maternal rākṣasa line** - Through Sumālī and Sukeśa, Rāvaṇa inherits the older rākṣasa dynasty once led by **Mālyavān, Sumālī, Mālī** — the original lords of Laṅkā driven out by Viṣṇu before Kubera held it (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 5–8). See [Beings & Bestiary — rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/README.md).

## II. The ten heads — meanings and variants

- **Daśagrīva / Daśānana** - "Ten-Necks / Ten-Faces." The default Rāmāyaṇa portrayal: ten heads and twenty arms, a monstrous birth-form (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 9).
- *Variant (allegorical):* the ten heads represent **mastery of the four Vedas and six Śāstras** (Vedāṅgas) — i.e. Rāvaṇa as the supreme scholar. [folk/devotional]
- *Variant (allegorical):* the ten heads symbolize ten vices — **kāma, krodha, moha, lobha, mada, mātsarya** (lust, anger, delusion, greed, pride, envy) plus manas, buddhi, citta, ahaṅkāra — the inner enemies a devotee must conquer at **Daśaharā / Vijayadaśamī**. [folk]
- *Variant (nine heads):* he is sometimes shown with **nine** heads, having **cut one off** during his austerities (see §III). [variant]
- *Variant (self-multiplied):* some Śaiva tellings make the heads a yogic feat — heads he could regrow, tied to the boon (see §VI on his death). [late text]

## III. The tapas and Brahmā's boon (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 10)

- **Eviction by Viśravas; the brothers' austerities** - Counselled (in some tellings by their grandfather) to win power the lawful way, the three brothers undertake fierce **tapas**. Rāvaṇa performs penance for a vast span (commonly **10,000 years**) (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 10).
- **The self-decapitation offering** - To force Brahmā's hand, Rāvaṇa **severs his own heads one by one** and offers them into the fire; as he is about to offer the tenth (last) head, **Brahmā appears** and stops him, restoring the offered heads (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 10; widely retold).
- **The boon — invincibility with a fatal omission** - Rāvaṇa asks for **immortality**; refused, he instead asks to be **invulnerable to gods, dānavas, gandharvas, yakṣas, rākṣasas, piśācas, nāgas, and all celestial/demonic beings** — but, in his contempt, **omits men and animals (and monkeys)**, thinking such lowly creatures could never threaten him (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 10). This omission is the hinge of the whole epic: only a **human (Rāma)** aided by **vānaras** can kill him.
  - *Variant:* some versions add that he also won that his **heads, if cut, would regenerate** — a boon Rāma must defeat with the Brahmāstra (see §VI). [variant]
- **Kumbhakarṇa's boon goes wrong** - Kumbhakarṇa, his tongue twisted by **Sarasvatī** (at the gods' plea), asks for *nidrā* (sleep) instead of *nirdevatvam* (a world without gods) — and is cursed/boon-bound to **sleep six months and wake one day** (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 10). Vibhīṣaṇa asks only for steadfast dharma.

## IV. Conquest of the three worlds (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 11–34)

- **Seizing Laṅkā from Kubera** - Rāvaṇa marches on **Laṅkā** (held by his half-brother **Kubera**, lord of wealth), defeats and expels him, and makes the golden city his capital and seat of the rākṣasa empire (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 11–15).
- **The Puṣpaka Vimāna** - He carries off Kubera's **flying chariot, the Puṣpaka** — a self-moving aerial car that travels at the speed of thought, expands to any size, and obeys its rider; it becomes Rāvaṇa's signature vehicle (and is later returned to Rāma, who flies it back to Ayodhyā). See [Artifacts — vimānas](../../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/README.md).
- **Digvijaya — conquest of the directions** - Rāvaṇa subdues yakṣas, gandharvas, nāgas, and kings across the worlds; he is said to have **defeated Yama (Death) himself**, fought Indra's court, and bound up the **Lokapālas** (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa). His son **Meghanāda** defeats **Indra** and earns the name **Indrajit** ("Conqueror of Indra").
- **Lifting Mount Kailāsa** - Affronted when **Nandī** halts his Puṣpaka over **Mount Kailāsa**, Rāvaṇa tries to **uproot the mountain**; **Śiva** presses it down with a toe, crushing Rāvaṇa's arms/fingers beneath. Trapped, Rāvaṇa **sings Śiva's praises for a thousand years** until Śiva, pleased, releases him and grants him the sword **Candrahāsa** ("moon-laugh"). It is from his **scream/roar** of pain here that one tradition derives the very name **Rāvaṇa** ("the screamer/roarer"), given by Śiva (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 16; the **Rāvaṇānugraha** theme in Purāṇic/Śaiva art).
  - *Variant:* this Kailāsa episode is prominent in Purāṇic and Tamil Śaiva tradition but has **little basis in the core Vālmīki text** beyond Uttara-kāṇḍa allusions. [late text/regional]
- **Curbed by mightier kings** - The Rāmāyaṇa concedes Rāvaṇa was not unbeaten: he was **captured by Kārtavīrya Arjuna (Sahasrārjuna)**, the thousand-armed Haihaya king, and bound until released; and was **humbled by the vānara king Vālī**, who tucked him under his arm during his sandhyā worship (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 32–34). These foreshadow that a non-divine being can subdue him.
- **Wives and sons** - Chief queen **Mandodarī** (daughter of the asura-architect **Maya** and the apsarā Hemā) — virtuous and loyal to the end. Sons include **Meghanāda/Indrajit**, **Akṣayakumāra**, **Atikāya**, **Narāntaka**, **Devāntaka**, **Triśira** — most slain in the [Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md). The *Sundara-kāṇḍa* describes a vast harem.

## V. The curses that doomed him — Vedavatī, Rambhā/Nalakūbara, Jaya-Vijaya

- **Vedavatī → Sītā** - A chaste ascetic woman (daughter of the sage **Kuśadhvaja**), doing *tapas* to win **Viṣṇu** as husband, is accosted by Rāvaṇa, who seizes her by the hair. She **cuts off the defiled hair, enters the fire**, and curses that she will be **reborn to cause his destruction** — reborn as **Sītā** (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 17; elaborated in later texts).
  - *Variant:* a strand of tradition makes **Sītā literally Vedavatī reborn** and even identifies her as Rāvaṇa's own daughter (abandoned), heightening the tragic irony. [folk/regional]
- **Rambhā & Nalakūbara's curse** - Rāvaṇa **rapes the apsarā Rambhā**, who was betrothed to **Nalakūbara** (Kubera's son). Nalakūbara curses that **if Rāvaṇa ever again forces himself on an unwilling woman, his head will shatter into seven pieces** (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 26). This curse is precisely **why Rāvaṇa cannot touch the captive Sītā** in the Aśoka-vana — he can only plead and threaten.
- **Other curses (variant rosters)** -
  - *Variant (Punjikasthalā):* a parallel telling names the assaulted celestial woman differently, with the same seven-pieces curse. [variant]
  - *Variant (cow/sage curses):* regional tellings multiply minor curses earned during his conquests. [folk]
- **The Jaya–Vijaya frame** - The *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* overlays a theological cause: Rāvaṇa and Kumbhakarṇa are **Jaya and Vijaya**, Viṣṇu's Vaikuṇṭha gatekeepers, in the **2nd of three demonic births** (Hiraṇyakaśipu/Hiraṇyākṣa → Rāvaṇa/Kumbhakarṇa → Śiśupāla/Dantavakra), so that Rāvaṇa's death is a cursed soul's return to God by *dveṣa-bhakti* (devotion-through-hatred) (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 7.1, 7.10). Full treatment: [Jaya & Vijaya](../jaya-vijaya.md).
  - *Variant:* the Rāmāyaṇa itself does **not** know this frame — it gives Rāvaṇa a self-contained Pulastya ancestry; the gatekeeper-overlay is retrofitted by the Purāṇas. [late text]
  - *Pairing note:* usually **Jaya → Rāvaṇa, Vijaya → Kumbhakarṇa**.

## VI. The war and death (Rām. Yuddha-kāṇḍa)

- **The abduction** - Provoked by Śūrpaṇakhā's mutilation and inflamed by her account of Sītā's beauty, Rāvaṇa has **Mārīca** take the golden-deer form to lure Rāma away, then **abducts Sītā** in the Puṣpaka, slaying **Jaṭāyu** who tries to stop him ([Araṇya-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md)).
- **Sītā in Aśoka-vana** - Held captive but unviolated (because of Nalakūbara's curse, §V), Sītā refuses him; **Hanumān** finds her in the [Sundara-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md) and burns Laṅkā.
- **Vibhīṣaṇa's defection** - Counselling Rāvaṇa to return Sītā and avoid ruin, the righteous brother **Vibhīṣaṇa** is spurned and exiled; he joins Rāma and reveals Rāvaṇa's secrets ([Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md)).
- **The fall of his champions** - Kumbhakarṇa is roused and slain; **Indrajit**, master of the **Brahmāstra** and invisibility (and the Nāgāstra that fells Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa), is finally killed by **Lakṣmaṇa** — breaking Rāvaṇa's last great hope ([Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md)).
- **Rāma vs. Rāvaṇa — the final duel** - In a cosmic battle Rāma severs Rāvaṇa's heads, but **they regrow** as fast as cut. **Agastya** teaches Rāma the **Āditya-hṛdaya** hymn for strength; on Mātali's (Indra's charioteer's) advice Rāma looses the supreme **Brahmāstra** (a weapon of Brahmā, gifted via Agastya), which strikes Rāvaṇa's **chest/navel** — **where the nectar (amṛta) that kept regenerating him was stored** — and kills him (Rām. Yuddha-kāṇḍa 108–110).
  - *Variant:* the **amṛta-in-the-navel** detail (the secret revealed by Vibhīṣaṇa) is prominent in many regional Rāmāyaṇas (e.g. Tulsīdās's *Rāmcaritmānas*); Vālmīki emphasizes the Brahmāstra to the heart. [variant]
- **A dignified end** - Dying, Rāvaṇa is honored: **Rāma sends Lakṣmaṇa to Rāvaṇa's deathbed to learn statecraft and wisdom** from the fallen sage-king (a beloved folk episode), and orders him **cremated with full rites**, telling Vibhīṣaṇa that "enmity ends with death." [folk/regional for the Lakṣmaṇa-lesson]
- **Aftermath** - Vibhīṣaṇa is crowned king of Laṅkā; Sītā's chastity is affirmed (the **Agni-parīkṣā**); Rāma flies home in the recovered **Puṣpaka** for the coronation that founds **Rāma-rājya** (return narrated in [Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md)).

## VII. The other Rāvaṇa — devotee, scholar, artist

- **Parama-Śiva-bhakta** - Rāvaṇa is among the greatest devotees of **[Śiva](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md)**: the Kailāsa episode (§IV), his lifelong worship, and his composition of the **Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra** define him as much as his villainy. The Śaiva *Rāvaṇānugraha* ("Śiva's grace to Rāvaṇa") is a major iconographic theme.
- **Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra** - The thunderous Sanskrit hymn (in *pañcacāmara* metre) praising Śiva's cosmic dance — traditionally attributed to Rāvaṇa, sung (in one tradition) while pinned beneath Kailāsa, or built from a **vīṇā fashioned from one of his severed heads strung with his own sinews** (the **Rāvaṇahatthā / "Rāvaṇa-hasta vīṇā"**, a bowed instrument named for him). [devotional; Tamil Śaiva *Tēvāram* tradition]
- **The Ātma-liṅga** - Pleased, Śiva grants Rāvaṇa the **Ātma-liṅga** to carry to Laṅkā, on condition it never touch the ground; **Gaṇeśa** (as a boy) tricks Rāvaṇa into setting it down at **Gokarṇa** (Karnataka), where it roots forever as **Mahābaleśvara** (Sthala-purāṇa of Gokarṇa). [folk/regional Sthala-purāṇa]
- **Master of the Vedas** - A learned brāhmaṇa by his father's line: reputed master of the **four Vedas and six Vedāṅgas**, a great **Sāma-veda chanter**, and (in later tradition) author of works on **Ayurveda (Arka-prakāśa), astrology, and the Śaiva āgamas** — even **Rāvaṇa-saṃhitā** astrological texts bear his name. [late attributions]
- **Yajña-officiant for Rāma** - In a striking irony of some retellings, Rāma asks the dying or living Rāvaṇa (the supreme brāhmaṇa-scholar) to **officiate the pre-war yajña** to consecrate the bridge / worship Śiva at Rāmeśvaram. [folk]
- **Regional veneration** - Rāvaṇa is honored, not burned, in pockets of India and Sri Lanka — e.g. among some communities in Madhya Pradesh (Bisrakh, claimed birthplace), Karnataka, and as a king/ancestor figure in Sri Lankan tradition. [regional]
