# Aṅgada — crown prince of the Vānaras

> The **vānara** prince of **Kiṣkindhā** — son of **Vālī (Bāli)** and queen **Tārā**, orphaned by [Rāma](./rama.md)'s arrow, adopted as *yuvarāja* by his uncle **Sugrīva**, and made the field-commander of the decisive **southern search-party** and the defiant **envoy to Rāvaṇa's court**. His story threads the [Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md), [Sundara-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md) and [Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md).
> Sits in **[Tretā-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current 28th Mahāyuga.
> Parent: [Characters](README.md) · Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md)

**Aṅgada** (Sanskrit *Aṅgada*, literally "armlet / bracelet-of-the-upper-arm") is the son of the slain vānara king **Vālī** and the wise queen **[Tārā](./sugriva-and-vali.md)**, and thus the nephew of **Sugrīva**. Where his father is the epic's tragic strongman and his uncle its wavering ally-king, Aṅgada is the corpus of the **younger generation of vānaras**: reared to the throne, disinherited by his father's death, and re-elevated as crown prince by the very uncle whose arrow-death he was made to witness. He is at once a grief-charged, half-rebellious youth and the most trusted **line-officer** of Rāma's campaign — commander of the party that finds Sītā's whereabouts, the ambassador who humbles Laṅkā, and a front-rank duellist in the war. His defiant embassy — culminating, in vernacular tradition, in the celebrated **planting of his foot** that no rākṣasa can move — is his signature scene, though (see §V) it belongs to later retelling, not to Vālmīki.

> **Disambiguation.** This Aṅgada — Vālī's son — is not to be confused with **Aṅgada, elder son of Lakṣmaṇa** (twin of Candraketu), whom Rāma later installs over the kingdom of *Aṅgadīyā* in the North-West (Vālmīki, Uttara-kāṇḍa). The two share only the name. See [Bharata, Śatrughna & their wives](./bharata-shatrughna-and-their-wives.md) for the Ikṣvāku princes' sons.

## I. Birth and parentage (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa; Uttara-kāṇḍa)

- **Son of Vālī and Tārā** - Aṅgada is the only son of **Vālī** (Bāli), the Indra-begotten king of Kiṣkindhā, and **Tārā**, the wise vānara queen (daughter of the physician-chief **Suṣeṇa**). Through his father he is grandson of **Indra**; his mother is later counted among the **Pañcakanyā**. (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa.)
- **A prince of the blood** - As the son of the reigning king, Aṅgada is Kiṣkindhā's natural heir; the whole drama of his youth is that Vālī's feud with Sugrīva and Vālī's death by Rāma's hand strip him of that birthright and then restore it in the diminished form of *heir to his uncle*. (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa.)
  - *Variant (Tārā's origin):* where Tārā is made an **apsaras** born of the [Samudra-manthana](../samudra-manthana.md), Aṅgada acquires a churning-born, semi-divine maternal line. [variant] See [Sugrīva & Vālī §I](./sugriva-and-vali.md).
- **Name** - *Aṅgada* is a common noun for the jeweled armlet worn on the upper arm; it is also borne by other figures in the tradition (Lakṣmaṇa's son, above), so the epithet **"Vālisūnu / Vāliputra"** ("son of Vālī") is used to fix this prince. (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa.)

## II. Vālī's death and Aṅgada's commendation (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 18–22)

- **The dying father's charge** - Struck by Rāma's concealed arrow in the duel with Sugrīva, the mortally wounded **Vālī** — reconciled to the justice of his death — draws Aṅgada near and **commends his son and Tārā to the protection of Rāma and Sugrīva**, begging that the boy be treated not as an enemy's child but as a son, and never mistreated. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 18, 22.)
- **Aṅgada's grief** - The young prince laments over his father's body; his sorrow, with Tārā's celebrated *vilāpa* (lament), forms the emotional weight of the sarga. The resentment planted here — that his uncle's ally killed his father from cover — resurfaces at moments of despair in the search (§IV). (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa.)
- **Rāma's assurance** - Rāma accepts the charge, affirming that Aṅgada will be honoured; the boy is folded into the new dispensation rather than eliminated as a rival claimant — a deliberate act of dynastic mercy that the epic marks as dharmic. (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa.)

## III. Made crown prince (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 25–26)

- **Sugrīva's consecration** - Because Rāma, bound by his own vow of exile, will not enter a city, he sends **Lakṣmaṇa** into Kiṣkindhā to consecrate **Sugrīva** as king of the vānaras. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 25.)
- **Aṅgada installed as *yuvarāja*** - At the same consecration **Aṅgada, son of Vālī, is installed as *yuvarāja* (crown prince / heir-apparent)** — a settlement that both honours Vālī's dying wish and secures the succession without civil war, since the throne now passes Sugrīva → Aṅgada rather than being extinguished. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 25–26.)
- **The dynastic logic** - The arrangement keeps two royal lines in a single house: Sugrīva (Sūrya's son) reigns, but the crown-princeship is reserved for the line of Vālī (Indra's son), so Aṅgada's disinheritance is formally undone. (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa.)

## IV. Commander of the southern search-party (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 41–65)

- **Leader of the decisive quarter** - When Sugrīva musters the world's vānaras and dispatches search-parties to the four directions (east under **Vinata**, west under **Suṣeṇa**, north under **Śatabali**), the **south** — the direction in which Sītā actually lies — is entrusted to **Aṅgada as commander**, with **Hanumān, Jāmbavān (the bear-king), Nīla, Tāra, Suṣeṇa, Nala** and others under him, and a one-month deadline. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 41.)
  - *Note:* Aṅgada holds nominal command by rank, but **Hanumān** is the party's true instrument; Sugrīva privately marks Hanumān as the one on whom success depends. The pairing — princely commander and heroic agent — runs through the whole southern narrative. (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa.)
- **Svayamprabhā's cave** - The party, lost and past its deadline, wanders into the enchanted underground realm of the ascetic **Svayamprabhā**, who feeds them and magically returns them to the ocean shore. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 50–52.)
- **The fast unto death (prāyopaveśa)** - On the southern shore, deadline broken and Sītā unfound, Aṅgada — dreading Sugrīva's wrath and recalling bitterly that this same uncle's ally had killed his father — leads the party in resolving to **fast to death** rather than return in disgrace; he voices open distrust of Sugrīva in his despair. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 55–56.)
- **The Sampāti meeting** - Their lament over the dead **Jaṭāyu** is overheard by his elder brother, the wingless vulture **Sampāti**, who — revived at the mention of his kin — tells them **Sītā is held by Rāvaṇa in Laṅkā, a hundred yojanas across the sea**, giving the search its target. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 56–59.) — full treatment: [Jaṭāyu & Sampāti](./jatayu-and-sampati.md).
- **Weighing the leap** - Before the hundred-yojana ocean the chiefs measure their leaping powers; each falls short. **Aṅgada** reckons he could perhaps reach Laṅkā but might lack the strength to return — so **Jāmbavān** rouses **Hanumān** to his forgotten Vāyu-born might, and Hanumān makes the leap that opens the [Sundara-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md). (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 64–66.)

## V. The defiant embassy to Rāvaṇa's court (Rām. Yuddha-kāṇḍa 41)

- **Sent as Rāma's envoy** - On the eve of battle, once the army has crossed the [Setu](../../06-sacred-geography/kishkindha-hampi-anegundi.md), Rāma sends the prince **Aṅgada** as *dūta* (ambassador) into Laṅkā, demanding that Rāvaṇa **restore Sītā and submit — on pain of death and the enthronement of Vibhīṣaṇa**. (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa 41.)
- **The Vālmīki embassy** - In the critical text, Aṅgada delivers Rāma's ultimatum with fearless scorn; the enraged **Rāvaṇa orders him seized**. Aṅgada lets four rākṣasas cling to him, then **leaps to the roof of Rāvaṇa's palace**, wrecking a turret/spire under his weight, hurls the guards off, and returns to Rāma's camp defiant and unharmed — a calculated humiliation of Laṅkā before the first blow. (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa 41.)
- **The planted foot (Aṅgada-rāyabāra)** - In the celebrated vernacular and folk elaboration, Aṅgada **plants his foot firmly on the floor of the assembly and challenges the entire court — "if any of you can move my foot, Rāma will return home and abandon Sītā."** One after another the rākṣasa champions fail; when even **Rāvaṇa himself rises to try**, Aṅgada withdraws his foot and taunts that Rāvaṇa should instead lay his head at **Rāma's** feet, not his. The scene is the emotional climax of the embassy in performance.
  - *Attribution:* this foot-planting challenge is **not in Vālmīki**; it is prominent in the Tamil **Kamba Rāmāyaṇam** and Tulsīdās's **Rāmcaritmānas** (Laṅkā-kāṇḍa), and is a beloved set-piece of folk theatre — the Telugu/Kannada **"Aṅgada Rāyabāram"** (Aṅgada's Embassy) of Yakṣagāna and Harikathā. [folk] [regional] See [Smṛti–Itihāsa epics](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md).
- **The embassy's function** - Whether in Vālmīki's turret-wrecking or the folk foot-planting, the scene serves the same narrative end: it **exposes Rāvaṇa's court as unable to answer even a single vānara envoy**, foreshadowing Laṅkā's fall, and it lets a prince of the enemy generation, rather than a god or a monkey-hero, speak Rāma's law. (Yuddha-kāṇḍa; retellings.)

## VI. Exploits in the war (Rām. Yuddha-kāṇḍa)

- **A front-rank champion** - Once battle is joined, Aṅgada fights as one of the army's foremost duellists, second in prowess among the vānaras only to Hanumān, and is repeatedly named leading assaults on Laṅkā's gates. (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa.)
- **Slayer of Vajradaṃṣṭra** - Among the rākṣasa commanders who fall in the successive sorties, **Aṅgada kills Vajradaṃṣṭra** (as Hanumān kills Dhūmrākṣa and Akampana, and Nīla the chief general Prahasta). (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa 51–58.)
  - *Variant (roster):* recensions distribute the minor rākṣasa kills differently among Hanumān, Aṅgada, Nīla and Dvivida; the assignment of Vajradaṃṣṭra to Aṅgada follows the mainstream Vālmīki tradition. [variant]
- **Against Indrajit and the great rākṣasas** - Aṅgada takes the field in the battles against **Indrajit (Meghanāda)**, **Kumbhakarṇa** and Rāvaṇa's host, fighting alongside Hanumān and the other chiefs through the long duels of the kāṇḍa. (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa.)
- **Revived with the host** - Like the rest of the fallen vānara army, any who die in the fighting are restored at the war's end by the boon of the gods (**Indra**); the surviving chiefs, Aṅgada among them, witness Rāma's reunion with Sītā and return north on the **Puṣpaka vimāna**. (Vālmīki, Yuddha-kāṇḍa.)

## VII. After the war — king of Kiṣkindhā (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa tradition)

- **Honoured at Ayodhyā** - Aṅgada accompanies the victorious party to **Ayodhyā** for Rāma's coronation (rājyābhiṣeka), among the vānara chiefs feasted and dismissed with gifts before returning to Kiṣkindhā. (Vālmīki, Yuddha/Uttara-kāṇḍa.)
- **Succession to the throne** - In the tradition of Rāma's final departure (**mahāprasthāna**), when **Sugrīva** follows Rāma into the Sarayū / into retirement, he first **crowns Aṅgada king of Kiṣkindhā** — so the disinherited prince at last inherits his father's throne in full. (Vālmīki, Uttara-kāṇḍa tradition.) [late text] See [Vānaras & Ṛkṣas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/vanaras-and-rikshas.md).
  - *Variant:* some retellings have Sugrīva continue to reign and Aṅgada remain crown-prince to the end; the "Aṅgada crowned at Rāma's departure" thread is a later-stratum (Uttara-kāṇḍa) closure. [disputed] [late text]

## VIII. Character and reception

- **The grieving heir** - Aṅgada is drawn as brave, quick-tempered and loyal, but shadowed by his father's killing; his near-mutinous despair on the southern shore (§IV) is the epic's study of a wronged young prince held to duty by the counsel of elders (Hanumān, Jāmbavān). (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa.)
- **The model envoy** - In later devotional and performance tradition he becomes the archetype of the **fearless ambassador** who speaks truth to a tyrant's face; "Aṅgada's foot" is proverbial for an immovable stand, and **Aṅgada-rāyabāra** is a standing genre of Yakṣagāna, Harikathā and Telugu theatre. [folk] [regional]
- **Devotional reading** - Bhakti tradition reads the embassy allegorically: the surrendered devotee (Aṅgada, orphaned and adopted into Rāma's service) confronts egoistic power (Rāvaṇa) with nothing but faith, and cannot be moved. [folk]
