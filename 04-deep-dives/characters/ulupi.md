# Ulūpī — Nāga Princess and Wife of Arjuna

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Ulūpī**, daughter of the Nāga king **Kauravya**, the serpent-realm wife of [Arjuna](./arjuna.md), mother of [Irāvān / Arāvāṇ](./aravan-iravan.md), and the hidden engineer of the [Babhruvāhana](./babhruvahana.md) duel and Arjuna's death-and-resurrection. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). Her courtship belongs to the [Ādi Parva](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md); her decisive act to the [Āśvamedhika Parva](../mahabharata/14-ashvamedhika-parva.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* this sits in the present 28th Mahāyuga of the 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara, across late Dvāpara Yuga — the courtship in Arjuna's twelve-year *tīrtha*-exile, the revival in the year after the Mahābhārata war (Kali Yuga begins 3102 BCE at the war's close); the BORI Critical Edition of the *Mahābhārata* is the scholarly textual baseline. [scholarly]

## Lineage and the Nāga realm

- **Daughter of Kauravya** — Ulūpī is the daughter of **Kauravya**, a Nāga king of the **Airāvata** serpent line, dwelling in the underworld waters beneath the Gaṅgā; she is a princess of the Nāgaloka / Pātāla, not a mortal (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~206*). See the [Nāgas & serpents](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md) catalog for her maternal world.
- **The name** — *Ulūpī* (also **Ulupī, Ulupi**); a widow before she meets Arjuna in some readings, she is consistently styled the Nāga-kanyā ("serpent-maiden") and *pannaga-ātmajā* ("daughter of the serpents") (*Mahābhārata*). [scholarly]
- **One of Arjuna's four wives** — Ulūpī ranks among Arjuna's wives alongside **Draupadī** (shared queen), **Subhadrā** (mother of Abhimanyu), and **Citrāṅgadā** of Maṇipura (mother of Babhruvāhana); she is his Nāga consort (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). See [Arjuna](./arjuna.md) and [Citrāṅgadā](./chitrangada.md).

## The courtship during the exile

- **The cause of the exile** — Arjuna breaks the brothers' pact governing access to Draupadī (entering Yudhiṣṭhira's chamber to fetch his weapons) and accepts a self-imposed **twelve-year *tīrtha*-pilgrimage** in expiation; the wandering takes him along the Gaṅgā, where Ulūpī finds him (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~206*). See [Arjuna](./arjuna.md).
- **The abduction into the water** — as Arjuna bathes / enters the Gaṅgā at **Gaṅgādvāra (Haridvāra)**, Ulūpī, smitten with desire, **draws him down beneath the river** into her father Kauravya's serpent-palace in the underworld (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~206*).
- **The wooing and the *brahmacarya* objection** — Ulūpī declares her love and asks Arjuna to wed her; Arjuna protests that he is bound to **celibacy (*brahmacarya*)** for the term of his vow. She argues the vow restricts him only with respect to **Draupadī**, not other women, and that to refuse a desiring woman who has surrendered herself would be a breach of *dharma* — saving a life in distress outweighs the partial vow (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~206*). [scholarly]
- **The one-night marriage and the boon** — persuaded, Arjuna spends a single night with Ulūpī; at parting she grants him a **boon of invincibility / immunity in water** — that all aquatic creatures shall obey him and he shall be unconquerable in any water (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~206*).
  - *Variant (numbering of episodes):* the Critical Edition places this Nāga episode early in the pilgrimage, before the Citrāṅgadā marriage at Maṇipura; Ganguli's vulgate enumerates it around Ādi Parva sections 213–219 within the "Arjuna-vanavāsa" sub-parva. [scholarly]

## Son: Irāvān / Arāvāṇ

- **Mother of Irāvān** — from the union is born **Irāvān** (Tamil **Arāvāṇ / Aravān**), reared by Ulūpī in the Nāga world after Arjuna departs; he comes to his father only at Kurukṣetra and dies a war-hero (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~206; Bhīṣma Parva*). See [Irāvān / Arāvāṇ](./aravan-iravan.md) for his battlefield death and the Tamil Kūttāṇṭavar cult.
- **The single Nāga heir** — Irāvān is Arjuna's only son by Ulūpī; she rears him apart, abandoned in some strands by a Nāga uncle's line, until the reunion in **Indraloka** or at the war (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). See the [Nāgas & serpents](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md) catalog.

## The Āśvamedhika Parva — engineer of Arjuna's death and revival

- **The wandering horse reaches Maṇipura** — after the war, Yudhiṣṭhira's **Aśvamedha** sends the consecrated stallion roaming with Arjuna as protector; it enters **Maṇipura**, kingdom of **Babhruvāhana**, Arjuna's son by Citrāṅgadā (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~79*). See [Āśvamedhika Parva](../mahabharata/14-ashvamedhika-parva.md) and [Babhruvāhana](./babhruvahana.md).
- **Babhruvāhana's peaceful welcome and Arjuna's rebuke** — the young king greets his father with tribute, not arms; Arjuna, bound to fight any realm the horse enters, **reproaches him as unwarriorlike** and demands battle (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~79–80*). See [Arjuna](./arjuna.md).
- **Ulūpī rises and goads the son to fight** — **Ulūpī emerges from the earth** and **incites the reluctant Babhruvāhana to take up arms against his father**, overriding his hesitation — the pivotal act of her deep-dive (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~80*). See [Babhruvāhana](./babhruvahana.md).
- **Babhruvāhana slays Arjuna** — father and son fight a fierce archery duel; the son's arrows **strike Arjuna dead** on the field — a near-unique instance of Arjuna's death in the epic — and the wounded Babhruvāhana swoons beside him (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~81*).
  - *Variant (South-Indian Yakṣagāna *Babruvāhana* cycle):* regional drama has the son **behead** Arjuna outright, heightening the grief-and-revival for the stage (*regional folk tradition*). [folk] [regional]
- **Citrāṅgadā's reproach** — the co-wife **Citrāṅgadā** rushes out, laments, and **reproaches Ulūpī** for engineering a son's slaying of his father (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~81*). See [Citrāṅgadā](./chitrangada.md).
- **Revival by the Nāgamaṇi** — Ulūpī summons the serpent-realm jewel of resurrection — the **Mṛtasaṃjīvanī (Saṃjīvana-maṇi / Nāgamaṇi)** — and has it placed on Arjuna's chest; **Arjuna returns to life**, the death undone (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~81*). [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Jaiminīya Aśvamedha) [late text] [folk]:* the independent **Jaiminīya Aśvamedhaparvan** (attrib. Jaimini; c. 12th c.) expands the revival into Babhruvāhana's war on the serpents in **Pātāla** to seize the gem — a quest absent from the Critical Edition (*Jaiminīya Aśvamedha Parva*).

## The hidden purpose — expiating the Vasus' curse on Bhīṣma

- **The orchestration disclosed** — Ulūpī reveals she **engineered the whole episode for Arjuna's good**: the **Eight Vasus** (of whom **Bhīṣma** was the incarnate Dyaus/Prabhāsa), grieved with **Gaṅgā**, had cursed Arjuna for slaying Bhīṣma **unfairly — from behind Śikhaṇḍī** — in the war; the curse could be discharged only if Arjuna **fell by his own son's hand** and was restored (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~81–82*). See the [Babhruvāhana](./babhruvahana.md) deep-dive for the full curse-mechanics.
- **The theology of the act** — Ulūpī's "abetment" of parricide is thus reframed as **expiation engineered through love**: she contrives the death precisely to *prevent* the deeper karmic harm of the unexpiated curse, then reverses it with the gem — the dutiful son blameless, the Nāga wife the unseen redeemer (*scholarly note*). [scholarly]
- **Reconciliation and the sacrifice** — Arjuna restored, the family is reconciled; Ulūpī, Citrāṅgadā, and Babhruvāhana are invited to attend the sacrifice at **Hastināpura**, and the horse continues its circuit (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~82*).

## After the epic

- **The Nāga return at Arjuna's death** — in a late, devotionally satisfying tradition, when the Pāṇḍavas undertake the **Mahāprasthāna** (the final journey), Ulūpī and the Nāga kin **receive Arjuna's body** or take her into the Gaṅgā's waters, closing the water-circle her courtship opened; this coda is post-canonical and regional. [folk] [disputed]
- **A recurring Arjuna-cycle figure** — across the epic Ulūpī functions as the structural bridge between the **Nāga world** and the **Pāṇḍava line**: she opens the exile-courtship, mothers a war-hero, and resolves the post-war curse — appearing precisely at the cycle's hinge-points (*scholarly note*). [scholarly]
