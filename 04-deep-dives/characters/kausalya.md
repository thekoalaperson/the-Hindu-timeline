# Kausalyā — Chief Queen of Daśaratha, Mother of Rāma

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Kausalyā** — senior queen (*paṭṭamahiṣī*) of Daśaratha, mother of [Rāma](../ramayana/README.md), and first-tier Ikṣvāku matriarch. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). Her story belongs to the exile narrative of the [Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/2-ayodhya-kanda.md); her daughter-in-law is [Sītā](./sita.md); her co-queen and sister-in-counsel is [Sumitrā](./sumitra.md); the dynastic backdrop of the Raghu line runs back through [Aja & Indumatī](../aja-indumati.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* her life falls in the Tretā Yuga of the present 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara; the scholarly baseline is the Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa* (Baroda Critical Edition), with the *Rāmacaritamānasa* and Purāṇic recensions layered above it. [scholarly]

## Names, identity, and rank
- **Kausalyā (कौसल्या)** - her name marks her as the princess of **Kosala** (the region around Ayodhyā itself, or, in the common reading, of the **Dakṣiṇa Kosala / South Kosala** house); the name is geographic-dynastic rather than personal (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa*).
  - *Variant (her father):* later tradition names her father **Sukauśala** or **Bhānumant**, king of Kosala; Vālmīki does not foreground a patronymic, so the genealogy is supplied by Purāṇic and commentarial sources (*Purāṇic/commentarial*). [late text]
- **Senior queen (jyeṣṭhā / paṭṭamahiṣī)** - she is Daśaratha's **first and chief wife**, ranking above [Sumitrā](./sumitra.md) and Kaikeyī; as mother of the eldest son and heir-apparent her status is the highest in the antaḥpura (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa*).
- **Rāma-mātṛ / "mother of the world's lord"** - in the devotional frame her identity is wholly defined by her motherhood of Rāma, the seventh avatāra of Viṣṇu; she is venerated as the womb that bore the Lord (*Rāmacaritamānasa, Bāla-kāṇḍa*).

## The three queens — a comparative profile
- **Kausalyā — the senior, dignified mother** - eldest queen, mother of [Rāma](../ramayana/README.md) alone; her register through the epic is **grief borne with dignity** and dharmic counsel rather than action (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa*).
- **Kaikeyī — the favored, then fallen queen** - the Kekaya princess, Daśaratha's best-loved wife, mother of **Bharata**; her invocation of the two boons drives the exile and makes her the engine of the plot's tragedy (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā 7–13*). See her full arc in the [Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/2-ayodhya-kanda.md).
- **Sumitrā — the wise, self-effacing queen** - the second queen, mother of the **twins Lakṣmaṇa and Śatrughna**; remembered for the counsel that sends Lakṣmaṇa to serve Rāma in the forest (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā 40*). See [Sumitrā](./sumitra.md).
  - *Note on the triad:* the three queens map onto a devotional-symbolic reading — Kausalyā the dignified senior, Kaikeyī the instrument of fate, Sumitrā the selfless sage; the epic itself draws the contrast chiefly through their differing responses to the exile. [scholarly]

## The Putrakāmeṣṭi and the divine pāyasa
- **Daśaratha's sonless grief** - childless after long reign, Daśaratha undertakes the **Aśvamedha** and, on Ṛṣyaśṛṅga's counsel, the **Putrakāmeṣṭi** (son-seeking sacrifice) (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa 14–15*).
- **The Prājāpatya being and the pāyasa** - from the sacrificial fire a radiant being (in the devotional reading, **Agni / a messenger of the gods**) rises bearing a golden vessel of divine **pāyasa** (sweet milk-rice, *kheer*) — the gods' answer, charged to beget sons in whom Viṣṇu's portions will descend (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa 15–16*).
- **Kausalyā's half-share** - Daśaratha divides the pāyasa: he gives **one half to Kausalyā**, from which **Rāma** is conceived — the largest single portion, fitting the senior queen and the principal avatāra (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa 16*).
  - *The standard division (Vālmīki, Bāla 16):* half to Kausalyā (→ Rāma); of the remaining half, half to **Sumitrā**, a quarter (an eighth of the whole) to **Kaikeyī** (→ Bharata), and the last portion again to **Sumitrā** — so Sumitrā receives twice and bears the **twins Lakṣmaṇa & Śatrughna**, while Kausalyā's single full half yields the one supreme son.
  - *Variant (the order of giving):* recensions differ on the exact sequence of the second and third shares and whether Kaikeyī or Sumitrā is served first; the arithmetic that Kausalyā gets the foremost half and Sumitrā the doubled remainder is the constant (*Vālmīki recensions; later retellings*). [disputed]
- **Birth of Rāma** - in the bright fortnight of **Caitra**, under **Punarvasu** with the Sun, Mars, Saturn, Jupiter, and Venus exalted, Kausalyā bears **Rāma**, marked with the signs of Viṣṇu (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa 18*). This is the **Rāma-Navamī** birth.

## The exile — grief and dignity (Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa)
- **Joy at the coronation announced** - when Rāma's consecration as *yuvarāja* is proclaimed, Kausalyā, having waited a lifetime for her son's elevation, prepares worship and thanksgiving (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā 20*).
- **Kausalyā's lament** - learning of the fourteen-year exile, Rāma's mother grieves bitterly and at first **would forbid him to go**, pleading that a son's duty to a mother equals his duty to a father (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā 20–21*).
- **Rāma reconciles her to dharma** - Rāma answers that obedience to a father's word is the highest dharma and cannot be set aside even at a mother's wish; he asks not her permission only but her **blessing**, and she yields, performing rites of protection (*svastyayana*) for his safety (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā 21, 24–25*).
- **The mother's blessing (āśīrvāda)** - Kausalyā invokes every guardian power — the deities, the directions, the rivers, the great sages — to ward Rāma through the Daṇḍaka forest; this benediction is among the most cited passages of maternal devotion in the epic (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā 25*).
- **Reproach to Daśaratha** - after Rāma departs, Kausalyā, in her anguish, **rebukes Daśaratha** for his weakness before Kaikeyī — a rare moment of the senior queen's anger — yet she does not abandon him, and the dying king's last torment is guilt before her (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā 42–43*).
- **Daśaratha's confession (the Śravaṇa-kumāra curse)** - in his final hours the broken king tells Kausalyā the secret of his youth: how he once killed the ascetic boy **Śravaṇa** by sound-aimed arrow and was cursed by the boy's blind parents to die of grief for a son — a curse now come to term (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā 63–64*).
- **Widowhood and the long wait** - Daśaratha dies of grief; Kausalyā, now widowed queen-mother, endures the fourteen years in Ayodhyā under Bharata's regency, sustaining herself on hope of Rāma's return (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā 66; Yuddha Kāṇḍa*).

## Devotion, counsel, and character
- **Embodiment of dignified forbearance** - across the epic Kausalyā never schemes and never wields the boon-power Kaikeyī does; her greatness is **patience, fidelity to dharma, and a mother's surrendering love** — the moral counterweight to Kaikeyī (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa*).
- **Care for Sītā** - she blesses [Sītā](./sita.md) as a daughter on the eve of the exile, instructing her in wifely dharma and entrusting Rāma to her care in the forest (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyā 26, 39*).
- **Reunion and the return** - at Rāma's victorious homecoming and coronation (*paṭṭābhiṣeka*), the queen-mothers are restored to joy; Kausalyā at last sees her son enthroned as king of Ayodhyā (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa 128*).

## The Kausalyā-suprabhātam framing
- **"Kausalyā-supraja Rāma..."** - the most famous Vaiṣṇava **suprabhātam** (dawn-awakening hymn), the *Veṅkaṭeśa Suprabhātam* of **Prativādibhayaṅkaram Aṇṇaṅgarācārya** (14th–15th c.), opens by **invoking Kausalyā** — *"Kausalyā-supraja Rāma! pūrvā-sandhyā pravartate..."* — quoting Viśvāmitra's call to wake the boy Rāma (Bāla Kāṇḍa) and applying it to the awakening of the deity (*Veṅkaṭeśa Suprabhātam; cf. Vālmīki, Bāla Kāṇḍa*). [scholarly]
- **Kausalyā as the auspicious mother-name** - because this verse is sung at dawn in countless Viṣṇu temples (preeminently Tirumala), Kausalyā's name is, for many devotees, the **first word of the liturgical day** — an afterlife of veneration far exceeding her narrative footprint (*Śrīvaiṣṇava liturgical tradition*). [regional]
- **Tulsīdās's Kausalyā** - in the *Rāmacaritamānasa*, Kausalyā's vision of the infant Rāma revealing his cosmic Viṣṇu-form, and her tender bewilderment, became a beloved devotional set-piece, deepening her into a figure of *vātsalya-bhakti* (motherly love of God) (*Tulsīdās, Rāmacaritamānasa, Bāla-kāṇḍa*). [regional]
