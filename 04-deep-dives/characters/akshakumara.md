# Akṣakumāra — the Son Rāvaṇa Lost Before the War

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Akṣa (Akṣakumāra)** — a young son of **[Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md)**, sent out against **[Hanumān](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md)** in the wrecked Aśoka grove and killed there; the last champion Laṅkā spends before **[Indrajit](./indrajit-meghnad.md)** is called, and the only one of Rāvaṇa's sons to die before the war begins. His whole episode is [Sundara Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md) 47; his house is catalogued at [Paulastya / rākṣasa vaṃśa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/paulastya-rakshasa-vamsha.md) and [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* his death falls in the **[Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the present 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara, in the months between Sītā's abduction and the crossing of the sea. On the academic clock the episode sits in the **Sundara Kāṇḍa**, part of the epic's oldest and least disputed core. [scholarly]

## Name and identity

- **Akṣa (अक्ष) / Akṣakumāra** — the form Vālmīki's own sarga uses is the short **Akṣa**, with *kumāra* ("prince, boy") appended as a title rather than a name: *Akṣa-kumāra*, "the prince Akṣa" (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍa* 47).
- **Akṣayakumāra** — the longer form under which he is far better known, and the one most retellings and most rosters use. It is a distinct word (*akṣaya*, "undecaying") and the corpus records both without ruling on how the substitution arose. [disputed — form of the name]
- **A son of Rāvaṇa; the mother unstated** — Vālmīki names him simply as Rāvaṇa's son and gives no mother. Later tradition commonly reckons him **[Mandodarī](./mandodari.md)**'s, which would make him full brother to Indrajit; the text does not say so, and Rāvaṇa's other sons (Atikāya by Dhānyamālinī, and the rest) are borne by co-wives (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* 47; motherhood per later tradition). [disputed]
- **Young, and drawn as young** — the epic is explicit that he is a boy sent out among veterans: violent, brilliantly skilled, and utterly without the caution of the commanders who preceded him. His youth is the point of the scene, and Hanumān is made to notice it (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* 47).

## The ladder of champions — where he stands (Sundara Kāṇḍa 42–48)

Vālmīki spends seven sargas raising the stakes on the intruder in the Aśoka grove, one rank at a time, and Akṣa is the second-to-last rung.

- **The kiṅkaras (42)** — Rāvaṇa's household guard, sent first and destroyed with an iron bar; Hanumān makes his war-manifesto over their bodies.
- **The caitya-prāsāda and its hundred guards (43)** — Hanumān climbs the city's sanctuary-tower, uproots a gold-worked pillar, whirls it until the friction fires the building, and kills the hundred who come at him.
- **Jambumālī (44)** — **[Prahasta](./prahasta.md)'s** son, great-tusked and bow-armed, sent separately as the first named champion; killed with the same iron bar.
- **The seven sons of the chief minister (45)** — Prahasta's remaining sons, killed at the gateway with nothing but palms, feet, fists, nails, chest and thighs.
- **The five senāpatis (46)** — **Virūpākṣa, Yūpākṣa, Durdhara, Praghasa** and **Bhāsakarṇa**, the named army-commanders, killed with a chariot-crash, a sāla tree and a hurled mountain-top, and their troops with them.
  - *Note (a namesake collision the text does not resolve):* three of these five names also stand in the Uttara Kāṇḍa's cousin-roster — **Praghasa** and **Bhāsakarṇa** among **Sumālī's** ten sons, **Virūpākṣa** among **Mālyavān's** seven (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5) — and retellings and rosters routinely take them for the same rākṣasas. Vālmīki does not: *Sundara* **46.2–3** gives the five no patronymic and no house, only their rank and their courage. Against the merge stands the epic's own reuse of the names — a **Praghasa** killed by Sugrīva and a **Virūpākṣa** by Lakṣmaṇa at *Yuddha* **43.24–25**, and a further **Virūpākṣa** felled by Sugrīva at *Yuddha* **96.30–32**, all long after the grove-battle. The corpus records the collision and merges nothing. [disputed] See [Paulastya / rākṣasa vaṃśa §7](../../03-lineages-vamsha/paulastya-rakshasa-vamsha.md).
- **Akṣa (47)** — and only then does Rāvaṇa send his own son.
- **Indrajit (48)** — and only after Akṣa falls does he send the crown prince, with the Brahmāstra.

## The duel (Sundara Kāṇḍa 47)

- **The commission** — with five generals dead, Rāvaṇa turns to Akṣa, and the boy goes gladly. He rides out in a chariot **plated with armour of highly refined gold**, yoked to **eight excellent horses**, carrying a bow and a sword — the equipment of a prince, and better than anything sent before him (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* 47).
- **He hurts Hanumān** — the fight is not one-sided. Akṣa looses arrows with **golden shafts and golden feathering** that find their mark and draw blood, fighting, the text says, with a prowess the celestials might own. Vālmīki gives him the only sustained exchange of the whole grove-battle (*Sundara* 47).
- **Hanumān's assessment** — the vānara stops to admire him: *"the mighty Akṣa, with the lustre of the rising sun, is performing a great deed."* Then, noting that the boy's strength is **growing** as the fight goes on rather than flagging, he draws the conclusion that ends it: **"it is not proper to neglect a spreading fire"** (*Sundara* 47). The line is the epic's justification for killing a child in single combat, and it is put in the killer's mouth as a compliment.
- **The kill** — Hanumān first destroys the chariot, striking down the **eight horses with blows of his palms**. Akṣa springs into the sky with bow and sword still in hand; Hanumān seizes him **by the feet**, whirls him about "as one seizes a great serpent," and dashes him to the ground, breaking his arms, thighs, hips and neck (*Sundara* 47).
- **A killing without contempt** — the corpus flags the contrast with the surrounding sargas. The kiṅkaras, the guards and the generals are destroyed in a line or two apiece; Akṣa gets a full sarga, a description of his gear, an assessment of his courage, and a death by hand rather than by weapon. Vālmīki uses him to establish what Hanumān is fighting at the top of Laṅkā's order, not at the bottom. [scholarly]

## Consequences

- **Indrajit is called (Sundara 48)** — Rāvaṇa hears of his son's death, feels the anger, and **composes himself** rather than acting on it: "reconciling his mind," he sends his eldest, **Meghanāda/Indrajit**, with a long charge on his mastery of the mystic missiles and an order to stop the destruction of the army. Akṣa's death is what puts Indrajit into the story, and therefore what puts the **Brahmāstra** and the capture into it (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* 48).
- **The court scene it poisons** — the embassy that follows is unlike any other in the epic. Bound and dragged before the throne (*Sundara* 48–52), Hanumān delivers Rāma's demand to a king who is **hearing it from his son's killer**, and Rāvaṇa's order for his death — commuted by **Vibhīṣaṇa** on the law that an envoy may be mutilated but not killed — is argued over that fact. The tail-burning follows (*Sundara* 52–53).
- **The first family death** — Laṅkā's losses to this point have all been servants and officers. Akṣa is the first of Rāvaṇa's own blood to fall, and he falls **before the war**, to a single unarmed messenger — the demonstration on which Rāma's whole strategy of crossing the sea is afterwards built (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* 47; see [Sundara Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md)). [scholarly]

## Variants and afterlife

- *Variant (Tulsīdās, Rāmcaritmānas, Sundarkāṇḍ):* the episode survives the compression that removes most of the grove-battle's named champions — Hanumān's killing of **Akṣayakumāra** is retained as the provocation that brings Meghanāda out, though without the chariot-duel's detail or Hanumān's admiring soliloquy (*Tulsīdās, Rāmcaritmānas*; see [Variant & Regional Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md)). [late text]
- *Variant (mother):* the identification of his mother as Mandodarī is widespread in later genealogy and absent from Vālmīki (see above). [disputed]
- *Performance tradition:* the Aśoka-grove sequence — pillar, tail, and the whirling of the prince by the heels — is one of the most reliably staged passages of Rāmlīlā and of the Southeast Asian dance-dramas, where Akṣa is a named role. [folk] [regional]
- *Roster note:* Rāvaṇa's sons across the epic and the later lists — **Meghanāda/Indrajit, Akṣa, Atikāya, Narāntaka, Devāntaka, Triśiras** — vary in count and in mother-assignment by text; only Indrajit and Akṣa are individually developed by Vālmīki (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*; later genealogies). [open-ended roster]
