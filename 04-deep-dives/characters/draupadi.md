# Draupadī

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on the fire-born Pāñcāla princess, common wife of the five Pāṇḍavas, whose disrobing in the Kuru sabhā is the moral hinge of the **Mahābhārata**. Up to [Epic Characters](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md).
> Story-arc context: she enters in the [Ādi Parva](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md), is staked in the [Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md), endures exile in the [Vana](../mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md) and [Virāṭa](../mahabharata/04-virata-parva.md) Parvas, and falls on the [Mahāprasthānika](../mahabharata/17-mahaprasthanika-parva.md) ascent. Two clocks: the epic is *itihāsa* within this 28th Mahāyuga, the war falling "in the interval between Kali and Dvāpara" (*Mahābhārata* 1.2.9; commonly 3138 BCE in traditional reckoning, a few decades before the Kali epoch of 3102 BCE); academic dating of the text spans c. 400 BCE–400 CE. [scholarly]
> Citation key: bare numbers (e.g. 1.155.50) are the Pune Critical Edition (CE); "*1827" = a star passage in the CE apparatus; "App. I no. 100" = a longer passage relegated to the CE appendix; "Ganguli §" = the vulgate translation's section.

## Names & Epithets
- **Draupadī** - "daughter of Drupada," the Pāñcāla king; her commonest patronymic across the epic (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Kṛṣṇā** - her *birth name*, given by the brahmins at the rite "because she was dark in colour" (*kṛṣṇety evābruvan kṛṣṇāṃ kṛṣṇābhūt sā hi varṇataḥ*, *Mahābhārata* 1.155.50; Ganguli §169). The feminine of Kṛṣṇa; reading this as a deliberate pairing with Kṛṣṇa Vāsudeva is interpretive. [scholarly] He is not her cousin but her husbands' (Kuntī/Pṛthā is the daughter of Śūra, Vasudeva's father, 1.104.1); she calls herself his *sakhī*, "friend" (3.13.53).
- **Yājñasenī** - "daughter of Yajñasena," another name of Drupada (1.153.7, 1.182.1); the gloss "she born of the yajña (sacrificial fire)" is a popular secondary etymology, not the text's. [folk]
- **Pāñcālī** - "the Pāñcāla woman," after her father's kingdom (*Mahābhārata*).
- **Sairandhrī** - her incognito alias in Virāṭa's court. She explains that sairandhrīs are unprotected serving-women who go about the world (*sairandhryo 'rakṣitā loke bhujiṣyāḥ*), and she offers herself as "skilled at dressing hair" (*Mahābhārata* 4.3.16–17, 4.8).
- **Ayonijā** - "not womb-born," a class she shares with Sītā; she rises as a maiden (*kumārī*) from the middle of the altar. The epic uses the word of her: "Kṛṣṇā's being not womb-born, at Drupada's great rite" (1.153.8), and Indra calls her *ayonijā* in heaven (18.4.9).
- **Pañcakanyā** - one of the "five maidens" of the popular śloka *ahalyā draupadī sītā tārā mandodarī tathā / pañcakanyāḥ smaren nityaṃ mahāpātakanāśinīḥ* (Ahalyā, Draupadī, Sītā, Tārā, Mandodarī); a widespread reading puts **Kuntī** in place of Sītā. Recollecting them destroys great sins (*later devotional śloka*). Primary locus not verified. [late text]

## Birth of Fire — the Pāñcāla Yajña
- **The vengeance-sacrifice of Drupada** - captured by Arjuna for Droṇa and left with only half his kingdom (1.128), King Drupada seeks a son to kill Droṇa. The younger brahmin **Upayāja** refuses him for a year, then sends him to his elder brother **Yāja**; Yāja performs the rite with Upayāja's help (*Mahābhārata* 1.155.1–33). The epic adds that Drupada privately always wanted to give Kṛṣṇā to Arjuna (1.176.8).
- **Dhṛṣṭadyumna first, then Draupadī** - at the end of the oblation Yāja calls the queen, **Pṛṣatī**, to receive the twin offspring. She asks him to wait: her mouth is not yet cleansed and she is wearing sacred perfumes. Yāja replies that an oblation he cooked and Upayāja consecrated will bear fruit "whether you come or stay" and offers it into the fire (1.155.34–37). From the fire springs **Dhṛṣṭadyumna**, crowned and armoured, fated to slay Droṇa; then from the middle of the altar rises Draupadī, dark, lotus-eyed, with dark curling hair and a blue-lotus fragrance carrying for a krośa, with "no equal on earth" (1.155.37–43). Pṛṣatī then asks that the two "know no mother but me," and Yāja agrees (1.155.47–48). This continuous CE narrative is the textual ground of her *ayonijā* status.
- **A celestial voice** - a bodiless voice proclaims that this dark woman, best of all women, will lead the kṣatriyas to destruction, accomplish the gods' purpose in time, and bring great danger on the kṣatriyas (*Mahābhārata* 1.155.44–45; Ganguli §169 renders "...many a danger will overtake the Kauravas").

## Prior-Birth Traditions (catalog of variants)
The epic gives *more than one* explanation for her polyandry, and recensions and Purāṇas add more — preserve all. Note that in the CE, Vyāsa joins A and B into one account: the ascetic's daughter *is* the heavenly Śrī, reborn for the Pāṇḍavas (1.189.47–49).
- *Variant A — Incarnation of Śrī/Lakṣmī, the "Five Indras" (Pañcendra-upākhyāna) (Mahābhārata 1.189):* Vyāsa tells Drupada that Indra, following a weeping woman whose tears turn to golden lotuses in the Gaṅgā, finds Śiva playing dice with a young woman on the Himālaya and arrogantly claims lordship of the universe. Śiva's glance paralyses him, and Śiva shuts him in a cave with four former Indras. All five are to be born as men: the four former Indras become four Pāṇḍavas, and the current Indra gives a portion of himself as the fifth, Arjuna. The weeping woman, **Śrī**, is appointed their common wife (1.189.10–33). Vyāsa then grants Drupada divine sight to see their former bodies (1.189.35–39). The Svargārohaṇa Parva confirms her in heaven as Śrī (18.4.9). → cf. [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](../krishna-lila.md) on Viṣṇu's company descending (Viṣṇu's black and white hairs become Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, 1.189.31).
- *Variant B — The ascetic's daughter (Mahābhārata 1.157.6–14, retold 1.189.41–46):* an unnamed sage's daughter, beautiful but unable to find a husband "through her own past deeds," pleases Śiva with austerities and asks "again and again" for a husband endowed with every virtue. Śiva grants five husbands; when she protests, he answers that she asked five times (*pañcakṛtvas tvayā uktaḥ patiṃ dehi*) and it will come true in another body. The CE boon contains **no virginity clause**. The marvel that she "became a virgin again each day" as the brothers married her on successive days is told separately, at the wedding (1.190.13–14). Ganguli's vulgate has the same.
  - *Variant (Southern recension, CE App. I no. 100):* here the boon itself is extended, and the maiden is Nālāyanī (Variant C). When she objects that a woman with many husbands falls from dharma, Śiva answers that "women of old were unrestricted, and are purified by their menses" (l. 105). She then asks that her virginity (*kaumāra*) be restored at every union, and Śiva grants that in her next body she will regain it with each of the five (App. I no. 100, ll. 91–114). [interpolation]
- *Variant C — Nālāyanī / Indrasenā (Southern recension; CE App. I no. 100 after 1.188.22, recalled in no. 102 after 1.189.46) [disputed]:* devoted wife of the aged, leprous sage **Maudgalya (Mudgala)**. When his thumb (*aṅguṣṭha*) drops off into his food as he eats, she takes it out and eats the food without disgust. Pleased, he grants her a boon, and at her wish divides himself fivefold to enjoy her, roaming the worlds with her as mountain and river, tree and creeper. Sated, he turns to renunciation. When she begs him to continue, he curses her to be born as Drupada's daughter with five husbands. She performs the **five-fires** penance (*pañcatapā*), and Śiva grants five husbands "of Mahendra's form and valour" (App. I no. 100, ll. 7–90). Excluded from the CE text. Ganguli's vulgate lacks it. The CE elsewhere knows "Nāḍāyanī Indrasenā" only as a wife who served her aged husband (4.20.8). [late text]
- *Variant D — Composite goddess (recensional and sectarian) [disputed]:*
  - *Variant (Southern recension, CE App. I no. 102):* the same appendix layers several identifications. The Pāṇḍavas are Indra's dharma, strength and virility shared out to Yama, Vāyu and the Aśvins, so "these Pāṇḍavas are one Indra" and "Draupadī is the one Paulomī (Śacī)." They are the five faces of Śiva, with Draupadī as Pārvatī. Arjuna is Viṣṇu and the other four his arms, with Pāñcālī as Kamalā. They are the five fires, with Kṛṣṇā as Svāhā (App. I no. 102, ll. 5–32). [interpolation]
  - *Variant (Mādhva tradition, Madhva's Mahābhārata-tātparya-nirṇaya 19.155):* the consorts of the five Indras — Śyāmalā (Yama/Dharma), Bhāratī (Vāyu), Śacī (Indra), and Uṣā (the Aśvins) — are present in Draupadī as partial incarnations (*aṃśa-avatāras*), so each brother weds his own goddess. Known here only from a secondary quotation; the Sanskrit locus was not verified. The frequently repeated attribution of a five-goddess list (adding Pārvatī) to the "Nārada / Vāyu Purāṇa" could not be traced; locus not verified.
- *Variant E — Śacī, wife of the one Indra (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa 5) [late text]:* the Purāṇa opens with Jaimini asking how Draupadī could be wife of five. The birds answer that Indra lost three things through three wrongs. His splendour (*tejas*) entered Dharma when he slew Tvaṣṭṛ's son (Viśvarūpa/Triśiras). His might entered Vāyu when he broke the ṛṣis' treaty and slew Vṛtra. His beauty went to the Aśvins when he violated Ahalyā in Gautama's form. From these portions came Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma and the twins; Arjuna was born from half of Indra's own power. So Indra became incarnate in five forms, and his wife was born from Agni as Kṛṣṇā, "the wife of Indra alone, and of no one else" (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* canto 5, Pargiter's translation). Verse numbers not verified.
- *Variant F — Vedavatī / Māyā-Sītā (Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.16; Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa) [late text]:* **Vedavatī**, daughter of Kuśadhvaja, curses Rāvaṇa and is reborn as Sītā. Agni hides the true Sītā and leaves a shadow-Sītā (Māyā- or Chāyā-Sītā), whom Rāvaṇa abducts. After the fire-ordeal restores the real Sītā, the shadow asks Rāma and Agni what she should do. They send her to do penance at **Puṣkara**, where she becomes "Svarga-Lakṣmī" (cf. *svargaśrīḥ*, *Mahābhārata* 1.189.48) and rises from Drupada's sacrificial pit. She is "*trihāyaṇī*": Vedavatī in the Satya Yuga, Sītā in the Tretā, Draupadī in the Dvāpara (Devī-Bhāgavata 9.16.49–53). Asked why she had five husbands, Nārāyaṇa explains that at Puṣkara she asked Śiva "grant me a husband" five times (9.16.55–63; Vijñānānanda's translation; Mani, *Purāṇic Encyclopaedia* s.v. Pāñcālī, agrees). The parallel Brahmavaivarta passage (Prakṛti-khaṇḍa ch. 14) was seen only in V. D. N. Rao's summary (locus not verified). This threads Draupadī into the same fire-and-shadow lineage as Sītā. → cf. the chāyā-Sītā in the [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md).
- *Variant G — "Fourteen husbands" amplification [folk]:* a reported folk expansion in which the maiden asks so often that she is first eligible for many more husbands (often "fourteen") before the number is fixed at five. Not in the CE, its apparatus or Ganguli; no written source located (locus not verified). [folk]

## The Svayaṃvara — Winning Draupadī
- **The test of the aerial machine** - Drupada has a stiff bow made that is almost impossible to bend, and an artificial machine (*yantra*) hung in the air with a golden target. Whoever strings the bow and, with five arrows, hits the target through the hole in the machine wins his daughter (*Mahābhārata* 1.176.9–11, 1.176.34; Ganguli: "shoot the mark through the orifice of the machine").
  - *Variant (popular/regional retellings):* the target is a revolving fish whose eye must be pierced by looking only at its reflection in water below. This image is absent from the CE and from Ganguli's vulgate. [folk]
- **Arjuna in disguise** - the Pāṇḍavas, thought dead after the lac-house (Lākṣāgr̥ha), attend as brahmins. **Arjuna** strings the bow and pierces the target, which falls to the ground through the hole. Kṛṣṇā places the garland on him and follows him out (*Mahābhārata* 1.179.16–23). The assembled kings rage that Drupada would give her to a brahmin, because a svayaṃvara is for kṣatriyas (1.180.1–6).
- **Karṇa at the bow — variant-dependent:**
  - *Variant (CE constituted text):* no individual attempt by Karṇa is narrated. The kings fail to string the bow (1.178.15–17), and the brahmins later count Karṇa and Śalya among the famous kings who "could not bend it" (1.179.4). Draupadī speaks no word against him.
  - *Variant (vulgate and some manuscripts; CE apparatus *1827 at 1.178.17; Ganguli):* Karṇa strings the bow and fits the arrows, and Draupadī cries aloud "I will not choose a sūta" (*nāhaṃ varayāmi sūtam*). He laughs bitterly, glances at the Sun, and casts the bow aside. [interpolation]
  - *Variant (other manuscripts; CE apparatus *1830, *1834):* Karṇa raises the bow but it springs back and strikes him when it is "a hair's breadth" (*romamātre*) from being strung. Reportedly the Southern (Kumbhakonam) reading. The insult is thus a recensional amplification. → see [Karṇa](../karna.md).
- **The brawl and Balarāma/Kr̥ṣṇa** - Kr̥ṣṇa recognizes the "brahmins" as the Pāṇḍavas and points them out to Balarāma (1.178.9–10). When the kings attack, Bhīma and Arjuna fight them off: Arjuna turns back Karṇa and Bhīma throws Śalya. Kr̥ṣṇa calms the kings, saying she was won fairly (*Mahābhārata* 1.181).

## Wife of the Five Pāṇḍavas
- **Kuntī's accidental command** - Bhīma and Arjuna announce her at the potter's hut as "alms" (*bhikṣā*). **Kuntī**, inside and unseeing, says "all of you enjoy it together," then sees the girl and is dismayed at what she has said (*Mahābhārata* 1.182.1–5). A mother's (guru's) word must hold (1.188.15–16).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's ruling & Vyāsa's sanction** - seeing all his brothers smitten and fearing a split between them, Yudhiṣṭhira declares Draupadī wife of all five (1.182.11–15). To Drupada's objection he cites the precedent of Jaṭilā Gautamī, wife of seven ṛṣis (1.188.14). **Vyāsa** privately recounts her prior births to Drupada (1.189), and Drupada consents to the unprecedented polyandry (1.190.1–4). She weds the five on successive days, Yudhiṣṭhira first (1.190.5–13).
- **Nārada's warning and the brothers' compact** - sage **Nārada** tells them the tale of the asura brothers **Sunda and Upasunda**, who killed each other over Tilottamā, and urges them to prevent any rift over Draupadī (1.200–204). In his presence the brothers themselves agree that whoever sees another seated with Draupadī shall live twelve years in the forest as a brahmacārin (*Mahābhārata* 1.204.27–28). Arjuna breaks it, entering the armoury where Yudhiṣṭhira sits with Kṛṣṇā, to recover a brahmin's stolen cattle. He refuses Yudhiṣṭhira's pardon and goes into exile (1.205.5–30).
  - *Variant (some manuscripts; CE apparatus *2012 at 1.204.27):* the compact adds that Kṛṣṇā should live a year in each brother's house in turn — the "one-year rotation" of popular retellings. It is not in the CE text or in Ganguli. [interpolation]
- **The Upapāṇḍavas (Draupadeyas)** - one son by each husband, born a year apart: **Prativindhya** (Yudhiṣṭhira), **Sutasoma** (Bhīma), **Śrutakarman** (Arjuna), **Śatānīka** (Nakula), **Śrutasena** (Sahadeva) (*Mahābhārata* 1.213.71–79). The CE itself varies: Arjuna's son is **Śrutakīrti** at 1.57.102 and 1.90.82, and 1.90.82 also makes Sahadeva's son **Śrutakarman**. All five are slaughtered asleep in Aśvatthāman's night-raid (*Sauptika Parva* — see [Sauptika Parva](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md)). In heaven they are revealed as gandharvas (18.4.11).
- **Co-wife of note** - she is chief queen but not sole wife: Yudhiṣṭhira weds Devikā, Bhīma Baladharā, Arjuna Subhadrā, Nakula Kareṇuvatī, Sahadeva Vijayā (1.90.83–87). A famous Vana Parva dialogue with **Satyabhāmā** has Draupadī expound how a wife wins and keeps her husbands (*Mahābhārata* 3.222–224).

## The Dice Game & the Disrobing (Vastrāharaṇa)
The episode of the [Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md) — the epic's ethical fulcrum.
- **The rigged game** - **Śakuni** plays for **Duryodhana**. Yudhiṣṭhira loses wealth, kingdom, his brothers and himself, and then, with Śakuni naming her, **stakes Draupadī** (*Mahābhārata* 2.58).
- **Dragged by the hair** - the usher Prātikāmin is sent first, and she sends back her question. Then **Duḥśāsana** (Duryodhana's brother) seizes her by her long dark hair and hauls her into the men's assembly, though she pleads that she is menstruating and wears a single cloth (*Mahābhārata* 2.60.1–25).
- **The unanswerable question** - through the usher, Draupadī asks: *whom did you lose first, yourself or me?* (2.60.7–8). In the hall, **Bhīṣma** answers that dharma is too subtle for him to decide. One who is not his own master cannot stake another's property, he says, yet a wife is subject to her husband, and Yudhiṣṭhira himself has said "I am won" (2.60.40–42). The kings sit silent. **Vikarṇa**, a Kaurava, alone argues for her (2.61.12–24): Yudhiṣṭhira staked her while gripped by the gambler's vice; she is the common wife of all five; he staked her after he had already lost himself; and it was Śakuni who named her. Later, after the disrobing, **Vidura** rebukes the assembly for leaving her question unanswered and tells the tale of Prahlāda and Sudhanvan (2.61.51–80).
- **Karṇa's insult** - Karṇa retorts that the gods ordained one husband for a woman. One subject to many is a *bandhakī* (harlot), so bringing her in a single cloth, or even naked, is no wonder. He orders Duḥśāsana to strip the Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī of their garments (*Mahābhārata* 2.61.26–38).
- **The endless cloth:** as Duḥśāsana pulls at her garment, "another garment of the same kind appeared, again and again." Garments heap up until he sits down, exhausted and ashamed, and Draupadī stays covered (*Mahābhārata* 2.61.40–48). The CE text names no agent.
  - *Variant (many manuscripts; CE apparatus *542–*550 at 2.61.40):* she calls on **Kr̥ṣṇa**/Govinda ("Govinda, dweller in Dvārakā, beloved of the gopīs..."). In *543 Kṛṣṇa is moved and comes on foot, and *551 credits "his grace" (*prasādāt*). Other apparatus readings name **Dharma** as the agent: "Dharma, unseen, covered her with heaps of garments" (*544) and "by Dharma's protection" (*553 at 2.61.41). The vulgate (Ganguli) combines the two: she prays to Kṛṣṇa, he is moved and sets out, and "the illustrious Dharma, remaining unseen, covered her." [interpolation]
  - *Variant (popular/devotional):* she lets go of her own sārī, throws up both arms and cries to Kr̥ṣṇa, who supplies the infinite cloth in repayment of a debt. Once she tore a strip of her sārī to bind his bleeding finger, a story now tied to Rakṣā-bandhan. The finger story is not in the Mahābhārata. [folk] → see [Kr̥ṣṇa-līlā](../krishna-lila.md).
  - *Variant (text-critical) [scholarly]:* the CE keeps the miracle but relegates Kr̥ṣṇa's intervention to the apparatus. **Franklin Edgerton**, editor of the CE Sabhā Parva (1944), is reported to treat Draupadī's prayer to Kṛṣṇa as a later addition and to leave the miracle unexplained in the constituted text. His wording was not seen (locus not verified). Yet the CE Udyoga Parva has Kr̥ṣṇa recall that "Kṛṣṇā cried 'Govinda!' to me when I was far away," a debt that "has grown and does not leave my heart" (5.58.21). So the CE elsewhere presupposes the appeal. [disputed]
- **The vows** - **Bhīma** swears to break open Duḥśāsana's chest and drink his blood (*Mahābhārata* 2.61.43–46). When Duryodhana bares his left thigh to Draupadī, Bhīma swears to smash it with his mace (2.63.10–14). Both are fulfilled in the war (10.16.30; Bhīma later tells Gāndhārī the blood never passed his lips and teeth, 11.14.15–18).
  - *Draupadī's hair:* in the CE she leaves for exile with **hair unbound**, foretelling that the Kuru women will one day enter Hāstinapura the same way (2.70.9, 2.71.18–20). Before the war she holds out to Kṛṣṇa "this hair seized by Duḥśāsana's hand" and wants to see Duḥśāsana's arm cut off and lying in the dust (5.80.33–39).
  - *Variant (Veṇīsaṃhāra) [late text]:* in Bhaṭṭa Nārāyaṇa's Sanskrit play *Veṇīsaṃhāra* ("The Binding of the Braid"), the vow is mainly **Bhīma's**: he will bind Draupadī's hair with a hand wet with **Duryodhana's** blood (Act I), and he does so in Act VI. Draupadī makes a complementary vow. Hiltebeitel calls the play the first clear expression of the theme, while arguing that the epic hints at it.
  - *Variant (popular and regional tradition) [regional]:* Draupadī herself vows to leave her hair unbound until she can bind or wash it in the blood of **Duḥśāsana** (or of Duryodhana). Neither form of the vow is in the CE. It is central to the South Indian Draupadī cult (Hiltebeitel, "Draupadī's Hair").
- **The two boons** - a jackal howls at Dhr̥tarāṣṭra's fire-sacrifice and asses and birds answer. When **Gāndhārī** and **Vidura** report the omen, the blind **Dhr̥tarāṣṭra** grants Draupadī boons (2.63.22–27). First she asks for Yudhiṣṭhira's freedom, so that her son Prativindhya will not be called a slave's son. Second, she asks for the other four with their chariots and bows. She refuses a third: greed destroys dharma; a vaiśya may ask one boon, a kṣatriya woman two, a king three, a brahmin a hundred (*Mahābhārata* 2.63.27–36).
- **The second dice game** - a fresh game sends the Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī into **thirteen years' exile** (twelve in the forest, one incognito), the root of Kurukṣetra (*Mahābhārata* 2.67.9–12).

## Exile — Vana Parva Highlights
- **Jayadratha's abduction** - the Sindhu king **Jayadratha**, Duryodhana's brother-in-law, carries Draupadī off from the Kāmyaka hermitage. The brothers pursue and rescue her. Bhīma spares his life only because Arjuna reminds him of Yudhiṣṭhira's order to spare him for their cousin Duḥśalā's sake (3.255.43, 3.256.6). Bhīma crops his hair into **five tufts** with a crescent-headed arrow and makes him declare "I am a slave" before freeing him (*Mahābhārata* 3.248–256, esp. 3.256.6–11).
- **Counsel and complaint** - early in the forest years Draupadī presses Yudhiṣṭhira to act rather than forbear. They debate anger and forgiveness, fate and human effort (*Mahābhārata* 3.28–33), and she remains the brothers' goad throughout the exile.

## Sairandhrī & the Kīcaka Slaying (Virāṭa Parva)
- **The maid of Sudeṣṇā** - in the thirteenth (incognito) year Draupadī serves Queen **Sudeṣṇā** of Matsya as **Sairandhrī**, a hairdresser-maid, hiding her identity (*Mahābhārata* 4.8).
- **Kīcaka's harassment** - **Kīcaka**, the queen's brother and Virāṭa's commander, lusts after her, seizes her, and kicks her in front of the king (4.13–15). At night she appeals to **Bhīma**, disguised as the cook Ballava (*Mahābhārata* 4.16–20).
- **Bhīma in disguise** - Draupadī lures Kīcaka to a night tryst in the king's dancing-hall (*nartanaśālā*). **Bhīma**, waiting in her place, pounds Kīcaka into a shapeless ball of flesh. Kīcaka's kinsmen, the 105 Upakīcakas, try to burn Draupadī on his pyre and are all killed (*Mahābhārata* 4.21–22).

## After the War & the Great Departure
- **Mother of the slain** - the night-raid leaves all five Draupadeyas dead. Draupadī vows to fast to death unless Aśvatthāman is killed, and asks for the gem he was born with on his head (*Mahābhārata* 10.11.13–20). Bhīma pursues him and brings back the gem. Aśvatthāman's life is spared as a brahmin and the guru's son. She accepts the gem: "the guru's son is my guru." At her word Yudhiṣṭhira wears it on his head (10.16.25–35). Her grief shapes the war's bitter close (*Sauptika/Strī Parvas*).
- **Empress, then renunciation** - reigning queen during the Pāṇḍava rule, she joins the brothers' final renunciation after the destruction of the Vṛṣṇis and Kṛṣṇa's departure, walking last behind the five, with a dog following (*Mahābhārata* 17.1.22–31).
- **First to fall on the ascent** - on the Himālayan climb toward heaven, Draupadī, "her yoga failing," is the **first of the six to drop dead**. When Bhīma asks why, **Yudhiṣṭhira** answers: she had "a great partiality for **Dhanañjaya (Arjuna)** above the others; she reaps the fruit of that today" (*Mahābhārata* 17.2.3–6). He walks on without looking at her (17.2.7).
- **Restored as Śrī** - in the [Svargārohaṇa Parva](../mahabharata/18-svargarohana-parva.md) Yudhiṣṭhira sees her in heaven, and Indra explains: "this is Śrī, who in Draupadī's form went among humans for your sake, not born of a womb... fashioned by the Trident-bearer (Śiva) for your delight." This closes prior-birth Variant A (*Mahābhārata* 18.4.7–10).
