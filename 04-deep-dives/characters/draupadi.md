# Draupadī

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on the fire-born Pāñcāla princess, common wife of the five Pāṇḍavas, whose disrobing in the Kuru sabhā is the moral hinge of the **Mahābhārata**. Up to [Epic Characters](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md).
> Story-arc context: she enters in the [Ādi Parva](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md), is staked in the [Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md), endures exile in the [Vana](../mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md) and [Virāṭa](../mahabharata/04-virata-parva.md) Parvas, and falls on the [Mahāprasthānika](../mahabharata/17-mahaprasthanika-parva.md) ascent. Two clocks: the epic is *itihāsa* within this 28th Mahāyuga (post-3102 BCE for the war); academic dating of the text spans c. 400 BCE–400 CE. [scholarly]

## Names & Epithets
- **Draupadī** - "daughter of Drupada," the Pāñcāla king; her commonest patronymic across the epic (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Kṛṣṇā** - her *birth name*, "the dark one," from her dark complexion; the feminine of Kṛṣṇa, marking a deliberate twinship with her divine cousin-friend (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva 1.169*).
- **Yājñasenī** - "daughter of Yajñasena," another name of Drupada; the title doubles as "she born of the yajña (sacrificial fire)" (*Mahābhārata*).
- **Pāñcālī** - "the Pāñcāla woman," after her father's kingdom (*Mahābhārata*).
- **Sairandhrī** - "skilled chamber-maid," her incognito alias in Virāṭa's court (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva*).
- **Ayonijā** - "not womb-born," a class she shares with Sītā; she rises full-grown from the altar (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Pāñcakanyā** - listed among the five ideal women (Ahalyā, Draupadī, Kuntī/Tārā, Sītā/Mandodarī) whose recollection cleanses sin (*later devotional ślokas; sm̥ti tradition*). [late text]

## Birth of Fire — the Pāñcāla Yajña
- **The vengeance-sacrifice of Drupada** - humiliated and stripped of half his kingdom by Droṇa (via Arjuna), King Drupada seeks a son to kill Droṇa; the brahmin brothers **Yāja** and **Upayāja** conduct the great fire-rite (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Dhṛṣṭadyumna first, then Draupadī** - from the flames first springs **Dhṛṣṭadyumna**, armored and prophesied to slay Droṇa; immediately after rises Draupadī, a full-grown dark maiden, "the most beautiful woman on earth," destined to bring on the destruction of the kṣatriyas (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva 1.169*).
  - *Variant (Ādi Parva):* the priest's wife, told to consume the offering to conceive, asks to bathe and perfume first; **Yāja**, unwilling to wait, pours the oblation directly into the altar — so Draupadī is born of pure fire, not of a womb. This grounds the *ayonijā* status.
- **A celestial voice** - an aśarīravāṇī announces at her birth that this dark woman will lead the kṣatriya race to ruin and accomplish the gods' purpose (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).

## Prior-Birth Traditions (catalog of variants)
The epic offers *multiple, mutually incompatible* explanations for her polyandry — preserve all.
- *Variant A — Incarnation of Śrī/Lakṣmī (Ādi Parva 1.196) [Critical Edition]:* Indra speaks insolently to Śiva at dice and is cursed; five proud "Indras" are sent to earth as the Pāṇḍavas, and **Śrī (Lakṣmī)**, goddess of sovereignty, descends with them as Draupadī — hence one wife for the five. The Svargārohaṇa Parva confirms her seated as Śrī in heaven. → cf. [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](../krishna-lila.md) on Viṣṇu's company descending.
- *Variant B — The ascetic's daughter (Ādi Parva 1.189) [Critical Edition]:* an unnamed sage's daughter, unmarried, pleases Śiva and asks five times for a virtuous husband (whether by yearning or repetition); Śiva grants five husbands in her next life, with the boon that she **regains her virginity after each union** and is purified monthly. The fivefold ask is read as binding.
- *Variant C — Nālāyanī / Indrasenā (Southern recension only) [disputed]:* devoted wife of the aged, leprous sage **Maudgalya (Mudgala)**; he assumes five handsome forms to please her, then tires and returns to asceticism. When she begs continuance he curses her to five husbands; she does penance amid the **five fires** and Śiva grants the boon of five Indra-equal husbands. Explicitly **absent in the Northern version and excluded from the BORI Critical Edition**. [late text]
- *Variant D — Composite goddess avatāra (Nārada / Vāyu Purāṇa) [folk/Purāṇic]:* Draupadī is a fivefold descent — Śyāmalā (Dharma), Bhāratī (Vāyu), Śacī (Indra), Uṣā (the Aśvins), Pārvatī (Śiva) — matching each Pāṇḍava to a divine consort. Reconciles the polyandry as five marriages, not one. [disputed]
- *Variant E — The Vedavatī / Māyā-Sītā parallel [folk]:* in some retellings she is linked to **Vedavatī**, who cursed Rāvaṇa; Vedavatī comes "in place of Sītā" to cause Rāvaṇa's death while **Agni hides the true Sītā** (the shadow-Sītā / Māyā-Sītā motif). This threads Draupadī into the same fire-and-shadow lineage as Sītā. → cf. the chāyā-Sītā in the [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md). [disputed]
- *Variant F — "Fourteen husbands" amplification [folk]:* in folk expansions the ascetic-daughter repeats her plea so many times that Śiva counts the syllables; a popular telling has her eligible for far more husbands (often glossed as fourteen) before the boon is fixed at five. Not in the Critical Edition. [folk]

## The Svayaṃvara — Winning Draupadī
- **The test of the rotating fish** - Drupada sets a near-impossible mark: string a colossal bow and pierce the eye of a revolving fish seen only in its reflection in water below (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Arjuna in disguise** - the Pāṇḍavas, thought dead after the lac-house (Lākṣāgr̥ha), attend as brahmins; **Arjuna** strings the bow and pierces the target, winning her hand while the assembled kings rage (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Karṇa refused — variant-dependent:** 
  - *Variant (some Northern recensions):* Draupadī rejects **Karṇa** before he shoots, declaring "I will not wed a sūta (charioteer's son)" — a wound Karṇa nurses to the dice-game.
  - *Variant (Critical Edition and most others):* Draupadī raises **no objection**; Karṇa simply fails or is restrained. The insult is a later dramatic amplification. → see [Karṇa](../karna.md).
- **The brawl and Balarāma/Kr̥ṣṇa** - Kr̥ṣṇa recognizes the "brahmins" as the Pāṇḍavas and calms the angry suitors; Bhīma and Arjuna fight off the kings (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).

## Wife of the Five Pāṇḍavas
- **Kuntī's accidental command** - the brothers return saying "see what we have brought"; **Kuntī**, unseeing, says "share it among yourselves." The word, once uttered by a mother, must hold (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's ruling & Vyāsa's sanction** - Yudhiṣṭhira declares Draupadī wife of all five; **Vyāsa** privately recounts her prior-birth boons to Drupada, who consents to the unprecedented polyandry (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Nārada's rotation rule** - sage **Nārada**, warning of the strife two-plus brothers once suffered over a woman (Sunda–Upasunda), sets the law: Draupadī dwells one year with each husband in turn; any brother intruding on another's term goes into exile — the rule that later sends Arjuna wandering (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **The Upapāṇḍavas (Draupadeyas)** - one son by each husband: **Prativindhya** (Yudhiṣṭhira), **Sutasoma** (Bhīma), **Śrutakarman/Śrutakīrti** (Arjuna), **Śatānīka** (Nakula), **Śrutasena** (Sahadeva). All five are slaughtered asleep in Aśvatthāman's night-raid (*Mahābhārata; Sauptika Parva* — see [Sauptika Parva](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md)).
- **Co-wife of note** - she is chief queen but not sole wife (Arjuna weds Subhadrā, etc.); a famous Vana-Parva dialogue with **Satyabhāmā** has Draupadī expound the ideal wife's conduct (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva*).

## The Dice Game & the Disrobing (Vastrāharaṇa)
The episode of the [Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md) — the epic's ethical fulcrum.
- **The rigged game** - **Śakuni** plays for **Duryodhana**; Yudhiṣṭhira loses kingdom, wealth, brothers, himself — and then **stakes Draupadī** (*Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva*).
- **Dragged by the hair** - **Duḥśāsana** (Duryodhana's brother) seizes the menstruating Draupadī, clad in a single cloth, by the hair and hauls her into the men's assembly (*Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva*).
- **The unanswerable question** - Draupadī repeatedly demands: *did Yudhiṣṭhira stake himself first and so lose his freedom before staking me? — a slave cannot wager another.* The sabhā of Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Vidura falls silent, unable to rule on the dharma (*Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva*). **Vikarṇa**, a Kaurava, alone speaks for her: no one may stake a wife under śāstra.
- **Karṇa's insult** - Karṇa calls her a wanton/courtesan for having five husbands and orders her stripped, declaring a woman so won is no longer chaste (*Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva*).
- **The endless cloth — Kr̥ṣṇa's grace:** as Duḥśāsana pulls, the garment **multiplies without end**; piles of cloth heap up until he collapses exhausted and Draupadī stays covered (*Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva*).
  - *Variant (popular/devotional):* she lets go of her own sārī, throws up both arms and cries to **Kr̥ṣṇa**, who supplies the infinite cloth — repayment of a debt: once she tore a strip of her sārī to bind his bleeding finger (the Rakṣā-bandhan / Govinda-cīra tradition). → see [Kr̥ṣṇa-līlā](../krishna-lila.md).
  - *Variant (text-critical) [scholarly]:* the overt miracle of Kr̥ṣṇa's intervention is, per **Franklin Edgerton** and the BORI editors, a **later interpolation**; the oldest stratum has her simply protected/unstripped without naming Kr̥ṣṇa. [disputed]
- **The vows** - **Bhīma** swears to break Duḥśāsana's chest and drink his blood, and to smash Duryodhana's thigh; **Draupadī vows to leave her hair unbound until she binds it with Duḥśāsana's blood** (*Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva*). Both vows are fulfilled in the war.
- **The two boons** - omens (a jackal's howl) frighten the blind **Dhr̥tarāṣṭra**, who grants Draupadī two boons: the freedom of Yudhiṣṭhira, then of all the Pāṇḍavas with their arms. She refuses a third, saying greed ill-befits her (*Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva*).
- **The second dice game** - a fresh game sends the Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī to **thirteen years' exile** (twelve in forest, one incognito), the casus belli of Kurukṣetra (*Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva*).

## Exile — Vana Parva Highlights
- **Jayadratha's abduction** - the Sindhu king **Jayadratha** tries to carry Draupadī off from the forest hermitage; the brothers rescue her and shave his head in shame (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva*).
- **Counsel and complaint** - throughout the forest years Draupadī is the brothers' goad, repeatedly arguing for action against passive forbearance, debating dharma, fate, and human effort with Yudhiṣṭhira (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva*).

## Sairandhrī & the Kīcaka Slaying (Virāṭa Parva)
- **The maid of Sudeṣṇā** - in the thirteenth (incognito) year Draupadī serves Queen **Sudeṣṇā** of Matsya as **Sairandhrī**, a hairdresser-maid, hiding her identity (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva*).
- **Kīcaka's harassment** - **Kīcaka**, the queen's brother and Virāṭa's commander, lusts after and assaults her; she appeals to **Bhīma** (disguised as the cook Ballava) (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva*).
- **Bhīma in disguise** - Draupadī lures Kīcaka to a night tryst in the dancing-hall; **Bhīma**, waiting in her place, pounds Kīcaka into a shapeless ball of flesh. His kinsmen (the Upakīcakas) try to burn Draupadī on his pyre and are likewise killed (*Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva*).

## After the War & the Great Departure
- **Mother of the slain** - the night-raid leaves all five Draupadeyas dead; she demands Aśvatthāman's head, accepts instead the gem from his crest, and her grief shapes the war's bitter close (*Mahābhārata, Sauptika/Strī Parvas*).
- **Empress, then renunciation** - reigning queen during the Pāṇḍava rule, she joins the brothers' final renunciation when Kr̥ṣṇa departs and Kali advances (*Mahābhārata, Mahāprasthānika Parva*).
- **First to fall on the ascent** - on the Himālayan climb toward heaven, Draupadī is the **first of the six to drop dead**; when Bhīma asks why, **Yudhiṣṭhira** answers: though equal to all, "she had a great partiality for **Dhanañjaya (Arjuna)** — she reaps the fruit of that today" (*Mahābhārata, Mahāprasthānika Parva*). He does not look back.
- **Restored as Śrī** - in the [Svargārohaṇa Parva](../mahabharata/18-svargarohana-parva.md) Yudhiṣṭhira finds her in heaven, revealed as **Goddess Śrī/Lakṣmī**, closing the prior-birth Variant A (*Mahābhārata, Svargārohaṇa Parva*).
