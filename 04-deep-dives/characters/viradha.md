# Virādha

> The **man-eating rākṣasa of the Daṇḍaka forest** who is the **first demon Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa fight in exile** — and the first being to seize **[Sītā](./sita.md)** — in the opening sargas of the [Araṇya-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md). Invulnerable to weapons by Brahmā's boon, he is overpowered, his arms broken, and **buried alive** in a pit; dying, he sloughs the monstrous body and rises as the **gandharva Tumburu**, freed from a curse of **Kubera**. He sets the template — combat-as-liberation — for the whole forest sojourn.
> Sits in **[Tretā-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md). Episode: [Araṇya-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md), sargas **2–4**, at the threshold of the exile-forest.
> Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md) · His kind: [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md) · His mirror at the kāṇḍa's close: [Kabandha](./kabandha.md).

**Virādha** (Sanskrit *Virādha*) is a gigantic, dark, man-eating **[rākṣasa](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md)** who haunts the entrance to the **Daṇḍaka forest**, and the very first adversary [Rāma](../ramayana/README.md) and [Lakṣmaṇa](./lakshmana.md) confront once their exile leads them into the deep wilderness. He is met immediately after the brothers and Sītā cross into Daṇḍaka: a towering shape with a sunken belly, blazing eyes, a great spear, and the carcasses of slain beasts hung about him. His distinction is twofold — he is the **first to lay hands on Sītā** (long before Rāvaṇa), and he is a **cursed celestial in monstrous form**, the gandharva **Tumburu** condemned by **Kubera** to wear rākṣasa flesh until Rāma should free him. Because Brahmā's boon makes him **proof against every weapon**, the brothers cannot kill him by arrow or sword; they break his body and **bury him alive**, and his "death" is in truth his **release**. He thus inaugurates the Araṇya-kāṇḍa's recurring pattern — Rāma's violence as grace — that culminates in [Kabandha](./kabandha.md).

---

## I. The encounter — first blood in the forest (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 2)

- **Entry into Daṇḍaka** — As the three (Rāma, [Lakṣmaṇa](./lakshmana.md), [Sītā](./sita.md)) penetrate the **Daṇḍaka forest** at the start of the exile proper, they meet a terrible rākṣasa "vast as a mountain peak," roaring, his frame gaunt, carrying lions, tigers, wolves, and an elephant impaled on his iron spear. (Vālmīki, Araṇya 2.)
- **The seizure of Sītā** — Virādha rushes upon them, takes up **Sītā in his arms**, and steps back, demanding to know who these ascetics are who tresspass with a woman into his domain; he declares he will devour the brothers and keep Sītā as his own. This is the **first abduction-attempt of the epic** — the structural rehearsal for Rāvaṇa's later theft. (Vālmīki, Araṇya 2.)
  - *Note (foreshadowing):* Vālmīki frames the moment so that Sītā in a demon's grasp **prefigures the Laṅkā crisis**; commentators read Virādha as the "first warning" the forest gives. [scholarly]
- **Rāma's despair** — Seeing Sītā seized, Rāma is momentarily unmanned with grief, calling it a calamity worse than the loss of the kingdom — a beat the poem repeats, intensified, at the true abduction. (Vālmīki, Araṇya 2–3.)

## II. The fight and the boon of invulnerability (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 3)

- **Self-introduction** — Challenged, the monster names himself **Virādha, son of Java and Śatahradā**, and boasts of a **boon from Brahmā** by which **no weapon — no missile, no edged blade — can cut or pierce his body**; he is, in the literal sense, unkillable by arms. (Vālmīki, Araṇya 3.)
  - *Variant (parentage):* his father is given as **Java (Jaya)** and mother **Śatahradā** ("hundred-lightnings") in the standard recension; the names vary slightly across manuscripts. [variant]
- **The arrows that fail** — Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa loose volleys of arrows; the shafts strike home but **do not kill** him — they only enrage him. He drops Sītā to fight, then catches up **both brothers, one under each arm**, and carries them off into the wood to devour them — the same "one in each arm" capture later echoed by [Kabandha](./kabandha.md). (Vālmīki, Araṇya 3.)
- **Sītā's plea** — Sītā cries out, begging the demon to take her and spare Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, willing to sacrifice herself — a note of her characteristic self-offering. (Vālmīki, Araṇya 3.)

## III. The breaking and the burial alive (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 4)

- **Severing the arms** — Realising weapons cannot slay him, the brothers turn to **brute force**: they **hack off both his arms** at the shoulders (Rāma the right, Lakṣmaṇa the left, in the common reading), so that the maimed giant collapses to the ground, his grip broken. (Vālmīki, Araṇya 4.)
- **Crushing and the decision to bury** — Battered, Virādha still cannot be killed outright. Rāma resolves that since **steel will not end him**, he must be **interred** — committed to the earth, the one thing the boon does not guard against. He orders Lakṣmaṇa to **dig a great pit**. (Vālmīki, Araṇya 4.)
- **Buried alive** — While Lakṣmaṇa digs, Rāma sets his foot on the demon's neck; then the brothers **cast Virādha living into the pit and bury him**, heaping earth upon him — death by interment, the boon circumvented. (Vālmīki, Araṇya 4.)
  - *Note (why burial, not beheading):* the **logical key** of the episode is that Brahmā's boon shields against *śastra* (weapons), not against **suffocation in the earth**; the brothers win by **out-thinking the boon**, a motif the epic reuses against other boon-protected foes. [scholarly]

---

## IV. The release of the gandharva Tumburu (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 4)

- **The revelation** — As he dies, Virādha's monstrous nature falls away and he speaks: he was in truth the **gandharva Tumburu**, a celestial musician of **Kubera's (Vaiśravaṇa's) court**, cursed to wander as a rākṣasa. (Vālmīki, Araṇya 4.)
- **The curse of Kubera** — Tumburu had **offended Kubera** — most accounts: by failing to attend / serve him, distracted by his love for the **apsaras Rambhā** — and Kubera cursed him to the rākṣasa-body of Virādha. The curse carried its own terminus: he would be **freed only when Rāma, son of Daśaratha, slew him in battle**. (Vālmīki, Araṇya 4.)
  - *Variant (the offence):* the lapse is most often Tumburu's **truancy from Kubera's service** out of infatuation with **Rambhā**; some retellings leave the precise fault unstated and stress only the appointed release by Rāma. [variant]
- **The deliverance** — Released by his "death," the celestial **rises from the earth restored** — radiant, divine — pays homage to Rāma, and **ascends to the gandharva-world**. His burial is therefore not destruction but **liberation (mokṣa from the curse)**. (Vālmīki, Araṇya 4.)
- **Counsel given (in some recensions)** — Before departing, the freed Tumburu **directs Rāma onward** to the hermitage of the sage **Śarabhaṅga** nearby — beginning the chain of ṛṣi-encounters (Śarabhaṅga, Sutīkṣṇa, [Agastya](../agastya.md)) that organises the early Araṇya-kāṇḍa. (Vālmīki, Araṇya 4–5.)

## V. Why Virādha matters — the opening key of the forest

- **The first demon of the exile** — Virādha is the **threshold-guardian** of Daṇḍaka: defeating him is the brothers' formal initiation into the demon-haunted south, the first of the Araṇya-kāṇḍa's escalating rākṣasa encounters that run to **Khara, Dūṣaṇa, and Triśiras** and on to the abduction. [scholarly]
- **The rehearsal of the abduction** — By **seizing Sītā first**, Virādha gives the epic a **miniature dry-run of the central catastrophe**: the demon-grasp on Sītā, Rāma's collapse into grief, her self-offering plea — all replayed, magnified, when Rāvaṇa strikes. [scholarly]
- **The redeemed-monster pattern** — Virādha (Tumburu) and [Kabandha](./kabandha.md) (Viśvāvasu) **bracket the Daṇḍaka sojourn** as twin cursed-gandharvas freed by Rāma — one at the kāṇḍa's **opening**, one at its **close** — a deliberate frame read by scholars as the forest's "two gates," each a being for whom **Rāma's violence is grace**. Kin in pattern to [Tāḍakā](./tataka.md) and other cursed-form demons. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (doubling):* the close parallel — both seize the brothers "one in each arm," both are slain by **dismemberment + earth/fire** rather than the sword, both turn out to be cursed celestials who counsel Rāma onward — is sometimes read as a single motif **doubled** to open and shut the Daṇḍaka episode. [disputed]
- **Not Kabandha** — Virādha is frequently **confused with [Kabandha](./kabandha.md)**, the other arm-seizing cursed-gandharva of the same kāṇḍa. They are **distinct**: Virādha (Araṇya **2–4**, near the start, freed by **burial**) versus Kabandha (Araṇya **69–73**, near the end, freed by **cremation**, and the one who names [Sugrīva](./sugriva-and-vali.md)). [scholarly]
