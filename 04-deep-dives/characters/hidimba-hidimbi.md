# Hiḍimba and Hiḍimbī — Bhīma's Rākṣasī Wife and Her Brother

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on the rākṣasa siblings of the Pāṇḍava forest-exile — **Hiḍimba**, the man-eater slain by [Bhīma](./bhima.md) (the *Hiḍimba-vadha*), and his sister **Hiḍimbī (Hiḍimbā)**, the rākṣasī who weds Bhīma and bears [Ghaṭotkaca](./ghatotkacha.md), the maternal line of one of the war's pivotal heroes. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). The episode is canonical Vyāsa, set inside the [Ādi Parva](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md). [scholarly]
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* this falls early in the Pāṇḍava generation of the present 28th Mahāyuga of the 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara, on the Dvāpara/Kali cusp (war's close 3102 BCE); the BORI Critical Edition of the *Mahābhārata* is the scholarly textual baseline. [scholarly]

## Setting — the forest after the lac-house

- **The escape that precedes them** — after the **Vāraṇāvata lac-house (jatugṛha)** is fired by Purocana on Duryodhana's order, the Pāṇḍavas and [Kuntī](./kunti.md) flee through the tunnel and into the deep forest, exhausted; Bhīma carries his mother and brothers on his shoulders and back (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva, Hiḍimba-vadha Parva* ~154). See the escape inline in [Ādi Parva](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md).
- **The forest of the rākṣasas** — they halt in a dense, trackless wood; the others sleep from fatigue while **Bhīma keeps watch**, and it is in this wood that the cannibal Hiḍimba dwells in a *śāla* tree (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva* ~154).

## Hiḍimba — the cannibal brother

- **Name and nature** — **Hiḍimba** (Hiḍimbaka), a *puruṣādaka* (man-eating) rākṣasa of terrible aspect — yellow eyes, copper beard, sharp tusks, a mouth split to the ears — who craves human flesh and rules the wood with his sister (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva* ~154).
- **The scent of prey** — smelling the sleeping humans, Hiḍimba sends **Hiḍimbī to fetch them as food**, ordering her to kill them so the siblings may feast on their flesh and blood (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva* ~154).
- **The brother's wrath** — when his sister fails to return with prey (having fallen for Bhīma), Hiḍimba descends in fury, sees her transformed into beauty and inclined to the human, and rushes to kill her for her treachery; Bhīma intervenes (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva* ~154).
- **The Hiḍimba-vadha (slaying of Hiḍimba)** — Bhīma, refusing to wake the others, fights the rākṣasa hand to hand; they uproot trees and hurl boulders, and Bhīma at last **lifts Hiḍimba overhead, whirls him, and breaks his back / wrenches his body apart**, killing him — the first demon Bhīma slays in the epic and the *vadha* the sub-parva is named for (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva, Hiḍimba-vadha Parva* ~155).
- **First of Bhīma's demon-kills** — the slaying inaugurates Bhīma's career as the **devourer of rākṣasas** (Baka of Ekacakrā, Kirmīra, Jaṭāsura follow), marking his role as the family's monster-killer (*Mahābhārata, Ādi/Vana Parvas*). See [Bhīma](./bhima.md).

## Hiḍimbī (Hiḍimbā) — the rākṣasī wife

- **Name and forms** — **Hiḍimbī**, also **Hiḍimbā**, sister of Hiḍimba; a *kāmarūpiṇī* (shape-shifter) who can assume any form at will, conventionally taking a beautiful human woman's shape to approach Bhīma (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva* ~154).
- **Sent to kill, smitten instead** — dispatched by her brother to seize the sleepers, she beholds Bhīma — broad-shouldered, lion-like — and is **overcome with desire (kāma)**; she warns him of her brother and offers to carry him and his family to safety, declaring her love (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva* ~154).
- **Loyalty against her own kin** — she chooses Bhīma over Hiḍimba, betraying her brother's cannibal design; her turn from her own people is the hinge of the episode (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva* ~154).
- **The proposal and Kuntī's role** — after Hiḍimba's death she presses her suit; the brothers wake, and the marriage is debated. [Kuntī](./kunti.md) and **Yudhiṣṭhira** assent on conditions, recognizing dharma in not refusing a suppliant who has aided them (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva* ~155).
- **The marriage compact** — Bhīma weds Hiḍimbī on terms set by Yudhiṣṭhira: she may have him by **day, roaming the skies and beautiful places, but he must return each night/dawn to the Pāṇḍavas**; and the union is to last **only until a son is born** (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva* ~155). This is the **first non-human marriage of the exile** and the first of several Pāṇḍava unions outside human society.
- **Birth of Ghaṭotkaca** — by rākṣasa speed she **conceives and bears a son the same day**, [Ghaṭotkaca](./ghatotkacha.md), born bald with a pot-shaped head (*ghaṭa* + *utkaca*); the boy matures to full youth at once and departs with the boon to come at a thought (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva* ~155).
- **Marriage dissolves on the boon's terms** — the compact fulfilled by the son's birth, Hiḍimbī takes her child and **returns to the forest/rākṣasa realm**, raising Ghaṭotkaca apart; Bhīma rejoins the Pāṇḍavas, and the family moves on toward Ekacakrā (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva* ~155).
- **Faithful in widowhood** — the epic frames her as devoted; later tradition makes her the steadfast single mother who rears a king and sends him to die for his father's cause. [scholarly]

## Significance

- **A dharma test in the wilderness** — the episode poses the question of whether a Kṣatriya may wed a rākṣasī and honor a demon-woman's suit; Yudhiṣṭhira's conditional assent treats gratitude and the duty to a benefactor as binding (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva* ~155).
- **Seeding the war's pivot** — Hiḍimbī's son Ghaṭotkaca becomes the night-fighter whose self-offering draws out **Karṇa's once-only Vāsavī Śakti**, the strategic hinge that spares Arjuna; the forest marriage thus reaches forward to the 14th night of Kurukṣetra (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*). See [Ghaṭotkaca](./ghatotkacha.md).
- **Pattern of exogamous unions** — Hiḍimbī begins a series — Arjuna's Nāga wife Ulūpī, the Maṇipura princess Citrāṅgadā, Bhīma's later forays — in which Pāṇḍava marriages cross the boundaries of species and people during exile. [scholarly]

## Regional, oral, and folk afterlife

- **Hiḍimbā Devī temple, Manali (Himachal Pradesh)** — Hiḍimbā is worshipped as a **goddess (Devī)** in the Kullu valley, with the famed cedar-wood **Hiḍimbā Devī (Dhungri) temple** (rebuilt 1553 CE); the local Kullu Daśahrā tradition treats her as a tutelary deity and as grandmother of the ruling line through Ghaṭotkaca. [regional] [folk]
- **Ghaṭotkaca shrine nearby** — a small Ghaṭotkaca shrine stands near the Hiḍimbā temple at Manali, binding mother and son in the same local cult. [regional] [folk]
- **Maternal-line legitimacy** — Himachali royal/folk genealogy invokes descent through Ghaṭotkaca (hence Hiḍimbā) to dignify lineage; the rākṣasī is recast as a benevolent mountain goddess rather than a demon. [regional] [folk]
- **Devotional softening** — popular and televised retellings (e.g., modern serials) sentimentalize Hiḍimbā as a loving, self-sacrificing wife and Hiḍimba as a straightforward ogre-villain, smoothing the epic's terser, more ambivalent portrait. [folk]
- **Variant readings of the marriage terms** — *Variant (regional/oral):* some retellings stress that Bhīma's nights-with-the-Pāṇḍavas clause is a vow of continued duty to his brothers; others foreground Kuntī as the decisive voice sanctioning the match. [folk]
