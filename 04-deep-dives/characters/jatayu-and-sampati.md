# Jaṭāyu & Sampāti

> The **vulture brothers** (*gṛdhra-rāja*) of the [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md) — demi-divine raptors of the line of **Aruṇa**, nephews of **[Garuḍa](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/garuda.md)**. **Jaṭāyu** dies fighting **[Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md)** to save **Sītā** and receives Rāma's own funeral rites; **Sampāti**, wingless on the Vindhyas, supplies the intelligence that locates Sītā in Laṅkā. Together they form the epic's twin "bird-helpers," book-ending the abduction (death at the deed, discovery of the prison).
> Sits in **[Tretā-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md). Jaṭāyu's death and rites: [Araṇya-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md); Sampāti's revelation: [Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md).
> Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md) · Kin: [Garuḍa](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/garuda.md) · Antagonist: [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md).

**Jaṭāyu** (Sanskrit *Jaṭāyu / Jaṭāyus*) and **Sampāti** (*Sampāti*) are aged, semi-divine **vultures (gṛdhra)**, sons of **Aruṇa** the dawn-charioteer and grandsons of the sage **Kaśyapa** through **Vinatā** — making them **brothers' sons (nephews) of [Garuḍa](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/garuda.md)** and kin to the eagle-line. They are friends of **Daśaratha** (Jaṭāyu) and immensely long-lived, having flown in the sky "since the gods churned the ocean." In the Rāmāyaṇa they bracket the central catastrophe: Jaṭāyu **dies in single combat with Rāvaṇa** trying to stop Sītā's abduction (Araṇya-kāṇḍa), and Sampāti, his wings burnt away long before, **points the vānara search-party to Laṅkā** with his far-sight (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa). Vālmīki gives the brothers heroic dignity: the bird who fights and the bird who guides.

---

## I. Ancestry and the line of Aruṇa (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 14)

- **Aruṇa** - The reddish charioteer of **Sūrya** and personification of dawn; born of **Kaśyapa** and **Vinatā** (the half-formed elder egg-son), hence **elder brother of Garuḍa**. Father of the vulture-brothers (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 14; cf. [Garuḍa](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/garuda.md)).
- **Śyenī (Śyenī)** - Mother of Jaṭāyu and Sampāti in Vālmīki's genealogy (Araṇya-kāṇḍa 14), wife of Aruṇa.
- **Jaṭāyu** - Younger of the two brothers; old friend of **Daśaratha**, king of Ayodhyā, which underlies his protective stake in Sītā (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 14).
- **Sampāti** - Elder brother, named in the long bird-genealogy Jaṭāyu recites to Rāma; later the wingless seer of the Vindhyas (Araṇya-kāṇḍa 14; Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 56–63).
- **Garuḍa** - Their paternal uncle (father's brother), the divine eagle-mount of Viṣṇu; the kinship marks the brothers as **noble birds of the solar-Vaiṣṇava sphere**, not mere carrion-fowl. See [Garuḍa](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/garuda.md).
  - *Variant (extended genealogy, Araṇya-kāṇḍa 14):* Jaṭāyu narrates a vast avian/Prajāpati lineage from **Kaśyapa → Vinatā → Aruṇa → Sampāti & Jaṭāyu**, situating the vultures within the cosmic descent of birds and beasts. [scriptural]
  - *Variant (parentage):* later/popular tellings sometimes name the mother differently or omit Śyenī, simply giving them as "sons of Aruṇa." [folk]

## II. The sun-flight: how Sampāti lost his wings (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 58–59)

- **The youthful contest** - In their pride, the young brothers fly toward **Sūrya** to test their wings; ascending at **midday**, the heat begins to **scorch Jaṭāyu's wings** (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 58).
- **Sampāti's sacrifice** - The elder brother **flies above Jaṭāyu and spreads his own wings as a shield**, taking the sun's fire upon himself; **Sampāti's wings are burnt away** and he plummets to the **Vindhya** range, while Jaṭāyu is saved (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 58). The episode is the moral root of Sampāti's later self-image as the brother who once protected.
- **Outcome** - Jaṭāyu survives intact (and will later fly to fight Rāvaṇa); Sampāti is left **flightless on the Vindhyas for ages** until the vānaras arrive — the two halves of the brothers' destiny set here.
  - *Variant (who flew higher):* some retellings make **Jaṭāyu** the one who recklessly outflew his brother toward the sun, with **Sampāti overtaking him** to shield him — the version most popular devotionally; Vālmīki centers Sampāti's protective act. [folk]
  - *Variant (rationalist gloss):* commentators read the "burnt wings" as old age / a fall rather than literal solar fire. [scholarly]

## III. Jaṭāyu and the abduction of Sītā (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 49–51)

- **The vigil over Sītā** - Old Jaṭāyu, friend of Daśaratha, watches over Rāma's household in **Pañcavaṭī** during the forest exile; he is the sentinel of the woods (Araṇya-kāṇḍa 49).
- **Rāvaṇa's theft** - While Rāma chases the golden deer (**Mārīca**) and Lakṣmaṇa is drawn off, **Rāvaṇa seizes Sītā** and carries her south in his **Puṣpaka** chariot. Sītā's cries rouse Jaṭāyu (Araṇya-kāṇḍa 49–50). See [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md).
- **Jaṭāyu's challenge** - The aged vulture rebukes Rāvaṇa for the cowardice of stealing another's wife, names himself friend of Daśaratha, and **demands Rāvaṇa release Sītā or fight** (Araṇya-kāṇḍa 50).
- **The aerial duel** - "Two vast winged mountains": Jaṭāyu **tears at Rāvaṇa's bow, armor, and chariot**, kills his **donkeys/charioteer and breaks the chariot**, forcing Rāvaṇa briefly to the ground (Araṇya-kāṇḍa 50–51). It is the only effective resistance Sītā gets en route.
- **Jaṭāyu's mutilation** - Enraged, **Rāvaṇa draws his sword and severs Jaṭāyu's wings, feet, and sides**; the great bird falls, dying, and Rāvaṇa flies on with Sītā (Araṇya-kāṇḍa 51).

## IV. Jaṭāyu's death and Rāma's last rites (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 67–68)

- **Rāma finds the dying bird** - Searching for Sītā, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa first fear a forest-demon devoured her, then find the **bloodied, broken Jaṭāyu**, who gasps out the truth (Araṇya-kāṇḍa 67–68).
- **The dying testimony** - Jaṭāyu reports the **fight with Rāvaṇa**, that Sītā was carried **south**, and the hour of the abduction (a *vinda* twilight detail in some readings) before expiring in Rāma's arms (Araṇya-kāṇḍa 68). His intelligence sets the entire southern campaign in motion.
- **Rāma's grief and the funeral** - Rāma laments him as he would a kinsman; he and Lakṣmaṇa **cremate Jaṭāyu**, perform the **pinda-dāna / kapāla-kriyā (śrāddha) rites normally reserved for a son toward a father**, and Rāma pronounces that Jaṭāyu has won the heavenly worlds (gati) earned by the virtuous (Araṇya-kāṇḍa 68).
- **Theological weight** - By performing **next-of-kin obsequies for a bird**, Rāma elevates Jaṭāyu to **filial status** and models that *dharma* and sacrifice, not birth or species, earn the funeral honors and the higher realms — a touchstone Rāmāyaṇa teaching. Cf. [Pitṛs & ancestral rites](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/pitrs-and-ancestors.md).
  - *Variant (moksha):* devotional tradition holds Jaṭāyu **attained mokṣa / Vaikuṇṭha** directly through Rāma's touch and rites — the bird who died for the Lord is liberated. [folk/devotional]
  - *Variant (place):* a temple/site at **Jaṭāyu Earth's Center / Jaṭāyu Para (Chadayamangalam, Kerala)** and other South-Indian *Jaṭāyu* sites are venerated as where he fell. [folk]
  - *Variant (Daśaratha's friend, not brother):* some popular retellings make Jaṭāyu Daśaratha's **brother-in-arms / sworn brother**, sharpening the "father-figure" funeral logic. [folk]

## V. Sampāti and the locating of Sītā (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 56–63)

- **The starving search-party** - The southern vānara host — **Aṅgada, Hanumān, Jāmbavān, Nala, Nīla** and others, sent by Sugrīva — reaches the **southern sea-shore** exhausted and despairing, having found no trace of Sītā (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 56). See [Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md).
- **Sampāti's first appearance** - The huge wingless vulture, smelling food, eyes the gathered monkeys as prey; **Aṅgada loudly mourns the noble birds Jaṭāyu and Sampāti** (recounting Jaṭāyu's death for Sītā), and Sampāti, hearing his brother's name and fate, descends in grief rather than hunger (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 56–57).
- **He learns of Jaṭāyu's death** - Sampāti asks the monkeys to carry him down and tells his own tale (the sun-flight, the burnt wings, his ages on the Vindhyas); he grieves that he could not aid his younger brother (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 57–59).
- **The sage Niśākara's prophecy** - Sampāti recounts that the sage **Niśākara** (Candramā/Niśākara muni), whom he served on the mountain, **foretold** that Sampāti would one day **meet vānaras searching for Sītā, that telling them her whereabouts would be his great deed, and that his wings would then regrow** (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 60–62).
- **His son's report** - Sampāti's son **Supārśva (Suparśva)** had once seen **Rāvaṇa carrying a weeping woman south toward Laṅkā**; this is how the wingless seer knows the route (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 62).
- **The far-sight revelation** - With a vulture's superhuman vision, Sampāti declares Sītā is held **across the sea, 100 yojanas south, in Rāvaṇa's Laṅkā**, in the **Aśoka grove**, guarded by rākṣasīs — pinpointing the target and prompting Hanumān's ocean-leap (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 58, 62–63). This single disclosure converts a hopeless search into the [Sundara-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md) mission.
- **Wings restored** - As he finishes serving Rāma's cause, **new feathers sprout and his wings regrow** before the astonished monkeys — Niśākara's prophecy fulfilled; Sampāti flies off rejoicing, his youth and prowess returned (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 63).
- **The brothers' symmetry** - In several tellings Sampāti, restored, **performs Jaṭāyu's śrāddha / funeral rites** himself — closing the fraternal arc that Rāma had begun (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa tradition; widely retold).
  - *Variant (son's name):* the son who saw Rāvaṇa is named **Supārśva** in Vālmīki; the **Brahma Purāṇa** gives Sampāti other sons, **Babhru** and **Śīghraga** (Sighraga). [variant/late text]
  - *Variant (distance):* the gap to Laṅkā is stated as **100 yojanas** (Vālmīki); modern glosses variously convert this. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (birthplace):* **Gṛddhrāj Parvat** (Griddhraj/Gidhha, Satna district, Madhya Pradesh) is venerated locally as the brothers' / Sampāti's birthplace and the Vindhya refuge. [folk]

## VI. Meaning, motifs, and reception

- **The two bird-helpers** - Jaṭāyu (action, sacrifice, death) and Sampāti (knowledge, far-sight, survival) form a deliberate pair: one **stops** the abductor and dies, the other **finds** the captive and lives. Together they are the non-human allies who, like the vānaras, embody *dharma* outside the human caste-order.
- **Sacrifice rewarded** - Jaṭāyu's death earns the **rites of a son and the heaven of the righteous**; Sampāti's youthful sacrifice of his wings for his brother is **mirrored ages later** by the regrowth of those very wings when he serves Rāma — sacrifice repaid by grace.
- **Dharma above birth** - That a **vulture** receives full funeral honors from an avatāra, and that birds carry the plot's decisive intelligence, is a recurring Rāmāyaṇa argument that virtue, not species or status, defines the noble. Cf. [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md) (the high-born villain) as foil.
- **Cultic afterlife** - Jaṭāyu is venerated at fall-sites in Kerala and Tamil regions; the vulture-brothers appear across temple sculpture and the "Burnt Rāmāyaṇa" and other miniature traditions depicting the Jaṭāyu–Rāvaṇa combat.
- *Open roster:* the **full Araṇya-kāṇḍa 14 bird-genealogy** (the many daughters of Dakṣa/Kaśyapa and the descent of birds) is long and version-dependent; treat exhaustive name-lists as [disputed] across recensions. [scholarly]
