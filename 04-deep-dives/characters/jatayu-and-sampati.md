# Jaṭāyu & Sampāti

> The **vulture brothers** (*gṛdhra-rāja*) of the [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md) — demi-divine raptors of the line of **Aruṇa**, nephews of **[Garuḍa](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/garuda.md)**. **Jaṭāyu** is cut down fighting **[Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md)** to save **Sītā** and receives Rāma's own funeral rites; **Sampāti**, wingless on the Vindhyas, supplies the intelligence that locates Sītā in Laṅkā. Together they form the epic's twin "bird-helpers," book-ending the abduction (death at the deed, discovery of the prison).
> Sits in **[Tretā-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md). Jaṭāyu's death and rites: [Araṇya-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md); Sampāti's revelation: [Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md). Other tellings: [Rāmopākhyāna](../ramopakhyana.md) · [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md) · [Rāmcaritmānas](../ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md).
> Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md) · Kin: [Garuḍa](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/garuda.md) · Antagonist: [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md).

**Jaṭāyu** (Sanskrit *Jaṭāyu / Jaṭāyus*) and **Sampāti** (*Sampāti*) are aged, semi-divine **vultures (gṛdhra)**, sons of **Aruṇa** the dawn-charioteer; Jaṭāyu calls himself the son of **Śyenī**. Aruṇa is Vinatā's son and Garuḍa's brother, which makes the vultures **nephews of [Garuḍa](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/garuda.md)** and kin to the eagle-line. Jaṭāyu is the friend of **Daśaratha** and tells Rāvaṇa he is sixty thousand years old (Araṇya 50.20); Sampāti claims to have watched the ocean churned for the nectar (Kiṣkindhā 58.13). In the Rāmāyaṇa they bracket the central catastrophe: Jaṭāyu is **mortally wounded in single combat with Rāvaṇa** trying to stop Sītā's abduction and lives just long enough to tell Rāma (Araṇya-kāṇḍa), and Sampāti, his wings burnt away long before, **points the vānara search-party to Laṅkā** with his far-sight (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa). Vālmīki gives the brothers heroic dignity: the bird who fights and the bird who guides.

**Numbering.** Verse references below are to the Southern vulgate as printed and translated at valmikiramayan.net (VR). The Baroda Critical Edition (CE) numbers differ: Araṇya VR 14 = CE 3.13, VR 50–51 = CE 3.48–49, VR 67–68 = CE 3.63–64; Kiṣkindhā VR 56–63 = CE 4.55–62. Each key verse is given with its CE number where it was checked against the GRETIL CE text.

## I. Ancestry and the line of Aruṇa (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 14)

- **The first meeting** - On the road to **Pañcavaṭī** Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa see a huge bird and **take it for a rākṣasa**, asking "who are you?". The bird answers that he is their **father's friend** (*vayasyaṃ pitur ātmanaḥ*). Asked his lineage, he recites the descent of all creatures. Then he offers to live near them and **guard Sītā when the brothers are away** (*sītāṃ ca tāta rakṣiṣye tvayi yāte salakṣmaṇe*). Rāma embraces him and goes on to Pañcavaṭī with him (Araṇya 14.1–5, 34–36; CE 3.13.1–5, 34–36).
  - *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa):* Rāma calls for his bow to kill this "devourer of sages"; the frightened bird says he is Daśaratha's dear friend Jaṭāyu, and promises to live at Pañcavaṭī and guard Sītā (Adhyātma, Araṇya 4.1–6). There is no genealogy. [late text]
- **Aruṇa** - The reddish charioteer of **Sūrya** and personification of dawn; son of **Vinatā** and **brother of Garuḍa** (*dvau putrau vinatāyās tu garuḍo 'ruṇa eva ca*). Jaṭāyu names him as the father of both brothers: *tasmāj jāto 'ham aruṇāt saṃpātiś ca mamāgrajaḥ* (Rām. Araṇya 14.32–33; CE 3.13.32–33; cf. [Garuḍa](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/garuda.md)).
- **Śyenī** - Jaṭāyu calls himself **śyenī-putram**, "son of Śyenī" (Araṇya 14.33). In Vālmīki's own genealogy Śyenī is one of the **five bird-daughters of Tāmrā** — with Krauñcī, Bhāsī, Dhṛtarāṣṭrī and Śukī — and she is the mother of hawks and vultures as a kind (14.17–19). Vālmīki names her as Jaṭāyu's mother but **never states her marriage to Aruṇa**.
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata, Ādi-parvan):* the marriage is explicit in the Mahābhārata's genealogy of creatures: *aruṇasya bhāryā śyenī* bore the two mighty sons **Sampāti and Jaṭāyu** (MBh 1.60.67, BORI CE). The same chapter has its own different tree for the birds (*analāyāḥ śukī putrī*, 1.60.66). [late text]
- **Vālmīki's line of descent is not the Purāṇic one** - The standard Purāṇic scheme makes **Vinatā** a daughter of Dakṣa and a wife of Kaśyapa. Araṇya 14 routes her quite differently: of Kaśyapa's eight Dakṣa-born wives, **Tāmrā** bears the five bird-daughters; **Śukī** bears **Natā**, and **Natā's daughter is Vinatā** (*śukī natāṃ vijajñe tu natāyā vinatā sutā*) — so Vinatā stands three generations below Tāmrā, and is Śukī's granddaughter (*vinatā ca śukī pautrī*), not Kaśyapa's wife (Rām. Araṇya 14.11, 17–20, 31–32; the CE prints the same genealogy at 3.13). The corpus's Purāṇic tree and the epic's own are **different trees**, and Jaṭāyu recites the epic's. See also [Kaśyapa](../kashyapa.md). [scholarly]
- **Jaṭāyu** - Younger of the two brothers; old friend of **Daśaratha**, king of Ayodhyā, which underlies his protective stake in Sītā (Rām. Araṇya 14.3; repeated at 57.9–10 of the Kiṣkindhā).
- **Sampāti** - Elder brother (*mamāgrajaḥ*), named at the close of the bird-genealogy Jaṭāyu recites to Rāma; later the wingless seer of the Vindhyas (Araṇya 14.33; Kiṣkindhā 56–63).
- **Garuḍa** - Their paternal uncle (father's brother), the divine eagle-mount of Viṣṇu; the kinship marks the brothers as **noble birds of the solar-Vaiṣṇava sphere**, not mere carrion-fowl. Sampāti himself credits the vultures' far sight to their birth from Vinatā's son (*vainateyāc ca no janma*, Kiṣkindhā 58.29; the valmikiramayan.net translators take this as Garuḍa), and in the vulgate calls their sight Garuḍa-like (*sauparṇa*, 58.31; the CE reads *sauvarṇa*, 4.57.28). See [Garuḍa](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/garuda.md).
  - *Variant (the Purāṇic tree):* the descent usually given for the brothers — **Kaśyapa → Vinatā → Aruṇa → Sampāti & Jaṭāyu**, with Vinatā a Dakṣa-daughter wed to Kaśyapa — is the **Purāṇic** scheme, and it is the one nearly every retelling and roster carries. The *Matsya Purāṇa* gives Tāmrā's bird-daughters (Śyenī among them), then "Vinatā's line": Garuḍa and Aruṇa, and **Sampāti and Jaṭāyu, both sons of Aruṇa** (Matsya 6.30–35). Vālmīki's own Araṇya 14 places Vinatā three generations lower (above); the two trees agree only from Aruṇa down. [late text]
  - *Variant (mother named Gṛdhrī):* the *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa* lists six daughters of Tāmrā (Gṛdhrī, Bhāsī, Śukī, Krauñcī, Śyenī, Dhṛtarāṣṭrikā) and says that **Gṛdhrī**, Aruṇa's wife, bore Sampāti and Jaṭāyu (Brahmāṇḍa 2.7.445–447, GRETIL numbering). [late text]

## II. The sun-flight: how Sampāti lost his wings (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 60–62)

Vālmīki tells the sun-flight **twice**. The short version comes first, at sarga 58, when Sampāti answers the vānaras just before he names Laṅkā (58.4–7; CE 4.57.4–7). The long version comes after, framed as Sampāti's own account to the sage **Niśākara**: the fall on the Vindhyas and the descent to the sage (60), the flight and the burnt wings (61), and the sage's promise (62). The two give the flight a different occasion.

- **The flight at Vṛtra's slaying (sarga 58)** - "Long ago, when Vṛtra was being slain," the two brothers, **each wanting to win** (*jayaiṣiṇau*), flew toward the blazing sun. At **midday** Jaṭāyu began to falter in the heat (Kiṣkindhā 58.4–5; CE 4.57.4–5).
- **The wager on Kailāsa (sarga 61)** - Told to Niśākara, the flight is a **bet laid before the sages on the peak of Kailāsa**: out of pride, the brothers would follow the sun until it reached the western mountain of sunset (*kailāsaśikhare baddhvā munīnām agrataḥ paṇam*). They rose until towns looked like chariot-wheels and rivers like threads, and then sweat, fear and a swoon overtook them both (Kiṣkindhā 61.3–13; CE 4.60.4).
- **Sampāti's sacrifice** - Both tellings agree on the core. The elder brother **covers Jaṭāyu with his own wings** out of affection (*pakṣābhyāṃ chādayām āsa snehāt*); Jaṭāyu escapes unburnt, **Sampāti's wings are burnt away**, and he falls on the **Vindhya** (Kiṣkindhā 58.6–7, CE 4.57.6–7; 61.14–15, CE 4.60.14). In sarga 61, Jaṭāyu starts to fall first and Sampāti drops after him to shield him. The episode is the moral root of Sampāti's later self-image as the brother who once protected; in 61.2 he also speaks of **shame at not having protected** Jaṭāyu.
  - *Manuscript note:* the valmikiramayan.net translators, introducing sarga 59, say that **sargas 59–63 are missing from various manuscripts** and contain contradictory statements; they are kept because the old commentators glossed them at length. The **Baroda Critical Edition nonetheless prints them** (as CE 4.58–62: Supārśva 4.58.8, Niśākara 4.59.8, the wager 4.60.4, the regrown wings 4.62.9). The doubt touches the retelling, Supārśva, Niśākara and the wing-regrowth — **not** the core sun-flight, which sarga 58 already tells. [interpolation] [disputed]
- **The fall** - Sampāti lay senseless for **six days**, then recognised the Vindhya above the southern ocean from its birds, caves and peaks (60.4–7). He assumed Jaṭāyu had come down in Janasthāna (61.16) and, having lost wings, kingdom, brother and strength, wanted to throw himself from the mountain (61.17).
- **Outcome** - Jaṭāyu survives intact (and will later fly to fight Rāvaṇa); Sampāti is left **flightless on the Vindhyas for ages** until the vānaras arrive — the two halves of the brothers' destiny set here.
  - *Variant (Jaṭāyu turns back; no shielding — Rāmcaritmānas):* in Tulsīdās, Jaṭāyu **could not bear the sun's heat and turned back**, while Sampāti, "proud" (*abhimānī*), flew on alone toward the sun and was burnt: *teja na sahi saka so phiri āvā, mai abhimānī rabi niarāvā* (Mānas, Kiṣkindhā, caupāī before dohā 28). Here the burning follows from pride, and there is no act of protection. [late text]
  - *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa):* keeps Vālmīki's shielding — the brothers fly toward the sun's disc in the pride of youth, and Sampāti covers the scorched Jaṭāyu with his wings and falls on the Vindhya peak. It has him unconscious for **three days**, not six (Adhyātma, Kiṣkindhā 8.2–6). [late text]
  - *Variant (Rāmopākhyāna):* the brothers rose "in rivalry with each other" to the sun's court; Sampāti's wings were burnt, Jaṭāyu's were not. No shielding is stated (MBh 3.266.48–49). [late text]

## III. Jaṭāyu and the abduction of Sītā (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 49–51)

- **The vigil over Sītā** - Old Jaṭāyu had promised at their first meeting to guard Sītā when the brothers were away (Araṇya 14.34). When Rāvaṇa seizes her, he is **asleep in a tree**: Sītā, carried through the air, sees him perched there and calls to him. She does **not** ask him to fight — he could not hold back this armed demon, she says — but begs him to **tell Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa** everything (Araṇya 49.36–40). Her cry wakes him (50.1).
- **Rāvaṇa's theft** - While Rāma chases the golden deer (**Mārīca**) and Lakṣmaṇa is drawn off, **Rāvaṇa seizes Sītā** and carries her south in a golden chariot drawn by **asses** (*khara*, "ghost-faced", 51.15; the valmikiramayan.net translation renders them "mules"). Vālmīki does not call this car the Puṣpaka. See [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md).
- **Jaṭāyu's challenge** - The aged vulture, still on his tree, names himself **Jaṭāyu, king of vultures**, rebukes Rāvaṇa for the cowardice of stealing another's wife, and tells him to give Sītā back (Araṇya 50.2–16). He is old and unarmed, he says, and Rāvaṇa is young, armoured, armed and charioted: "**sixty thousand years** have passed since I was born" — yet Rāvaṇa will not carry her off unharmed while he lives (50.20–21, 26–28; CE 3.48.19–20). He will act as Rāma and Daśaratha would wish, even at the cost of his life (50.27).
- **The aerial duel** - "Like two winged mountains" (51.3): Jaṭāyu takes Rāvaṇa's arrows, gashes him with his talons, **breaks his bow** twice, **tears off his armour**, kills the **asses**, **smashes the chariot**, fells the parasol and fan-bearers, and **bites off the charioteer's head** (Araṇya 51.4–18; CE 3.49). Chariotless, Rāvaṇa drops to the ground holding Sītā, and the forest-beings and siddhas praise the bird (51.19–20). Seeing him tired by age, Rāvaṇa rises again into the sky with her (51.21). It is the only effective resistance Sītā gets en route, and it costs Rāvaṇa his vehicle: from here he completes the flight to Laṅkā **carrying her bodily** (51.19–21; Araṇya 52).
- **The ten left arms** - Jaṭāyu drops on Rāvaṇa's back, rakes him with his claws, plucks his hair, and with his beak **tears off the ten left arms** in which Rāvaṇa is holding Sītā (*vāmabāhūn daśa*); they spring back at once like snakes from an anthill (Araṇya 51.33–39; CE 3.49.33). Rāvaṇa then sets Sītā down and fights the bird with fists and feet (51.40).
- **Jaṭāyu's mutilation** - Drawing his sword, Rāvaṇa **cuts off Jaṭāyu's wings, feet and flanks** (*pakṣau pādau ca pārśvau ca khaḍgam uddhṛtya so 'cchinat*); the great bird falls, dying (Araṇya 51.42–43; CE 3.49.36). Sītā runs to him "as to her own kinsman" and embraces him, weeping (51.44–46; 52.1–4). Then Rāvaṇa seizes her by the hair and goes on (52.6–8).
  - *Variant (Rāmopākhyāna):* Jaṭāyu, son of Aruṇa and Sampāti's brother, is Daśaratha's friend, and Sītā is his "daughter-in-law" (*snuṣā*). He tears Rāvaṇa with claws, wings and beak until the demon streams blood; Rāvaṇa cuts off his wings (*bhujau*, lit. "arms") with a sword and flies on with Sītā (MBh 3.263.1–6). The frame summary says Rāvaṇa carried her off through the air "resorting to māyā", having killed the vulture (*māyām āsthāya tarasā hatvā gṛdhraṃ jaṭāyuṣam*, 3.258.2). [late text]
  - *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa):* Jaṭāyu smashes the chariot with his beak, kills the draught-animals (*vāhān*) with his feet and breaks the bow. Rāvaṇa puts Sītā down, cuts off the bird's wings, and flies on in **another chariot** (*punar anyarathena*) (Adhyātma, Araṇya 7.53–58). [late text]
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas):* Jaṭāyu calls Sītā "daughter" (*putri*), grabs Rāvaṇa by the hair, tips him out of his chariot to the ground, and wounds him with his beak until Rāvaṇa swoons for a moment; Rāvaṇa then cuts off his wings with his sword (Mānas, Araṇya, caupāīs before dohā 29). [late text]

## IV. Jaṭāyu's death and Rāma's last rites (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 67–68)

- **Rāma finds the dying bird** - Searching for Sītā, Rāma finds the bloodied Jaṭāyu and **takes him for a rākṣasa in vulture form who has eaten Sītā** (*gṛdhrarūpam idaṃ vyaktaṃ rakṣo bhramati kānanam*). He rushes at him with an arrow on the string (Araṇya 67.9–13; CE 3.63.11). Vomiting blood, Jaṭāyu stops him: Rāvaṇa has taken Sītā and his own life; here are the broken bow, armour and chariot, and the charioteer he killed; "do not kill one already killed" (67.14–20). Rāma throws down his bow, embraces him and collapses in grief, calling him his father's friend (67.21–29).
- **The dying testimony** - Jaṭāyu says Rāvaṇa raised a storm and cloud by sorcery, cut off the tired bird's wings, and went **south** with Sītā (Araṇya 68.9–10). Then comes the one piece of hope: Rāvaṇa took her in the hour called **Vinda**, in which lost wealth is **soon regained by its owner**, and he will perish like a fish that has swallowed the hook (*vindo nāma muhūrto 'sau*, 68.12–13; CE 3.64.12–13). His last words: Rāvaṇa is "the son of Viśravas, the brother of Vaiśravaṇa (Kubera)…" — and he dies with Rāma begging him to say more (68.15–18). His intelligence sets the entire southern campaign in motion.
- **Rāma's grief and the funeral** - Rāma says his grief for this vulture is greater than his grief over Sītā's abduction (*sītāharaṇajaṃ duḥkhaṃ na me … yathā vināśo gṛdhrasya*). He holds Jaṭāyu "as worthy of worship and honour as Daśaratha" (Araṇya 68.25–26; CE 3.64.25). He kindles the fire himself and **cremates Jaṭāyu as he would his own kinsman** (*svabandhum iva*). He hunts large *rohi* deer and lays the flesh on grass as the offering for the bird, and recites the **ancestral (*pitrya*) mantras** that brāhmaṇas use to send the dead to heaven (*pitrya* is the vulgate's reading; the CE has *tasya*, 3.64.34). Then he and Lakṣmaṇa bathe in the **Godāvarī** and offer water (68.27–36; CE 3.64.31–34). Rāma sends him to the worlds of sacrificers, of keepers of the sacred fires, of warriors who never turn back, and of givers of land (*yā gatir yajñaśīlānām āhitāgneś ca yā gatiḥ*, 68.29–30; CE 3.64.29), and the bird goes to his own auspicious world, "consecrated by one like a great sage" (68.37).
- **Theological weight** - Rāma says it outright: "everywhere one sees good beings who walk in dharma, brave and a refuge to others, even among those born as animals" (*sādhavo dharmacāriṇaḥ … tiryagyonigateṣv api*, Araṇya 68.24). By performing **a kinsman's obsequies for a bird**, Rāma treats him as he would his father, and models that *dharma* and sacrifice, not birth or species, earn the funeral honours and the higher realms — a touchstone Rāmāyaṇa teaching. Cf. [Pitṛs & ancestral rites](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/pitrs-and-ancestors.md).
  - *Variant (mokṣa — Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa):* the dying bird recognises Rāma as **Viṣṇu, the Supreme Self in human guise**, and says that seeing him at death has freed him; he asks to be touched by Rāma's hands and dies. After the cremation and the meat-offering, Rāma grants him **sārūpya** (a form like his own). Jaṭāyu rises in a divine form **bearing conch, discus, mace and lotus, in yellow silk, on a shining vimāna**, honoured by four of Viṣṇu's attendants, sings a hymn to Rāma, and goes to Viṣṇu's supreme abode (Adhyātma, Araṇya 8.24–56; the hymn 8.44–53). The Adhyātma's Kiṣkindhā recalls it as **sāyujya**, a liberation hard even for yogis (7.34, 41). [late text]
  - *Variant (mokṣa — Rāmcaritmānas):* Rāma lays his hand on the bird's head and the pain leaves him. Rāma asks him to keep his body; Jaṭāyu declines, now that he has seen the one whose name at death frees even the low. Rāma sends him to his own abode and asks him **not to tell Daśaratha of the abduction: if I am Rāma, Rāvaṇa will come and tell him himself, with all his clan**. Jaṭāyu takes Hari's dark four-armed form, praises Rāma, asks for unbroken devotion and goes to Hari's abode, and Rāma performs his rites with his own hands (Mānas, Araṇya, dohās 30–32). [late text]
  - *Variant (Rāmopākhyāna):* the dying Jaṭāyu can only show with **movements of his head** that Rāvaṇa went south, and dies; Rāma gives him funeral rites, honouring his father's friend (MBh 3.263.15–21). [late text]
  - *Variant (place):* a temple/site at **Jaṭāyu Earth's Center / Jaṭāyu Para (Chadayamangalam, Kerala)** and other South-Indian *Jaṭāyu* sites are venerated as where he fell. [folk]
  - *Variant (place — Lepakshi):* local tradition at **Lepakshi** (Anantapur district, Andhra Pradesh) says Jaṭāyu fell there and Rāma told him "*le, pakṣi*" ("rise, bird" in Telugu), which gave the village its name. The etymology is uncertain: the site's inscriptions, the earliest dated 1493 CE, call the town Lepaksha, Lepakshi or Lepakshipura, and "*lepa* + *akṣi*" ("embalmed eye") is also proposed. [folk] [disputed]

## V. Sampāti and the locating of Sītā (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 56–63)

- **The starving search-party** - The southern vānara host — **Aṅgada, Hanumān, Jāmbavān, Nīla** and others, sent by Sugrīva (Kiṣkindhā 41.1–5; CE 4.40.1–5) — has overrun its month in the Ṛkṣabila cave and sits down on a mountain plateau by the southern sea to **fast to death** (Kiṣkindhā 53–55). See [Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md), [Aṅgada](./angada.md) and [Svayamprabhā](./svayamprabha-and-hema.md).
- **Sampāti's first appearance** - The huge wingless vulture comes out of his cave in the **Vindhya** and is delighted: food has come by fate, and he will eat the monkeys **one a day** (Kiṣkindhā 56.1–5). In fear, **Aṅgada laments aloud**: the vulture-king **Jaṭāyu** died happy doing Rāma's work, while they have done nothing, and all their troubles go back to Kaikeyī's boons (56.6–16). Hearing his brother's name and death, Sampāti cries out to know who is speaking of Jaṭāyu, "dearer to me than my life", and asks to be carried down, since his sun-burnt wings cannot spread (56.17–24).
- **He learns of Jaṭāyu's death** - Wary but resigned (if he eats them, they die anyway), the monkeys bring him down, and Aṅgada tells the whole story: Rāma's exile, the abduction, Jaṭāyu's fight and death, and the rites Rāma gave him (Kiṣkindhā 57.1–18). Sampāti answers that Jaṭāyu was his younger brother, that old age and lost wings keep him from avenging him, and tells the short version of the sun-flight (58.1–7).
- **The far-sight revelation** - Sampāti claims great age: he has seen the worlds below, Viṣṇu's three strides, the battles of gods and asuras and the **churning of the ocean** (Kiṣkindhā 58.13; CE 4.57.13). He himself **saw a beautiful woman carried off** by Rāvaṇa, crying "Rāma! Lakṣmaṇa!" and throwing down her ornaments (58.15–18). He names the abductor (son of Viśravas, brother of Kubera) and describes **Laṅkā**, built by Viśvakarman on an island **a full hundred yojanas** off (58.19–22; CE 4.57.20). Sītā, he says, is **held in Rāvaṇa's inner palace (*rāvaṇāntaḥpure ruddhā*), guarded by rākṣasīs** (58.23; CE 4.57.21). He explains the birds' flight-levels, the highest being that of Vinatā's sons; vultures, born of Vinatā's son (*vainateya*), **see a hundred yojanas** — "from here I see Rāvaṇa and Jānakī" (58.26–32). This single disclosure converts a hopeless search into the [Sundara-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md) mission.
- **His son's report** - In sarga 59 the source changes. Sampāti says his son **Supārśva**, who fed him, once came back at dusk without meat. Hovering over the passage at **Mount Mahendra**, the son had seen a being "like a heap of collyrium" carrying off a woman bright as dawn; the stranger **begged to be let through**, and Supārśva did not attack a suppliant. Sages in the sky then told him the pair were Rāvaṇa and Rāma's wife (Kiṣkindhā 59.7–21; CE 4.58.8). Later Sampāti says he **rebuked his son** for not saving Sītā (63.6–8). Sarga 58 and sarga 59 thus give **two different sources** for the same intelligence, one of the inconsistencies behind the doubt about sargas 59–63 (§II).
- **The sage Niśākara's prophecy** - In this telling, Sampāti dragged himself down from the Vindhya to the hermitage of the sage **Niśākara** (Kiṣkindhā 60.8; CE 4.59.8). He and Jaṭāyu had often visited the sage, taking human form to touch his feet (60.11, 19–20). The sage **foretold** Rāma's exile and Sītā's abduction, that **Indra would send her a nectar-like food** in captivity (62.8–10; CE 4.61.7–8), and that **Rāma's monkey messengers would come, and Sampāti must tell them where she was**. He said Sampāti's **wings would grow again**: he could give them back at once, but Sampāti must wait and do this deed for the world (62.2, 11–14). Then the sage left his body (62.15).
  - *Note (time spans):* Sampāti says **eight thousand years** have passed on the mountain since Niśākara went to heaven (60.9; CE 4.59.9), but also that "a little more than a hundred years" have gone by while he waited on the sage's word (*sāgraṃ varṣaśataṃ*, 63.3; CE 4.62.3). [disputed]
- **Wings restored** - As he speaks, **new wings with reddish feathers** (*aruṇacchadaiḥ*) sprout before the monkeys. He feels his youthful strength return, tells them the regrowth is their proof that they will find Sītā, and **takes off from the mountaintop** to test his new wings (Kiṣkindhā 63.8–14; CE 4.62.9). Heartened, the vānaras set out south at the auspicious hour (63.15).
- **Sampāti's water-rite for his brother** - This is **plain Vālmīki text**, not a retelling. Having given the vānaras Laṅkā's bearing, Sampāti asks them: *"Carry me to the ocean, the abode of Varuṇa — **I would offer water for my great-souled brother who has gone to heaven**"* (*pradāsyāmy udakaṃ bhrātuḥ svargatasya mahātmanaḥ*). The vānaras lift the wing-burnt bird to the shoreline, he makes the **udaka-kriyā** for Jaṭāyu, and they carry him back to his place (Kiṣkindhā 58.35–37; CE 4.57.32; recalled at 60.1). The rite Rāma began on the forest floor, Sampāti completes at the sea — the fraternal arc closed by the brother himself.
  - *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa):* Sampāti comes out of a cave in **Mount Mahendra**, not the Vindhya (Kiṣkindhā 7.29). He asks to be taken to the water to offer his brother water **before** he says anything, and only then tells them that Laṅkā stands on **Mount Trikūṭa** and that Sītā is in the **Aśoka grove**, a hundred yojanas off and visible to his vulture's eyes (7.47–53). The sage is named **Candramā** (*candramā nāma munirāṭ*), and before the prophecy he gives Sampāti a long teaching on the body, karma and the Self (8.8–47). The wings regrow at once (8.52–53). [late text]
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas):* Sampāti asks to be taken to the shore to offer *tilāñjali* to his brother, performs the rite, and then tells his story. The sage is again **Candramā**, who teaches him at length and foretells the monkeys' coming. Laṅkā is on **Trikūṭa** and Sītā sits in the **Aśoka garden**; "I see, you cannot: a vulture's sight is boundless". He is too old to help, and invites them to see what Rāma's grace has done to his body (Mānas, Kiṣkindhā, dohās 27–29). [late text]
  - *Variant (Sampāti's sons):* Sampāti's son **Supārśva** appears only in Kiṣkindhā 59; the Yuddha-kāṇḍa's Supārśva (CE 6.80.51) is a rākṣasa minister, not this bird. The *Matsya Purāṇa* names Sampāti's sons **Babhru and Śīghraga**, and Jaṭāyu's sons **Karṇikāra and Śatagāmin** (Matsya 6.35–36); the *Vāyu Purāṇa* also names Babhru and Śīghraga (Vāyu 69.327, cited from the Cologne Purāṇa Index, not checked against the text). The *Brahmāṇḍa* gives Sampāti's sons as **Vijaya and Prasaha** (Brahmāṇḍa 2.7.447; the verse is garbled). A different Sampāti in the Brahmāṇḍa, son of the elephant Supratīka, is not this bird (2.7.341). [late text]
  - *Variant (distance):* the gap to Laṅkā is **100 yojanas** in Vālmīki (58.20), and the same in the Rāmopākhyāna (MBh 3.266.57), the Adhyātma (Kiṣkindhā 7.52–53) and the Mānas (after dohā 28). Modern glosses variously convert the yojana. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (birthplace):* **Gṛddhrāj Parvat** (Griddhraj/Gidhha, Satna district, Madhya Pradesh) is venerated locally as the brothers' / Sampāti's birthplace and the Vindhya refuge. [folk]

## The Rāmopākhyāna version (Mahābhārata 3.263, 3.266)

The Mahābhārata's own short Rāma-story, told by Mārkaṇḍeya to Yudhiṣṭhira, keeps both birds but much more briefly. See [Rāmopākhyāna](../ramopakhyana.md).

- **Jaṭāyu** - "Daśaratha's friend, Jaṭāyu, son of Aruṇa, the mighty king of vultures, whose brother was Sampāti" (*sakhā daśarathasyāsīj jaṭāyur aruṇātmajaḥ*). He sees Sītā on Rāvaṇa's lap, attacks, and is cut down by Rāvaṇa's sword (MBh 3.263.1–6). Rāma at first suspects a rākṣasa and draws his bow. The bird names himself Daśaratha's friend, shows the way south only by moving his head, and dies; Rāma cremates him (3.263.15–21).
- **Sampāti** - In **Hanumān's own report to Rāma**, the fasting monkeys on Mount **Malaya** are talking about Jaṭāyu when a huge bird "like a second Garuḍa" appears and means to eat them. Then he asks who is speaking of his brother. He is Sampāti, Jaṭāyu's elder brother, whose wings were burnt in the race to the sun while Jaṭāyu's were not. Told of Jaṭāyu's death, he says he knows Rāvaṇa and has seen Laṅkā **on the far shore, in a valley of Mount Trikūṭa**, where Sītā must be (MBh 3.266.43–55).
- **What is absent** - No Niśākara, no prophecy, no regrown wings, no Supārśva and no water-rite. Hanumān then leaps the hundred yojanas (3.266.57).

## VI. Meaning, motifs, and reception

- **The two bird-helpers** - Jaṭāyu (action, sacrifice, death) and Sampāti (knowledge, far-sight, survival) form a deliberate pair: one **stops** the abductor and dies, the other **finds** the captive and lives. Together they are the non-human allies who, like the vānaras, embody *dharma* outside the human caste-order. Cf. [Kabandha](./kabandha.md), the third non-human guide of the search.
- **Sacrifice rewarded** - Jaṭāyu's death earns the **rites of a kinsman and the heaven of the righteous**; Sampāti's youthful sacrifice of his wings for his brother is **mirrored ages later** by the regrowth of those very wings when he serves Rāma — sacrifice repaid by grace. In the Mānas, where Sampāti was burnt for his pride and did not shield his brother, the regrowth is grace alone.
- **Dharma above birth** - That a **vulture** receives full funeral honours from an avatāra, and that birds carry the plot's decisive intelligence, is a recurring Rāmāyaṇa argument that virtue, not species or status, defines the noble (stated by Rāma at Araṇya 68.24). Cf. [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md) (the high-born villain) as foil.
- **From heaven to liberation** - Vālmīki sends Jaṭāyu to the heavens of the virtuous (Araṇya 68.29–30, 37). The Adhyātma and the Mānas turn the same death into **mokṣa**: a form like Viṣṇu's and a hymn of praise. This shows how the devotional retellings re-read the episode (§IV).
- **Cultic afterlife** - Jaṭāyu is venerated at sites claimed as the place where he fell, in Kerala (Jaṭāyu Para) and Andhra Pradesh (Lepakshi). The Jaṭāyu–Rāvaṇa combat is a favourite subject of temple sculpture and miniature painting — e.g. a folio of the **"Burnt" Rāmāyaṇa** (Datia, Bundelkhand, c. 1605; Cleveland Museum of Art 2013.306), which shows the vulture wounded and the asses of the chariot already dead.
- *Open roster:* the **full Araṇya-kāṇḍa 14 bird-genealogy** (the many daughters of Dakṣa/Kaśyapa and the descent of birds) is long, and the trees differ between texts — Vālmīki (Araṇya 14 = CE 3.13), the Mahābhārata (Ādi 1.60), the *Matsya* (6) and the *Brahmāṇḍa* (2.7) each route the birds differently. Treat exhaustive name-lists as [disputed] across recensions. [scholarly]
