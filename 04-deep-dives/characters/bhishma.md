# Bhīṣma

> Where this sits: a **Dvāpara-era life of extraordinary length**, spanning four generations of the Kuru house — from the reign of [Śāntanu](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md) to the [Kurukṣetra War](../mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md) and the dharma-teachings that follow it. Bhīṣma belongs to the [Mahābhārata deep-dives](../mahabharata/) and to the [28th Mahāyuga, Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md), at the very junction with the [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).
> Up: [characters](./README.md) · [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md) · His war-book: [06 — Bhīṣma Parva](../mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md) · His teaching-books: [12 — Śānti Parva](../mahabharata/12-shanti-parva.md), [13 — Anuśāsana Parva](../mahabharata/13-anushasana-parva.md)

*How to read the citations.* The chapter ranges in the section headings below are older, approximate numbers that mostly follow K. M. Ganguli's section numbering (the vulgate). The bullets cite the **BORI Critical Edition** (CE) as book.chapter.verse — e.g. CE 1.94.88 — and add the Ganguli section as "G" where it was checked. The two numberings differ by up to fourteen chapters in places.

**Bhīṣma** (Sanskrit *Bhīṣma*, "the terrible / the one of the dreadful vow"), born **Devavrata** ("vowed to the gods"), is the **grand-uncle, regent, patriarch, and conscience** of the [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/). He is the eighth son of the river-goddess **Gaṅgā** and King **Śāntanu** of the lunar (Candravaṃśa) line; the incarnation of the Vasu named **Dyaus** in the epic's narrative; the taker of the **terrible vow** of throne-renunciation and lifelong celibacy; the holder of **icchā-mṛtyu** (death only at his own will, his father's boon); the abductor of the Kāśi princesses whose act dooms him through **Ambā/Śikhaṇḍin**; the **first commander-in-chief** of the Kaurava host, falling on the *śaratalpa* / *śaraśayyā* ("bed of arrows") on the tenth day; and, dying, the **mouthpiece of the epic's longest discourse on dharma**. His epithets — **Gāṅgeya / Gaṅgāsuta / Āpageya** (son of Gaṅgā; e.g. CE 5.153.33, 6.76.7), **Śāṃtanava** (son of Śāntanu), **Devavrata**, **Pitāmaha** ("grandfather"), and **Tālaketu / Tāladhvaja** (palm-banner; his chariot carries a golden palm standard, CE 6.16.41, 6.56.21) — track the stages of that life.

## I. Divine origin — the Vasu and the curse (Mbh Ādi Parva ~91–100)

- **One of the eight Vasus** - Bhīṣma is the mortal incarnation of a **Vasu**, one of the eight attendant gods. The genealogy calls him "born of the Vasus' energy" (*vasuvīryāt*, CE 1.57.76). He is the one Vasu who actually took the cow, so he alone serves a full human life (CE 1.93.37, 1.93.42; G 99).
  - *On the name:* the narrative names the offending Vasu only **Dyaus** ("the Sky"; CE 1.93.17, 26, 37, 42; Ganguli renders "Dyu"). The Ādi Parva's own list of the eight Vasus (CE 1.60.17–19: Dhara, Dhruva, Soma, Ahas, Anila, Anala, Pratyūṣa, Prabhāsa) has no Dyaus. The common equation of Dyaus with **Prabhāsa**, the eighth name in that list, is a harmonisation made in later reference works (e.g. V. Mani, *Purāṇic Encyclopaedia*, 1975, p. 134, as cited by Wikipedia's "Bhishma"). CE 1.93 does not make it.
- **Two accounts of the offence, within the same text** - The epic gives two reasons for the curse, a few chapters apart:
  - *Variant (CE 1.91.11–13):* the Vasus, met by Gaṅgā on the road, say they were cursed for a "small fault": in their confusion they passed over Vasiṣṭha (Āpava) unawares while he sat at his twilight worship.
  - *Variant (CE 1.93.5–33; G 99):* Gaṅgā later tells Śāntanu the longer story, the theft of the cow.
- **The theft of the cow** - In Vasiṣṭha's hermitage on the flank of Meru lived his oblation-cow, the offspring of **Surabhi** by Kaśyapa (CE 1.93.6–9). The wife of the Vasu Dyaus wanted her for a mortal friend, **Jinavatī** (Ganguli's text reads "Jitavati", G 99), daughter of the royal sage **Uśīnara**, so that the friend might drink the milk and live free of age and sickness (CE 1.93.19–25). Dyaus and his brothers, "Pṛthu" and the rest, carried the cow off (CE 1.93.26–27). [The CE does not name the cow in this episode. Ganguli supplies "(Nandini)" in parentheses (G 99). The name Nandinī for Vasiṣṭha's cow appears in the Viśvāmitra story, CE 1.165.16–22.]
- **Vasiṣṭha's curse** - Seeing by inner vision who took her, Vasiṣṭha curses all eight Vasus to be born among men (CE 1.93.30–31). The Vasus beg him, and he does not withdraw the curse, but he limits it. The other seven will be freed "after a year" (*anu saṃvatsarāt*, CE 1.93.36). Dyaus, for whose deed they were cursed, will live long among men. He will father no children, will be righteous and learned in every śāstra, will devote himself to his father's welfare, and will **give up the enjoyment of women** (CE 1.93.37–39). The curse thus already contains the shape of the later vow.
- **The Vasus seek Gaṅgā as mother** - The Vasus ask **Gaṅgā** to become a woman, bear them, and throw each newborn into her waters (CE 1.91.14–18). She asks that one son survive, so that her union with the king is not fruitless. Each Vasu gives **one-eighth of his energy** (*turīyārdham*, CE 1.91.20) to that son, who "will have no offspring among mortals" (CE 1.91.21).

## II. Birth: Gaṅgā and Śāntanu (Mbh Ādi Parva ~96–100)

- **Mahābhiṣa — Śāntanu's previous life (in the CE text itself)** - This is not a later retelling. The CE opens the story with it (CE 1.91.1–8; G 96). **Mahābhiṣa**, a king "born of the Ikṣvāku line" (CE 1.91.1), had won heaven by sacrifice. When the wind lifted Gaṅgā's garment in Brahmā's court, he alone kept looking at her. Brahmā sent him to be reborn among mortals, and he chose **Pratīpa** as his father. Gaṅgā, for her part, kept thinking of him (CE 1.91.8). He is reborn as Pratīpa's son **Śāntanu** (CE 1.92.18). [Mahābhiṣa is Śāntanu's own former birth, not his ancestor.]
- **Gaṅgā and Pratīpa** - Gaṅgā first approaches King Pratīpa and sits on his **right thigh**. He tells her that the right thigh is the seat of children and daughters-in-law, the left that of a beloved, and asks her to become his son's wife (CE 1.92.2–16; G 97). Before he retires to the forest, Pratīpa tells Śāntanu that if a heavenly woman comes to him, he must never ask who she is or question what she does (CE 1.92.20–22).
- **Śāntanu meets Gaṅgā** - Hunting along the Gaṅgā, Śāntanu meets a radiant woman and asks her to be his wife. She consents on one condition: whatever she does, good or bad, he must not stop her or speak harshly to her, or she will leave him (CE 1.92.26–36; G 97).
- **The seven drowned sons** - She bears sons one after another and **throws each newborn into the Gaṅgā**, saying "I please you" (CE 1.92.43–44). Śāntanu says nothing for fear of losing her (CE 1.92.45). [The text says eight sons were born, CE 1.92.43, and seven were drowned.]
- **The eighth son spared** - When the eighth child is born, the grieving king stops her: "Don't kill him! Who are you?" (CE 1.92.46–47). She reveals herself as Gaṅgā. The eight sons were the cursed Vasus, and her promise to free them is now kept. She bids him guard "this son of great vows" (CE 1.92.48–55). Then, after telling him the Vasus' story, she **vanishes, taking the infant with her** (CE 1.93.43). He is called **Devavrata** and **Gāṅgeya** (CE 1.93.44).
- **Education** - Gaṅgā lists the boy's teachers when she returns him (CE 1.94.32–35; G 100). He learned the Vedas and their limbs from **Vasiṣṭha**. He knows the whole śāstra of **Uśanas (Śukra)** and that of **the son of Aṅgiras (Bṛhaspati)**. He holds every weapon known to **Rāma Jāmadagnya (Paraśurāma)**. [The CE and Ganguli name no others. Cyavana, Indra and an upbringing "in heavenly realms", which the older version of this page gave, are not in these passages.]
- **Restored to his father** - For thirty-six years (16 + 8 + 4 + 8) Śāntanu took no delight in women and kept to the forest (CE 1.94.18). One day he finds the Gaṅgā running shallow. Upstream a tall youth has **dammed the whole river with arrows**, using a celestial weapon (CE 1.94.21–25). The boy bewilders his father with illusion and disappears. Gaṅgā then brings him back, adorned, and hands him over (CE 1.94.27–36). Śāntanu takes him to Hastināpura and installs him as **crown prince** (*yauvarājya*, CE 1.94.38).

## III. The terrible vow (Bhīṣma-pratijñā) and the boon of icchā-mṛtyu (Mbh Ādi Parva ~100)

- **Śāntanu's second love — Satyavatī** - Four years after the prince's return (CE 1.94.40), Śāntanu follows an exquisite fragrance by the Yamunā and finds a fisher-girl who ferries a boat by her father's order (CE 1.94.41–44). She is **Satyavatī** (also called Kālī, CE 1.99.21). Her father is the fisher-chief, **Dāśarāja**, but she was born of the seed of a noble king (CE 1.94.71; the birth-story, from King Uparicara Vasu, is CE 1.57). The fisher-chief names his price: her son must be crowned after Śāntanu, and no one else (CE 1.94.51). The king will not grant this. He goes home sick with longing (CE 1.94.52–53).
- **Devavrata learns the cause** - Asked why he is grieving, Śāntanu gives his son only a general reason. An only son is no son, he says; Devavrata is a warrior who can die only by a weapon, and then the line would end (CE 1.94.57–63). Devavrata learns the real cause from an old minister (CE 1.94.65–66). With elder kṣatriyas, he goes to the fisher-chief to ask for the girl on his father's behalf (CE 1.94.67).
- **The renunciation of the throne** - Before the assembled kings he promises: "the son born of her shall be our king" (CE 1.94.79).
- **The vow of celibacy** - The fisher-chief objects that Devavrata's own sons might still contest the crown (CE 1.94.84). Devavrata then makes the second, harder vow: "I gave up the kingdom before. Now, for the sake of offspring too, I make this resolve: from today I shall be a celibate (*brahmacarya*). Even without a son I shall have undecaying worlds in heaven" (paraphrase of CE 1.94.87–88).
- **The name "Bhīṣma"** - Apsarases, gods and seers rain flowers from the sky and cry "**This is Bhīṣma**" (*bhīṣmo 'yam*, CE 1.94.90). The assembled kings say the same (CE 1.94.93). He calls Satyavatī "mother", seats her in his chariot and brings her to his father (CE 1.94.91–92).
- **The boon of icchā-mṛtyu** - Seeing the deed, Śāntanu, "pleased, the father himself" (*tuṣṭaḥ pitā svayam*), grants him **svacchanda-maraṇa**, death at his own choosing (CE 1.94.94; G 100). Bhīṣma himself later attributes the boon to his father (CE 6.114.33, 6.114.99). Kṛṣṇa ties the same power to his devotion to his father: "you are devoted to your father … so death stands at your command like a servant" (CE 13.153.45).
  - *On the grantor:* the CE is unambiguous that the boon is Śāntanu's. The gods' part is the shower of flowers and the naming (CE 1.94.90). Retellings that make the gods co-grantors broaden the source text. They are not a variant reading of it.

This single vow is the **hinge of the whole epic**: it deprives the Kuru throne of Bhīṣma's own heirs, forces the niyoga (levirate) that produces Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Pāṇḍu, and binds Bhīṣma to the service of the throne of Hastināpura.

## IV. Regent and grand-patriarch of Hastināpura (Mbh Ādi Parva ~101–110)

- **Two half-brothers, two reigns** - Satyavatī bears Śāntanu two sons, **Citrāṅgada** and **Vicitravīrya** (CE 1.95.2–3). Śāntanu dies before the younger reaches manhood (CE 1.95.4). Bhīṣma crowns Citrāṅgada (CE 1.95.5). A gandharva king of the same name challenges him, fights him for three years at Kurukṣetra on the Hiraṇvatī, and kills him (CE 1.95.7–10). Bhīṣma then crowns the boy Vicitravīrya and governs in his name, "abiding by Satyavatī's counsel" (CE 1.95.12–14, 1.96.1). See [Vicitravīrya and Citrāṅgada](./vichitravirya-and-chitrangada.md).
- **The Kāśi abduction (see §V)** - Bhīṣma wins brides for Vicitravīrya by force at the Kāśi svayaṃvara (CE 1.96).
- **Vicitravīrya's death** - Vicitravīrya lives seven years with Ambikā and Ambālikā, then dies of consumption (*yakṣman*, CE 1.96.57–58).
- **The refusal and the niyoga** - Satyavatī asks Bhīṣma to beget sons on the widows, to take the throne, and to marry (CE 1.97.9–11). He refuses with his great oath. The earth might give up its scent, water its taste, the sun its light, Indra his valour and Dharma dharma, but he will not give up truth (CE 1.97.15–18). She pleads emergency-dharma (*āpaddharma*), and he refuses again (CE 1.97.21–24). Then he offers a way out himself. He cites the ancient kṣatriya precedents (CE 1.97.25–26, 1.98) and proposes that a qualified brāhmaṇa be invited to beget sons on the widows (CE 1.99.1–2). Satyavatī reveals her pre-marital son, the sage **Vyāsa** (CE 1.99.4–17). Bhīṣma approves, and she summons him (CE 1.99.18–21). Vyāsa fathers **Dhṛtarāṣṭra** on Ambikā, **Pāṇḍu** on Ambālikā, and **[Vidura](./vidura.md)** on a maid-servant (CE 1.100). Every Kaurava and Pāṇḍava thus descends from a chain set in motion by Bhīṣma's celibacy.
- **Regent for the princes; marriages he arranged** - Bhīṣma raises the three boys "like sons" and keeps the kingdom on the wheel of dharma (CE 1.102.12–15).
  - He sends for **[Gāndhārī](./gandhari.md)** for Dhṛtarāṣṭra, having heard she had a boon of a hundred sons (CE 1.103.9–11).
  - **Kuntī chooses Pāṇḍu herself** at her own svayaṃvara (CE 1.105.1–3).
  - Bhīṣma then goes to the Madra capital and **buys [Mādrī](./madri.md)** "with great wealth" as Pāṇḍu's second wife (CE 1.105.4–5).
  - He finds Vidura a bride, a daughter of King Devaka born of a śūdra mother (*pāraśavī*, CE 1.106.12).
- **Elder of the court** - He later fosters the Kauravas and Pāṇḍavas alike as **Pitāmaha** ("grandfather").
  - At Yudhiṣṭhira's Rājasūya, it is Bhīṣma who names **Kṛṣṇa** as the guest most worthy of the first honour (*arghya*, CE 2.33.26–30). That choice triggers Śiśupāla's tirade, which mocks Bhīṣma with fables of a hypocritical old swan (CE 2.38.28–38) and the *bhūliṅga* bird (CE 2.41.18–19).
  - At the dice game he is present and does not intervene in deed (§X).

## V. Ambā, Ambikā, Ambālikā — and the seed of his fall (Mbh Ādi Parva ~102; Udyoga Parva ~170–193)

The story is told twice. The Ādi Parva gives the short form (CE 1.96; G 102). In the Udyoga Parva, Bhīṣma tells the long form himself to Duryodhana on the eve of war, to explain why he will not strike Śikhaṇḍin (the *Ambopākhyāna*, CE 5.170–193). For the full arc see **[Ambā → Śikhaṇḍin](./amba-shikhandi.md)**.

- **The abduction at Kāśi** - Bhīṣma hears that the three daughters of the king of Kāśi are choosing husbands. With his mother's leave, he drives to Vārāṇasī **in a single chariot** (CE 1.96.3–4). He carries off **Ambā, Ambikā, Ambālikā**, challenging the assembled kings (CE 1.96.6–13). He beats the pursuing kings (CE 1.96.14–24). Then **Śālva, lord of Saubha**, comes after him; Bhīṣma kills his horses and charioteer and **lets him go alive** (CE 1.96.25–39).
- **Ambā's plea — first in the Ādi Parva** - At Hastināpura, as the wedding is prepared, the eldest says that in her heart she had already chosen the lord of Saubha, and he her (CE 1.96.47–49). Bhīṣma consults brāhmaṇas learned in the Veda and **lets Ambā go** (CE 1.96.50–51). He gives Ambikā and Ambālikā to Vicitravīrya (CE 1.96.52).
  - *Variant (Ādi passage outside the CE text, Appendix I No. 55, printed in GRETIL after CE 1.96.53):* here **Vicitravīrya himself** declines a bride who loves another; Ambā shuttles between Śālva and Bhīṣma for six years and demands that Bhīṣma, who won her, marry her; and Śikhaṇḍin gains manhood by exchange with a **gandharva** at Gaṅgādvāra, not with the yakṣa of the Udyoga account. The older version of this page drew on this strand without saying so.
- **Rejected by Śālva** - In the Udyoga retelling, Śālva refuses her: he will not take as wife a woman "who belonged to another" (*anyapūrvā*), won and carried off by Bhīṣma (CE 5.172.4–6).
- **Ambā's resolve** - Leaving Śālva's city, Ambā weighs who is to blame (CE 5.173.1–8):
  - herself, for not leaping from Bhīṣma's chariot during the fight;
  - her father, for holding a svayaṃvara with valour as the bride-price (*vīryaśulka*), by which she was sent off "like a woman for sale";
  - Śālva;
  - fate;
  - and Bhīṣma.
  She settles on Bhīṣma as the root of her misfortune and resolves on revenge, "by austerity or by battle" (CE 5.173.7–8). She will not go home to her father (CE 5.174.12). She takes refuge with the ascetics, the sage Śaikhāvatya among them (CE 5.173.9–12). There her maternal grandfather, the royal sage **Hotravāhana**, finds her and sends her to Paraśurāma (CE 5.174.14–25). Rāma's follower **Akṛtavraṇa** arrives at the hermitage and hears her case (CE 5.175.6–12).
- **Paraśurāma's demand** - Ambā asks **Rāma Jāmadagnya** to kill Bhīṣma ("jahi bhīṣmam", CE 5.177.1). Rāma says he takes up arms only for brāhmaṇas, and promises to make Bhīṣma obey by words (CE 5.177.2–4). At Kurukṣetra he orders Bhīṣma, his former pupil, to **take her back** (*pratigṛhṇīṣva*, CE 5.178.5–8). Bhīṣma refuses. He will not give her again to his brother, since she declared herself Śālva's, and fear will not make him abandon his kṣatriya duty (CE 5.178.9–11). [The Udyoga text has Bhīṣma, not Vicitravīrya, make this refusal. The older version of this page said Ambā demanded Bhīṣma marry her; in the CE text she asks Rāma for his death. The marriage demand belongs to the Ādi appendix passage noted above.]
- **The duel at Kurukṣetra** - Master and pupil fight. The CE itself gives the length: "twenty days, and three more" (*viṃśatiṃ … dināni … anyāni dināni trīṇi*, CE 5.183.27), that is, **twenty-three days**.
  - **Night of the 23rd:** Bhīṣma prays at night for a sign. In a dream, the **eight brāhmaṇas** who had caught him when he fell from his chariot reveal the **prasvāpa** ("sleep") weapon, known to him "in a former body". It will put Rāma to sleep without killing him (CE 5.184.6–15).
  - **The 24th day:** the two loose brahmāstras against each other, and the worlds are scorched (CE 5.185.15–21). As Bhīṣma readies the prasvāpa, a cry rises from heaven. **Nārada** and the eight brahmavādins tell him not to dishonour his guru, and he withdraws it (CE 5.186.1–7).
  - **Rāma concedes:** Rāma exclaims, "I am beaten by Bhīṣma" (*jito 'smi bhīṣmeṇa*, CE 5.186.8). His ancestors appear and tell him to lay down arms. Bhīṣma is a Vasu, and **Arjuna** has been appointed by the Self-born as Bhīṣma's death in due time (CE 5.186.9–20). Both fighters at first refuse to withdraw (CE 5.186.21–26). Then the sages, Ṛcīka and Nārada at their head, and **Gaṅgā herself** enter the field (CE 5.186.23, 27). "Bhīṣma cannot be slain by you, nor you by Bhīṣma" (CE 5.186.29). Rāma's pitṛs make him put down his weapon. Bhīṣma salutes him, and Rāma praises him as without equal among kṣatriyas (CE 5.186.30–35).
  - *Outcome, as the text frames it:* a **halt imposed from outside** on a pair declared mutually unkillable, with Rāma voicing defeat. The page used to call it a simple "stalemate"; that is the summary in modern retellings such as Wikipedia's "Bhishma" (which cites C. Rajagopalachari's retelling for it), not the CE wording. The twenty-three-day figure is the CE's own, not a later count.
- **Ambā's tapas; half of her becomes a river** - Ambā fasts and stands in austerity for **twelve years** (CE 5.187.20–22). She wanders the tīrthas of the Vatsa land (CE 5.187.23–28). Gaṅgā rises from the water, hears her purpose, and curses her: if she dies under this vow, she will become a crooked river, full of crocodiles and dry eight months of the year (CE 5.187.29–35). In the end Ambā becomes, "by half", the river **Ambā** in the Vatsa country, and remains, by the other half, a maiden (CE 5.187.39–40).
- **Śiva's boon and the pyre** - Before the ascetics, **Śiva** appears and grants her wish. She will kill Bhīṣma; she will gain manhood "after some time"; she will be born in Drupada's house as a great chariot-fighter; and she will **remember everything** in her next body (CE 5.188.7–14). She builds a pyre by the Yamunā and enters the fire "for the killing of Bhīṣma" (CE 5.188.16–18).
- **Rebirth as Śikhaṇḍin(ī)** - [Drupada](./drupada.md), childless, propitiates Śiva and is promised "a girl who will become a man" (CE 5.189.3–7). The child is born a girl, **Śikhaṇḍinī**, and raised and announced as a son (CE 5.189.12–16). Drupada arranges her marriage to the daughter of **Hiraṇyavarman** of Daśārṇa, and the deception is discovered (CE 5.190–191).
- **The yakṣa Sthūṇākarṇa** - Śikhaṇḍinī flees to the forest. The yakṣa **Sthūṇākarṇa** (CE 5.192.20, 26) agrees to exchange sexes with her **"for a little while"** (*kiṃ cit kālāntaram*, CE 5.193.5–9). The Daśārṇa king is satisfied (CE 5.193.25–28). Then **Kubera** finds Sthūṇa hiding in female form. He curses him to remain so, and at the yakṣas' plea limits the curse: Sthūṇa will regain his form **when Śikhaṇḍin is killed in battle** (CE 5.193.30–46).
  - *On "temporary or permanent":* the CE's Udyoga account tells one plot, not two. The loan was meant to be temporary, and Kubera's curse makes it last for Śikhaṇḍin's lifetime. The older version of this page presented this as a variation between tellings; the one real divergence is the Ādi appendix passage (gandharva, not yakṣa) noted above.
  - Drupada later gives Śikhaṇḍin to **Droṇa** as a pupil in archery (CE 5.193.56–57).
- **Why Bhīṣma will not fight him** - Bhīṣma ends the tale by stating his standing vow. He will not loose arrows at a woman, at one formerly a woman (*strīpūrvaka*), at one with a woman's name, or at one in a woman's form. Since he knows Śikhaṇḍin's birth, he will not strike him even in battle (CE 5.193.60–65).
  - *Variant [folk] / modern:* regional, theatrical and modern retellings differ on whether Śikhaṇḍin is fully male in war, a transgender warrior, or "once a woman". The point the CE turns on is only that Bhīṣma will not raise weapons against the reborn Ambā.

## VI. Commander of the Kaurava host — the ten days (Mbh Bhīṣma Parva ~6–119)

- **Appointed senāpati (Udyoga Parva, not Bhīṣma Parva)** - Duryodhana asks Bhīṣma, "equal to Uśanas", to lead the army (CE 5.153.11–15). Bhīṣma accepts with stated terms (CE 5.153.16–24):
  - the Pāṇḍavas are as dear to him as the Kauravas;
  - he will give them good counsel but fight for Duryodhana "by the agreement made";
  - he will not exterminate the sons of Pāṇḍu, but will kill **ten thousand** (*ayutam*) warriors a day;
  - "either **Karṇa** fights first, or I", since the sūta's son always rivals him.
  He is consecrated amid ill omens (CE 5.153.26–30).
- **The Karṇa condition — Karṇa's own vow** - Bhīṣma set the terms, but the refusal comes from **[Karṇa](../karna.md)**: "I shall not fight at all while Gāṅgeya lives. When Bhīṣma is slain, I shall fight Arjuna" (CE 5.153.25). The quarrel flares again in the census of warriors (CE 5.165.4–27).
  - Bhīṣma rates Karṇa only an **ardharatha** ("half a chariot-warrior"): he has lost his natural armour and earrings, and is under Rāma's curse and a brāhmaṇa's curse (CE 5.165.4–6). Droṇa agrees (CE 5.165.7–8).
  - Karṇa calls Bhīṣma a dotard and repeats his vow not to fight while Bhīṣma lives (CE 5.165.20–27).
  - So Karṇa sits out the first ten days. The older version of this page said Bhīṣma refused to fight while Karṇa was in the field; the CE puts the refusal in Karṇa's mouth.
- **The narrative frame** - The Bhīṣma Parva opens its war narrative with the ending. Sañjaya returns from the field and tells Dhṛtarāṣṭra that **Bhīṣma has fallen** and lies on a bed of arrows, slain "by Śikhaṇḍin" (CE 6.14.1–9). The ten days are then told in flashback.
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's blessing before battle** - Before the first clash, Yudhiṣṭhira takes off his armour, lays down his weapons and walks on foot to Bhīṣma (CE 6.41.7–12), clasps his feet and asks leave to fight (CE 6.41.30–44). Bhīṣma blesses him: he would have cursed Yudhiṣṭhira had he not come.
  - He then explains his side: "A man is the slave of wealth; wealth is no one's slave. This is true, great king: I am bound by wealth to the Kauravas" (*arthasya puruṣo dāso … baddho 'smy arthena kauravaiḥ*, CE 6.41.36).
  - He promises counsel, but not fighting, for Yudhiṣṭhira's side. Asked how he can be beaten, he says his time to die has not yet come: "come again" (CE 6.41.38–43).
- **Days of carnage** - For ten days Bhīṣma holds the Pāṇḍava host at bay. Sañjaya's summary says he slew "an *arbuda* (a hundred million) of warriors in ten days" (CE 6.14.12). On the tenth day alone, an *ayuta* (ten thousand) (CE 6.114.73). [These are rhetorical totals, not counts.]
- **Kṛṣṇa charges Bhīṣma — twice, not once** - The older version of this page merged two episodes.
  - *Day 3, with the discus (CE 6.52–55; G 59):* Kṛṣṇa is angered by Arjuna's "soft fighting". He leaps from the chariot, raises the razor-edged discus (*rathāṅga* / *cakra*, named **Sudarśana**, CE 6.55.83–89), and rushes at Bhīṣma. Bhīṣma calmly hails him: "Come, come, Lord of gods … bring me down from my chariot" (CE 6.55.94). Arjuna runs after him, grips his feet and stops him "at the tenth step". He swears to fight in earnest, and Kṛṣṇa remounts (CE 6.55.96–101).
  - *Day 9, with the whip (CE 6.102; G 107):* the same scene recurs. Kṛṣṇa jumps down "with his arms as his only weapons, whip in hand" (*pratodapāṇiḥ*, CE 6.102.53–54). Bhīṣma welcomes death at his hands: "slain by you, I am honoured by the three worlds" (CE 6.102.59–61). Arjuna again catches him at the tenth step and reminds him of his promise not to fight (CE 6.102.62–69).
- **The night counsel (end of day 9)** - After the ninth day (CE 6.103; G 108), Kṛṣṇa offers to kill Bhīṣma himself. Yudhiṣṭhira will not make him break his word. He recalls that Bhīṣma had promised to counsel him (CE 6.103.40–45), and laments having to kill "my father's father, who raised us when we were fatherless children" (CE 6.103.48–49).
  - The Pāṇḍavas and Kṛṣṇa go **unarmed, at night, to Bhīṣma's tent** and ask him how he can be killed (CE 6.103.53–64). Bhīṣma says they cannot win while he lives, and gives them leave to strike him (CE 6.103.65–67).
  - He lists those he will not fight (CE 6.103.71–73): one who has laid down arms or fallen, one fleeing or surrendering, a woman, one with a woman's name, the maimed, an only son, the childless.
  - Then he names the way: put **Śikhaṇḍin**, "formerly a woman", in front, and let **Arjuna** attack from behind him (CE 6.103.74–78). The patriarch effectively **consents to his own fall**.

Cross-links: the full battle-narrative is [06 — Bhīṣma Parva](../mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md); [Arjuna](./arjuna.md); [Yudhiṣṭhira](./yudhishthira.md).

## VII. The fall on the bed of arrows (Mbh Bhīṣma Parva ~119–120)

- **Śikhaṇḍin to the fore** - On the **tenth day** the Pāṇḍavas march out with Śikhaṇḍin at the head of the whole army, Bhīma and Arjuna guarding his wheels (CE 6.104.3–5). Arjuna advances behind him (CE 6.114.1, 6.114.53). Bhīṣma holds back: "for two reasons I shall not fight the Pāṇḍavas — because they are not to be slain, and because of Śikhaṇḍin's womanhood" (*strībhāvāc ca śikhaṇḍinaḥ*, CE 6.114.32; G 119–120).
- **"These are not Śikhaṇḍin's arrows"** - Pierced again and again, Bhīṣma tells Duḥśāsana that no one but Arjuna could hurt him like this. The verses repeat the refrain *neme bāṇāḥ śikhaṇḍinaḥ*: these shafts, cutting his vitals like thunderbolts and serpents, are not Śikhaṇḍin's but Arjuna's (CE 6.114.51–61; G 120).
  - He still hurls a spear, which Arjuna cuts into three. He takes up sword and shield, and Arjuna shatters the shield (CE 6.114.62–65).
- **The fall** - Struck until "not a finger's breadth of his body was unpierced" (CE 6.114.80), Bhīṣma falls from his chariot **head to the east**, a little before sunset (CE 6.114.81). Held up by the shafts, **his body does not touch the earth** (*dharaṇīṃ nāspṛśat*, CE 6.114.84). He lies on the **śaratalpa**, "the bed of arrows" (CE 6.114.85).
- **The boon takes hold — and the swans** - As he falls, Bhīṣma notes the season: the sun is in its southern course (CE 6.114.86–89). A divine voice asks how so great a man could die in **dakṣiṇāyana**, and he answers, "I am still here" (*sthito 'smi*, CE 6.114.88–89).
  - Gaṅgā sends great **seers in the form of swans** from Lake Mānasa. They circle him and remark that he would be dying in the southern course (CE 6.114.90–95).
  - He tells them he will not go while the sun moves south. He will hold his breaths until **uttarāyaṇa**, as his father's boon allows (CE 6.114.96–100).
  - He then lies reciting the *Mahopaniṣad*, waiting for his time (CE 6.114.112).
- **The pillow of arrows** - His head hangs unsupported and he asks for a pillow. The kings bring soft cushions; he refuses them as unfit for a hero's bed (CE 6.115.32–33). He asks **Arjuna**, who, with Bhīṣma's leave, props up his head with **three sharp arrows** (CE 6.115.36–44; G 121–122). [Neither the CE nor Ganguli says the arrows were driven into the ground, as many retellings picture it.] He says he will be burned on these same arrows (CE 6.115.54).
- **Water from the earth** - Next morning, burning with thirst, Bhīṣma refuses the food and cool water-jars the kings bring. He is beyond human enjoyments now (CE 6.116.10–13). At his request, Arjuna drives round him in his chariot. He charges an arrow with the **Parjanya weapon** (*parjanyāstra*) and pierces the earth **on Bhīṣma's right side** (*pārśve dakṣiṇe*; the word also means "southern", and Ganguli has "a little to the south"). A pure, cool jet of water "like nectar" springs up for him (CE 6.116.19–24; G 123).
  - *Variant [later retellings]:* the CE does not say the water was Gaṅgā herself rising for her son, or that it came from pātāla. Those identifications belong to retellings. The text names only the Parjanya weapon.
- **A last plea for peace** - Bhīṣma tells Duryodhana that no one but Arjuna or Kṛṣṇa could have done this (CE 6.116.37–39). He urges peace while there is still time: give the Pāṇḍavas half the kingdom, let Yudhiṣṭhira rule at Indraprastha, "and let my end be the end of strife" (CE 6.116.40–50). Duryodhana is dejected (CE 6.116.36) but makes no peace. Bhīṣma falls silent and the kings go back to their camps (CE 6.116.51–6.117.1).
- **Karṇa at the bedside** - **Karṇa** hurries to him and introduces himself as "Rādheya". Bhīṣma sends the guards away and tells him: "You are Kaunteya, not Rādheya; I learned it from Nārada, from Vyāsa and from Keśava" (*kaunteyas tvaṃ na rādheyaḥ*, CE 6.117.7–9; G 124). He says he bears Karṇa no hatred. He spoke harshly to curb Karṇa's pride and to prevent a split in the family, and he rates Karṇa Arjuna's equal (CE 6.117.10–14). He urges him to join his brothers so that the feud ends with Bhīṣma (CE 6.117.19–20). Karṇa admits he knows he is Kuntī's son, but he will not abandon Duryodhana. Bhīṣma gives him leave to fight (CE 6.117.21–31).

## VIII. The teachings from the bed of arrows (Mbh Śānti & Anuśāsana Parvas)

- **Why the dying man is asked to teach** - After the war and Yudhiṣṭhira's coronation, Yudhiṣṭhira finds **Kṛṣṇa** sitting in meditation. Kṛṣṇa says he is thinking of Bhīṣma and urges him to learn from Bhīṣma before Bhīṣma departs at the sun's turning (CE 12.46.1–29).
  - At the arrow-bed, Kṛṣṇa tells Bhīṣma that **fifty-six days** of his life remain (*pañcāśataṃ ṣaṭ ca … śeṣaṃ dinānām*, CE 12.51.14).
  - Bhīṣma protests that he is too wounded to speak, and asks why Kṛṣṇa, the source of all teaching, does not teach (CE 12.52.6–13).
  - Kṛṣṇa grants a boon: no faintness, pain, hunger or thirst, and every branch of knowledge clear to him (CE 12.52.15–21).
  - He explains that he wants Bhīṣma's fame to grow. What Bhīṣma tells Yudhiṣṭhira will endure on earth like the Veda (CE 12.54.24–31).
- **Śānti Parva — three great sections** - The teaching, given over many days in answer to Yudhiṣṭhira's questions, falls into three blocks (conventional CE ranges):
  1. **Rāja-dharma**, the duties of a king and statecraft (Bhīṣma begins at CE 12.55–56; block to 12.128).
  2. **Āpad-dharma**, the ethics of emergency (CE 12.129–167).
  3. **Mokṣa-dharma**, the dharma of liberation, with extensive Sāṃkhya, Yoga and Nārāyaṇīya teaching (CE 12.168–353; its first question, at CE 12.168.1, moves from "the dharmas based on kingship" to the dharma of the āśramas).
  See [12 — Śānti Parva](../mahabharata/12-shanti-parva.md). [The older version of this page announced three sections and listed two.]
- **Anuśāsana Parva — instruction continued** - The teaching flows on into the gift-dharmas (*dāna-dharma*), the duties of varṇa and āśrama, the power of brāhmaṇas, women's dharma, vows and pilgrimage, mostly through parables and ancient legends (CE 13.1–152; [13 — Anuśāsana Parva](../mahabharata/13-anushasana-parva.md)).
- **The Viṣṇu-sahasranāma** - Yudhiṣṭhira asks, "What is the one god in the world, the one supreme refuge? What is the highest dharma? By reciting what is a creature freed?" In answer, Bhīṣma recites the **Thousand Names of Viṣṇu** (CE 13.135.2–3 ff.; 284 half-verse lines in the GRETIL text). It is **CE 13.135**, Ganguli/vulgate section **149** (G 149). [The older version of this page gave "~135 in the vulgate", which reverses the two numberings.]
- **Why Bhīṣma may teach** - The frame makes the point that a man who spent his life upholding dharma, even while bound to the losing side, earns the right to **transmit dharma at his death**. Kṛṣṇa gives his own reasons: to spread Bhīṣma's fame, and because, aged and learned, he is one in whom no one has seen a fault since birth (CE 12.54.27–35). Bhīṣma, the witness of Draupadī's humiliation, becomes the great teacher of righteousness.

## IX. Death at uttarāyaṇa (Mbh Anuśāsana Parva ~167–168)

- **The appointed day** - After fifty nights in the city (CE 13.153.5), Yudhiṣṭhira sees that the sun has turned north. He sets out for Kurukṣetra with Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Gāndhārī, Kuntī, his brothers, Kṛṣṇa, Vidura, Yuyutsu and Sātyaki, bringing ghee, garlands, silks and sandalwood for the funeral (CE 13.153.6–12). Vyāsa, Nārada and Asita Devala are already attending the patriarch (CE 13.153.13; G 167).
- **Bhīṣma's own reckoning** - "The sun has turned. **Fifty-eight nights** have passed while I have lain on sharp arrows, and they have felt like a hundred years" (CE 13.153.26–27). "This holy month of **Māgha** has come, and this should be the bright fortnight, *tribhāgaśeṣa*" (*tribhāgaśeṣaḥ pakṣo 'yaṃ śuklaḥ*, CE 13.153.28). The compound can mean "with three parts (quarters) remaining" or "with a third remaining". Ganguli renders this as "a fourth part of it ought by this to be over" (G 167).
- **Last words** - He comforts Dhṛtarāṣṭra and tells him not to grieve for his wicked sons (CE 13.153.30–35). He hails Kṛṣṇa as god and asks his leave to go. He recalls telling Duryodhana "where Kṛṣṇa is, there is dharma; where dharma is, there is victory" (*yataḥ kṛṣṇas tato dharmo yato dharmas tato jayaḥ*, CE 13.153.39). Kṛṣṇa gives leave: "Go to the Vasus; I see no wrong in you" (CE 13.153.44). Bhīṣma asks all present for leave to release his breaths and urges them to truth (CE 13.153.47–48).
- **Conscious release of the breaths** - He fixes his self in yogic concentrations (*dhāraṇā*) one after another, and his restrained breaths rise upward. As he releases each limb, **its arrows fall away**, until in a moment his body is free of them (CE 13.154.2–4). His self, held in all the bodily openings, **breaks through the crown of the head** and shoots into the sky "like a great meteor" (CE 13.154.5–6; G 168).
- **Funeral and Gaṅgā's lament** - The Pāṇḍavas, Vidura and Yuyutsu build the pyre. Yudhiṣṭhira and Vidura cover him with silks and garlands, and Yuyutsu holds the parasol. The priests perform the ancestral rite (*pitṛmedha*) and the Sāman-singers chant (CE 13.154.8–14). At the Bhāgīrathī, when the water-offering is made, **Gaṅgā rises weeping**. Her son, never beaten even by Jāmadagnya, has been "slain by Śikhaṇḍin" (CE 13.154.18–25).
  - *Correction within the text:* Kṛṣṇa consoles her. Her son was a Vasu who became human through a curse. He was slain fighting by **Dhanaṃjaya, not by Śikhaṇḍin**, and went to heaven by his own will, since not even Indra could have killed him with his bow raised (CE 13.154.27–32). The two attributions of the death, to Śikhaṇḍin (Sañjaya at CE 6.14.5–7, Gaṅgā here) and to Arjuna (Bhīṣma at CE 6.114.60, Kṛṣṇa here), both stand in the text.
- **The date — Bhīṣma Aṣṭamī (later observance)** - The calendar tradition keeps his death-day as **Māgha śukla aṣṭamī**, the eighth lunar day of the bright fortnight of Māgha (Jan–Feb). It is observed as **Bhīṣma Aṣṭamī**, with **Ekoddiṣṭa-śrāddha** offered for the sonless patriarch (date: drikpanchang.com, dekhopanchang.com; Ekoddiṣṭa-śrāddha: drikpanchang.com).
  - *Divergence:* the epic's own verse (CE 13.153.28) does not name a tithi, and its compound is ambiguous. Read as Ganguli reads it ("a fourth part … over"), it points to about the fourth day of the bright fortnight. Read as "a third remaining", it points to about the tenth. On neither literal reading does the verse give the eighth; the Sanskrit commentaries were not checked for this page. The Aṣṭamī date is an observance tradition, and it is recorded here beside the verse, not reconciled with it.
  - *Number of nights on the arrow-bed:* the CE states **fifty-eight nights** outright (CE 13.153.27), so the figure is not a later harmonisation, as the older version of this page claimed. A second in-text number sits beside it: Kṛṣṇa's "fifty-six days remain" (CE 12.51.14), said some time after the war. The two figures belong to different moments and are not reconciled in the text. They are not reconciled here either.
- **Why the chosen day matters** - Bhīṣma dies in uttarāyaṇa, by his own will, fully conscious, his teaching done. He achieves the **ideal warrior's death**: not slain against his will, but releasing himself in the auspicious season toward the path of the gods. Kṛṣṇa's words, "death stands at your command like a servant" (CE 13.153.45), close the arc of the boon.

## X. Character, meaning, and open questions

- **The tragedy of the binding vow** - Bhīṣma is the epic's great study of a **vow that outlives its purpose**. His celibacy secures one succession but unravels the dynasty. His fidelity to the throne of Hastināpura chains him to Duryodhana's war, and he himself gives the reason in material terms: "I am bound by wealth to the Kauravas" (CE 6.41.36). His greatness and his guilt are the same fidelity.
- **What he said in the sabhā** - He is not silent at the dice game, but he declines to judge. When Draupadī asks whether she was lawfully staked, Bhīṣma replies that **because dharma is subtle** he cannot answer properly (CE 2.60.40–42).
  - He sets out both sides: one who has lost himself cannot stake what is not his, yet a wife is under her husband's control.
  - Asked again, he says that in the world, what a strong man sees as dharma is called dharma, and that Yudhiṣṭhira himself must decide (CE 2.62.14–21).
  - The Mahābhārata never fully exonerates this refusal to rule. His later teachings on obedience to an unjust master read, in retrospect, as a meditation on that failure.
- **Two clocks** -
  - *Scriptural:* Bhīṣma's life sits at the **close of Dvāpara Yuga**, the war marking the threshold of [Kali Yuga (3102 BCE)](../../00-time-and-cosmology/).
  - *Academic [scholarly]:* the Bhīṣma material is layered across the long redaction of the Mahābhārata. The bulk is commonly placed between about the 3rd c. BCE and the 3rd c. CE, reaching its final form by the early Gupta period (c. 4th c. CE) (summary in Wikipedia, "Mahabharata").
  - The **Śānti/Anuśāsana didactic books and the Viṣṇu-sahasranāma are widely held to be later strata** [late text]. One piece of manuscript evidence is often cited. The parvan list in the **Spitzer Manuscript** (radiocarbon c. 130 CE; palaeographically c. 200–230 CE, per Eli Franco) does not name the Anuśāsana or Virāṭa parvans (D. Schlingloff, "The Oldest Extant Parvan-List of the Mahābhārata", *JAOS* 89, 1969, pp. 334–338, as summarised in Wikipedia, "Spitzer Manuscript"). Others, such as John Brockington, caution that the material may have sat under other parvan titles. [Secondary summaries; Schlingloff's article itself was not consulted for this page.]
- **Fixed and loose figures** - The duel with Paraśurāma (23 days, CE 5.183.27) and the nights on the arrow-bed (58, CE 13.153.27) are figures the critical text itself states. The daily kill-tallies of his ten days (an *ayuta* a day, an *arbuda* in all) are rhetorical. The Aṣṭamī death-date is calendar tradition (§IX).
