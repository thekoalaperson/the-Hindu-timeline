# Sātyaki (Yuyudhāna) — the Vṛṣṇi Hero on the Pāṇḍava Side

> Where this sits: a **Dvāpara-era life** at the Dvāpara–Kali junction — **Sātyaki** (given name **Yuyudhāna**), the Vṛṣṇi (Yādava) chief who is [Kṛṣṇa's](../krishna-lila.md) kinsman and [Arjuna's](./arjuna.md) archery disciple, fights *for the Pāṇḍavas* at Kurukṣetra as the mirror-image of [Kṛtavarma](./kritavarma.md) (who fights for the Kauravas), survives the war, and dies in the Yādava self-slaughter of the [Mausala Parva](../mahabharata/16-mausala-parva.md). Two clocks: the epic narrates *itihāsa* (war trad. ~3138 BCE; Kali begins 3102 BCE); academic dating of the text differs [scholarly].
> Up: [Epic Characters](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md).

**Sātyaki** ("son of Satyaka") is the Mahābhārata's great **Yādava on the Pāṇḍava side** — the structural counterweight to [Kṛtavarma](./kritavarma.md), the Yādava on the Kaurava side. A grandson of Śini (whence his other patronymic **Śaineya**), he is bound to the Pāṇḍava cause twice over: by clan loyalty to Kṛṣṇa and by discipleship to Arjuna, who taught him the bow. His war is one of the epic's most sustained displays of second-tier brilliance — above all the Day-14 solo drive through Droṇa's triple array in Arjuna's wake — but it is scarred by a single act the epic itself brands *adharma*: the beheading of the disarmed, fasting **Bhūriśravas**. Like Kṛtavarma, he survives Kurukṣetra only to die thirty-six years later at Prabhāsa, where the two Yādavas' old battlefield sins — the Sauptika night-raid and the Bhūriśravas killing — are hurled back and forth as taunts until each destroys the other, igniting the extinction of the clan.

---

## I. Lineage & names — the Śini line of the Vṛṣṇis

- **Śini → Satyaka → Sātyaki** — He descends from the Vṛṣṇi sept of **Śini**: Śini's son **Satyaka**, and Satyaka's son **Yuyudhāna**, called **Sātyaki** (patronymic from Satyaka) and **Śaineya** (descendant of Śini). See the full clan tree in [Yādava-vaṃśa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/yadava-vamsha.md). (Mbh, throughout; Bhāgavata IX.24)
- **Names** — **Yuyudhāna** ("eager to fight") is his given name in the war-books; **Sātyaki** the more common patronymic; **Śaineya** the clan-style used in his duels (Mbh, war parvas).
- **Kinsman of Kṛṣṇa** — As a Vṛṣṇi noble of Dvārakā he belongs to the same Yādava confederacy as [Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md) and [Balarāma](./balarama.md); he is one of the trusted inner circle of the Dvārakā court (Mbh; Bhāgavata IX.24).
- **Disciple of Arjuna** — Sātyaki learned the science of arms from **[Arjuna](./arjuna.md)**; the epic repeatedly names him Arjuna's *śiṣya* (pupil), and the bond of teacher and student underwrites his Pāṇḍava allegiance and Arjuna's fierce protectiveness of him on Day 14 (Mbh Droṇa P.).
- **The clan split** — The Yādavas divide for the war: Kṛṣṇa drives Arjuna's chariot unarmed, Balarāma abstains and goes on pilgrimage, **Sātyaki leads a Vṛṣṇi force for the Pāṇḍavas**, and **[Kṛtavarma](./kritavarma.md) leads one for the Kauravas** — the fissure that will curdle into the Mausala fratricide (Mbh Udyoga P.; see [Yādava-vaṃśa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/yadava-vamsha.md)). ## II. Before the war — Rājasūya, Upaplavya, the embassy (Mbh Sabhā & Udyoga Parvas)

- **At the Rājasūya** — At Yudhiṣṭhira's imperial consecration in Indraprastha, Sātyaki is among the honoured Vṛṣṇi guests and serves in the coronation rites — in Ganguli's text he **holds the royal umbrella** over the anointed king (Mbh Sabhā P.).
- **The war-party voice at Upaplavya** — In the councils before the war, when **Balarāma** counsels conciliation with the Kauravas, **Sātyaki angrily rebuts him**, declaring that Yudhiṣṭhira's right cannot be begged for and that war is the kṣatriya's answer to the dice-hall outrage — one of the hottest pro-war speeches of the Udyoga Parva (Mbh Udyoga P.; see [Udyoga Parva](../mahabharata/05-udyoga-parva.md)).
- **Escort of the peace embassy** — When Kṛṣṇa drives to Hastināpura on the final peace mission, **Sātyaki accompanies him** as companion and guard, and is present through the embassy's failure (Mbh Udyoga P.).
- **One of the seven marshals** — Yudhiṣṭhira appoints **seven leaders** over the seven Pāṇḍava akṣauhiṇīs — Drupada, Virāṭa, Dhṛṣṭadyumna, Śikhaṇḍin, **Sātyaki**, Cekitāna and Bhīma — with Dhṛṣṭadyumna over all; Sātyaki commands with the rank of *atiratha* (Mbh Udyoga P.).

## III. Kurukṣetra, Days 1–13 — the steady Vṛṣṇi pillar (Mbh Bhīṣma → Droṇa Parvas)

- **Against Kṛtavarma, again and again** — From the opening days Sātyaki and Kṛtavarma seek each other out — Yādava against Yādava — in repeated inconclusive duels that run through the [Bhīṣma](../mahabharata/06-bhishma-parva.md) and [Droṇa](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md) Parvas; the epic keeps the pairing alive so the Mausala can complete it (Mbh VI–VII).
- **Guardian of Arjuna's wheel** — Sātyaki's standing assignment is the protection of Arjuna's chariot-rear and of Yudhiṣṭhira's person; he fights Bhīṣma's guards, duels Aśvatthāman, Bhūriśravas and the Trigartas, and is counted among the handful the Kaurava councils name as the enemy's most dangerous men (Mbh VI–VII).
- **The ten sons slain** — In the fighting around Day 14, **Bhūriśravas kills ten sons of Sātyaki** in battle — a grief that stands behind the ferocity of their duel that afternoon (Mbh Droṇa P.).

## IV. Day 14 — the rampage on Arjuna's trail (Mbh Droṇa Parva)

- **Yudhiṣṭhira's command** — Deep in the day of Arjuna's oath to kill [Jayadratha](./jayadratha.md), Yudhiṣṭhira — hearing Kṛṣṇa's conch and fearing Arjuna has fallen far inside Droṇa's triple array — orders **Sātyaki to follow Arjuna's trail** and bring back word. Sātyaki protests that Arjuna charged him to guard the king, but obeys, entrusting Yudhiṣṭhira to Bhīma and the Pāñcālas (Mbh 7, Jayadratha-vadha section; see [Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md)).
- **Through the wall of Droṇa** — Sātyaki drives into the Śakaṭa–Padma–Sūcī formations along the lane Arjuna broke, **circumventing Droṇa** as Arjuna had (declining the master's challenge as a pupil declines his teacher's teacher), and smashes through the Bhoja division of **Kṛtavarma**, defeating his fellow Yādava and passing on (Mbh 7).
- **Jalasandha and Sudarśana slain** — In the drive he kills **Jalasandha**, the Magadha prince, in a savage chariot-duel, and cuts down **Sudarśana**; he defeats the mleccha and Kāmboja troops set to bar him, so that the bards say **three cars alone crossed the whole Kaurava ocean that day — Arjuna's, Sātyaki's, and Bhīma's** (Mbh 7).
- **Duryodhana humbled** — Along the trail Sātyaki also defeats **[Duryodhana](./duryodhana.md)** in an archery duel, sparing him but stripping him of chariot and pride (Mbh 7).
- **Bhīma follows** — Yudhiṣṭhira, still anxious, next sends **Bhīma** after Sātyaki; the two fight their way to Arjuna's side in time for the killing of Jayadratha at the false sunset (Mbh 7; see [Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md)).

## V. The Bhūriśravas beheading — the controversy (Mbh Droṇa Parva)

- **An inherited feud** — The epic supplies a pre-history: in an earlier generation **Śini**, Sātyaki's grandfather, had carried off **Devakī** at her svayaṃvara on behalf of Vasudeva and defeated **Somadatta** (Bhūriśravas's father) in the ensuing fight; Somadatta then won from **Śiva** the boon of a son who would one day **hurl Śini's descendant down in battle** — the duel of Day 14 is that boon maturing (Mbh Droṇa P.).
- **Sātyaki overpowered** — Exhausted by the day's rampage, Sātyaki is challenged by **Bhūriśravas**; both lose their chariots, and in the wrestling that follows Bhūriśravas throws Sātyaki down, **drags him by the hair and raises his sword to behead him** (Mbh 7.142).
- **Arjuna's foul stroke** — At Kṛṣṇa's urgent prompting, **Arjuna shoots off Bhūriśravas's sword-arm** from a distance, unannounced. Bhūriśravas bitterly reproaches Arjuna for striking a man engaged with another, lays down his weapons and sits in ***prāya*** — the yogic fast-unto-death — on the field (Mbh 7.142–143).
- **The beheading** — **Sātyaki rises and beheads the meditating, one-armed Bhūriśravas** over the shouted protests of both armies. He defends the act by his sworn **vow** that any enemy who struck him down alive in battle would not live, and by the day's griefs; the armies — and the text — nonetheless brand the deed a **breach of the war-code**, the Pāṇḍava side's counterpart to the killings of [Abhimanyu](./abhimanyu.md) and [Droṇa](./drona.md) (Mbh 7.143; see [Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md)).
  - *Variant (apportioning blame):* the tradition splits the guilt — some voices in the epic and later commentary weight **Arjuna's arrow** (striking one not engaged with him) as the first breach that made the second possible; others hold Sātyaki alone answerable for slaying a man in *prāya*. Both readings are anchored in the text's own debate. [disputed]
  - *Variant (the fated frame):* the Somadatta–Śiva boon reading treats the whole duel as destiny discharging itself — Bhūriśravas fated to humble Śini's grandson, Sātyaki fated to survive it — a frame the epic offers without letting it cancel the *adharma*. (Mbh Droṇa P.)
- **The taunt that waits** — Kṛtavarma stores this killing up: at Prabhāsa, thirty-six years later, "you slew the unarmed, fasting Bhūriśravas" is his counter-blow to Sātyaki's Sauptika accusation (Mausala P. 3; see [Kṛtavarma](./kritavarma.md)).

## VI. The war's end — survival (Mbh Karṇa → Sauptika Parvas)

- **Through Karṇa's and Śalya's days** — Sātyaki fights on through the last parvas as one of the Pāṇḍava host's surviving pillars; in the [Śalya Parva](../mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md) he **kills Śalva**, the mleccha king fighting on a great elephant (Mbh IX).
- **Sanjaya seized** — In the final rout Sātyaki captures **[Sañjaya](./sanjaya.md)**, Dhṛtarāṣṭra's narrator-charioteer, and raises his sword to kill him; **Vyāsa intervenes** and Sañjaya is released to walk out of the dead field — the release that lets the epic's own eyewitness survive to finish telling it (Mbh IX; see [Śalya Parva](../mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md)).
- **Absent from the doomed camp** — On the night of the massacre, the five Pāṇḍavas, Kṛṣṇa and **Sātyaki** are away from the main camp; when Aśvatthāman, Kṛpa and Kṛtavarma butcher and burn the sleeping host, Sātyaki is thus among the **seven survivors of the Pāṇḍava side** (the five brothers, Kṛṣṇa, Sātyaki) (Mbh X; see [Sauptika Parva](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md)).

## VII. Death at Prabhāsa — the Mausala fratricide (Mbh Mausala Parva)

- **Thirty-six years on** — Doomed by the sages' curse on Sāmba and by Gāndhārī's curse on Kṛṣṇa's clan, the Yādavas gather at **Prabhāsa** and fall to drinking (Mbh XVI; Bhāgavata XI.30; see [Mausala Parva](../mahabharata/16-mausala-parva.md)).
- **The accusation** — Inflamed by wine, **Sātyaki taunts Kṛtavarma** with the Sauptika crime — "what kṣatriya slays sleeping men?" — dredging up the night-massacre and the old war grievances (Mausala P. 3).
- **The counter-taunt** — Kṛtavarma retorts with **Bhūriśravas**: Sātyaki, he says, beheaded an unarmed man fasting in yoga on the field. **[Pradyumna](./pradyumna.md)**, Kṛṣṇa's son, backs Sātyaki; the quarrel turns lethal (Mausala P. 3).
- **Sātyaki beheads Kṛtavarma** — Sātyaki springs up and **cuts off Kṛtavarma's head** — the last completion of the war's Yādava-versus-Yādava pairing (Mausala P. 3; see [Kṛtavarma](./kritavarma.md)).
- **Death under the mob** — The enraged **Bhojas and Andhakas** fall on Sātyaki en masse; **Pradyumna dies fighting at his side**, and Sātyaki is beaten down and killed. Kṛṣṇa's fury at their deaths ignites the general slaughter in which, with the erakā reeds turning to iron clubs in every hand, **the Vṛṣṇi race annihilates itself** (Mausala P. 3–4; Bhāgavata XI.30; see [Yādava-vaṃśa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/yadava-vamsha.md)).
  - *Variant (karmic symmetry):* the tradition reads the double death as twinned justice — the Sauptika gate-keeper beheaded for slaying sleepers, and the slayer of the fasting Bhūriśravas himself cut down unarmed in a brawl; each Yādava dies by the shape of his own war-sin. [scholarly]

## VIII. Themes & significance

- **The mirror of Kṛtavarma** — Sātyaki and [Kṛtavarma](./kritavarma.md) are the epic's matched pair: same clan, same Kṛṣṇa-kinship, opposite camps, one flagrant war-crime each (the Bhūriśravas beheading; the Sauptika gate-keeping), joint survival, and mutual destruction at Prabhāsa. The epic uses the pair to show that **kinship did not dictate allegiance** — and that the war's unfinished accounts were settled inside the clan itself. [scholarly]
- **The disciple's war** — As Arjuna's pupil, Sātyaki's Day-14 drive is framed as the proof of the teaching: he crosses the array his teacher crossed, refuses to duel his teacher's teacher (Droṇa), and is one of only three to pierce the whole host. His one great transgression comes not from his bow but from his **vow** — the epic's recurring engine of tragedy.
- **Threshold note (two clocks)** — Scripturally the war sits at the **Dvāpara–Kali junction** (war trad. ~3138 BCE; Kali from 3102 BCE) within the [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md), and the Mausala destruction — Sātyaki's death among the first — marks Kṛṣṇa's departure and the Kali age's full onset. Academically, the Vṛṣṇi–Andhaka confederacy reflects a historical west-Indian clan-polity whose internal feuding the Mausala episode mythologizes [scholarly].
