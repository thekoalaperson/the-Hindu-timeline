# Kaikasī — the Rākṣasī Who Commissioned Rāvaṇa

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Kaikasī** — daughter of the rākṣasa lord **Sumālī**, second wife of the brahmin sage **[Viśravas](./vishravas.md)**, and mother of [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md), [Kumbhakarṇa](./kumbhakarna.md), [Śūrpaṇakhā](./shurpanakha.md) and [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md). Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). Her whole narrative sits in the [Uttara Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md)'s prehistory, with one appearance in the [Yuddha Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md); the dynastic frame is [Paulastya / rākṣasa vaṃśa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/paulastya-rakshasa-vamsha.md), the race-context [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* her life falls in the **[Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the present 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara. On the academic clock her entire textual existence belongs to the **Uttara Kāṇḍa**, widely held a later stratum of the Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa* — with one exception, the Yuddha 34 report, which sits in the epic's oldest surviving core. [scholarly]

## Name and identity

- **Kaikasī (कैकसी)** — a rākṣasī princess of the house of Sukeśa, daughter of **Sumālī** and his wife **Ketumatī**, and one of four sisters — **Rākā, Puṣpotkaṭā, Kaikasī, Kumbhīnasī** — born alongside Sumālī's ten sons (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5). Vālmīki offers no etymology for the name and no description of her beyond the courtesy formulae; what the text gives her instead is a purpose.
- **Kaikeyī, the other one** — several recensions and translations of the Uttara Kāṇḍa spell her **Kaikeyī**, colliding exactly with Daśaratha's queen [Kaikeyī](./kaikeyi.md) of the Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa. The two women are unrelated and the collision is orthographic; the corpus keeps **Kaikasī** for the rākṣasī throughout, and the reader who meets "Kaikeyī" in an Uttara translation should check which book it sits in. [disputed — spelling]
- **Nikaṣā** — the alternate name under which she is best known in Purāṇic and later usage, and the form the patronymic *Naikaṣeya* ("son of Nikaṣā," an epithet of Rāvaṇa) is built from. It is not the Uttara Kāṇḍa's own word for her. [late text]
- **Half of a hybrid house** — her marriage is the join that makes the Laṅkā dynasty what it is: brahmin *tapas* and Vedic descent from [Pulastya](./pulastya.md) on the father's side, rākṣasa force and grievance from Sukeśa's line on hers. Every one of her children is read by the epic through that seam — Rāvaṇa as the sage-scholar who behaves like a demon, Vibhīṣaṇa as the demon who behaves like a sage (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9).

## Sumālī's design — the marriage as policy

The marriage is not romance and Vālmīki does not present it as one. It is a dispossessed king's plan to breed his way back to a throne, and Kaikasī is the instrument he names.

- **The sight that starts it** — Sumālī, long since driven out of Laṅkā and living in **Rasātala** after [Viṣṇu's war on his brothers](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md), comes up into the world of men and sees **Vaiśravaṇa (Kubera)** passing overhead in the **Puṣpaka**, on his way to visit his father. The splendour of it drives him back down to Rasātala asking himself one question — *by what means shall our power be increased?* (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9).
- **The instruction** — His answer is his unmarried daughter. He tells Kaikasī her youth is passing and sends her to choose **Viśravas, of the line of Pulastya**, for the stated reason that by him *"you will beget sons equal to that Lord of Wealth"* — sons who can match, and so displace, Kubera. The dynastic ambition is spoken aloud before the first child is conceived (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9).
- **She obeys** — Vālmīki gives her no objection at this stage and no counter-proposal; she goes. The one moment of will he does grant her comes later, and it is the moment that produces Vibhīṣaṇa (below).

## The twilight approach (Uttara Kāṇḍa 9)

- **The hour is the whole scene** — Kaikasī reaches Viśravas's hermitage at **dusk**, in the middle of the sage's **sandhyā** devotions — the inauspicious hour, and the detail the entire genealogy hangs on. She does not announce herself: she stands before him with her eyes cast down on her own feet, **scratching the earth from time to time with her toe** (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9). It is one of the epic's most economical portraits — a woman executing an assignment she has not chosen and cannot disown.
- **Viśravas's ruling** — The sage knows her purpose by his own sight and does not refuse her; he pronounces on the hour instead. Because she has come at that time, he tells her, she will bring forth **offspring of dark aspect, delighting in the company of doers of evil deeds** — a taint fixed by the moment of the union, not by her lineage or his (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9).
- **The one thing she asks for** — Kaikasī does not accept the sentence. She prostrates herself and objects outright: *"I do not desire such sons whose nature is depraved, from you — be gracious unto me!"* Viśravas concedes exactly one child: **the last-born will be like myself, and virtuous**. That concession is the origin of [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md), and it is the only thing in the Rāmāyaṇa that Kaikasī obtains by asking (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9).
  - *Reading note:* the passage is the reason the corpus does not treat Kaikasī as a flat villain. Vālmīki gives her the request, gives it a result, and never revisits it — the mother who was sent to breed monsters bargains one son out of the curse and gets the one who survives the war. [scholarly]

## The four children

- **The birth-order, as Uttara 9 gives it** — **Rāvaṇa (Daśagrīva)**, born ten-headed and twenty-armed amid ill omens; **Kumbhakarṇa**, of monstrous bulk; **Śūrpaṇakhā**, the daughter, of hideous aspect; and **Vibhīṣaṇa** last, whose birth alone is marked by a rain of flowers and celestial drums. Śūrpaṇakhā is therefore Vibhīṣaṇa's **elder** sister, and the order Rāvaṇa → Kumbhakarṇa → Śūrpaṇakhā → Vibhīṣaṇa is the text's, not a harmonization (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9).
- **Kubera is not hers** — **Kubera (Vaiśravaṇa)** is Viśravas's son by his first wife **Ilavidā / Devavarṇinī**, daughter of [Bharadvāja](../bharadvaja.md), born before Kaikasī comes to the hermitage. He is her stepson and her children's elder half-brother, and the rivalry she plants in Rāvaṇa is aimed squarely at him (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 3, 9).
- **Khara is her nephew, not her son** — the Janasthāna warlord **Khara** is the son of **one of Kaikasī's sisters**; Rāvaṇa names him to Śūrpaṇakhā as *"the son of the sister of thy mother."* The Araṇya Kāṇḍa's loose "brother" for him is idiom (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 24; see [Makarākṣa](../makaraksha-son-of-khara.md) and [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md)).

## "Strive to be like Vaiśravaṇa" — the sentence that starts the epic

- **The goad** — Years later, with her sons grown, Kaikasī again sees Kubera pass in the Puṣpaka on his way to Viśravas, and turns to her eldest with the line that sets the whole *Rāmāyaṇa* in motion: *behold your state, you who are of the same family* — **"you who art of immeasurable might, strive to be like Vaiśravaṇa himself"** (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9).
- **What it produces** — Rāvaṇa's answer to his mother is the **penance at Gokarṇa**: ten thousand years of austerity, a head offered into the fire every thousand years, and finally Brahmā's boon naming the seven classes of being that shall not kill him — with men and monkeys contemptuously left off the list (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 10; see [Rāvaṇa §III](./ravana.md) and [Gokarṇa](../../06-sacred-geography/gokarna-mahabaleshwar-atma-linga.md)). Every later disaster of the house runs back through that boon to this sentence.
- **The structural point** — the corpus's antagonist is manufactured in two conversations: Sumālī's instruction to his daughter, and his daughter's instruction to her son. Neither speaker ever lifts a weapon. [scholarly]

## Kaikasī in the war-book (Yuddha Kāṇḍa 34)

- **The one appearance outside the Uttara** — deep in the siege, **Saramā** — Vibhīṣaṇa's wife, sent by Sītā to listen at the council — reports back what was said: *"Excellent words were spoken by Kaikasī, the mother of Rāvaṇa, as well as Aviddha the aged minister, asking for your release, O Sītā!"* (the name is printed **Avindhya** in other recensions — the form under which the *Rāmopākhyāna* develops him into Sītā's protector). Rāvaṇa refused them both, holding to Sītā as a miser holds his hoard (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 34).
- **Why the citation matters** — this is not the late Uttara Kāṇḍa but the core war-book, and it places Kaikasī, in her own right, among the voices of Laṅkā who told Rāvaṇa to give Sītā back — the roster that already includes **Vibhīṣaṇa**, **Kumbhakarṇa**, **[Mālyavān](./sumali-and-malyavan.md)** and **[Mandodarī](./mandodari.md)**. The mother who commissioned him is also, at the end, among those who tried to stop him, and the corpus records both without reconciling them (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 34).
- **Reported, not staged** — the epic keeps her offstage even here: the plea reaches the reader at two removes, through Saramā's report to Sītā. Vālmīki never gives Kaikasī a scene of her own after Uttara 9.

## What Vālmīki does not give her

- **No lament** — the great lament over Rāvaṇa's body belongs to **Mandodarī** (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 111); the mother is not brought to the field, and the epic records no death, no farewell and no later mention of Kaikasī at all. [open-ended]
- **No cult, no curse, no boon** — unlike almost every other figure in Rāvaṇa's ancestry she neither pronounces nor receives one. Her instruments are two sentences of ordinary speech, which is precisely the corpus's argument for her weight.
- **No stated end** — where the tradition fills the silence it does so regionally (below), never canonically.

## Other traditions

- *Variant (Rāmopākhyāna, Mahābhārata):* the epic's condensed Rāma-story dispenses with Kaikasī entirely. There, **Kubera sends three rākṣasīs** — **Puṣpotkaṭā, Rākā and Mālinī** — to placate the angry sage Viśravas, and the demon brood is distributed among them rather than born to one mother. Vālmīki's single-mother scheme and the Mahābhārata's three-mother scheme are not to be merged (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva*; canonical home: [Rāmopākhyāna](../ramopakhyana.md)). [disputed]
- *Variant (Purāṇic genealogies):* later lists redistribute Viśravas's rākṣasa children across Sumālī's other daughters — Khara to **Rākā**, Rāvaṇa and Kumbhakarṇa to **Puṣpotkaṭā**. Uttara 9 gives all four to Kaikasī alone (*Purāṇic genealogical tradition*; see [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md)). [disputed]
- *Variant (Gokarṇa-māhātmya / Sahyādri sthala-tradition):* the west-coast legend of the **Ātma-liṅga** opens on Kaikasī, who worships a **liṅga of sand** at the seashore each day until a tide carries it off, and laments that she has never worshipped a true and indestructible one. It is to answer his mother's grief that Rāvaṇa goes to Kailāsa for Śiva's own Prāṇa-liṅga — the errand Gaṇeśa then spoils. This is the one tradition that makes Kaikasī pious rather than political (*Gokarṇa-māhātmya*; canonical home: [Gokarṇa / Mahābaleśvara](../../06-sacred-geography/gokarna-mahabaleshwar-atma-linga.md), with the Kailāsa frame at [Rāvaṇānugraha](../ravana-kailasa-ravananugraha.md)). [regional]
- *Modern retellings:* contemporary novels and Rāvaṇa-centred fiction have made Kaikasī a full character — variously the humiliated exile's daughter, the architect of the dynasty, or the mother who loses four children to one ambition. These are literary works building on the two Uttara conversations, not scripture. [scholarly]

## Why she matters

- **The dynasty's second engine** — Sukeśa's line loses Laṅkā to Viṣṇu and Kubera by force of arms; it takes Laṅkā back by a marriage. Kaikasī is where the rākṣasa house stops fighting the gods and starts marrying into their genealogy, and the whole later argument over Rāvaṇa's brahmin learning descends from her wedding-night (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9). [scholarly]
- **The pairing with Mantharā and Śūrpaṇakhā** — the *Rāmāyaṇa* repeatedly puts its hinges in the mouths of women who never fight: [Mantharā](./manthara.md) speaks the exile into being, [Śūrpaṇakhā](./shurpanakha.md) speaks the abduction into being, and Kaikasī speaks the antagonist himself into being one generation earlier. The corpus treats the pattern as deliberate, and notes that the tradition has punished the three very unequally. [scholarly]
- **The half-brahmin problem** — because of her, Rāvaṇa is a **brahma-rākṣasa** in the literal sense: killing him raises the question of brahmin-slaying that Vālmīki, Tulsīdās and the commentators all handle differently, and every treatment of it starts at Uttara 9.
