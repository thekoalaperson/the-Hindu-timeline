# Arāvāṇ / Irāvān (Kūttāṇṭavar) — the One-Night Bridegroom

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Irāvān** (Sanskrit *Irāvat / Airāvata*; Tamil **Arāvāṇ, Aravān**), son of [Arjuna](./arjuna.md) and the Nāga princess **Ulūpī** — the warrior who offers himself to Kālī on the eve of the war and whose wedding to Kṛṣṇa-as-Mohinī seeds the Tamil cult of **Kūttāṇṭavar**. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). His battlefield death is canonical Vyāsa; the *kaḷappali* sacrifice, the Mohinī marriage, and the Kūvākam cult belong to the [Tamil Mahābhārata and temple/oral tradition](../../90-literature-corpus/regional-and-oral-epics.md). [folk] [regional]
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* this sits at the Dvāpara/Kali cusp of the present 28th Mahāyuga of the Vaivasvata Manvantara (the war opening ~3139–3102 BCE by tradition); academically the sacrificial and cultic material is a South-Indian post-epic accretion, undated and absent from the BORI Critical Edition. [scholarly] [disputed]

## Birth and lineage
- **Son of Arjuna and Ulūpī** — begotten during Arjuna's twelve-year exile, when the wandering Pāṇḍava is drawn beneath the Gaṅgā by **Ulūpī**, daughter of the Nāga king **Kauravya** (Airāvata line); their *gāndharva* union produces Irāvān, raised in the serpent-realm (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva* ~206). See [Arjuna](./arjuna.md) and the [Nāgas & serpents](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/nagas-and-serpents.md) for the Nāga maternal line.
- **The name** — Sanskrit *Irāvat* links to *irā* ("water, refreshment") and to **Airāvata**; the Tamil reflex **Aravān / Arāvāṇ** is the form under which the South worships him. [scholarly]
- **Reared apart, fatherless in upbringing** — Ulūpī rears him in the Nāga world after Arjuna departs; in the canonical account Irāvān has been abandoned by an uncle (the Nāga Takṣaka strand) and comes to Arjuna only at the war (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Reunion in Indra's heaven** — father and son meet when Arjuna sojourns in **Indraloka**; Irāvān pledges to fight for the Pāṇḍavas, and rides to Kurukṣetra out of filial love (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).

## Battlefield exploits — canonical Vyāsa
- **A frontline cavalry champion** — Irāvān fights mounted, leading horse against the Kaurava ranks; on the war's early days he routs the cavalry of **Śakuni's** Gāndhāra brothers, slaying several of them (*Mahābhārata, Bhīṣma Parva*).
- **Death at the hands of Alambuṣa** — the rākṣasa **Alambuṣa** (Ṛśyaśṛṅga's son, kin-avenger of the demons) is set against him; through illusion-war Alambuṣa beheads Irāvān, who falls on (in most reckonings) the war's early days — a death that grieves Arjuna and is one of the war's first great losses (*Mahābhārata, Bhīṣma Parva*).
  - *Variant (counting tradition):* his fall is variously placed on the **first day** or the **eighth day** of the eighteen-day war; the Tamil cult emphasises the eve-of-war sacrifice rather than the parva death-count. [disputed]

## The kaḷappali — battlefield self-sacrifice to Kālī [folk] [regional]
- **The Tamil overlay** — beyond Vyāsa, the **Tamil Mahābhārata** (the *Pāratam* of Peruntēvanār, and especially **Villiputtūrār's** *Makāpāratam*) and the Kūttāṇṭavar temple tradition develop a sacrificial episode unknown to the Sanskrit epic. (Tamil Mahābhārata; Kūttāṇṭavar temple tradition) [folk]
- **The demand of the Goddess** — to secure Pāṇḍava victory a **kaḷappali** ("battlefield sacrifice") of a flawless warrior must be made to the war-goddess **Kālī** (Koṟṟavai); the victim must bear the **thirty-two perfections** (*muppattiraṇ*ṭu *lakṣaṇa*) of an ideal man. Only three qualify — Kṛṣṇa, Arjuna, and **Aravān** — and only Aravān freely volunteers his own head. (Tamil Mahābhārata; Kūttāṇṭavar temple tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **The self-offering** — Aravān consents to be sacrificed before the war so that the side he loves may win; his self-decapitation / offering of the head (*talaikāṇikkai*, "head-as-tribute") is the cultic core that the South commemorates, distinct from his later parva death by Alambuṣa. [folk]
- **The boon to watch the war** — in many tellings the severed but living head is granted sight of the whole battle (a motif paralleling [Barbarīka](./barbarika-khatu-shyam.md)); the head is enshrined and worshipped as the deity's face. [folk] [regional]

## The three last wishes and the Mohinī marriage
- **The dying boons** — granted last wishes before the sacrifice, Aravān asks (1) to die heroically in battle, (2) a thirty-three-day vigil/celebration before death, and — the decisive third — (3) **not to die unmarried**, for an unwed man's corpse may not be honoured and he must be mourned. (Tamil Mahābhārata; Kūttāṇṭavar temple tradition) [folk] [regional]
- **The bride no one will be** — no woman will marry a man doomed to die the next dawn and be widowed within a day; the request threatens to fail for want of a bride. [folk]
- **Kṛṣṇa as Mohinī** — to fulfil the vow, **Kṛṣṇa** takes the female form **Mohinī** (his enchantress avatāra; the same form of the *amṛta*-churning) and weds Aravān, granting him a true wedding night as his wife. (Tamil Mahābhārata; Kūttāṇṭavar temple tradition) [folk]
- **Mohinī's widowhood** — after Aravān is sacrificed at dawn, Kṛṣṇa-as-Mohinī **mourns him as a widow** — breaking her bangles, loosening her hair, beating her breast — before resuming the male Kṛṣṇa form. This grief of the god-as-widow is the emotional centre of the festival. [folk] [regional]

## Worship as Kūttāṇṭavar
- **The deity Kūttāṇṭavar / Aravān** — across the northern Tamil country (and into the Draupadī-Amman cult network) Aravān is enshrined as **Kūttāṇṭavar** ("lord/king of the *kūttu*," the ritual theatre); the shrine icon is characteristically a large **disembodied head**, often moustached and demon-faced, recalling the offered head. [folk] [regional]
- **The Kūvākam festival** — the principal eighteen-day festival is held at **Kūvākam (Koovagam)**, Villupuram district, Tamil Nadu, around the Tamil month of **Cittirai** (Apr–May), re-enacting the marriage, sacrifice, and widow's mourning. The temple priest ties the *tāli* (marriage emblem) on devotees, who become Aravān's brides for the night, then ritually mourn him as widows at the sacrifice. [folk] [regional]
- **The Aravāṇi / Thirunaṅgai community** — the festival is the great annual gathering of the **Aravāṇi** (named for Aravān) and **Thirunaṅgai** transgender / *hijṛā* communities of Tamil Nadu, who marry the god and enact his widowhood; the cult thus gives a sacred charter to a living social community, a feature unique among Mahābhārata afterlives. [folk] [regional]
- **Relation to the canon** — none of the kaḷappali, the thirty-two perfections, the Mohinī marriage, or the Kūvākam cult appears in the BORI Critical Edition; the figure is a Tamil regional-devotional and oral-epic development threaded back onto the canonical warrior. See the corpus note on [regional and oral epics](../../90-literature-corpus/regional-and-oral-epics.md). [scholarly] [disputed]
