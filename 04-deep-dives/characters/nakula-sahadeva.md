# Nakula and Sahadeva — The Twin Pāṇḍavas

> Where this sits: a paired deep-dive on **Nakula and Sahadeva**, the twin sons of **[Mādrī](./madri.md)** by the **Aśvins** and the youngest of the five Pāṇḍavas, whose lives run from birth on Mount Śataśṛṅga through the [Kurukṣetra war](../mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md) at the close of the [Dvāpara Yuga of this 28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) to their falls on the final northward walk in the dawn of Kali.
> Up: [Epic Characters](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md)

The epic keeps the twins as a matched but contrasted pair: **Nakula**, the elder, is its byword for **male beauty, swordsmanship, and horse-lore**; **Sahadeva**, the younger, its byword for **quiet wisdom, cattle-mastery, and knowledge of the stars**. They are the least foregrounded of the five brothers — and the epic knows it, giving each of them exactly one blazing moment of prominence: Nakula the western conquest and the horse-master's year at Virāṭa, Sahadeva the vow against [Śakuni](./shakuni.md) and its fulfilment on the war's last day.

---

## Names, birth, and Aśvin parentage

- **Sons of the twin gods** — Barred from fatherhood by the Kindama curse, **Pāṇḍu** asks [Kuntī](./kunti.md) to share Durvāsā's invocation-mantra with his junior queen [Mādrī](./madri.md); Mādrī shrewdly summons the **twin Aśvins**, the physician-gods of the dawn, and by that single invocation bears **twin sons in one birth** (Mbh, Ādi Parva). See [Mādrī](./madri.md) for the mantra-arithmetic and [the Thirty-three Devas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/the-thirty-three-devas.md) for the Aśvins themselves.
- **The voice at their birth** — As at each Pāṇḍava birth on **Mount Śataśṛṅga**, a bodiless voice speaks over the twins, declaring that in **energy and beauty they will surpass even the Aśvins** who begot them (Mbh, Ādi Parva, Saṃbhava).
- **Named by the forest ṛṣis** — The ascetics of Śataśṛṅga name the elder **Nakula** and the younger **Sahadeva**; the pair carry the matronymic **Mādreya** ("son of Mādrī") and the patronymic **Āśvineya** ("son of the Aśvins"), distinguishing them from Kuntī's three **Kaunteyas** (Mbh, Ādi Parva).
- **Kuntī refuses a second round** — When Pāṇḍu asks Kuntī to lend the mantra again so Mādrī might have more sons, Kuntī refuses, having been outdone once by the twin-trick; the Pāṇḍava count is thereby fixed at **five** (Mbh, Ādi Parva).

## Orphaned: Mādrī's pyre, Kuntī's fostering

- **Pāṇḍu's death and the entrusting of the twins** — When the Kindama curse kills Pāṇḍu in Mādrī's embrace, Mādrī insists on following him onto the pyre and **commits Nakula and Sahadeva to Kuntī**, arguing the elder queen can raise all five impartially; Kuntī rears the twins as her own, which is why she is styled mother of five though she bore three (Mbh, Ādi Parva). See [Mādrī](./madri.md) and [Kuntī](./kunti.md).
- **A fraternity that never splits** — Across the whole epic the two-mothers line never fractures the brotherhood; the twins' obedience to [Yudhiṣṭhira](./yudhishthira.md) is absolute, through dice-hall, forest, and war (Mbh, throughout).
- **The uncle across the line** — Their mother's brother **[Śalya](./shalya.md)** of Madra, trapped onto the Kaurava side by Duryodhana's hospitality-trick, faces the twins at Kurukṣetra yet never seeks their lives (Mbh, Udyoga/Śalya Parvas).

## Marriages and sons

- **Draupadī, the shared wife** — Both twins are husbands of **[Draupadī](./draupadi.md)** under the five-brother marriage; her son by Nakula is **Śatānīka**, by Sahadeva **Śrutasena** — two of the five Upapāṇḍavas later slain in Aśvatthāman's night raid (Mbh, Ādi Parva; [Sauptika Parva](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md)).
- **Nakula and Kareṇumatī** — Nakula also weds **Kareṇumatī**, a princess of **Cedi**, who bears him **Niramitra** (Mbh, Ādi Parva genealogies).
- **Sahadeva and Vijayā** — Sahadeva also weds **Vijayā**, daughter of **Dyutimat of Madra**, his mother's country, who bears him **Suhotra** (Mbh, Ādi Parva genealogies).

## Nakula: swords, horses, and the handsomest man alive

- **The epic's standard of beauty** — Nakula is repeatedly called the **most beautiful man of his generation**; the trait is his signature exactly as strength is Bhīma's — and it returns, fatally, as the flaw named at his final fall (Mbh, throughout; [Mahāprasthānika Parva](../mahabharata/17-mahaprasthanika-parva.md)).
- **Master of sword and horse** — Under Droṇa's training Nakula becomes an expert **swordsman and cavalry-fighter**, and the epic makes him a supreme **aśva-vidyā (horse-lore) man**: handling, healing, and judging horses (Mbh, Ādi/Virāṭa Parvas).
  - *Attribution (later tradition) [late text]:* a Sanskrit hippiatric treatise, the **Aśvacikitsita/Aśvaśāstra "of Nakula,"** circulates under his name in the Śālihotra veterinary tradition; the ascription to the epic hero is conventional, the text itself medieval [scholarly].
- **Western digvijaya for the Rājasūya** — For Yudhiṣṭhira's imperial sacrifice Nakula marches **west**: he subdues Rohītaka, the Śibis, Trigartas and the western peoples, and comes to **Madra**, where his uncle **[Śalya](./shalya.md)** accepts Pāṇḍava suzerainty **out of affection**; the tribute he brings home is said to load **ten thousand camels** (Mbh, [Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md), Digvijaya).
- **Spared by Karṇa** — On Day 17 Nakula duels **[Karṇa](../karna.md)** and is overwhelmed; Karṇa, bound by his promise to Kuntī to slay none of her sons but Arjuna, **spares him**, dismissing him with a taunt and a touch of the bow — one of the epic's quietest mercies (Mbh, [Karṇa Parva](../mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md)).
- **Vengeance on Karṇa's sons** — On Day 18 Nakula slays three sons of Karṇa — **Citrasena, Satyasena, and Suṣeṇa** — in the morning fighting of the last day (Mbh, [Śalya Parva](../mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md)).

## Sahadeva: wisdom, stars, and cattle

- **"Wisest of men"** — Sahadeva is the family's reputed **quiet sage**: Yudhiṣṭhira leans on his judgment, and the epic's summary epithets make him foremost in **nīti (counsel) and learning** among the five (Mbh, throughout).
- **Knowledge of the heavens** — His learning includes **jyotiṣa** — the reading of stars and times; this trait, glancing in the epic, swells enormously in later tradition.
  - *Variant (folk/regional) [folk] [late text]:* widespread later tellings make Sahadeva an **all-knowing astrologer** who foresaw the entire war but was bound (by curse or vow) to answer only what he was asked — including the tale that **Duryodhana consulted him for the auspicious hour** to begin the war and that Sahadeva, honest to his science, gave it truly, while **Kṛṣṇa** contrived an early amāvāsyā to spoil the timing. None of this is in the Critical Edition; it is a beloved folk expansion of his epithet for wisdom.
- **Master of cattle** — Sahadeva's declared professional skill is the **management, breeding, and doctoring of kine** — the very trade he adopts in disguise at Virāṭa's court (Mbh, [Virāṭa Parva](../mahabharata/04-virata-parva.md)).
- **Southern digvijaya for the Rājasūya** — Sahadeva marches **south**: he defeats the Śūrasenas, **Dantavakra**, and **Bhīṣmaka of Vidarbha**, and at **Māhiṣmatī** fights king **Nīla**, whose city the fire-god **Agni** himself defends until Sahadeva propitiates him with prayer; pressing on he levies tribute from the Pāṇḍyas and Dravidas and, by envoys, from **Vibhīṣaṇa of Laṅkā** (Mbh, [Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md), Digvijaya).
- **The arghya to Kṛṣṇa** — At the Rājasūya it is **Sahadeva** who, at Bhīṣma's direction, presents the **first-honour offering (arghya) to Kṛṣṇa** — the act that detonates Śiśupāla's fatal outburst (Mbh, Sabhā Parva).

## The dice, the vows, the forest

- **Staked and lost** — In the dice-match Yudhiṣṭhira stakes and loses his brothers; the twins go with the household into the thirteen-year exile that follows the second game (Mbh, [Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md)).
- **Sahadeva's vow against Śakuni** — Amid the vows sworn after Draupadī's humiliation, **Sahadeva swears to slay [Śakuni](./shakuni.md)**, the architect of the dice-fraud, while **Nakula vows to slay the partisan sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra** (Mbh, Sabhā Parva, dice-hall vows; wording varies across recensions).
- **The lake of the Yakṣa** — In the exile's last stretch the four younger brothers, sent one by one for water, ignore a Yakṣa's challenge and fall dead at the lake — **Nakula first**; when Yudhiṣṭhira answers the [Yakṣa's questions](../yaksha-prashna.md) and is offered one brother back, he chooses **Nakula**, so that **Mādrī's line, like Kuntī's, will keep a living son** — and the Yakṣa (Dharma himself) revives all four (Mbh, [Vana Parva](../mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md), Āraṇeya; see [Yakṣa Praśna](../yaksha-prashna.md)).

## The Virāṭa year in disguise

- **Nakula the horse-keeper** — For the incognito thirteenth year at [Virāṭa's](./virata-and-his-children.md) court, Nakula presents himself as **Granthika** (also **Dāmagranthi**), a master of horses lately in Yudhiṣṭhira's service, and is made keeper of the **royal stables** (Mbh, [Virāṭa Parva](../mahabharata/04-virata-parva.md)).
- **Sahadeva the cowherd** — Sahadeva takes service as **Tantipāla** (in the Ganguli text also styled **Ariṣṭanemi**), superintendent of Virāṭa's **cattle-herds**, professing skill in counting, breeding, and curing kine (Mbh, Virāṭa Parva).
- **The cattle-raid battle** — When Suśarman's Trigartas seize Virāṭa's herds at the year's end, the twins fight in the rescue under their disguises, in the battle that precedes Arjuna's solo rout of the Kuru army (Mbh, Virāṭa Parva, Go-haraṇa).

## Kurukṣetra and the vow fulfilled

- **Eighteen days in the line** — Both twins fight through the whole war as commanders under Dhṛṣṭadyumna, repeatedly wounded (notably by their uncle Śalya on Day 18) but never broken (Mbh, war books; see [Śalya Parva](../mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md)).
- **Sahadeva slays Ulūka and Śakuni** — On the afternoon of **Day 18**, Sahadeva first kills Śakuni's son **Ulūka**, then, reminding the gambler of the dice, **beheads Śakuni with a broad-headed arrow** — the dice-hall vow discharged in the parva of his uncle's death (Mbh, [Śalya Parva](../mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md)).
- **After the victory** — In Yudhiṣṭhira's post-coronation appointments the twins take palace charges — Nakula over the army's musters and provisioning, Sahadeva in constant personal attendance on the king as his protector (Mbh, [Śānti Parva](../mahabharata/12-shanti-parva.md) 41; assignments vary slightly across recensions).

## The last walk and the falls (into the Kali dawn)

- **The mahāprasthāna** — After Kṛṣṇa's departure and the sea's swallowing of Dvārakā — the scriptural hinge of **Kali Yuga, 3102 BCE** — the five brothers and Draupadī renounce the kingdom to Parīkṣit and walk north toward the high snows (Mbh, [Mahāprasthānika Parva](../mahabharata/17-mahaprasthanika-parva.md)).
- **Sahadeva's fall** — Sahadeva falls **second**, after Draupadī; asked why so righteous a man fell, Yudhiṣṭhira answers: **"he thought none his equal in wisdom"** — pride in the very trait that defined him (Mbh, Mahāprasthānika Parva 2).
- **Nakula's fall** — Nakula falls **third**; Yudhiṣṭhira names his flaw likewise: **he believed no one equalled him in beauty** (Mbh, Mahāprasthānika Parva 2).
- **Restored among the Aśvins** — In the epic's last book Yudhiṣṭhira, touring heaven, beholds the twins shining **in the station of the Aśvins**, their divine fathers — each Pāṇḍava reabsorbed into the god who begot him (Mbh, [Svargārohaṇa Parva](../mahabharata/18-svargarohana-parva.md)).

## Themes and the two clocks

- **The perfected subordinate** — The twins are the epic's study of excellence **without command**: supreme in their crafts, loyal without rivalry, each given one decisive act (the arghya, the vow-kill) rather than an arc. Their falls on the mountain sharpen the point — even self-effacing men harbour one secret superiority.
- **Symmetry with their fathers** — Beauty and healing craft (Nakula) and knowledge (Sahadeva) mirror the Aśvins, the beautiful twin physicians of the dawn; the Svargārohaṇa closes the loop by seating them in the Aśvins' own place (Mbh; see [the Thirty-three Devas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/the-thirty-three-devas.md)).
- **Two clocks** — Scripturally the twins' lives close the **Dvāpara Yuga** (war traditionally c. 3138 BCE) and open Kali (3102 BCE) in the [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md). Academically the Mahābhārata is a layered text composed roughly **c. 400 BCE – 400 CE**, and the "astrologer Sahadeva" and the Nakula horse-treatise are later accretions on the epic figures [scholarly].
