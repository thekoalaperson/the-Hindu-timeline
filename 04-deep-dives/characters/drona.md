# Droṇa — the Brahmin Weapons-Master of the Kuru Line

> Where this sits: the **Dvāpara-era life** of the preceptor who trained both warring camps and died by deceit on the 15th day of the Kurukṣetra War. Droṇa belongs to the [epic-character deep-dives](README.md) within the [04-deep-dives](../README.md) layer, and to the [28th Mahāyuga, Dvāpara Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md), at the junction with the [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).
> Up: [Characters index](README.md) · Parent epic: [Mahābhārata](../mahabharata/) · His death-book: [07 — Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md) · master [index](../../README.md)

**Droṇa** (Sanskrit *Droṇa*, "wooden vessel / trough" — for the vessel in which he was conceived; hence **Droṇācārya**, "teacher Droṇa") is the brahmin master of arms who, against the grain of his caste, takes up the warrior's craft and becomes preceptor of both the [Pāṇḍavas](../mahabharata/01-adi-parva.md) and the Kauravas. Son of the sage **Bharadvāja**, husband of **Kṛpī**, father of **[Aśvatthāman](ashvatthama.md)**, he is the figure who binds the epic's generations of archers together — and whose private feud with **[Drupada](drupada.md)** plants the seed (literally, in the sacrifice that yields **[Dhṛṣṭadyumna](dhrishtadyumna.md)** and **Draupadī**) of his own destruction. His arc is the epic's deepest meditation on the brahmin-who-bears-arms, on partiality (his love for **[Arjuna](arjuna.md)**, his cruelty to **[Ekalavya](../ekalavya.md)**), and on how even the most righteous are broken by the war's slide into *adharma*.

> **How to read the citations.** **CE** = the BORI Critical Edition, cited as book.chapter.verse from the GRETIL e-text. A reference marked **`*nnnn`** (a starred passage) or **App. I No. n** is found only in the CE's critical apparatus: some manuscripts carry it, but the editors left it out of the constituted text. **G** = Ganguli's English translation (1883–96), cited by its own section numbers, which run ahead of the CE chapters. Ganguli translates the vulgate, so it often prints passages that the CE relegates to the apparatus. The "~" chapter numbers in some headings below are older, approximate Ganguli-style references, kept because the website links to those headings; the bullets give the exact loci.

## I. Birth and lineage (before the Pāṇḍava generation; Mbh Ādi Parva)

- **Conception in a vessel** - The great sage **Bharadvāja**, "moving about at the place of the oblations" (*havirdhāne caran*), sees the apsarā **Ghṛtācī**, who has just bathed. A wind sweeps her garment away, his seed falls, and he places it in a *droṇa*. From that vessel (*kalaśa*) Droṇa is born, and he learns the Vedas and their auxiliaries (CE 1.121.3–5; G CXXXI). Vaiśaṃpāyana's summary of the dynasty in the Ādi Parva gives the same story in brief (CE 1.57.89). Droṇa is thus *ayonija* ("not womb-born"), like his future in-laws Kṛpa and Kṛpī.
  - *Variant (CE `*1331`; G CXXXI, CLXVIII):* the vulgate places Bharadvāja at **Gaṅgādvāra**, where the Gaṅgā enters the plains, going down to the river to bathe. In this version Ghṛtācī is the one already at the river. The CE leaves this setting in the apparatus.
- **A portion of Bṛhaspati** - In the Ādi Parva's list of divine partial incarnations, Droṇa, "Bharadvāja's son, not womb-born", springs from a portion (*aṃśa*) of **Bṛhaspati**, preceptor of the gods. His son Aśvatthāman is born from Mahādeva and Antaka (Death), and from Desire and Anger, all fused into one (CE 1.61.63–67).
- **Bharadvāja's line** - Droṇa belongs to the Āṅgirasa brahmin line through Bharadvāja. He names his birth "in the family of Aṅgiras" to Paraśurāma (CE `*1341`; G CXXXI), and the narrator calls him "best of the Āṅgirasas" (*aṅgirasāṃ varaḥ*, CE 1.123.49, 68). The lineage links him to the Vedic ṛṣi tradition and sharpens the irony of his life of weapons.
- **Boyhood with Drupada** - Bharadvāja's friend King **Pṛṣata** of Pāñcāla has a son, **Drupada**, who comes daily to the āśrama and plays and studies with Droṇa (CE 1.121.8–9). In Droṇa's own later account the two study together under **Agniveśya**. There Drupada promises that when his father installs him as king, "the kingdom shall be yours to enjoy (*tvadbhojyaṃ*) … my pleasures, wealth and comforts will be at your disposal" (CE 1.122.26–30; G CXXXIII). The CE wording promises no *half* of the kingdom. "Half" first appears when Droṇa later imposes it as victor (CE 1.128.10).
- **The Āgneya weapon and the Agniveśya line** - **Bharadvāja** gives the **Āgneya** weapon to the sage **Agniveśya**, who was born of Agni. Agniveśya in turn passes it to Droṇa, his own teacher's son (CE 1.121.6–7; G CXXXI). Droṇa later tells Bhīṣma that he lived for many years with Agniveśya as a celibate student with matted hair, to learn the science of the bow (CE 1.122.24–25).
  - *Variant (CE App. I No. 80, lines 17–18, after 1.128.18):* in this passage Droṇa tells Arjuna that his guru Agniveśya was himself a pupil of **[Agastya](../agastya.md)** in archery, and that the Brahmaśiras he hands on came to him with his guru's warning never to loose it on men. The constituted CE does not have the Agastya link.

## II. The weapons of Paraśurāma (Mbh Ādi Parva)

- **The race for Paraśurāma's gifts** - After his father has gone to heaven and his son is born (see §III), Droṇa hears that **[Paraśurāma](../parashurama.md)** (Rāma Jāmadagnya) is giving all his wealth away to brahmins. He reaches Rāma as Rāma is setting out for the forest (*vanaṃ prasthitam*) and introduces himself as one who has come for wealth (CE 1.121.15–17; G CXXXI).
  - *Variant (CE `*1341`; G CXXXI):* the vulgate sends Droṇa with his disciples to the **Mahendra** mountain to find Rāma. The CE leaves this in the apparatus.
- **Choosing the astras over gold** - Rāma replies that his gold and goods have already gone to brahmins, and the whole earth to **Kaśyapa**. Only his body and his weapons remain, and Droṇa may choose (CE 1.121.18–20). Droṇa asks for **all the weapons "with their withdrawal (*saṃhāra*), their application and their secrets"**. Rāma gives him those, and the entire *dhanurveda* with its secret vows (CE 1.121.21–22).
  - *Note:* the epic makes Paraśurāma a teacher of three Kaurava champions. Droṇa receives the weapons openly here. [Bhīṣma](bhishma.md) is Rāma's pupil and later fights him (CE 1.61.70; the Ambā episode of the Udyoga Parva). [Karṇa](../karna.md) obtains weapons by posing as a brahmin and is cursed for it (Śānti Parva 12.2–3).

## III. Marriage, poverty, and the broken vow (Mbh Ādi Parva)

- **Marriage to Kṛpī** - After Bharadvāja goes to heaven, Droṇa marries **Kṛpī**, daughter of **Śaradvat**. He does so at his ancestors' direction and from desire for a son (*pitṛniyuktātmā putralobhāt*, CE 1.121.11; G CXXXI). Kṛpī and her twin [Kṛpa](kripa.md) were born from Śaradvat Gautama's seed, which fell on a clump of reeds when Indra sent the apsarā **Jālapadī** to break his austerities. One of King Śantanu's soldiers found the twins and brought them to the king, who raised them "out of compassion" (*kṛpayā*) and so named them (CE 1.120.2–18; G CXXX). Kṛpa becomes the Kuru princes' first arms-teacher (CE 1.120.20–21). The match ties Droṇa to the Kuru court before he ever enters it.
- **Birth of Aśvatthāman** - The son neighs at birth like the horse **Uccaiḥśravas**. An invisible being in the sky declares that, since his "strength (*sthāma*) like a horse's (*aśva*)" sounded to every quarter, he shall be called **Aśvatthāman** (CE 1.121.12–14; G CXXXI). He will become Droṇa's beloved and lethal heir, one of the [Cirañjīvīs](../the-chiranjivis.md).
- **The milk and the piṣṭodaka** - *(CE App. I No. 75, after 1.122.31; G CXXXIII: not in the constituted text.)* The child Aśvatthāman cries on seeing rich men's sons drink cow's milk. Droṇa wanders from region to region in search of a righteous gift of a milch cow and finds none. Then playmates give the boy **rice-flour water** (*piṣṭodaka*). He drinks it, dances, and cries "I have drunk milk!", while onlookers jeer: "Fie on the penniless Droṇa, whose son dances after drinking flour-water for milk!" Stung, Droṇa resolves never to take hateful service for gain, and goes with wife and son to his old friend.
- **The journey to Drupada** - Now king, **Drupada** rebuffs him. Friendship, he says, wears out with time. It was "bound by need" (*arthanibandhanam*), and it can exist only between equals: "the poor man is no friend of the rich … friendship and marriage are between those equal in wealth and birth … the one who is not a king is no friend of a king" (CE 1.122.2–9, retold by Droṇa at 1.122.33–37; G CXXXII–CXXXIII).
- **The vow of revenge** - Seething, Droṇa reflects for a moment, fixes his purpose against the Pāñcāla, and goes to Hastināpura (CE 1.122.10–11). There he tells Bhīṣma that he has come "seeking pupils of merit" (CE 1.122.38). His private feud is now the hidden engine of much that follows. → see the [feud's harvest](#vi-the-drupada-feud-and-its-harvest) below.

## IV. Preceptor of the Kuru princes (Mbh Ādi Parva)

- **The retrieved ball** - The princes' play-ball (*vīṭā*) falls into a well, and they cannot get it out. Droṇa mocks their "kṣatriya strength" and recovers it with a chain of mantra-charged *iṣīkā* grass-blades, each piercing the one before. The princes report the feat to **Bhīṣma**, who recognises Droṇa, receives him, and hands him the Kaurava and Pāṇḍava boys as pupils (CE 1.122.12–40; G CXXXIII).
  - *Variant (CE `*1365`, `*1368`; G CXXXIII):* in the vulgate Droṇa also drops **his own ring** into the dry well and asks for a meal as his fee. When Yudhiṣṭhira offers lasting alms with Kṛpa's leave, Droṇa recovers the ring by piercing it with a single arrow. (The ring is Droṇa's own, not Yudhiṣṭhira's.)
- **The secret task** - Droṇa tells the boys privately that he has a purpose in his heart, which they must fulfil once trained. The Kauravas fall silent. Only **Arjuna** promises, and Droṇa embraces him and weeps for joy (CE 1.122.41–44; G CXXXIV).
- **Other pupils, Karṇa among them** - Princes of the Vṛṣṇis, the Andhakas and other lands come to learn from him. So does **Karṇa**, Rādhā's son, who rivals Arjuna and attaches himself to Duryodhana (CE 1.122.46–47).
- **The promise to Arjuna** - Droṇa secretly tells the cook never to serve Arjuna food in the dark. One night a wind blows out the lamp while Arjuna is eating. Arjuna notices that his hand still finds his mouth by habit, and he begins to practise archery at night. Hearing the twang of his bowstring, Droṇa embraces him and promises: "I shall strive so that no other archer in the world will be your equal" (CE 1.123.2–6; G CXXXIV). He then trains Arjuna in fighting from chariot, elephant and horse, on foot, and with mace, sword and spears (CE 1.123.7–8). This promise is what the Ekalavya episode turns on.
  - *Variant (CE App. I No. 76, after 1.122.47; G CXXXIV):* Droṇa gives the other boys narrow-mouthed pitchers to fill with water, but gives his son a wide-mouthed one, so that Aśvatthāman returns early for extra teaching. Arjuna fills his pitcher at once with the **Vāruṇa** weapon and keeps pace with him.
- **Master of all the cousins** - The text sums up their distinct excellences: **Duryodhana** and **Bhīma** in the mace, **Aśvatthāman** in the secret lore (*rahasya*) of weapons, **Nakula–Sahadeva** in swordplay, **Yudhiṣṭhira** as the best chariot-fighter (*rathaśreṣṭhaḥ*), and **Arjuna** foremost in everything, the one *atiratha* among them (CE 1.123.40–43; G CXXXIV).
- **The artificial-bird test** - Droṇa has an artificial **bhāsa** bird (a bird of prey; Ganguli has "vulture") set on a treetop. He asks each prince what he sees. Yudhiṣṭhira, the Kauravas and the others say they see the bird, the tree, Droṇa and their brothers, and Droṇa sends them away. **Arjuna** says he sees only the bird, then: "I see the **head** of the bird, not its body." Told to shoot, he cuts off the head with a razor-arrow, and Droṇa thinks Drupada as good as beaten (CE 1.123.45–67; G CXXXIV–CXXXV).
  - *Variant (popular retelling):* the familiar version in which Arjuna sees only the bird's **eye** is not in the CE or in Ganguli. In both, the target is the bird's head.
- **The crocodile and the Brahmaśiras** - At the Gaṅgā a crocodile seizes Droṇa by the shank. Though able to free himself, he calls on his pupils to save him. Arjuna alone acts, killing it under water with five arrows while the others stand bewildered. Droṇa then gives him the **Brahmaśiras** weapon, with its release and withdrawal. Arjuna must never use it against humans, "for it could burn up the world", but only against a superhuman foe (CE 1.123.68–78; G CXXXV).
  - *Variant (CE App. I No. 80, after 1.128.18):* here Droṇa speaks of the Brahmaśiras in open court, naming his guru Agniveśya as Agastya's pupil. As his fee he asks that Arjuna fight him in battle if Droṇa ever opposes him, and Arjuna agrees.
- **The graduation tournament** - Droṇa asks Dhṛtarāṣṭra to hold the public exhibition of arms at Hastināpura (CE 1.124.1–5; G CXXXVI). Arjuna shines there, and **Karṇa** appears to challenge him, the scene that launches their rivalry (CE 1.124–127; G CXXXVI–CXXXIX).

## V. The thumb of Ekalavya (Mbh Ādi Parva)

- **The rejected pupil** - **Ekalavya**, son of **Hiraṇyadhanus**, king of the **Niṣādas**, comes to Droṇa for instruction. Droṇa, "knowing dharma", does not accept him as a pupil in archery, "thinking, *he is a Niṣāda*", and out of regard for his Kuru pupils (*teṣām evānvavekṣayā*) (CE 1.123.10–11; G CXXXIV). The text gives both reasons in the same verse. They are not alternative versions.
- **Self-taught before the clay image** - Ekalavya touches Droṇa's feet with his head, goes into the forest, makes a **clay image of Droṇa**, treats it as his teacher, and trains with great devotion until he becomes extraordinarily swift (CE 1.123.12–14). On a hunt, the princes' dog barks at him, and he shoots **seven arrows into its mouth** at once, aiming by sound. The dog runs back "with its mouth full of arrows" (CE 1.123.17–21). The CE does not say the dog was unhurt.
- **The guru-dakṣiṇā of the thumb** - Arjuna reminds Droṇa privately of his promise that no pupil would surpass him (CE 1.123.26–28). Droṇa goes to Ekalavya with Arjuna and says: "If you are my pupil, pay the fee." He asks for **the thumb of the right hand** (*aṅguṣṭho dakṣiṇaḥ*), which the text itself calls a "cruel" (*dāruṇa*) demand. Ekalavya cuts it off cheerfully and gives it. Afterwards he draws the bow with his fingers but is no longer as swift. "Arjuna was glad, his fever gone; and Droṇa was made true to his word" (CE 1.123.29–39; G CXXXIV). → full treatment in the dedicated file: [Ekalavya](../ekalavya.md).
  - *Reading [scholarly]:* the episode is the epic's starkest indictment of Droṇa. It has been read as protection of the kṣatriya monopoly on arms, as a teacher's jealous partiality, or, in apologetic tellings, as cosmic ordering of svadharma. Beyond calling him "knowing dharma" as he turns Ekalavya away, the narrator offers no praise of Droṇa here; the closing line says only that he was true to his word to Arjuna.

## VI. The Drupada feud and its harvest (Mbh Ādi Parva)

- **Drupada as guru-dakṣiṇā** - When training is complete, Droṇa asks his pupils for their fee: capture **Drupada** in battle and bring him to him (CE 1.128.1–2; G CXL). In the constituted CE the pupils go together, sack Drupada's city and bring him in with his ministers (CE 1.128.3–5). A later verse credits the conquest to **Pārtha** (Arjuna) (CE 1.128.18).
  - *Variant (CE App. I No. 78; G CXL):* in the vulgate's long battle-narrative, Duryodhana, Karṇa and the Kauravas attack first and are routed by the Pāñcālas. Arjuna, with Bhīma in the van and the twins guarding his wheels, then defeats Satyajit and seizes Drupada.
- **Half a kingdom, repaid** - Droṇa reminds the captive of the "friendship" a non-king cannot have. He reassures him that "we brahmins are forgiving", releases him, and restores **half the kingdom**. Drupada keeps the land **south of the Bhāgīrathī (Gaṅgā)**: Mākandī, Kāmpilya, and the southern Pāñcālas as far as the Carmaṇvatī river. Droṇa takes the north, with **Ahicchatrā** (CE 1.128.6–18; G CXL). The CE does not say that Droṇa installed Aśvatthāman there.
- **The sacrifice of vengeance** - Unable to beat Droṇa by kṣatriya might, Drupada roams the brahmin settlements by the Gaṅgā looking for a son who will kill him (CE 1.155.1–5). The younger of two Kāśyapa brahmins, **Upayāja**, refuses him for a year and then points to his elder brother **Yāja**. Yāja agrees, for an *arbuda* (a vast number) of cows, to perform the rite "for Droṇa's destruction" (CE 1.155.7–30). The CE calls it a Vedic fire-rite (*karma vaitānam*, *mahāmakha*) and does not use the name *putrakāmeṣṭi*. From the fire rises an armoured, flame-coloured youth, and a voice from the sky declares him "born for the slaying of Droṇa": this is **Dhṛṣṭadyumna**. From the middle of the altar (*vedi*) rises, unasked, **Kṛṣṇā (Draupadī)** (CE 1.155.37–50; G CLXIX).
- **Teaching his own killer** - Droṇa takes Dhṛṣṭadyumna into his own house and teaches him weapons. He holds destiny to be inescapable, and acts "to protect his own fame" (*ātmakīrty anurakṣaṇāt*) (CE 1.155.51–52; G CLXIX). In the Sabhā Parva he states it himself: Drupada sacrificed in anger for a son to kill him, and Dhṛṣṭadyumna, "famed in the world as born for my death", has gone over to the Pāṇḍavas (CE 2.71.38–42).
  - *Reading [scholarly]:* his acceptance of his destined killer marks Droṇa's fatalism and his fidelity to the teacher's duty, a counterweight to the Ekalavya cruelty.

## VII. Before the war — counsel and loyalty (Mbh Sabhā–Udyoga Parvas)

- **At the dice-hall** - Droṇa sits in the assembly through the dice game and the dragging-in of Draupadī. Draupadī cries that "Droṇa and Bhīṣma have no spirit (*sattva*) left" (CE 2.60.34). Droṇa joins Bhīṣma and Vidura in calming Bhīma (CE 2.62.38). Before the second game he is among those who say "No dice! Let there be peace" (CE 2.66.25–26). Yet he does not stop either game.
- **Bound to the Kauravas** - When the Pāṇḍavas leave for exile, Duryodhana, Karṇa and Śakuni make Droṇa their "island" (*dvīpa*) of refuge and offer him the kingdom. Droṇa answers that he cannot abandon those who have taken refuge with him, though "the Pāṇḍavas are sons of gods and cannot be killed". He predicts ruin in the fourteenth year and urges Duryodhana to make peace while he can (CE 2.71.32–45). On the first morning of the war, as the lines are drawn up, he tells Yudhiṣṭhira: "**A man is the slave of wealth, but wealth is no one's slave** (*arthasya puruṣo dāso dāsas tv artho na kasya cit*); I am bound to the Kauravas by wealth" (CE 6.41.51; G Bhīṣma XLIII). Bhīṣma and Kṛpa say the same in the same chapter. The popular phrase "he had eaten their salt" paraphrases this line.
- **Warnings to Duryodhana** - In the Udyoga Parva Droṇa tells Duryodhana that he honours **Arjuna** "even more than my son Aśvatthāman", that Arjuna owes his supremacy to Droṇa's own grace, and that no one can defeat a Yudhiṣṭhira whose counsellor is Janārdana and whose brother is Dhanañjaya. He urges peace (CE 5.137.4–22; again 5.146). The counsel is ignored.
- **The Virāṭa cattle-raid** - When the Kurus raid Virāṭa's herds, Droṇa reads the omens and knows from the roar of the approaching chariot that it can be no one but Arjuna (CE 4.41.19–23). Arjuna resolves to strike his teacher only after Droṇa strikes first (CE 4.50.8). In their duel the sky-voices praise Droṇa for even standing against Arjuna, and Arjuna finally gives him room to withdraw (CE 4.53.1–68).
- **The blessing and the secret of his death** - Before the first day's fighting, Yudhiṣṭhira crosses the lines to ask his teacher's leave. Droṇa grants it ("victory is sure for you, whose counsellor is Hari … *where dharma is, there is Kṛṣṇa; where Kṛṣṇa is, there is victory*") and then reveals how he can be killed. No one can kill him while he fights. He could be killed only once he has laid down his arms and become insensible, as in *prāya*, and **he will lay down his arms on hearing something deeply grievous from a man whose word he trusts** (CE 6.41.46–61; G Bhīṣma XLIII). The ruse of the fifteenth day is built on this disclosure.

## VIII. Commander-in-chief — the Droṇa Parva (Mbh Droṇa Parva)

- **Assuming command (day 11)** - After **Bhīṣma** falls on day 10, it is **Karṇa** who proposes Droṇa as the next *senāpati*, as the teacher of all the warriors. Duryodhana installs him (CE 7.5.12–21). Droṇa commands for **five days (days 11–15)** (CE 7.172.94: *yuddhaṃ kṛtvā dinān pañca*). → the parva's full arc: [07 — Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md).
- **The vow to capture Yudhiṣṭhira** - Asked to name a boon, Duryodhana asks Droṇa to take Yudhiṣṭhira **alive** (*jīvagrāham*). His reasoning is that killing him would only bring Arjuna's vengeance, whereas a captured Yudhiṣṭhira could be beaten again at dice and sent back to the forest. Seeing the crooked motive, Droṇa grants the boon only "with a gap" (*sāntaram*): it can work only if Arjuna is drawn away from his brother (CE 7.11.6–29; G XII). Arjuna thwarts every attempt.
- **The Saṃśaptaka decoy** - The Trigarta **Saṃśaptakas** ("sworn-to-death" warriors) take an oath to fight Arjuna to the death and lure him to a far flank, so that Droṇa can strike at Yudhiṣṭhira (CE 7.16–17). The same manoeuvre opens the field for the Cakravyūha on day 13.

## IX. The Cakravyūha and the death of Abhimanyu (Mbh Droṇa Parva)

- **The wheel-array** - On day 13, with Arjuna drawn away, Droṇa forms the **Cakravyūha** (wheel array) (CE 7.33.12). Yudhiṣṭhira says that only four can break it: Abhimanyu, Arjuna, Kṛṣṇa or Pradyumna. "A fifth there is not" (CE 7.34.15).
- **Abhimanyu's half-knowledge** - **[Abhimanyu](abhimanyu.md)**, Arjuna's young son, says that his **father** taught him how to break into the array, but he could not come out of it in an emergency (CE 7.34.19). He smashes in and devastates the Kaurava ranks.
  - *Variant (CE `*0259`, at 7.34.18):* some manuscripts add that he learned the entry while still in the womb. The full womb story, in which the telling breaks off before the exit is explained, belongs to later retellings.
- **The sealing and the unfair kill** - **[Jayadratha](jayadratha.md)**, holding a boon from Śiva to hold back all the Pāṇḍavas except Arjuna, seals the breach behind him (CE 7.41.13–18; some manuscripts add "for one day", `*0303`). Inside, the boy is surrounded by six chariot-warriors: **Droṇa, Kṛpa, Karṇa, Aśvatthāman, Bṛhadbala and Kṛtavarman** (CE 7.45.19; 7.46.4). Abhimanyu himself kills **Bṛhadbala**, king of Kosala (CE 7.46.21–22). Stripped of chariot and bow, Abhimanyu fights on with sword and then with a wheel, and he and Duḥśāsana's son fell each other with maces. The son of Duḥśāsana rises first and kills Abhimanyu with a **mace-blow to the head** as he is getting up: "thus was one slain by many" (CE 7.47.33–7.48.13).
- **Droṇa's part in the killing** - Droṇa first praises the boy openly to Kṛpa: "I see no archer equal to him." Duryodhana retorts that the teacher is sparing his pupil's son out of affection (CE 7.38.9–20). Later, when Karṇa asks how the boy can be beaten, Droṇa explains that his armour cannot be pierced, since Droṇa himself taught Arjuna how to put it on. He advises cutting his bow, bowstring, reins and horses, and making him "chariotless and bowless". Droṇa himself then cuts Abhimanyu's sword off at the grip (CE 7.47.17–37). Both scenes are in the constituted text, not variants. Together they are the most damning act against his dharma after the Ekalavya thumb.
- **Arjuna's vow and Jayadratha's death** - On day 14 Arjuna vows to kill Jayadratha before sundown or burn himself. Droṇa builds a **cart-and-wheel array** (*cakraśakaṭa*), with a lotus array and a hidden needle array behind it, and stands at the cart's mouth himself (CE 7.63.21–28). Arjuna calls Droṇa "like a father to me". After a brief exchange of arrows, at Kṛṣṇa's urging he walks around him in reverence and passes on (CE 7.66.4–31). With mantras, Droṇa then binds on Duryodhana a golden armour that no arrow can pierce (CE 7.69.35–39). Near sunset Arjuna beheads Jayadratha and sends the head into the lap of his father, Vṛddhakṣatra (CE 7.121.15–39; G CXLV).
  - *Variant (CE `*1009`, `*1025`, App. I No. 16; G CXLV):* the "false sunset", in which Kṛṣṇa's yoga darkens the sky so that Jayadratha shows himself, is not in the constituted CE text. Ganguli prints it. See [07 — Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md) for the forms of this variant.
- **The night battle** - The fighting continues into the night of day 14. In it Karṇa spends Indra's *śakti* on **Ghaṭotkaca** (CE 7.154; G CLXXIX).

## X. Death by the Aśvatthāman ruse (Mbh Droṇa Parva ~190–193)

*(The exact loci are CE 7.161–165, which Ganguli prints as G CLXXXVI and CXC–CXCIII.)*

- **The dawn of day 15: Drupada's death** - At dawn Droṇa kills three of Drupada's grandsons, and then **Drupada** himself and **Virāṭa** with two broad-headed arrows (CE 7.161.29–35; G CLXXXVI). The boyhood friendship ends at Droṇa's own hand.
- **Why fair means fail** - With the Pāṇḍavas terrified, **Kṛṣṇa** tells **Arjuna** that Droṇa cannot be beaten in fair fight even by Indra: "let us use a stratagem (*yoga*) for victory, setting dharma aside". If Aśvatthāman were dead, Droṇa would not fight, so let someone tell him so. **Arjuna disliked this**; the others agreed, and Yudhiṣṭhira did so only with difficulty (CE 7.164.66–70; G CXCI). The lever matches what Droṇa himself had disclosed (CE 6.41.61).
- **The elephant Aśvatthāman** - **Bhīma** kills with his mace a great elephant of his own army named **Aśvatthāman**, which belonged to King **Indravarman** of Mālava. Then, "abashed", he shouts to Droṇa, "Aśvatthāman is slain!" (CE 7.164.71–73, 101–102). Droṇa, knowing his son's prowess, doubts the news (CE 7.164.74–75).
- **The Brahmāstra and the rebuke of the seers** - Droṇa turns the **Brahmāstra** on the Pāñcālas and slaughters thousands (CE 7.164.79–85). The ṛṣis then come, led by Agni: **Viśvāmitra, Jamadagni, Bharadvāja, Gautama, Vasiṣṭha, Kaśyapa, Atri**, with many others. They tell him: "You have fought by *adharma*; your time of death has come. Lay down your weapon. Such cruelty does not become a brahmin" (CE 7.164.86–92; G CXCI). In the CE this rebuke comes **before** Yudhiṣṭhira speaks.
- **Yudhiṣṭhira's half-truth** - Droṇa, who has trusted Yudhiṣṭhira's truthfulness since boyhood, asks him whether his son is alive or dead (CE 7.164.94–96). Kṛṣṇa urges Yudhiṣṭhira that "here a lie would be better than the truth; one who lies to save life is not stained" (CE 7.164.97–99), and Bhīma urges him too (CE 7.164.100–104). Sunk in fear of the untruth and set on victory, Yudhiṣṭhira says, "**indistinctly**, '*hataḥ kuñjara*' — 'the elephant is slain'" (*avyaktam abravīd … hataḥ kuñjara ity uta*, CE 7.164.106). Ganguli's text reads that he said "distinctly" that Aśvatthāman was dead and added the word "elephant" indistinctly (G CXCI). Yudhiṣṭhira's chariot had always moved **four fingers' breadth above the earth**. After these words its horses touched the ground (CE 7.164.107; G CXCI).
  - *Variant (later retellings):* in the familiar telling, **Kṛṣṇa has conches or drums blown** to drown the word "elephant", and Yudhiṣṭhira uses the formula *naro vā kuñjaro vā* ("man or elephant"). Neither the conches nor the formula appears in the CE text, among the starred or Appendix I passages printed with it at this point, or in Ganguli.
- **Laying down arms** - Grief-stricken and remembering the seers' words, Droṇa loses the will to live (CE 7.164.108–110). After a last bout with Dhṛṣṭadyumna (CE 7.164.111–7.165.26), Bhīma rebukes him: if trained brahmins did not fight, the kṣatriyas would not be destroyed; non-violence is the highest dharma, and a brahmin is its root; yet Droṇa has slain multitudes for the sake of one son, who now lies dead as Yudhiṣṭhira has said (CE 7.165.28–32). Droṇa casts down his bow and calls out, "Karṇa, Karṇa, great archer! Kṛpa! Duryodhana! Strive on in battle", and he calls for his son. He sets his weapons down in the chariot, grants **safety to all beings**, and sits in yoga (CE 7.165.33–35; G CXCII).
- **The ascent, and the beheading by Dhṛṣṭadyumna** - As **Dhṛṣṭadyumna** leaps from his chariot sword in hand, Droṇa, "become light", passes in yoga to heaven. It seemed as if there were two suns. Only five humans saw him go: Sañjaya (the narrator), Arjuna, Kṛpa, Kṛṣṇa and Yudhiṣṭhira (CE 7.165.36–45; G CXCII–CXCIII). Dhṛṣṭadyumna, cursed by all beings, then seizes the head of the body "from which life had gone" and severs it with his sword (CE 7.165.46–47). Arjuna had been shouting "Bring the teacher alive! Do not kill him!", and the soldiers cried "He must not be slain" (CE 7.165.50–52). Dhṛṣṭadyumna hurls the head before the Kaurava ranks, which flee (CE 7.165.54–55). Sañjaya adds that the dark-skinned Droṇa, white-haired to the ears and **eighty-five years old**, had fought on the field like a youth of sixteen (CE 7.165.49; G CXCIII).
- **Aftermath: the Nārāyaṇāstra** - It is **Aśvatthāman**, not Droṇa, who looses the **Nārāyaṇāstra**, after his father's death and in rage at it. Droṇa had received the weapon from Nārāyaṇa himself and passed it to his son (CE 7.166.43–51, 7.170.15). The weapon grows stronger against all who resist it, and Kṛṣṇa saves the Pāṇḍava army by having every warrior dismount and lay down his arms (CE 7.170.40–43). Aśvatthāman's next weapon, the Āgneya, fails as well. Vyāsa then tells him that Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna are Nārāyaṇa and Nara; he bows to Rudra and withdraws the army for the night (CE 7.172.91–92; G CXCV–CCI). A Pāṇḍava quarrel over the killing follows, with Arjuna reproaching Yudhiṣṭhira and Dhṛṣṭadyumna and Sātyaki nearly coming to blows (CE 7.167–169).
- **Who commands next** - **Karṇa** takes command next ([Karṇa Parva](../mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md)). Droṇa's death is the hinge after which the war's ethical fabric tears wholesale, leading to the night-massacre of the [Sauptika Parva](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md). In the Śalya Parva, Kṛṣṇa himself names Droṇa, with Bhīṣma, Karṇa and Bhūriśravas, among the four *atirathas* who "could not be slain by dharma" and so fell to his stratagems, and he adds Duryodhana (CE 9.60.55–61).

## XI. Afterlife of the figure — Aśvatthāman's vengeance and legacy

- **Aśvatthāman's grief-vow** - In the Sauptika Parva, Aśvatthāman's stated grievance is that "my father, who had laid down his arms, was felled by Dhṛṣṭadyumna" (CE 10.5.17, 32). In the night raid on the sleeping Pāṇḍava camp he seizes **Dhṛṣṭadyumna** by the hair, pins him down and kills him with kicks "like a sacrificial beast" (*paśumāram*). When Dhṛṣṭadyumna begs for death by a weapon, Aśvatthāman refuses: there are no good worlds for a teacher-killer (CE 10.8.12–24). Kṛṣṇa then curses him to wander the earth for **three thousand years** (*trīṇi varṣasahasrāṇi cariṣyasi mahīm imām*, CE 10.16.10). → [Sauptika Parva](../mahabharata/10-sauptika-parva.md); [Aśvatthāman](ashvatthama.md); [Cirañjīvīs](../the-chiranjivis.md).
- **Drona-tīrtha and place-traditions [folk]** - Local lore claims sites of his birth, hermitage or austerities, notably around **Dehradun** (popularly *Droṇa-nagarī*) and at several **Droṇa-tīrtha** ponds. These are post-epic regional attachments, not textual.
- **The Droṇācārya Award [modern]** - India's highest sports honour for coaches, instituted in 1985 and administered by the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports, is named after him (PIB, National Sports Awards note, 2024). It seals his cultural image as the archetypal *guru*, an image in tension with the epic's own ambivalence about the man.
- **Reading the man [scholarly]** - The Mahābhārata frames Droṇa as a study in contradiction. He is the supreme teacher, yet partial (Arjuna over Ekalavya, and in App. I his own son over the other boys). He is a brahmin whom Bhīma and the seers reproach for abandoning his own dharma, yet the war's deadliest astra-wielder. He is loyal to a patron he knows is in the wrong, "bound by wealth" (CE 6.41.51). And he is undone by a lie aimed at the one love that exceeded his duty, his son, and aimed exactly where he had told his enemies to aim (CE 6.41.61). His death by truth twisted into falsehood stands with the deaths of Bhīṣma, Karṇa, Bhūriśravas and Duryodhana among the stratagem-killings that Kṛṣṇa himself defends (CE 9.60.57–62).
