# Jayadratha

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Jayadratha** (Saindhava), king of Sindhu and husband of **Duḥśalā**, the lone Kaurava sister — the antagonist who attempts Draupadī's abduction in the [Vana Parva](../mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md) and who, by a boon from Śiva, seals the *cakravyūha* mouth so that [Abhimanyu](./abhimanyu.md) is killed, triggering Arjuna's sunset-vow and his own beheading in the [Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md). > Two clocks: as *itihāsa* this falls in the present 28th Mahāyuga of the 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara, at the Dvāpara–Kali junction (Kali begins 3102 BCE at the war's close); the BORI Critical Edition of the *Mahābhārata* is the scholarly textual baseline. [scholarly]

## Identity, names, and family
- **King of Sindhu (Saindhava)** — ruler of the Sindhu–Sauvīra country in the far north-west; his patronymic-epithet **Saindhava** ("the Sindhu man") is his most common name in the epic (*Mahābhārata*).
- **Son of Vṛddhakṣatra** — his father is the ascetic-king **Vṛddhakṣatra**, whose protective boon over his son's head later recoils fatally (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).
- **Other names / epithets** — **Saindhava**, **Sauvīra(-rāja)**, **Vārddhakṣatri** ("son of Vṛddhakṣatra"), **Sindhurāja** (*Mahābhārata*).
- **Husband of Duḥśalā** — married **Duḥśalā** (also spelled Duḥśīlā), the only daughter of Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Gāndhārī among the hundred Kauravas; thus brother-in-law to Duryodhana and the whole Kaurava host, which binds him to their cause (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). This marriage-tie is why the Pāṇḍavas spare him at Draupadī's abduction.

## The abduction of Draupadī (Vana Parva)
- **The attempt** — while the Pāṇḍavas are away hunting from their forest hermitage, Jayadratha — passing by on his way to a wedding, smitten on seeing Draupadī — first sends an envoy (Kotikāsya), then carries **Draupadī** off by force onto his chariot; the priest **Dhaumya** follows the chariot rebuking him (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva 263–267; the embedded* Draupadī-haraṇa *or* Jayadratha-vimokṣaṇa Parva). See [Vana Parva](../mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md).
- **Pursuit and capture** — warned, the Pāṇḍavas overtake him; his escort is scattered and Jayadratha abandons Draupadī and flees, but **Bhīma** and **Arjuna** run him down and take him captive (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva 268–271*).
- **Life spared for Duḥśalā's sake** — Bhīma would kill him, but **Yudhiṣṭhira** (and Draupadī herself), mindful that his death would widow their cousin **Duḥśalā** and grieve Gāndhārī, orders him spared (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva 271*).
- **The humiliation** — instead of death Bhīma shaves Jayadratha's head leaving **five tufts** of hair, and forces him to declare publicly that he is the Pāṇḍavas' **slave** before releasing him — a disgrace meant to be worse than death for a king (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva 271*).
  - *Variant (detail of the shaming):* retellings vary on the five tufts being a mark of slavery / a *dāsa* shaving; the core — head-shaving plus forced confession of bondage — is stable across recensions. [disputed]

## The boon from Śiva
- **Tapas for revenge** — burning with the humiliation, Jayadratha performs severe austerities and propitiates **Śiva** (Mahādeva) for the power to defeat all the Pāṇḍavas (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva 271–272*).
- **The limited boon** — Śiva grants that on **one day** of battle Jayadratha will be able to **check / hold back the four Pāṇḍavas** (Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma, Nakula, Sahadeva) — but **explicitly not Arjuna**, whom no boon can stop (*Mahābhārata, Vana Parva 272; recalled in Droṇa Parva*). The god refuses the larger wish of total victory; only this single-day, four-brother restraint is given. See [Śiva myth-cycle](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md).
- **Why it matters** — this exact boon is the mechanism that strands Abhimanyu inside the *cakravyūha*: it lets Jayadratha alone hold the formation's mouth against the four pursuing brothers while Arjuna is decoyed far away (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).

## The 13th day — sealing the cakravyūha (the death of Abhimanyu)
- **Drona's wheel-array** — on the 13th day Droṇa, now Kaurava commander, forms the army as a spiralling *cakravyūha* and lures **Arjuna** away with the Saṃśaptaka (oath-bound) warriors, since only Arjuna can break it cleanly (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva, Abhimanyu-vadha Parva*).
- **Abhimanyu breaks in** — the boy [Abhimanyu](./abhimanyu.md), who knows the entry but not the exit, shatters the formation's mouth and storms in, the four other Pāṇḍavas meaning to follow close behind (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).
- **Jayadratha seals the gap** — using Śiva's boon, **Jayadratha holds the entry-point shut**, turning back Bhīma, Yudhiṣṭhira, Nakula and Sahadeva and stranding Abhimanyu alone inside, where the ring of Kaurava champions kills him (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva 41*). This single act — not the killing-blow itself, which others deal — is what makes Jayadratha the prime target of Arjuna's wrath.

## Arjuna's vow and the false-sunset slaying (Drona Parva, Day 14)
- **The night-vow (Pratijñā)** — returning to find his son dead and learning Jayadratha barred the rescue, **Arjuna** swears to slay Jayadratha **before sunset the next day** or else enter the fire himself; the whole Kaurava strategy on Day 14 becomes keeping Jayadratha alive until dusk (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva, Pratijñā Parva 72–84*). See [Arjuna](./arjuna.md) and [Droṇa Parva](../mahabharata/07-drona-parva.md).
- **Drona's triple wall** — to run out the clock, Droṇa arrays three nested formations — a *Śakaṭa* (cart) vyūha, a *Padma* (lotus) behind it, and a *Sūcī* (needle) core — with Jayadratha guarded far at the rear by Droṇa, Karṇa, Aśvatthāman, Kṛpa, Bhūriśravas and Vṛṣasena (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva 86–87*).
- **Arjuna's all-day drive** — Arjuna with **Kṛṣṇa** as charioteer smashes through formation after formation, fighting and refusing to be detained, racing the descending sun (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).
- **The eclipse-ruse / false sunset** — with sunset near and Jayadratha still hidden, **Kṛṣṇa covers the sun** so it appears to set; the Kauravas relax and Jayadratha exposes himself to gloat, whereupon Kṛṣṇa says "There is your enemy — the sun returns," and Arjuna beheads him (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva, Jayadratha-vadha Parva 146*).
  - *Variant (mechanism of the "sunset"):* (a) Kṛṣṇa veils the sun with his **Sudarśana cakra**; (b) Kṛṣṇa's **yoga-māyā** casts an illusory darkness; (c) a natural cloud obscures the sun. Ganguli's translation favors the divine illusion; all versions agree the apparent sunset was a ruse. [disputed]
- **The falling-head boon recoils** — Jayadratha's father **Vṛddhakṣatra** had decreed that whoever caused his son's head to fall **to the ground** would have his own head burst into a hundred pieces. On Kṛṣṇa's instruction Arjuna shoots the severed head *through the air* into the lap of the meditating Vṛddhakṣatra, who starts, lets it roll to the earth, and his **own** head explodes — the protective boon killing its own maker (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva 146*).
- **Vow fulfilled** — Jayadratha dies before true sunset, Arjuna is spared the fire, and the most flagrant Kaurava breach of war-code (the gang-killing of Abhimanyu) is answered (*Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva*).

## Character and legacy
- **A coward made consequential** — the epic frames Jayadratha as a vain, lustful and rather cowardly king (the failed abduction, the slave-shaming) whom a single Śiva-boon raises, for one day, into the hinge on which the war's gravest tragedy turns (*Mahābhārata*). [scholarly]
- **Widowing of Duḥśalā** — his death leaves **Duḥśalā** a widow; in some later traditions she and her descendants resurface during the post-war [Aśvamedhika] campaigns, where Arjuna's horse passes through Sindhu (*Mahābhārata, Aśvamedhika Parva*). [late text]
- **Past-life framing** — some Purāṇic/commentarial readings treat Jayadratha, like other key antagonists, within the larger scheme of the gods' burden-lifting descent, his role pre-ordained as part of the earth's unburdening. [late text] [disputed]
