# Mandodarī — Queen of Laṅkā, Voice of Dharma, Pañcakanyā

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Mandodarī** — daughter of the asura-architect [Maya Dānava](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/maya-danava.md), chief queen of [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md), mother of [Indrajit/Meghanāda](./indrajit-meghnad.md), and one of the five **Pañcakanyā**. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). Her story runs through the [Sundara](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md), [Yuddha](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md) and [Uttara](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md) Kāṇḍas of the [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* her life falls in the **Tretā Yuga** of the present 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara; the scholarly baseline is the Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa* (core c. 5th c. BCE–3rd c. CE, with the Uttara Kāṇḍa a later stratum), over which Jain, Śākta and vernacular retellings layer from the mid-1st millennium CE onward. [scholarly]

## Name and identity

- **Mandodarī (मन्दोदरी)** — conventionally glossed "she of the slender/soft belly" (*manda* + *udara*); chief queen (*paṭṭamahiṣī*) of Rāvaṇa of Laṅkā and the most consistently virtuous figure of his court — tradition remembers her as beautiful, pious, and loyal to a husband whose central crime she condemned (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*).
- **A dānava princess among rākṣasas** — by birth Mandodarī is not a rākṣasī at all but the daughter of a **Dānava** (Maya) and an **apsarā** (Hemā); her marriage grafts the asura-architect's line onto the rākṣasa dynasty of Laṅkā (see the [rākṣasa world](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md)) (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa* tradition).
- **One of the Pañcakanyā** — she closes the traditional verse of the **five ideal women** — *Ahalyā Draupadī Kuntī Tārā Mandodarī tathā…* — whose daily remembrance is said to destroy great sin; see [Ahalyā](./ahalya.md) for the full verse and its logic (*later subhāṣita / smṛti tradition*). [late text]

## Birth and parentage (Uttara Kāṇḍa)

- **Daughter of Maya and Hemā** — the Uttara Kāṇḍa's back-story (told to Rāma after the war) relates that the divine architect of the asuras, [Maya Dānava](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/maya-danava.md), lived with the apsarā **Hemā**, granted to him by the gods; Mandodarī is their daughter, with two brothers, **Māyāvī** and **Dundubhi** (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa 12* tradition).
- **Hemā recalled; Maya left with his daughter** — Hemā is withdrawn back to the celestial sphere, leaving Maya wandering with the unmarried Mandodarī — the situation in which Rāvaṇa finds them (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* tradition).
  - *Variant (folk/regional — the frog-maiden Madhurā):* in regional tellings Mandodarī's origin involves a frog (*mandūka*) — a frog-maiden or a frog dropped into a pot of milk/venom whom sages transform into a girl, raised by Maya and Hemā; the tale is offered as a folk etymology of her name. [folk] [regional] (*folk Mandodarī legends; cf. the survey in the [variant-Rāmāyaṇas file](../ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md)*)
- **Marriage to Rāvaṇa; the gift of the śakti** — meeting Maya in the forest, Rāvaṇa asks for Mandodarī; Maya, learning the suitor is Viśravas's son, gives her in marriage and bestows on his son-in-law an **unfailing śakti (javelin)** won by tapas — the very weapon Rāvaṇa later hurls at Lakṣmaṇa in the war (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa 12* tradition; the śakti strikes Lakṣmaṇa in the *Yuddha Kāṇḍa*).
- **Mother of Meghanāda** — Mandodarī bears Rāvaṇa's eldest son **Meghanāda**, so named because his birth-cry boomed like a thundercloud; by his conquest of Indra he becomes [Indrajit](./indrajit-meghnad.md), Laṅkā's crown prince (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa*).
  - **Akṣayakumāra** — the young son of Rāvaṇa slain by Hanumān in the Aśoka-grove battle is commonly reckoned Mandodarī's son as well, though Vālmīki does not name his mother; other sons (e.g. Atikāya, by Dhānyamālinī) are borne by co-wives (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* for Akṣa's death; motherhood per later tradition). [disputed]

## In the Sundara Kāṇḍa: mistaken for Sītā

- **Hanumān's error in the harem** — searching Rāvaṇa's sleeping palace by night, Hanumān comes upon a queen of supreme beauty asleep in splendour and momentarily rejoices, thinking he has found Sītā; he then reasons that Sītā, separated from Rāma, could never sleep adorned and at ease, rebukes his own lapse, and moves on — the woman is Mandodarī (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍa 9–11*; see the [Sundara Kāṇḍa file](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md)).
- **The narrative point** — the scene measures Mandodarī against [Sītā](./sita.md): so radiant that even Hanumān confuses the two, yet defined by the difference — one a queen amid luxury, the other a captive in grief (*Sundara Kāṇḍa 10*). [scholarly]

## The voice of dharma in Laṅkā

- **Counsel against the abduction's course** — tradition consistently ranks Mandodarī with [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md), Kumbhakarṇa and the elder Mālyavān among the voices urging Rāvaṇa to return Sītā and make peace with Rāma; her pleas, like theirs, are refused (*Rāmāyaṇa tradition across recensions*).
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas):* Tulsīdās develops this fully — Mandodarī repeatedly begs Rāvaṇa to restore Sītā, first after Hanumān burns Laṅkā and again as the army lands, declaring that Rāma is no mere man but the Supreme Lord, and that opposing him means ruin; Rāvaṇa laughs off her "womanly fear" (*Rāmcaritmānas, Sundara & Laṅkā Kāṇḍa*). [late text]
  - *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa):* the devotional recension likewise gives Mandodarī speeches recognising Rāma as Viṣṇu incarnate and urging surrender — the queen of Laṅkā as an unheeded *bhakta* (*Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa*; see [variant Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md)). [late text]
- **Restraining Rāvaṇa's worst impulse** — in the Yuddha Kāṇḍa, when Rāvaṇa in fury resolves to kill Sītā after Indrajit's death, it is a counsellor (Supārśva) who stays his hand in Vālmīki; later retellings transfer or add Mandodarī's intercession for the captive's life, consonant with her role as Laṅkā's conscience (*Vālmīki Yuddha Kāṇḍa 92*; later retellings). [disputed]
- **The interrupted rite** — in several vernacular tellings, when Rāvaṇa undertakes a secret sacrifice to become invincible, Rāma's monkeys break it by dragging Mandodarī by the hair before the king's eyes, goading him to abandon the rite to defend her honour — an episode of the Bengali Kṛttivāsī and other regional Rāmāyaṇas, absent from Vālmīki's critical text (*Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇa and regional tellings*). [regional] [folk]

## The lament of the Yuddha Kāṇḍa

- **Widowed on the field** — after Rāma slays Rāvaṇa, Mandodarī comes out with the women of the harem and falls lamenting over the body of her husband — the emotional summit of her role in Vālmīki (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa 110–111*; see the [Yuddha Kāṇḍa file](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md)).
- **She names the true cause** — her lament is a moral audit of Rāvaṇa's fall: the conqueror of gods and dānavas has been destroyed by a man on foot — therefore, she reasons, Rāma must be no mere man but the eternal Viṣṇu/the Supreme Person, and it was **lust for Sītā** — a woman she judges nobler than herself in austerity, though not her superior in birth or beauty — that doomed him; had he restored Sītā, the house would stand (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa 111*).
- **The wife's reproach and grief** — she reproaches him for the massacre of his kin, mourns Kumbhakarṇa and Indrajit already fallen, and grieves that a hero unconquerable by devas died for a woman's sake; the lament earned Mandodarī tradition's respect as a truth-teller even in bereavement (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa 111*).
- **The funeral** — at Rāma's direction ("enmity ends at death"), [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md) performs Rāvaṇa's full funeral rites; Mandodarī and the women make the water-offering, after which Vibhīṣaṇa is crowned king of Laṅkā (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa 111–112*).

## Remarriage to Vibhīṣaṇa

- **Queen once more** — a widespread later tradition holds that after Rāvaṇa's cremation Mandodarī, **at Rāma's own counsel**, married [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md) and remained queen of Laṅkā — a union usually read politically (legitimating the new king and securing the kingdom) and defended in tradition by the precedent of Tārā's marriage to Sugrīva. The episode is **not in the Vālmīki critical edition**; it is carried by later Purāṇic/regional retellings. [late text] [disputed]
- **The Pañcakanyā logic** — commentators link this second marriage (like Tārā's) to her place in the Pañcakanyā verse: the five are honoured *through*, not despite, lives that broke the simple norm of one-husband chastity — virtue measured by inner fidelity to dharma. [scholarly]

## The Sītā-as-Mandodarī's-daughter variant

- **Jain tellings (Guṇabhadra / Saṅghadāsa)** — in Guṇabhadra's *Uttarapurāṇa* (9th c. CE) and Saṅghadāsa's *Vasudevahiṇḍī*, **Sītā is born to Rāvaṇa and Mandodarī**; astrologers foretell the child will destroy the family, so the infant is exposed/buried and recovered by Janaka — making Rāvaṇa's later abduction an unwitting near-incest. See the full catalog in [Jain & Buddhist Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/recensions/jain-and-buddhist-ramayanas.md). [regional] [late text]
  - *Note:* the older Jain line (Vimalasūri's *Paumacariya*) keeps Sītā as Janaka's daughter — the two Jain strands disagree and are kept distinct. [scholarly]
- **Śākta telling (Adbhuta Rāmāyaṇa)** — the *Adbhuta Rāmāyaṇa* likewise makes Sītā (there the supreme Śakti) born of Mandodarī rather than found in the furrow (see [variant Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md)). [late text]
- **Folk mechanism — the jar of blood** — folk retellings supply the means: Mandodarī drinks a sealed pot of sages' blood (collected by Rāvaṇa as poison), conceives, and the child is sealed in a jar and buried in Mithilā's soil for Janaka's plough to find; cf. the variants cataloged under [Sītā](./sita.md). [folk]

## Afterlife in tradition

- **The ideal wife of a wicked husband** — tradition holds Mandodarī up as the type of the woman who keeps her own dharma inside an adharmic house: chaste, truthful, unheeded, and finally vindicated by events — a foil both to Rāvaṇa and to the fickleness his court embodies (*devotional and didactic retellings*). [late text]
- **Regional memory — Mandore** — the old Marwar capital **Mandore** (near Jodhpur, Rajasthan) is claimed by local tradition as Mandodarī's natal home and the site of her wedding to Rāvaṇa; some Rajasthani and Madhya-Bhārat communities accordingly treat Rāvaṇa as a son-in-law and mark Dashahra with funeral-style observances rather than celebration. [folk] [regional]
- **Stage and vernacular life** — she is a fixture of Rāmlīlā and of the vernacular Rāmāyaṇas (Kamban's Tamil *Irāmāvatāram*, Kṛttivāsī Bengali, Tulsī's Hindi), usually with her counsel-scenes and lament expanded; in Kamban her lament is a celebrated set-piece of Tamil poetry. [regional]
