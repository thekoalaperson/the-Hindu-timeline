# Mandodarī — Queen of Laṅkā, Voice of Dharma, Pañcakanyā

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Mandodarī** — daughter of the asura-architect [Maya Dānava](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/maya-danava.md), chief queen of [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md), mother of [Indrajit/Meghanāda](./indrajit-meghnad.md), and one of the five **Pañcakanyā**. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). Her story runs through the [Sundara](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md), [Yuddha](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md) and [Uttara](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md) Kāṇḍas of the [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* her life falls in the **Tretā Yuga** of the present 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara; the scholarly baseline is the Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa* (core c. 5th c. BCE–3rd c. CE, with the Uttara Kāṇḍa a later stratum), over which Jain, Śākta and vernacular retellings layer from the mid-1st millennium CE onward. [scholarly]
> Citation key: **CE** = Baroda Critical Edition (GRETIL text); **VR** = the vulgate/Southern text and numbering of valmikiramayan.net. Where the two differ in content, the difference is stated.

## Name and identity

- **Mandodarī (मन्दोदरी)** — conventionally glossed "she of the slender/soft belly" (*manda* + *udara*). Vālmīki's own titles for her are *jyeṣṭhā patnī priyā*, Rāvaṇa's "eldest and beloved wife" (CE 6.99.1; VR 6.111.1), and *antaḥpureśvarī*, "mistress of the inner apartments" (CE 5.8.48; VR 5.10.51–52). Tradition remembers her as beautiful, pious, and loyal to a husband whose central crime she condemned.
- **Daughter of Maya, by an apsarā** — by birth Mandodarī is not a rākṣasī but the daughter of Maya and the **apsarā Hemā** (Uttara 7.12.10, 16). Her marriage grafts the asura-architect's line onto the rākṣasa dynasty of Laṅkā (see the [rākṣasa world](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md)).
  - *Textual seam (Vālmīki):* the epic is not consistent about her father's class. The Uttara calls Maya "son of Diti" and *daityendra* (CE 7.12.3, 16), whereas the Yuddha calls him *dānavendra* (CE 6.7.6; VR 6.7.7), and in the vulgate lament she calls him *dānavarāja* (VR 6.111.41). In her lament she also calls herself *yātudhānasya dauhitrī*, "daughter's daughter of a mighty yātudhāna (rākṣasa) who never fled in battle" (CE 6.99.26; VR 6.111.82). That sits oddly beside an apsarā mother, and the text does not explain it. The valmikiramayan.net translation names this grandfather as Sumālin; that name is the translators' gloss and is not in the verse. [disputed]
- **One of the Pañcakanyā** — she closes the traditional verse of the **five ideal women**, *Ahalyā Draupadī Kuntī Tārā Mandodarī tathā…*, whose daily remembrance is said to destroy great sin. Some recitations put Sītā in Kuntī's place. See [Ahalyā](./ahalya.md) for the full verse and its logic (*later subhāṣita / smṛti tradition*). [late text]
- **Not to be confused with** two namesakes in Vettam Mani's *Puranic Encyclopaedia* (s.v. "Mandodarī II"; s.v. "Ghaṇṭākarṇa"). One is a princess of Siṃhala in a Devī tale told by Mahiṣāsura. The other is a Mandodarī who is the wife of Dāruka and is also called Maya's daughter; in the *Dārukavadha* story Mani cites she becomes the presiding deity of smallpox. [regional]

## Birth and parentage (Uttara Kāṇḍa)

- **Daughter of Maya and Hemā** — Agastya tells this back-story to Rāma after the war. Rāvaṇa, out hunting, meets Maya alone in the forest with a girl. Maya explains himself. The gods had given him the apsarā **Hemā**, as Paulomī was given to Indra, and he built the city of **Hemapura** for her. She then left "on the gods' business", and it is now the fourteenth year since. Grieving, he has taken his daughter, "who grew in her womb" (*tasyāḥ kukṣau vivardhitā*), into the forest to look for a husband for her. Hemā also bore him two sons, **Māyāvī** and **Dundubhi** (CE 7.12.3–12). This is the same pair whose feud with Vālī opens the Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa. See [Sugrīva & Vālī](./sugriva-and-vali.md). Rāvaṇa's brothers-in-law are thus Vālī's two great victims, a link the epic itself never remarks on.
  - *Textual seam:* the Kiṣkindhā itself wavers. Sugrīva's introduction calls Māyāvī Dundubhi's elder brother (*pūrvajo dundubheḥ*, CE 4.9.4), in line with Uttara 7.12.12. But Vālī's words, as Sugrīva reports them, call him "Dundubhi's son" (*dundubheḥ sutam*, CE 4.10.17). [disputed]
- **The offer** — when he learns that the stranger is Daśagrīva, son of the brahmarṣi Viśravas, Maya is delighted. He says, "this is my daughter, borne by the apsarā Hemā, the maiden named Mandodarī; take her as your wife," and Rāvaṇa marries her before a kindled fire (CE 7.12.14–17). The text adds that Maya gave her knowing that Rāvaṇa came of Pitāmaha's (Brahmā's) line, but without knowing of the ascetic's curse that hung over him (7.12.18).
  - *Variant motive (Yuddha Kāṇḍa):* Rāvaṇa's counsellors remember it differently: Maya gave his daughter "out of fear of you, seeking your friendship" (CE 6.7.6; VR 6.7.7). The two accounts are not reconciled.
- **The gift of the śakti; the brothers' marriages** — Maya also gives his son-in-law an **unfailing (*amoghā*) śakti** won by great tapas, "with which he struck Lakṣmaṇa" (CE 7.12.19). Before meeting Maya, Rāvaṇa had already given his sister **Śūrpaṇakhā** to **Vidyujjihva**, lord of the Kālakeya dānavas (7.12.1–2). Back in Laṅkā he marries **Vajrajvālā**, the *daughter's daughter* (*dauhitrī*) of Virocana, to Kumbhakarṇa. Vibhīṣaṇa marries **Saramā**, daughter of the gandharva king **Śailūṣa**; she was born on the shore of Lake Mānasa, and her name comes from her mother's cry to the rising lake, *saro mā vardhata*, "lake, do not rise" (7.12.20–24).
  - **The weapon in the war:** the dowry-weapon returns at CE 6.88.28–35 (VR 6.100.28–36). Lakṣmaṇa has already shot down a first śakti that Rāvaṇa threw at Vibhīṣaṇa (6.88.19–21). Rāvaṇa then raises a second, an eight-belled, roaring śakti "fashioned by Maya through māyā, unfailing", against Vibhīṣaṇa. When Lakṣmaṇa's arrows save Vibhīṣaṇa, Rāvaṇa turns it on Lakṣmaṇa and fells him (*mayena māyāvihitām amoghāṃ śatrughātinīm*, 6.88.30). It is not Indra's Vāsavī Śakti, which belongs to Karṇa and the *Mahābhārata*. It is also distinct from the **Brahmā-given śakti** (*svayambhudattā*) that had struck Lakṣmaṇa earlier in the war (CE 6.47.101–103). In the same fight Rāvaṇa also looses another missile "made by Maya" at Rāma (CE 6.88.2; VR 6.100.2). So the gift that came with Mandodarī is the weapon that nearly kills Lakṣmaṇa.
  - *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa):* the Adhyātma's retelling of these marriages makes Maya "the Viśvakarmā of the rākṣasas and son of Diti", and has him give Mandodarī together with an unfailing lance (Baij Nath tr., Uttara ch. 2, vv. 39–43).
- **Mother of Meghanāda** — Mandodarī bears Rāvaṇa's son **Meghanāda**, so named by his father because at birth his cry boomed like a thundercloud and stunned Laṅkā. He grows up in the women's apartments, "guarded by excellent women, like fire hidden in wood" (CE 7.12.26–29). By conquering Indra he becomes [Indrajit](./indrajit-meghnad.md), and in the vulgate lament she counts it her pride that her son was Indra's conqueror (VR 6.111.41).
  - **[Akṣayakumāra](./akshakumara.md)** — Vālmīki *does* name Akṣa's mother. In Hanumān's report to the vānaras he says he seized by the feet "that prince, **Mandodarī's son**" (*taṃ tu mandodarī putraṃ kumāraṃ raṇapaṇḍitam*, CE 5.56.106; VR 5.58.123–124). Another son, **Atikāya**, is explicitly the son of the co-wife **Dhānyamālinī** (CE 6.59.29).
  - *Variant (reference literature):* Mani's *Puranic Encyclopaedia* (s.v. "Mandodarī I") gives her three sons, Meghanāda, Atikāya and Akṣakumāra, which conflicts with CE 6.59.29. [disputed]
- *Variant origin legends (non-Vālmīki):* these replace the birth from Hemā's womb with an adoption. All are [regional] or [folk], and none is in the Vālmīki Uttara Kāṇḍa, which says she grew in Hemā's womb (7.12.10).
  - *The frog-maiden Madhurā:* the apsarā **Madhurā** visits Śiva on Kailāsa while Pārvatī is away. Pārvatī finds his ash on her breast and curses her to live twelve years as a frog in a well. Śiva promises that she will become a beautiful woman and marry a great hero. The well lies near the spot where Maya and Hemā are doing tapas for a daughter. When the curse ends they hear the girl cry from the well, adopt her, and name her Mandodarī. Mani attributes this story to an "Uttara Rāmāyaṇa", which is not Vālmīki's Uttara Kāṇḍa (Mani, *Puranic Encyclopaedia*, s.v. "Mandodarī I", "Maya", "Dundubhi"). [regional]
  - *Sulakṣaṇā:* Mani's entry on Meghanāda tells another version. **Sulakṣaṇā**, a maiden who rose from the churned Milk Ocean, serves Pārvatī. Śiva unites with her, and Pārvatī curses her into a frog in a pond. At Śiva's urging Pārvatī later restores her as the maiden Mandodarī, and Śiva gives her to Maya. Her first son by Rāvaṇa is therefore called **Kānīna**, "son of a maiden" (Mani, s.v. "Meghanāda"). [regional]
  - *Telugu, Ānanda and Odia variants:* the Wikipedia survey reports three more frog or substitute-Pārvatī tellings. In the Telugu *Raṅganātha Rāmāyaṇa*, Pārvatī shapes a doll that Śiva animates. In the *Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa*, Viṣṇu makes Mandodarī from sandal paste as a stand-in for Pārvatī; the survey cites Bhattacharya 2004 for both. The third, from the Odia *Dharma Purāṇa*, is a tale of a frog that saves two sages from poisoned milk; the survey cites Bhattacharya 2004 for it too. None of the three was checked against a primary text for this page. [regional] [folk]

## In the Sundara Kāṇḍa: mistaken for Sītā

- **Hanumān's error in the harem** — searching Rāvaṇa's sleeping palace by night, Hanumān sees a queen of supreme beauty asleep apart from the rest, golden-fair and adorned with pearls and gems. She is "Mandodarī, mistress of the inner apartments" (CE 5.8.46–48; VR 5.10.50–52). Taking her for Sītā, he gives way to monkey-joy: he slaps his arms, kisses his tail, sings, and climbs and drops from the pillars (CE 5.8.49–50; VR 5.10.53–54). Then he reasons that Sītā, parted from Rāma, would neither sleep, eat, adorn herself nor drink, and would never go near another man. He decides that this is someone else and moves on (CE 5.9.1–3; VR 5.11.1–3). See the [Sundara Kāṇḍa file](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md).
- **The narrative point** — the scene measures Mandodarī against [Sītā](./sita.md): so radiant that even Hanumān confuses the two, yet defined by the difference, one a queen amid luxury, the other a captive in grief. [scholarly]
- **The Mahābhārata's mention** — in the *Rāmopākhyāna*, Rāvaṇa's proposal to the captive Sītā names her as the model of what he offers: "be my wife, fair-hipped one, as Mandodarī is" (*bhāryā me bhava suśroṇi yathā mandodarī tathā*, MBh CE 3.265.16). A search of the other parvans of the critical text (the Śānti and Āśramavāsika were not searched) found her named only in an appendix passage of the Sabhāparvan (after CE 2.30.25), not in the constituted text.

## The voice of dharma in Laṅkā

- **In Vālmīki: counsel recalled, not staged** — Vālmīki never shows a scene in which Mandodarī advises Rāvaṇa before the war. That reputation rests on her own words afterwards. In her lament she says: "'Make no enmity with Rāghava', I told you, and you would not take it; this is the fruit" (CE 6.99.13; VR 6.111.21). The vulgate goes further and lists the unheeded advice of Mārīca, Kumbhakarṇa **and her father** (*mārīcakumbhakarṇābhyāṃ vākyaṃ mama pitus tathā*, VR 6.111.80). That line is [vulgate only]. The on-stage dissenters of the Yuddha Kāṇḍa are men: [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md), Kumbhakarṇa and Mālyavān.
- **Staying Rāvaṇa's hand against Sītā** — Vālmīki gives two versions of this moment, and the corpus keeps both.
  - *Hanumān's report (Sundara):* Sītā's taunts enrage Rāvaṇa, and he raises his fist to kill her while the women cry out. "From among the women sprang his wife, the noble **Mandodarī**, and she restrained him." Her argument is not a moral one. "What have you to do with Sītā? Enjoy yourself with me; Jānakī is not better than I" (*mayā saha ramasvādya madviśiṣṭā na jānakī*, CE 5.56.67). The women then lead him back to his palace (CE 5.56.64–69; VR 5.58.73–78). The half-verse "Jānakī is not better than I" is in the CE only; in the vulgate she simply asks what he wants with Sītā and bids him enjoy the daughters of gods, gandharvas and yakṣas (VR 5.58.76–77).
  - *The narration of the scene itself:* at the same moment in the Aśoka grove, the woman who embraces Rāvaṇa and draws him away with almost the same words ("sport with me, great king; what is this Sītā to you?") is the rākṣasī **Dhānyamālinī** (CE 5.20.37–38; VR 5.22.39–40).
  - *Later, in the Yuddha:* after Indrajit's death, when Rāvaṇa rushes sword in hand to kill Sītā, the one who stops him is the counsellor **Supārśva** (CE 6.80.51ff.; VR 6.92). Mandodarī plays no part in that scene in Vālmīki.
  - *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa):* the Adhyātma follows Hanumān's version. When Rāvaṇa lifts his sword against Sītā, Mandodarī wards him off: "leave this humbled, wasted, grieving woman," for many daughters of gods, gandharvas and nāgas desire him (Baij Nath tr., Sundara ch. 2, vv. 37–39). [late text]
- *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas):* Tulsīdās develops the counsel fully. After Hanumān has burned Laṅkā and the city is in dread, she clasps Rāvaṇa's feet in private and begs him to give up his hostility to Hari and send Sītā back with a minister. Rāma's arrows are snakes, she says, and the rākṣasas are frogs; Sītā has come like a winter night upon the lotus-grove of his clan. Rāvaṇa laughs: "a woman's nature is truly timid" (*Rāmcaritmānas*, Sundara Kāṇḍa 36.2–5, dohā 36, 37.1–3). She pleads again in the Laṅkā Kāṇḍa, declaring that Rāma is no mere man but the Lord who slew Madhu and Kaiṭabha, and is again refused. The Laṅkā dohā numbers were not retrieved for this page. [late text]
- *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa):* in the devotional recension her great speech comes after the interrupted rite (below) and before the last battle. Rāghava, she says, is the Supreme Lord, the Puruṣa who rules Prakṛti. As Fish he saved Manu, as Tortoise he bore the golden mountain (Baij Nath: "Sumeru") at the churning, and he was also Boar, Man-lion, Vāmana and Paraśurāma. She urges Rāvaṇa to give Sītā back, install Vibhīṣaṇa, and go to the forest with her. Rāvaṇa answers that he has always known Rāma to be Viṣṇu and Sītā to be Lakṣmī, and that he carried Sītā off so that he would die at Rāma's hands and reach Viṣṇu's abode (Baij Nath tr., Yuddha ch. 10, vv. 44–61). [late text]
- **The interrupted rite** — this episode is absent from the Vālmīki critical text.
  - *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa:* on Śukra's advice Rāvaṇa withdraws into a sealed cave to perform a homa for victory. Vibhīṣaṇa's wife **Saramā** points out the place. [Aṅgada](./angada.md) kicks open the cave and the monkeys wreck the rite; Hanumān snatches the ladle from Rāvaṇa's hand. Rāvaṇa does not stir. Then Aṅgada drags Mandodarī in by the hair and tears her jewelled garments before him. She cries out that a husband who can watch this and go on with his homa had better be dead, and cries to the dead Meghanāda that this could not happen if he were alive. Rāvaṇa rises, sword in hand, strikes Aṅgada, and the rite is broken. Afterwards Rāvaṇa consoles her with a short Vedāntic discourse. He then orders her, should he fall, to perform his funeral rites, kill Sītā, and enter the fire with him (Baij Nath tr., Yuddha ch. 10, vv. 16–43). Baij Nath's note says that none of this is in Vālmīki. [late text]
  - *Vālmīki manuscripts:* the Wikipedia survey cites Goldman et al., *The Rāmāyaṇa of Vālmīki* vol. VI (2009), p. 1320, for the report that some manuscripts carry an Aṅgada–Mandodarī story of this kind and that the commentator Satyatīrtha rejects it as spurious. That was not checked here. [interpolation]
  - *Vernacular:* the Wikipedia survey reports that in the Bengali *Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇa* the monkeys drag her and tear off her clothes, and gives other regional forms. Not checked here. [regional] [folk]

## The lament of the Yuddha Kāṇḍa

- **Widowed on the field** — after Rāma slays Rāvaṇa, the women of the harem pour out of the northern gate with their hair unbound and fall on the body (CE 6.98; VR 6.110). It is the queens together who say that had Sītā been given back, "this root-destroying calamity" would not have come (CE 6.98.20–22). Then the eldest and beloved wife, Mandodarī, looks on her husband and laments (CE 6.99.1–29; VR 6.111.1–92). This is the emotional summit of her role in Vālmīki. See the [Yuddha Kāṇḍa file](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md).
- **She names the true cause** — her lament is a moral audit of Rāvaṇa's fall. He before whom even Indra trembled has been beaten by "a mere man, a forest-wanderer" (CE 6.99.3–6). Having first conquered his senses and then the three worlds, he has now been conquered by those same senses, "as if they remembered the old enmity" (6.99.9). The recension determines who she thinks Rāma really is:
  - *Critical Edition:* she guesses that **Indra (Vāsava) himself** has come in Rāma's form, working an unimagined māyā to destroy him. She adds that Rāma was plainly no man from the day Khara fell in Janasthāna (CE 6.99.10–11).
  - *Vulgate:* the vulgate first offers **Yama** or Indra (VR 6.111.12–13) and then adds four verses naming Rāma the eternal **Viṣṇu**, conch-, discus- and mace-bearer with the Śrīvatsa, come in human form among gods born as monkeys (*vyaktam eṣa mahāyogī paramātmā sanātanaḥ*, VR 6.111.14–17). These verses are not in the CE text. [vulgate only]
- **Her verdict on Sītā** — "Sītā, whom you outraged, is more distinguished than Arundhatī and Rohiṇī" (CE 6.99.15; VR 6.111.23). In the same breath she says that Maithilī is neither her superior nor her equal in birth, beauty or graciousness (*dākṣiṇya*), and that only infatuation kept him from seeing it (CE 6.99.16; VR 6.111.30). In the CE she says only that his death was "marked out by Maithilī" (6.99.17). The vulgate names the power at work: Rāvaṇa has been "consumed by the tapas of that husband-devoted woman" (VR 6.111.24–25). [vulgate only]
- **The wife's reproach and grief** — Sītā will now live free of sorrow with Rāma, while she, "of little merit", has fallen into an ocean of grief, cut off from the pleasure-flights to Kailāsa, Meru and Caitraratha (CE 6.99.18–20). Her brother-in-law Vibhīṣaṇa spoke the truth when he foretold the ruin of the rākṣasa chiefs (6.99.21). She ends: "I should not grieve for you, famed for strength; yet a woman's nature turns my mind to pity," and "fie on my heart, that it does not break into a thousand pieces" (6.99.23, 29). The vulgate's much longer lament adds several passages:
  - it names Indrajit's death (VR 6.111.59);
  - it lists Kumbhakarṇa among the unheeded advisers (6.111.80);
  - it adds that the curses of the many virtuous wives he had widowed have fallen on him (6.111.66–68);
  - the CE names neither Indrajit nor Kumbhakarṇa in her speech. [vulgate only]
- **The funeral** — Rāma tells Vibhīṣaṇa to perform the rites and send the women home. Vibhīṣaṇa at first refuses to honour "an enemy in the form of a brother", and Rāma answers *maraṇāntāni vairāṇi*, "enmities end at death; he is mine as he is yours" (CE 6.99.30–40; VR 6.111.93–103). In Vālmīki it is **Vibhīṣaṇa** who performs the rites and lights the pyre. He then consoles the women, who go back into the city (CE 6.99.41–43; VR 6.111.104–126). The vulgate expands the rite: Mālyavān assists (6.111.108), and Vibhīṣaṇa bathes and, in wet clothes, offers water mixed with sesame and darbha (6.111.123–124). [vulgate only] The queens' water-offering is not in Vālmīki. Vibhīṣaṇa is then crowned (CE 6.100; VR 6.112). On the Puṣpaka flight home, Rāma points out to Sītā the spot where "Mandodarī, his wife, lamented him, surrounded by a thousand co-wives" (CE 6.111.8; VR 6.123.14).
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas):* Mandodarī's lament ends in praise. "Alas, lord, there is no ocean of mercy like Raghunātha: the Lord has given you the goal that hosts of yogis can hardly reach" (Laṅkā Kāṇḍa, dohā 104). Then "Mandodarī and all the women" give him the sesame-water offering (*tilāñjali*) and return to the palace, inwardly reciting Rāma's virtues (dohā 105). [late text]
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa):* the rākṣasa women come out of Laṅkā in thousands **with Mandodarī** (*mandodaryā samaṃ*, BhP 9.10.24). Their lament says that, overcome by desire, he did not reckon with Sītā's spiritual power (*tejo 'nubhāvaṃ sītāyāḥ*, 9.10.27). Vibhīṣaṇa then performs the rites with Rāma's approval (9.10.29). [late text]
  - *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa):* the Adhyātma has no long lament here. Mandodarī and the women fall weeping before the body, and Vibhīṣaṇa, after Lakṣmaṇa's Vedāntic consolation, comforts "the highly intelligent queen Mandodarī" before the cremation (Baij Nath tr., Yuddha ch. 12, vv. 5–8, 34–35). [late text]

## Remarriage to Vibhīṣaṇa

- **Queen once more?** — a widely repeated later tradition says that after Rāvaṇa's cremation Mandodarī married [Vibhīṣaṇa](./vibhishana.md), **at Rāma's counsel**, and remained queen of Laṅkā. The union is usually read politically, as legitimating the new king, and is defended by the precedent of Tārā's marriage to Sugrīva (see [Tārā](./tara-vanara-queen.md)). The tradition is **not in Vālmīki**: in both the CE and the vulgate the women simply go back into the city after the rites (CE 6.99.42–43; VR 6.111.125), and the text says nothing more about her fate; she reappears only in Rāma's passing recall of her lament on the Puṣpaka flight (CE 6.111.8). The Wikipedia survey reports the marriage from modern surveys (S. S. Shashi, *Encyclopaedia Indica* vol. 21–35, 1998, p. 222; Pradip Bhattacharya, "Panchkanya: Women of Substance") and names no primary text for it. Those surveys were not opened here, and the marriage was not found in any of the texts opened for this page. [late text] [disputed]
- **The attested endings disagree:**
  - *Kamban (Tamil):* at the end of her lament she rises, clasps his chest, and her life leaves her (*uyir nīṅkiṉāḷ*). Vibhīṣaṇa cremates "Maya's daughter" on the same pyre as her husband (*Irāmāvatāram*, Yuddha Kāṇḍam, *Maṇṭōtari pulampuṟu paṭalam* vv. 29, 31–32). [regional]
  - *Jain (Raviṣeṇa's Padmapurāṇa):* after her sons Indrajit and Meghavāhana take initiation under the monk Anantavīrya, the nun Śaśikāntā consoles Mandodarī. She then becomes an *āryikā* (nun) with many other women, lives in a white sari, and after austerities goes to heaven (Pannalal Jain's introduction to the Bhāratīya Jñānapīṭha edition). [regional]
- **The Pañcakanyā logic** — commentators, notably Bhattacharya, link this second marriage, like Tārā's, to her place in the Pañcakanyā verse. On this reading the five are honoured *through*, not despite, lives that broke the simple norm of one-husband chastity, and virtue is measured by inner fidelity to dharma. The argument depends on the remarriage tradition above. [scholarly]

## The Sītā-as-Mandodarī's-daughter variant

- **Jain tellings (Guṇabhadra / Saṅghadāsa)** — in Guṇabhadra's *Uttarapurāṇa* (9th c. CE) and Saṅghadāsa's *Vasudevahiṇḍī*, **Sītā is born to Rāvaṇa and Mandodarī**. Astrologers foretell that the child will destroy the family, so the infant is abandoned. In the *Uttarapurāṇa* she is buried in a casket in Mithilā's soil, and Janaka recovers her. Rāvaṇa's later abduction is therefore an unwitting near-incest. See the full catalog in [Jain & Buddhist Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/recensions/jain-and-buddhist-ramayanas.md). [regional] [late text]
  - *Note:* the older Jain line (Vimalasūri's *Paumacariya*, followed by Raviṣeṇa) keeps Sītā as Janaka's daughter. The two Jain strands disagree and are kept distinct. In Raviṣeṇa, Mandodarī is the daughter of the vidyādhara king Maya of Asurasaṅgīta, a city on the southern range of Vijayārdha, and of **Hemavatī**. She counsels Rāvaṇa repeatedly: she calms him when Khara-Dūṣaṇa carry off his sister Candranakhā, and she tries to turn him back after he has brought Sītā to Laṅkā (Pannalal Jain, introduction to Raviṣeṇa's *Padmapurāṇa*). [scholarly]
- **Śākta telling (Adbhuta Rāmāyaṇa, canto 8)** — here Sītā is born of Mandodarī rather than found in a furrow. The story runs as follows (Adbhuta 8.17–38, with 6.15–17 for the milk; see [variant Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md)): [late text]
  1. Rāvaṇa draws blood from the forest sages and stores it in a pitcher (8.17–18). He leaves it with Mandodarī with the warning that it is "more potent than poison" and must be neither given away nor drunk (8.22–23).
  2. The pitcher already holds the milk that the sage **Gṛtsamada** had charged with mantras so that Lakṣmī would be born as his daughter (8.19–21).
  3. Rāvaṇa sports with captive daughters of gods, dānavas and gandharvas on Mandara, Sahya and Meru. Mandodarī judges death better than such neglect and drinks the blood to die (8.24–29).
  4. Instead she conceives a child "radiant as fire". She has not been with her husband for a year, so she flies to **Kurukṣetra** on the pretext of a pilgrimage and buries the embryo in the ground (8.30–34). Janaka's plough later turns up the girl there (8.36–38).
  - *Note:* the older form of this bullet called this sequence a "folk mechanism" with burial in Mithilā; it is in fact the Adbhuta's own narrative.
- *Variant (Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa, as reported by Mani):* Rāvaṇa finds Padmā, an incarnation of Lakṣmī, as five jewels in a fire and brings them to Laṅkā in a box. Mandodarī opens it and sees a maiden. Judging her a danger to the family and kingdom, she presses Rāvaṇa to cast the box out, and it is buried in Mithilā, where a ploughman brings it to Janaka. In this version Mandodarī is not Sītā's mother. Mani cites "Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa 7.6" (Mani, s.v. "Sītā"). This was not checked against the text. [late text]
- *Variant (Devī Bhāgavata, as reported by Mani):* Maya warns Rāvaṇa that Mandodarī's horoscope shows that her first child will destroy its clan. Rāvaṇa buries that child in a box in Janaka's city, and the child is Sītā (Mani, s.v. "Sītā"). The Devī Bhāgavata passage was not located for this page. [disputed]

## Afterlife in tradition

- **The ideal wife of a wicked husband** — tradition holds Mandodarī up as the woman who keeps her own dharma inside an adharmic house: chaste, truthful, unheeded, and finally vindicated by events. She is a foil both to Rāvaṇa and to the fickleness his court embodies (*devotional and didactic retellings*). [late text]
- **Regional memory — Mandore** — local tradition claims the old Marwar capital **Mandore**, near Jodhpur in Rajasthan, as Mandodarī's natal home and the site of her wedding to Rāvaṇa. Some Rajasthani and Madhya-Bhārat communities accordingly treat Rāvaṇa as a son-in-law and mark Dashahra with funeral-style observances rather than celebration. [folk] [regional]
- **Stage and vernacular life** — she is a fixture of Rāmlīlā and of the vernacular Rāmāyaṇas (Kamban's Tamil *Irāmāvatāram*, Kṛttivāsī Bengali, Tulsī's Hindi), usually with her counsel scenes and lament expanded. [regional]
  - *Kamban's lament* is a celebrated set-piece of Tamil poetry (*Maṇṭōtari pulampuṟu paṭalam* vv. 21–28). In its most quoted image, Rāma's arrow searched every sesame-seed's space of the body that had lifted Śiva's mountain, as if looking for the love of Jānakī that Rāvaṇa kept hidden in his heart (v. 23).
  - Two verses that call Rāma Nārāyaṇa and Sītā the mother of all the worlds stand among that paṭalam's *mikai* (supernumerary) verses, 28-1 and 28-2, rather than in the main text. [interpolation]
