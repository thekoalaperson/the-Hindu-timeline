# Mādrī — Second Queen of Pāṇḍu, Mother of the Twins

> Where this sits: a dedicated deep-dive on **Mādrī (Mādreyī)** — princess of **Madra**, junior queen of Pāṇḍu, mother of the twin Pāṇḍavas **Nakula and Sahadeva** by the **Aśvins**, the wife whose embrace fulfils the Kindama curse and kills Pāṇḍu, and the matriarch who immolates herself on his pyre. ## Identity & names

- **Mādrī (माद्री)** — "the Madra woman"; her personal name is effectively her place-name, *the princess of Madra*, the north-western Punjab realm with capital **Śākala/Sāgala** (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Mādravatī / Mādreyī** — alternate forms; the matronymic **Mādreya** ("son of Mādrī") attaches to her twins **Nakula** and **Sahadeva**, distinguishing them from the **Kaunteya** trio of [Kuntī](./kunti.md) (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Junior queen** — she is Pāṇḍu's **second wife**, married after Kuntī, and through the epic is the quieter, less foregrounded of the two co-queens — yet the mother of two of the five Pāṇḍavas (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).

## Lineage & the bride-price marriage

- **Princess of Madra, sister of Śalya** — Mādrī is the sister of **Śalya (Madrarāja)**, king of Madra, which makes Śalya the maternal uncle (*mātula*) of the twins and a kinsman of the whole Pāṇḍava house (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). See [Śalya](./shalya.md).
- **The śulka (bride-price) match** — Madra custom required a **bride-price (śulka)** for a daughter of the house; **[Bhīṣma](./bhishma.md)**, securing a second queen for Pāṇḍu, obtains Mādrī by giving great wealth (gold, gems, elephants, horses) to her people, a transaction the epic notes the Madras themselves practised (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
  - *Variant (who negotiates):* the standard telling has **Bhīṣma** journey to Madra and arrange the match as the Kuru elder; some summaries fold the errand into Pāṇḍu's own post-conquest progress. Both agree the marriage is sealed by the bride-price (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). [disputed]
  - *Note (Madra reputation):* the much later, partisan slander of the Madra people as low-born (voiced by Karṇa in the Karṇa Parva) is **invective inside a quarrel**, not a verdict on Mādrī, and is trimmed in the Critical Edition. [disputed]
- **Co-queen with Kuntī** — Pāṇḍu had already won **Kuntī** at her *svayaṃvara*; Mādrī enters the household as the loved junior wife, and the two queens accompany Pāṇḍu when he renounces the throne (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).

## The forest, the curse of Kindama

- **Withdrawal to the Himālaya** — after his conquests Pāṇḍu, afflicted by a pallor-illness (and in some readings by remorse), renounces kingship to **Dhṛtarāṣṭra** and retires to the slopes of the Himālaya with both queens to live as an ascetic (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **The deer-sage Kindama** — hunting, Pāṇḍu shoots a mating pair of deer that are in truth the sage **Kindama** and his wife coupled in deer form; the dying sage **curses Pāṇḍu** to die the moment he approaches a wife in desire (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). This curse — set out fully in the [Kuntī](./kunti.md) and Ādi Parva accounts — is the engine of everything that follows for both queens.
- **The bar on natural fatherhood** — barred from the marriage-bed on pain of death, Pāṇḍu is left without heirs of his body; the path to sons must run through *niyoga* and divine agency (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).

## Mother of the twins (the lent mantra)

- **Kuntī's secret boon** — Kuntī reveals to Pāṇḍu the **invocation-mantra** given her by **Durvāsa**, by which she may summon any deity to beget a child; by it she bears **[Yudhiṣṭhira](./yudhishthira.md)** (Dharma), **Bhīma** (Vāyu) and **Arjuna** (Indra) (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Pāṇḍu's plea for Mādrī** — wishing his junior queen also to have offspring, Pāṇḍu privately asks Kuntī to **share the mantra with Mādrī** so that she too may bear sons; Kuntī consents and grants Mādrī **one use** of the invocation (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
  - *Variant (Kuntī's reluctance):* some retellings stress that Kuntī, having limited the gift to a single invocation, is later **vexed** to find Mādrī has outwitted the allotment; the critical text presents her grant plainly, the resentment as a human undertone (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva; popular retellings*). [folk]
- **The clever choice of the Aśvins** — Mādrī invokes not one but the **twin Aśvins**, the beautiful physician-gods of the dawn; bound by the mantra, **both** appear and beget upon her **twin sons in a single act**, so that one use of the mantra yields two children (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Nakula and Sahadeva** — the twins are born: **Nakula**, famed for beauty and horsemanship, and **Sahadeva**, famed for wisdom and knowledge of cattle and the stars; both are styled **Mādreya** and **Aśvineya** ("sons of the Aśvins") (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Outstripping the allotment** — by summoning a *pair*, Mādrī quietly **matches two of Kuntī's three god-sons with a single invocation**; when Pāṇḍu asks Kuntī for one more use so Mādrī might have still more, Kuntī **refuses**, unwilling to be repeatedly outdone by a cleverer co-wife (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). This refusal fixes the Pāṇḍava count at five.

## Death of Pāṇḍu and the sati

- **The fatal spring** — years on, in a flowering season Pāṇḍu, alone with **Mādrī** in the forest, is overmastered by desire at her beauty; heedless of the curse he embraces her, and **the curse strikes him dead at the touch** (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
  - *Variant (Mādrī's resistance):* the epic has Mādrī **try to refuse and warn him off**, fearing the curse, but Pāṇḍu, in the grip of the season and the god of love, cannot be restrained — locating the responsibility with the curse and the moment, not with her (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). [disputed]
- **Mādrī's grief and self-reproach** — Mādrī blames **herself** as the immediate occasion of her husband's death, reproaching her own beauty and the embrace; her lament is among the parva's most pointed passages (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **The contest of devotion with Kuntī** — both queens wish to follow Pāṇḍu onto the pyre; Mādrī argues that she **must** die, since it was *with her* that desire was left unsatisfied and she could not bear to deny him in death what she gave in life — and that **Kuntī, the elder, is better fitted to raise all five boys impartially** (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
- **Entrusting the twins to Kuntī** — Mādrī **commits her sons Nakula and Sahadeva to Kuntī's care**, asking that she rear them as her own with no distinction from her three; Kuntī accepts the charge, which is why she is counted **mother of five** though she bore but three (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*). See [Kuntī](./kunti.md).
- **Sati on Pāṇḍu's pyre** — Mādrī ascends Pāṇḍu's funeral pyre and is consumed with him, an act of *anumaraṇa* (following in death); the ascetics of the forest then escort Kuntī and the five orphaned boys to **Hāstinapura** (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).
  - *Variant (reading the sati):* devotional tradition frames her self-immolation as the supreme *pativratā* act; the epic itself presents it as **her insistent choice**, won by argument against Kuntī, rather than a compelled rite — a distinction worth keeping against later normative readings of *satī*. [scholarly]

## Afterlife in the narrative

- **The twins as Kuntī's sons** — raised by Kuntī, **Nakula and Sahadeva** stand with their three half-brothers as a single fraternity of five; their loyalty to Kuntī and to [Yudhiṣṭhira](./yudhishthira.md) never fractures along the two-mothers line (*Mahābhārata*, throughout).
- **The uncle across the line** — at Kurukṣetra the twins' maternal uncle **Śalya** is trapped onto the Kaurava side yet never seeks his nephews' lives, and Sahadeva is the one fated to slay **Śakuni** — kin-ties traceable back to Mādrī's Madra house (*Mahābhārata, Śalya Parva*). See [Śalya](./shalya.md).
- **Remembered as the loved junior queen** — across the epic Mādrī survives chiefly as **the beautiful second wife**, the bearer of the twins, and the wife by whom the Kindama curse came due; her brief, decisive presence frames the whole question of the Pāṇḍavas' divine paternity (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva*).

## Roster note

- The exact wording of the mantra-allotment (one use vs. the twin-trick arithmetic), the degree of Mādrī's resistance at Pāṇḍu's death, and the framing of her sati are **points of emphasis that vary across recensions and retellings**; each is flagged above. Treat the BORI critical text as the baseline and devotional/popular layers as cataloged variants, not corrections. [scholarly] [open-ended]
