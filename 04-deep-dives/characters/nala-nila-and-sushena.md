# Nala, Nīla & Suṣeṇa — the Vānara Command

> The three **staff officers** of Rāma's army in the [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md), each carrying a named set-piece of his own: **Nala** son of Viśvakarman, who bridges the ocean; **Nīla** son of Agni, the army's general, who musters the host in fifteen days and kills the rākṣasa commander-in-chief **Prahasta**; and **Suṣeṇa** son of Varuṇa, the physician, who diagnoses the fallen Lakṣmaṇa and names the herbs. Their work runs from the [Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md) muster to the last sargas of the [Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md).
> Sits in **[Tretā-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md). Parent: [Characters](README.md) · Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md) · Class-level home: [Vānaras & Ṛkṣas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/vanaras-and-rikshas.md)
> **Naming care.** This **Nala** is the vānara engineer and has nothing to do with **[Nala of the Nalopākhyāna](../nala-damayanti.md)**, the Niṣadha king of Damayantī's story in the Mahābhārata — a different man in a different epic. **Nīla** is not the Nīla of the Nīlamata or the several Purāṇic nāgas of that name. And **Suṣeṇa** is a name Vālmīki uses more than once (§IV).

The Rāmāyaṇa's war is won by a small number of named specialists, and Vālmīki is careful about which of them does what. **Nala** builds the causeway because a god of craft fathered him and gave his mother a boon that the son would equal the father; **Nīla** holds the eastern gate and the office of *senāpati* because a god of fire fathered him; **Suṣeṇa** reads a wound and prescribes for it because a god of waters did. Together they are the epic's argument that the monkey host is not a mob but an **army with a staff** — engineering, command, medicine — and each of the three carries exactly one scene that the retellings remember and usually get wrong: the bridge that does not float, the general who is not Hanumān, and the mountain that is not called Droṇagiri.

## I. Three sons of three gods (Rām. Bāla-kāṇḍa 17)

- **The commission** - On Brahmā's command the gods beget an army for Rāma's advent, each deva fathering a vānara who carries his own quality; the three below sit in that roster with their functions already fixed by parentage. (Vālmīki, **Bāla 17**.) Full roster: [Vānaras & Ṛkṣas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/vanaras-and-rikshas.md).
- **Nala, son of Viśvakarman** - "The divine architect Viśvakarman procreated the great monkey called Nala" — the craft-line of the host. (Vālmīki, **Bāla 17.12**.) See [Viśvakarman & Tvaṣṭar](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/vishvakarma-and-tvashtar.md).
- **Nīla, son of Agni** - "The bright son of Fire is Nīla, whose blaze is like that of fire, and who excels other monkeys in resplendence, renown and courage." (Vālmīki, **Bāla 17.13**.) See [Agni](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/agni.md).
- **Suṣeṇa, son of Varuṇa** - Varuṇa begets Suṣeṇa in the same passage, immediately before Parjanya begets Śarabha. (Vālmīki, **Bāla 17.15**.) See [Varuṇa](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/varuna.md).

## II. Nala — the bridge is craft, not buoyancy (Rām. Yuddha-kāṇḍa 20–22)

- **Sāgara names the builder** - Rāma, refused passage, lays an arrow on the bow; the ocean-god **Sāgara** appears and pleads his own nature — he cannot make himself passable and remain what he is. What he offers instead is to **bear the bridge**: to hold still and carry its weight, and to keep his crocodiles off the crossing army for as long as it takes. And he names the one who can build it. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 22**.)
- **The boon is a son, not a stone** - Nala's qualification, stated by Nala himself, is a **boon given to his mother on Mount Mandara by Viśvakarman: "a son equal to me will be born to you."** He adds: *"I am a son born of Viśvakarman's own loins; I am equal to Viśvakarman."* The boon is **craft**, transmitted by descent — nothing in it concerns floating, and Nala does not claim it does. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 22.49–50**.)
- **Five days** - Under his direction the vānaras throw mountains, trees and boulders into the sea and he binds them: **fourteen yojanas the first day, twenty the second, twenty-one the third, twenty-two the fourth, twenty-three the fifth** — a causeway **a hundred yojanas long and ten broad**, finished to the far shore, and the army crosses. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 22.66–74**.) Sacred-geography home: [Rāmeśvaram & the Setu-bandha](../../06-sacred-geography/rameswaram-setu-bandha.md).
- **Nothing floats in Vālmīki** - Sarga 22 contains **no floating stone and no buoyancy of any kind**. Rocks and trees go into the water, Nala's engineering holds them, and Sāgara underneath takes the load. The most famous image in the whole Setu tradition is not in the text that invented the Setu. (Vālmīki, Yuddha 22.)
  - *Variant (the Name-inscribed floating stones):* the stones that float because **Rāma's name** is written on them belong to **Tulsīdās's [Rāmcaritmānas](../ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md)** and the devotional tradition that follows it, where the point is the power of the Name rather than the skill of the builder. [late text]
  - *Variant (Nala's boyhood curse):* a widespread regional and folk etiology explains the floating by a curse or boon of Nala's childhood — the boy who threw sages' images or belongings into the water was told that **whatever he cast into water would never sink**. It is a folk graft onto the Vālmīki craft-boon, not an alternative reading of it. [folk] [regional]
  - *Variant (the squirrel):* the little squirrel who carries grains of sand and is stroked by Rāma into her three stripes is **folk**, unattached to any Vālmīki sarga, and beloved; catalogue it, do not adopt it. [folk]
- **In the field** - Nala is not only an engineer: in the first general engagement he is paired against the rākṣasa **Pratāpana**, and he serves through the war among the front-rank chiefs. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 43**.)
  - *Roster note:* Nala is **not** named in the southern search-party's own muster-list at Kiṣkindhā 41, though rosters and retellings routinely place him there. Take the sarga's names as the roster and the fuller lists as tradition. [disputed] See [Aṅgada](./angada.md).

## III. Nīla — the general (Rām. Kiṣkindhā 29; Yuddha-kāṇḍa 37, 43, 58, 59, 67)

- **The fifteen-day muster, on pain of death** - Roused at last by **Hanumān's** rebuke, Sugrīva turns to **Nīla, the commander of his army**, and orders him to summon every vānara host from every quarter — with a term attached: **any who arrives more than fifteen nights hence forfeits his life**, and no further deliberation is to be allowed. It is the epic's first hard deadline, and the office it is given to identifies Nīla as *senāpati*. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 29**, the order at 29.32.)
  - *Note (sarga number):* the muster-order is the substance of sarga **29** itself; Sugrīva's relapse into dissipation follows at 29–30, and citations that put the "(29)" label on the relapse have moved the order off its own sarga.
- **The eastern gate** - When Rāma allots Laṅkā's four gates, **Nīla takes the eastern gate against Prahasta**, with Aṅgada south against Mahāpārśva and Mahodara, Hanumān west, and Rāma with Lakṣmaṇa north. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 37.26**.)
- **First duels** - In the opening general engagement Nīla is matched against **Nikumbha**. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 43**.)
- **The killing of Prahasta** - Rāvaṇa's **commander-in-chief Prahasta** — his uncle, his prime minister and the counsellor who talked him into the founding crime — comes out at the head of the army and is met by Nīla. Nīla **hurls a boulder onto his head as he fights with the mace**, and the rock shatters Prahasta's skull; he falls "like a tree cut at the root," and the leaderless rākṣasas break and run back into the city "like water reaching a breached bridge." It is the first death of a top-rank rākṣasa commander in the war. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 58.53–55**.)
- **The duel with Rāvaṇa, and why he survives it** - Rāvaṇa himself takes the field. Nīla tears up trees and hurls them at him, then **shrinks to a diminutive form and leaps onto the tip of Rāvaṇa's standard**, dancing out of the reach of every arrow — until Rāvaṇa looses the **Āgneya astra**, the fire-missile, which strikes and burns him. Nīla drops to his knees but does not die: Vālmīki says explicitly that he lives **"by virtue of the greatness of his father and his own vigour"** — the son of Fire cannot be killed by fire. In the same sarga Hanumān takes Rāma on his back to face Rāvaṇa. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 59.82, 59.91, 59.126**.)
- **Against Kumbhakarṇa** - Nīla cheers the army and throws a mountain-top at Kumbhakarṇa, who smashes it with his fist and puts Nīla down with a knee — one of the five great chiefs broken in that wave before Hanumān's assault tells. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 67**.) See [Kumbhakarṇa](./kumbhakarna.md).

## IV. Suṣeṇa — the physician who names the herbs (Rām. Kiṣkindhā 41–42, 65; Yuddha 43, 50, 101)

- **The three Suṣeṇas problem** - Vālmīki uses the name more than once and never equates the bearers. **Bāla 17.15** gives a Suṣeṇa begotten by **Varuṇa**; the **Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa** gives **[Tārā](./tara-vanara-queen.md)** a father named Suṣeṇa; the **Yuddha-kāṇḍa** gives the army a **physician** named Suṣeṇa. Tradition merges all three and the corpus follows tradition for convenience — but the identification is **inference, not statement**, and carries a flag wherever it bears weight. The commentators reading Kiṣkindhā 41 go further and state flatly that the Suṣeṇa of the southern muster is *not* Tārā's father. [disputed]
- **Commander of the western party** - A Suṣeṇa leads the **western** search-party past Surāṣṭra and Bālhīka to the western ocean — the **Vaḍavāmukha**, the mare's-mouth fire that drinks the sea, and the hermitage of **Merusāvarṇi**, whose daughter keeps the cave in [Svayamprabhā & Hemā](./svayamprabha-and-hema.md). A Suṣeṇa is *also* named in the southern muster at 41 — one of several places where the four-quarter rosters overlap. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 41–42**.)
- **Eighty yojanas** - On the ladder of leaps at the southern shore, Suṣeṇa's declared range is **eighty yojanas**, the highest of the chiefs before **Jāmbavān's** ninety. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 65**.) See [Jāmbavān](./jambavan.md).
- **In the line** - He fights: the first general engagement pairs him against **Vidyunmālī**. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 43**.)
- **The proposal that is overtaken** - When Indrajit's **nāga-pāśa** binds Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, Suṣeṇa recalls how **Bṛhaspati** treated the wounded gods with spells and herbs after a war of devas and asuras, and proposes fetching two celestial herbs — ***saṃjīva-karaṇī*** and ***viśalyā*** — from **two mountains standing in the Ocean of Milk, Candra and Droṇa**, where the nectar was once churned. The errand is never run: **[Garuḍa](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/garuda.md)** arrives, touches the brothers, and their wounds close of themselves. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 50**.)
  - *Note (this is not "Droṇagiri"):* a mountain called **Droṇa** does stand in Vālmīki — in the **Milk Ocean**, in a proposal that is **never executed**, in a sarga that has nothing to do with either herb-flight. It is not the Himālayan herb-peak, it is not carried anywhere, and **"Droṇagiri" is not a Vālmīki place-name at all**. Object-side canonical home: [Rāmāyaṇa Objects & Tokens](../../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/ramayana-objects-and-tokens.md).
- **The diagnosis** - When **Rāvaṇa's** Maya-made *śakti* fells Lakṣmaṇa and Rāma believes him dead, it is Suṣeṇa who reads the body and refuses the conclusion: the face has not changed colour or darkened, the palms are still lotus-hued, the eyes bright — **none of which belongs to the dead** — and the heart beats. "Lakṣmaṇa, the augmenter of prosperity, has not gone into death." (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 101.26**.)
- **The prescription** - He then names the mountain and the four herbs: **Oṣadhi**, also called **Mahodaya**, and on it ***viśalya-karaṇī*** (which expels weapons and closes wounds), ***sāvarṇya-karaṇī*** (which restores the skin's colour), ***saṃjīva-karaṇī*** (which restores consciousness) and ***sandhānī*** (which knits broken bone). Hanumān, again unable to tell the glowing plants apart, brings the peak; **Suṣeṇa crushes the herb and administers it through Lakṣmaṇa's nose**, and the spear-wound closes. The physician, not the flier, is the one who completes the cure. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 101**.)
  - *Note (two flights, two prescribers, two mountains):* the **earlier** herb-errand, after the brahmāstra, is sent by **[Jāmbavān](./jambavan.md)** to a mountain **between Ṛṣabha and Kailāsa that Vālmīki leaves unnamed** (Yuddha 74). Retelling merges the two into a single flight; the corpus keeps them apart. See [Meru, Kailāsa & the Cosmic Mountains](../../06-sacred-geography/meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md).

## V. Why these three belong together

- **A staff, not a mob** - Vālmīki's vānara army has a **commander** (Nīla), an **engineer** (Nala), a **physician** (Suṣeṇa), a **quartermaster of memory and counsel** ([Jāmbavān](./jambavan.md)), a **prince-commander** ([Aṅgada](./angada.md)) and an **agent** ([Hanumān](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md)). The division of labour is the poem's answer to Rāvaṇa's boast that beasts cannot make war. [scholarly]
- **Each carries one scene the retellings invert** - the bridge becomes a miracle of floating stones; the general's kill of Prahasta is reassigned or forgotten; the physician's named mountain is renamed after a peak the epic never mentions. All three inversions run the same way: Vālmīki's war is won by **competence**, and the later strata prefer wonder. [scholarly]
- **Their fate** - All three are among the chiefs revived at the war's end by **Indra's** boon to Rāma — Indra's own grant, not a collective act of "the gods" — and among those feasted and dismissed with gifts after the coronation at Ayodhyā. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 120**; Yuddha/Uttara-kāṇḍa.)
