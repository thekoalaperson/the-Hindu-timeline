# Sumālī & Mālyavān — the Grandfathers of Laṅkā

> Where this sits: joint deep-dive on **Sumālī** and **Mālyavān**, two of the three sons of **Sukeśa** who held golden Laṅkā before Kubera and before [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md) — the war-generation that lost the city to **Viṣṇu**, and then took it back by a marriage. Sumālī is Rāvaṇa's maternal grandfather and the author of the whole dynastic project; Mālyavān is the elder who survives into the war-book as the one counsellor Rāvaṇa cannot shout down without being ashamed. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). Their prehistory is the [Uttara Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md); Mālyavān's last scene is in the [Yuddha Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md). Race-level roster: [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md); dynastic frame: [Paulastya / rākṣasa vaṃśa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/paulastya-rakshasa-vamsha.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* their careers run from the deep prehistory of the rākṣasa race down to the **[Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the present 28th Mahāyuga — an immensely long life-span, licensed by the boons of their house. On the academic clock the brothers exist wholly in the **Uttara Kāṇḍa**'s later stratum, with the single exception of Mālyavān's counsel at Yuddha 35–36, which sits in the epic's oldest core. [scholarly]

## The three brothers and their house

- **Sons of Sukeśa and Devavatī** — **Mālyavān** (eldest), **Sumālī** (middle) and **Mālī** (youngest) are the sons of **Sukeśa**, the foundling blessed by Śiva and Pārvatī, by **Devavatī**, daughter of the gandharva **Grāmaṇī**. Their descent runs back not to Pulastya but to Brahmā's original **water-guardians** — Heti and Praheti, Vidyutkeśa, Sukeśa — the rākṣasa race proper, which is why the Laṅkā dynasty later becomes a *hybrid* rather than a continuation (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa* 4–5). Canonical home for the pre-dynastic roster: [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md).
- **Laṅkā was already built, and not for them** — the three do not commission the city. **Viśvakarmā** had raised golden **Laṅkā on Mount Trikūṭa** in the southern sea **at Śakra's (Indra's) command**, thirty leagues in extent with walls and terraces of gold, and it is Viśvakarmā himself who tells the brothers of it. They take up residence in a city built for another; the client is Indra and the rākṣasas are only its first occupants (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5).
- **Their households** — Sumālī by **Ketumatī** fathers ten sons — **Prahasta, Akampana, Vikaṭa, Kālakāmukha, Dhūmrākṣa, Daṇḍa, Suparśva, Saṃhrādi, Praghasa, Bhāsakarṇa** — and four daughters, **Rākā, Puṣpotkaṭā, Kaikasī** and **Kumbhīnasī**. Mālyavān by **Sundarī** fathers **Vajramuṣṭi, Virūpākṣa, Durmukha, Suptaghna, Yajñakopa, Matta** and **Unmatta**, with a daughter **Analā**. Mālī by **Vasudā** fathers **Anala, Anila, Hara** and **Sampāti**, who survive the fall of the house to become **Vibhīṣaṇa's four counsellors** (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5). The full roster, with the recensional spelling-drift and the standing misfiling of Mālī's sons into Sumālī's list, is held at [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md).
  - *Note (three names that recur in the war-books):* **Praghasa** and **Bhāsakarṇa** of Sumālī's ten, and **Virūpākṣa** of Mālyavān's seven, are also names of the grove-*senāpatis* Hanumān kills at *Sundara* 46 and of rākṣasas still fighting at *Yuddha* 43.24–25 and 96.30–32. Nothing in the text identifies the sets, and the generations are kept apart here. [disputed] — see [Paulastya / rākṣasa vaṃśa §7](../../03-lineages-vamsha/paulastya-rakshasa-vamsha.md).
- **Two of the four daughters carry the plot** — **Kaikasī** becomes the mother of Rāvaṇa's generation; one of her sisters is the mother of **Khara**, the Janasthāna warlord Rāvaṇa calls "the son of the sister of thy mother." Mālyavān's granddaughter, Analā's daughter **Kumbhīnasī**, is the woman **Madhu** carries off — the chain that runs to **Lavaṇa** and to Śatrughna's founding of Mathurā (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5, 24, 25; see [Bharata, Śatrughna & their wives](./bharata-shatrughna-and-their-wives.md)).

## The war with Viṣṇu — the fall of the first Laṅkā (Uttara Kāṇḍa 6–8)

- **The provocation** — grown insolent on the boons their austerity had won, the three brothers harry the devas and the sages until the gods appeal for relief and **Viṣṇu** undertakes their destruction (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 5–6).
- **The death of Mālī (Uttara 7)** — the youngest brother is the one who dies. In the general battle Mālī strikes **Garuḍa** with his mace, and the wounded bird carries Viṣṇu away from the field; enraged, the god looses his **discus, bright as the solar orb**, and it takes Mālī's head, which rolls down amid torrents of blood. He is the only one of the three the epic kills on the field (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 7).
- **Mālyavān's duel (Uttara 8)** — the eldest fights Viṣṇu directly after his army is broken, striking the god's chest with a spear and then coming on with a spiked iron club; **Garuḍa** ends it, driving him back with the blast of his wings "as a violent tempest disperses a heap of withered leaves." Mālyavān withdraws to Laṅkā covered with shame; Sumālī retreats with his troops the same way (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 8).
- **The abandonment** — the survivors do not hold the city. The rākṣasas **quit Laṅkā altogether and go down to dwell in Pātāla / Rasātala** with their wives, Sumālī leading them, and the golden city stands empty — which is the state in which **[Viśravas](./vishravas.md)** later finds it, and names it as the dwelling for his son **Kubera** (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 8; 3). The rākṣasas lose Laṅkā to no one: they abandon it, and a yakṣa moves in afterwards.

## Sumālī — the architect of the second Laṅkā

- **The sight in the sky (Uttara 9)** — Sumālī comes up out of the nether world into the world of men and sees **Vaiśravaṇa (Kubera)** passing overhead in the **Puṣpaka**, going to visit his father. Amazed, he returns to Rasātala with one question: *by what means shall our power be increased?* Everything that follows in the *Rāmāyaṇa* is his answer (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9).
- **The daughter as policy** — his answer is his unmarried daughter **[Kaikasī](./kaikasi.md)**. He tells her to seek out **Viśravas of the line of Pulastya**, expressly so that *"you will beget sons equal to that Lord of Wealth"* — the ambition is stated aloud, generations before it is achieved. Kaikasī obeys, and bears Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa, Śūrpaṇakhā and Vibhīṣaṇa (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9). Sumālī does not fight for Laṅkā a second time; he arranges to be its grandfather instead.
- **The claim pressed (Uttara 11)** — once Rāvaṇa holds Brahmā's boon, Sumālī emerges from Pātāla with his companions and urges his grandson to take Laṅkā back from Kubera. Rāvaṇa hesitates out of respect for a brother; it is **[Prahasta](./prahasta.md)** — Sumālī's own son and the grandson's minister — who supplies the argument that moves him, and Prahasta who goes as envoy. Kubera consults **Viśravas**, is told to yield rather than fight his brother, and withdraws to Kailāsa. **Laṅkā changes hands without a blow** (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 11). The corpus keeps this cession strictly apart from the later **yakṣa war** at Uttara 13–15, which the retellings fuse into it.
- **The death of Sumālī (Uttara 27)** — in the general war between the gods and the rākṣasas, Sumālī meets **Savitra**, one of the eight **Vasus**. Savitra destroys his serpent-drawn chariot with arrows, then takes up a mace "with its flaming point, resembling the Rod of Death," and brings it down on Sumālī's head: nothing of the rākṣasa remained to be seen, "neither bone nor head nor flesh," for the mace had reduced him to dust. The rout that follows leaves the field to his great-grandson **Meghanāda**, who goes on to bind Indra and earn the name Indrajit (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 27). The man who designed the dynasty does not live to see it fall.
- **His house stands in the city** — Hanumān's burning of Laṅkā runs through a named roll of great mansions, and **Sumālī's** is among them (*Sundara Kāṇḍa* 54) — the compounds of the founding generation still standing in Rāvaṇa's capital.

## Mālyavān — the elder who tells Rāvaṇa the truth (Yuddha Kāṇḍa 35–36)

- **The last of the three** — Mālyavān outlives his brothers, his nephew's whole conquest, and every other voice of the founding generation. He carries no command in the war and never takes the field; his standing is purely that of the house's eldest surviving man (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 35).
- **The speech** — after Rāma has crossed and the spies have reported his host encamped on **[Suvela](../../06-sacred-geography/suvela.md)** — and before the city is invested at *Yuddha* 41 — Mālyavān rises in council and gives the war-book's most complete statement of the case against the war. He argues it in three registers at once:
  - **Statecraft.** A king weaker than or equal to his enemy should make **peace**; the ruler who follows the prudent principles keeps his sovereignty longest. Rāvaṇa should treat with Rāma (*Yuddha* 35).
  - **Dharma as a cosmic balance.** The devas *are* dharma and the rākṣasas *are* adharma; where dharma prevails the age is a golden one and where adharma prevails it is Kali. By exalting the rākṣasas and slighting the righteous, Rāvaṇa has put his own side on the losing arm of the scale (*Yuddha* 35).
  - **Omens.** He recites them at length — clouds raining blood, beasts weeping and losing their lustre, carrion-eaters gathering over the city, dogs fouling the offerings, cows calving asses and cats coupling with tigers, and a frightful shape of Death standing to watch the houses (*Yuddha* 35).
- **"Not a mere man"** — his conclusion is the sentence the epic keeps for its most authoritative enemy voices: *"I deem Rāma of firm fortitude as **Viṣṇu dwelling in human form**. This Rāma is not a mere human being."* His counsel is to **give Sītā back** and save the race from annihilation (*Yuddha* 35.36–37).
- **The refusal** — Rāvaṇa answers with contempt: Mālyavān exaggerates the enemy, takes the enemy's side, and *"I have closed my ears to the speech you have made."* He will not return Sītā, and swears Rāma's army will not go back alive (*Yuddha* 36).
- **The exit** — seeing the king's rage, Mālyavān is ashamed and does not answer. He pronounces the formal blessing on the king's victory that courtesy requires, asks leave, and **goes home**. He is not seen again; Vālmīki records no death for him (*Yuddha* 36). [open-ended]
- **The kinship crux** — Yuddha **35.6** calls Mālyavān Rāvaṇa's **mātāmaha**, "maternal grandfather." By the Uttara Kāṇḍa's own genealogy that title belongs to **Sumālī**, and Mālyavān is Sumālī's elder brother — Rāvaṇa's maternal **great-uncle**. The corpus records the discrepancy rather than smoothing it: Sanskrit kinship terms for the senior maternal generation are elastic, and the war-book was very likely composed without the Uttara's genealogy in front of it. Where precision is needed the corpus uses **maternal great-uncle**, and flags the Yuddha 35.6 wording. [disputed]
- **Not the mountain** — three distinct **Mālyavān**s stand in the canon and are routinely confused: (1) this rākṣasa elder; (2) **Mount Mālyavān / Prasravaṇa** near Kiṣkindhā, the hill where Rāma waits out the monsoon (*Kiṣkindhā* 27) and from which **Kesarī** went to Gokarṇa to kill Śambasādana (*Sundara* 35); and (3) the **Mālyavān range** of Purāṇic cosmography, the western "tusk" flanking Ilāvṛta beyond which lies Ketumāla-varṣa (*Viṣṇu* and *Bhāgavata* Purāṇas; see [Bhāratavarṣa & Purāṇic geography](../../06-sacred-geography/bharatavarsha-and-puranic-geography.md)). No text connects them.

## Why the pair matters

- **Two ways to lose and one way to win** — Mālī fights Viṣṇu and dies; Mālyavān fights Viṣṇu and is blown off the field; Sumālī, who also loses, is the one who works out that the house cannot take Laṅkā by force and marries into a Prajāpati's line instead. The *Rāmāyaṇa*'s antagonist exists because one defeated king drew the right lesson from a defeat (*Uttara Kāṇḍa* 7–9). [scholarly]
- **The bookends of the counsel-motif** — the epic gives Laṅkā a standing chorus telling Rāvaṇa to return Sītā: **Vibhīṣaṇa**, **Kumbhakarṇa** on policy, **[Mandodarī](./mandodari.md)**, **Kaikasī** and the minister Aviddha (*Yuddha* 34), **Prahasta** by his own account (*Yuddha* 57), and Mālyavān most fully of all. Rāvaṇa refuses every one; the corpus notes that the war is not caused by bad advice but by advice ignored. [scholarly]
- **The generational frame** — Sumālī opens the dynastic project by looking up at Kubera's chariot; Mālyavān closes it by reading the omens over Rāvaṇa's city. Between those two scenes lies the entire rise and fall of rākṣasa Laṅkā, and both men are spectators to the war that ends it.
