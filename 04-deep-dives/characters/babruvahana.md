# Babruvāhana

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Babhruvāhana** (Babruvāhana), Arjuna's son by **Citrāṅgadā** of Maṇipura, the matrilineal heir who — goaded by the Nāga princess **Ulūpī** — kills his own father in single combat during Yudhiṣṭhira's horse-sacrifice and revives him with the serpent-realm gem, fulfilling the Vasus' curse for Bhīṣma's death. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md). The episode is the climax of the [Āśvamedhika Parva](../mahabharata/14-ashvamedhika-parva.md); his father's account is in [Arjuna](./arjuna.md) §X; his half-brother is [Abhimanyu](./abhimanyu.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* this falls in the present 28th Mahāyuga of the 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara, in late Dvāpara Yuga, in the year after the Mahābhārata war (Kali Yuga begins 3102 BCE); the Critical Edition of the *Mahābhārata* (BORI) is the scholarly textual baseline. [scholarly]

## Birth, name, and the Maṇipura succession

- **Son of Arjuna and Citrāṅgadā** — born to **Citrāṅgadā**, warrior-princess of **Maṇipura (Maṇalūra)**, whom Arjuna wed during his twelve-year tīrtha-exile; he is thus Arjuna's son by his third marriage and half-brother to Abhimanyu (Subhadrā's), Irāvān (Ulūpī's), and Śrutakarman (Draupadī's) (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~207–208; Āśvamedhika Parva ~79*). See [Arjuna](./arjuna.md) and [Abhimanyu](./abhimanyu.md).
- **The matrilineal marriage-pact** — Citrāṅgadā's father, King Citravāhana, gave her only on the condition that **her son remain in Maṇipura as its heir** (her line being one of single-child succession), so Babhruvāhana grew up a king in his mother's realm and **never joined the Pāṇḍava household** at Hastināpura/Indraprastha (*Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~207*).
- **The name** — glossed as "bearing a tawny/brown (*babhru*) mount/standard (*vāhana*)"; he is also called **Babruvāhana** in many transliterations and **Maṇipura-rāja** (king of Maṇipura) (*Mahābhārata*).
- **Reared by his grandfather** — in the epic he is raised and crowned at Maṇipura by **Citravāhana**, succeeding to the throne rather than to a Kuru inheritance (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~79*). [scholarly]

## The horse reaches Maṇipura

- **Yudhiṣṭhira's Aśvamedha** — after the war, Yudhiṣṭhira performs the imperial **horse-sacrifice** as atonement and seal of sovereignty; the consecrated stallion roams free for a year with **Arjuna** as its protector, conquering or pacifying every realm it enters (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~71–73*). See [Āśvamedhika Parva](../mahabharata/14-ashvamedhika-parva.md).
- **Babhruvāhana's peaceful welcome** — when the wandering horse reaches Maṇipura, the young king **comes out in homage**, humbly, with priestly gifts and tribute, to greet his father — not in arms (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~79*).
- **Arjuna rebukes him** — bound by kṣatriya code to give battle to any kingdom the horse enters, Arjuna is **displeased** and **reproaches his son as unmanly / unwarriorlike** for offering submission instead of a fight, demanding combat (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~79–80*).

## Ulūpī's goading and the slaying

- **Ulūpī rises from the earth** — **Ulūpī**, the Nāga princess (Arjuna's serpent-wife and Babhruvāhana's step-mother), emerges from the ground and **incites Babhruvāhana to take up arms against his father**, overriding his reluctance (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~80*).
- **The duel** — father and son fight a fierce archery duel before Maṇipura; the son proves a match for the unconquerable Arjuna (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~80–81*).
- **Babhruvāhana kills Arjuna** — his arrows strike Arjuna down, and the great Pāṇḍava archer **falls dead** on the field — a near-unique instance of Arjuna being slain in the epic (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~81*).
  - *Variant (Critical/Vyāsa core):* Arjuna is felled by arrows piercing his chest; the death is by ordinary (if mortal) wounds, no decapitation (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~81*). [scholarly]
  - *Variant (South-Indian / Yakṣagāna *Babruvāhana* cycle):* the popular drama has Babhruvāhana **behead** Arjuna outright, raising the stakes of the grief-and-revival for high theatre — beyond Vyāsa's terser narrative (*regional folk tradition*). [folk] [regional]
- **The double grief** — overcome at having killed his father, Babhruvāhana resolves to **die beside him**; his mother **Citrāṅgadā** rushes out, laments, and **reproaches Ulūpī** for engineering a son's slaying of his father (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~81*).

## Revival by the Nāga gem and the hidden purpose

- **The Saṃjīvana / Nāgamaṇi** — Ulūpī summons the serpent-realm jewel of resurrection — the **Mṛtasaṃjīvanī (Saṃjīvana-maṇi / Nāgamaṇi)** — and places it on Arjuna's chest; **Arjuna returns to life**, the death undone (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~81*).
- **Expiation of Bhīṣma's death — the Vasus' curse** — Ulūpī reveals she **orchestrated the whole episode** for Arjuna's good: the **Eight Vasus** (of whom Bhīṣma was the incarnation Dyaus/Prabhāsa) had cursed Arjuna for slaying Bhīṣma **unfairly — from behind Śikhaṇḍī** — on the battlefield; the curse, and Gaṅgā's grief, could only be discharged if Arjuna **fell by his own son's hand**. Babhruvāhana's deed thus **expiated the sin** of Bhīṣma's killing, and the gem reversed the death (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~81–82*). See [Abhimanyu](./abhimanyu.md) for the parallel curse-and-fate logic of the epic's deaths.
- **Reconciliation and the journey home** — Arjuna, restored, is reconciled with his son; Babhruvāhana, Citrāṅgadā, and Ulūpī are later invited to attend the sacrifice at Hastināpura, and the horse continues its circuit (*Mahābhārata, Āśvamedhika Parva ~82*).

## The Jaiminīya Aśvamedha variant — Babhruvāhana's underworld quest

- **An independent, hugely popular alternate** — the **Jaiminīya Aśvamedhaparvan** (attributed to Jaimini, a disciple of Vyāsa; c. 12th c.) is a separate, devotional retelling of the horse-sacrifice that **dwarfs Vyāsa's terse account** with many extra adventures and is far more widely staged and recited in the regional traditions (*Jaiminīya Aśvamedha Parva*). [late text] [folk]
- **The descent to Pātāla for the cure** — in this version, after Babhruvāhana kills Arjuna, he does **not** have the gem to hand; he **descends into Pātāla / Nāgaloka** and **wages war against the serpents** to seize the **amṛta** and/or the **Saṃjīvanī jewel** to revive his father — a multi-chapter expansion entirely absent from the critical epic (*Jaiminīya Aśvamedha Parva*). [late text]
- **The Maṇṭ̌ūka / mongoose host** — some tellings give Babhruvāhana allied armies (including, in popular versions, a **mongoose host**) for the serpent-war, weaving in the Aśvamedha's mongoose motif (*Jaiminīya Aśvamedha Parva; folk retellings*). [folk]
- **Companion marvels of the Jaiminīya** — the same text frames Babhruvāhana's episode among its signature set-pieces: **Mayūradhvaja (Mayūradhwaja)**, who saws himself in half to honour a guest; King **Yauvanāśva**; and the goddess **Caṇḍī** — all markers that this is a distinct devotional cycle, not the BORI Mahābhārata (*Jaiminīya Aśvamedha Parva*). [late text]
- **Why it matters** — for many regional audiences the **Jaiminīya** *is* the Babhruvāhana story; the Vyāsa core (slaying → gem-revival → curse-disclosure) is the older, sparer scriptural baseline beneath it (*scholarly note on recensions*). [scholarly] [disputed]

## Regional, folk, and devotional variants

- *Variant (softened provocation) [folk] [regional]:* some traditions stress that Babhruvāhana fights **only under duty / Ulūpī's compulsion**, reading the episode as a lesson that even the invincible Arjuna must bow to **karmic justice** — the son is blameless, the instrument of fate.
- *Variant (Yakṣagāna / Kannada *Babruvāhana*) [folk] [regional]:* one of the most beloved stage cycles of coastal Karnataka, centred on the beheading, Citrāṅgadā's and Ulūpī's grief, and the gem-revival as climactic spectacle.
- *Variant (Telugu / Harikathā) [folk]:* devotional recitations frame Babhruvāhana as the perfect son whose terrible act is divinely scripted to free his father from the Vasus' curse — fate, not parricide.
- *Variant (lineage note):* as Maṇipura's heir Babhruvāhana does **not** carry the Kuru/Pāṇḍava throne — that line passes through Abhimanyu's son **Parīkṣit**; Babhruvāhana remains king of Maṇipura (*Mahābhārata*). See [Abhimanyu](./abhimanyu.md).
