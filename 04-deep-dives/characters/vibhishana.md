# Vibhīṣaṇa

Breadcrumb: [Characters](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · [Master Index](../../README.md)
The righteous rākṣasa brother of Rāvaṇa who counsels the return of Sītā, takes refuge with Rāma (śaraṇāgati), reveals Laṅkā's secrets, is crowned king of Laṅkā, and endures as a *cirañjīvī* (immortal). Catalogs variants across Vālmīki, Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, Rāmcaritmānas, Kamba, Jain and folk traditions.
Related: [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md) · [The Cirañjīvīs](../the-chiranjivis.md) · [Yuddha Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md) · [Uttara Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md) · [Variant Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md)

## Identity and Names

- **Vibhīṣaṇa (विभीषण)** - **youngest of the four children** of the brahmin sage Viśravas and the rākṣasī Kaikasī; the birth order of Uttara Kāṇḍa 9 is **Rāvaṇa → Kumbhakarṇa → Śūrpaṇakhā → Vibhīṣaṇa**, so his sister is his elder and he is last-born of the house. Born a rākṣasa by descent but sāttvika in nature. (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, *Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9).
- **Lineage** - paternal grandson of the sage Pulastya (a Prajāpati / mind-born son of Brahmā), great-grandson of Brahmā, placing him in the same Paulastya/Pulastya clan as Rāvaṇa and Kubera. (Uttara Kāṇḍa).
- **Epithets** - "the righteous rākṣasa," "Laṅkeśvara" (lord of Laṅkā, after coronation), counted among the **Sapta/Aṣṭa Cirañjīvī** (seven/eight immortals). (Cirañjīvī Stotram tradition). See [The Cirañjīvīs](../the-chiranjivis.md).

## Birth, Tapas, and Boon

- **Joint austerities of the three brothers** - Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa and Vibhīṣaṇa together performed severe tapas at Gokarṇa to win boons from Brahmā; Rāvaṇa sought invincibility by a seven-fold roster of beings that omitted men and animals, and **Sarasvatī entered Kumbhakarṇa's mouth** at the gods' request so that he asked for sleep instead. Vibhīṣaṇa alone asked for nothing but righteousness. (Vālmīki, *Uttara Kāṇḍa* 10). See [Kumbhakarṇa](./kumbhakarna.md) for the sleep-boon's two textual layers.
- **Vibhīṣaṇa's boon** - he asked three things: that his mind never stray from the path of dharma even in the gravest peril, that his thoughts always conform to virtue, and that he be able to **wield the brahmāstra without ever having been taught it**. Brahmā granted all three, and — unasked, pleased by so virtuous a wish — **added immortality** (a-maraṇa / cirañjīvitva). That unrequested grant, not any later benediction, is the textual origin of his cirañjīvī status. (Vālmīki, *Uttara Kāṇḍa* 10).
  - *Variant (Bhakti/Rāmcaritmānas tradition):* his sole prayer was that his mind remain fixed at the lotus feet of Bhagavān (Viṣṇu/Rāma) and that he obtain the darśana of the Lord — reframing the boon as pure devotion rather than abstract dharma. [late text]
- **Tapas length** - texts give the brothers' austerities as enduring across ten thousand years / multiple sets of years; rosters of exact durations vary by recension. [open-ended roster]

## Family

- **Wife — Saramā** - **daughter of the gandharva king Śailūṣa**, not a rākṣasī by birth; Rāvaṇa gave her to Vibhīṣaṇa in the round of marriages that also matched Kumbhakarṇa to Vajrajvālā and Śūrpaṇakhā to Vidyujjihva. Her name is an infant etymology: playing at the Mānasa lake as its waters rose, she was saved when her mother cried *"sara mā"* — "do not overflow, O lake!" — and the words became her name. (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, *Uttara Kāṇḍa* 12). In the Aśoka grove she consoles the captive Sītā and carries her Rāvaṇa's council deliberations, and it is **Saramā** — not Trijaṭā — whom the vulgate credits with exposing the illusory severed head of Rāma as Rāvaṇa's māyā. (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 33).
- **Daughter — Trijaṭā** - the kindly rākṣasī who guards Sītā in the Aśoka-vana, dreams of Rāma's victory, and reassures her (Vālmīki *Sundara Kāṇḍa* 27). The identification of her as **Vibhīṣaṇa and Saramā's daughter is not in Vālmīki** — he gives her no parentage at all — and belongs to later tradition, where it is near-universal. [late text]
  - *Variant:* the daughter who carries Saramā's intelligence to Sītā is named **Kalā** in some recensions, **Nandā** or **Analā** in others — a variant naming of the same informant figure. [disputed]
- **Daughter — Anala** - some lists treat Anala as a distinct daughter married into the vānara/celestial line; rosters of Vibhīṣaṇa's children are inconsistent. [open-ended roster]

## Counsel to Rāvaṇa (the Voice of Dharma)

- **Urging the return of Sītā** - throughout the Laṅkā episode Vibhīṣaṇa repeatedly counsels Rāvaṇa to restore Sītā to Rāma honourably, warning that abducting another's wife is adharma that will doom the realm; he argues true sovereignty rests on ethical conduct, not conquest or lust. (Vālmīki, *Yuddha Kāṇḍa*).
- **Reproof of Indrajit's outrages** - he condemns the killing/abuse of envoys and the burning sent against Rāma's camp, insisting Laṅkā honour the laws of war and diplomacy. (Yuddha Kāṇḍa).
- **The fatal council** - in open court Vibhīṣaṇa warns of Rāma's might and the ill omens over Laṅkā; Rāvaṇa, enraged, spurns and insults him (in several tellings spurning him with his foot or calling him a traitor), driving the final break. (Yuddha Kāṇḍa).
  - *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa / Rāmcaritmānas):* Vibhīṣaṇa's counsel is cast in explicitly devotional terms — he extols Rāma as the Supreme Lord and pleads with Rāvaṇa to win liberation by surrender; his speeches anticipate the bhakti "Vibhīṣaṇa Gītā." [late text]

## Defection and Śaraṇāgati (Refuge with Rāma)

- **Leaving Laṅkā** - rejected by Rāvaṇa, Vibhīṣaṇa departs with four trusted rākṣasa ministers, crosses to the mainland, and hovers in the air above Rāma's camp, declaring he comes to surrender body and soul to Rāma. (Vālmīki, *Yuddha Kāṇḍa* sargas 17–19).
- **The council of acceptance** - Sugrīva, Aṅgada, Śarabha, Jāmbavān and Mainda counsel suspicion or even killing him — "he who deserts his own brother in peril, whom will he not betray?" Hanumān argues in favour. (Yuddha Kāṇḍa, sarga 17).
- **Rāma's vow of refuge (the śaraṇāgati doctrine)** - Rāma declares he will never reject one who comes seeking friendship, even with a flaw, and pledges abhaya (assurance of safety) to anyone who says even once "I am yours" — "let it be Vibhīṣaṇa or even Rāvaṇa himself." This passage is the locus classicus of the Vaiṣṇava doctrine of self-surrender (prapatti). (Vālmīki, *Yuddha Kāṇḍa*; foundational for Śrīvaiṣṇava theology).
- **Anointed king-in-exile** - upon acceptance, Rāma at once consecrates Vibhīṣaṇa as the future king of Laṅkā, before the war is even won, sealing the alliance. (Yuddha Kāṇḍa).
  - *Variant (motive):* one strand has his mother Kaikasī herself advise him to side with Rāma; the bhakti recensions stress he defected out of devotion to recognise Rāma's divinity, softening any charge of treachery. [folk]/[late text]

## Service in the War — Laṅkā's Secrets Revealed

- **Strategic intelligence** - as a rākṣasa insider Vibhīṣaṇa identifies Rāvaṇa's commanders, the strengths of the city's defences and gates, and the dispositions of the enemy host, advising Rāma's war council. (Vālmīki, *Yuddha Kāṇḍa*).
- **The Nikumbhilā yajña secret** - he discloses that once Indrajit (Meghanāda) reaches the sanctuary called **Nikumbhilā** and completes his oblations he will be unconquerable "even by the celestials including Indra," and that the counter is to strike **before the offering is finished**; he names Lakṣmaṇa for the task, leads the strike-party to the grove himself, and Indrajit is killed there. Vālmīki gives the altar **no presiding deity**: the family-goddess reading, and her identification with Pratyaṅgirā, belong to the commentarial and tantric stratum. (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 84.14–23, 86–87). See [Nikumbhilā](../../06-sacred-geography/nikumbhila.md) and [Indrajit](./indrajit-meghnad.md).
- **The secret of Rāvaṇa's death** - **in Vālmīki, Vibhīṣaṇa reveals no such secret and there is no amṛta.** When Rāma's arrows fail and the severed heads keep regrowing, it is **Mātali**, Indra's charioteer, who reminds Rāma of the Brahmā-made arrow Agastya gave him on the field; it takes Rāvaṇa in the chest, tears out his heart, and returns to the quiver. (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 108.1–4, 108.17–20).
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas and regional Rāmāyaṇas):* the nectar of immortality is lodged in Rāvaṇa's **navel**, and it is Vibhīṣaṇa who discloses it, sending the arrow there to dry it up. This is the version most retellings and stage traditions carry, and the disclosure is Vibhīṣaṇa's signature act in them — but it is not the Vālmīki text. [variant]
- **Aiding the rescue of Lakṣmaṇa** - he participates in the crisis when Lakṣmaṇa falls (the Sañjīvanī herb episode), helping coordinate the response. (Yuddha Kāṇḍa; later tradition).
- **Last rites for Rāvaṇa** - after Rāvaṇa's death Vibhīṣaṇa at first refuses to perform funeral rites for the brother who wronged him; Rāma persuades him that enmity ends at death ("maraṇāntāni vairāṇi") and that Rāvaṇa was great, so Vibhīṣaṇa performs the obsequies. (Vālmīki, *Yuddha Kāṇḍa*).

## Coronation as King of Laṅkā

- **Consecration** - after victory Rāma fulfils his pledge and has Vibhīṣaṇa formally crowned king of Laṅkā, establishing a dharmic rākṣasa rule in place of Rāvaṇa's tyranny. (Vālmīki, *Yuddha Kāṇḍa*).
- **Witness to Sītā's ordeal** - Vibhīṣaṇa is present at the agni-parīkṣā and the reunion, and arranges Sītā's honourable conveyance from the Aśoka grove. (Yuddha Kāṇḍa).
- **Honoured guest at Ayodhyā** - he accompanies Rāma's party (in many tellings aboard the Puṣpaka vimāna) to witness the coronation of Rāma at Ayodhyā before returning to rule Laṅkā. (Yuddha/Uttara Kāṇḍa).
- **Mention in the Mahābhārata** - Vibhīṣaṇa is named in the *Mahābhārata*'s Rāmopākhyāna retelling and elsewhere as the dharmic survivor of the Rākṣasa line. See [Rāmopākhyāna](../ramopakhyana.md). (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva*).

## Immortality and the Cirañjīvī Status

- **The term Vālmīki gives, and where** - the epic's own grant is made at the **Mahāprasthāna**: among his last commands Rāma tells Vibhīṣaṇa to stay in Laṅkā "as long as people exist," his empire enduring **as long as the moon and sun shine and men speak of Rāma** — the same scene in which Hanumān is given his term of the story's telling and Jāmbavān his until the coming of Kali. That is the sun-and-moon term, not a Kalpa-term, and it is not at the coronation. (Vālmīki, *Uttara Kāṇḍa* 108) — see [Uttara Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md) and [The Cirañjīvīs](../the-chiranjivis.md).
- **Cirañjīvī of the Rāma cycle** - the later roster-tradition restates the grant as life until the end of the present Kali Yuga, charged with guiding people toward truth and dharma — a different term from Vālmīki's, and the one the *stotra* literature carries. (Cirañjīvī tradition) [late text]
- **The Cirañjīvī verse** - he appears in the *Sapta-/Aṣṭa-Cirañjīvī Stotram* alongside Aśvatthāman, Bali, Vyāsa, Hanumān, Vibhīṣaṇa, Kṛpa, and Paraśurāma (and Mārkaṇḍeya in eight-fold lists); daily remembrance is said to confer health and longevity. (Stotra tradition). [variant rosters of 7 vs 8 names]

## Later Legends and Regional Variants

- **Śrīraṅgam / Ranganātha legend** - returning south to Laṅkā, Vibhīṣaṇa carries the Raṅga-vimāna icon of Viṣṇu (Ranganātha) gifted by Rāma; he sets it down at Śrīraṅgam (Tiruchirāpalli) and it refuses to be lifted again, so the deity remains there facing south toward Laṅkā, while Vibhīṣaṇa is held to journey to worship it perpetually as an immortal devotee. (Śrīraṅgam Ranganāthaswāmy temple sthala-purāṇa). [folk]
  - *Variant:* a local king **Dharmavarma** consecrates the vimāna at the site at Vibhīṣaṇa's behest. [folk]
- **Vibhīṣaṇa Gītā** - the *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa* (and its Awadhi descendant, Tulsīdās's *Rāmcaritmānas*) frame his counsel and devotion as a discourse on the "chariot of dharma" by which Rāma conquers Rāvaṇa — a celebrated bhakti set-piece. (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa). [late text] See [Variant Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md).
- **Kamba Rāmāyaṇam (Tamil)** - heightens the pathos of his break with Rāvaṇa and the nobility of his surrender, a key episode in the Tamil tradition. [variant]
- **Jain Rāmāyaṇa (Paumacariya etc.)** - in Jain retellings Vibhīṣaṇa (Bibhīsaṇa) is a Vidyādhara prince of the rākṣasa-dvīpa line, a layman-devotee figure; the supernatural "navel nectar" and demonic elements are rationalised, and Lakṣmaṇa, not Rāma, kills Rāvaṇa. [variant]
- **Southeast Asian Rāmāyaṇas** - he appears as Bibhek/Phiphek (Thai *Ramakien*), the astrologer-brother whose defection and prophetic knowledge doom Rāvaṇa (Thotsakan); in such tellings his foreknowledge of Laṅkā's fate is central. [folk]
- **Devotional veneration** - regarded as a model of the devotee who chooses dharma and the Lord over kin and kingdom; invoked in śaraṇāgati liturgy and named in the cirañjīvī morning remembrance. [late text]
