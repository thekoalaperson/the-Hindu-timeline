# Vibhīṣaṇa

Breadcrumb: [Characters](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · [Master Index](../../README.md)
The righteous rākṣasa brother of Rāvaṇa who counsels the return of Sītā, takes refuge with Rāma (śaraṇāgati), serves as his guide to Laṅkā's men and sorceries, is crowned king of Laṅkā, and endures as a *cirañjīvī* (immortal). Catalogs variants across Vālmīki (Baroda Critical Edition and Southern vulgate kept apart), the Mahābhārata, Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, Rāmcaritmānas, Kamba, Kṛttivāsa, Jain and Southeast Asian traditions.
Related: [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md) · [The Cirañjīvīs](../the-chiranjivis.md) · [Yuddha Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md) · [Uttara Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md) · [Variant Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/adhyatma-and-variant-ramayanas.md)

Locator key: **VR** = Southern vulgate as numbered on valmikiramayan.net (book.sarga.verse); **CE** = Baroda Critical Edition (book.sarga.verse); **MBh** = BORI critical Mahābhārata. Where VR and CE differ, the difference is stated.

## Identity and Names

- **Vibhīṣaṇa (विभीषण)** - **youngest of the four children** of the brahmin sage [Viśravas](./vishravas.md) and the rākṣasī [Kaikasī](./kaikasi.md); the birth order of Uttara Kāṇḍa 9 is **Rāvaṇa → Kumbhakarṇa → Śūrpaṇakhā → Vibhīṣaṇa**, so his sister is his elder and he is last-born of the house (*vibhīṣaṇaś ca dharmātmā kaikasyāḥ paścimaḥ sutaḥ*, CE 7.9.26–27). Viśravas had promised Kaikasī that her last son would be righteous and worthy of his own line (CE 7.9.20), and as a boy he lived self-controlled, devoted to Vedic recitation (CE 7.9.30). Born a rākṣasa by descent but sāttvika in nature. (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, *Uttara Kāṇḍa* 9).
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata, Rāmopākhyāna):* Viśravas's children have three rākṣasī mothers: **Puṣpotkaṭā** bears Rāvaṇa and Kumbhakarṇa, **Mālinī** bears Vibhīṣaṇa alone, and **Rākā** bears Khara and Śūrpaṇakhā. Vibhīṣaṇa surpasses all of them in beauty and is "a guardian of dharma, devoted to rites" (MBh 3.259.5–9).
- **Lineage** - paternal grandson of the sage [Pulastya](./pulastya.md) (called a son of Prajāpati, CE 7.2.4), placing him in the same Paulastya clan as Rāvaṇa and [Kubera](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/kubera.md), his paternal half-brother. On his mother's side he is a grandson of Sumālī and grand-nephew of Mālyavān (CE 7.25.23–24); see [Sumālī and Mālyavān](./sumali-and-malyavan.md). (Uttara Kāṇḍa).
- **Epithets** - "the righteous rākṣasa" (*dharmātmā* is Vālmīki's standing epithet for him), "Laṅkeśvara" (lord of Laṅkā, after coronation; Tulsīdās's Rāma addresses him as *laṃkesa* from the moment of refuge, Rāmcaritmānas *Sundar Kāṇḍ* 46–49), counted among the **Sapta/Aṣṭa Cirañjīvī** (seven/eight immortals). (Cirañjīvī Stotram tradition). See [The Cirañjīvīs](../the-chiranjivis.md).

## Birth, Tapas, and Boon

- **Joint austerities of the three brothers** - stung by Kaikasī's comparison with the splendid Kubera, Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa and Vibhīṣaṇa together performed severe tapas at **Gokarṇa** to win boons from Brahmā (CE 7.9.31–37). Rāvaṇa asked first for immortality, then for invulnerability to suparṇas, nāgas, yakṣas, daityas, dānavas, rākṣasas and gods, dismissing men and other creatures as straw (CE 7.10.16–18); at the gods' request **Sarasvatī entered Kumbhakarṇa's mouth** so that he asked for sleep instead (CE 7.10.31–41). (Vālmīki, *Uttara Kāṇḍa* 10). See [Kumbhakarṇa](./kumbhakarna.md) for the sleep-boon's textual layers.
- **Tapas length** - in the Critical Edition Vibhīṣaṇa **stood on one foot for 5,000 years** (at its end apsarases danced and flowers rained), then **for 5,000 more stood with head and arms raised, following the sun**, his mind fixed on recitation: 10,000 years in all, passed as if he were in Nandana (CE 7.10.6–9).
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata):* he lived on **a single withered leaf**, delighting in fasts and always intent on japa (MBh 3.259.17–18); a star passage adds 10,000 years on air for him and Rāvaṇa at Gokarṇa (MBh 3.259.19 *1236). [interpolation]
- **Vibhīṣaṇa's boon (Critical Edition)** - he made **one request**: that in whatever condition of life he found himself, every thought that arose in him be dharmic and that he keep that dharma (*yā yā me jāyate buddhir yeṣu yeṣv āśrameṣv iha / sā sā bhavatu dharmiṣṭhā*, CE 7.10.27–28). Brahmā granted it and, **unasked**, added immortality, "because, though born in a rākṣasa womb, your mind does not turn to adharma" (*nādharme jāyate buddhir amaratvaṃ dadāmi te*, CE 7.10.30). That unrequested grant is the textual origin of his cirañjīvī status. (Vālmīki, *Uttara Kāṇḍa* 10).
  - *Variant (Southern vulgate and Mahābhārata):* the request also includes the power to **wield the brahmāstra without having been taught it**. The vulgate gives three requests (steadfast dharma even in the greatest adversity, the untaught brahmāstra, and thoughts always conformed to virtue — Shastri, *Uttara* ch. 10); the Mahābhārata gives two: "even in utmost peril may my mind never turn to adharma, and may the brahmāstra, untaught, come to me" (*paramāpadgatasyāpi nādharme me matir bhavet / aśikṣitaṃ ca bhagavan brahmāstraṃ pratibhātu me*, MBh 3.259.30). In both, Brahmā answers with the same grant of immortality (MBh 3.259.31). The brahmāstra clause is not in the CE's Uttara 10.
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas):* asked by Brahmā to choose a boon, he asked for **pure love for the lotus feet of the Lord** (*tehiṃ māgeu bhagavaṃta pada kamala amala anurāgu*, *Bāl Kāṇḍ* dohā 177), recasting the boon as devotion rather than dharma. [late text]

## Family

- **Wife — [Saramā](./sarama.md)** - **daughter of the gandharva king Śailūṣa**, not a rākṣasī by birth; Rāvaṇa gave her to Vibhīṣaṇa in the round of marriages that also matched Kumbhakarṇa to Vajrajvālā and Śūrpaṇakhā to Vidyujjihva (CE 7.12.2, 7.12.20–22). Her name is an infant etymology: born by the Mānasa lake as its waters rose, she was saved when her mother cried out to the lake "do not rise!" (*saro mā vardhata*), and so became Saramā (CE 7.12.24). (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, *Uttara Kāṇḍa* 12). In the Aśoka grove a rākṣasī named **Saramā** consoles the captive Sītā and carries her Rāvaṇa's council deliberations, and it is **Saramā** — not Trijaṭā — whom Vālmīki credits with exposing the illusory severed head of Rāma as Rāvaṇa's māyā (VR 6.33–34 = CE 6.24–25). The war-book does not call her Vibhīṣaṇa's wife; that identification is made by commentators on the war-book (noted at VR 6.33) and by the Uttara's marriage list.
- **Sons** - unnamed in Vālmīki; announcing his defection, Vibhīṣaṇa says he has come "leaving my sons and my wife" (*tyaktvā putrāṃś ca dārāṃś ca*, VR 6.17.16 = CE 6.11.14).
  - *Variant (Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇ, Bengali):* a son, **Tāraṇīsen**, a devout Rāma-bhakta who fights on Rāvaṇa's side out of duty while chanting Rāma's name and is slain by Rāma. See [Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇ](../ramayana/recensions/krittivasi-ramayan.md). [regional]
- **Daughter — Analā** - **Vālmīki names her**: Sītā tells Hanumān that "Analā, the eldest daughter of Vibhīṣaṇa," sent to her by her own mother, told her that Vibhīṣaṇa had pleaded for her return and that Rāvaṇa would not consent (*jyeṣṭhā kanyānalā nāma vibhīṣaṇasutā kape / tayā mamaitad ākhyātaṃ mātrā prahitayā svayam*, VR 5.37.11 = CE 5.35.11; context 5.35.9–10). The sandhi *kanyānalā* can be divided as *kanyā Analā* or *kanyā Nalā*: the valmikiramayan.net gloss reads "Nalā", and this page follows the reading Analā. (Not to be confused with **Anala**, a male minister of his, below.)
  - *Variant:* other recensions name this informant-daughter **Kalā** or **Nandā** (Bulcke, "Sita's Friend Trijata," pp. 106–7, as summarised in Wikipedia, "Sarama (Ramayana)"). [disputed]
- **Daughter — [Trijaṭā](./trijata.md)?** - the kindly rākṣasī who guards Sītā in the Aśoka-vana, dreams of Rāma's victory, and reassures her (VR 5.27). **Vālmīki gives her no parentage at all.** The later identification is text-specific (Bulcke pp. 105–8, via Wikipedia, "Trijata"): she is Vibhīṣaṇa's daughter in the Kamba Rāmāyaṇa and in Govindarāja's commentary on Vālmīki; his **wife** in the *Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa*; his **sister** in Eknāth's Marathi *Bhāvārtha Rāmāyaṇa*; and Rāvaṇa's sister in the Jain *Vasudevahiṇḍī*. [late text]
  - *Trijaṭā's dream and Vibhīṣaṇa (vulgate only):* amid the ruin of Laṅkā she sees **Vibhīṣaṇa alone** under a white parasol, in white garlands and unguents, mounted on a four-tusked elephant and rising into the sky with four ministers — a forecast of his kingship (VR 5.27.31–33). The CE's version of the dream (CE 5.25) does not contain these verses.
- **The four ministers** - he always moves with four rākṣasa companions, but Vālmīki names them three ways: **Anala, Panasa, Sampāti, Pramati** (VR 6.37.7); **Anala, Śarabha, Sampāti, Praghasa** (CE 6.28.7); and in the Uttara's genealogy **Anala, Anila, Hara, Sampāti**, the sons of Mālī (Sumālī's brother) by the gandharvī Vasudā — "these are Vibhīṣaṇa's ministers" (CE 7.5.37–39). [open-ended roster]

## Before the War — Laṅkā, Kubera and the Sundara Kāṇḍa

- **Kubera's envoy** - when Kubera sends a messenger to reprove Rāvaṇa's outrages, it is Vibhīṣaṇa who receives him in Laṅkā with due honour and brings him to the court; Rāvaṇa answers with a sword-stroke and gives the envoy to his rākṣasas to eat (CE 7.13.11–14, 7.13.38). (Vālmīki, *Uttara Kāṇḍa* 13).
- **The abducted women and Kumbhīnasī** - returning from his conquests with captive wives of gods, dānavas and rākṣasas, Rāvaṇa is rebuked by Vibhīṣaṇa for conduct that destroys fame, wealth and family. He tells him that, while Indrajit was at a sacrifice and he himself was performing austerities under water, the rākṣasa **Madhu** carried off their cousin **Kumbhīnasī** (Mālyavān's daughter's daughter, a "sister by dharma"), and says that the wrong was borne and Madhu left unslain, since a girl must in any case be given to a husband. When Rāvaṇa marches on Madhu, "Vibhīṣaṇa, the righteous, stayed in Laṅkā practising dharma" (CE 7.25.18–28, 7.25.35). (Vālmīki, *Uttara Kāṇḍa* 25).
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata):* when Rāvaṇa drives Kubera out of Laṅkā, Vibhīṣaṇa, "remembering the dharma of the good," **follows Kubera** to Gandhamādana, and Kubera makes him commander of the yakṣa and rākṣasa armies (MBh 3.259.32–37).
- **Hanumān's search** - Hanumān searches Vibhīṣaṇa's mansion along with Kumbhakarṇa's (CE 5.5.17). Sītā tells Hanumān that Vibhīṣaṇa has earnestly entreated Rāvaṇa to return her, without effect (VR 5.37.9 = CE 5.35.9).
- **The envoy may not be killed** - when Rāvaṇa orders the captured Hanumān put to death, Vibhīṣaṇa objects that killing a messenger is against royal dharma and universal custom; disfigurement, whipping, shaving the head and branding are the punishments for an envoy, never death, and Rāvaṇa should punish those who sent him, not the messenger (VR 5.52.2–20 = CE 5.50). Rāvaṇa accepts, and the burning of Hanumān's tail follows (Hanumān's own report, CE 5.56.125–128).
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas):* Hanumān first meets Vibhīṣaṇa in Laṅkā, drawn to his house by the Hari shrine and tulasī, and learns from him how to find Sītā (*Sundar Kāṇḍ* 5–6). [late text]

## Counsel to Rāvaṇa (the Voice of Dharma)

- **Urging the return of Sītā** - as Rāvaṇa's commanders boast of killing Rāma, Vibhīṣaṇa restrains them: force is for when the three other means fail, against a careless or fate-stricken foe, not against a vigilant Rāma who has done Rāvaṇa no wrong; abducting his wife has brought great danger, and the refrain of his speech is "let Maithilī be given back to him" (*dīyatām asya maithilī*, CE 6.9.7–22; VR 6.9). (Vālmīki, *Yuddha Kāṇḍa*).
  - *Recension note:* the vulgate's further rounds of counsel — the ill omens in the city and palace (VR 6.10), the power of Rāma's arrows (VR 6.14), and the exchange with Indrajit (VR 6.15) — are not in the CE's constituted text, which passes from Vibhīṣaṇa's speech (CE 6.9) straight to Rāvaṇa's retort (CE 6.10 = VR 6.16). [vulgate only]
- **Reproof of Indrajit** - when Indrajit mocks him for fearing two men, Vibhīṣaṇa calls him an immature boy who, though called a son, is his father's enemy, and repeats that Sītā should be returned with gifts (VR 6.15). [vulgate only] They meet again at Nikumbhilā (below).
- **The fatal council** - Rāvaṇa, "driven by Time," answers that one may live with an enemy or an angry cobra but not with a foe who poses as a friend; kinsmen always rejoice at a kinsman's ruin; and "if anyone else had spoken so he would not live a moment — shame on you, disgrace of the family!" (*tvāṃ tu dhik kulapāṃsanam*, CE 6.10.1–11 = VR 6.16.1–16). Vibhīṣaṇa rises into the air, mace in hand, with four rākṣasas, and replies that an elder brother is like a father and must be honoured even off the path of dharma, but that he cannot bear such words; men who speak unwelcome truths are rare, he cannot ignore a man in Death's noose, and he leaves Rāvaṇa to guard the city without him (VR 6.16.17–27). In Vālmīki the rebuff is in words only.
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas):* Rāvaṇa **kicks** him (*asa kahi kīnhesi carana prahārā*), and Vibhīṣaṇa, clasping his feet, answers "you are like a father; it is well you struck me" before leaving through the air with his ministers (*Sundar Kāṇḍ*, caupāīs after dohā 40, and dohā 41). [late text]
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas):* Vibhīṣaṇa's counsel is cast in explicitly devotional terms — he extols Rāma as the Supreme Lord and urges Rāvaṇa to give up everything and worship him (*Sundar Kāṇḍ* dohā 38–40). [late text]
- **Rāvaṇa's regret** - after Kumbhakarṇa's death Rāvaṇa admits that Vibhīṣaṇa's good counsel, which he spurned, has come true, and that he drove out a righteous brother (CE 6.56.16–18). The queens and Mandodarī say the same in their laments (VR 6.94; 6.111.72, 79).

## Defection and Śaraṇāgati (Refuge with Rāma)

- **Leaving Laṅkā** - rejected by Rāvaṇa (VR 6.16 = CE 6.10), Vibhīṣaṇa crosses at once to the mainland with his four companions, and stops **in the air** above the northern shore. He announces himself as Rāvaṇa's younger brother, says he urged Sītā's return again and again, was reviled and humiliated "like a slave," and has come to Rāma for refuge, leaving his sons and wife (VR 6.17.1–17 = CE 6.11).
- **The council of acceptance** - Sugrīva calls him a spy, warns that rākṣasas are shape-shifters, and urges that he and his ministers be **killed** (VR 6.17.18–29). Asked for their views, **Aṅgada** wants him tested before he is trusted, **Śarabha** wants a spy set on him, **Jāmbavān** suspects him for coming at the wrong time and place, and **Mainda** wants him questioned gently. **Hanumān** alone argues for acceptance: questioning would only alienate a willing friend; there is no guile in his speech or face; and he has come because he sees Rāma's merit and Rāvaṇa's vice and hopes for the kingdom of the rākṣasas, Vālin's death and Sugrīva's crowning being his precedent (Aṅgada 17.38–42, Śarabha 17.43–44, Jāmbavān 17.45–46, Mainda 17.47–49, Hanumān 17.50–68). (Vālmīki, *Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 17).
- **Rāma's reply and Sugrīva's second objection** - Rāma says he will not abandon anyone who comes in friendship, even one with faults (VR 6.18.3 = CE 6.12.3). In the CE, Sugrīva at once agrees (CE 6.12.4–7). In the vulgate he objects again — "what does it matter whether he is good or bad, if he can desert his own brother in such a crisis? whom will he not betray?" (VR 6.18.5) — and Rāma answers with the politics of kinship (rival kinsmen strike in a crisis; Vibhīṣaṇa wants the kingdom; "not all brothers are like Bharata," VR 6.18.9–15) before Sugrīva's repeated demand to imprison him (VR 6.18.17–19). That exchange, VR 6.18.4–20, is [vulgate only].
- **Rāma's vow of refuge (the śaraṇāgati doctrine)** - whether he is good or bad, Rāma says, he can do Rāma no harm (VR 6.18.22 = CE 6.12.9); he recalls the **dove** that received the fowler who had taken its mate and fed him with its own flesh (VR 6.18.24–25 = CE 6.12.11), and the sage **Kaṇḍu**'s verses that an enemy who comes with joined palms seeking refuge must be protected even at the cost of one's life (VR 6.18.26–32 = CE 6.12.13–19). Then the vow: "To one who comes to me even once and says 'I am yours,' I give freedom from fear of all beings; this is my vow" (*sakṛd eva prapannāya tavāsmīti ca yācate / abhayaṃ sarvabhūtebhyo dadāmy etad vrataṃ mama*, VR 6.18.33 = CE 6.12.20) — "bring him, be he Vibhīṣaṇa or Rāvaṇa himself" (VR 6.18.34). This passage is the locus classicus of the Vaiṣṇava doctrine of self-surrender (prapatti). (Vālmīki, *Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 18; foundational for Śrīvaiṣṇava theology).
- **Anointed king-in-exile** - Vibhīṣaṇa descends, falls at Rāma's feet, and places "kingdom, life and happiness" at his disposal (VR 6.19.1–5). Asked about the enemy, he describes Rāvaṇa's boon, Kumbhakarṇa, Prahasta (who beat Maṇibhadra on Kailāsa), Indrajit's invisibility, the commanders and the host of Laṅkā (VR 6.19.8–16). Rāma swears by his brothers to kill Rāvaṇa and make Vibhīṣaṇa king (VR 6.19.19–21), and has **Lakṣmaṇa consecrate him king of the rākṣasas with sea-water** there among the vānaras, before the war has even begun (VR 6.19.24–26 = CE 6.13.7–9). Vibhīṣaṇa then advises that Rāma seek the help of the Ocean, dug by Rāma's ancestor Sagara (VR 6.19.30–31 = CE 6.13.13–14). The briefing and the oath (VR 6.19.6–22) are not in the CE, where the consecration follows directly on his surrender (CE 6.13.3–9). [vulgate only]
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas):* Rāma applies the *tilaka* himself, with sea-water, saying "though you have no craving, my sight never fails to bear fruit" (*Sundar Kāṇḍ* 49.4–5). [late text]
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata):* Vibhīṣaṇa comes **after the bridge is built**; Rāma welcomes him, Sugrīva suspects a spy, and Rāma honours him only after watching his conduct and bearing, then consecrates him over all the rākṣasas and makes him a counsellor and friend to Lakṣmaṇa; the crossing is made on his advice (MBh 3.267.46–50).
  - *Variant (motive):* the bhakti recensions stress that he defected out of devotion, recognising Rāma's divinity, softening any charge of treachery; in the *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa* he greets Rāma with a hymn of praise (see "Vibhīṣaṇa Gītā" below). [late text]

## Service in the War — Laṅkā's Secrets Revealed

- **Strategic intelligence** - as a rākṣasa insider Vibhīṣaṇa unmasks Rāvaṇa's spies **Śuka and Sāraṇa** in vānara disguise and seizes them, though Rāma lets them go (VR 6.25.13–21 = CE 6.16.13); of a second band of spies he has only **Śārdūla** seized as the wicked one (VR 6.29.25–27). His ministers scout Laṅkā and report the defence of each of its four gates (VR 6.37.6–20); Rāma places him at the centre of the army with Sugrīva and Jāmbavān and counts him among the seven who will fight in human form (VR 6.37.32–35). Rāma acts "with Vibhīṣaṇa's consent" in sending Aṅgada as envoy, and Aṅgada tells Rāvaṇa that Laṅkā's sovereignty will pass to Vibhīṣaṇa (CE 6.31.49, 57, 70). He identifies enemy champions for Rāma, among them Prahasta (VR 6.58.1–3) and Atikāya (CE 6.59.10–29). In the first general battle he duels the rākṣasa Mitraghna (CE 6.33.8).
- **The serpent-arrows** - when Indrajit binds Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa with serpent-arrows, Vibhīṣaṇa alone, "by māyā," sees his invisible nephew (CE 6.36.9–10 = VR 6.46.9–10). He consoles the weeping Sugrīva, wipes his eyes with a wet hand, and rallies the fleeing vānaras (VR 6.46.30–44 = CE 6.36.25–38); the vulgate adds that he first charmed the water with a spell (VR 6.46.35, not in the CE). When he returns mace in hand, the vānaras scatter, taking him for Indrajit, until Jāmbavān rallies them; he then weeps over the fallen princes (VR 6.50.7–20). Rāma, reviving, reproaches himself that his promise to make Vibhīṣaṇa king is still unkept (VR 6.49.22 = CE 6.39.22).
- **The night of the brahmāstra** - after Indrajit's Brahmā-weapon fells both brothers and sixty-seven crores of vānaras, Vibhīṣaṇa and Hanumān, still on their feet, search the field **with torches** (VR 6.74.7 = CE 6.61.7). They find the wounded Jāmbavān, who knows Vibhīṣaṇa by his voice and asks first whether Hanumān lives, because Hanumān alive means the army lives; Hanumān is sent to the Himālaya for the four herbs (VR 6.74.13–21 ff.). (Vālmīki, *Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 74).
- **The Nikumbhilā yajña secret** - when Indrajit kills a magical image of Sītā and Rāma collapses, Vibhīṣaṇa tells him it is impossible that Rāvaṇa would kill Sītā; it is a trick to cover Indrajit's withdrawal to the sanctuary called **Nikumbhilā**, where, once his oblations are complete, he will be invisible and unconquerable "even by the gods"; the counter is to strike **before the offering is finished**, and Lakṣmaṇa should go (VR 6.84.8–23). He gives the terms of Brahmā's boon: whoever strikes Indrajit before he reaches Nikumbhilā and completes the fire-offering will be his death (VR 6.85.14–15). Rāma sends him with Lakṣmaṇa as the one "who knows all Indrajit's sorceries" (VR 6.85.23). He directs the attack on the guarding host and leads Lakṣmaṇa to the dark **banyan** where Indrajit makes his offering to the spirits (VR 6.86.1–6; 6.87.1–6 = CE 6.74.3–6). Vālmīki gives the altar **no presiding deity**: the family-goddess reading, and her identification with Pratyaṅgirā, belong to the commentarial and tantric stratum. See [Nikumbhilā](../../06-sacred-geography/nikumbhila.md) and [Indrajit](./indrajit-meghnad.md).
- **Uncle and nephew** - seeing Vibhīṣaṇa, Indrajit taunts him: born and raised here, his father's own brother, he has betrayed kin for the enemy, and one who deserts his side is destroyed by the other side after his kin are gone. Vibhīṣaṇa answers that though born in a cruel race his nature is not a rākṣasa's; that one rightly shakes off a sinful man like a snake from the hand; that theft, another's wife and distrust of friends are the three destroying faults; and that because of these he left Rāvaṇa, and Indrajit will not reach the banyan alive (VR 6.87.10–30). In the duel he fights beside Lakṣmaṇa, rallies the vānaras, takes three of Indrajit's arrows in the face, **kills Indrajit's horses with his mace**, and has a spear meant for him cut down by Lakṣmaṇa (VR 6.89.1–19; 6.90.40–45); Lakṣmaṇa kills Indrajit with the Aindra weapon (VR 6.90). (Vālmīki, *Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 84–90).
- **Facing Rāvaṇa** - in the last battles Vibhīṣaṇa **kills Rāvaṇa's horses with his mace**; Rāvaṇa hurls a spear at him, which Lakṣmaṇa cuts in three, then readies a second, and Lakṣmaṇa covers Vibhīṣaṇa with arrows; Rāvaṇa turns on Lakṣmaṇa instead — "since you saved Vibhīṣaṇa" — and fells him with the bell-hung spear made by Maya (VR 6.100.17–36). (Vālmīki, *Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 100).
- **The secret of Rāvaṇa's death** - **in Vālmīki, Vibhīṣaṇa reveals no such secret and there is no amṛta.** When Rāma's arrows fail and the severed heads keep regrowing, it is **Mātali**, Indra's charioteer, who reminds Rāma of the Brahmā-made arrow Agastya gave him on the field; it takes Rāvaṇa in the chest, tears out his heart, and returns to the quiver. (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 108.1–4, 108.17–20).
  - *Variant (Rāmcaritmānas and regional Rāmāyaṇas):* the nectar of immortality is lodged in Rāvaṇa's **navel** (*nābhikuṃḍa piyūṣa basa yākeṃ*), and it is Vibhīṣaṇa who discloses it; Rāma's arrow dries up the navel-pool (*sāyaka eka nābhi sara soṣā*) (*Laṅkā Kāṇḍ*, caupāīs after dohā 101 and after dohā 102). This is the version most retellings and stage traditions carry, and the disclosure is Vibhīṣaṇa's signature act in them — but it is not the Vālmīki text. [late text]
- **Last rites for Rāvaṇa** - in the vulgate, Vibhīṣaṇa first **laments** his fallen brother — what he foretold has come to pass — and praises his generosity, valour, sacred fires and Vedic learning (*… 'hitāgniś ca mahātapāś ca vedāntagaḥ*, VR 6.109.1–23). That whole sarga is [vulgate only]: the CE passes from Rāvaṇa's death (CE 6.97) to the queens' lament (CE 6.98) and Mandodarī's (CE 6.99.1–29). When Rāma bids him perform the obsequies, he **refuses** to honour a cruel, lying violator of others' wives — "an enemy in the form of a brother" — while deferring to Rāma; Rāma answers that Rāvaṇa, whatever his sins, was mighty and never beaten by the gods, that "enmities end at death" (*maraṇāntāni vairāṇi*), and that "he is mine as much as yours" (CE 6.99.30–40 = VR 6.111.93–102). Vibhīṣaṇa lights the pyre with due rites and consoles the queens (CE 6.99.41–43); in the vulgate Mālyavān joins him for the rites (VR 6.111.108). In the CE the "enmities end at death" line comes once, after the refusal; the vulgate also has it after the eulogy (VR 6.109.25).
  - *Variant (Jain, Paumacariya):* at Rāvaṇa's death Vibhīṣaṇa tries to kill himself, and Rāma restrains and comforts him (Paumacariya canto 74, per Kulkarni p. 58). [variant]

## Coronation as King of Laṅkā

- **Consecration** - after Mātali departs, Rāma tells Lakṣmaṇa that it is his dearest wish to see Vibhīṣaṇa consecrated in Laṅkā; Lakṣmaṇa sprinkles him from a **golden pot** (filled, in the vulgate, with sea-water fetched by the vānaras) amid the rākṣasas, and his ministers and loyal rākṣasas rejoice. Vibhīṣaṇa reassures his subjects and offers the citizens' auspicious gifts to Rāma (CE 6.100.8–17 = VR 6.112.9–22). (Vālmīki, *Yuddha Kāṇḍa*).
- **Bringing Sītā** - Rāma sends Vibhīṣaṇa to fetch Sītā; when she asks to come without bathing, he tells her to do as her husband commands, and brings her bathed and adorned in a palanquin. When his attendants drive back the crowd, Rāma rebukes him angrily and has Sītā come on foot to be seen by all (VR 6.114.6–34). In his speech of rejection Rāma names Vibhīṣaṇa among those she may now turn to (VR 6.115.22–23). (Yuddha Kāṇḍa).
- **Honoured guest at Ayodhyā** - Vibhīṣaṇa begs Rāma to stay and accept his hospitality; Rāma declines, and Vibhīṣaṇa summons the **Puṣpaka**, the aerial car of "Kubera my brother" seized by Rāvaṇa (VR 6.121.1–29). At Rāma's word he showers the vānaras with gold and gems; Rāma bids him rule the Laṅkā he has given him, but at the allies' plea lets him and his ministers board the Puṣpaka for Ayodhyā (VR 6.122.1–23). In the procession into Ayodhyā, and again at the coronation, he holds a moon-white whisk over Rāma (VR 6.128.28–29, 6.128.68–69), and after the gift-giving he returns to Laṅkā with his rākṣasas (VR 6.128.86–90). (Yuddha Kāṇḍa).
- **Mention in the Mahābhārata** - Vibhīṣaṇa is named in the *Mahābhārata*'s Rāmopākhyāna retelling and elsewhere as the dharmic survivor of the Rākṣasa line. See [Rāmopākhyāna](../ramopakhyana.md) and "The Mahābhārata's Vibhīṣaṇa" below. (Mahābhārata, *Vana Parva*).

## After the War — the Uttara Kāṇḍa

- **Gifts and farewell** - Rāma shares the allies' gifts with Sugrīva and Vibhīṣaṇa (CE 7.38.10–12), then charges him: "rule Laṅkā by dharma; never set your mind on adharma; remember me always, with Sugrīva," and embraces him at parting (CE 7.39.8–11, 7.39.23). (Vālmīki, *Uttara Kāṇḍa* 38–39).
- **Rāma's Aśvamedha** - invited with his rākṣasas (CE 7.82.10), Vibhīṣaṇa waits on the ascetics at the horse-sacrifice "as a servant" (*kiṃkaraḥ*), while the vānaras serve the brahmins food (CE 7.83.7–8). (Vālmīki, *Uttara Kāṇḍa*).

## Immortality and the Cirañjīvī Status

- **Two grants in Vālmīki** - the first is **Brahmā's**, unasked, at the boon-scene of *Uttara* 10 (CE 7.10.30; above). The second is **Rāma's charge at the Mahāprasthāna**: as the rākṣasas, bears and vānaras ask to follow him, Rāma tells Vibhīṣaṇa that "as long as creatures endure" he is to stay in Laṅkā and hold his realm, protecting his subjects by dharma, "and do not answer me" (*yāvat prajā dhariṣyanti tāvat tvaṃ vai vibhīṣaṇa / rākṣasendra mahāvīrya laṅkāsthaḥ svaṃ dhariṣyasi*, CE 7.98.21–23). In the same scene Hanumān's term — to live as long as Rāma's story is told — is reaffirmed (CE 7.98.24–25), having first been granted at the allies' farewell (CE 7.39.16–19). That is not a Kalpa-term, and it is not at the coronation. (Vālmīki, *Uttara Kāṇḍa* 108 vulgate = CE 7.98) — see [Uttara Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md) and [The Cirañjīvīs](../the-chiranjivis.md).
  - *Vulgate wording:* Shastri's vulgate adds that his empire is to last **as long as the moon and sun shine and men speak of Rāma**, and that he is to **worship Jagannātha, the family deity of the Ikṣvākus** (Shastri, *Uttara* ch. 108); the same chapter gives Jāmbavān a term "until the Kali Yuga begins." None of these three clauses is in CE 7.98. [vulgate only]
- **Cirañjīvī of the Rāma cycle** - the later roster-tradition restates the grant as life until the end of the present Kali Yuga, charged with guiding people toward truth and dharma — a different term from Vālmīki's, and the one the *stotra* literature carries. (Cirañjīvī tradition) [late text]
- **The Cirañjīvī verse** - he appears in the *Sapta-/Aṣṭa-Cirañjīvī Stotram* alongside Aśvatthāman, Bali, Vyāsa, Hanumān, Vibhīṣaṇa, Kṛpa, and Paraśurāma (and Mārkaṇḍeya in eight-fold lists); daily remembrance is said to confer health and longevity. (Stotra tradition). [variant rosters of 7 vs 8 names]

## The Mahābhārata's Vibhīṣaṇa

The Mahābhārata's own Rāma story (Mārkaṇḍeya's Rāmopākhyāna, *Āraṇyaka Parva* 3.258–275) gives Vibhīṣaṇa a larger fighting role than Vālmīki does. These are its variants, not Vālmīki's:

- **Birth and boon** - son of Mālinī, the boon of the untaught brahmāstra and unasked immortality, and service under Kubera (MBh 3.259.8–9, 3.259.30–37; above).
- **Against invisible enemies** - when piśācas and lesser rākṣasas attack unseen, Vibhīṣaṇa, who knows the means, destroys their invisibility so the vānaras can kill them (MBh 3.269.1–4). After Indrajit's arrows fell the princes, he revives them with the *prajñāstra*, and brings water sent by Kubera through a guhyaka from the White Mountain: touched to the eyes, it lets Rāma, Lakṣmaṇa and the vānara chiefs see invisible beings (MBh 3.273.5–14).
  - A star passage has Vibhīṣaṇa revive Lakṣmaṇa, struck by Rāvaṇa's spear, with divine herbs fetched by Hanumān (MBh 3.273.14 *1290). [interpolation]
- **He kills Prahasta** - Vibhīṣaṇa and Prahasta exchange arrows; Prahasta strikes him with a mace and he stands unmoved, then hurls a great spear hung with a hundred bells and takes off Prahasta's head (MBh 3.269.13–3.270.4). In Vālmīki it is the vānara **Nīla** who kills [Prahasta](./prahasta.md) (VR 6.58).
- **Indrajit and Rāvaṇa** - Lakṣmaṇa attacks Indrajit "on Vibhīṣaṇa's counsel" before he has performed his daily rites (*akṛtāhnikam*, MBh 3.273.16–17). When Mātali brings Indra's chariot, Rāma suspects a rākṣasa trick, and Vibhīṣaṇa assures him it is no māyā of Rāvaṇa's (MBh 3.274.12–17).
- **After the war** - Rāma gives Laṅkā to Vibhīṣaṇa, Sītā is brought out with Vibhīṣaṇa going before her, and he accompanies Rāma by Puṣpaka to Kiṣkindhā and Ayodhyā before being sent home after the coronation (MBh 3.275.5–6, 3.275.51–56, 3.275.66).
- **Sahadeva's southern conquest** - from Bharukaccha, Sahadeva sends envoys to "the great-souled Paulastya, Vibhīṣaṇa," who accepts his command as the work of Time and sends gems, sandalwood, aloe and divine ornaments for Yudhiṣṭhira's Rājasūya (MBh 2.28.50–52). The longer telling in which **Ghaṭotkaca** is the envoy and is received by Vibhīṣaṇa on a golden throne is Appendix I No. 15 to the Sabhā Parva (after MBh 2.28.53), and Vibhīṣaṇa's attendance at Kubera's court is Appendix I No. 3 (after MBh 2.10.22). [interpolation] See [Ghaṭotkaca](./ghatotkacha.md).

## Later Legends and Regional Variants

- **[Śrīraṅgam](../../06-sacred-geography/srirangam-ranganatha.md) / Ranganātha legend** - returning south to Laṅkā, Vibhīṣaṇa carries the Raṅga-vimāna icon of Viṣṇu (Ranganātha) gifted by Rāma; he sets it down at Śrīraṅgam (Tiruchirāpalli) and it refuses to be lifted again, so the deity remains there facing south toward Laṅkā, while Vibhīṣaṇa is held to journey to worship it perpetually as an immortal devotee. (Śrīraṅga-māhātmya; Śrīraṅgam Ranganāthaswāmy temple sthala-purāṇa). [folk] The nearest Vālmīki text is the vulgate's charge to worship Jagannātha, the Ikṣvāku family deity (Shastri, *Uttara* ch. 108; not in the CE).
  - *Variant:* Vibhīṣaṇa gives the vimāna to a local king, **Dharmavarma**, a Chola, who is also remembered as the temple's first builder (Wikipedia, "Ranganathaswamy Temple, Srirangam," summarising the temple legend). [folk]
  - *Variant (the Gaṇeśa trick):* Gaṇeśa, as a cowherd boy, sets the vimāna down while Vibhīṣaṇa bathes; the Ucchi Piḷḷaiyār shrine on Tiruchirappalli's Rock Fort marks the spot. See [Śrīraṅgam](../../06-sacred-geography/srirangam-ranganatha.md). [regional]
- **Vibhīṣaṇa Gītā** - the name is given to two different passages, which should not be merged:
  - *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa:* the *Vibhīṣaṇagītā* is **Vibhīṣaṇa's hymn of praise to Rāma** when Sugrīva brings him before Rāma after his flight (*Yuddha Kāṇḍa* 3.13–37). Offered a boon, he asks not for pleasures but for knowledge, meditation and "devotion always fixed at your lotus feet" (*tvatpādakamale saktā bhaktir eva sadāstu me*, 3.37). See [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md). [late text]
  - *Rāmcaritmānas:* in the last battle Vibhīṣaṇa is distressed to see Rāvaṇa in a chariot and Rāma on foot (*rāvanu rathī biratha raghubīrā*), and Rāma describes the **chariot of dharma** that wins the war — courage and fortitude its wheels, truth and virtue its banner and flag (*Laṅkā Kāṇḍ*, caupāīs after dohā 79). This is the passage popularly published as the "Vibhīṣaṇa Gītā." See [Rāmcaritmānas](../ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md). [late text]
- **Kamba Rāmāyaṇam (Tamil)** - heightens the pathos of his break with Rāvaṇa and the nobility of his surrender, a key episode in the Tamil tradition; it is among the texts that make Trijaṭā his daughter (Bulcke). See [Kamban's Irāmāvatāram](../ramayana/recensions/kamban-iramavataram.md). [variant]
- **Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇ (Bengali)** - besides his son Tāraṇīsen, it gives him the counsel that Rāma offer 108 blue lotuses to Durgā for victory. See [Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇ](../ramayana/recensions/krittivasi-ramayan.md). [regional]
- **Jain Rāmāyaṇa (Vimalasūri's *Paumacariya*)** - the rākṣasas are a Vidyādhara dynasty, and the story runs differently (canto numbers per V. M. Kulkarni's summary). Hearing a prophecy that Rāvaṇa will die at the hands of Daśaratha's son because of Janaka's daughter, Vibhīṣaṇa vows to remove the danger and goes to Ayodhyā to kill Daśaratha, but strikes down only a clay replica the ministers had made (canto 23). Later he pleads with Rāvaṇa to give up Sītā (46), is abused by Rāvaṇa and Indrajit and expelled, and goes with his army to Haṃsadvīpa, where Rāma accepts him (55). **Lakṣmaṇa, as Nārāyaṇa, kills Rāvaṇa with the discus** after Vibhīṣaṇa's last appeal (73); Vibhīṣaṇa then attempts suicide and is restrained (74), and he hosts Rāma at his mansion with its shrine of the Jina Padmaprabha and prepares Rāma's abhiṣeka, which Rāma declines for Bharata's sake (77). See [Jain and Buddhist Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/recensions/jain-and-buddhist-ramayanas.md). [variant]
- **Southeast Asian Rāmāyaṇas** - he appears as **Phiphek** in the Thai *Ramakien*, the astrologer-brother who reads Sīdā's birth as fatal to the rākṣasa race, so that she is set adrift; and as **Bibasenam** in the Malay *Hikayat Seri Rama*, where he foretells that Mahārāja Wana's life and death hang on her. In such tellings his foreknowledge of Laṅkā's fate is central. See [Southeast Asian Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/recensions/southeast-asian-ramayanas.md). [folk]
- **Devotional veneration** - regarded as a model of the devotee who chooses dharma and the Lord over kin and kingdom; invoked in śaraṇāgati liturgy and named in the cirañjīvī morning remembrance. [late text]
