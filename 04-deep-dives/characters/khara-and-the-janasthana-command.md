# Khara and the Janasthāna Command

> **Khara**, Rāvaṇa's maternal cousin and rākṣasa overlord of **Janasthāna** — the standing garrison Rāvaṇa planted in the [Daṇḍaka](../../06-sacred-geography/dandakaranya-rama-forest.md) to protect his widowed sister — and the twelve-sarga war in which **[Rāma](./rama.md), alone**, destroys him, his commander-in-chief **Dūṣaṇa**, his captain **Triśiras**, fifteen named chiefs and **fourteen thousand** rākṣasas in a muhūrta and a half. This file is the command's dossier: the Uttara-kāṇḍa posting that creates it, the full officer roster, the battle sarga by sarga, and the disambiguations the corpus needs (Khara is **not** Rāvaṇa's brother; Dūṣaṇa and Triśiras are **not** [Śūrpaṇakhā's](./shurpanakha.md) brothers; there are **two** Triśiras in the epic).
> Sits in **[Tretā-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md). The war is [Araṇya-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md) 19–30; the posting is *Uttara-kāṇḍa* 23–24; the sequel is his son [Makarākṣa](../makaraksha-son-of-khara.md) at *Yuddha* 78–79.
> Up: [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md) · Demon-kind: [rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md) · Antagonist-master: [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md).

**Khara** (Sanskrit *Khara*, "harsh, sharp, cruel"; also the word for an ass) is the rākṣasa Rāvaṇa installs over **Janasthāna**, the demon-held district of the Daṇḍaka around the Godāvarī in which Rāma will build his hut at [Pañcavaṭī](../../06-sacred-geography/panchavati-nashik-godavari.md). He is the first military power in the epic to move against Rāma, and the only enemy commander Rāma destroys **without Lakṣmaṇa** — Vālmīki removes the brother and Sītā to a cave expressly so that the count is unambiguous. His war is not a skirmish: it runs twelve sargas, fields a named officer corps of fifteen, and ends with Rāma standing alone over fourteen thousand dead. Everything the epic does afterward is a consequence of it. Śūrpaṇakhā carries the news to Laṅkā; Akampana carries the strategy; Rāvaṇa's first thought of Sītā, the coercion of [Mārīca](./maricha.md), the abduction and the war all begin at Janasthāna's annihilation. Khara is also the last enemy Rāma kills as a private man — after this, he is at war with a kingdom.

---

## I. Who Khara is — the kinship the epic states once

- **Not Rāvaṇa's brother** — Vālmīki states the relation exactly once and exactly: consoling the widowed Śūrpaṇakhā, Rāvaṇa calls Khara *"the son of the sister of your mother"* — the son of one of **Kaikasī's** sisters, and so Rāvaṇa's and Śūrpaṇakhā's **maternal cousin** (*Uttara* 24). He is **no son of Viśravas**: Vālmīki's sons of Viśravas are Kubera by Ilavidā, and Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa, Śūrpaṇakhā and Vibhīṣaṇa by Kaikasī (*Uttara* 9). The Araṇya-kāṇḍa's loose vocative "brother" for a cousin is idiom, not pedigree, and reading it as pedigree is the oldest and commonest error about him. → [rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md) · [Makarākṣa, son of Khara](../makaraksha-son-of-khara.md).
- **His branch of the house** — the claim runs through **Kaikasī's line**, the **Sumālī** side, not through the Paulastya/Viśravas side. Khara is Laṅkā's kin by the mother, which is precisely why he is the right man to be given charge of Rāvaṇa's sister.
- **What he holds** — **Janasthāna**, the Daṇḍaka district; his command is a garrison of **fourteen thousand** rākṣasas "obedient to my will," with the forest's ascetics as their prey (*Araṇya* 22.8). He is a provincial warlord with a standing army, not a courtier.
- **His son** — **Makarākṣa**, who comes out of Laṅkā years later expressly to avenge him and dies the same death, weapon for weapon (*Yuddha* 78–79). → [Makarākṣa](../makaraksha-son-of-khara.md).

## II. The posting — why the garrison exists (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 23–24)

- **The marriage** — in the round of dynastic matches that also gives Mandodarī to Rāvaṇa and Saramā to Vibhīṣaṇa, Śūrpaṇakhā is married to **Vidyujjihva**, a lord of the **Kālakeya** dānavas (*Uttara* 12).
- **The killing** — in Rāvaṇa's later destruction of the Kālakeya host, Vidyujjihva falls by Rāvaṇa's own hand, and Śūrpaṇakhā comes to her brother grieving that he has made her a widow (*Uttara* 23). Whether the killing was inadvertent — Vidyujjihva unrecognised in the melee — or knowing, the text does not say; the apologetic reading is the commoner one. [disputed]
- **The consolation-grant** — to appease her, Rāvaṇa gives his sister the **free range of the Daṇḍaka forest** and installs Khara there as her protector: *"having spoken thus, Daśagrīva placed an army of fourteen thousand rākṣasas under Khara's command,"* and Khara went down to Daṇḍaka and established his authority, Śūrpaṇakhā dwelling beside him (*Uttara* 24). **Dūṣaṇa** goes with him as commander.
- **Why this matters to the plot** — the Janasthāna garrison is not a random demon-nest. It is **Rāvaṇa's own arrangement for his sister's comfort**, staffed with his mother's kin. When Rāma destroys it, he has not merely cleared a forest; he has erased a standing act of the king's family policy, and the insult is structural. That is the connective tissue between Śūrpaṇakhā's mutilation and a naval war. → [Śūrpaṇakhā](./shurpanakha.md).

## III. The command roster — fifteen named officers

Vālmīki names Khara's officer corps in three tiers, and the roster is one of the fullest in the epic outside the Laṅkā war.

- **Dūṣaṇa** — Khara's **senāpati**, "army commander": the text's own word, *khara sena patim* (*Araṇya* 22.7). It is Dūṣaṇa who is ordered to muster the fourteen thousand and bring up Khara's chariot. He commands the second wave in person with a division of five thousand, and dies when Rāma **severs both his arms** as he charges with an uprooted mace (*Araṇya* 22, 26).
  - *Variant (brother/twin):* demon-catalogs and retellings commonly pair Dūṣaṇa with Khara as **brother or twin**. Vālmīki gives him no parentage at all and only the office; the fraternity is a later tidying of a title into a family. [folk]
- **Triśiras** ("three-headed") — the third of the standing command. He **asks Khara's leave to fight Rāma first**, swearing on his weapon, and offers Khara the role of umpire: *"be the judge of whether I become his death, or he mine"* (*Araṇya* 27).
- **Dūṣaṇa's three chiefs** — **Mahākapāla**, **Sthūlākṣa** and **Pramāthin**, who charge together after their commander falls and are killed one after another in the same sarga (*Araṇya* 26.17–22).
- **The twelve captains** — after the three, Khara orders forward **twelve** chiefs at once, and Rāma kills all twelve and the remainder of the host with them (*Araṇya* 26.26–28):

  **Śyenagāmī** · **Pṛthugrīva** · **Yajñaśatru** · **Vihaṅgama** · **Durjaya** · **Karavīrākṣa** · **Paruṣa** · **Kālakārmuka** · **Hemamālī** · **Mahāmālī** · **Sarpāsya** · **Rudhirāśana**

  - *Note (the last name):* printings differ between **Rudhirāśana** ("blood-eater") and **Rudhirākṣa** ("blood-eyed"); the corpus records the first with the second flagged. The other eleven are stable across the vulgate witnesses. [scholarly]
- **The fourteen** — a separate, earlier detachment: the **fourteen rākṣasas** Khara sends out first with Śūrpaṇakhā as guide, unnamed, killed by Rāma with fourteen arrows (*Araṇya* 19–20).
- **The fourteen thousand** — the garrison itself, *kāma-rūpiṇaḥ*, "wearing what shapes they will" (*Araṇya* 22.8, 30.31).
- **Akampana** — the one rākṣasa of Janasthāna the roster does not account for, because he **runs**. He escapes the field and reaches Laṅkā first, and it is his counsel — take the woman, and grief will kill the man — that puts the abduction into Rāvaṇa's head before Śūrpaṇakhā ever arrives (*Araṇya* 31). The survivor is the most consequential member of the command. → [Mārīca §IV](./maricha.md).

## IV. The provocation (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 17–21)

- **Śūrpaṇakhā mutilated** — she propositions Rāma at Pañcavaṭī, is deflected to Lakṣmaṇa and back, turns on Sītā, and Lakṣmaṇa **cuts off her ears and nose** (*Araṇya* 17–18). → [Śūrpaṇakhā](./shurpanakha.md).
- **She reaches Khara** — blood-soaked, she falls before him, describes the two brothers "in the flush of youth, handsome, gentle and greatly mighty," describes **Sītā** at length, and demands to **drink the blood of all three** (*Araṇya* 19). The demand for Sītā's blood is already, in embryo, the demand for Sītā.
- **The first fourteen** — Khara detaches **fourteen** rākṣasas to go with her and kill the three. Rāma annihilates them; she comes back to her cousin shrieking a second time and taunts him with the failure (*Araṇya* 20–21). The two-stage provocation is deliberate: Khara's honour is engaged twice before he moves himself.

## V. The march (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 22–24)

- **The muster** — Khara calls up **Dūṣaṇa** by office and orders the fourteen thousand made ready — "trained, obedient to my will, ardent in war, never retreating" — and his own chariot brought (*Araṇya* 22.7–8).
- **The chariot** — Vālmīki dwells on it: a car with a cabin roofed like a ridge of Meru, gold-plated body, golden wheels, gem-set fittings, worked with **fish, flowers, trees, hills, sun and moon and auspicious birds**, hung with bells, flying flags and racked with swords, drawn by well-bred horses (*Araṇya* 22). It is the most elaborately described vehicle in the kāṇḍa, and it is scrap by sarga 28.
- **The omens** — the march is met by the fullest omen-catalogue in the Rāmāyaṇa: an **ass-coloured cloud raining bloody water**; a black corona ringed with red about the sun and a **headless trunk-shape** beside it; stars visible like fireflies in daylight; meteors falling with a roar; an eagle settling on the golden flagstaff; carrion birds and vultures massing; **she-jackals howling** — the sign the text names as specifically inauspicious to demons in war; Khara's own **horses stumbling on level, flower-strewn road**; his **left shoulder throbbing**, his voice quavering, his eyes watering, his forehead aching; the earth quaking (*Araṇya* 23).
- **Khara's answer to them** — he brushes the lot aside: the portents are dreadful, "but by my own valour I am unbothered" — he could challenge **Indra** himself, and will not turn back (*Araṇya* 23). The refusal of a legible omen is Vālmīki's standard mark of a doomed commander, and Khara delivers the purest instance of it in the epic.
- **Rāma clears the field** — hearing the host, Rāma sends **Lakṣmaṇa with Sītā into an inaccessible mountain cave**, bow strung and arrows to hand, over Lakṣmaṇa's objection: *"I long to destroy all these night-walkers myself"* (*Araṇya* 24). He reads his own omens — his right arm throbbing, Lakṣmaṇa's face brightened — as victory. Then he buckles on his armour and takes up the bow, and Vālmīki says the man **whose deeds harm no one has taken on the aspect of wrathful Rudra**; gods and gandharvas come down to watch one man face fourteen thousand (*Araṇya* 24).

## VI. The battle (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 25–27)

- **The first clash and the gāndharva missile** — the host closes from every side; Rāma warns them, then answers with arrows in torrents, and at the height of it **launches the gāndharva-astra** into the massed demons (*Araṇya* 25.37). The text is pointed that **no god intervenes on his side** — the whole battle is fought as a man's battle.
- **Dūṣaṇa's charge and death** — Dūṣaṇa brings up **five thousand** of his own; Rāma breaks them, and when Dūṣaṇa comes on with a mace, Rāma **cuts off both his arms** and he falls dead — the same dismemberment-then-fall that Rāma's arrows had dealt Tāṭakā and will deal Kabandha (*Araṇya* 26).
- **The three, then the twelve** — **Mahākapāla, Sthūlākṣa and Pramāthin** charge over their commander's body and are killed in sequence; Khara then throws in the **twelve captains**, and Rāma kills them and what remains of the army with them. When the sarga ends only **Khara and Triśiras are left standing** on a field of fourteen thousand (*Araṇya* 26).
- **Triśiras** — he takes his turn by request, strikes Rāma on the forehead, and is answered with contempt — the blows fall "like flowers." Rāma then dismantles him by parts: **fourteen arrows** into his chest, **four** to kill the horses, **eight** for the charioteer, **one** to cut down the flag; and as Triśiras leaps from the wrecked car, Rāma tears his chest open and **takes the three heads with three arrows** (*Araṇya* 27). The surviving demons break; Khara has to rally them himself.

## VII. The duel and the death (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 28–30)

- **Khara comes on** — shaken by both deaths, he attacks "as the demon Namuci went at Indra." He fights well: he damages Rāma's armour with seven arrows and follows with a thousand more (*Araṇya* 28).
- **The chariot stripped** — Rāma answers with the **bow Agastya gave him**, and takes the vehicle apart in order — flagstaff, yoke, the four horses, the charioteer, and finally **Khara's bow**. Chariot-less, horseless, driverless, Khara comes to the ground with a mace, and the watching gods worship (*Araṇya* 28).
- **The speech that precedes the kill** — Rāma tells him why he is dying: that cruelty to harmless beings, above all to the **sages of Daṇḍaka**, ripens fast, like poisoned food — the vow of *Araṇya* 6 spoken back over the man it was sworn against. Khara answers that real warriors do not praise themselves before a fight, that he is an **unshakable, jagged mountain**, and that he cannot go home with fourteen thousand of his own unavenged (*Araṇya* 29). Then he hurls the blazing mace, and Rāma **shatters it in the air** before it can fall (*Araṇya* 29).
- **The sāla tree** — weaponless, Khara **uproots a great sāla tree** with both hands, braying, and throws it; Rāma cuts it down and wounds him (*Araṇya* 30).
- **The arrow** — Rāma then fits the shaft the text is careful to source: an arrow **like the sacrificial fire, second only to the Brahmāstra**, which **Indra had given to Agastya, and Agastya to Rāma**; it goes out like a thunderclap and **enters Khara's chest** (*Araṇya* 30.25). This is the **Indra-given** arrow of Agastya's four-item deposit, distinct from the single **Brahmā-given** arrow that will kill Rāvaṇa at *Yuddha* 108. Vālmīki keeps the two donors apart, and the object registry follows him. → [Agastya §5](../agastya.md) · [Divyāstras](../../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md).
- **The tally and the time** — Vālmīki closes with the arithmetic: *artha-adhika-muhūrtena* — **in a muhūrta and a half** — Rāma's sharp arrows destroyed **fourteen thousand shape-shifting rākṣasas, with Khara and Dūṣaṇa at their head** (*Araṇya* 30.30–31). The gods beat their celestial drums and rain flowers, and the sages of the forest come down to bless the man who kept the promise of sarga 6.

## VIII. What the annihilation sets off (Rām. Araṇya-kāṇḍa 31–34)

- **Akampana first** — the escapee reaches Laṅkā ahead of everyone, reports Janasthāna's destruction, and proposes the abduction as strategy: Rāma cannot be beaten in arms, so **take Sītā and let grief do it**. Rāvaṇa flies to Mārīca on that advice, is talked out of it, and goes home (*Araṇya* 31).
  - *Textual note:* sarga 31 stands in the vulgate this corpus cites and is **dropped from the Baroda Critical Edition**; a Critical-Edition reader gets only one visit to Mārīca and no Akampana. [disputed] [interpolation]
- **Śūrpaṇakhā second** — she crosses to Laṅkā and confronts Rāvaṇa in open court, taunting him with negligence: **Janasthāna has fallen, Khara and Dūṣaṇa are dead, and a man did it** — then changes his motive from policy to desire by describing Sītā (*Araṇya* 32–34). What Akampana could not do with strategy she does with a description.
- **The revenge that closes the loop** — years later at Laṅkā, Khara's son **Makarākṣa** rides out naming his father, and Rāma answers by reciting the Janasthāna tally back at him before killing him with the Āgneyāstra (*Yuddha* 78–79). → [Makarākṣa, son of Khara](../makaraksha-son-of-khara.md).

## IX. Disambiguations

- **Two Triśiras** — the three-headed **Triśiras of Janasthāna**, Khara's captain, killed by **Rāma** in the forest (*Araṇya* 27), is a different being from the three-headed **Triśiras of Laṅkā**, a **son of Rāvaṇa**, killed by **Hanumān** in the war-book alongside Devāntaka (*Yuddha* 70). The corpus keeps them apart; retellings routinely merge them. [scholarly] → [Yuddha Kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md).
- **Neither Dūṣaṇa nor Triśiras is Śūrpaṇakhā's brother** — only **Khara** is kin to her, and as a cousin. Tellings that make all three "her brothers" are importing the Araṇya-kāṇḍa's idiom and reading it as descent.
- **"Brahmadatta" is not the arrow's name** — *brahma-datta* is the adjective "Brahmā-given," and it belongs to the *other* arrow of the deposit. The shaft that kills Khara is the **Indra-given** one (*Araṇya* 30). Four items, three donors, and no proper names among them.
- **The Jain re-casting** — in Vimalasūri's ***Paümacariya***, the earliest Jain Rāmāyaṇa, "Khara-Dūṣaṇa" is a **single figure**, ruler of Pātālaṅkā and husband of **Candraṇakhā** (the Jain Śūrpaṇakhā) — the tradition compresses the command into one man and makes him her husband rather than her guardian. [scholarly] → [Jain & Buddhist Rāmāyaṇas](../ramayana/recensions/jain-and-buddhist-ramayanas.md).
- **The Rāmopākhyāna** — the Mahābhārata's condensed Rāma-story makes Śūrpaṇakhā the **uterine sister of Khara**, a different mother-scheme from Vālmīki's; the corpus records it as that text's own, not as background to Vālmīki's. [disputed] → [Rāmopākhyāna](../ramopakhyana.md).

## X. Why Khara matters

- **The proof of concept** — Janasthāna is where the epic establishes, in front of witnesses it summons for the purpose, that **one man with Agastya's arms can annihilate an army**. Every later assessment of Rāma — Śūrpaṇakhā's to Rāvaṇa, Mārīca's, Rāvaṇa's own — cites this battle as evidence. It is the epic taking the measure of its own hero, once, in public.
- **The garrison, not the man, is the target** — Rāma is not avenging an insult; he is executing the vow of *Araṇya* 6 on the exact force the sages complained of. The Khara war is the **only** time in the epic that Rāma's violence is discharged directly against the thing he promised to destroy, with no personal loss driving it. Everything after Pañcavaṭī is personal.
- **The doomed commander as a type** — omens read and dismissed, a magnificent chariot, a boast against Indra, a stripped car, an uprooted tree, a shattered mace, a chest-shot. Vālmīki reuses the whole sequence on **Makarākṣa** deliberately, and echoes it on Rāvaṇa; Khara is the template. [scholarly]
