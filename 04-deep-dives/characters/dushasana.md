# Duhshasana

> Where this sits: a **Dvāpara-era life** on the [Dvāpara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)→[Kali](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) cusp — the second of the hundred Kauravas and [Duryodhana](duryodhana.md)'s right hand, the man who drags [Draupadī](draupadi.md) by the hair into the dice-hall and attempts her disrobing, and whose blood [Bhīma](bhima.md) vows to drink. Two clocks: the epic narrates *itihāsa* (war trad. ~3138 BCE; Kali begins 3102 BCE in the scriptural reckoning); academic dating of the text differs sharply.
> Up: [Epic Characters](README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md).

**Duḥśāsana** (Sanskrit *Duḥśāsana*, "hard to govern / difficult to rule," a pejorative against the auspicious birth-form **Suśāsana**, "well-governing" — the same victors'-renaming pattern as **Suyodhana → [Duryodhana](duryodhana.md)**) is the **second-eldest of the hundred Kauravas**, sons of the blind king **Dhṛtarāṣṭra** and **Gāndhārī**. In the *Mahābhārata*'s scheme of incarnations he is, with his ninety-eight younger brothers, an aṃśa of the **Paulastya rākṣasas** (descendants of Pulastya), while Duryodhana alone is the aṃśa of **Kali** (the asura of strife). Duḥśāsana is less a character than a **function**: he is Duryodhana's instrument and echo, the hand that executes his elder brother's cruelty. He commits the single act that becomes the epic's **moral fulcrum** — the dragging and attempted stripping of Draupadī in the [Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md) — and he dies the epic's most gruesome death, **his chest torn open and his blood drunk by Bhīma** in the [Karṇa Parva](../mahabharata/08-karna-parva.md), the precise fulfilment of the vow that outrage provoked. His arc is therefore a closed loop: the crime and its answering vengeance bracket the whole war.

---

## I. Birth & cosmic identity (Mbh Ādi Parva)

- **One of the hundred from the flesh-ball** - Duḥśāsana is the **second piece** to ripen from Gāndhārī's *māṃsapeśī* (the lifeless ball of flesh divided by **[Vyāsa](../vyasa.md)** into a hundred-and-one thumb-sized parts, each sealed in a pot of ghee), born immediately after Duryodhana (Mbh Ādi Parva ~107). [scriptural]
- **Aṃśa of the Paulastya rākṣasas** - The **Aṃśāvataraṇa Parva** (Ādi Parva ~1.61) assigns the ninety-nine brothers other than Duryodhana to portions of the **rākṣasas of Pulastya's line**, framing them collectively as incarnate demonic force rather than individuals (Mbh Ādi Parva). [scriptural]
- **The pejorative name** - As with his brother, the form **Duḥśāsana** ("ungovernable") is read by scholars as the **victors' renaming** of an originally auspicious **Suśāsana**, a clue to the epic's redactional layering. [scholarly]

---

## II. Early life & the Kaurava faction (Mbh Ādi / Sabhā Parvas)

- **Trained alongside the cousins** - Reared with the Pāṇḍavas under **Droṇa** and **Kṛpa**, Duḥśāsana grows up the most prominent of Duryodhana's brothers and his constant companion in the schemes against the Pāṇḍavas (Mbh Ādi Parva).
- **Junior partner in the early plots** - He is named among the conspirators in the cabal of **Duryodhana, Karṇa, Śakuni, and Duḥśāsana** that recurs through the lac-house plan and the dice scheme — almost always listed as the fourth, Duryodhana's enforcer rather than a strategist (Mbh Ādi / Sabhā Parvas).

---

## III. The dice-hall outrage — the moral fulcrum (Mbh Sabhā Parva ~60–63)

- **Dragging Draupadī by the hair** - After Yudhiṣṭhira loses Draupadī at the loaded dice (staked while she is in her menstrual seclusion and in a single garment), Duryodhana orders Duḥśāsana to fetch her; when the messenger fails, **Duḥśāsana himself seizes Draupadī by the hair and hauls her into the men's assembly**, taunting that she is now a slave (Sabhā Parva 60). [scriptural]
- **Draupadī's unanswerable question** - Dragged before the hall, Draupadī puts the *praśna* that paralyses the elders — *did Yudhiṣṭhira lose himself first or her, and could a man who had already lost himself lawfully stake her?* — which neither Bhīṣma nor anyone can resolve (Sabhā Parva 60).
- **The attempted disrobing (*vastraharaṇa* / *cīraharaṇa*)** - Egged on by Karṇa, who declares her a slave with no claim to modesty, Duḥśāsana **pulls at Draupadī's sari to strip her in open court** — but as each length is torn away **another garment endlessly appears**, until Duḥśāsana, exhausted, sinks beside a heap of cloth, unable to bare her (Sabhā Parva 61). This is the epic's central image of injustice and the act for which he is remembered.
  - *Variant (Vulgate / popular, devotional retellings):* Draupadī cries out to **Kṛṣṇa (Govinda)**, who from afar miraculously supplies the inexhaustible cloth — the cherished *akṣaya-vastra* scene of bhakti tradition and all televised versions. [late text]
  - *Variant [scholarly] (BORI Critical Edition):* the CE **omits Draupadī's prayer to Kṛṣṇa and any named agent**; the garments simply renew themselves, read by the Sabhāparvan editor **F. Edgerton** as **dharma / cosmic justice itself** preventing the stripping of a chaste woman, with no Kṛṣṇa-intervention in the oldest layer.
  - *Variant [disputed]:* a minority of scholars argue the whole disrobing is a late interpolation; the mainstream retains the miracle in its impersonal form and treats only the explicit Kṛṣṇa-rescue as accretion.
- **Bhīma's twin vows provoked here** - The outrage detonates the oaths that drive the rest of the epic. Watching Duḥśāsana's hands on Draupadī, **Bhīma vows to tear open Duḥśāsana's chest and drink his blood**, and — when Duryodhana bares and slaps his left thigh inviting Draupadī to sit on it — **to break Duryodhana's thigh** (Sabhā Parva 61–63). The two vows are sworn together; their fulfilments fall in two different war-books. [scriptural]
- **Draupadī's own vow** - Draupadī vows to **leave her hair unbound until it is washed / dressed in Duḥśāsana's blood** — the loosed hair becoming a standing reproach across the thirteen exile years (Sabhā Parva 61–63). It is Duḥśāsana's act that creates this vow, and his death that closes it.
- **The intervention** - Frightened by ill omens (jackals howling, asses braying) and Vidura's warning, **Dhṛtarāṣṭra grants Draupadī boons** that free the Pāṇḍavas — but Duḥśāsana's deed cannot be unmade (Sabhā Parva 63).

## IV. Exile and the road to war (Mbh Vana → Udyoga Parvas)

- **Mockery at the second insult** - In several tellings Duḥśāsana is among those who jeer when **Arjuna (as Bṛhannalā)** routs the Kaurava host at the **Virāṭa** cattle-raid — a humiliation that mirrors the dice-hall in reverse (Mbh Virāṭa Parva).
- **In the failed-peace cabal** - During Kṛṣṇa's embassy, Duḥśāsana sits with **Duryodhana, Śakuni, and Karṇa** in the plot to **seize Kṛṣṇa** in open court, which Kṛṣṇa foils by revealing his cosmic form (*Viśvarūpa*); peace dies and war becomes certain (Mbh Udyoga Parva ~129–130).

---

## V. The war and his death (Mbh Bhīṣma → Karṇa Parvas)

- **A frontline Kaurava warrior** - Duḥśāsana fights through the major engagements of the eighteen-day war as one of Duryodhana's chief supports, clashing repeatedly with **Bhīma**, who hunts him and the other brothers in fulfilment of his vows (Mbh Bhīṣma / Droṇa Parvas).
- **The slaying of Duḥśāsana (Mbh Karṇa Parva 61 / 83)** - On the **17th day**, Bhīma at last corners Duḥśāsana, smashes him from his chariot, **tears open his chest, and drinks his blood** — the literal discharge of the twin dice-hall vow to break his chest and drink his blood. The text dwells on Bhīma's terrible joy; bystanders recoil from the sight (Karṇa Parva). [scriptural]
  - *Variant:* whether Bhīma truly swallows the blood or only smears his lips and proclaims the vow fulfilled varies by retelling; the Critical Edition keeps the act stark and literal.
- **Draupadī's hair bound at last** - Bhīma carries the blood to **Draupadī**, who **binds up the hair she had left loose since the dice-hall**, washing or dressing it in Duḥśāsana's blood — the long-deferred vengeance completed and the vow-loop that opened in the Sabhā Parva finally closed (Karṇa Parva, *vadha* section). [scriptural]
  - *Variant:* whether Draupadī literally washes her hair in the blood or Bhīma simply brings it as proof varies by retelling; the loosed-hair vow itself is fixed in the [Sabhā Parva](../mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md).
- **Duryodhana's thigh — the twin vow's other half** - The companion vow, to **break Duryodhana's bared thigh**, is fulfilled separately by Bhīma's foul mace-blow in the [Śalya Parva](../mahabharata/09-shalya-parva.md) (~58), at Kṛṣṇa's reminding signal — so the two oaths sworn over Draupadī's outrage are discharged in two consecutive war-books. [scriptural]

## VI. Family & afterlife in tradition

- **Wife and son** - Duḥśāsana's wife is unnamed in the core text; his son **Durmukha** (or **Durmaṣana** in variant lists) fights and falls in the war among the Kaurava ranks. [regional]
  - *Variant [disputed]:* genealogies of the hundred Kauravas differ on which sons attach to which brother; assignments to Duḥśāsana are not stable across recensions.
- **The structural villain** - Tradition remembers Duḥśāsana almost solely for the *cīraharaṇa*; he carries little independent biography, functioning as the **embodied hand of Kaurava cruelty** and the trigger for the epic's defining vows — the reason Draupadī's loosed hair and Bhīma's bloodied mouth become the *Mahābhārata*'s two most enduring images of injustice and its repayment.
