# Jāmbavān — the Ṛkṣa-rāja who Remembers Trivikrama

> The **bear-king** (*ṛkṣa-rāja*) of the [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md) — brought forth by **Brahmā** before the vānara host, the oldest living creature in the epic, and the counsellor whose one speech on the southern shore gives **[Hanumān](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md)** back his own strength. He carries the epic's deepest memory (he circled **Viṣṇu-Trivikrama** in the [Vāmana avatāra](../vamana-bali.md)), rules the search-party's mandate at Laṅkā's threshold, sends the **first** Sañjīvanī flight, and is granted life until the Kali Yuga.
> Sits in **[Tretā-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md), and again in **Dvāpara** at the [Syamantaka](../syamantaka-jewel.md) cave-fight — one of the very few beings the corpus tracks across two yugas in the flesh.
> Parent: [Characters](README.md) · Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md) · Class-level home: [Vānaras & Ṛkṣas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/vanaras-and-rikshas.md)

**Jāmbavān** (Sanskrit *Jāmbavat / Jāmbavanta*, "he of the *jāmbū*-dark hue") is the king of the **ṛkṣas** — the bear-race that marches beside the monkeys and the cow-tailed *golāṅgūlas* in Rāma's host. He is the epic's **elder**: created before the mass begetting of the vānaras, old enough to have walked the world when Viṣṇu measured it in three strides, and old enough that his part in the war is counsel rather than combat. Vālmīki gives him three functions and keeps them distinct — he is the army's **memory** (what the gods did, what a man is, what a bear once could leap), its **conscience** (the ruling that a mandate exceeded is a mandate forfeited), and its **physician of last resort** (the first errand for the healing herbs is his). His single most consequential act is not a blow struck but a recitation: on the shore at the end of the [Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md) he tells Hanumān who Hanumān is, and the [Sundara-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md) follows from it.

> **Disambiguation.** *Jāmbavān* the bear-king is not **Jāmbavatī**, his daughter, who becomes one of Kṛṣṇa's eight chief queens — see [The Syamantaka Jewel](../syamantaka-jewel.md). He is also distinct from **Dhūmra**, called lord of all the bears in the same war-catalogue that makes Jāmbavān Dhūmra's younger brother (§I).

---

## I. The two birth-accounts, unreconciled (Rām. Bāla-kāṇḍa 17; Yuddha-kāṇḍa 27)

- **Brahmā's yawn** - When the gods are commanded to beget an army for Rāma, **Brahmā names Jāmbavān as already made**: "I created the eminent bear Jāmbavat in earlier times, as he came forth suddenly from my yawning face" — so the ṛkṣa-king predates the whole host he will serve in, and is the Creator's own issue rather than any deva's son. (Vālmīki, **Bāla 17.7**.)
- **Dhūmra's younger brother** - The other account is not a legend but a battlefield catalogue: **Sāraṇa**, naming Laṅkā's enemies for Rāvaṇa from the wall, points out **Dhūmra**, *lord of all the bears*, on Mt. Ṛkṣavat by the Narmadā — and then the mountain-like chief beside him, "the younger brother of Dhūmra, like his brother in form but more distinguished in prowess," **a commander over the commanders themselves**: that is Jāmbavān. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 27.8–11**.)
  - *Note (an internal doublet the epic never resolves):* a Jāmbavān born from Brahmā's yawn before all creatures, and a Jāmbavān who is somebody's **younger brother**, both stand in the received text. Vālmīki does not choose between them and neither does this corpus; the roster's brotherhood is **textual**, not "tradition." [disputed] See [Vānaras & Ṛkṣas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/vanaras-and-rikshas.md).
- **The deva-war service and its boons** - The same catalogue records what the intervening ages were spent on: **Jāmbavān rendered Indra great help in the war of the gods and the asuras**, and won many boons for it. Vālmīki states the service and the reward without itemising either, and nothing elsewhere in the epic fills them in. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 27.12**.)
- **Ṛkṣas, not monkeys** - The bears are a race of their own, counted alongside the vānaras from Bāla 17 onward and settled on **Mt. Ṛkṣavat**; the frequent flattening of Jāmbavān into "an old monkey" erases a distinction the text keeps. (Vālmīki, Bāla 17; Yuddha 27.) See [Vānaras & Ṛkṣas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/vanaras-and-rikshas.md).

## II. The Trivikrama memory and the ladder of leaps (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 65)

- **The ladder** - On the southern shore, with a hundred yojanas of sea between the party and Laṅkā, the chiefs measure themselves aloud in rising order: **Gaja ten** yojanas, **Gavākṣa twenty**, **Śarabha thirty**, **Ṛṣabha forty**, **Gandhamādana fifty**, **Mainda sixty**, **Dvivida seventy**, **Suṣeṇa eighty** — and then the old bear. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 65**.)
- **Ninety, and what ninety used to be** - Jāmbavān answers **ninety**, and explains the shortfall by producing the epic's deepest memory: in his prime he **circumambulated Viṣṇu as the Lord strode the worlds in the Trivikrama form** — the moment of the [Vāmana avatāra](../vamana-bali.md) — and "such as I was, I am now old and decrepit." The confession is the hinge of the sarga: the strongest of the elders can name the era of the Dwarf and still cannot cross the water. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 65.16**.)
- **Aṅgada's hundred, and why it will not serve** - **Aṅgada** alone claims the full hundred, but doubts he could leap back, and as crown prince he cannot be spent on a wager; the party is out of candidates. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 65.) See [Aṅgada](./angada.md).
- **The chronological weight** - Because the Trivikrama stride belongs to the Vāmana descent and Jāmbavān saw it, his lifetime is the corpus's longest single human-scale span inside the epic — and the reason later tradition reaches for him when it wants a ninth immortal (§VII). (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 65.)
  - *Variant (how old, exactly):* devotional and Purāṇic retellings extend the memory to whole **manvantaras** and make Jāmbavān a witness of the churning and of Vāmana's begging-bowl alike; Vālmīki claims only the circumambulation. [folk]

## III. The shore-speech: giving Hanumān back to himself (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 66; Uttara-kāṇḍa 36)

- **Why the reminding is needed** - Hanumān sits apart, modest and silent, because he does not know what he is: as a child he harried the hermitages of the **Bhṛgu and Aṅgiras** sages, who cursed him to **forget his own strength until another should remind him of it** — the release-clause written into the curse itself. This is core Vālmīki, not devotional embroidery. (Vālmīki, **Uttara 36**.) See [Hanumān](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md).
- **The recitation** - Jāmbavān turns on him and **recites him to himself**: son of **Vāyu** by **Añjanā**; the infant who sprang at the rising sun mistaking it for fruit; the child who took **Indra's** thunderbolt on the jaw and was answered with the boons of every god. The curse's condition is met in the telling, and the strength returns. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 66**.)
- **What the speech is for** - It is the epic's clearest statement that the decisive power in the war already existed and was merely **unremembered** — the plant whose payoff is the ocean-leap that opens the [Sundara-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md). Jāmbavān contributes nothing but knowledge, and it is enough. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 65–66.)
  - *Variant (placement):* **Tulsīdās** moves the reminding to the shore of the war-crossing and other retellings to still other moments; only the *placement* differs from Vālmīki, never the mechanic. The scene itself is not a Rāmcaritmānas invention. [late text] See [Rāmcaritmānas](../ramayana/recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md).

## IV. Elder of the council (Rām. Kiṣkindhā 26, 41; Sundara-kāṇḍa 58–60)

- **At Sugrīva's consecration** - Jāmbavān stands among the vānara chiefs — Gaja, Gavākṣa, Gavaya, Śarabha, Gandhamādana, Mainda, Dvivida, Hanumān — who **pour the abhiṣeka water over Sugrīva themselves** inside Kiṣkindhā, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa both being barred from the city by the exile-vow. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 26**.)
- **Assigned to the south** - When the four search-parties go out, Jāmbavān is placed in the **southern** party under Aṅgada's command with Hanumān, Nīla and the rest — the quarter in which Sītā actually lies. (Vālmīki, **Kiṣkindhā 41**.)
- **Ṛkṣabila and the broken deadline** - He is with the party through the ordeal in the enchanted cave of **[Svayamprabhā](./svayamprabha-and-hema.md)** and the resolve to fast to death on the shore, and among those to whom **Sampāti** at last gives Laṅkā's bearing. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 50–59.) See [Jaṭāyu & Sampāti](./jatayu-and-sampati.md).
- **The mandate ruling** - Hanumān returns from Laṅkā and, at **Madhuvana** and after, **Aṅgada argues that the party should storm Laṅkā themselves** and bring Sītā back by its own strength and honour. **Jāmbavān rules against him**: they were sent to *find* her, not to fight, and a party that exceeds its mandate forfeits it — the report goes to Sugrīva and Rāma first. It is the most explicitly constitutional speech any vānara or ṛkṣa makes. (Vālmīki, **Sundara 59–60**.)
- **The second telling** - He is the chief auditor of Hanumān's full recapitulation on the shore — Surasā, Siṃhikā, the grove, the burning — the epic's deliberate re-narration, by which the party fixes what will be reported and what will not. (Vālmīki, **Sundara 58**.)

## V. The war: the bridge, Kumbhakarṇa, and the first herb-flight (Rām. Yuddha-kāṇḍa 22, 67, 74)

- **At the Setu** - Jāmbavān's bears work in the host that throws the causeway across the sea under **[Nala's](./nala-nila-and-sushena.md)** direction — a hundred yojanas long and ten broad, raised in five days, **Sāgara bearing its weight from beneath**. Nothing floats in Vālmīki's account; the bridge is craft, not levitation. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 22**.) Full treatment: [Nala, Nīla & Suṣeṇa](./nala-nila-and-sushena.md) and [Rāmeśvaram & the Setu](../../06-sacred-geography/rameswaram-setu-bandha.md).
- **Against Kumbhakarṇa** - The great chiefs go in against Kumbhakarṇa in waves and are broken one after another — Nīla, Ṛṣabha, Śarabha, Gavākṣa, Gandhamādana, Aṅgada all struck down before Hanumān's assault tells. Jāmbavān is not among the named duellists of that sarga; his war is fought from the council and the field-hospital, not the melee. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 67**.) See [Kumbhakarṇa](./kumbhakarna.md).
- **Wounded, and the only question he asks** - After **Indrajit's** brahmāstra lays Rāma, Lakṣmaṇa and the host flat, **Vibhīṣaṇa and Hanumān find Jāmbavān pierced with hundreds of arrows and barely alive** — and the dying bear does not ask after Rāma. He asks whether **Hanumān** lives: *"If that brave Hanumān survives, the whole army, even killed, is unhurt; if Hanumān has given up his life, we are all dead, though living."* (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 74**.)
- **The first Sañjīvanī errand is his** - It is Jāmbavān, not Suṣeṇa, who sends the first herb-flight, naming four herbs — ***mṛta-sañjīvanī*, *viśalya-karaṇī*, *suvarṇa-karaṇī*, *sandhānī*** — on a blazing herb-mountain **between Mount Ṛṣabha and Kailāsa**, which **Vālmīki leaves unnamed**. Unable to tell the glowing plants apart, Hanumān brings the whole peak. (Vālmīki, **Yuddha 74**.)
  - *Note (two flights, two mountains, two prescribers):* the second errand — after **Rāvaṇa's** Maya-made *śakti* fells Lakṣmaṇa — is prescribed by the physician **Suṣeṇa** to a mountain the text *does* name, **Oṣadhi/Mahodaya** (Yuddha 101). Retelling merges the two into one flight; the corpus keeps them apart. **"Droṇagiri" is not a Vālmīki place-name.** Object-side canonical home: [Rāmāyaṇa Objects & Tokens](../../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/ramayana-objects-and-tokens.md); see also [Meru, Kailāsa & the Cosmic Mountains](../../06-sacred-geography/meru-kailash-and-cosmic-mountains.md).

## VI. The Mahāprasthāna grant (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa 108)

- **Fixed on earth until Kali** - At Rāma's final departure from the world he disposes of his companions one by one, and **Jāmbavān, with Mainda and Dvivida, is granted life on earth until the Kali Yuga begins** — a fixed term, alongside **Vibhīṣaṇa's** rule for as long as sun and moon endure and **Hanumān's** life for as long as Rāma's story is told among men. (Vālmīki, **Uttara 108**.)
  - *Note (where the boons actually sit):* all three of these grants are made **here**, at the Mahāprasthāna, not at the Yuddha-kāṇḍa coronation; Yuddha 128 contains only Sītā's gift of her pearl necklace. Retellings that place Hanumān's deathlessness at the coronation compress two scenes into one. See [Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md) and [Uttara-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md).
- **The kingdom he leaves behind** - In the same audience **Sugrīva** installs **Aṅgada** on the throne of Kiṣkindhā and follows Rāma out of the world, entering the sun's orb; the ṛkṣa-king outlives the vānara king he served. (Vālmīki, Uttara 108.) See [Aṅgada](./angada.md).

## VII. Into Dvāpara — the Syamantaka fight

- **The bear in the cave** - Generations later Jāmbavān kills a lion and takes the sun-jewel **Syamantaka**, giving it to his infant son to play with in his mountain cave. **Kṛṣṇa**, tracking the gem to clear himself of the theft, fights him there for **twenty-eight days** without rest (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.56).
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.13):* the contest "lasted twenty-one days," and Kṛṣṇa's companions wait only seven or eight before giving him up for dead — the shorter count, and the older of the two tellings.
- **The recognition** - As his strength fails, Jāmbavān realises his opponent can be none but the Lord he once served as **Rāma**, praises him as that same Viṣṇu, surrenders the jewel and gives his daughter **Jāmbavatī** in marriage — she becomes one of the eight chief queens and mother of **Sāmba**. Full treatment: **[The Syamantaka Jewel](../syamantaka-jewel.md)**. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.56.)
- **The ninth immortal** - Because he sees both Rāma and Kṛṣṇa in the flesh, tradition often appends Jāmbavān to the eight **chirañjīvis** as a ninth — a list-extension, not a scriptural grant, and in tension with the fixed until-Kali term of Uttara 108. [folk] See [The Chirañjīvis](../the-chiranjivis.md).

## VIII. Character and reception

- **The counsellor, not the champion** - Vālmīki gives Jāmbavān almost no kills and nearly every decisive **judgement**: whose strength to use, whether to fight, which herbs, who must live. Where Hanumān is the epic's instrument and Aṅgada its wounded prince, Jāmbavān is its institutional memory — the figure who knows what has already been done and what may not be done. (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā–Yuddha.)
- **Age as authority** - The Trivikrama boast and the ninety-yojana confession are the same speech: the epic's oldest creature establishes his standing by admitting his decline. It is the strongest argument in the poem for counsel outranking prowess. [scholarly]
- **Devotional reading** - Bhakti tradition reads the shore-speech as the guru's office in miniature — the devotee's power is not conferred but **recollected**, and the teacher does nothing but name what is already there. [folk]
- **Cultic afterlife** - Jāmbavān is venerated in the Bhāgavata's wake as father-in-law of Kṛṣṇa; regional and tribal traditions across the Deccan claim descent or association, and cave-sites (notably in Gujarat and Karnataka) are shown as the Syamantaka cave. [folk] [regional]
