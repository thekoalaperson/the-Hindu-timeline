# Bharata and Shatrughna and Their Brides — Māṇḍavī and Śrutakīrti

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive completing the **fourfold Mithilā wedding** of the [Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/README.md) — the two younger pairs left out of the [Sītā](./sita.md) and [Lakṣmaṇa](./lakshmana.md)–Ūrmilā spotlight: **Bharata** (Daśaratha's son by Kaikeyī, the regent who rules from Rāma's sandals) with **Māṇḍavī**, and **Śatrughna** (twin of Lakṣmaṇa, son of [Sumitrā](./sumitra.md), slayer of Lavaṇāsura and founder of Mathurā) with **Śrutakīrti**. Māṇḍavī and Śrutakīrti are the daughters of **Kuśadhvaja**, brother of Janaka — first cousins of Sītā and Ūrmilā. Up to the [Epic Characters index](./README.md) · [Deep Dives](../README.md) · master [index](../../README.md).
> Two clocks: as *itihāsa* this all falls in the **[Tretā-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the present 28th Mahāyuga, 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara; the scholarly baseline is the **[Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa](../ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md)** (Bāla-kāṇḍa 73 for the quadruple wedding, Uttara-kāṇḍa for Lavaṇāsura), with Purāṇic and devotional layers added later. [scholarly]

**This file** gives the two neglected Mithilā brides the dynastic and marital treatment their elder sisters/cousins receive, framed inside profiles of their husbands. **Bharata** is the dharmic regent whose refusal of a throne he could have kept is the moral pivot of the Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa; **Śatrughna** ("slayer of foes"), Lakṣmaṇa's twin and shadow of Bharata, is the brother who acts only at the very end — destroying Lavaṇāsura and raising **Madhurā / Mathurā**.

---

## I. The two brides — Māṇḍavī and Śrutakīrti

- **Daughters of Kuśadhvaja, not Janaka** — Māṇḍavī and Śrutakīrti are the daughters of **Kuśadhvaja**, the younger brother of **Janaka** (Sīradhvaja) of Mithilā; they are therefore **first cousins** of Sītā and Ūrmilā, not full sisters (Rām. Bāla-kāṇḍa 71–73).
  - *Variant (Kuśadhvaja's seat):* Vālmīki has Janaka summon his brother Kuśadhvaja, described as ruling at **Sāṃkāśya** (Sānkāśyā) on the **Ikṣumatī** river, to give his two daughters in the joint wedding. Some genealogies instead seat Kuśadhvaja at **Kāśī**; the constant is that he is Janaka's brother and the brides' father. [variant]
- **Māṇḍavī — bride of Bharata** — Named first of Kuśadhvaja's two daughters; given to **Bharata**, Daśaratha's son by Kaikeyī (Rām. Bāla-kāṇḍa 73).
- **Śrutakīrti — bride of Śatrughna** — The second daughter; given to **Śatrughna**, the younger twin of Sumitrā's sons (Rām. Bāla-kāṇḍa 73). Her name means "of renowned fame / one whose fame is heard."
- **The fourfold symmetry** — Janaka offers the match so that all four princes wed on a single day: **Sītā–Rāma** and **Ūrmilā–[Lakṣmaṇa](./lakshmana.md)** (Janaka's own daughters) paired with **Māṇḍavī–Bharata** and **Śrutakīrti–Śatrughna** (his nieces). Sage **Vasiṣṭha**, with Viśvāmitra and Śatānanda, conducts the rites at the sacrificial altar; the four couples circumambulate the fire together (Rām. Bāla-kāṇḍa 73). See the full sequence in the [Bāla-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/1-bala-kanda.md).
- **Sparse afterlife** — Unlike Sītā and Ūrmilā, the two cousins recede from the narrative after the wedding; Vālmīki names them at marriage and gives them sons, but no independent episodes. Their importance is **dynastic and structural** — sealing the alliance of Ayodhyā (Ikṣvāku) with Mithilā (Videha). [scholarly]

## II. Bharata — the regent in the sandals

- **Parentage** — Second-born son of **Daśaratha**, the only son of his favourite queen **Kaikeyī**; half-brother of Rāma (by Kausalyā) and of the twins Lakṣmaṇa and Śatrughna (by [Sumitrā](./sumitra.md)) (Rām. Bāla-kāṇḍa 18).
- **Aṃśa identification** — In the Vaiṣṇava scheme that distributes Viṣṇu's body and attributes among the four brothers (Rāma as Viṣṇu, Lakṣmaṇa as **Śeṣa**), **Bharata is the aṃśa of the conch (Śaṅkha)** and Śatrughna of the **discus (Sudarśana cakra)** — so the quartet embodies the deity and his emblems (Purāṇic/devotional). [devotional]
- **Absent at the catastrophe** — When Kaikeyī, prompted by **Mantharā**, extracts the two boons (Rāma's fourteen-year exile and Bharata's coronation), **Bharata is away** at his maternal grandfather's house (the Kekaya kingdom, with Śatrughna). He returns to find his father dead and Rāma gone — and is **innocent of the plot** (Rām. Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa).
- **Rejection of his mother's gift** — Bharata reacts to the news with **horror and rage at Kaikeyī**, repudiating the throne won for him; he condemns her act as the ruin of the dynasty and refuses the kingship outright (Rām. Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa).
- **Cidakūṭa — the appeal to Rāma** — Bharata leads the people, queens, and ministers to **Citrakūṭa** to beg Rāma to return and rule. Rāma refuses, bound by his vow and his father's word; the brothers' debate over dharma and duty is one of the epic's great set-pieces (Rām. Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa).
- **The pādukā / sandals** — Unable to move Rāma, Bharata receives Rāma's **golden sandals (pādukā)** and bears them home, installing them on the throne as the **true sovereign**. Bharata rules only as **regent in Rāma's name** (Rām. Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa).
- **Nandigrāma — the ascetic regency** — Bharata refuses to live in the palace or wear royal dress. He withdraws to **Nandigrāma** (a village outside Ayodhyā), dons bark/ascetic garb, sleeps on the ground, eats plain fare, and conducts the kingdom's affairs as a **vassal of the sandals** for the whole fourteen years — vowing to enter fire if Rāma does not return on the appointed day (Rām. Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa). This self-abnegating regency is the heart of Bharata's fame.
- **The return** — When Rāma, victorious over Rāvaṇa, flies back by the **Puṣpaka vimāna**, Hanumān goes ahead to reassure the anguished Bharata. The reunion at Nandigrāma and Rāma's **coronation (Rāma-rājya)** end Bharata's vigil; he hands back the kingdom he never claimed (Rām. Yuddha-kāṇḍa; Uttara-kāṇḍa).
- **Bharata's sons — and a conquest** — Bharata and **Māṇḍavī** have two sons, **Takṣa** and **Puṣkala**. In the Uttara-kāṇḍa, on his maternal uncle **Yudhājit's** prompting, Bharata wars on the **Gandharvas** of the **Gāndhāra** country and founds two cities for his sons: **Takṣaśilā (Taxila)** for Takṣa and **Puṣkalāvatī** for Puṣkala (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa).
  - *Variant:* the Gandharva campaign and the city-foundations are given in the Uttara-kāṇḍa (a later stratum of Vālmīki); some retellings omit it entirely. [late text]

## III. Śatrughna — the last brother to act

- **Parentage and twinship** — Fourth-born; the **twin of [Lakṣmaṇa](./lakshmana.md)**, both sons of **[Sumitrā](./sumitra.md)** from her doubled portion of the sacrificial pāyasa. As Lakṣmaṇa attaches to Rāma, **Śatrughna attaches to Bharata** — the second pairing the pāyasa-logic is read to explain (Rām. Bāla-kāṇḍa 18).
- **Aṃśa identification** — In the Vaiṣṇava reading he is the **Sudarśana cakra (discus)** of Viṣṇu (Purāṇic/devotional). [devotional]
- **The shadow of Bharata** — Through the exile years Śatrughna remains with Bharata, sharing the Nandigrāma vigil rather than the forest. He is the **least independently active** of the four brothers in the first six kāṇḍas — his role is loyalty to Bharata, mirroring Lakṣmaṇa's to Rāma.
- **Striking down Mantharā** — On returning from Kekaya, Śatrughna's one early flash of agency: catching the hunchback **Mantharā** (whose scheming caused the exile), he seizes and beats her in fury, until Bharata restrains him on the grounds that a woman must not be killed (Rām. Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa).
- **Lavaṇāsura — Śatrughna's great deed (Uttara-kāṇḍa)** — In Rāma's reign, the **asura Lavaṇa**, son of **Madhu** and nephew of Rāvaṇa's line, terrorizes the Madhuvana region wielding an invincible **trident (śūla)** gifted by **Śiva** — but the trident is lethal only when **in his hand**. Rāma assigns the campaign to **Śatrughna**, consecrating him king of the region to be won. Śatrughna ambushes Lavaṇa when he has gone out to hunt and **left the trident behind**, and slays him with a divine arrow Rāma gave him (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa).
- **Founding Madhurā / Mathurā** — On the site of Lavaṇa's defeated realm (the **Madhuvana** forest), Śatrughna raises the city of **Madhurā** — identified with **Mathurā**, later the birthplace of Kṛṣṇa — and rules it for twelve years (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa).
  - *Variant:* the toponym appears as **Madhurā / Madhupura / Mathurā** across recensions; the tradition uniformly makes Śatrughna its founder, knitting the Rāmāyaṇa to the later Kṛṣṇa cycle. [scholarly]
- **The return and renunciation** — Near the close of the Uttara-kāṇḍa, sensing Rāma's approaching departure from the world, Śatrughna comes back toward Ayodhyā, sets his **two sons over Mathurā**, and shares in the dynasty's final passing (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa).
- **Śatrughna's sons** — Śatrughna and **Śrutakīrti** have two sons, **Subāhu** and **Śatrughātī** (also given as Śūrasena); Subāhu is installed at **Mathurā** and the other over an adjacent realm (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa).
  - *Variant:* the second son's name varies (**Śatrughātī / Śūrasena / Bāhuśruti**) by recension and Purāṇic genealogy. [variant]

## IV. The two brides in later tradition and theme

- **Pairing the four wives with the four husbands** — Devotional tradition sometimes extends the brothers' aṃśa-scheme to their wives, treating the four Mithilā brides as portions of **Lakṣmī** mirroring the four portions of Viṣṇu — though only **Sītā** is firmly the Lakṣmī-aṃśa in mainstream texts; Māṇḍavī and Śrutakīrti's divinization is a minor, late embellishment. [devotional] [late text]
- **The silence as virtue** — As with the brothers Bharata and Śatrughna, whose greatness lies in **service and restraint** rather than the hero's spotlight, the two brides embody the dharmic ideal of the wife who sustains the household in the husband's long absence — Māṇḍavī through Bharata's ascetic regency, Śrutakīrti through Śatrughna's years away at Mathurā — without independent narrative. Modern retellings that recover **Ūrmilā** (e.g. Gupta's *Sāket*) occasionally extend sympathy to Māṇḍavī and Śrutakīrti as the further-forgotten wives. [scholarly]
- **Genealogical function** — Their chief textual purpose is to seal the **double alliance** between the houses of **Janaka** (Videha) and **Kuśadhvaja** on one side and **Daśaratha** (Ikṣvāku/Ayodhyā) on the other, and to furnish the next Ikṣvāku generation (Takṣa, Puṣkala, Subāhu, Śatrughātī) who found cities across the northwest. [scholarly]

## Sources

- **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa** — **Bāla-kāṇḍa 71–73** (Kuśadhvaja brought from Sāṃkāśya; the betrothal and the **quadruple wedding** of the four princes — Māṇḍavī to Bharata, Śrutakīrti to Śatrughna — conducted by Vasiṣṭha); **Bāla-kāṇḍa 18** (birth, naming, and the twins' attachments); **Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa** (Bharata's return, rejection of the throne, the Citrakūṭa appeal, the pādukā and the **Nandigrāma** regency; Śatrughna and Mantharā); **Uttara-kāṇḍa** (Śatrughna and **Lavaṇāsura**, the founding of **Madhurā/Mathurā**; Bharata's **Gandharva** conquest and Takṣaśilā/Puṣkalāvatī; the sons of all four brothers). The base text — terse on the two brides but the source of the wedding, the regency, and the Lavaṇa campaign.
- **Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa & Purāṇic theology** — the four brothers as Viṣṇu and his emblems (**Bharata = conch, Śatrughna = discus**); the devotional extension to the four brides as Lakṣmī-portions. [devotional]
- **Purāṇic genealogies** (Viṣṇu, Vāyu, Bhāgavata) — variant names and seats for **Kuśadhvaja** and for the sons of Bharata and Śatrughna; Mathurā's lineage linking the Rāmāyaṇa to the Kṛṣṇa cycle. [variant]
