# Sugrīva & Vālī

> The two **vānara** half-brothers of **Kiṣkindhā** — **Vālī (Bāli)**, son of **Indra**, and **Sugrīva**, son of **Sūrya** — whose fratricidal feud over the throne and over a sealed cave drives the whole of the [Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md). Their quarrel pulls **[Rāma](../ramayana/README.md)** into the most-debated act of his career: the killing of Vālī from concealment, and the alliance with Sugrīva that launches the search for **Sītā**.
> Sits in **[Tretā-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md).
> Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md) · Antagonist whom Sugrīva's army will help destroy: [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md) · The vānara general of this alliance: see [Beings & Bestiary](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/README.md).

**Vālī** (Sanskrit *Vālin*, "the Tailed One"; also **Bāli**) and **Sugrīva** ("Fair-Necked / Beautiful-Necked") are the ruling **vānara** brothers of the forest-kingdom **Kiṣkindhā**, set on the southern Ṛṣyamūka–Mātaṅga range. They are half-brothers by a single mother but by two divine fathers — Vālī begotten of **Indra**, Sugrīva of **Sūrya** — which marks Vālī as the elder, stronger, divinely-favoured king and Sugrīva as the gentler, displaced younger. Their story is a tragedy of mistaken death, sealed caves, and broken trust: Sugrīva walls his brother into a cave believing him dead, Vālī returns to seize Sugrīva's throne **and wife**, and the exiled Sugrīva strikes an alliance with Rāma — sealed before the sacred fire — whereby Rāma kills Vālī with a single arrow shot from cover. The killing is the Rāmāyaṇa's central **dharma-puzzle**; the alliance is its engine, for it is Sugrīva's vānara hosts (with **Hanumān**) who locate Sītā and build the bridge to Laṅkā.

## I. Birth and parentage — the two divine fathers (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa; Uttara-kāṇḍa)

- **Ṛkṣarajas — the vānara progenitor** - The **oldest layer of the text simply makes Ṛkṣarajas their father**: Kiṣkindhā **57.5** and Sundara **63** both name him as father/grandfather of the line, without explanation, in the core narrative.
- **The enchanted-pool birth-story — a late stratum, and flagged as such** - The famous account has Ṛkṣarajas drink from or bathe in an enchanted pool and be **turned into a woman**, in which form he is approached by both **Indra** and **Sūrya**:
  - **Vālī** - born when **Indra** sheds seed onto his *hair* (vāla) — hence **Vālin**; he inherits Indra's might and a golden pendant. The elder, the king.
  - **Sugrīva** - born when **Sūrya** sheds seed onto his *neck* (grīva) — hence **Sugrīva**, "Fair-Necked"; he inherits the sun's swiftness and is the younger brother.
  - *Textual status:* this story is **not simply "Uttara-kāṇḍa" material** — it is **prakṣipta even within the Uttara Kāṇḍa**, printed by Shastri as an appendix to chapter 37 and standing outside the Critical Edition's constituted text: a stratum later than the already-late Uttara. It also **contradicts two older, core-text witnesses** (Kiṣ 57.5, Sundara 63) that know nothing of a transformation. Carry it as a flagged later legend, and let the spine rest on the older witnesses. [interpolation] [late text]
- *Variant (Kathāsaritsāgara):* Vālī is son of **Indra** and **Aruṇa/Aruṇi** (Indra's charioteer in female form); the mother named **Aruṇā**. [variant]
- *Variant (rearing):* Sugrīva is in places said to have been **raised under the care of Ahalyā** (Gautama's wife). [folk]
- **Brahmā's settlement at Kiṣkindhā** - In some tellings **Brahmā** directs the brothers to dwell in the **Kiṣkindhā** mountains, founding the vānara realm there. [late text]
- **Tārā — Vālī's queen, afterwards Sugrīva's** - daughter of the vānara chief **Suṣeṇa**, mother of **Aṅgada**, and the shrewdest political head in the kāṇḍa: she reads Sugrīva's second challenge correctly (§VIII) and disarms Lakṣmaṇa's rage (§X). She is the fourth name of the **Pañcakanyā** verse. Her three scenes, the churning-born-apsaras variant, and the three-Suṣeṇas harmonisation are all held at **[Tārā, queen of Kiṣkindhā](./tara-vanara-queen.md)**.
- **Aṅgada** - son of Vālī and Tārā; later made *yuvarāja* (crown prince) and a key commander in Rāma's war. See [Ramayana](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md).
- **Rumā — Sugrīva's queen** - seized by Vālī during the feud and restored to Sugrīva after Vālī's death; that seizure is the one named offence in Rāma's defence of the killing (§IX). Vālmīki gives her no parentage and no speech. Full dossier: **[Rumā](./ruma.md)**.

## II. Vālī's strength and the half-strength boon

- **Indra's golden pendant (kāñcana-mālā)** - Vālī wears a divine golden chain given by his father **Indra**. Vālmīki says exactly one thing about its power, and says it at Vālī's death: *udārā śrīḥ sthitā hy asyām* — "**the bounteous goddess of triumph is ensconced in it**," and she will leave it the moment he dies, rather than touch a corpse. So Vālī gives it, dying, to **Sugrīva** and tells him to put it on (Rām. Kiṣkindhā **22.16–18**). The gift is the transfer of victory itself from the elder brother to the younger, made by the elder's own hand — and it is the single most under-read moment in the feud. The common gloss that the necklace "restores energy and multiplies his power in combat" is not in the text.
- **The half-strength boon** - In widely-told tradition, Vālī has a boon that **whoever fights him face-to-face loses half his strength to Vālī** — making frontal combat suicidal and forming the standard *explanation* for why Rāma struck from concealment.
  - *Variant (textual caveat):* **no such boon is recorded in Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa or in Tulsīdās's Rāmcaritmānas**; it appears in later/folk and commentarial tradition as a post-hoc justification, grafted onto the Indra-given golden pendant that Vālmīki *does* give him. [disputed][folk]
- **Vālī's feats — and the seven sāla trees, correctly** - He daily circled the four oceans in his pradakṣiṇā, and had once **seized and humiliated Rāvaṇa**, pinning him under his arm (§VI). As for the famous trees: Vālmīki's Vālī does **not** shoot them. Sugrīva shows Rāma seven great sāla trees and says Vālī **can strip each of them leafless by sheer strength — one at a time** (*niṣpatrayitum ojasā*, Kiṣ 11.67, 70); vānaras fight with nails, teeth and tree-trunks, not bows. The **arrow** belongs to Rāma alone. See §VII.

## III. Dundubhi and Māyāvī — the seeds of the feud (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 9, 11)

- **Sons of Maya** - Vālmīki is precise about the pair: **Māyāvī is the elder brother of Dundubhi, and both are sons of the dānava architect Maya** (*dundubheḥ pūrvajo… mayasya sutaḥ*, Kiṣ **9.4**) — which makes them **Mandodarī's brothers**, and Rāvaṇa's brothers-in-law. Vālī's two great feud-victims are the queen of Laṅkā's own kin, a linkage the epic never draws attention to. See [Mandodarī](./mandodari.md) and [Maya Dānava](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/maya-danava.md).
- **Māyāvī and the quarrel over a woman** - The **enmity between Māyāvī and Vālī was over a woman** (*strī-kṛtam*, Kiṣ 9.4). Māyāvī comes by night to Kiṣkindhā's gate, blaring a challenge while the city sleeps, and Vālī goes out to him — the fight that leads into the cave (§IV).
- **Dundubhi the buffalo-demon** - The younger brother, in the form of a giant **buffalo (mahiṣa)**, craving battle, challenges the ocean and the Himālaya, both of whom send him to **Vālī**. Vālī fights and kills him, then **swings the carcass up in both arms and flings it a full yojana in a single flick** (Kiṣ **11.47**); blood-drops from its mouth, carried on the wind, fall in sage **Mātaṅga's** hermitage.
- **Mātaṅga's curse** - Enraged at the defilement, Mātaṅga curses that the monkey who threw the corpse **must not set foot within one full yojana of the hermitage, and if he does, he dies on the spot** (Kiṣ **11.53–55**) — not, as the retellings have it, that his head will burst. The sage further orders Vālī's dependants out of his woods. This is what makes **Ṛṣyamūka** the one refuge Vālī cannot follow Sugrīva into — and the place where Rāma meets them.
  - *Variant:* the cave-challenger is named **Dundubhi himself** in some retellings, conflating the two brothers. [variant]

## IV. The cave, the sealing, and the usurpation (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 9–10)

- **The pursuit into the cave** - Vālī chases the fleeing demon (Māyāvī) into a deep **cave** and orders Sugrīva to **wait at the mouth**. Sugrīva keeps watch for a long span — commonly **a year**.
- **The deceiving signs** - At last Sugrīva sees **blood / foam flow out** of the cave and hears the demon's roars (but not Vālī's); concluding that **Vālī is dead** and the demon may emerge to ravage the kingdom, he **rolls a great boulder over the cave-mouth** to trap the demon, performs Vālī's funerary rites, and returns to Kiṣkindhā.
- **Sugrīva crowned** - The ministers, believing the king dead, install **Sugrīva** on the throne — by his account, reluctantly and only to prevent anarchy.
- **Vālī's return and rage** - Vālī (having in fact slain the demon) breaks out, returns to find Sugrīva enthroned, and reads it as **deliberate treachery and attempted murder** (the boulder meant to entomb *him*). He refuses every explanation.
- **The double dispossession** - Vālī **banishes Sugrīva, seizes his kingdom, and takes Sugrīva's wife Rumā** for himself — the act later cited as Vālī's decisive *adharma* (it makes Vālī an **ātatāyin**, an aggressor against whom lethal force is sanctioned).
- **Exile on Ṛṣyamūka** - Sugrīva flees with four faithful ministers — **Hanumān, Nala, Nīla, Tāra** (and others) — to **Ṛṣyamūka**, the one hill Vālī cannot enter (Mātaṅga's curse, §III).
  - *Variant (Sugrīva's culpability):* the ethics debate turns on whether Sugrīva genuinely erred in good faith or **opportunistically usurped** the throne while his living brother depended on him — making Vālī's anger partly just. [disputed]

## V. The alliance with Rāma — fire-witnessed friendship (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 4–7)

- **The meeting via Hanumān** - Seeing the wandering **Rāma** and **Lakṣmaṇa** approach Ṛṣyamūka, the wary Sugrīva sends **Hanumān** (in brāhmaṇa-student disguise) to test them; recognising allies, Hanumān carries them on his shoulders to Sugrīva.
- **Sītā's jewels** - Sugrīva produces a bundle of **ornaments** that Sītā had **dropped from Rāvaṇa's flying chariot** as it passed over Ṛṣyamūka; Rāma identifies them, fixing the alliance's purpose.
- **The agni-sākṣi pact** - Rāma and Sugrīva swear friendship **with fire as witness (agni-sākṣi)**, circling a sacred flame: **Rāma will kill Vālī and restore Sugrīva's throne and wife; Sugrīva will marshal the vānaras to find and recover Sītā.** This reciprocal vow is the structural pivot of the epic.

---

## VI. Vālī and Rāvaṇa — the pinned emperor (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa)

- **The humiliation of Rāvaṇa** - When **Rāvaṇa** came to challenge Vālī, Vālī (mid-prayer at the four oceans) simply **tucked Rāvaṇa under his arm** and carried him while completing his circuit, leaving the rākṣasa emperor helpless. The two then made friendship over the fire. This back-story magnifies Vālī's might — underscoring that **only Rāma** could fell him. (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa.)

---

## VII. The proof Rāma must give, and the first duel (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 11–12)

- **Sugrīva's doubt, and the exact test he sets** - Sugrīva fears Rāma cannot defeat the prodigious Vālī, and names two conditions. **If Rāma can rend one tree out of the seven with a single arrow** (Kiṣ **11.71**), and **if he can lift and kick Dundubhi's skeleton two hundred bow-lengths with one foot** (11.72), then he will believe Vālī already dead.
- **Rāma's twin proofs** - Rāma **flicks the buffalo's skeleton away with his big toe** — far past the distance asked — and then looses one arrow that pierces **all seven sāla trees**, and the mountain behind, and the earth beneath, and **returns to his quiver** (Kiṣ **12**). He is asked for one tree and gives seven: the surplus is the point. Sugrīva is convinced.

## VIII. The death of Vālī (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 12–22)

- **The first challenge fails** - In the same sarga as the proof, Sugrīva goes to Kiṣkindhā and **roars a challenge**; Vālī comes out and the brothers fight. Because they are **identical in form**, Rāma — hidden — **cannot tell them apart** and dares not shoot; Vālī thrashes Sugrīva, who flees back to Ṛṣyamūka and bitterly asks why Rāma stood by. Rāma apologises: he could not tell one brother from the other, and tells Sugrīva to **wear a garland** for the rematch (Kiṣ **12**).
- **The garland-mark** - **Lakṣmaṇa** ties the **gajapuṣpī** garland around Sugrīva's neck (Kiṣ **12**). Retellings that give the act to Hanumān are at best a variant. [variant]
- **Tārā's counsel, ignored** - Before the second duel **Tārā** warns Vālī not to go out — she has heard from Aṅgada that Sugrīva has returned so quickly and so confidently only because he has made an ally of Rāma, whose prowess she recounts. Vālī dismisses her, and goes (Kiṣ **15**).
- **The fatal arrow** - During the second duel Rāma shoots Vālī **from concealment**; the arrow strikes Vālī's chest and he falls mortally wounded (Kiṣ **16**).
- **Vālī's dying reproach** - Vālī, dharma-learned, rebukes Rāma in a famous speech: *What crime had I done you? You, a kṣatriya prince, struck me — a creature you were not even fighting — from hiding, like a hunter felling game. Had you asked, I would have brought you Sītā and bound Rāvaṇa in a day.* He charges Rāma with **adharma, hypocrisy, and killing for another's gain**.
- **Vālī's reconciliation, and the handing over of victory** - Hearing Rāma's reply (§IX), the dying Vālī **accepts the justice of it and begs forgiveness**. He then calls Sugrīva to him, tells him to honour Tārā's counsel in all things and to keep faith with Rāma "because you befriended him before the fire," takes off the **golden pendant Indra gave him** and tells his brother to put it on — the goddess of triumph lives in it and will leave it at his death — and commends **Aṅgada** to Sugrīva's care. Then he dies (Kiṣ **22.14–22**).
- **Tārā's lament** - Tārā's grief-speech over Vālī's body is among the Rāmāyaṇa's celebrated *vilāpa* (lament) passages.
  - *Variant (Tārā's curse):* in several tellings Tārā, by the power of her chastity, **curses Rāma** that he too will soon lose his wife after regaining her. In the Bengali **Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇa** she adds that **in his next birth Rāma will be killed by Vālī**. [variant][folk]

## IX. The ethics of Vālī-vadha — the great dharma debate

The killing of Vālī from concealment is the single most-debated act in the Rāmāyaṇa. The accusations, and the classical replies, run:

- **Charge 1 — Killing from ambush is unksatriya.** Reply: Vālī was not Rāma's battlefield foe in single combat but **game to be culled**; kings and hunters lawfully take beasts (vānaras) by trap or hidden shot, and dharma permits striking a fugitive sinner where found.
- **Charge 2 — This was a private quarrel between brothers; Rāma had no standing.** Reply (attributed to Rāma/Lakṣmaṇa): Rāma acts as agent of **Bharata's** sovereignty (the **Ikṣvāku** writ to punish wrong throughout the land — the **daṇḍa** authority of the crown), so the realm's king-by-proxy may punish a criminal anywhere.
- **Charge 3 — Why not a fair fight?** Reply (standard): the **half-strength boon** (§II) made frontal combat hopeless — but that boon is **not in Vālmīki** [disputed], so the argument has no textual footing; the commentators' surviving reply is that in a fair fight a **defeated Vālī would have surrendered at Rāma's feet, forcing Rāma to spare him**, after which Vālī would resume his crimes — so the hidden shot was the only sure justice.
- **Charge 4 — Vālī's specific guilt.** Reply: by **seizing his living brother's wife Rumā**, Vālī became an **ātatāyin** (a class of grave aggressor — including one who abducts another's wife) whom śāstra says a king must **slay on sight, without trial**. Rāma's wording is the hinge: a man has three fathers — his own, his elder brother, his teacher — and correspondingly a **younger brother is reckoned one's own son**, so that brother's wife is one's ***snuṣā***, **daughter-in-law**. What Vālī did to Rumā is therefore not adultery but incest by the rule of the household, and that is the crux (Kiṣ **18.13–14**, **18.18–19**). See [Rumā](./ruma.md).
- **Charge 5 — Rāma's own bias.** Reply: Rāma had **sworn friendship before fire** to Sugrīva; upholding the vow was itself dharma.
- *Variant (karmic balancing — the Jara rebirth):* the **Mahānāṭaka** and **Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa** hold that the "unfair" killing is repaid in the next yuga: **Vālī is reborn as the hunter Jara** (Jarā) who, mistaking a foot for a deer, kills **[Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md)** (Rāma's later avatāra) with an arrow — closing the karmic circle. Not in canonical Vālmīki. [folk][late text] See [The Chiranjivis / avatāra continuity](../the-chiranjivis.md).
- *Variant (allegorical/devotional):* Vālī = the soul's ego/desire that must be slain "from behind" (i.e. by grace working unseen) for liberation; the dying Vālī's reconciliation reads as the ego's surrender. [folk]

## X. Sugrīva's coronation, the broken promise, and Lakṣmaṇa's wrath (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 25–36)

- **Coronation** - With Vālī dead, **Sugrīva is enthroned** at Kiṣkindhā and **Aṅgada** made *yuvarāja* at Rāma's suggestion; Rumā is restored to Sugrīva, and Tārā honoured. The rite is performed **by the vānara chiefs inside the city**: Rāma's exile-vow bars him and Lakṣmaṇa alike from entering any town, so both brothers wait outside (Kiṣ **26**). See [Aṅgada](./angada.md).
- **The forgetting** - The monsoon (**varṣā**) sets in; Rāma waits in a cave on Mālyavān/Pravarṣaṇa hill. Sugrīva, drowning in **drink, music, and the pleasures of two queens (Rumā and Tārā)**, utterly **forgets his vow** to search for Sītā.
- **Hanumān rouses him first — and this is the text, not a variant** - Before Lakṣmaṇa ever sets out, **Hanumān**, as a minister should, goes to Sugrīva and reminds him of the debt owed to Rāma. Sugrīva responds by ordering **Nīla** to muster every vānara host **within fifteen days on pain of death** — and then walks straight back into his pleasure-chambers, the order given and forgotten (Rām. Kiṣkindhā **29**). The fifteen-day muster and the counsel that produced it are core Vālmīki.
- **Lakṣmaṇa's fury, in three movements** - As autumn (**śarad**) comes and still nothing happens, the enraged **Lakṣmaṇa** storms Kiṣkindhā threatening to send Sugrīva "the way of Vālī." Sugrīva sends **Tārā** out to meet him at the gate and she brings him inside (Kiṣ **33**); Lakṣmaṇa delivers his reproach to Sugrīva (**34**); and Tārā answers it at length — the Viśvāmitra–Ghṛtācī precedent, Rāvaṇa's numbers, the host already on the roads — after which the chastened Sugrīva apologises and mobilises (**35–36**). The speech is treated in full at [Tārā](./tara-vanara-queen.md).

## XI. The search for Sītā — vānaras to the four directions (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 37–66)

- **The great muster** - Sugrīva summons vānara armies from across the world and dispatches search-parties to the **four quarters**, with deadlines (commonly one month):
  - **East** — under **Vinata**.
  - **West** — under **Suṣeṇa** (Tārā's father).
  - **North** — under **Śatabali**.
  - **South** — under **Aṅgada**, with **Hanumān, Jāmbavān (the bear-king), Nala, Nīla, Tāra** — the decisive party, for Sītā lies south in Laṅkā.
- **The southern party's ordeal** - They wander, near despair, into **Svayamprabhā's** enchanted cave; emerging late and fearing death for failing the deadline, they resolve to fast unto death — until the vulture **Sampāti** (Jaṭāyu's brother) tells them Sītā is held in **Laṅkā across the ocean**.
- **Hanumān's leap** - Reminded of his powers by Jāmbavān, **Hanumān** leaps the sea to Laṅkā — the action that carries the epic into the [Sundara-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md).
- **The war debt repaid** - Sugrīva and his hosts (Aṅgada, Hanumān, Nala the bridge-builder, Nīla, Jāmbavān) then fight in the [Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md), honouring the alliance and destroying [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md).

---

## XII. Later fates and afterlife of the figures

- **Sugrīva** - Rules Kiṣkindhā as Rāma's steadfast ally. At Rāma's **mahāprasthāna** he first **installs his nephew Aṅgada** on the throne of Kiṣkindhā, then follows Rāma and **enters the orb of the sun**, his father — while Jāmbavān, with Mainda and Dvivida, is granted life until the Kali Yuga (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa **108**).
- **Aṅgada** - Vālī's son; a great war-hero (his unmovable foot in Rāvaṇa's court is celebrated), inherits Kiṣkindhā.
- **Tārā** - Passes into Sugrīva's household and is counted among the **Pañcakanyā**; her lament endures in the rhetorical tradition, and the curse on Rāma that later tellings give her (not in Vālmīki, §VIII) travels with her name. See [Tārā](./tara-vanara-queen.md).
- *Variant (Vālī = Jara):* see §IX — the karmic rebirth thread linking Vālī's death to Kṛṣṇa's. [folk][late text]
