# Sugrīva & Vālī

> The two **vānara** brothers of **Kiṣkindhā** — **Vālī (Bāli)**, son of **Indra**, and **Sugrīva**, son of **Sūrya** — whose fratricidal feud over the throne and over a sealed cave drives the whole of the [Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md). Their quarrel pulls **[Rāma](../ramayana/README.md)** into the most-debated act of his career: the killing of Vālī from concealment, and the alliance with Sugrīva that launches the search for **Sītā**.
> Sits in **[Tretā-yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current [28th Mahāyuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md).
> Up: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md) · Antagonist whom Sugrīva's army will help destroy: [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md) · The vānara general of this alliance: see [Beings & Bestiary](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/README.md).

**Vālī** (Sanskrit *Vālin*, "the Tailed One"; also **Bāli**) and **Sugrīva** ("Fair-Necked / Beautiful-Necked") are the ruling **vānara** brothers of **Kiṣkindhā**, a city built inside a mountain cave (*guhā*, VR 4.27.1, 4.33.4), near the **Ṛṣyamūka** hill and the Pampā lake. Vālmīki gives them a double paternity: both are sons of the vānara king **Ṛkṣarajas**, and Vālī is also the son of **Indra** and Sugrīva the son of **Sūrya**. Vālī is the elder, the stronger and the king; Sugrīva is the younger. Their story turns on a mistaken death, a sealed cave and broken trust. Sugrīva seals his brother into a cave believing him dead. Vālī comes back and takes Sugrīva's throne **and wife**. The exiled Sugrīva then makes an alliance with Rāma before the sacred fire, and Rāma kills Vālī with a single arrow shot from cover. The killing is the Rāmāyaṇa's central **dharma-puzzle**. The alliance is its engine, for it is Sugrīva's vānara hosts (with **Hanumān**) who find Sītā and build the bridge to Laṅkā.

## Editions and citations

- **VR** = the Southern vulgate as printed and translated on valmikiramayan.net (Desiraju Hanumanta Rao), cited kāṇḍa.sarga.verse. **CE** = the Baroda Critical Edition (GRETIL plain text). In the Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa the two agree in sarga numbers up to 23. The CE has no equivalent of VR 24, so from sarga 25 onward VR *n* = CE *n*−1. Verse numbers inside a sarga often differ, and both are given where it matters.
- **Sh** = H. P. Shastri's translation of the vulgate Uttara-kāṇḍa (wisdomlib), whose chapter numbers include the *prakṣipta* (interpolated) chapters 37b–37f. The CE Uttara has 100 sargas; vulgate 108 = CE 98.
- **MBh** = the Bhandarkar Critical Edition of the Mahābhārata. Its *Rāmopākhyāna* (3.257–275) is a short, independent telling of the same story (see the section at the end).
- Notes by the valmikiramayan.net translator are cited as "VR note". They are commentary, not verse text.

## I. Birth and parentage — the two divine fathers (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa; Uttara-kāṇḍa)

- **Both fathers are in the core text.** Vālmīki names the vānara king **Ṛkṣarajas** as the brothers' father and also calls them sons of Indra and Sūrya, without explaining how the two fit together. The witnesses are these:
  - **Ṛkṣarajas as father.** Aṅgada tells Sampāti that his grandfather was the vānara king Ṛkṣarajas, whose two sons were Vālī and Sugrīva (VR 4.57.5–6). Dadhimukha says Madhuvana was never thrown open "by Ṛkṣarajas, nor by you, nor by Vālī" (VR 5.63.4 = CE 5.61.4). In the Uttara, Ṛkṣarajas is "father of Vālī and Sugrīva" and king of all the vānaras. When he died, the ministers made Vālī king in his father's place and put Sugrīva in Vālī's former place (CE 7.36.35–37). This matches Sugrīva's own account that the ministers crowned Vālī as the elder (VR 4.9.2).
  - **Indra and Sūrya as fathers.** In the Bāla-kāṇḍa, when the gods beget vānaras to help Viṣṇu, Indra begets Vālī and the Sun begets Sugrīva (VR 1.17.10). The CE has only the epithets "Sūrya's son Sugrīva and Śakra's son Vālī" (CE 1.16.19). Kabandha calls Sugrīva *ṛkṣarajasaḥ putraḥ* and *bhāskarasyaurasaḥ putraḥ*, "Ṛkṣarajas's son" and "the Sun's own son", in a single verse (CE 3.68.16; the vulgate's numbering splits it across VR 3.72.20–21). Himavat calls Vālī Indra's son (VR 4.11.21; the CE reads "equal to Śakra in valour", *śakratulyaparākramaḥ*), and Vālī wears a golden garland "given by his father Mahendra" (CE 4.11.37 = VR 4.11.39). The Mahābhārata has the same pair of epithets (MBh 3.147.25).
- **How the two fathers are reconciled — a late stratum, and flagged as such.** The famous birth-story is in the vulgate Uttara's interpolated chapter **37b** (Sh 37b), after Rāma asks Agastya who the brothers' mother was. It is absent from the CE. In it, Ṛkṣarajas is born from Brahmā's tears. Seeing his reflection in a lake on Meru, he takes it for an enemy, plunges in, and comes out as a woman. **Indra** and **Sūrya**, passing by, desire her:
  - **Vālī** - Indra's seed falls on the woman's *hair* (*vāla*), hence **Vālin**. Indra gives the child a lasting golden chain.
  - **Sugrīva** - the Sun's seed falls on her *neck* (*grīvā*), hence **Sugrīva**. The Sun appoints **Hanumān** to serve him.
  - The next morning Ṛkṣarajas becomes a monkey again. Brahmā then has his messenger install Ṛkṣarajas as king in **Kiṣkindhā**, a city Viśvakarmā built (Sh 37b). The chapter ends by saying that Ṛkṣarajas was both father and mother to the two.
  - *Textual status:* the chapter is *prakṣipta* even within the Uttara and lies outside the CE. It is later than the already-late Uttara. It **elaborates** on the older double paternity, which is already in Kabandha's verse (CE 3.68.16); it does not contradict it. What is late is the transformation story, not the divine fathers. [interpolation] [late text]
- *Variant (Aruṇa as mother, Ahalyā as foster-mother):* this telling is found in Vettam Mani's *Purāṇic Encyclopaedia* (1975), s.vv. "Aruṇa I" and "Bāli", which cites "M.B. Ādi Parva 107; Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa 42; Uttara Rāmāyaṇa and Kampa Rāmāyaṇa, Pūrvakāṇḍa". In it the Sun's charioteer **Aruṇa** takes a female form (**Aruṇīdevī**) to watch the apsaras dance. Indra fathers Vālī on her, and the Sun then fathers Sugrīva the same way. Both boys are raised by **Ahalyā**, until **Gautama** curses them into monkeys. Indra then gives them to the childless Ṛkṣarāja of Kiṣkindhā and blesses Vālī with the half-strength boon (§II). I have not checked the texts Mani cites. Popular summaries credit this story to the *Kathāsaritsāgara*, but I could not verify that. [variant] [late text] [unverified]
- **Tārā — Vālī's queen, afterwards Sugrīva's** - the daughter of **Suṣeṇa** (*suṣeṇaduhitā*, VR/CE 4.22.13). Sugrīva greets Suṣeṇa as his own father-in-law when he sends him west (VR 4.42.2). She is the mother of **Aṅgada** and the shrewdest political head in the kāṇḍa: she reads Sugrīva's second challenge correctly (§VIII) and disarms Lakṣmaṇa's rage (§X). She is the fourth name in the **Pañcakanyā** verse.
  - *Variant (CE Uttara):* the CE's Uttara 7.34.4 names **Tāra**, "Tārā's father", as the minister who answers Rāvaṇa at Kiṣkindhā. Shastri's vulgate has "Tārā, her father Suṣeṇa and Sugrīva" (Sh 34). [variant]
  - Her three scenes, the churning-born-apsaras variant and the three-Suṣeṇas harmonisation are all at **[Tārā, queen of Kiṣkindhā](./tara-vanara-queen.md)**.
- **Aṅgada** - son of Vālī and Tārā. He is made *yuvarāja* (crown prince) at Rāma's suggestion (VR 4.26.12–13, 26.38) and is a key commander in Rāma's war. See [Aṅgada](./angada.md).
- **Rumā — Sugrīva's queen** - Vālī takes her during the feud (VR 4.10.27), and she is restored to Sugrīva after Vālī's death (VR 4.26.42). That seizure is the named offence in Rāma's defence of the killing (§IX). She has no speech in Vālmīki. Only the vulgate gives her a father, and even it does not name him: at the muster "Rumā's father, Sugrīva's father-in-law" arrives with a thousand crores of vānaras (VR 4.39.16; the verse is absent from CE 4.38). Full dossier: **[Rumā](./ruma.md)**.

## II. Vālī's strength and the half-strength boon

- **Indra's golden garland (kāñcana-mālā)** - Vālī wears a divine golden chain given by his father **Indra** (CE 4.11.37 = VR 4.11.39). Tārā, mourning him, says Indra gave it to him "pleased with him in battle" (CE 4.23.28). Vālmīki says two things about its power, and neither is about multiplying strength in combat:
  - After the arrow strikes, the garland given by Śakra "held" Vālī's life-breaths, his lustre and his splendour (*śakradattā varā mālā … dadhāra … prāṇān tejaḥ śriyaṃ ca*, VR/CE 4.17.4–5). This is why he can live long enough to argue with Rāma.
  - Dying, he gives it to **Sugrīva** and tells him to put it on. *udārā śrīḥ sthitā hy asyām*, "the bounteous goddess of fortune dwells in it", and she will leave it once he is dead (VR/CE 4.22.16–18).
  - The gift is the transfer of royal fortune from the elder brother to the younger, made by the elder's own hand, and it is the most under-read moment in the feud. The common gloss that the necklace "restores energy and multiplies his power in combat" is not in the verse text. The VR translator's note at 4.11.44 makes that claim: wearing the pendant drains half an opponent's strength.
- **The half-strength boon** - In widely-told tradition, Vālī has a boon that **whoever fights him face-to-face loses half his strength to him**. This makes frontal combat suicidal and is the standard *explanation* for why Rāma struck from concealment.
  - *Textual caveat:* **the boon is not in the verse text of Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa (vulgate or CE) or in Tulsīdās's Rāmcaritmānas.** The sources that carry it name two different givers. [disputed] [commentarial] [folk]
    - **Brahmā** — an unnamed verse quoted in the VR note to 4.17.48 says Brahmā summoned Vālī and gave him the boon (*āhūya vālinaṃ brahmā dadau varam anuttamam*).
    - **Indra / "the gods"** — Vettam Mani has Indra give the boon at the end of the Aruṇī story (§I). In his summary of the *Kampa Rāmāyaṇa*, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa, Rāma himself names the boon to the dying Vālī as one reason for not facing him (Mani, "Bāli" §7).
    - **The garland** — the VR note at 4.11.44 grafts the effect onto Indra's garland, the one item Vālmīki *does* give Vālī.
- **Vālī's feats** - Before sunrise he strides untired from the western ocean to the eastern, and from the southern to the northern. He climbs mountain peaks, tosses them up and catches them again (CE/VR 4.11.4–5). The Uttara turns the ocean circuit into a daily *sandhyā* rite at the four oceans, during which he once **seized Rāvaṇa** and carried him along (§VI).
- **The seven sāla trees, correctly** - Vālmīki's Vālī does **not** shoot the trees. Sugrīva shows Rāma seven great sālas and says Vālī can strip any one of them leafless by sheer strength (*yatraikaṃ ghaṭate vālī niṣpatrayitum ojasā*, CE 4.11.47; "one at a time", VR 4.11.67, 70). The Bāla-kāṇḍa arms the vānaras with rocks, trees, nails and teeth (CE 1.16.13). The **arrow** belongs to Rāma alone. See §VII.

## III. Dundubhi and Māyāvī — the seeds of the feud (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 9, 11)

- **Sons of Maya** - Kiṣkindhā 9.4 calls **Māyāvī** "the elder brother of Dundubhi, a son" (*pūrvajo dundubheḥ sutaḥ*, VR/CE 4.9.4) but does not name the father. The word "Maya" is the VR translator's bracket. The parentage is stated in the Uttara: the dānava **Maya** tells Rāvaṇa that his wife Hemā bore him two sons, "Māyāvī first, then Dundubhi" (CE 7.12.12; Sh 12), in the same speech in which he gives Rāvaṇa their sister **Mandodarī**. So Vālī's two great feud-victims are the queen of Laṅkā's own brothers and Rāvaṇa's brothers-in-law. The epic's verses state each half of this, in different kāṇḍas, but never connect them; the commentator Govindarāja does, reading Kiṣ 9.4 through Maya's Uttara speech (VR note to 4.46.3). See [Mandodarī](./mandodari.md) and [Maya Dānava](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/maya-danava.md).
- **Māyāvī and the quarrel over a woman** - The **enmity between Māyāvī and Vālī was "over a woman"** (*strīkṛtam*, VR/CE 4.9.4); the woman is not named. Māyāvī comes to Kiṣkindhā's gate at night while the city sleeps and roars a challenge. Vālī rushes out despite the women and Sugrīva trying to hold him back (VR/CE 4.9.5–8). This is the fight that leads into the cave (§IV).
- **Dundubhi the buffalo-demon** - The younger brother takes the form of a giant **buffalo** (*mahiṣa*), with the strength of a thousand elephants, maddened by pride and by a boon he has won (CE 4.11.7–8). He challenges the Ocean, who sends him to Himavat, who sends him to **Vālī** (CE 4.11.9–23). At Kiṣkindhā's gate Dundubhi taunts the drunken Vālī. Vālī sends Tārā and the women back, raises Indra's garland, seizes him by the horns and dashes him to the ground (CE 4.11.32–39). The vulgate adds a further fight with fists, knees, feet, rocks and trees before the buffalo dies (VR 4.11.40–46, the weapons at 4.11.43); in the CE he dies of the dashing. He then lifts the carcass in both arms and hurls it a full yojana in one throw (*cikṣepa … vegenaikena yojanam*, CE 4.11.40 = VR 4.11.47). Drops of blood from its mouth, carried on the wind, fall in sage **Mātaṅga's** hermitage.
- **Mātaṅga's curse** - The curse is reported in two forms, and both are Vālmīki's.
  - **Sugrīva's first telling (sarga 11).** In the vulgate, the monkey who threw the corpse must not set foot within one full yojana of the hermitage, and dies on the spot if he does (VR 4.11.53–55). Any of Vālī's monkeys found in the forest the next day will be turned to stone (VR 4.11.57–58). The CE has no radius: "he must not enter here; if he enters, his death follows" (CE 4.11.42). Vālī begs, and is refused (VR 4.11.62–63).
  - **Sugrīva's second telling (sarga 46).** Hanumān remembers the curse as "if Vālī enters, his head will split a hundredfold" (*mūrdhāsya śatadhā bhavet*, CE 4.45.15 = VR 4.46.22). The head-bursting of the retellings is therefore textual too.
  - Either way, this is why **Ṛṣyamūka** is the one refuge Vālī will not enter, or even look at (CE 4.11.44), and the place where Rāma meets the exiles.
  - *Variant (within Vālmīki):* in Sugrīva's second account the cave-enemy is **Dundubhi**, not Māyāvī. Vālī drives the buffalo to Mount **Malaya** and follows it into a cave there (CE 4.45.3–4 = VR 4.46.3–4). The VR note at 46.3 flags the clash with sarga 11, where Dundubhi dies at Kiṣkindhā. Later compilations follow this conflation; Mani's entry "Sugrīva I", for example, has Vālī chase Dundubhi into the cave. [variant]

## IV. The cave, the sealing, and the usurpation (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 9–10)

- **The pursuit into the cave** - The terrified demon (Māyāvī) flees by moonlight into a grass-covered hole in the earth (VR/CE 4.9.10–11). Vālī orders Sugrīva to **wait at the mouth**. When Sugrīva asks to come in too, Vālī makes him swear by his feet to stay, and goes in (VR/CE 4.9.13–14). Sugrīva waits "a year and more" (*sāgraḥ saṃvatsaraḥ*, VR 4.9.15).
- **The deceiving signs** - At last foaming blood flows out of the cave, and Sugrīva hears the roars of asuras but not his brother's voice (VR/CE 4.9.17–18). He concludes that **Vālī is dead** and closes the cave-mouth with a mountain-sized rock (*śilayā girimātrayā*). He offers the funeral water-libation and returns to Kiṣkindhā (CE 4.9.19).
  - *His motive for the rock.* In the second account Sugrīva says he blocked the cave so that the buffalo could not get out and would die inside (CE 4.45.7 = VR 4.46.7).
  - *Variant (milk or blood):* Mani's entry "Bāli" §4 adds a pre-arranged sign. He cites Vālmīki Kiṣkindhā 10 and the *Kampa Rāmāyaṇa*, but the sign is not in VR or CE sarga 10. Vālī tells Sugrīva that milk at the cave-mouth means the asura is dead and blood means Vālī is, and the demon's magic makes the blood appear. Not in Vālmīki. [variant]
- **Sugrīva crowned** - Sugrīva says he tried to hide what had happened, but the ministers found it out and consecrated him (*gūhamānasya me tattvam … mantribhiḥ śrutam*, CE 4.9.20). He later tells Vālī they made him king by force, to keep a kingless realm in hand (CE 4.10.6).
- **Vālī's return and rage** - Vālī has in fact killed the demon "with all his kin" (VR 4.10.20). He finds the exit blocked, shouts "Sugrīva!" again and again with no answer, and kicks the rock away (VR 4.10.21–24). Finding Sugrīva crowned, he puts Sugrīva's ministers in chains (CE 4.9.22). Sugrīva touches his brother's feet with his crown (VR 4.9.26; not in the CE), offers back the royal umbrella and calls the kingdom a trust held for him (CE 4.10.2–5). Vālī answers with abuse. Before the assembled citizens he calls the sealing a plot to trap him so that Sugrīva could have the kingdom (VR 4.10.13–25).
- **The double dispossession** - Vālī drives Sugrīva out with only one garment on (VR 4.10.26) and **takes his wife Rumā** (VR 4.10.27). This is the act Rāma later names as Vālī's guilt (§IX).
  - **What Sugrīva himself admits (sarga 46).** In his second account Sugrīva says that as king he held "the great kingdom, and **Tārā together with Rumā**" (*rājyaṃ ca sumahat prāptaṃ tārā ca rumayā saha*, CE 4.45.8 = VR 4.46.8–9). He also says he handed the kingdom back out of respect and fear, and that Vālī then chased him across the earth (CE 4.45.9–13), through all four quarters in the vulgate (VR 4.46.10–20). So in Vālmīki's own text, each brother at some point holds the other's wife.
- **Exile on Ṛṣyamūka** - Sugrīva flees with **four** companions (*caturbhiḥ saha vānaraiḥ*, CE 3.68.12, 4.8.44; MBh 3.263.41). He wanders the whole earth before Hanumān remembers Mātaṅga's curse and they settle on **Ṛṣyamūka**, the one hill Vālī cannot enter (VR 4.46.12–24; §III). That flight is where Sugrīva learned the geography he later uses to brief the search parties (VR 4.46.1–2).
  - *Who the four are:* Vālmīki's verses up to Kiṣkindhā 11 name only Hanumān as their head (*hanumatpramukhāḥ*, CE 4.8.35). The list **Hanumān, Nala, Nīla, Tāra** comes from the VR translator's note to 4.6.11. The Mahābhārata's Tārā names a different set of Sugrīva's counsellors: Mainda, Dvivida, Hanumān and Jāmbavān (MBh 3.264.23). [variant]
  - *Why Hanumān did not fight Vālī:* the Uttara says that under a sages' curse Hanumān did not know his own strength when the brothers fell out (CE 7.36.39–40; see [Hanumān](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md)).
  - *Variant (Sugrīva's culpability):* the ethics debate turns on whether Sugrīva honestly erred or **opportunistically usurped** the throne while his living brother depended on him. On the second reading Vālī's anger is partly just. Vālī's dying words concede only that Sugrīva's succession *after his death* is proper (VR 4.17.52). [disputed]

## V. The alliance with Rāma — fire-witnessed friendship (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 4–7)

- **Kabandha's direction** - The alliance is first proposed by the freed **[Kabandha](./kabandha.md)**. He tells Rāma to go to Sugrīva, who lives on Ṛṣyamūka with four vānaras, and to make him a friend "with the blazing fire as witness" (*dīpyamāne vibhāvasau*, CE 3.68.11–17 = VR 3.72.11–21).
- **The meeting via Hanumān** - Seeing **Rāma** and **Lakṣmaṇa** approach, the wary Sugrīva fears they are Vālī's agents. He sends **Hanumān**, who drops his monkey form and appears as a mendicant (*bhikṣurūpa*, VR 4.3.2; CE 4.3.3) to test them. Once he is satisfied, Hanumān takes his own form, sets the two princes on his **back** (*pṛṣṭham āropya*, VR 4.4.34), and brings them to Sugrīva, who has moved to Mount **Malaya** (VR 4.5.1).
- **The agni-sākṣi pact** - Sugrīva holds out his hand (VR 4.5.11). Hanumān kindles a fire by friction, honours it with flowers and sets it between them, and Rāma and Sugrīva walk around it (VR 4.5.13–16). Each then states his side of the bargain. Rāma promises Vālī's death by his arrows (VR 4.5.25–28). Sugrīva asks for his wife and kingdom back (VR 4.5.30) and promises to recover Sītā "whether she is in the nether worlds or in heaven" (VR 4.6.5–6). This reciprocal vow is the structural pivot of the epic.
- **Sītā's jewels** - Sugrīva says that he was one of five vānaras on the mountaintop when Sītā, carried overhead by Rāvaṇa, dropped her upper cloth and ornaments among them (VR 4.6.11). He brings out the bundle from a cave, and Rāma breaks down on recognising it (VR 4.6.14–20). The verse in which Lakṣmaṇa knows only the anklets (VR 4.6.22) is absent from the CE.

## VI. Vālī and Rāvaṇa — the pinned emperor (Rām. Uttara-kāṇḍa)

- **The humiliation of Rāvaṇa** - Agastya tells this to Rāma (CE 7.34; Sh 34).
  - Rāvaṇa, recently released by Kārtavīrya Arjuna, comes to Kiṣkindhā to challenge Vālī. He is told that Vālī is away doing his *sandhyā* at the four oceans, and is shown the heaps of bones of earlier challengers (CE 7.34.1–9).
  - He finds Vālī at the southern ocean and creeps up to seize him from behind. Vālī hears his footsteps and, without turning round, grabs him and hooks him under his arm (*kakṣāvalambinam*; Shastri renders it "girdle"). Rāvaṇa's ministers give chase, but Vālī flies on and completes his rites at the western, northern and eastern oceans with Rāvaṇa still hanging at his side (CE 7.34.12–31).
  - Set down at last in a grove at Kiṣkindhā, the exhausted Rāvaṇa praises his strength and asks for friendship before the fire. They become brothers over a kindled fire, with wives, sons, city and wealth to be shared, and Rāvaṇa stays a month at Kiṣkindhā "like a second Sugrīva" (CE 7.34.32–42).
  - Agastya closes by saying that Rāma consumed Vālī, for all his strength, as a fire consumes a moth (CE 7.34.43–44).
- *Variant (the tail):* Mani's "Bāli" §6, cited to an "Uttara Rāmāyaṇa", has Vālī wind his **tail** around Rāvaṇa and bind him like a bundle of sticks. In this version Vālī's minister Tāra has already told Rāvaṇa of the half-strength boon. Vālmīki's Uttara (CE 7.34) has the armpit, not the tail. [variant]
- The episode magnifies Vālī's might and underscores that **only Rāma** could fell him. It also makes Vālī Rāvaṇa's sworn fire-witnessed brother, which sharpens Vālī's dying boast that he would have brought Rāvaṇa bound by the neck (VR 4.17.49–50; §VIII).

## VII. The proof Rāma must give, and the first duel (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 11–12)

- **Sugrīva's doubt, and the exact test he sets** - Sugrīva fears that Rāma cannot defeat Vālī. Lakṣmaṇa asks what deed would convince him (VR 4.11.69). In the vulgate Sugrīva names two tests: rend **one tree out of the seven** with a single arrow (VR 4.11.71), and kick Dundubhi's skeleton **two hundred bow-lengths** (VR 4.11.72). The CE has neither Lakṣmaṇa's question nor the kick-test. There Sugrīva asks Rāma to pierce "these sālas", in the plural, with one arrow (*yadi bhindyād bhavān sālān imāṃs tv ekeṣuṇā*, CE 4.11.49).
- **Rāma's twin proofs** - Rāma flicks the buffalo's skeleton away with his big toe to a distance of **ten yojanas** (VR 4.11.84–85; CE 4.11.50, *daśayojanam*). Sugrīva objects that the bones are now dry and light, while Vālī threw a fresh carcass full of flesh and blood, so that this proves nothing, and asks for the tree-test (VR 4.11.86–92; CE 4.11.51–52). Rāma's single golden arrow then pierces **all seven sālas**, the mountain and the earth, and returns to his quiver (VR/CE 4.12.2–4). In the vulgate he is asked for one tree and splits seven: the surplus is the point. Sugrīva falls at his feet (VR 4.12.5–9).

## VIII. The death of Vālī (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 12–22)

- **The first challenge fails** - In the same sarga the party goes to Kiṣkindhā and hides among trees, and Sugrīva roars a challenge (VR 4.12.13–15). The brothers fight. Because they are **identical** in ornaments, dress, build, gait, voice and look, Rāma cannot tell them apart and does not shoot (VR 4.12.19–20, 30–31). Beaten and bloodied, Sugrīva flees to Ṛṣyamūka. Vālī stops at its edge for fear of the curse, calling out "you are let off" (VR 4.12.21–23). Sugrīva bitterly asks why Rāma stood by (VR 4.12.26–27). Rāma explains the likeness and tells him to fight again wearing a mark (VR 4.12.29–38).
- **The garland-mark** - At Rāma's word, **Lakṣmaṇa** ties a flowering **gajapuṣpī** creeper around Sugrīva's neck (VR 4.12.39–40).
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata):* in the Rāmopākhyāna there is no failed first duel. When the two brothers prove indistinguishable, **Hanumān** hangs a garland on Sugrīva's neck (*sugrīvasya tadā mālāṃ hanūmān kaṇṭha āsajat*, MBh 3.264.33). [variant]
- **Tārā's counsel, ignored** - Before the second duel **Tārā** urges Vālī to wait until morning. She says Sugrīva would not return so soon after his beating unless he had found support. She has heard through Aṅgada, from spies, that he has allied with Rāma, whose qualities she describes. She advises Vālī to make Sugrīva *yuvarāja* and end the feud (VR 4.15.6–30). Vālī refuses to bear the challenge. He trusts that a righteous man like Rāma will do no wrong, and promises her that he will only beat Sugrīva and **not take his life** (VR 4.16.2–10).
- **The fatal arrow** - Sugrīva tires in the second duel. Rāma, hidden, shoots, and the arrow strikes Vālī **in the chest**. He falls like Indra's festival-banner thrown to the ground (VR 4.16.31–37). The garland keeps life in him (§II).
- **Vālī's dying reproach** - Vālī, versed in dharma, rebukes Rāma at length (VR/CE 4.17). He was not fighting Rāma, and Rāma, born of Daśaratha, struck from hiding like a hunter. A vānara's skin and bones are not to be used and its flesh is not to be eaten, so a monkey is no lawful game (VR 4.17.39–40). Had Rāma faced him in open fight, Rāma would have died (VR 4.17.47; CE 4.17.39). He was killed "by one unseen", like a sleeper bitten by a snake (VR 4.17.48; CE 4.17.40). Had Rāma asked, Vālī would have brought him Rāvaṇa bound by the neck and fetched Sītā even from the sea or the nether world (CE 4.17.41–42; the vulgate adds "in a single day", VR 4.17.49–50). He charges Rāma with **adharma, hypocrisy and killing for another's gain**.
- **Vālī's reconciliation, and the handing over of fortune** - Hearing Rāma's reply (§IX), the dying Vālī accepts it and asks forgiveness. He commends **Aṅgada** and Tārā to Rāma's protection (VR 4.18.44–66). Then he turns to Sugrīva (VR/CE 4.22), and the order of what he says is:
  - He tells Sugrīva not to judge him by his faults, says the two of them were never fated to be happy together, and bids him take the kingship at once (VR 4.22.3–6).
  - He asks Sugrīva to foster Aṅgada as his own son (VR 4.22.8–12).
  - He tells him to follow Tārā's judgement, which is never wrong (VR/CE 4.22.13–14).
  - He tells him to accomplish Rāma's task, warning that failure would be *adharma* and that Rāma, slighted, might destroy him (*syād adharmo hy akaraṇe tvāṃ ca hiṃsyād vimānitaḥ*, CE 4.22.15). The VR translation adds "because you befriended him before an altar of fire", but that phrase is not in the Sanskrit.
  - He gives Sugrīva the golden garland in which fortune dwells (VR/CE 4.22.16–18).
  - He counsels Aṅgada to serve Sugrīva patiently and keep to a middle path (VR 4.22.19–23).
  - Then he dies (VR 4.22.24).
- **Tārā's lament** - Tārā's grief over Vālī's body (VR 4.19–20, 23) is among the Rāmāyaṇa's celebrated *vilāpa* (lament) passages. At CE 4.23.28 she misses Indra's garland on his chest. Only the vulgate has a further sarga (VR 24) in which Sugrīva, remorseful, wishes to die on the pyre and Tārā asks to be killed with her husband. The CE lacks it.
  - *Variant (Tārā's curse):* in several later tellings Tārā, by the power of her chastity, **curses Rāma** to lose his wife soon after regaining her. In the Bengali **Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇa** she adds that **in a future birth Rāma will be killed by Vālī**. This is reported by Bhattacharya (*Manushi* 141, 2004) and by Stewart and Dimock in *Questioning Ramayanas* (2001). The curse is not in the vulgate laments (VR 4.19–24, checked) or in the CE text. Guruge (1991) reports it in North-western manuscripts of Vālmīki; I have not seen that report myself. [variant] [folk]

## IX. The ethics of Vālī-vadha — the great dharma debate

The killing of Vālī from concealment is the single most-debated act in the Rāmāyaṇa. Vālī's charges are in VR/CE 4.17 and Rāma's answer in VR/CE 4.18. The accusations, and the replies, run:

- **Charge 1 — Killing from ambush is unkṣatriya.** Rāma's own reply: hunters lawfully strike beasts whether they are running or standing, wary or unwary, facing them or turned away, and in the vulgate royal sages versed in dharma hunt too (VR 4.18.37–40). Vālī is a *śākhāmṛga*, a "branch-animal", so it makes no difference whether he was fighting Rāma or not (VR 4.18.36–40; CE 4.18.34–36). Vālī has already argued that a monkey is not lawful game (VR 4.17.39–40). The VR note quotes Griffith's objection that he cannot see how Vālmīki could put such an excuse into Rāma's mouth. [disputed]
- **Charge 2 — This was a private quarrel between brothers; Rāma had no standing.** Rāma's reply: the whole earth, with its forests and mountains, belongs to the **Ikṣvākus**, and **Bharata** now rules it. Rāma and other kings travel the earth under Bharata's righteous command, punishing those who leave the path of dharma (VR 4.18.6–11, 23–25). This is the *daṇḍa* (punishing) authority of the crown, exercised by proxy.
- **Charge 3 — Why not a fair fight?** The popular reply is the **half-strength boon** (§II). But that boon is **not in Vālmīki's verse text** [disputed], so the argument has no footing there. The reply the commentators actually develop is reported in the VR note to sarga 18, from the *Dharmākūtam*: seeing Rāma in front of him, Vālī might have fled with Rumā, taken refuge with Rāvaṇa, surrendered to Rāma himself, or called out his whole army and dragged the war out. Rāma could not kill one who had surrendered, and his promise to kill Vālī that very day and give Sugrīva his wife and kingdom would have lapsed, so the hidden shot was the only way to keep the vow. [commentarial]
- **Charge 4 — Vālī's specific guilt.** Rāma's reply rests on kinship. A man who walks the way of dharma has three fathers: his own father, his elder brother, and his teacher. Correspondingly, **a younger brother, a son and a good disciple are to be reckoned one's sons** (VR 4.18.13–14). So Rumā is Vālī's ***snuṣā***, his **daughter-in-law**, and he has lived with her while Sugrīva is alive (VR 4.18.18–19; CE *snuṣāyāṃ*, 4.18.19).
  - The remembered penalty for a man who goes to his daughter, his sister or his younger brother's wife is death (VR 4.18.22–23; CE 4.18.22, *tasya daṇḍo vadhaḥ smṛtaḥ*).
  - Rāma cites two verses he attributes to Manu: punished sinners go to heaven, and a king who fails to punish takes on the sin (VR 4.18.30–32, = Manusmṛti 8.318 and 8.316).
  - He cites his ancestor **Māndhātā** as a precedent for royal punishment (VR 4.18.33).
  - He ends by saying that kings are gods in human form and are not to be reviled (VR 4.18.41–42).
  - Rāma never uses the word ***ātatāyin***, the śāstric class of aggressor who may be killed on sight and which includes an abductor of another's wife. Calling Vālī an *ātatāyin* is a later dharmaśāstric framing of the case, not Vālmīki's. See [Rumā](./ruma.md). [commentarial]
- **Charge 5 — Rāma's own bias.** Rāma's reply: his friendship with Sugrīva is like his bond with Lakṣmaṇa. He gave his promise before the vānaras and cannot break it, and one guided by dharma must help his friend (VR 4.18.26–29).
- **Rāma's consolation** - Both the punisher and the punished are freed by just punishment, and Vālī, cleansed, has regained his own good nature (VR 4.18.59–64).
- *Variant (karmic balancing — the Jarā rebirth):* in later tradition the "unfair" killing is repaid in the next yuga. The VR note to sarga 18 quotes the ***Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa*** (Sārakāṇḍa). There Rāma tells Vālī that at the end of the Dvāpara, in the form of a *bhilla* (tribal hunter), Vālī will pierce Rāma's foot with an arrow at **Prabhāsa**, "out of the old enmity". Bhattacharya (*Manushi* 141, 2004) also cites the ***Mahānāṭaka*** for the hunter who kills **[Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md)** being Vālī reborn; I have not seen that passage. The Mahābhārata's account of the hunter **Jarā** wounding Kṛṣṇa's foot does not name Vālī. The VR note adds that the *Bhāgavata* and *Padma* Purāṇas carry no such grant. Not in Vālmīki. [folk] [late text] See [The Chiranjivis / avatāra continuity](../the-chiranjivis.md).
- *Variant (allegorical/devotional):* Vālī stands for the soul's ego and desire, which must be slain "from behind", by grace working unseen, for liberation; the dying Vālī's reconciliation reads as the ego's surrender. The devotional retellings point the same way. In the *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa* the dying Vālī recognises Rāma as the Supreme. In Tulsīdās, Rāma offers to restore Vālī's life and Vālī declines. See [Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/4-kishkindha-kanda.md). [folk]

## X. Sugrīva's coronation, the broken promise, and Lakṣmaṇa's wrath (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 25–36)

- **Coronation** - With Vālī cremated (VR 4.25), Hanumān invites Rāma into Kiṣkindhā. Rāma replies that, keeping his father's command, he may not enter a village or a city for fourteen years (VR 4.26.9–10; CE 4.25.9). Sugrīva is therefore consecrated inside the city by the brāhmaṇas and the vānara chiefs (VR 4.26.29–37). At Rāma's suggestion he makes **Aṅgada** *yuvarāja* (VR 4.26.12–13, 26.38). He regains Rumā along with the kingdom (VR 4.26.42; CE 4.25.38). Rāma fixes the terms: Sugrīva is to rest through the four rain-months beginning with Śrāvaṇa, and to begin the search when **Kārttika** comes (VR 4.26.14–17; CE 4.25.12, 15). See [Aṅgada](./angada.md).
- **The forgetting** - The monsoon (**varṣā**) sets in. Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa live in a cave on Mount **Prasravaṇa**, on the ridge of **Mālyavān** (VR 4.27.1–4; CE 4.26.1, 4.27.1). Sugrīva, given over to drink, music and the women of his palace, with Tārā and Rumā beside him (VR 4.31.36–38), **forgets his vow** to search for Sītā.
- **Hanumān rouses him first — and this is the text, not a variant** - Before Lakṣmaṇa ever sets out, **Hanumān**, as a minister should, goes to Sugrīva and reminds him of the debt owed to Rāma (VR 4.29.1–27). Sugrīva orders **Nīla** to muster every vānara host, and any vānara who arrives after fifteen days is to be put to death (VR 4.29.29–32; CE 4.28.31). Then he goes straight back into his private chambers (VR 4.29.33). The fifteen-day muster and the counsel that produced it are core Vālmīki.
- **Lakṣmaṇa's fury, in three movements** - Autumn (**śarad**) comes and still nothing happens. Rāma sends the enraged **Lakṣmaṇa** to warn Sugrīva that "the path by which Vālī went is not closed" (VR 4.30.81; CE 4.29.48).
  - At the city gate Lakṣmaṇa is met by armed vānaras, and **Aṅgada** goes in to rouse his uncle (VR 4.31.23–38).
  - Lakṣmaṇa then enters the cave-city, passes seven courts, and twangs his bowstring near the women's quarters (VR 4.33.1–26).
  - The startled Sugrīva sends **Tārā** to him, reasoning that great men are not angry with women. Tārā comes, unsteady with wine, and calms him (VR 4.33.27–61).
  - Lakṣmaṇa delivers his reproach to Sugrīva (VR 4.34). Tārā answers it at length: the precedent of Viśvāmitra and Ghṛtācī, Rāvaṇa's numbers as Vālī used to tell them, and the hosts already on the roads (VR 4.35). The chastened Sugrīva apologises and mobilises (VR 4.36).
  - The speech is treated in full at [Tārā](./tara-vanara-queen.md).

## XI. The search for Sītā — vānaras to the four directions (Rām. Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa 37–66)

- **The great muster** - Sugrīva summons vānara armies from across the world (VR 4.37–39). Among the leaders are Śatabali, "Tārā's father" (the verse gives no name; the translation supplies Suṣeṇa), Hanumān's father Kesarī and the bear-king Jāmbavān (VR 4.39.14–39). He sends search-parties to the **four quarters**, each with a deadline of **one month**. The east and west parties are told that overstaying means death (VR 4.40.69–70, 42.52–53); the south party is promised a reward for news brought back within the month (VR 4.41.47–48):
  - **East** — under **Vinata** (VR 4.40.16–19).
  - **West** — under **Suṣeṇa**, Tārā's father and Sugrīva's father-in-law (VR 4.42.1–2).
  - **North** — under **Śatabali** (VR 4.43.1–3).
  - **South** — under **Aṅgada**, with Nīla, **Hanumān**, **[Jāmbavān](./jambavan.md)** and others named at VR 4.41.2–5 (Suhotra, Gaja, Gavākṣa, Gavaya, Mainda, Dvivida, Gandhamādana and more). This is the decisive party, for Sītā lies south in Laṅkā. **Tāra** travels with them (VR 4.53.25). **Nala** is not in the list.
- **Rāma's ring** - Trusting Sugrīva's confidence in Hanumān, Rāma gives him a ring engraved with his own name as a token for Sītā (VR 4.44.8–15).
- **The southern party's ordeal** - The party loses its way in the cave **Ṛkṣabila**, where **Svayamprabhā** feeds them and sets them outside (VR 4.50–52). Finding the month gone, they resolve to fast unto death (VR 4.53–55). The vulture **Sampāti**, Jaṭāyu's brother, then tells them that Sītā is held in **Laṅkā across the ocean** (VR 4.58).
- **Hanumān's leap** - Reminded of his powers by Jāmbavān (VR 4.66), **Hanumān** climbs Mount Mahendra (VR 4.67) and leaps the sea to Laṅkā, which carries the epic into the [Sundara-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/5-sundara-kanda.md).
- **The war debt repaid** - Sugrīva and his hosts (Aṅgada, Hanumān, Nala the bridge-builder, Nīla, Jāmbavān) then fight in the [Yuddha-kāṇḍa](../ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md), honouring the alliance and destroying [Rāvaṇa](./ravana.md).

## XII. Later fates and afterlife of the figures

- **Sugrīva** - Rules Kiṣkindhā as Rāma's steadfast ally. When Rāma prepares to leave the world, the vulgate has Sugrīva come to follow him, saying he has already installed **Aṅgada** as king (Sh 108). Rāma tells Jāmbavān to remain on earth until the Kali Yuga begins (Sh 108). After the ascent, Brahmā's closing speech reports that **Sugrīva has entered the orb of the sun**, his father (Sh 110).
  - *CE:* all three details are vulgate-only. In the CE Rāma charges Vibhīṣaṇa to rule while creatures endure and Hanumān to keep his vow of life, then tells all the bears and vānaras to come with him (CE 7.98.21–26). They enter the Sarayū, and each vānara and bear returns to the deity from whom he was born (CE 7.100.18–19). That implies Sugrīva's return to Sūrya without naming him.
- **Aṅgada** - Vālī's son; a great war-hero (his unmovable foot in Rāvaṇa's court is celebrated in later retellings), and inheritor of Kiṣkindhā.
- **Tārā** - Passes into Sugrīva's household and is counted among the **Pañcakanyā**. Her lament endures in the rhetorical tradition, and the curse on Rāma that later tellings give her (not in the vulgate or CE text, §VIII) travels with her name. See [Tārā](./tara-vanara-queen.md).
- *Variant (Vālī = Jarā):* see §IX — the karmic rebirth thread linking Vālī's death to Kṛṣṇa's. [folk] [late text]

## The Mahābhārata's Rāmopākhyāna — a second, shorter telling

The sage Mārkaṇḍeya tells the Rāma story to Yudhiṣṭhira (MBh 3.257–275). His version of the feud differs from Vālmīki's in ways that should not be merged into the Rāmāyaṇa account:

- The gandharva **Viśvāvasu**, released from the slain Kabandha's body, sends Rāma to Sugrīva. Sugrīva lives by Pampā near Ṛśyamūka with four ministers and is "brother of the gold-garlanded vānara king Vālī" (MBh 3.263.35–41).
- Rāma befriends Sugrīva and **consecrates him king of the vānaras himself, before the duel**. He then vows Vālī's death, and Sugrīva vows Sītā's recovery (MBh 3.264.11–15).
- Tārā warns Vālī, naming Rāma, Lakṣmaṇa and Sugrīva's counsellors Mainda, Dvivida, Hanumān and Jāmbavān. Vālī rebuffs her and suspects that her heart is with Sugrīva (MBh 3.264.16–26).
- There is a single duel. Hanumān marks Sugrīva with a garland, and Rāma shoots Vālī through the heart. Vālī sees Rāma standing nearby with Lakṣmaṇa, reproaches him and falls (MBh 3.264.27–38). Sugrīva takes Kiṣkindhā and Tārā (MBh 3.264.39). Rāma spends four months on Mālyavān (MBh 3.264.40).
- Later Rāma sends Lakṣmaṇa to rebuke the ungrateful (*kṛtaghna*) Sugrīva, "whom I consecrated in the kingdom", for whose sake Vālī was killed (MBh 3.266.5–9). Suṣeṇa appears as "Vālī's father-in-law" (MBh 3.267.2).
- In Hanumān's account to Bhīma, the brothers are "Sūrya's son Sugrīva and Śakra's son Vālī", and Sugrīva was wronged by his brother "for some reason" (MBh 3.147.25–27).
- *Variant (Tārā as Sugrīva's wife):* Bhattacharya (*Manushi* 141, 2004) reports that the *Nṛsiṃha Purāṇa* (50.21–27) and the *Mahānāṭaka* (5.51) make Tārā originally Sugrīva's wife, taken by Vālī. I have not checked those texts. In Vālmīki, Tārā is Vālī's queen, and Sugrīva holds her only as regent (CE 4.45.8) and after Vālī's death. [variant] [unverified]
