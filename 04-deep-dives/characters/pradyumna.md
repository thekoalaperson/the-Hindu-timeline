# Pradyumna — Kāmadeva Reborn, Kṛṣṇa's Eldest Son

> Where this sits: dedicated deep-dive on **Pradyumna** — firstborn of [Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md) and Rukmiṇī, the god of love **Kāmadeva reborn**, infant-stolen by the demon Śambara and cast into the sea, reared by **Māyāvatī** (Rati incarnate) until he slew Śambara and returned to Dwārakā; father of [Aniruddha](../banasura-usha-aniruddha.md), a Yādava war-leader who perishes in the [Mausala](../mahabharata/16-mausala-parva.md) fratricide; and the **third of the Pāñcarātra vyūhas**. > Two clocks: as *itihāsa* this sits in the present 28th Mahāyuga of the 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara, on the Dvāpara/Kali cusp (Kṛṣṇa's departure = 3102 BCE by tradition); the principal textual baseline is the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** with the older **Harivaṃśa** and **Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5**. [scholarly]

## Identity, names, and divine background
- **Eldest son of Kṛṣṇa and Rukmiṇī** — born at Dwārakā to Kṛṣṇa's chief queen (*paṭṭa-mahiṣī*) **Rukmiṇī**, Lakṣmī incarnate; he is the first of Rukmiṇī's ten sons and reckoned Kṛṣṇa's senior son (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.55; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5*). See [Rukmiṇī-haraṇa](../rukmini-harana.md).
- **Kāmadeva reborn** — the gods declare him to be **Kāma** (Cupid), the god of desire whom Śiva had earlier incinerated with his third eye; after Kāma's burning he is said to have entered Kṛṣṇa to be reborn through Rukmiṇī, so that Pradyumna is the love-god's promised re-embodiment (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.55; Harivaṃśa*).
  - *Variant (Sanat-kumāra / disembodied Kāma):* some tellings hold that after Śiva burnt him, Kāma remained *ananga* ("bodiless") until taking flesh again precisely as Pradyumna — making this birth the restoration of Kāma's physical form. [late text]
- **Names** — **Pradyumna** ("the pre-eminently mighty / blazing one"); patronymics and epithets include **Kārṣṇi** ("son of Kṛṣṇa"), **Rauimeya/Raukmiṇeya** ("son of Rukmiṇī"), and **Makaradhvaja / Manmatha** (Kāma's own titles, the *makara*-bannered one) (*Purāṇic usage*).
- **Beauty as proof of identity** — texts stress that Pradyumna's surpassing beauty exactly resembled Kṛṣṇa's, so that the women of Dwārakā mistook him for Kṛṣṇa himself; this likeness later authenticates him on his return (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.55*).

## The abduction by Śambara and rescue at sea
- **Stolen as a ten-day infant** — the *asura* **Śambara**, fated to die by Pradyumna's hand, abducts the newborn from the maternity chamber (in the tenth night / before the birth-rites) and **flings him into the sea** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.55; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5*).
- **Swallowed by a fish** — a great fish swallows the floating child; the fish is netted by fishermen and presented to Śambara's own household (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.55*).
- **Found by Māyāvatī (Rati incarnate)** — the fish is given to **Māyāvatī**, Śambara's cook/housekeeper, who slitting it open finds a living boy; the sage **Nārada** reveals to her who he is. Māyāvatī is **Rati**, Kāma's wife, awaiting her husband's rebirth, and she rears him in secret (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.55; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5*).
  - *Variant (the foster-mother's name):* she is **Māyāvatī** in the *Bhāgavata*; the *Harivaṃśa/Viṣṇu Purāṇa* tradition also frames her as Rati under the magical disguise (*māyā*) by which she serves Śambara. [scholarly]
- **From nurse to wife — the Rati motif** — when Pradyumna reaches youth Māyāvatī courts him; he recoils as from a mother, but she explains that he is **Kāma** and she his wife **Rati**, that no incest obtains since his infancy with her was a foster-bond, and she teaches him *māyā* (illusion-magic / sorcery) to overcome Śambara (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.55; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5*).
  - *Note (theological gloss):* the "foster-mother becomes wife" sequence is read as the cosmic reunion of **Kāma and Rati** (desire and delight) rather than a human relationship — Rati never bore him; she preserved her own husband. [scholarly]

## Slaying Śambara and the return to Dwārakā
- **The duel of illusions** — armed with the *māyā*-power Māyāvatī gave him, Pradyumna challenges Śambara; the *asura* hurls successive magical illusions which Pradyumna counters and dispels, finally **beheading Śambara** and fulfilling the prophecy (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.55; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5*).
- **Flight back through the air** — Māyāvatī carries Pradyumna through the sky to Dwārakā, alighting within Kṛṣṇa's inner palace among the queens (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.55*).
- **Reunion and recognition** — Rukmiṇī and the women, struck by his exact likeness to Kṛṣṇa, intuit he is her lost son; Kṛṣṇa, who knew the truth, keeps silent until **Nārada narrates the whole history** — the theft, the sea, the fish, Māyāvatī-Rati, and the slaying — whereupon Dwārakā rejoices at the recovered prince and his bride (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.55*).
- **Chronology** — this whole arc unfolds in Kṛṣṇa's Dwārakā years, after the [Rukmiṇī marriage](../rukmini-harana.md) and well before the Kurukṣetra war; see the Dwārakā stretch of [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](../krishna-lila.md). [scholarly]

## Marriages, and father of Aniruddha
- **Wives** — besides **Rukmavatī**, daughter of Rukmiṇī's brother **Rukmī** of Bhojakaṭa (a marriage at the Vṛṣṇi-Vidarbha court), Pradyumna's principal consort is **Māyāvatī/Rati** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.61; Harivaṃśa*).
- **Father of Aniruddha** — by Rukmavatī he begets **Aniruddha**, who inherits Kṛṣṇa's martial brilliance; at Aniruddha's wedding-festivities **Balarāma kills Rukmī** in a rigged dice-quarrel (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.61*). The next generation's drama — Aniruddha's abduction by **Uṣā**, daughter of the thousand-armed **Bāṇāsura**, and Kṛṣṇa's war on Bāṇa — is told separately. See [Bāṇāsura–Uṣā–Aniruddha](../banasura-usha-aniruddha.md).
- **Grandfather of the surviving Vṛṣṇi heir** — through Aniruddha's son **Vajra** the Yādava line continues past the clan's destruction, making Pradyumna a genealogical bridge to the post-Mausala Yādavas (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5; Mausala Parva*). See [Mausala Parva](../mahabharata/16-mausala-parva.md).

## Yādava captain — and death in the Mausala
- **War-leader of the Vṛṣṇis** — in the Dwārakā polity Pradyumna ranks among the foremost Yādava commanders alongside Sātyaki, Kṛtavarmā, Gada, and Sāmba; he is named in the defence of the city and in the campaigns of Kṛṣṇa's later years (*Harivaṃśa; Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Mahābhārata*).
- **Death in the fratricide** — thirty-six years after the war, at **Prabhāsa**, the drunken Vṛṣṇi-Andhakas turn the cursed reeds-become-iron-clubs on one another; **Pradyumna falls in the Mausala slaughter** with almost the whole clan (Sātyaki, Kṛtavarmā, Sāmba, Aniruddha, and Balarāma's host) (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.30; Mahābhārata, Mausala Parva 3*). See [Mausala Parva](../mahabharata/16-mausala-parva.md).
- **Note (the love-god's mortal end)** — that Kāma-reborn dies in a meaningless drunken brawl is part of the Mausala's bleak design: even the divine emanations of the Yādava house are not spared the curse-driven self-annihilation that closes Kṛṣṇa's *avatāra*. [scholarly]

## Pradyumna the vyūha — Pāñcarātra theology
- **Third of the caturvyūha** — in **Pāñcarātra** (Bhāgavata/Sātvata) theology the supreme Vāsudeva manifests as four eternal "arrays" (*vyūha*): **Vāsudeva → Saṅkarṣaṇa → Pradyumna → Aniruddha**. Pradyumna is the **third**, emanating from Saṅkarṣaṇa and giving rise to Aniruddha (*Nārāyaṇīya, Mahābhārata Śānti Parva; Pāñcarātra Āgamas*). See the [Nārāyaṇīya](../narayaniya.md).
- **Cosmic function** — Pradyumna the *vyūha* is correlated with **manas** (mind) and with the **creative/world-projecting principle** (*sṛṣṭi*); in the ṣaḍguṇa scheme he is paired with the attributes of lordship and virility (*aiśvarya, vīrya*) (*Pāñcarātra Āgama tradition*). [late text]
- **Two Pradyumnas, one name** — the theology deliberately overlays the **eternal divine vyūha** onto the **mortal son of Kṛṣṇa**: the human Kāma-reborn is the worldly counterpart of the transcendental emanation, the *vibhava* (avatāric) and *vyūha* (emanatory) registers sharing the name. The Bhāgavata restates the same four-fold at **SB 11.5.29–30** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa; Nārāyaṇīya*). [scholarly]
  - *Variant (later schools):* Madhva's Dvaita and the Gauḍīya tradition inherit Vāsudeva-Saṅkarṣaṇa-Pradyumna-Aniruddha as transcendental plenary expansions, distinct from the deity of desire who is Kṛṣṇa's son. [late text]
