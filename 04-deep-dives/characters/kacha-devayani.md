# Kaca, Devayānī & Yayāti's Queens

> Where this sits: a Chandravamsha (Lunar dynasty) backstory and marriage-cycle, narrated as ancestral history in the great epics. Parent: [characters](./README.md) · Section: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [../../README.md](../../README.md)
> Companion files: the dynastic payload (curse of old age, youth-exchange, renunciation, Mādhavī, the Chandravamsha split) is treated in [../yayati.md](../yayati.md); ancestry in [../nahusha.md](../nahusha.md) and [../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md). This file is the **deep dive on the prelude**: how Kaca won the revival-mantra, how Devayānī's love became a chain of curses, and how the Devayānī–Śarmiṣṭhā rivalry seeded the two-queen household.

This is the opening movement of the Yayāti cycle in the **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva (Sambhava Parva, ~71–82)**, with parallels in the **Matsya** and **Agni Purāṇas** and a compressed version in the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.18**. It explains three things the later dynastic tale takes for granted: why the devas could no longer be permanently killed by the asuras (Kaca steals the revival science); why a sage's daughter (Devayānī) ends up married to a kṣatriya king (Yayāti) rather than a brahmin; and why brahmins are forbidden wine. It is also a rare Mahābhārata episode where two women — Devayānī and Śarmiṣṭhā — drive the plot.

---

## I. The setting — the war over death itself

- **The deva–asura stalemate** - In the perennial war, the asuras hold a decisive edge: their preceptor **Śukrācārya (Uśanas Kāvya)** possesses the **Mṛtasañjīvanī vidyā** (the mantra to restore the dead to life), so every slain asura is revived, while the devas' dead stay dead. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~71)
  - *Variant (origin of the vidyā):* folk and later retellings hold Śukra obtained the Mṛtasañjīvanī by tapas to **Śiva**; the Mahābhārata itself simply credits Śukra with the science without a Śiva grant. [folk]
- **The devas' counter-plot** - The gods send **Kaca**, eldest son of **Bṛhaspati** (their own preceptor) and the nymph **Tārā/Śubhā**, to enroll as Śukra's pupil and obtain the mantra by service — a thousand-year discipleship in the enemy camp. (Ādi Parva ~71–72)
- **Kaca's two-fold strategy** - He resolves to win the secret both by faithful service to the guru and, explicitly, by pleasing **Devayānī**, Śukra's daughter — courting her favour as cover and as leverage. (Ādi Parva 72)

## II. Kaca slain and revived — the heart of the legend

- **Devayānī's attachment** - Kaca's youth, music, dancing, and gifts of flowers win the affection of Devayānī; she grows devoted to him over the years of his service. (Ādi Parva 72)
- **The asuras' suspicion** - Realizing Kaca means to carry the Sañjīvanī to the devas, the asuras (Dānavas) resolve to kill him while he tends Śukra's cattle in the forest. (Ādi Parva 72)
- **First killing — fed to beasts** - The asuras slay Kaca and give his body to wolves/jackals. Devayānī, alarmed at his absence, begs her father; Śukra revives Kaca by the Sañjīvanī, and he returns whole. (Ādi Parva 72)
  - *Variant (method):* sources differ on the first method — "fed to wolves" (most common), or simply killed and left in the forest. The number of killings is canonically **three**, though the methods catalogued vary (see below).
- **Second killing — paste in the sea** - The asuras kill Kaca again, **pound his body to paste and scatter it in the ocean** (or mix it with seawater). Again Devayānī pleads, again Śukra revives him by summoning. (Ādi Parva 72)
  - *Variant (method):* some retellings make the second killing the wolf-feeding and the first a plain forest murder; the constant is that the first two deaths are undone by Śukra at his daughter's pleading.
- **Third killing — burnt to ash, mixed in wine** - The asuras burn Kaca's body to ashes and **mix the ash into Śukra's wine (surā)**, which the unsuspecting guru drinks. This time Kaca is *inside* Śukra. (Ādi Parva 72–76)
- **The belly dilemma** - When Devayānī again demands her father restore Kaca, Śukra discovers his pupil speaking from within his own belly. To revive Kaca now, Śukra would have to let him burst out — which would kill Śukra himself. (Ādi Parva 76)
- **The reciprocal teaching** - Śukra resolves the trap by **teaching the Mṛtasañjīvanī mantra to Kaca while he is still in the belly**, so that when Kaca emerges (rending Śukra) he can immediately use the mantra to bring Śukra back to life. The plan works: Kaca bursts forth, kills Śukra, and revives him. (Ādi Parva 76) — *this is the dramatic irony: the asura-guru's own secret is transferred to the deva-spy through the guru's body.*
- **Śukra's wine-curse** - Sobered by having unwittingly drunk his disciple and nearly died for it, Śukra decrees that **henceforth any brahmin who drinks surā (wine/liquor) loses his righteousness and incurs a sin equal to brahminicide** — the scriptural charter for the brahminical prohibition on alcohol. (Ādi Parva 76)

## III. The mutual curses — why Devayānī cannot marry a brahmin

- **Devayānī's proposal** - With the discipleship complete and Kaca about to depart for heaven, Devayānī declares her love and asks him to marry her, recalling all she did to save him. (Ādi Parva 76)
- **Kaca's refusal** - Kaca declines on grounds of **dharma and kinship**: having been reborn from Śukra's body he is now Śukra's "son," making Devayānī his "sister"; a guru-putrī is also as a sister to a pupil. He honours her but cannot wed her. (Ādi Parva 76)
- **Devayānī's curse** - Stung by rejection after her devotion, Devayānī curses that **the Sañjīvanī vidyā will never work when Kaca himself wields it** (his learning will fail at the crucial moment). (Ādi Parva 76)
- **Kaca's counter-curse** - Kaca answers that she cursed him though he kept his dharma; he curses that **no sage's son (no brahmin) will ever marry her** — which is precisely why she will later wed the kṣatriya **Yayāti**. (Ādi Parva 76)
  - *Note (the loophole):* Kaca adds that though *he* cannot use the mantra, he can **teach it to others**; thus the devas still gain the science, the asuras lose their monopoly on resurrection, and the war's balance shifts. (Ādi Parva 76)
  - *Variant (folk softening):* devotional retellings frame the parting as mutual sorrow and Kaca's refusal as pure dharma, downplaying the cursing; Amar Chitra Katha and similar popular versions keep all three killings and both curses. [folk]

## IV. The quarrel by the well — Devayānī vs. Śarmiṣṭhā

- **The two girls** - **Devayānī** (sage's daughter) is companion to **Śarmiṣṭhā**, daughter of the asura king **Vṛṣaparvan**, whom Śukra serves. The two bathe together in a forest pool. (Ādi Parva 78)
- **The mixed garments** - **Indra**, as a gust of wind, swaps their clothes; emerging, Śarmiṣṭhā unknowingly dons Devayānī's garment. The quarrel erupts over rank: Devayānī asserts the brahmin-guru's daughter outranks the asura-king's daughter (her father is the one *worshipped*, not the worshipper); Śarmiṣṭhā retorts that her father supports Devayānī's family. (Ādi Parva 78)
- **The well** - In fury Śarmiṣṭhā strips Devayānī, strikes her, and **casts her into a dry/waterless well**, leaving her for dead. (Ādi Parva 78)
- **Yayāti's rescue** - King **Yayāti** (son of Nahuṣa), hunting and thirsty, finds Devayānī in the well; he takes her **right hand** to lift her out. She construes the hand-clasp as a betrothal (a brahmin maiden's hand taken by a man), but Yayāti, mindful of varṇa, withdraws and departs to his city. (Ādi Parva 78–81; Bhāgavata 9.18)
- **The leverage** - Devayānī refuses to go home until avenged. Through her maid Ghūrṇikā she reports the outrage; **Śukra threatens to abandon Vṛṣaparvan**, which would leave the asuras without the revival-mantra in war. (Ādi Parva 78–81)
- **The settlement** - Forced to appease his indispensable preceptor, Vṛṣaparvan capitulates: **Śarmiṣṭhā, with a thousand maidens, is given to Devayānī as a servant** — a princess made bondmaid to the woman she wronged. (Ādi Parva 81; Bhāgavata 9.18)

## V. The marriage and the two-queen household

- **Marriage of Yayāti and Devayānī** - When Yayāti again encounters Devayānī, she insists he marry her (their hands having met). Śukra consents and **weds Devayānī to Yayāti**, with one binding condition. (Ādi Parva 81–82; Bhāgavata 9.18)
- **The forbidden bed** - Śukra's condition: **Yayāti must never summon Śarmiṣṭhā to his bed.** Śarmiṣṭhā enters Yayāti's palace as part of Devayānī's dowry-train of servants, housed in the aśoka grove. (Ādi Parva 82; Bhāgavata 9.18)
- **Śarmiṣṭhā's appeal** - Of age and childless, Śarmiṣṭhā privately petitions Yayāti to father her child, arguing the king's dharma is to grant a son to a maiden who asks in her season; Yayāti yields, secretly. (Ādi Parva 83; Bhāgavata 9.18) — *continues in [../yayati.md §III](../yayati.md).*
- **The two queens' sons** - **Devayānī** bears **Yadu** (eldest) and **Turvaśu**; **Śarmiṣṭhā** bears **Druhyu**, **Anu**, and **Pūru** (youngest of all five). The breach of Śukra's oath is what later provokes the curse of old age. (Ādi Parva 84; Bhāgavata 9.18) — full treatment in [../yayati.md](../yayati.md).
  - *Variant (Śarmiṣṭhā's status):* texts agree she is a co-mother and effective second queen though formally Devayānī's servant; her son **Pūru** becomes the dynastic heir, so the *bondmaid's* line, not the *brahmin-wife's*, inherits the empire — a pointed reversal.

## VI. The figures — relations at a glance

- **Kaca** - son of **Bṛhaspati** (deva-guru); deva-spy turned master of the Mṛtasañjīvanī; cursed never to use it himself but able to teach it. Restores the resurrection science to the gods. (Ādi Parva 71–76)
- **Devayānī** - daughter of **Śukrācārya**; loves Kaca, is rejected and curses him, is cursed in return to marry no brahmin; rescued from the well by and married to **Yayāti**; mother of Yadu and Turvaśu. (Ādi Parva 72–84)
- **Śukrācārya (Uśanas Kāvya)** - asura-preceptor, holder of the Sañjīvanī, father of Devayānī; author of the brahmin wine-prohibition; later father-in-law and curser of Yayāti. See also [../the-chiranjivis.md](../the-chiranjivis.md) (Śukra in immortal/long-lived rosters) and [../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/). (Ādi Parva 71–85)
- **Śarmiṣṭhā** - daughter of asura king **Vṛṣaparvan**; throws Devayānī into the well, is made her servant, becomes Yayāti's secret consort and mother of **Druhyu, Anu, Pūru**. (Ādi Parva 78–84)
- **Vṛṣaparvan** - Dānava king served by Śukra; forced to enslave his own daughter to retain his preceptor. (Ādi Parva 78–81)
- **Yayāti** - Chandravamsha king, son of [Nahuṣa](../nahusha.md); rescuer-husband of Devayānī, secret husband of Śarmiṣṭhā. His own arc (curse, youth-exchange, renunciation) is in [../yayati.md](../yayati.md). (Ādi Parva 78–93)

## VII. Why this prelude matters

- **Balance-of-power myth** - Kaca's theft of the Sañjīvanī ends the asuras' resurrection monopoly; it is the Mahābhārata's explanation for the eventual deva ascendancy. (Ādi Parva 76)
- **Etiology of the wine ban** - Śukra's curse after drinking Kaca-in-wine is the canonical origin of the brahminical surā-prohibition. (Ādi Parva 76)
- **Setup for the dynastic split** - Kaca's curse forces Devayānī into a kṣatriya marriage; the well-quarrel forces Śarmiṣṭhā into Devayānī's household; together they create the two-queen, five-son situation that, via Śukra's curse of old age, bifurcates the Lunar dynasty into **Yādava** and **Paurava** lines. Continue at [../yayati.md](../yayati.md) and the lineage tables in [../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
- **Women as movers** - Unusually, the chain of consequence is driven by two women's choices — Devayānī's love-and-curse and Śarmiṣṭhā's pride-then-petition — a point emphasized in modern literary readings (e.g. Girish Karnad's play *Yayati*). [scholarly]
