# Daśaratha and Śani — the king who stopped Saturn

> Cosmic address: a *Tretā-Yuga* tale of the present **[7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)**, 28th Mahāyuga — the Ikṣvāku emperor of Ayodhyā, father of Rāma, ascends the sphere of the asterisms to confront the slowest of the [Navagraha](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md) and comes away with a hymn recited to this day.
> Parent: [04 · Deep Dives](README.md) · See also: [Daśaratha (character)](characters/dasharatha.md) · [Rāma](characters/rama.md) · [Sūryavaṃśa & Candravaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) · [Vikramāditya-Śani cycle](simhasana-dvatrimshika-vikramaditya-cycle.md) · [Hāṭakeśvara-kṣetra, Vaḍnagar](../06-sacred-geography/vadnagar-hatakeshvara-kshetra.md)

The **Daśaratha-Śanaiścara-saṃvāda** — the "dialogue of Daśaratha and Saturn" — is the tradition's foremost tale of a *mortal king overawing a graha by sheer fearlessness*. Warned by his astrologers that **Śani (Saturn)** is about to enter the fatal position of the **Rohiṇī-śakaṭa-bheda** (the "splitting of Rohiṇī's cart"), which would loose a **twelve-year drought and famine** on the earth, King **Daśaratha** of Ayodhyā mounts his war-chariot, rides *up into the region of the nakṣatras*, and confronts Saturn bow in hand. Pleased rather than angered by a human daring to challenge him in the sky, Śani grants a boon; the king pleads for his subjects, and then praises Saturn with a hymn — the **Daśaratha-kṛta Śani-stotra** — which Śani seals with the promise that **whoever recites it is spared his affliction**. The episode is narrated in the **Skanda Purāṇa, Nāgara-khaṇḍa** (the Hāṭakeśvara-kṣetra section that glorifies the tīrtha of modern Vaḍnagar, Gujarat).

---

## Where it sits — text, frame, and the "two clocks"

- **Scriptural frame** — Daśaratha reigns in the **Tretā Yuga** of the current 28th Mahāyuga; he is the last pre-Rāma Ikṣvāku sovereign of the [Sūryavaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md), and the episode is set during his long, childless reign *before* the events of the *Rāmāyaṇa* proper (*Skanda Purāṇa, Nāgara-khaṇḍa*).
- **Academic frame** — the tale is **not in Vālmīki's *Rāmāyaṇa***; it is a Paurāṇic accretion carried by the **Nāgara-khaṇḍa**, one of the *later* strata of the vast, composite *Skanda Purāṇa*. [scholarly] The Nāgara-khaṇḍa as a māhātmya of Ānarta/Gujarat tīrthas is generally dated to the mid-to-late 1st millennium CE, well after the Vālmīki core. [scholarly] The **Daśaratha-kṛta Śani-stotra** itself circulates independently in stotra anthologies and *pañcāṅga* appendices, making its exact textual home hard to pin. [disputed]
- **Which Daśaratha** — this is the **Ayodhyā king, son of Aja, father of Rāma-Bharata-Lakṣmaṇa-Śatrughna** — not any homonym; the legend explicitly frames him as the great Sūryavaṃśī cakravartin whose merit lets him ascend the heavens on his chariot (*Nāgara-khaṇḍa, Daśaratha-Śanaiścara chapters*). See [Daśaratha (character)](characters/dasharatha.md), where this Paurāṇic episode does **not** appear — the corpus keeps the *Rāmāyaṇa* Daśaratha and this stotra-Daśaratha as one man, two source-layers.

## The omen — Rohiṇī-śakaṭa-bheda and the twelve-year famine

- **The dreaded transit** — Astrologers (in most tellings **Vasiṣṭha** and the court jyotiṣīs) inform Daśaratha that Śani is about to move into position for the **Rohiṇī-śakaṭa-bheda** — Saturn "piercing the cart of Rohiṇī," i.e. transiting between the asterism **Rohiṇī** (Aldebaran, in Vṛṣabha/Taurus) and the little V-of-stars imagined as her *śakaṭa* (cart). (*Nāgara-khaṇḍa, Daśaratha-Śanaiścara-saṃvāda*.)
- **What it portends** — when a slow, cruel graha breaks the cart of Rohiṇī, the śāstra reckons a **dvādaśa-varṣā anāvṛṣṭi** — a *twelve-year drought* — bringing famine, death, and the collapse of dharma across the earth. The omen is old and pan-textual: the **Mahābhārata (Bhīṣma-parva)** lists the graha "afflicting Rohiṇī" among the terrible portents on the eve of the Kurukṣetra war. [scripture]
- **A king's dharma** — Daśaratha, as a *rājā* bound to protect his people (**prajā-pālana**), refuses to let the famine fall. Rather than propitiate Saturn from below with rites, he resolves to **stop the transit physically**, treating the graha as an invader to be met in battle — the tale's central, startling premise. See [pāpa / puṇya & the fruits of action](../05-concepts-and-dharma/papa-punya-and-prayaschitta.md).

## The ascent — a mortal rides into the sphere of the stars

- **Mounting the heavens** — Daśaratha yokes his celestial-grade war-chariot and, by the power of his tapas and kingly merit, **ascends past the sphere of the wind into the region of the nakṣatras**, to the very place where Śani is approaching Rohiṇī (*Nāgara-khaṇḍa, Daśaratha-Śanaiścara chapters*). The motif literalizes his name — *Daśaratha*, "he whose chariot [runs to] the ten directions."
- **The challenge** — reaching Saturn, the king **strings his bow and fits his celestial astras**, prepared to loose them at the graha to drive him back from the cart of Rohiṇī and avert the famine. He does not beg; he threatens. (*Nāgara-khaṇḍa.*)
- **Saturn's response** — Śani, whose very *gaze (dṛṣṭi)* is said to blast gods, kings, and worlds, is **not enraged but delighted**: no being in the three worlds — not devas, not asuras, not ṛṣis — has ever dared face him weapon in hand. Pleased by the king's **abhaya** (fearlessness) and valor, he **offers a boon** (*Nāgara-khaṇḍa, Daśaratha-Śanaiścara-saṃvāda*).
  - *On Śani's dread gaze:* the corpus records Saturn as the "slow one," the cosmic auditor whose look brings ruin — the same power that, in a folk strand, severs infant Gaṇeśa's head. See the [Navagraha — Śani section](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md).

## The boon and the stotra

- **The plea for the people** — Daśaratha does not ask wealth or victory; he asks that **Śani never again enter the Rohiṇī-śakaṭa-bheda**, so that the *dvādaśa-varṣā* famine shall never fall on the earth and its beings. Saturn, honoring the king's selflessness, **grants it** — vowing to spare the cart of Rohiṇī henceforth (*Nāgara-khaṇḍa, Daśaratha-Śanaiścara chapters*).
- **The hymn of praise** — overwhelmed, Daśaratha then **extols Saturn with a stotra** — the **Daśaratha-kṛta Śani-stotra** — invoking him under a cascade of names and forms: the black one (*kṛṣṇa*), the tawny/red-brown (*babhru*, *piṅgala*), the fierce (*raudra*), the ender (*antaka*, *kṛtānta*), the son of the Sun (*saurī*), the slow-mover (*manda*, *śanaiścara*), the corner-standing (*koṇastha*), Yama's brother, the dispenser of the fruits of karma. [scripture]
  - *On the "ten names":* the closely related enumeration — **Koṇastha, Piṅgala, Babhru, Kṛṣṇa, Raudra, Antaka, Yama, Saurī, Śanaiścara, Manda** — is traditionally the **Pippalāda** decad of Saturn's names; the Daśaratha hymn overlaps with but is *distinct from* the ten-name Pippalāda verse. The corpus keeps them attributed separately rather than merged. [disputed]
- **The reciter's boon** — pleased by the hymn, Śani grants the crowning promise: **whoever reads or hears this stotra with devotion — especially on a Saturday or during Saturn's affliction (sāḍe-sātī, aṣṭama-śani, the daśā/antardaśā of Saturn) — shall be released from his cruel effects**, and no *pīḍā* (torment) of Saturn shall touch them. This boon is the reason the hymn is still recited today. (*Nāgara-khaṇḍa, Daśaratha-Śanaiścara-saṃvāda*.)
- **The descent** — his mission accomplished — the famine averted for his kingdom *and* a shield secured for all future devotees — Daśaratha returns to Ayodhyā and his reign continues toward the *Rāmāyaṇa*'s events (*Nāgara-khaṇḍa*).

## The stotra in living practice

- **A pañcāṅga and pūjā staple** — the Daśaratha-kṛta Śani-stotra is printed in Hindu almanacs, Śani-pūjā manuals, and stotra collections across regions, recited especially on **Śanivāra (Saturday)**, on **Śani Jayantī** (Vaiśākha/Jyeṣṭha amāvāsyā, by regional reckoning), and by those undergoing **sāḍe-sātī** (the seven-and-a-half-year Saturn cycle) or **kaṇṭaka-śani**. [regional]
- **At Śani kṣetras** — it is chanted at the principal Saturn shrines the corpus catalogs — **Tirunallar** (Tamil Nadu, chief Śani-kṣetra), **Śani Śiṅgṇāpur** (Maharashtra, the doorless village) — where black-sesame (*tila*), iron, and oil offerings accompany the recitation. See [Navagraha — Śani shrines](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/navagraha.md). [regional]
- **Companion hymns** — it stands beside the other great "king-humbled-by-Saturn" text, the **Śani-māhātmya / Vikramāditya cycle**, in which even the emperor Vikramāditya endures Saturn's full daśā before Śani relents — the mythic charter that *no one*, however great, escapes the cosmic auditor. See [Vikramāditya-Śani cycle](simhasana-dvatrimshika-vikramaditya-cycle.md). [late text]

## Themes and significance

- **Fearlessness as the one thing Saturn respects** — where gods flee Śani's gaze, the tale insists the graha *rewards the being who will not flinch*. Daśaratha wins not by force (he never looses an arrow) but by **abhaya** — the willingness to stand and fight — which pleases the very lord of fear.
- **Karma cannot be commanded, only befriended** — Saturn is the dispenser of the fruits of action, "he does not punish, he delivers what was earned." Daśaratha does not abolish Saturn's law; he wins a *specific mercy* (the cart of Rohiṇī) and a *conditional shield* (for reciters) — the transit-famine is deflected, not the moral order overturned. See [karma, saṃsāra, mokṣa](../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md).
- **The king who dies of grief once faced Saturn** — the tradition sets this fearless sky-rider against the *Rāmāyaṇa* Daśaratha who is *undone by grief* over Rāma's exile (itself the ripening of an old curse). The two portraits are held together, not reconciled — the corpus catalogs both. See [Daśaratha (character)](characters/dasharatha.md).
- **A tīrtha-māhātmya's reach** — that a hymn embedded in a *local* Gujarati sthala-purāṇa (the glorification of **Hāṭakeśvara at Vaḍnagar**) became a **pan-Indian remedial stotra** shows how māhātmya literature seeds living ritual far beyond its home shrine. See [Hāṭakeśvara-kṣetra, Vaḍnagar](../06-sacred-geography/vadnagar-hatakeshvara-kshetra.md).
