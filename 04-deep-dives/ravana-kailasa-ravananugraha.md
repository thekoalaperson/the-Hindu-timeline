# Ravananugraha (Ravana Lifting Mount Kailasa)

> *Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · A first-tier standalone Śaiva grace-myth and major iconographic theme — [Rāvaṇa](characters/ravana.md) uproots Kailāsa, is pinned by [Śiva](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)'s toe, and wins grace through a thousand-year hymn. Companion-piece to the [Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra](shiva-tandava-stotra.md); episode of the [Uttara Kāṇḍa](ramayana/7-uttara-kanda.md).*

**Rāvaṇānugraha** ("the grace [*anugraha*] shown to Rāvaṇa") names the celebrated episode in which the ten-headed rākṣasa king of Laṅkā, returning from conquest, attempts to **bodily lift and move Mount Kailāsa** — the abode of Śiva and Pārvatī — only to be **pinned beneath it by the pressure of a single toe**, and then, after a thousand years of song, **released and graced** by the very god he assaulted. It is at once a narrative inset of the *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* (Uttarakāṇḍa 16), the traditional frame-legend of the [Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra](shiva-tandava-stotra.md), and one of the **canonical forms of Śiva in temple sculpture** (the *Rāvaṇānugraha-mūrti*, e.g. at Ellora). [scholarly]

---

## The episode (Uttarakāṇḍa 16)

- **The Puṣpaka halted over Kailāsa** — Flush with conquest, **Rāvaṇa** flies the aerial car *Puṣpaka* (won from his half-brother **Kubera**) homeward and crosses the white peak of **Mount Kailāsa**; the car suddenly will not move, halted over the mountain. (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttarakāṇḍa 16)
- **Nandī's warning** — **Nandī(śvara)**, Śiva's bull-faced gaṇa-chief guarding the slope, tells Rāvaṇa the lord sports here with Pārvatī and no creature may pass; Rāvaṇa mocks Nandī's monkey-like face. (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttarakāṇḍa 16)
  - *Variant (Nandī's curse):* in the epic Nandī, insulted, **curses Rāvaṇa** that monkey-like beings (*vānaras*) shall be the cause of his destruction — an etiology binding this scene to the [Yuddhakāṇḍa](ramayana/6-yuddha-kanda.md) war to come. (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttarakāṇḍa 16) [scholarly]
- **The uprooting of the mountain** — Enraged, **Rāvaṇa thrusts his twenty arms under the base of Kailāsa and heaves it up**, shaking the mountain so that its peaks tremble, its caves roar, beasts scatter, and even **Pārvatī clings to Śiva** in alarm. (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttarakāṇḍa 16)
- **The pressing-down by a toe** — **Śiva**, smiling and unmoved, presses the mountain back down with **the great toe of one foot**, sinking it onto Rāvaṇa's hands and **crushing his arms** beneath the weight. (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttarakāṇḍa 16)
- **The roar and the naming** — Pinned and in agony, Rāvaṇa lets out a **scream so terrible the three worlds shudder**; from this cry Śiva (in the Purāṇic/Śaiva gloss) gives him the very name **Rāvaṇa** — "he who makes [the worlds] roar / cry out" (√*ru*). (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttarakāṇḍa 16; Rāvaṇānugraha tradition) [scholarly]
- **A thousand years of praise** — Trapped, Rāvaṇa turns to **propitiation**, praising Śiva (in the epic for a long span; tradition fixes it at **a thousand years**) until the god, pleased, lifts the pressure and frees his arms. (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttarakāṇḍa 16)
- **The boons of release** — Śiva grants Rāvaṇa **strength restored**, his life, and gifts — most famously the invincible sword **Candrahāsa** ("moon-laugh / moon-bright blade"), with the warning that misuse will return his ruin upon him. (*Rāmāyaṇa*, Uttarakāṇḍa 16) [scholarly]

## The hymn — Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra

- **The song of release** — The **Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra**, the thunderous *pañcacāmara*-meter praise of Śiva's cosmic dance (opening *jaṭāṭavī-galaj-jala...*), is **traditionally identified as the very hymn Rāvaṇa sang while pinned**, by which he won grace. (Rāvaṇānugraha legend) — full treatment at [Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra](shiva-tandava-stotra.md). [folk]
  - *Variant (the vīṇā from his own head):* a beloved **Tamil Śaiva** telling holds that, lacking an instrument beneath the mountain, Rāvaṇa **tore off one of his ten heads and strung a vīṇā from it with his own sinews**, playing it to accompany the hymn — the namesake of the bowed **rāvaṇahattha** instrument. (Tamil Śaiva / *Tēvāram*-associated tradition) [regional] [folk]
  - *Variant (Tēvāram allusions):* the **Nāyaṉār** hymns of the *Tēvāram* (Campantar, Appar, Cuntarar) repeatedly hail Śiva as **the lord who crushed the ten-headed demon beneath the mountain and then graced him** — a stock image of grace-after-pride in Tamil Śaivism. (*Tēvāram*) [regional]
- **Text-critical caveat** — The *Vālmīki* episode has Rāvaṇa "praise" Śiva but does **not** quote the polished classical *stotra*; the ascription of the finished poem to Rāvaṇa is a **devotional frame**, not a manuscript date. Keep the scriptural attribution distinct from the literary dating. [scholarly] [disputed]

## The Ātma-liṅga sequel (Gokarṇa sthala-purāṇa)

A distinct, **later sthala-purāṇa** narrative often chained to the Kailāsa grace, set among the western-coast Śaiva kṣetras; it belongs to local *māhātmya* literature rather than the *Vālmīki* text. [late text] [regional]

- **Tapas for the Ātma-liṅga** — To make Laṅkā invincible (in some tellings at his mother **Kaikasī's** urging), **Rāvaṇa** does fierce *tapas* — offering his **ten heads one by one into the fire** — and wins from Śiva the **Ātma-liṅga (Prāṇa-liṅga)**, the god's own "soul-liṅga." (Gokarṇa-māhātmya; *Skanda Purāṇa*, Sahyādri-khaṇḍa)
  - *Variant (Vaidyanātha):* in the *Śiva Purāṇa* layer Śiva, pleased, **reattaches and heals Rāvaṇa's severed heads** as the divine physician *Vaidya* — etiology of the **Vaidyanātha jyotirliṅga**; this tradition is also attached to **Deoghar (Baidyanāth Dhām)**. (*Śiva Purāṇa*) — see [12 Jyotirliṅgas](../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md). [disputed]
- **The unbreakable condition** — Śiva grants the liṅga on the strict condition that **it must never be set on the ground** before reaching Laṅkā, or it will root there forever. (Gokarṇa-māhātmya)
- **The gods' alarm and the trick** — Fearing an invincible Laṅkā, the **devas** send **[Gaṇeśa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)** disguised as a **brahmin cowherd-boy**, while **Viṣṇu** veils the sun to fake dusk so Rāvaṇa must stop for his *sandhyā* prayers. (Gokarṇa-māhātmya)
- **The liṅga rooted at Gokarṇa** — Rāvaṇa hands the liṅga to the boy to hold; by prior pact the boy **sets it on the ground** the moment Rāvaṇa is delayed, and it **roots forever** at **Gokarṇa** (Karnataka) as **Mahābaleśvara** ("lord of great strength," for none could lift it). (Gokarṇa-māhātmya; *Skanda Purāṇa*) — see [Sthala-purāṇas of Major Temples](../06-sacred-geography/sthala-puranas-major-temples.md).
  - *Variant (the scattered wrappings — coastal liṅga chain):* in his fury Rāvaṇa hurls away the **casket and wrappings**; where each piece falls a liṅga springs up — **Sajjeśvara, Dhāreśvara, Guṇavanteśvara, Mūrudeśvara** — etiologically tying a chain of coastal Śiva-kṣetras to the one episode. (templepurohit / karnatakatourism traditions) [folk]
  - *Variant (Gaṇeśa's dent):* Rāvaṇa strikes the cowherd-boy on the head, explaining a dent in Gokarṇa's **Mahā-Gaṇapati** image, worshipped before Mahābaleśvara. [folk]

## Iconography — the Rāvaṇānugraha-mūrti

- **A canonical Śaiva form** — **Rāvaṇānugraha-mūrti**: Śiva and Pārvatī enthroned atop a tiered **Mount Kailāsa**, with the many-armed, many-headed **Rāvaṇa shown beneath**, straining upward — a standard image-type in the **Śilpa/Āgama** prescriptions for Śiva. (Śaiva iconography) — see [09 Artifacts, Symbols & Arts](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/README.md). [scholarly]
- **Famous examples** — Celebrated reliefs at the **Kailāsanātha (Kailāsa) cave-temple, Ellora** and at **Elephanta**, among many: Śiva calm above, **Pārvatī clutching Him in alarm**, gaṇas scattering, and the demon's massed arms below. (art-historical record) [scholarly]
- **Theological reading** — The image fixes the legend's lesson: **brute power humbled, then graced** — *anugraha* given precisely to the one who attacked, once he turns from force to song. The crushing toe and the granted boon are the two faces of one dancing lord. [scholarly]

## Cosmic placement — the "two clocks"

- **In-narrative time** — As an episode of **Rāvaṇa's** earlier career, the Kailāsa grace sits in the **Tretā-yuga** of the *Rāmāyaṇa* world, before his war with Rāma; the Uttarakāṇḍa places it among his conquests and clashes with the gods. [scholarly]
- **Text-critical time** — The *Vālmīki* Uttarakāṇḍa is itself a **later stratum** of the epic; the *stotra*-ascription, the vīṇā-from-his-head detail, and the whole **Ātma-liṅga / Gokarṇa sequel** are **Purāṇic, Śaiva, and sthala-purāṇa elaborations** (medieval coastal Śaivism), catalogued here as variants, not as one continuous primary narrative. [scholarly] [disputed]

## Sources

**Primary texts**
- *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, **Uttarakāṇḍa 16** — the Puṣpaka halted over Kailāsa, Nandī's warning (and curse), Rāvaṇa's uprooting of the mountain, Śiva's pressing it down by His toe, the crushing of his arms, the roar and the name "Rāvaṇa," the thousand-year praise, and the grant of the sword Candrahāsa. Translation/resources at [Wisdomlib: Rāmāyaṇa of Vālmīki](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-ramayana-of-valmiki).
- *Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra*, attributed to **Rāvaṇa** — the *pañcacāmara* hymn traditionally identified as his song of praise beneath the mountain (see [Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra](shiva-tandava-stotra.md)).
- *Skanda Purāṇa* (Sahyādri-khaṇḍa) / *Gokarṇa-māhātmya* — the Ātma-liṅga boon, the no-grounding condition, Gaṇeśa's cowherd trick, and the rooting of the liṅga as Mahābaleśvara at Gokarṇa.
- *Tēvāram* (Tamil Śaiva canon of Campantar, Appar, Cuntarar) — recurrent invocations of Śiva who crushed and then graced the ten-headed demon beneath the mountain.

**Iconographic / art-historical**
- *Rāvaṇānugraha-mūrti* reliefs at the **Kailāsa temple, Ellora** and **Elephanta**, and the Śaiva Āgama/Śilpa prescriptions for the form.
