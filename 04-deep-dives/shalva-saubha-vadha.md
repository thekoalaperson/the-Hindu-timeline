# Śālva, the Saubha & the Siege of Dvārakā (Saubha-vadha)

> A deep-dive on the **Saubha-vadha upākhyāna** — Kṛṣṇa's own first-person war-story, told to the Pāṇḍavas in the Kāmyaka forest: how the *daitya*-allied king **Śālva**, riding the Maya-built flying city **Saubha**, besieged Dvārakā to avenge [Śiśupāla](shishupala.md), and how the Sudarśana ended both king and city.
> Placed in the **Dvāpara-yuga** of the current [Mahāyuga 28](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md) — between Yudhiṣṭhira's Rājasūya and the dice-game's aftermath (see [Dvāpara-yuga spine](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)).
> Up one level: [Deep Dives README](README.md).

The episode survives in two main tellings that this file keeps distinct throughout: the **Mahābhārata's Saubhavadha-upākhyāna** (Āraṇyaka/Vana Parva, Critical Edition 3.15–23; Ganguli Vana Parva sections XIV–XXII), where **Kṛṣṇa himself narrates** the war to Yudhiṣṭhira to explain why he was absent during the dice-game; and the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa's** third-person version (10.76–77, with the sequel 10.78). The Mahābhārata frame is the canonical gap this file fills — the [Vana-parva deep-dive](mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md) notes only in passing that Kṛṣṇa was "absent during the dice-game, fighting Śālva."

---

## The narrative frame — a king's apology in the forest

- **Kṛṣṇa's confession at Kāmyaka** — Visiting the newly exiled Pāṇḍavas, Kṛṣṇa declares that had he been at Hāstinapura the dice-game would never have happened; Yudhiṣṭhira asks where he was, and Kṛṣṇa answers with the whole Saubha war, told in the first person — one of the epic's few extended self-narrations by Kṛṣṇa. (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva; CE 3.13–15 / Ganguli XIV.)
- **Timing on the Dvāpara clock** — The siege of Dvārakā unfolds **while Kṛṣṇa is away at Indraprastha for the Rājasūya**; the sea-battle in which Śālva dies follows immediately after Kṛṣṇa's return to Dvārakā — i.e. the whole affair sits between the Rājasūya ([Sabhā-parva](mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md)) and Kṛṣṇa's arrival in the forest ([Vana-parva](mahabharata/03-vana-parva.md)). (Vana Parva, CE 3.15–16; Bhāgavata 10.76–77 agrees on the sequence.)

## Which Śālva? — the man and his grievance

- **Śālva, lord of Saubha** — The Mahābhārata styles him **Saubha-pati**, "lord of Saubha," king of **Mārtikāvata**, a friend and sworn ally of **Śiśupāla** of Cedi. On hearing that Kṛṣṇa beheaded Śiśupāla at the Rājasūya, Śālva vows to destroy the Vṛṣṇis and marches on Dvārakā in Kṛṣṇa's absence. (Vana Parva, CE 3.15 / Ganguli XIV; see [Śiśupāla — the Hundred Offenses](shishupala.md).)
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.76):* the grudge is older — Śālva was among the suitor-kings, allied with Śiśupāla, **routed by the Yādavas at Rukmiṇī's abduction-marriage**; humiliated, he swears in the assembly of kings to rid the earth of Yādavas. The Śiśupāla-killing then rekindles the vow. (See [Rukmiṇī-haraṇa](rukmini-harana.md).)
- **Not Ambā's Śālva** — This Śālva must be distinguished from the **Śālva king of the Śālva country** whom **Ambā** loved and who rejected her after Bhīṣma's abduction of the Kāśī princesses (Mahābhārata, Ādi/Udyoga Parva). The two are separate figures in separate story-cycles, though the shared name invites confusion. (See [Ambā / Śikhaṇḍī](characters/amba-shikhandi.md).) [disputed — some later retellings and popular summaries conflate the two; the epic keeps them apart]

## The Śiva-boon and Maya's flying city

- **Śālva's tapas to Paśupati** — In the Bhāgavata's account, Śālva worships **Śiva as Paśupati** with an extreme vow — eating only a handful of dust each day — for a full year, until the lord of Umā offers a boon. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.76.)
- **The boon: an unassailable vehicle** — Śālva asks for a vehicle (*vimāna*) that **cannot be destroyed by devas, asuras, humans, gandharvas, nāgas or rākṣasas**, that travels wherever he wills, and that will be a terror to the Vṛṣṇis. Śiva assents. (Bhāgavata 10.76.)
- **Maya Dānava builds Saubha** — At Śiva's direction, [**Maya Dānava**](../08-beings-and-bestiary/maya-danava.md) — the asura architect who built Tripura and, later, the Pāṇḍavas' own Sabhā — constructs **Saubha**: a **city of iron** (*saubhaṁ… ayasmayam*), sky-going, moving at will, wrapped in darkness/illusion, appearing now many, now one, now visible, now vanished — and delivers it to Śālva. (Bhāgavata 10.76.)
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata):* the Vana Parva calls Saubha "the car of precious metal" that goes anywhere at will (*kāma-ga*) and treats it as an aerial fortress-city already in Śālva's possession; the epic's own upākhyāna does **not** narrate the Śiva-boon and Maya's construction — that back-story is the Bhāgavata's contribution. (Vana Parva, CE 3.15–23.)
- **Saubha in the vimāna roster** — Saubha stands with Puṣpaka and Indra's car among the epic flying vehicles; see [Vāhanas & celestial vehicles](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/vahanas-and-celestial-vehicles.md). Modern claims that such passages describe historical aircraft are pseudo-scientific readings, not scholarship. [scholarly]

## The siege of Dvārakā

- **The city fortified** — Kṛṣṇa (narrating) describes [Dvārakā](../06-sacred-geography/dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md) made ready in his absence: ramparts, moats, engines and spiked defenses manned, entry restricted, and — by order of the Vṛṣṇi elders under **Ugrasena/Āhuka** — drinking and festivity **banned** for the duration, lest revelry breed betrayal. (Vana Parva, CE 3.16 / Ganguli XV–XVI.)
- **Śālva's assault from the sky** — Śālva invests the city and its gardens from the hovering Saubha, raining missiles on Dvārakā while his troops assault the gates; many of the young Vṛṣṇi defenders fall in the opening assault. (Vana Parva, CE 3.16 / Ganguli XV.)
- **The Vṛṣṇi princes' sorties** — Kṛṣṇa's kinsmen counter-attack: **Sāmba** (Kṛṣṇa's son) fights and drives back Śālva's general **Kṣemavṛddhi** and slays the *daitya* **Vegavat**; **Cārudeṣṇa** (another son of Kṛṣṇa) kills the dānava bowman **Vivindhya**. (Vana Parva, CE 3.16–17 / Ganguli XVI.)

## Pradyumna's stand

- **Pradyumna takes the field** — [**Pradyumna**](characters/pradyumna.md), Kṛṣṇa's son by Rukmiṇī, rallies the defenders, forbids panic in the city, and engages Śālva's host — and then Śālva himself — as Dvārakā's champion-in-chief. (Vana Parva, CE 3.17–18 / Ganguli XVI–XVII; Bhāgavata 10.76.)
- **Struck down and carried off** — Pierced by a powerful strike (the Mahābhārata gives Śālva's dart; the Bhāgavata names a **mace-blow from Dyumān**, Śālva's minister), Pradyumna swoons; his charioteer — **the son of Dāruka** — bears him off the battlefield to save his life. (Vana Parva, CE 3.18–19 / Ganguli XVII; Bhāgavata 10.76.)
- **The rebuke and the charioteer's dharma** — Reviving, Pradyumna **rebukes his charioteer** for the disgrace of retreat — no Vṛṣṇi leaves a battle — and the charioteer answers with the *sūta's* own dharma: preserving an unconscious warrior is duty, not cowardice; each is right by his own code. This exchange is the moral set-piece of the episode in both tellings. (Vana Parva, CE 3.19–20 / Ganguli XVIII; Bhāgavata 10.76 — where Pradyumna laments being carried off "like a eunuch" while his elders will mock him.)
- **The withheld death-blow** — Returning to the fight, Pradyumna staggers Śālva and fixes an unfailing arrow to kill him — but is stopped: Śālva's death is **reserved for Kṛṣṇa** by fate, and Pradyumna must withhold. (Vana Parva, CE 3.20–21 / Ganguli XIX — the message is delivered by **Nārada and the wind-god** at Indra's behest; Bhāgavata 10.76 instead has Pradyumna strike Dyumān and simply fight on until Kṛṣṇa's return.)

## Kṛṣṇa's return and the battle by the ocean

- **Evil omens on the road home** — Returning from the Rājasūya, Kṛṣṇa reads inauspicious signs, reaches a battered Dvārakā, hears the full report of the siege, and sets out at once against Śālva, tracking him to the **ocean** — the Saubha found hovering over the sea. (Vana Parva, CE 3.21–22 / Ganguli XIX–XX.)
- **Pāñcajanya and the opening clash** — Kṛṣṇa blows **Pāñcajanya**, with **Dāruka** as charioteer, and battles the dānava host of Saubha; the fight is prolonged and Kṛṣṇa himself is hard-pressed by Śālva's weapons and wizardry. (Vana Parva, CE 3.21–22 / Ganguli XX–XXI; the Bhāgavata (10.77) stretches the Kṛṣṇa–Śālva engagement across **twenty-seven days**.)
- **Śālva's māyā-warfare** — The Saubha ceases to fight fair: it vanishes and reappears, stands now on the earth, now in the sky, now on the water, multiplies into many, and rains weapons from invisibility — Maya's illusion-architecture in combat. (Vana Parva, CE 3.22; Bhāgavata 10.77.)
- **The sound-seeking arrow** — Against the invisible foe Kṛṣṇa fixes an astra that **strikes by sound alone** (*śabda-vedhī*), slaughtering the shrieking dānavas he cannot see — one of the epic's notable weapon-feats. (Vana Parva, CE 3.22 / Ganguli XXI; see [Divyāstras & divine weapons](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md).)

## The māyā-beheading of "Vasudeva"

- **The false messenger** — In mid-battle a man arrives, weeping, with word from Dvārakā: **Vasudeva — Kṛṣṇa's own father — has been seized (or slain) by Śālva**; Śālva then displays before Kṛṣṇa's eyes a figure of Vasudeva and **beheads it from the Saubha**. (Vana Parva, CE 3.22–23 / Ganguli XXI–XXII; Bhāgavata 10.77 — where the messenger claims to be sent by Devakī.)
- **Kṛṣṇa's moment of grief** — Kṛṣṇa confesses (in the epic's first-person telling) that for a moment he was unmanned by sorrow — then reasons it out: Dvārakā's defenses under Balarāma and the Vṛṣṇi elders could not have fallen, so the beheading **must be Śālva's māyā** — and his battle-fury returns doubled. (Vana Parva, CE 3.23 / Ganguli XXII.)
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata 10.77):* Śukadeva reports that "some sages say" Kṛṣṇa grieved like an ordinary man, but **rejects the notion as incoherent** — how can delusion touch the Lord of infinite knowledge? — turning the epic's raw moment into a theological problem about whether māyā can bind māyā's master.
  - *Thematic echo:* the counterfeit-Vasudeva trick belongs to the same Dvāpara family of impostures as [Pauṇḍraka, the false Vāsudeva](paundraka-the-false-vasudeva.md) — there a man fakes Kṛṣṇa; here an illusion fakes Kṛṣṇa's father.

## The destruction of Saubha and the death of Śālva

- **The chakra cleaves the flying city** — Kṛṣṇa hurls the **Sudarśana cakra** at the Saubha; the discus splits the great iron city **in two, as a saw splits timber** — the epic likens the feat to Maheśvara's burning of [Tripura](tripura-dahana.md) (fittingly, another Maya-built sky-city) — and the sundered Saubha **falls into the ocean**. (Vana Parva, CE 3.23 / Ganguli XXII.)
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata 10.77):* Kṛṣṇa smashes the Saubha with his **club/mace**, and it crashes into the sea; Śālva abandons it and rushes at Kṛṣṇa on foot with a mace.
- **Śālva beheaded** — The discus, loosed again (in the Bhāgavata, after a broad-headed arrow severs Śālva's mace-arm), **takes off Śālva's head**, blazing like the sun — "as Purandara (Indra) beheaded Vṛtra." The dānava army breaks; the gods rain praise. (Vana Parva, CE 3.23 / Ganguli XXII; Bhāgavata 10.77.)
- **Return and departure** — Kṛṣṇa re-enters a rejoicing Dvārakā — and closes his tale to Yudhiṣṭhira: *this* is why he was not at Hāstinapura when the dice were thrown; had he been, Duryodhana's game would never have stood. (Vana Parva, CE 3.23 / Ganguli XXII.)

## Aftermath — Dantavakra and Vidūratha (Bhāgavata sequel)

- **Dantavakra's charge** — In the Bhāgavata's continuation, **Dantavakra** of Karūṣa — cousin of Kṛṣṇa and comrade of both Śiśupāla and Śālva — attacks **on foot, mace in hand**, to avenge his slain friends; Kṛṣṇa leaps down with his mace **Kaumodakī** and crushes his chest; a subtle light rises from the body and enters Kṛṣṇa. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.78.)
- **Vidūratha follows** — Dantavakra's brother **Vidūratha**, mad with grief, rushes in with sword and shield and is **beheaded by the Sudarśana**. (Bhāgavata 10.78.)
- **The Jaya–Vijaya cycle closes** — With Śiśupāla and Dantavakra dead at the Lord's own hand, the **third and final demonic birth** of Viṣṇu's cursed gatekeepers ends and the pair return to Vaikuṇṭha — the arc traced in full at [Jaya–Vijaya](jaya-vijaya.md) and [Śiśupāla](shishupala.md); the Bhāgavata-side summary sits in [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md). (Bhāgavata 10.78; the gatekeeper frame is Bhāgavata 7.1.)
  - *Note (scope):* the Mahābhārata's upākhyāna ends with Śālva's death; Dantavakra and Vidūratha belong to the **Bhāgavata's** telling, not the Vana Parva's. The two closures are catalogued here, not merged.

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural/cyclic** — The war belongs to the late **Dvāpara-yuga** of the 28th Mahāyuga, in the window between Yudhiṣṭhira's Rājasūya and the Pāṇḍavas' forest exile, within Kṛṣṇa's Dvārakā period. (Mahābhārata internal chronology; Bhāgavata 10.)
- **Academic/historical** — [scholarly] The Mahābhārata's Āraṇyaka Parva took shape within the epic's long compilation, conventionally c. 400 BCE–400 CE (BORI Critical Edition text); the Bhāgavata Purāṇa's version is much later, commonly dated c. 8th–10th century CE — its Śiva-boon/Maya back-story and the Dantavakra sequel are best read as the later text's systematizing of the epic episode.
