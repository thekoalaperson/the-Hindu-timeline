# The war of Āḍi and Baka — Vasiṣṭha and Viśvāmitra as fighting birds

> Cosmic address: the closing episode of the longest ṛṣi feud in Hindu tradition, set in the **[Tretā Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the present 28th Mahāyuga (Śveta-Vārāha Kalpa › 7th [Vaivasvata] Manvantara), immediately after the ordeal of the Sūryavaṃśa king Hariścandra. Two brahmarṣis curse each other into giant birds — an **Āḍi** and a **Baka** — who fight for ages at Lake **Mānasa** until **Brahmā** parts them and reconciles them.
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Master index: [README](../README.md) · The feud: [Viśvāmitra & Vasiṣṭha](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md) · The ordeal that triggers it: [Hariścandra](hariscandra.md)

The Āḍi-Baka war (*āḍi-baka-yuddha*) is the **canonical coda** to the Viśvāmitra–Vasiṣṭha rivalry. After a lifetime of escalation — the wish-cow theft, the burning of Viśvāmitra's sons, the slaughter of Vasiṣṭha's hundred sons, the Triśaṅku affair, and finally Viśvāmitra's cruel testing of King [Hariścandra](hariscandra.md) — the two sages, still not spent, **curse each other into the bodies of birds** and wage a war so vast it endangers the worlds. It is the great feud's last act and its resolution: the two ṛṣis whose whole quarrel was over rank, tapas, and truth are reduced to a pair of shrieking birds tearing at each other with beaks and claws, until the Grandfather himself must descend, un-bird them, and shame them into an embrace. The tale is preserved in two texts — the **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa** (Pargiter's Canto IX, "The Battle of the Mainā and the Heron") and the **Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (Book VI, chs. 12–13) — which agree on the shape but differ in numbers, names, and theology. Both are cataloged below, not merged.

---

## Where it sits — the two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic frame** — The war follows directly on the Hariścandra ordeal in the **[Tretā Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current Mahāyuga. Its trigger is temporal: Vasiṣṭha had been absent **twelve years** (in tapas by the Gaṅgā, or immersed underwater) and returns to learn what Viśvāmitra did to his royal protégé. Both protagonists are of the **[Sūryavaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md)** orbit — Vasiṣṭha the Ikṣvāku purohita and a [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md), Viśvāmitra (Kauśika) the kṣatriya-turned-brahmarṣi. On the cosmic clock this closes the Tretā-Yuga Viśvāmitra cycle.
- **Academic / historical frame** — The **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa** is among the oldest Purāṇas; the *Hariścandra-upākhyāna* and its bird-fight coda belong to its older narrative core, dated **[scholarly] c. 3rd–6th century CE** (F. E. Pargiter's English translation, 1904). The **Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa**, a Śākta *upapurāṇa* that retells the same episode with a Goddess-centred moral, is considerably later — **[scholarly] c. 9th–14th century CE** (Swami Vijñānānanda's translation, 1921–22). The tale claims Tretā-Yuga antiquity; the texts that carry it are mid-to-late first millennium CE. The two clocks are kept apart.

---

## The two narrative frames

- **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa — spoken by the wise birds** — In this Purāṇa the whole Hariścandra cycle (and thus the bird-war) is narrated by the **four Dharma-birds** of the Vindhya frame — sages reborn in avian form — to the enquirer **Jaimini**. A tale of two sages *become birds* is therefore delivered *by* birds: a deliberate literary resonance. (Do not confuse these frame-birds with the Āḍi and Baka — they are separate. [scholarly]) The battle is Pargiter's **Canto IX**. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* VII–IX)
- **Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa — Vyāsa to Janamejaya** — In the Śākta retelling, **Vyāsa** narrates the *Āḍi-baka-ākhyāna* to King **Janamejaya** in the Sixth Book (Skandha VI, chs. 12–13), immediately after the Śunaḥśepa discourse. Here the story is an exemplum of **ego (ahaṃkāra)** and **tamas** binding even the greatest ṛṣis — a Devī-theology lesson that the guṇas of Prakṛti rule all embodied beings, brahmarṣis included. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* VI.12–13) [late text]

## The provocation — Vasiṣṭha's wrath (the Hariścandra coda)

- **The absent sage returns** — Vasiṣṭha, purohita of the Ikṣvākus, had been away — twelve years by the **Gaṅgā** in the Mārkaṇḍeya telling — while Viśvāmitra stripped King Hariścandra of kingdom, wife, and son and set him to tend the burning-ground of Kāśī. On returning, Vasiṣṭha learns the whole degradation of his protégé. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* IX; *Devī-Bhāgavata* VI.12)
- **Anger greater than for his own sons** — The texts stress that Vasiṣṭha's rage at Viśvāmitra for ruining the truthful king was **even fiercer than his grief when Viśvāmitra had contrived the death of his hundred sons** (see the feud's earlier acts in [Viśvāmitra & Vasiṣṭha](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md)). His serenity fails him at last. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* IX)
  - *Connection to the Hariścandra file:* [hariscandra.md](hariscandra.md) records the same moment in compressed form — "Vasiṣṭha, learning how Viśvāmitra had tormented his protégé, is wroth; Brahmā pacifies him" — but there the pacification is a single line. **This file is the full expansion of that coda:** the wrath does not subside into words, but erupts into a mutual curse and a bird-war that Brahmā must physically end.

## The mutual curse — Āḍi and Baka

- **Vasiṣṭha curses first** — Enraged, Vasiṣṭha curses Viśvāmitra: *"Become a crane (baka)!"* — condemning his rival to the body of a **baka**, the long-necked crane/heron of the marshes. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* IX; *Devī-Bhāgavata* VI.12)
- **Viśvāmitra's counter-curse** — Viśvāmitra retorts in kind, cursing Vasiṣṭha to become an **Āḍi bird** (*"you too shall be an āḍi!"*). Both curses take at once; the two brahmarṣis lose their human forms and are hurled into avian bodies. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* IX; *Devī-Bhāgavata* VI.12)
- **The two bird-names (variant nomenclature)** — The identity of Vasiṣṭha's bird differs by text and translator:
  - *Vasiṣṭha = Āḍi (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa):* Pargiter renders the obscure Sanskrit **āḍi** as **"the mainā"** (myna); hence his canto title, "The Battle of the Mainā and the Heron." (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* IX, Pargiter tr.)
  - *Vasiṣṭha = Śarāri (Devī-Bhāgavata):* the Devī-Bhāgavata names Vasiṣṭha's bird **Śarāri** (another marsh-bird), while still titling the war *Āḍi-Baka*. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* VI.12–13)
  - *Viśvāmitra = Baka:* both texts agree the crane/heron is **Viśvāmitra**. The Sanskrit *baka* (crane) here has **nothing to do with the rākṣasa Bakāsura** of Ekacakrā — a pure name-collision to be kept apart. [scholarly]
- **The irony of the forms** — The proud contest for *brahmarṣi*-hood and for the crown of *satya* ends with both antagonists reduced to **squawking waterbirds** — the ego (ahaṃkāra) that drove the whole feud made literal and ridiculous. The Devī-Bhāgavata draws this moral explicitly. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* VI.13) [scholarly]

## The war at Lake Mānasa

- **Nesting on Mānasarovara** — In the Devī-Bhāgavata the two birds build their nests on separate trees on the shore of **Lake Mānasa (Mānasarovara)**, the sacred lake below [Kailāsa](../06-sacred-geography/manasarovar-kailash-yatra.md); their shrieking becomes a torment to all creatures, and they fall to fighting daily. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* VI.13)
  - *Variant (place):* the **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa** does not name a lake, giving only the combat and its cosmic effects; the **Mānasarovara** setting is the Devī-Bhāgavata's contribution.
- **Many years of combat** — The Devī-Bhāgavata says the two ṛṣi-birds, bound by each other's curse, *"passed many years there"* locked in battle; their bodies grew so covered with cuts and wounds and smeared with blood that they *"began to look like Kiṃśuka"* (palāśa) trees in red bloom. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* VI.13)
  - *Variant (duration):* the **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa** gives no figure for the combat's duration at all; the vague "many years" is the Devī-Bhāgavata's, and neither text names a fixed span.
- **Titanic scale (Mārkaṇḍeya)** — The Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa alone gives the birds monstrous size: *"The Mainā increased in size to **two thousand yojanas**; the Heron increased to **three thousand and ninety**"* — i.e. Vasiṣṭha's Āḍi at 2,000 yojanas and Viśvāmitra's Baka the larger at 3,090. At any reckoning of the yojana this is a scale of tens of thousands of miles — cosmic hyperbole. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* IX)
- **The worlds shaken (Mārkaṇḍeya)** — Striking each other with **wing-blows**, the two birds hurl down mountains, make the earth **tremble and tilt**, and set the oceans surging; multitudes of creatures perish under falling peaks, floods, and quakes. The battle threatens the ruin of the worlds — the trigger for divine intervention. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* IX)

## Brahmā parts them — the reconciliation

- **The Grandfather descends** — Seeing the worlds tending to ruin, **Brahmā** comes to the field of battle. In the Mārkaṇḍeya he arrives **surrounded by all the gods**; in the Devī-Bhāgavata he comes **"filled with mercy."** He commands the two birds to cease. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* IX; *Devī-Bhāgavata* VI.13)
- **He un-birds them** — When they will not stop, Brahmā **dissipates their brute-nature** — dispelling the deluded animal state imposed by the curses — and restores Vasiṣṭha and Viśvāmitra to their **former human bodies**, freeing each from the other's curse. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* IX; *Devī-Bhāgavata* VI.13)
- **Brahmā's verdict — the feud was fruitful** — In the Mārkaṇḍeya telling Brahmā reproves them that their fight is **wasting the earth away**, and points out that Viśvāmitra had in truth **benefited** Hariścandra, whose ordeal won him **Svarga (heaven)** — so there is no wrong to avenge. The tester was, all along, an instrument of the king's exaltation. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* IX)
- **The embrace** — Shamed by the Grandfather's words, the two sages **grow ashamed, embrace lovingly, and forgive each other**; freed of their curses they return to their āśramas as friends. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* IX; *Devī-Bhāgavata* VI.13) This mutual forgiveness is the **terminus of the entire Viśvāmitra–Vasiṣṭha rivalry** — the point at which the corpus's longest feud is declared healed.
- **The Śākta moral (Devī-Bhāgavata)** — The Devī-Bhāgavata closes not on reconciliation alone but on doctrine: if two such brahmarṣis, masters of tapas, could be dragged by **ahaṃkāra and tamas** into a years-long animal war, then the **guṇas of Prakṛti / Māyā (the Devī)** bind every embodied being without exception — the humbling lesson of the whole ākhyāna. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* VI.13) [late text]

## Two tellings compared (kept distinct)

The corpus catalogs both recensions rather than fusing them:

- **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa (Canto IX)** — the older, *cosmological* telling. Emphasis on **gigantic scale** (2,000 vs 3,090 yojanas), earth-tilting devastation, and Brahmā's **theodicy** (Viśvāmitra actually did the king good, winning him heaven). Pargiter's "mainā and heron." No lake named, no fixed duration. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* IX) [scholarly]
- **Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa (VI.12–13)** — the later, *devotional-psychological* telling. Emphasis on the **place (Lake Mānasa)**, the **duration (many years)**, the birds bloodied like Kiṃśuka flowers, and the **ego/guṇa moral** framed within Śākta theology; Vasiṣṭha's bird is named **Śarāri**. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* VI.12–13) [late text]
- **How they relate** — Both are the *same episode* — the Hariścandra-triggered mutual curse, the Āḍi-vs-Baka war, Brahmā's parting and reconciliation — and both make **Viśvāmitra the Baka and Vasiṣṭha the Āḍi**. The differences are of register (cosmic vs moral) and detail (sizes vs place-and-time), not of plot; the timeline preserves both attributions. [scholarly]

## Motif parallels & significance

- **Mutual curse into non-human form** — The Āḍi-Baka war belongs to a family of "two great ones curse each other out of their bodies" tales. Its closest cousin is Vasiṣṭha's **other** mutual curse — with King **Nimi**, where sage and king curse each other to **bodilessness** — see [Nimi & Vasiṣṭha's mutual curse](../03-lineages-vamsha/nimi-vasishtha-mutual-curse.md). In both, Vasiṣṭha's composure snaps and a curse-duel follows. (comparative)
- **The ego humbled** — The reduction of two brahmarṣis to squabbling waterbirds is the tradition's sharpest comment on the vanity of the *whole* Viśvāmitra–Vasiṣṭha contest over rank: the men who fought for the word "brahmarṣi" end as animals who must be rescued by Brahmā from their own wrath. (*Devī-Bhāgavata* VI.13) [scholarly]
- **The tester vindicated** — Brahmā's verdict — that Viśvāmitra's torment of Hariścandra was ultimately the king's exaltation to Svarga — retroactively reframes the whole [Hariścandra](hariscandra.md) ordeal as a divine test rather than a crime, closing the moral account on which Vasiṣṭha's wrath was based. (*Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* IX)
- **Birds telling of birds** — In the Mārkaṇḍeya frame the tale of the sages-become-birds is itself recited by the four wise **Dharma-birds** to Jaimini — a self-mirroring that is characteristic of the Purāṇa's avian narrative architecture. [scholarly]
- **Name-collision guard** — *Baka* here is the common noun "crane/heron" naming Viśvāmitra's form; it is **not** the cannibal rākṣasa **Bakāsura** (of Ekacakrā, slain by Bhīma) nor any demon. The corpus keeps the two Bakas strictly apart. [scholarly]
