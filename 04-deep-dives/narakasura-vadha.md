# Narakāsura-vadha and the 16,100

> A standalone deep-dive on Kṛṣṇa's slaying of **Narakāsura (Bhauma)** of Prāgjyotiṣa, the rescue and marriage of the **16,100 (16,108)** captive women, the seizure of Aditi's earrings and the **Pārijāta** tree from Svarga, and the **Naraka-caturdaśī / Dīpāvalī** aetiology (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* X.59, X.69; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 5; *Harivaṃśa*). Set in the **Dvāpara-yuga** of our [Mahāyuga 28](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md).

The demon **Naraka**, called **Bhauma** ("son of the Earth"), is the son of **Bhūmi/Pṛthivī** (the Earth-goddess) by the boar-avatāra **Varāha** — so that the Earth's son is slain by Viṣṇu's own next avatāra. He rules **Prāgjyotiṣa** (traditionally **Kāmarūpa / Assam**) from a fortress ringed by mountains, wind-ropes, water, fire and the noose-snares of his general **Mura**. This is one of the few episodes where **Satyabhāmā** fights at Kṛṣṇa's side as warrior, not only as the wife whose wish drives the Pārijāta raid.

## The provocation (Bhāgavata X.59)

- **Naraka's tyranny** — Bhaumāsura, lord of Prāgjyotiṣa, plunders the three worlds: he carries off **Aditi's (kuṇḍala) earrings**, the **white parasol of Varuṇa**, and the **crest-jewel peak (Maṇi-parvata)** of the Devas/Mother-goddess, and seizes **16,100 daughters of kings, gods and gandharvas**, holding them captive (*Bhāgavata X.59*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5*).
- **Indra's appeal** — robbed of his mother **Aditi's** ornaments and unable to breach the fortress, **[Indra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/indra.md)** comes to Dwārakā and begs Kṛṣṇa to act — the recurring motif of the Deva-king turning to the avatāra for rescue (*Bhāgavata X.59*; *Harivaṃśa*).
- **Satyabhāmā's place** — Kṛṣṇa takes his queen **Satyabhāmā** (daughter of Satrājit, won in the [Syamantaka](syamantaka-jewel.md) affair) on the expedition; in many tellings the parasol/Pārijāta is fetched expressly to please her (*Bhāgavata X.59*). See [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md).

## The assault on Prāgjyotiṣa (Bhāgavata X.59)

- **The Garuḍa-flight** — Kṛṣṇa and Satyabhāmā mount **[Garuḍa](../08-beings-and-bestiary/garuda.md)** and fly to Prāgjyotiṣa, crossing the mountain-girt, weapon-warded defences in a single aerial assault — one of the signature Garuḍa-mount scenes of the Kṛṣṇa cycle (*Bhāgavata X.59*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5*).
- **Breaching the fortifications** — with **mace, sword, cakra and arrows** Kṛṣṇa shatters the ramparts of stone, the ramparts of water, fire and wind, and the **machine-defences (yantras)**; Garuḍa scatters the demon host with wings and beak (*Bhāgavata X.59*; *Harivaṃśa*).
- **The slaying of Mura** — the **five-headed** *daitya* **Mura**, who guards the watery moat with noose-snares, rises from the deep and hurls his trident; Kṛṣṇa beheads him with the **Sudarśana cakra**. From this feat comes Kṛṣṇa's epithet **Murāri** ("foe of Mura") (*Bhāgavata X.59*; *Harivaṃśa*).
  - **Mura's seven sons** — Mura's sons (Tāmra, Antarikṣa, Śravaṇa, Vibhāvasu, Vasu, Nabhasvān, Aruṇa) and his general **Pīṭha** charge in vengeance and are cut down in turn (*Bhāgavata X.59*).
- **The death of Naraka** — Bhauma rides out on the elephant **Suprītika** hurling the **Śataghnī** ("hundred-slayer") weapon; Kṛṣṇa severs his head with the cakra. As he dies the **Earth-goddess Bhūmi** appears, returns the stolen earrings, parasol and jewel-seat, and pleads for her grandson — and Kṛṣṇa grants Naraka's son **Bhagadatta** the throne with assurance of safety (*Bhāgavata X.59*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5*).
  - *Note:* **Bhagadatta**, son of Naraka, later rules Prāgjyotiṣa and fights for the Kauravas at **Kurukṣetra**, slain by Arjuna — tying this episode into the [Mahābhārata](mahabharata/) war a generation on (*Mahābhārata*, Droṇa Parva). [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Satyabhāmā the slayer):* a widespread tradition — strong in **Bhāgavata** commentarial and South-Indian retellings — holds that Naraka had a boon to die only at his **mother's hand**, so that when Kṛṣṇa falls (feigning a swoon from the Śataghnī), **Satyabhāmā**, herself an *aṃśa* of Bhūmi, looses the fatal arrow; mother thus kills son. This grounds the **Satyabhāmā = Bhūdevī** identification. [regional / late text]

## The 16,100 (Bhāgavata X.59, X.69)

- **The captives freed** — within Naraka's harem-prison Kṛṣṇa finds **16,100 (variantly 16,000) royal women** seized from across the worlds; he releases them and sends them ahead to Dwārakā (*Bhāgavata X.59*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5*).
- **The dharma of marriage** — the freed women, having dwelt in a demon's house, are deemed unmarriageable in ordinary society; **all having set their hearts on Kṛṣṇa as deliverer**, he weds them so they may live with honour and protection rather than be cast off — a rescue-marriage, not conquest (*Bhāgavata X.59*).
- **The 16,108 and the multiplication** — added to the **eight principal queens (aṣṭa-bhāryā)**, the total comes to the famous **16,108**. The Bhāgavata's narrative climax (X.69) has the sage **Nārada** tour Dwārakā and find Kṛṣṇa **simultaneously present** in every one of the 16,100 palaces — bathing, dicing, conversing, performing householder rites — each wife believing him hers alone, demonstrating his **yoga-māyā** and full Bhagavān-hood (*Bhāgavata X.69*).
  - *Variant (the count):* **16,100** freed + **8** principal = **16,108** is the standard Vaiṣṇava reckoning; many popular retellings round to **16,000**, and some to **16,001/16,008**. The number signals plenitude, not a census. [textual]
  - *Note (allegorical reading):* later Vaiṣṇava commentary glosses the 16,108 as the soul's faculties / the 16 kalās plus the *bhāryās* of the embodied self all turned toward the Lord — a *bhakti* allegory layered on the literal rescue. [scholarly]

## The Pārijāta and the defeat of Indra (Bhāgavata X.59; Harivaṃśa)

- **The visit to Svarga** — having restored Aditi's earrings, Kṛṣṇa (with Satyabhāmā) ascends to **Indra's heaven** to return the ornaments to Aditi; Aditi and Indra honour them (*Bhāgavata X.59*).
- **Seizing the Pārijāta** — to gratify **Satyabhāmā**, Kṛṣṇa uproots the celestial **Pārijāta (kalpa-)tree** of **Nandana-vana** and loads it on Garuḍa. **[Indra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/indra.md)** and the Devas give battle to keep it; Kṛṣṇa defeats Indra, who yields the tree — the Deva-king bested by his own avatāra (*Bhāgavata X.59*; *Harivaṃśa*, which elaborates the war at length). See [Asura Wars & Roster](../08-beings-and-bestiary/asura-wars-and-roster.md).
- **Planted at Dwārakā** — the Pārijāta is set in Satyabhāmā's courtyard; in the **Harivaṃśa** developed version a quarrel with **Rukmiṇī** over the tree (its flowers falling into the rival's yard) frames the whole raid as a co-wife rivalry resolved by Kṛṣṇa (*Harivaṃśa*, Viṣṇu Parva). [late text]
  - *Note:* the **Pārijāta** is itself one of the treasures churned from the [Kṣīra-sāgara](samudra-manthana.md); its removal to Earth and eventual return to heaven at Kṛṣṇa's departure bracket the avatāra's stay. [scholarly]

## Festival aetiology — Naraka-caturdaśī / Dīpāvalī

- **Naraka-caturdaśī (Choṭī Dīwālī)** — the killing of Naraka is the charter-myth for the **fourteenth lunar day (caturdaśī)** of the dark fortnight of Āśvina/Kārttika, the day before **Dīpāvalī (Dīwālī)** proper; it is widely called **Naraka-caturdaśī** (*Purāṇic-festival tradition; Padma / Skanda Purāṇa festival sections*). [regional]
- **The pre-dawn oil bath** — because the dying Naraka begged that his death be remembered with light and bathing rather than mourning, the day is marked by the **abhyaṅga-snāna** (ceremonial oil bath before sunrise) and rows of lamps; the lamps celebrate the freeing of the captives and the end of his terror (*festival tradition*). [folk / regional]
  - *Variant (regional names):* observed as **Naraka Chaturdashi** across India, **Kālī Caudas** in Gujarat/Rajasthan, and **Dīpāvalī / Deepavali** in **South India (Tamil, Telugu, Kannada)** where the Naraka-vadha is the *primary* Dīwālī legend (as against the North's Rāma-return story); women of the house perform the morning oil-bath ritual recalling the rescued queens. [regional / folk]
  - *Variant (Goa & the Konkan):* effigies of **Narkāsur** are built and burned at dawn before Dīwālī — a vivid folk dramatization of the slaying. [folk / regional]

## Two clocks

- **Scriptural clock:** the Naraka-vadha falls in the **Dvāpara-yuga** of the **28th Mahāyuga** of the present **Vaivasvata Manvantara**, in the Dwārakā years of Kṛṣṇa's householder life, on the Dvāpara/Kali cusp (*Bhāgavata*). See [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md).
- **Historical clock:** as a living festival, the Naraka-caturdaśī is observed annually in **Kali-yuga historical time**; the **Prāgjyotiṣa = Kāmarūpa/Assam** localization and the Bhagadatta link give the legend a fixed mytho-geographic anchor in the northeast. [scholarly]

## Sources

- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam) — **Canto X.59** (Indra's appeal, the Garuḍa-flight, the slaying of Mura and Naraka, the freeing and marriage of the 16,100, the return of the earrings and the Pārijāta raid); **X.69** (Nārada's tour of the 16,108 palaces and Kṛṣṇa's simultaneous presence). Cross-ref the [Bhāgavata skandha-map](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md).
- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa**, **Book 5** — a more compressed account of Bhauma's slaying, the captives, and the heaven-raid.
- **Harivaṃśa** (khila/appendix to the [Mahābhārata](mahabharata/)), **Viṣṇu Parva** — the older connected Kṛṣṇa biography; develops the **Pārijāta-haraṇa** and the war with Indra at length, with the Rukmiṇī–Satyabhāmā rivalry frame.
- **Mahābhārata**, Droṇa Parva — **Bhagadatta** of Prāgjyotiṣa, Naraka's son, in the Kurukṣetra war (genealogical sequel).
