# The Vraja Boyhood Demons — Vatsāsura, Bakāsura, Aghāsura, and Balarāma's Dhenuka and Pralamba

> *Where this sits: the **kaumāra** demon-slayings of the boy [Kṛṣṇa](./krishna-lila.md) and his elder brother **Balarāma** in [Vṛndāvana](./krishna-lila.md), after the move from Gokula — in the closing [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga. These episodes appear only as roster bullets in the parent files; this is their full treatment.*
>

This file gathers the early-Vṛndāvana demon-killings of the **boyhood (paugaṇḍa/kaumāra) phase** — the deeds that, in the [Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md)'s sequence, fall between the migration to Vṛndāvana ([Kṛṣṇa Bālya-līlā](./krishna-balya-lila.md)) and the serpent-dance of [Kāliya-mardana](./kaliya-mardana.md). Three are Krishna's: **Vatsāsura** and **Bakāsura** (Bhāgavata X.11) and **Aghāsura** (X.12); two are Balarāma's, establishing his martial role in Vraja: **Dhenukāsura** of the Tālavana (X.15) and **Pralambāsura** (X.18). The principal source is the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Canto X**, with the older **Harivaṃśa** (Viṣṇu Parva) and **Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Book 5** as parallels.

---

## The Pūtanā–Baka–Agha sibling triad

- **The three demon-siblings** — The Bhāgavata makes three of Kaṃsa's child-killing agents a **family**: the demoness **Pūtanā** (slain in infancy at Gokula — see [Kṛṣṇa Bālya-līlā](./krishna-balya-lila.md)), **Bakāsura** the crane-demon, and **Aghāsura** the python — all children of the demon **Baka** / kin sent by Kaṃsa (Bhāgavata X.6, X.11–12). [The triad is the Bhāgavata's narrative knot: Aghāsura comes specifically to **avenge** his slain brother and sister.]
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata X.12.13):* Aghāsura is named the **younger brother of Bakī (Pūtanā) and Baka (Bakāsura)**, and his attack is motivated as fraternal revenge for their deaths. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa / Viṣṇu Purāṇa):* the older texts narrate the slayings as discrete perils sent by Kaṃsa and do **not** uniformly bind the three into one sibling-set; the explicit kinship and the revenge-motive are most developed in the Bhāgavata. [scholarly]

## Krishna's trio

### Vatsāsura — the calf-demon

- **The disguise among the calves** — While Krishna and the cowherd boys (*gopas*) graze the herds, a demon, **Vatsāsura**, takes the **form of a stray calf** and mingles with the grazing calves to come at the boy unseen (Bhāgavata X.11). [folk: the demon's name, *vatsa* = "calf," is itself the disguise]
- **The slaying by the hind legs** — Krishna spots the impostor, steals up, **seizes the calf by the hind legs and tail, whirls it round, and dashes it against a kapittha (wood-apple) tree**; the demon falls dead in its true huge form, and the wood-apples shaken loose are shared out among the boys (Bhāgavata X.11; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).

### Bakāsura — the giant crane/heron-demon

- **The crane by the river** — As the boys bring the calves to drink, a colossal demon in the shape of a **crane/heron (baka)**, with a beak like a mountain crag, rushes up and **swallows Krishna whole** (Bhāgavata X.11). [The *baka* is variously rendered crane, heron, or stork in translation.]
- **The burning gullet and the tearing of the beak** — Inside the throat Krishna makes himself **scorching-hot like fire**, so that Bakāsura, burned, at once disgorges him unharmed; the crane attacks again with its sharp beak, and Krishna **seizes the two halves of the beak and tears the demon apart** as one splits a blade of grass (Bhāgavata X.11; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5). The gods rain down flowers in praise.

### Aghāsura — the python-demon swallowed-and-slain-from-within

- **The motive of revenge** — **Aghāsura**, younger brother of Pūtanā and Bakāsura and a general of Kaṃsa, comes to **avenge his slain siblings** and to destroy Vṛndāvana at a blow (Bhāgavata X.12).
- **The mile-long python** — He assumes the form of a stupendous **python (ajagara)**, miles long, lying across the path with **jaws open like a mountain cave** — lower lip on the earth, upper lip touching the clouds, fangs like peaks, breath a poisonous gale. The cowherd boys, taking the gaping cavern for a hill-cave or a scenic wonder, **walk in laughing with the calves**, and the python swallows them all (Bhāgavata X.12).
- **Krishna enters and kills from within** — Krishna, knowing the danger, **enters the mouth himself** to save his friends, then **swells his body** inside the serpent's throat until it chokes; the demon's life-airs, finding no exit, burst out through the crown of its head, and Aghāsura dies. Krishna revives the boys and calves with his glance and leads them out alive (Bhāgavata X.12).
- **The merging of the liberated spirit** — As the demon dies, a **radiant light (the liberated soul of Aghāsura) issues from the body, waits in the sky, and then merges into Krishna** when he emerges — granting the python *sāyujya*, union with the Lord, despite his enmity (Bhāgavata X.12). [scholarly: the "mokṣa of the demon slain by God" theme, shared with Pūtanā and later Kaṃsa]
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata X.12):* the gods marvel that even an *asura* who came to kill attains the liberation that *yogīs* strive for — the chapter's theological point. [scholarly]
- **Why this motivates the Brahma-vimohana** — In the Bhāgavata the boys do not immediately grasp that they were swallowed and saved; **Brahmā**, witnessing the feat and astonished, **steals away the calves and the cowherd boys** to test Krishna — and Krishna multiplies himself into every missing calf and boy for a year. This is the **[Brahma-vimohana](./brahma-vimohana.md)** ("the bewildering of Brahmā"), narrated directly out of the Aghāsura episode (Bhāgavata X.12–14).
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa / Viṣṇu Purāṇa):* the Aghāsura swallowing and the **Brahmā-bewilderment** are a distinctive elaboration of the **Bhāgavata**; the older texts give the python-peril (where present) without the year-long self-multiplication. [scholarly]

## Balarāma's pair — the brother's martial role in Vraja

These two slayings are **Balarāma's**, not Krishna's, and the tradition reads them as fixing the elder brother (the *halāyudha*, "plough-armed," and a portion of Śeṣa/Saṅkarṣaṇa) as Vraja's own strong-man and protector.

### Dhenukāsura — the donkey-demon of the Tālavana

- **The forbidden palm-grove** — Drawn by the boys' craving for the sweet ripe fruit of the **Tālavana** ("palm-forest"), Krishna and Balarāma enter a grove guarded by the donkey-demon **Dhenukāsura** and his herd of asses, where no one dares pick the *tāla* fruit (Bhāgavata X.15).
- **Balarāma shakes down the fruit** — **Balarāma shakes the palm-trees** so the ripe fruit rains down; Dhenukāsura charges, braying, and **kicks Balarāma in the chest with his hind hooves** (Bhāgavata X.15).
- **The slaying by the hind legs** — Balarāma **seizes the ass by the hind legs, whirls it overhead, and flings it into the top of a palm-tree**, killing it; the tree breaks under the carcass and topples its neighbours. The demon's ass-companions attack and are dispatched the same way by the two brothers, until the grove is strewn with dead and the Tālavana is opened to Vraja's cattle and boys (Bhāgavata X.15; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).
  - *Variant:* the whirl-and-fling killing of the donkey closely mirrors Krishna's killing of the calf-demon Vatsāsura — the same heroic motif assigned to each brother. [scholarly]

### Pralambāsura — the demon disguised as a cowherd, slain by Balarāma's fist

- **The infiltrator in the play-band** — A demon, **Pralambāsura**, sent by Kaṃsa, takes the **form of a cowherd boy** and joins Krishna's and Balarāma's band as they play at herding, meaning to carry off one of the brothers (Bhāgavata X.18).
- **The carrying-game** — The boys split into two teams for a contest in which the **losers carry the winners on their backs**; by the game's terms Pralamba, on the losing side, must carry **Balarāma** — and once Balarāma is on his back, the demon **assumes his huge true form and flees into the sky**, bearing him off (Bhāgavata X.18).
- **The shattering fist-blow** — Aloft, Balarāma grows heavy and unafraid; though momentarily alarmed (and, in the text, reminded of his own divinity), he **brings his fist down on the demon's head** with such force that the skull is **smashed and Pralamba falls dead to the earth**, vomiting blood, like a mountain struck by Indra's thunderbolt (Bhāgavata X.18; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5). The gods praise Balarāma and shower flowers.
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata X.18):* the killing is bracketed by the **two forest-fire (dāvāgni) episodes** of the same chapter and the next, in which Krishna swallows the blaze that traps the herd — the Pralamba slaying and the *dāvāgni* are catalogued together as the Muñjāraṇya-Bhāṇḍīravana deeds. [textual ordering]

## Two clocks

- **Scriptural time:** all five slayings fall in the **boyhood (kaumāra–paugaṇḍa)** of Krishna and Balarāma in Vṛndāvana, in the closing **[Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** of the current 28th Mahāyuga (a generation before the war and Krishna's departure at the onset of Kali Yuga, traditionally **3102 BCE**). The Bhāgavata's internal order is fixed: Vatsāsura → Bakāsura → Aghāsura → ([Brahma-vimohana](./brahma-vimohana.md)) → Dhenukāsura/Tālavana → [Kāliya](./kaliya-mardana.md) → Pralambāsura and the forest-fire.
- **Text-critical time:** the **oldest connected form is the Harivaṃśa** (core c. 1st–2nd c. BCE), restated in the **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** (~5th c. CE), and most elaborated in the **Bhāgavata** (~6th–10th c. CE) — which alone develops the Pūtanā–Baka–Agha **sibling triad**, Aghāsura's **liberation into Krishna**, and the **Brahma-vimohana** that grows out of it. The skeleton — the calf-demon, the crane, the swallowing python, the donkey of the palm-grove, and the cowherd-demon broken by Balarāma's fist — is constant across the three. [scholarly]

## Sources

**Primary texts**
- *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam), **Canto X** — Vatsāsura the calf-demon and Bakāsura the crane (**X.11**); Aghāsura the python, the swallowing of the boys, the killing from within, and the merging of his liberated spirit (**X.12**, leading into the Brahma-vimohana of X.13–14); Dhenukāsura and the opening of the Tālavana (**X.15**); Pralambāsura slain by Balarāma's fist (**X.18**).
- *Harivaṃśa* (Viṣṇu Parva) — the **oldest connected** account of the Vraja demon-slayings (the calf, the crane, the donkey, and Pralamba).
- *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*, **Book 5** — parallel narrative of the boyhood slayings (Vatsa, Baka, Dhenuka, Pralamba).
