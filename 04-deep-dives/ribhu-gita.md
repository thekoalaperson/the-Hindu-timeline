# Ribhu-Gītā

> *Up to [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · master [index](../README.md). The "Song of [the sage] Ṛbhu" — a vast (~2,200-verse, 44-chapter) Advaita Vedānta dialogue embedded in the **Śaiva** [Śivarahasya Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md), companion to the [lesser-Gītās catalog](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md), the [Śiva-gītā](./shiva-gita.md), and the radically non-dual [Aṣṭāvakra-gītā](./characters/ashtavakra.md).*

The **Ribhu-Gītā (Ṛbhu-Gītā)** is one of the longest and most uncompromising statements of *advaita* (non-duality) in the whole Hindu corpus. It is the discourse of the sage **Ṛbhu** to his disciple **Nidāgha**, set as the sixth *aṃśa* (and bridging amśas) of the **Śivarahasya Purāṇa** — an *upapurāṇa* ("secondary Purāṇa") that frames itself as the secret (*rahasya*) of Śiva narrated for the devotee. Unlike the Mahābhārata's didactic [lesser Gītās](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md), it is a single sustained teaching whose method is **relentless negation and affirmation of the one Self** — repeating, chapter after chapter, that all that appears is Brahman alone and nothing else exists. [scholarly] [Śaiva]

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural time** - In its own frame the dialogue belongs to the **timeless milieu of the great ṛṣis**: Ṛbhu is reckoned a mind-born son of **Brahmā** who received the knowledge of Śiva directly, and the teaching is presented as eternal *brahma-vidyā* re-spoken in our [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md). The narration is **cross-yuga**: it is recited within the Purāṇic frame (Sūta to the sages of Naimiṣa) but claims no single yuga of "occurrence." [scriptural]
- **Manuscript / academic time** - Text-critically the Śivarahasya Purāṇa is a **late South-Indian Śaiva compilation** (latter half of the 1st millennium CE and after), and the Ribhu-Gītā's polished *ajāta-vāda* ("no-origination") Advaita vocabulary places its received form firmly in the **post-Śaṅkara** Vedāntic milieu. Keep the scriptural ascription to primeval ṛṣis distinct from this manuscript dating. [scholarly]

## The dialogue & its teaching

- **Ṛbhu (Ṛibhu) instructs Nidāgha** — The frame is a guru–disciple pair: the ever-realized **Ṛbhu** answers the seeker **Nidāgha** on the nature of the Self, Śiva, and the unreality of the world. The same pair recurs across Advaita literature (notably in the [Viṣṇu Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md) "town/festival" parable of Ṛbhu humbling Nidāgha's lingering dualism). (Śivarahasya Purāṇa, Ṛbhu-Gītā section)
- **Identity of Śiva, Self, and Brahman** — Its signature move is to collapse the apparent three: the Śaiva **Śiva**, the Vedāntic **Brahman**, and the innermost **Ātman** are declared one and the same undivided awareness — sectarian Śaivism dissolved into pure non-dual *jñāna*. (Śivarahasya Purāṇa)
- **"All is Brahman; there is no world, no mind, no bondage, no liberation"** — Whole chapters are sustained *anuvāda* (refrain): the seeker is told that the body, the senses, the mind, the guru, the disciple, bondage, the path, and even liberation itself **do not exist** as separate realities — only the one Self *is*. This is *ajāta-vāda*, the doctrine that nothing was ever really born. (Śivarahasya Purāṇa, Ṛbhu-Gītā)
- **Method of affirmative repetition (*bhāvanā*)** — Rather than argue dialectically, the text has the disciple **dwell in and repeat** the conviction "I am Brahman, all is Brahman," chapter on chapter, as a contemplative *abhyāsa* designed to dissolve the sense of a separate self by saturation. This repetitive, almost mantric structure is what makes it both very long and famously potent for recitation. [scholarly]
- **Nidāgha's awakening** — The arc resolves in the disciple's recognition of his own identity with the formless Self, the standard *advaita* terminus: not an event in time but the falling-away of the illusion of separateness. (Śivarahasya Purāṇa)

## Reception in the Ramaṇa Mahaṛṣi lineage

- **Prized by Ramaṇa Mahaṛṣi** — The Ribhu-Gītā was singled out by **Bhagavān Śrī Ramaṇa Mahaṛṣi** (1879–1950) of Tiruvaṇṇāmalai as a supreme expression of his own teaching of the Self. He is recorded as recommending it for its direct, world-negating force. [scholarly]
- **Daily recitation at Sri Ramaṇāśramam** — Portions of the Ribhu-Gītā were (and are) **chanted daily at Ramaṇa's ashram**, the recitation reportedly continuing during the hours of the day; Ramaṇa valued the mere sound and repetition of its affirmations as itself conducive to stillness, even apart from intellectual analysis. [scholarly] [Ramaṇa lineage]
- **Tamil and Sanskrit transmission** — The text circulated both in the **Sanskrit** of the Śivarahasya and in a **Tamil verse rendering** (the *Tamiḻ Ṛbhu-Gītai* of Bhikṣu Śāstri / Ulaganātha Swāmigaḷ), the form most used in the South-Indian recitation tradition that reached Ramaṇa. [regional] [scholarly]

## The widely-circulating English condensation

- **The 122-verse condensation (Bhagavan / Bhikshu / "Essence")** — Because the full text runs to thousands of verses, a much-loved **abridgement of about 122 selected verses** has circulated widely in English-speaking Advaita circles (associated with the Ramaṇāśram milieu and the *Crossing the Stream* / *Heart of Ribhu Gita* style selections). It distills the affirmations into a manageable daily reading. [scholarly] [later condensation]
- **Full English translations** — A complete English Ribhu-Gītā (translated under the auspices of the **Society of Abidance in Truth, SAT**, Santa Cruz, from the Tamil and Sanskrit) made all 44 chapters available to non-Sanskrit readers; the **N. R. Krishnamoorthi Aiyer** condensation (*The Essence of Ribhu Gita*) is the best-known short form issued by Sri Ramaṇāśramam. [scholarly]
  - *Variant (verse/chapter counts):* tallies vary by recension and by whether the Tamil or Sanskrit base is counted — figures of **~2,000–2,300 verses across 44 chapters** are typically given; treat exact totals as edition-dependent. [disputed]

## Place among the Gītās

- **An independent Purāṇic Gītā** — Like the [Śiva-gītā](./shiva-gita.md) (Padma Purāṇa), [Devī-gītā](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md), and [Avadhūta-gītā](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md), the Ribhu-Gītā belongs to the **Purāṇic / independent** layer of Gītās *outside* the Mahābhārata — folded into the whole-of-Hinduism "60+ Gītās" tally but never part of the epic's Mokṣa-dharma cluster. See the [lesser-Gītās catalog → Purāṇic & independent Gītās](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md#purāṇic--independent-gītās-the-path-to-60--pointers-only). [folk]
- **Closest temperament: the [Aṣṭāvakra-gītā](./characters/ashtavakra.md)** — Among all Gītās it most resembles the Aṣṭāvakra-gītā in tone — both are **pure jñāna, world-negating, with almost no ritual, ethics, or devotional scaffolding** — but the Ribhu-Gītā is far longer and frames its non-dualism through **Śiva** rather than a courtly Janaka dialogue. [scholarly]
