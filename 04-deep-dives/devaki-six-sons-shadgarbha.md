# The return of Devakī's six sons (Ṣaḍgarbha)

> Where this sits: the quiet coda that *closes* the infanticide that [Kaṃsa-vadha](kamsa-vadha.md) opens but never resolves — the six infants Kaṃsa dashed to death at birth are the cursed **Ṣaḍgarbha** ("six wombs"), the fallen sons of the Prajāpati **Marīci**, held in safekeeping in the netherworld **Sutala** with **Bali**; late in his Dvārakā years [Kṛṣṇa](krishna-lila.md) and [Balarāma](characters/balarama.md) fetch them, [Devakī](krishna-lila.md) suckles them at her own breast, and — their curse spent — they ascend liberated to the abode of the gods.
>

Two clocks: as *itihāsa* this falls in Kṛṣṇa's Dvārakā period, on the Dvāpara/Kali cusp of the 28th Mahāyuga of the present ([Vaivasvata](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)) Manvantara; the textual baseline is **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85.27–59**, with the older **Harivaṃśa (Viṣṇu Parva)** and the later **Devī-Bhāgavata (Book 4)** as parallels.

---

This is the answer the corpus otherwise never gives. The [Kaṃsa-vadha](kamsa-vadha.md) file opens on the prophecy at Devakī's wedding-chariot and the slaughter of her **first six children**; it kills the tyrant and frees the parents — but it leaves the six dead infants dead. The Bhāgavata reserves their resolution for a single late chapter (10.85), long after Mathurā, as a private grief of Devakī's that Kṛṣṇa answers. The episode is thus a deliberate structural *bookend*: the six who were murdered to fulfil a curse are recovered once the curse has run its course.

## The Frame: Devakī's Grief

- **The trigger — Sāndīpani's precedent** — Devakī has heard how Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, as *guru-dakṣiṇā*, brought back their teacher's long-dead son from the realm of the ancestors / Yama (see [Sāndīpani & Pañcajana](sandipani-pancajana.md)). If they can retrieve a stranger's child, she reasons, they can retrieve her own (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85.27–33*).
- **The petition** — Weeping, Devakī addresses her sons as **masters of yoga and lords of the worlds** and begs to see, even once, the **six sons "killed by the King of Bhoja" (Kaṃsa)** before her eyes (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85.31–33*).
- **Kṛṣṇa's assent** — Moved by filial love, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma resolve to descend to **Sutala** and recover the infants by their own *yoga-māyā* (mystic potency) (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85.34*).
- **Placement in the life** — The episode is set well into the Dvārakā years, after the Mathurā and marriage cycles, during Vasudeva's sacrifices at Kurukṣetra in some framings; it functions as one of Kṛṣṇa's domestic "family" *līlās* rather than a martial one (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85*). [scholarly]

## The Backstory: Who the Ṣaḍgarbha Are

The six are not ordinary children but fallen divinities working through a chain of curses. The texts agree on the outline but **differ on the demon lineage** — cataloged, not merged, below.

- **Origin — the sons of Marīci** — In the first Manvantara (of **Svāyambhuva Manu**), six virtuous godling sons were born to **Ūrṇā**, wife of the Prajāpati **Marīci** (a mind-born son of Brahmā; see [the Prajāpatis](../08-beings-and-bestiary/the-prajapatis.md)) (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85.47*).
- **The offence and Brahmā's curse** — When **Brahmā**, seized by passion, moved toward his own daughter, the six sons **laughed and mocked** him. Brahmā cursed them to fall at once into **asura (demoniac) birth** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85.48–49*).
- **The six names** — They are named **Smara, Udgītha, Pariṣvaṅga, Pataṅga, Kṣudrabhṛt, and Ghṛṇī** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85.51*).
- **Yoga-māyā's transfer** — Born (per the Bhāgavata) as **sons of Hiraṇyakaśipu** (see [Prahlāda & Narasiṃha](prahlada-narasimha.md)), they were then, by the agency of **Yoga-māyā** (Viṣṇu's deluding power, the same śakti who shifted Balarāma from Devakī's womb to Rohiṇī's), carried into **Devakī's womb** to be born one by one — and there Kaṃsa killed them (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85.49–50; cf. 10.2*).
- **The cyclic logic** — Because they were cursed to die repeatedly, and because their slayer was likewise curse-driven, their infanticide is presented as a *karmic settlement*, not a mere atrocity: Kaṃsa is the instrument who discharges the sentence Brahmā passed (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85; Devī-Bhāgavata 4*). [scholarly]

### Variant — who was the demon father?

- *Variant (Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Book 4):* the six first take birth as the **sons of Kālanemi** (the asura later reborn as **Kaṃsa** himself), and *then* as the **sons of Hiraṇyakaśipu** — reborn a second time already possessed of knowledge, out of dread of the curse. The prophecy is explicit: when they are born from Devakī, "their father Kālanemi of the previous birth" will be Kaṃsa, and he will kill them at birth. This gives the "**slain by their own former father**" symmetry that the Bhāgavata leaves implicit (Kaṃsa = the reborn Kālanemi who once sired them). [late text]
- *Variant (Harivaṃśa, Viṣṇu Parva ~47–48):* the older *khila* treats the six slain infants within the Kālanemi–Kaṃsa identity and the demon-rebirth frame, the earliest layer of the "the six were fallen beings, their death was fated" logic that the Purāṇas inherit and elaborate. [scholarly]
- *Variant (as already noted in the corpus):* [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md) records the parallel gloss that "the six slain children are the sons of the demon Kālanemi / Hiraṇyakaśipu's line, reborn" — the same tradition surfacing in summary form.
- **Note on the collision** — the two demon fathers (**Kālanemi** in the Devī-Bhāgavata chain, **Hiraṇyakaśipu** in the Bhāgavata's own statement) are *not* a contradiction to resolve but two attested genealogies of the same six souls; the Bhāgavata names Hiraṇyakaśipu, the Devī-Bhāgavata inserts a prior Kālanemi birth to motivate the "killed by their father" irony. Do not conflate them. [scholarly]

## The Descent to Sutala

- **Entering the netherworld** — By *yoga-māyā* the two brothers pass down into **Sutala**, the subterranean realm (one of the seven *pātālas* / *bila-svarga*; see [Bila-svarga & the Pātālas](../00-time-and-cosmology/bila-svarga-and-the-patalas.md)) granted to **Bali** after Vāmana pressed him down (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85.34*).
- **Bali the host** — **Bali Mahārāja** — the Vairocana *dānava*-king of the [Samudra-manthana](samudra-manthana.md) and the [Vāmana](vamana-bali.md) episode, grandson of Prahlāda and the paradigm of the surrendered devotee — recognizes them as **the Soul of the universe**, washes their feet with water that sanctifies the worlds, worships them with all his wealth, and asks only for their service (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85.35–46*).
- **The six in safekeeping** — The infants slain by Kaṃsa had been held in Sutala with Bali until the moment of their release; Kṛṣṇa asks for them and Bali yields them without resistance (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85.50; and the chapter's frame*).
  - *Note:* Sutala is a fitting waystation — it is an asura realm, and the Ṣaḍgarbha are, in every version, fallen beings of the asura line; they are recovered from the world proper to their cursed condition, not from Kṛṣṇa's Vaikuṇṭha. [scholarly]

## The Milk of Liberation

- **The return and suckling** — Brought back to **Dvārakā**, the six infants are placed before **Devakī**, who embraces them and — her breasts flowing with maternal love — **gives them her milk** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85.52–54*).
- **The touch that liberates** — The Bhāgavata's theological point: the milk is **remnant nectar of Kṛṣṇa's own touch** (Kṛṣṇa having been nursed at the same breast), so that by sipping it the six regain their **original divine identities** — the transcendental contact, not the milk as food, is what redeems them (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85.54–55*). [scholarly]
- **The ascent** — Restored to their godly forms, the six **bow to Kṛṣṇa, Balarāma, their father Vasudeva and to Devakī**, and — **freed from the curse** — depart to **the abode of the demigods** (svarga / the world of the *devas*) before the assembly's eyes (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85.55–56*).
- **Devakī's wonder** — Devakī marvels at seeing her sons return and vanish again, understanding the whole as **Kṛṣṇa's māyā**; the chapter closes praising the narrative as sin-destroying and liberating to hear (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.85.57–59*).
- **The arc closed** — the six who died to satisfy a curse are, once the curse is spent, retrieved and *sent home* — the infanticide of [Kaṃsa-vadha](kamsa-vadha.md) is thereby reframed retroactively as a passage, not an annihilation. This is the pair to Kṛṣṇa's resurrection of [Sāndīpani's son](sandipani-pancajana.md): both are recoveries of the dead as *dakṣiṇā*/filial debt, one to the guru, one to the mother.

## Two Clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic time:** the retrieval falls in Kṛṣṇa's **Dvārakā years**, on the **Dvāpara/Kali cusp** of the current 28th Mahāyuga (Kali's onset traditionally **3102 BCE**). The *backstory*, however, spans deep cyclic time — the six's original fall is dated to the **first (Svāyambhuva) Manvantara**, long before the present Vaivasvata age, so the episode explicitly threads two manvantaras of the cosmic clock.
- **Text-critical time:** the demon-rebirth frame is oldest in the **Harivaṃśa** (core ~1st–2nd c. BCE, growing into the early CE centuries); the mature retrieval-from-Sutala narrative is the **Bhāgavata's** (**[scholarly] c. 9th–10th c. CE**); the elaborated Kālanemi-father curse-chain is the **Devī-Bhāgavata's** (**[scholarly] c. 11th–14th c. CE**), the latest layer. [scholarly]
