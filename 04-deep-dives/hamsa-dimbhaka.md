# Haṃsa and Ḍimbhaka — Jarāsandha's Unslayable Allies

> Where this sits: **[Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)**, the Kṛṣṇa era — between the [slaying of Kaṃsa](./kamsa-vadha.md) and the Rājasūya of Yudhiṣṭhira. Two Śaiva warrior-brothers, unslayable by any weapon, who each drown in the Yamunā on news of the other's death — the loss [Kṛṣṇa](./krishna-lila.md) cites in the Sabhā Parva as the reason **Jarāsandha** of Magadha is at last assailable.
>
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Up to master index: [../README.md](../README.md) · Related: [Sabhā Parva](./mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md) · [Harivaṃśa](./harivamsha.md) · [Kaṃsa-vadha](./kamsa-vadha.md) · [Śiśupāla](./shishupala.md)

Two texts carry the story, at two very different scales: a compressed **retrospective by Kṛṣṇa in the Mahābhārata's Sabhā Parva (2.13–14)**, and a sprawling **war-epic of its own in the vulgate Harivaṃśa's Bhaviṣya Parva** (a late accretion, absent from the critical edition's constituted text). The two accounts disagree on almost every mechanical detail of the brothers' deaths — as always in this corpus, the variants are cataloged, never merged.

**Disambiguation** - This Haṃsa is *not* the [Haṃsa avatāra](./hamsa-avatar.md) of Viṣṇu (the swan who teaches the Kumāras); their father Brahmadatta of Śālva is *not* the [Brahmadatta who weds Kuśanābha's hundred daughters](../03-lineages-vamsha/kushanabha-daughters-and-brahmadatta.md); and the Śālva connection is distinct from [Śālva lord of Saubha](./shalva-saubha-vadha.md), a separate enemy of Kṛṣṇa.

---

## Who They Are

- **Kṣatriya brothers of the Śālva country** — **Haṃsa** (elder) and **Ḍimbhaka** (younger; spelled *Ḍibhaka* in some editions/manuscripts), sons of King **Brahmadatta** of Śālva. (Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva 2.13–14; Harivaṃśa, Bhaviṣya Parva 104 vulgate)
- **Born of Śiva's boon** — Brahmadatta, childless, worships Śaṅkara single-mindedly for ten years with his two queens; Śiva appears in a dream and grants him two sons. In the same episode Brahmadatta's brāhmaṇa friend **Mitrasaha** sacrifices to Viṣṇu for five years and is granted one son, **Janārdana** — a brāhmaṇa boy pointedly bearing Viṣṇu's own name, who grows up as the two princes' inseparable companion. (Harivaṃśa, Bhaviṣya Parva 104 vulgate) [late text]
- **Unslayable by weapons** — the brothers' defining trait: by Śiva's grace they are *incapable of being slain by weapons* (Ganguli's rendering), invincible in battle. The Purāṇic-encyclopaedia tradition adds that Śiva bestowed great astras (Raudra, Māheśvara, Brahmaśiras) and protective attendants on them. (Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva 2.13–14; Harivaṃśa, Bhaviṣya Parva vulgate; Vettam Mani, *Purāṇic Encyclopaedia*, s.v. Haṃsa) [scholarly]
- **Pupils of the Bhārgava** — trained in weapons as disciples of Bhṛgu's descendant — identified in the encyclopaedia tradition with [Paraśurāma](./parashurama.md) as their preceptor. (Harivaṃśa, Bhaviṣya Parva 103 vulgate; Vettam Mani) [disputed — the vulgate says "disciple(s) of Bhṛgu's son" without elaboration]
- **Allies of Jarāsandha** — the brothers stand with **Jarāsandha**, the Bārhadratha emperor of Magadha (for his ancestry see [Uparicara Vasu](../03-lineages-vamsha/uparichara-vasu.md)); united with him they "boasted before all the world," and the world regarded the trio as capable of facing the three worlds. They back his campaign against Mathurā after Kṛṣṇa kills Kaṃsa, Jarāsandha's son-in-law. (Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva 2.13–14)
- **A demon ally** — the Harivaṃśa adds the daitya **Vicakra**, commander of eighty-eight thousand dānava troops, who has defeated the devas and seeks to divide the [Yādavas](../03-lineages-vamsha/yadava-vamsha.md); Janamejaya's opening question names the war against "Haṃsa, Ḍimbhaka, and Vicakra" as the untold battle he wants narrated. (Harivaṃśa, Bhaviṣya Parva 103 vulgate) [late text]

## The Epic Notice — Kṛṣṇa's Speech in the Sabhā Parva (2.13–14)

The oldest and most authoritative version is a flashback: counseling Yudhiṣṭhira on the Rājasūya (see [Sabhā Parva](./mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md)), Kṛṣṇa names Jarāsandha as the one obstacle to empire — and explains why the tyrant, once untouchable, can now be brought down.

- **The invincible trio** — Kṛṣṇa recalls that Haṃsa and Ḍimbhaka, unslayable by weapons and equal to Jarāsandha in prowess, made the Magadhan emperor effectively unconquerable while they lived. (Sabhā Parva 2.13–14; Ganguli Section XIV)
- **The other Haṃsa: an eighteen-day battle** — during the Mathurā wars a king *also named* **Haṃsa** is slain by **[Balarāma](./characters/balarama.md)** (Rāma) after an encounter of **eighteen days**. (Sabhā Parva 2.14)
- **Ḍimbhaka drowns on false news** — hearing people cry "Haṃsa has been slain," Ḍimbhaka takes it to mean his brother, resolves he cannot live without Haṃsa, and hurls himself into the **Yamunā**, drowning. The report was true of the wrong man. (Sabhā Parva 2.14)
- **Haṃsa drowns in answer** — when the real Haṃsa hears that Ḍimbhaka has killed himself for his sake, he too goes to the Yamunā and enters its waters. Neither hero is touched by any weapon; the boon holds to the letter and is void in effect. (Sabhā Parva 2.14)
- **Jarāsandha's "empty heart"** — hearing that both are dead, Jarāsandha abandons the campaign and returns to his kingdom "with an empty heart" (Ganguli). The Yādavas breathe again in Mathurā — though Kaṃsa's widows, Jarāsandha's daughters, keep goading their father, and his pressure ultimately drives the Yādavas' flight to [Dvārakā](../06-sacred-geography/dwarka-bet-dwarka-saurashtra.md) (see [Mucukunda & Kālayavana](./muchukunda-and-kalayavana.md)). (Sabhā Parva 2.14)
- **The strategic conclusion** — *this* is why Kṛṣṇa raises the story: with Haṃsa and Ḍimbhaka drowned and Kaṃsa slain, Jarāsandha is stripped of the supports that made him invincible; he cannot be beaten by armies — even by gods and asuras together — but he *can* now be killed in single combat. Kṛṣṇa points to **[Bhīma](./characters/bhima.md)** as the man for it, setting up the Jarāsandha-vadha (Sabhā Parva 2.20–24). Among the supports that remain, Kṛṣṇa names **[Śiśupāla](./shishupala.md)** as Jarāsandha's generalissimo. (Sabhā Parva 2.13–14)

## The Harivaṃśa's Full Epic — Bhaviṣya Parva, chs. ~103–129 (vulgate) [late text]

The vulgate Harivaṃśa expands the epic's few verses into a war-narrative dozens of chapters long, framed — like the whole [Harivaṃśa](./harivamsha.md) — as Vaiśampāyana answering **Janamejaya**, who asks for the battle "not told before." The entire episode belongs to the Bhaviṣya-Parva accretion: the BORI critical edition relegates it to the appendices as a later insertion. [scholarly]

- **Birth and youth** — the double boon to Brahmadatta and Mitrasaha (above); the three boys — Haṃsa, Ḍimbhaka, Janārdana — study the Vedas, logic, and archery together and become fast friends. (Bhaviṣya Parva 104)
- **The hunt and Kāśyapa's sacrifice** — hunting with their army, the princes rest at lake **[Puṣkara](../06-sacred-geography/pushkar-brahma-tirtha.md)**, hear Vedic recitation, and visit the sage **Kāśyapa's** sacrifice to Viṣṇu; Haṃsa grandly invites the sages to the **Rājasūya** their father Brahmadatta intends after a conquest of the directions, boasting of their Śaiva boons of invincibility. (Bhaviṣya Parva 106–107)
- **Contempt for the ascetics** — meeting **Durvāsas** in meditation on the north bank of Puṣkara, the brothers deride the renunciate path as foolishness and resolve to force the ascetics back into householder life. (Bhaviṣya Parva 107)
- **The outrage at Puṣkara** — "prompted by Time," Haṃsa and Ḍimbhaka smash the hermitage swings, break the sages' vessels and water-pots, and have hunters cook meat in the sacred precinct before riding home; the brāhmaṇa Janārdana follows them out of affection, grieving at the sacrilege. (Bhaviṣya Parva 110)
- **Durvāsas leads five thousand sages to Dvārakā** — the insulted ascetics, counseled by Durvāsas, carry their broken belongings to Dvārakā to lay the outrage before Kṛṣṇa in the Sudharmā assembly. The episode casts Kṛṣṇa as the refuge of the very ascetics the Śaiva princes despise. (Bhaviṣya Parva 110)
  - *Variant (Vettam Mani, condensing the vulgate):* Durvāsas **curses** the brothers to die at Mahāviṣṇu's hands and himself informs Kṛṣṇa. The translated vulgate chapters sampled here show the outrage and the complaint but no explicit curse-verse; the curse may be Mani's compression or rest on another recension. [disputed] [scholarly]
- **The embassy of the salt-tribute** — the brothers send their friend Janārdana as envoy to Dvārakā demanding that Kṛṣṇa pay tribute and furnish quantities of **salt** for Brahmadatta's Rājasūya. Kṛṣṇa answers with prolonged laughter — no one, he observes, has ever before demanded tribute of *him* — and the Yādava court's mirth sends the humiliated envoy home with defiance. War follows. (Bhaviṣya Parva 115)
- **The great battle; Sātyaki vs. Ḍimbhaka** — in the war-books the duels multiply; a set-piece single combat pits **[Sātyaki](./characters/satyaki.md)** against Ḍimbhaka through volleys of arrows, repeatedly severed bows, and thirty-two sword-techniques, while gods and gandharvas watch and rank the champions — neither can drop the other, for Ḍimbhaka cannot be slain by weapons. (Bhaviṣya Parva 125)
- **Death of Haṃsa: the Kāliya pool** — routed by Kṛṣṇa's Vaiṣṇava weapon, Haṃsa leaps from his chariot and flees into the **Yamunā** — into the very abyss-pool where Kṛṣṇa had once subdued [Kāliya](./kaliya-mardana.md), a pit said to reach the netherworld. Kṛṣṇa plunges after him and **kicks him down** into the depths; onlookers debate whether the kick killed him or whether he sank to the nether regions and was devoured by the serpents there — he never rose again. (Bhaviṣya Parva 128)
- **Death of Ḍimbhaka: grief in the same water** — learning that Haṃsa is gone, Ḍimbhaka flees the field pursued by Balabhadra ([Balarāma](./characters/balarama.md)), plunges into the Yamunā where his brother fell, and — mocked by Kṛṣṇa's grim suggestion that he "ask the river" where Haṃsa has gone — searches the waters, laments, and kills himself in the river (the vulgate adds the grisly detail that he tears out his own tongue by the root as he dies). (Bhaviṣya Parva 129)
- **Aftermath** — the news of the enemy's end spreads at once, gandharvas sing Kṛṣṇa's victory, and the narrative notes that **Yudhiṣṭhira's Rājasūya** followed upon it — the Harivaṃśa thus placing the war immediately before the Sabhā Parva's imperial sacrifice. (Bhaviṣya Parva 128)

## The Variants, Side by Side (never merged)

- **Who kills them** — *Mahābhārata:* nobody; both are suicides of grief, the elder on false news — the boon of weapon-immunity is never even tested at the end. *Harivaṃśa vulgate:* Kṛṣṇa destroys Haṃsa bodily (a **kick**, not a weapon — the boon circumvented, not broken), and Ḍimbhaka's suicide follows real, not mistaken, news. (Sabhā Parva 2.14 vs. Bhaviṣya Parva 128–129)
- **Order of deaths** — *Mahābhārata:* Ḍimbhaka drowns first (deceived), Haṃsa second. *Harivaṃśa:* Haṃsa dies first, Ḍimbhaka second. (Sabhā Parva 2.14 vs. Bhaviṣya Parva 128–129)
- **The "other Haṃsa"** — the tragic engine of the epic version (a homonymous king slain by Balarāma after eighteen days) is absent from the Harivaṃśa's telling. (Sabhā Parva 2.14)
- **Setting on Kṛṣṇa's timeline** — *Mahābhārata:* the drownings occur during Jarāsandha's [Mathurā](../06-sacred-geography/mathura-vrindavan-braj.md) campaign, *before* the Yādava migration to Dvārakā. *Harivaṃśa:* Kṛṣṇa already reigns from Dvārakā's Sudharmā hall, and the war ends just before the Rājasūya. The chronologies cannot be harmonized; the accretion sits loosely on the epic's frame. [scholarly]
- **Jarāsandha's place** — central in the epic version (the brothers are *his* supports; their loss is why he is now assailable); peripheral in the Harivaṃśa war, where the brothers fight for their father Brahmadatta's imperial ambitions with the daitya Vicakra as ally. (Sabhā Parva 2.13–14 vs. Bhaviṣya Parva 103 ff.)
- **Stray Purāṇic mention** — Ḍimbhaka is also cited in passing at Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa IV.29.122, per the standard Purāṇic indices. (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa; wisdomlib index) [scholarly]
- **Modern reception** — Devdutt Pattanaik and others have floated a queer reading of the brothers' inseparability-unto-death ("Discovering Gay Generals in Jarasandha's Army"); the reading gained national press in 2023 when RSS chief Mohan Bhagwat invoked the pair. This is interpretation and reception, not scripture — the texts themselves call them brothers. [scholarly] [disputed]

## Two Clocks

- **Scriptural/cyclic** — late **[Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** of the current 28th Mahāyuga (Vaivasvata Manvantara): after Kaṃsa-vadha, before Jarāsandha-vadha and the Rājasūya — within the final generation of the yuga, which closes at Kṛṣṇa's departure (Kali begins 3102 BCE by tradition).
- **Academic/text-critical** [scholarly] — the Sabhā Parva notice belongs to the Mahābhārata's core narrative strata (epic composed c. 400 BCE – 400 CE); the Harivaṃśa expansion belongs to the **Bhaviṣya-Parva accretion**, judged the latest layer of the Harivaṃśa and excluded from the constituted text of Vaidya's critical edition (BORI, 1969–71) — i.e., a mid-to-late first-millennium CE elaboration spun out of the epic's brief allusion, with Janamejaya's question ("tell the battle you have not yet told") functioning as the accretion's own admission of supplementarity. [late text]

## Why the Story Matters

- **The loophole theme** — the tale belongs to the recurring pattern of boon-proofed enemies undone without violating the boon: unslayable *by weapons*, they die by **water** and **grief** (epic) or by a **kick** into the serpent-deeps (Harivaṃśa) — compare Kṛṣṇa's disposal of Kālayavana through [Mucukunda's gaze](./muchukunda-and-kalayavana.md) and of Jarāsandha through Bhīma's tearing. (Sabhā Parva; Harivaṃśa)
- **The hinge of the Rājasūya** — in the epic's own architecture, the brothers' drowning is the *precondition* of everything that follows in the Sabhā Parva: no dead Haṃsa and Ḍimbhaka, no feasible Jarāsandha-vadha; no Jarāsandha-vadha, no Rājasūya; no Rājasūya, no dice-game. (Sabhā Parva 2.13 ff.)
- **A Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava fault line** — the Harivaṃśa telling is frankly sectarian: Śiva-boon princes who despise Viṣṇu's ascetics, a Viṣṇu-named brāhmaṇa companion, Kāśyapa's Vaiṣṇava sacrifice, and Kṛṣṇa as the sages' avenger — kin to the Bāṇāsura war of the Viṣṇu Parva as a Hari-versus-Hara set-piece. (Harivaṃśa, Bhaviṣya Parva) [late text]
- **The Yamunā as instrument** — the river that sheltered the infant Kṛṣṇa and housed Kāliya here becomes the executioner of the unslayable; see [sacred rivers](../06-sacred-geography/sacred-rivers-and-descent-myths.md). (Sabhā Parva 2.14; Bhaviṣya Parva 128–129)
