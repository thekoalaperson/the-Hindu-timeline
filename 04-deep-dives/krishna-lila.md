# Kṛṣṇa-līlā — The Life & Deeds of Krishna

> Where this sits: the complete earthly career of **Kṛṣṇa**, 8th avatāra of [Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md), born at the junction of [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga. His departure (3102 BCE by tradition) marks the onset of Kali Yuga. He is of the [Chandravamsha](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) (Yadu/Vṛṣṇi line).
>
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./) · Up to: [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) · Related: [Bhagavad Gītā](bhagavad-gita.md) · [Mahābhārata](./mahabharata/) · [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)

This file consolidates Krishna's life **beyond the Kurukṣetra war** (for the war and Gītā, see the [Mahābhārata](./mahabharata/) and [Gītā](bhagavad-gita.md) deep-dives). The principal source is the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (Canto X = childhood/Vraja/Mathurā; Canto XI = Dwārakā, Uddhava, departure), supplemented by the **Harivaṃśa** (appendix to the Mahābhārata, the oldest connected Krishna biography), **Viṣṇu Purāṇa Book 5**, Jayadeva's **Gīta-Govinda** (12th c.), the **Garga Saṃhitā**, and the **Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa**.

---

## Birth & Infancy (Bhāgavata X.1–6)

- **Prophecy against Kaṃsa** - The tyrant Kaṃsa of Mathurā hears a voice (ākāśavāṇī) that the 8th child of his sister Devakī (wife of Vasudeva) will kill him; he imprisons the couple (Bhāgavata X.1; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.1).
- **Slaughter of the first six** - Kaṃsa kills Devakī's first six infants. The 7th, Balarāma (Saṅkarṣaṇa), is mystically transferred (āṣ by Yogamāyā) to the womb of Rohiṇī in Gokula (Bhāgavata X.2).
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa / Viṣṇu Purāṇa):* the six slain children are the sons of the demon Kālanemi / Hiraṇyakaśipu's line, reborn — explaining their fate karmically.
- **Janma (birth)** - Kṛṣṇa born at midnight, Bhādrapada Kṛṣṇa-aṣṭamī (Janmāṣṭamī), as the dark-hued (śyāma) 8th child, displaying his four-armed Viṣṇu form to his parents before assuming infant shape (Bhāgavata X.3).
- **The river crossing** - Vasudeva carries the newborn across the flooding Yamunā (parted by the river / sheltered by Śeṣa's hood) to Nanda and Yaśodā in Gokula, swapping him for Yaśodā's just-born daughter (Yogamāyā) (Bhāgavata X.3).
- **Slaying of the goddess-child / Yogamāyā** - Kaṃsa dashes the substituted girl on a rock; she rises as the eight-armed goddess (Durgā/Yogamāyā), declares his slayer already born, and vanishes (Bhāgavata X.4). [This is a key Śākta tie-in — see [Devī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md).]

## Vraja / Gokula–Vṛndāvana Childhood (Bhāgavata X.6–14)

The demon-slayings of infancy — most sent by Kaṃsa — and the pastoral miracles:

- **Pūtanā** - The demoness, sent disguised as a wet-nurse with poisoned breast-milk, is drained of life by the suckling infant; granted liberation (mokṣa) for the maternal act (Bhāgavata X.6; Harivaṃśa).
- **Śakaṭāsura** - The cart-demon; baby Krishna overturns a heavy cart with a kick (Bhāgavata X.7).
- **Tṛṇāvarta** - The whirlwind-demon carries Krishna aloft; the child grows heavy and strangles him (Bhāgavata X.7).
- **The vision of the universe in his mouth** - Yaśodā, checking for eaten dirt, sees the entire cosmos inside infant Krishna's mouth (Bhāgavata X.8).
- **Dāmodara / Ūkhala-bandhana** - Yaśodā binds the butter-thief to a mortar; he drags it between two arjuna trees, freeing the cursed Yakṣas Nalakūbara and Maṇigrīva (Bhāgavata X.9–10).
- **Move to Vṛndāvana** - The cowherds migrate from Gokula to Vṛndāvana for better pasture (Bhāgavata X.11).
- **Vatsāsura & Bakāsura** - The calf-demon and the crane/heron-demon, both slain (Bhāgavata X.11).
- **Aghāsura** - The python-demon (Pūtanā's brother) swallows the boys; Krishna kills him from within (Bhāgavata X.12).
- **Brahmā's bewilderment (Brahma-vimohana)** - Brahmā steals the cowherd boys and calves to test Krishna; Krishna multiplies himself into identical copies of all of them for a year (Bhāgavata X.13).
- **Dhenukāsura** - The donkey-demon of the Tālavana palm-grove, killed by Balarāma (Bhāgavata X.15).
- **Kāliya-damana** - Krishna subdues the many-hooded serpent Kāliya poisoning the Yamunā, dancing on his hoods, then banishes him to Ramaṇaka (Bhāgavata X.16–17).
- **Swallowing the forest fire** - Krishna twice consumes a forest conflagration to save the herders (Bhāgavata X.17, X.19).
- **Pralambāsura** - Demon disguised as a cowherd; Balarāma kills him with a fist (Bhāgavata X.18).

## Rasa-līlā, the Gopīs & Rādhā (Bhāgavata X.21–33)

- **The vastra-haraṇa (stealing the gopīs' clothes)** - Krishna takes the bathing gopīs' garments to a kadamba tree, teaching surrender of ego (Bhāgavata X.22).
- **Wives of the brāhmaṇas** - The yajña-patnīs feed Krishna when their ritualist husbands refuse; devotion over ritualism (Bhāgavata X.23).
- **Govardhana-dhāraṇa** - Krishna persuades the cowherds to abandon Indra-worship for Govardhana/cow worship; the enraged Indra sends torrential rain; Krishna lifts Mount Govardhana on his little finger for seven days as an umbrella (Bhāgavata X.24–25; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).
- **Indra's submission / Govinda abhiṣeka** - Indra and the cow Surabhi consecrate Krishna as "Govinda" (Bhāgavata X.27).
- **Rasa-līlā (the circle dance)** - On the autumn full-moon night, summoned by Krishna's flute, the gopīs leave homes to dance the rāsa; he multiplies himself to partner each one; the "Gopī-gīta" laments his disappearance and return (Bhāgavata X.29–33). This is the theological heart of bhakti-rasa.
  - **Rādhā's textual status (catalog all variants):**
    - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa):* Rādhā is **never named**. Commentators (Jīva Gosvāmī, Viśvanātha Cakravartī, Sanātana Gosvāmī) read her *indirectly* into verse **X.30.28** — the one favored gopī Krishna leads away, whom the others trace by her footprints. [scholarly: her absence here is much-discussed]
    - *Variant (Gāhā Sattasaī of Hāla, ~1st–2nd c. CE):* earliest surviving mention of the name "Rādhā" with Krishna — a single Prakrit verse. [scholarly]
    - *Variant (Gīta-Govinda, Jayadeva, 12th c.):* Rādhā becomes the **central heroine**, Krishna's beloved (nāyikā) through union, jealousy (māna), separation, and reunion — the work that fixed Rādhā in pan-Indian devotion. [late text relative to the Purāṇas]
    - *Variant (Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa, late):* Rādhā is Krishna's eternal śakti/consort, equal/supreme — Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa as the supreme dyad; she even marries him (rites performed by Brahmā in Goloka). [late text]
    - *Variant (Padma, Brahmāṇḍa, Skanda, Devī-Bhāgavata, Garga Saṃhitā):* Rādhā named and exalted; the **Garga Saṃhitā** gives the fullest Vaiṣṇava Vraja-biography of Rādhā as eternal consort. [late texts]
    - *Variant (Nimbārka & Gauḍīya/Caitanya theology):* Rādhā as hlādinī-śakti (Krishna's bliss-potency); Caitanya worshipped as Krishna's interior experience of Rādhā's love. [folk/sectarian]

## Departure from Vraja & the Fall of Kaṃsa (Bhāgavata X.34–45)

- **Akrūra's mission** - Kaṃsa sends Akrūra to bring Krishna and Balarāma to Mathurā for a "bow sacrifice" (Dhanur-yajña), intending a trap; Akrūra has a vision of Krishna as Viṣṇu in the Yamunā (Bhāgavata X.38–39).
- **The gopīs' grief / Uddhava's later consolation** - Krishna departs Vṛndāvana; the famous separation (viraha) of the gopīs, never fully resolved on earth (Bhāgavata X.39).
- **The hunchback Trivakrā (Kubjā)** - Krishna straightens the deformed maidservant who gives him sandalwood (Bhāgavata X.42).
- **Breaking the bow & killing the elephant** - Krishna snaps Kaṃsa's great bow; slays the rogue elephant Kuvalayāpīḍa at the arena gate (Bhāgavata X.42–43).
- **Cāṇūra & Muṣṭika** - Krishna and Balarāma kill Kaṃsa's champion wrestlers (Bhāgavata X.44).
- **Vadha of Kaṃsa** - Krishna leaps the dais, drags Kaṃsa by the hair, kills him; frees Devakī, Vasudeva, and the deposed king Ugrasena, reinstating him (Bhāgavata X.44; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).
- **Education under Sāndīpani** - Krishna and Balarāma study the Vedas/64 arts at Avantī (Ujjain); as guru-dakṣiṇā they recover Sāndīpani's drowned son from the demon Pañcajana / sea-realm of Yama (Bhāgavata X.45).

## Mathurā, Jarāsandha & the Founding of Dwārakā (Bhāgavata X.46–52)

- **Uddhava's embassy to Vraja** - Krishna sends his friend/cousin Uddhava with a message to the grieving gopīs and parents; Uddhava is humbled by the gopīs' supreme love (Bhāgavata X.46–47, the "Bhramara-gīta").
- **Jarāsandha's invasions** - Kaṃsa's father-in-law Jarāsandha of Magadha attacks Mathurā seventeen times and is repelled (Bhāgavata X.50; Harivaṃśa).
- **Kālayavana & the founding of Dwārakā** - Facing the foreign king Kālayavana, Krishna strategically withdraws ("Raṇacchoḍa") and builds the island-city **Dwārakā** in the western sea for the Yādavas; he lures Kālayavana into the cave of the sleeping sage Mucukunda, whose gaze incinerates him (Bhāgavata X.50–52).

## Marriages & the Eight Principal Queens (Aṣṭa-bhāryā) (Bhāgavata X.52–61)

- **Rukmiṇī-haraṇa** - Krishna abducts princess Rukmiṇī of Vidarbha (who loves him) from her own wedding to Śiśupāla, at her secret request; defeats her brother Rukmī (Bhāgavata X.52–54). She is regarded as an incarnation of Lakṣmī and the chief queen.
- **The Syamantaka jewel & Jāmbavatī / Satyabhāmā** - The sun-gem Syamantaka (given by Sūrya to Satrājit) is suspected stolen by Krishna; he tracks it to a cave, fights the bear-king **Jāmbavān** 28 days, who recognizes him as Rāma's lord and gives both the jewel and his daughter **Jāmbavatī**; Satrājit, vindicated, gives Krishna his daughter **Satyabhāmā** (Bhāgavata X.56–57; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4).
- **The other queens** - **Kālindī** (the Yamunā goddess), **Mitravindā**, **Satyā/Nāgnajitī** (won by taming seven bulls), **Bhadrā**, and **Lakṣmaṇā** complete the eight (Bhāgavata X.58). Differences in the eighth name across texts:
  - *Variant:* Bhāgavata lists Rukmiṇī, Satyabhāmā, Jāmbavatī, Kālindī, Mitravindā, Nāgnajitī (Satyā), Bhadrā, Lakṣmaṇā.
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa / other lists):* names/order of the minor queens vary (e.g. "Mādrī" / "Sunāmā" in some recensions). [textual]
- **Naraka(asura)-vadha & the 16,100** - Krishna (with Satyabhāmā riding Garuḍa) slays the demon **Narakāsura** (Bhauma) of Prāgjyotiṣa (Assam), who had imprisoned **16,100 royal women** and stolen Aditi's earrings and Varuṇa's parasol; he frees the captives (Bhāgavata X.59; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5; Harivaṃśa).
  - **The 16,100 wives:** the freed women, unmarriageable in society, are accepted by Krishna as wives so they live with honor; he multiplies himself to maintain each household — total **16,108** (8 principal + 16,100). The Bhāgavata describes him simultaneously present in all palaces (Bhāgavata X.59, X.69).
  - *Variant:* some retellings give 16,000; the standard Vaiṣṇava count is 16,100 freed + 8 = 16,108. [textual]
- **The Pārijāta episode** - To please Satyabhāmā, Krishna takes the celestial Pārijāta tree from Indra's heaven, defeating Indra — the gods' lord bested by his own avatāra (Bhāgavata X.59; Harivaṃśa, where it is elaborated).
- **Pradyumna** - Krishna and Rukmiṇī's son (Kāmadeva reborn) is stolen by the demon Śambara, raised by Māyāvatī (Rati), and returns to slay him (Bhāgavata X.55).
- **Aniruddha & Uṣā / the war with Bāṇāsura** - Krishna's grandson Aniruddha weds Uṣā, daughter of the thousand-armed asura Bāṇa (devotee of Śiva); Krishna battles Bāṇa and even Śiva's forces (Bhāgavata X.62–63). [major Vaiṣṇava–Śaiva interface episode]

## Friendships, Kingly Deeds & the War's Periphery (Bhāgavata X.64–90)

- **King Nṛga the chameleon** - The Yādava boys find a giant chameleon in a dry well; Krishna lifts it out and it resumes the form of King **Nṛga**, cursed to that body for inadvertently giving one brāhmaṇa's strayed cow to another; released, he ascends — a royal object-lesson on the peril of misappropriating brāhmaṇa property (Bhāgavata X.64).
- **Pauṇḍraka, the false Vāsudeva, & the burning of Kāśī** - King **Pauṇḍraka** of Karūṣa, decked in imitation regalia (a counterfeit conch, disc, and Śrīvatsa), demands Krishna renounce the name "Vāsudeva"; Krishna beheads him and his ally the king of Kāśī. When the Kāśī king's son Sudakṣiṇa unleashes an abhicāra fire-demon (kṛtyā) against Dwārakā, the Sudarśana cakra drives it back and **incinerates the city of Kāśī** (Bhāgavata X.66).
- **Dvivida the ape** - The mountain-hurling gorilla **Dvivida** — Naraka's friend (and, in the vānara genealogies, Mainda's twin of Rāma's host, turned rogue), avenging Naraka by ravaging Ānarta and molesting its people — is battered to death by **Balarāma** on Raivataka hill (Bhāgavata X.67).
- **Sāmba's capture & the dragging of Hastināpura** - Krishna's son **Sāmba** (by Jāmbavatī) abducts Duryodhana's daughter **Lakṣmaṇā** from her svayaṃvara and is captured by the Kuru champions; **Balarāma**, come as peacemaker and slighted by Kaurava arrogance, **hooks his plough into Hastināpura's ramparts and drags the city toward the Gaṅgā** until the terrified Kurus release the couple with dowry and honors (Bhāgavata X.68). [For Sāmba's later leprosy and the Sun-cult it founds, see the [Bhaviṣya Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/bhavishya-purana.md).]
- **Slaying of Jarāsandha** - Krishna, with Bhīma and Arjuna in disguise, engineers Bhīma's wrestling victory that kills Jarāsandha, freeing the kings he had imprisoned — clearing the way for Yudhiṣṭhira's Rājasūya (Bhāgavata X.72; Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva).
- **Death of Śiśupāla** - At the Rājasūya, Krishna is honored first (agra-pūjā); Śiśupāla insults him past the 100 forgiven offenses and is beheaded by the Sudarśana cakra (Bhāgavata X.74; Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva).
- **Śālva's Saubha & Dantavakra** - Śiśupāla's ally **Śālva**, wielding the Śiva-granted aerial fortress-machine **Saubha**, besieges Dwārakā to avenge him; Krishna shatters the Saubha and beheads Śālva, then slays **Dantavakra** of Karūṣa, who attacks in revenge — discharging the third and last [Jaya–Vijaya](jaya-vijaya.md) birth-pair (Bhāgavata X.76–78; see [Śiśupāla](shishupala.md)).
- **Balarāma's tīrtha-yātrā — Romaharṣaṇa & Balvala** - Refusing to take sides at Kurukṣetra, **Balarāma** tours the sacred fords; at Naimiṣāraṇya he kills the sūta **Romaharṣaṇa** (Vyāsa's disciple, who failed to rise for him) with a blade of kuśa grass, expiates the deed by installing Romaharṣaṇa's son **Ugraśravas** as reciter, slays the sage-harassing demon **Balvala**, and completes the pilgrimage in time to witness the Bhīma–Duryodhana mace-duel (Bhāgavata X.78–79).
- **Sudāmā / Kucela** - Krishna's impoverished boyhood Brahmin friend brings a humble gift of flattened rice (pṛthuka); Krishna honors him lavishly and silently grants him wealth — paradigm of devotion and divine reciprocity (Bhāgavata X.80–81). [In South Indian usage the friend is called **Kucela**.]
- **Reunion at Kurukṣetra (solar eclipse)** - On an eclipse pilgrimage the Vraja folk, gopīs, Nanda, Yaśodā, and the Pāṇḍavas all reunite with Krishna at Samanta-pañcaka (Bhāgavata X.82).
- **Śrutadeva & Bahulāśva** - Visiting Mithilā (Videha) with a retinue of sages, Krishna **bilocates** — simultaneously guesting with the poor brāhmaṇa **Śrutadeva** and King **Bahulāśva** — honoring the equal devotion of pauper and monarch (Bhāgavata X.86).
- **The Śruti-gīta / Veda-stuti** - Answering Parīkṣit's question of **how the Vedas, which operate among the guṇas, can indicate the attributeless (nirguṇa) Brahman**, Śuka relays **Nārāyaṇa Ṛṣi's** teaching to Nārada at Badarikāśrama: the **personified śrutis' own hymn** to the Lord at the dawn of creation (Bhāgavata X.87; catalogued among the [lesser Gītās](lesser-gitas-catalog.md)).
- **Recovering the dead sons of a Brahmin** - Krishna and Arjuna travel beyond the world to retrieve a Brahmin's lost children from Mahā-Viṣṇu, revealing Krishna's supreme identity to Arjuna (Bhāgavata X.89).
- **The Kurukṣetra war role** - As Arjuna's charioteer and counselor he speaks the [Bhagavad Gītā](bhagavad-gita.md) and steers the Pāṇḍava victory without bearing arms (covered fully in the [Mahābhārata](./mahabharata/) deep-dive).

## Final Teachings & Departure (Bhāgavata XI; Mausala Parva)

- **Curse of the sages / Gāndhārī's curse** - Yādava boys mock ṛṣis (Viśvāmitra, Durvāsā, Nārada), who curse the clan to destruction by an iron club; Gāndhārī, bereaved by the war, also curses Krishna's line to perish (Bhāgavata XI.1; Mausala Parva 1).
- **The Uddhava-Gītā** - Krishna's last great discourse, to his devotee **Uddhava**, on jñāna, bhakti, vairāgya, the avadhūta's 24 gurus, and yoga — the "other Gītā" (Bhāgavata XI.7–29). Uddhava is sent to Badarikāśrama before the end.
- **Yādava fratricide (Mausala)** - At Prabhāsa, drunken Yādavas turn the cursed reeds-turned-clubs on each other; Balarāma, Sātyaki, Kṛtavarmā, Pradyumna and almost the whole clan die (Bhāgavata XI.30; Mausala Parva 3).
- **Balarāma's departure** - Balarāma (Saṅkarṣaṇa / aṃśa of Śeṣa) merges into yoga by the sea; a great white serpent issues from his mouth into the ocean (Bhāgavata XI.30; Mausala Parva).
- **Death of Krishna** - Resting in the forest, Krishna is shot in the sole of his foot (his one vulnerable spot) by the hunter **Jarā**, who mistakes the foot for a deer; Krishna forgives him and ascends to his abode (Bhāgavata XI.30–31; Mausala Parva 4).
  - *Variant:* Jarā is identified as Vālī reborn — settling the karmic debt of [Rāma](./ramayana/)'s killing of Vālī in a prior avatāra. [Purāṇic gloss]
- **Submersion of Dwārakā** - As Krishna departs, the sea reclaims Dwārakā; Arjuna evacuates the surviving women and children, who are then raided by bandits en route (Mausala Parva 7–8).
- **Onset of Kali Yuga** - Krishna's departure (traditionally **3102 BCE**) marks the start of [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md); the Pāṇḍavas soon undertake their final journey (mahāprasthāna).

## Theological Note: Svayam Bhagavān

- The **Bhāgavata** uniquely calls Krishna not merely an avatāra but *svayam bhagavān* — "God himself" — in 1.3.28 (`kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam`), the source of all avatāras rather than one among them. Gauḍīya, Nimbārka, Vallabha, and Puṣṭimārga traditions build on this; the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) lists him among Viṣṇu's ten (with Balarāma or Buddha substituting in regional lists). [scholarly/sectarian]
