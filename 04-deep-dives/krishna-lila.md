# Kṛṣṇa-līlā — The Life & Deeds of Krishna

> Where this sits: the complete earthly career of **Kṛṣṇa**, 8th avatāra of [Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) in the common ten-avatāra list, born at the junction of [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga. His departure (3102 BCE by tradition) marks the onset of Kali Yuga. He is of the [Chandravamsha](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) (Yadu/Vṛṣṇi line).
>
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./) · Up to: [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) · Related: [Bhagavad Gītā](bhagavad-gita.md) · [Mahābhārata](./mahabharata/) · [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md)


This file consolidates Krishna's life **beyond the Kurukṣetra war** (for the war and Gītā, see the [Mahābhārata](./mahabharata/) and [Gītā](bhagavad-gita.md) deep-dives). The principal source is the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (Canto X = childhood/Vraja/Mathurā/Dvārakā; Canto XI = Uddhava, the Yādava destruction, departure), supplemented by the **[Harivaṃśa](harivamsha.md)** (the khila or appendix to the Mahābhārata, generally regarded as the oldest connected Krishna biography [scholarly]), **[Viṣṇu Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/vishnu-purana.md) Book 5**, the **Mahābhārata** itself (Sabhā, Mausala and other parvas), Jayadeva's **Gīta-Govinda** (12th c.), the **Garga Saṃhitā**, and the **[Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/brahmavaivarta-purana.md)**. Bhāgavata references are skandha.adhyāya.verse in the GRETIL text; Mahābhārata references are to the BORI Critical Edition (CE); Harivaṃśa references are to the critical edition's adhyāya.verse (HV).

## Birth & Infancy (Bhāgavata X.1–6)

- **Prophecy against Kaṃsa** - The tyrant Kaṃsa of Mathurā hears a voice (ākāśavāṇī) that the 8th child of his sister Devakī (wife of Vasudeva) will kill him; he imprisons the couple (Bhāgavata X.1; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.1).
- **Slaughter of the first six** - Kaṃsa kills Devakī's first six infants. The 7th, Balarāma, is drawn out of Devakī's womb by Yogamāyā and placed in the womb of Rohiṇī, who lives in Nanda's Gokula; the drawing-out (*saṅkarṣaṇa*) gives him the name Saṅkarṣaṇa (Bhāgavata 10.2.8, 10.2.13).
  - *Who the six were: the texts disagree.*
    - *Variant (Harivaṃśa 47.11–27):* they are the six **Ṣaḍgarbhas**, sons of the asura **Kālanemi**. They won a boon of near-invulnerability from Brahmā, and **Hiraṇyakaśipu**, angered that they had worshipped Brahmā instead of him, cursed them to be killed in the womb by their own father reborn (Kaṃsa). Viṣṇu has Nidrā carry their life-breaths from Pātāla into Devakī's womb.
    - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.1.70–72):* they are the Ṣaḍgarbhas, here called sons of **Hiraṇyakaśipu** himself, brought from Pātāla by Yoganidrā at Viṣṇu's command.
    - *Variant (Bhāgavata 10.85.47–51):* they were the six sons of **Marīci** and Ūrṇā, cursed for laughing at Brahmā, born next as sons of Hiraṇyakaśipu and then of Devakī. Their names are Smara, Udgītha, Pariṣvaṅga, Pataṅga, Kṣudrabhṛt and Ghṛṇin. Krishna later brings them back to Devakī (see "Devakī's six sons" below).
- **Janma (birth)** - Kṛṣṇa is born at midnight (*niśīthe*, Bhāgavata 10.3.8) under the asterism of Brahmā (Rohiṇī, *ajana-janmarkṣa*, 10.3.1), as the dark-hued (śyāma) 8th child, displaying his four-armed Viṣṇu form to his parents before assuming infant shape (Bhāgavata X.3).
  - *The date:* the Bhāgavata gives no month or tithi. The Harivaṃśa gives midnight in the Abhijit muhūrta (HV 48.13); a passage that the Harivaṃśa critical edition prints only as a manuscript insertion adds "the eighth of the dark fortnight of Śrāvaṇa, under Rohiṇī". Counting months from new moon (amānta), that is Śrāvaṇa; counting from full moon (pūrṇimānta, the north Indian usage), the same day is Bhādrapada kṛṣṇa-aṣṭamī. This is the festival day of **Janmāṣṭamī**.
- **The river crossing** - Vasudeva carries the newborn across the flooding Yamunā to Nanda and Yaśodā in Gokula, swapping him for Yaśodā's just-born daughter (Yogamāyā). Śeṣa follows, sheltering the child with his hoods, and the river makes way for him "as the ocean did for the Lord of Śrī", which commentators read as the ocean giving way to Rāma (Bhāgavata 10.3.49–50; Harivaṃśa).
- **Slaying of the goddess-child / Yogamāyā** - Kaṃsa dashes the substituted girl on a rock; she slips from his hands, rises into the sky as the eight-armed goddess bearing weapons (*sāyudhāṣṭa-mahābhujā*, Bhāgavata 10.4.9–10), declares his slayer already born, and vanishes (Bhāgavata X.4). [This is a key Śākta tie-in — see [Devī](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/README.md).]

## Vraja / Gokula–Vṛndāvana Childhood (Bhāgavata X.6–14)

The demon-slayings of infancy — most sent by Kaṃsa — and the pastoral miracles. (This section runs on to Bhāgavata X.20.)

- **Pūtanā** - The child-killing rākṣasī comes disguised as a beautiful woman and offers her poisoned breast; the infant sucks out her life. Because she gave him her breast, even with intent to kill, she attains a good destination (*sad-gatim*), the heaven of a mother (*svargam … jananī-gatim*) (Bhāgavata 10.6.35, 10.6.38). Commentators read this as liberation (mokṣa).
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa 45.6; 50.20–24):* Pūtanā is Kaṃsa's nurse (*dhātrī*) and a daughter of Bali. She comes at midnight as a monstrous **bird** (*śakunī*), perches on the cart's axle and gives the child her breast while the herders sleep; he sucks her breast together with her life breath. His cry wakes the herders, who find her lying dead on the ground. In the Harivaṃśa the cart incident comes before Pūtanā.
- **The overturned cart (Śakaṭa)** - The three-month-old infant, lying under a cart, kicks it with his tender foot and overturns it, smashing its pots (Bhāgavata 10.7.7; 2.7.27). The Bhāgavata speaks only of a cart (*anas*, *śakaṭa*); the demon **Śakaṭāsura** said to possess it comes from commentators and later retellings.
- **Tṛṇāvarta** - The whirlwind-demon (*daitya*) carries Krishna aloft; the child grows heavy and strangles him (Bhāgavata 10.7.20–29; 10.11.25).
- **The vision of the universe in his mouth** - Twice in the Bhāgavata: once as Yaśodā fondles the yawning child after nursing (10.7.35–37), and again when, accused of eating dirt, he opens his mouth and she sees the whole cosmos, mountains, oceans and stars in it (10.8.32–45).
- **Dāmodara / Ūkhala-bandhana** - Yaśodā binds the butter-thief to a mortar; he drags it between two arjuna trees, freeing the cursed Yakṣas Nalakūbara and Maṇigrīva (Bhāgavata X.9–10).
- **Move to Vṛndāvana** - On the advice of the elder Upananda, who points to the great portents and the child's narrow escapes (Pūtanā, the cart, the whirlwind, the falling trees), the cowherds migrate from Gokula to Vṛndāvana (Bhāgavata 10.11.22–36).
- **Vatsāsura & Bakāsura** - The calf-demon and the crane/heron-demon, both slain (Bhāgavata X.11).
- **Aghāsura** - The python-demon, younger brother of Pūtanā and Baka (*bakī-bakānujaḥ*), swallows the boys; Krishna kills him from within (Bhāgavata 10.12.14 and X.12).
- **Brahmā's bewilderment (Brahma-vimohana)** - Brahmā steals the cowherd boys and calves to test Krishna; Krishna multiplies himself into identical copies of all of them for a year (*ābdam*, Bhāgavata 10.13.40); Brahmā's hymn follows (X.14).
- **Dhenukāsura** - The donkey-demon of the Tālavana palm-grove, killed by Balarāma (Bhāgavata X.15).
- **[Kāliya-damana](kaliya-mardana.md)** - Krishna subdues the many-hooded serpent Kāliya poisoning the Yamunā, dancing on his hoods, then orders him to leave the Yamunā for the sea (*samudraṃ yāhi*, 10.16.60), that is, back to the island of Ramaṇaka, which Kāliya had left in fear of Garuḍa; Krishna's footprints on his hoods will now protect him (Bhāgavata X.16–17; 10.16.60–63).
- **Swallowing the forest fire** - Krishna twice consumes a forest conflagration to save the herders (Bhāgavata X.17, X.19).
- **Pralambāsura** - Demon disguised as a cowherd; Balarāma kills him with a fist (Bhāgavata X.18).

## Rasa-līlā, the Gopīs & Rādhā (Bhāgavata X.21–33)

- **The vastra-haraṇa (stealing the gopīs' clothes)** - The unmarried gopīs keep a month-long vow to the goddess **Kātyāyanī**, praying for "the son of Nanda the cowherd" as husband (Bhāgavata 10.22.4). Krishna takes their garments as they bathe and climbs a kadamba tree (*nīpa*, 10.22.9), returning them only when they come out with joined hands (Bhāgavata X.22). Devotional readings take it as a lesson in surrendering ego and shame. [interpretation]
- **Wives of the brāhmaṇas** - The yajña-patnīs feed Krishna when their ritualist husbands refuse; devotion over ritualism (Bhāgavata X.23).
- **[Govardhana-dhāraṇa](govardhana-dharana.md)** - Krishna persuades the cowherds to abandon Indra-worship for Govardhana/cow worship; the enraged Indra sends torrential rain; Krishna lifts Mount Govardhana **with one hand** (*ekena hastena*) "as a child holds a mushroom" and holds it as an umbrella for **seven days** (Bhāgavata 10.25.19, 10.25.23; 2.7.32; Harivaṃśa 61.58, seven nights; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.11.16, *ekakareṇa*).
  - *The little finger:* the image of Krishna holding the mountain on his little finger belongs to later devotional retelling and iconography; the Bhāgavata, Harivaṃśa and Viṣṇu Purāṇa all say "one hand". [later tradition]
- **Indra's submission / Govinda abhiṣeka** - Indra and the cow Surabhi consecrate Krishna as "Govinda" (Bhāgavata X.27).
- **Nanda rescued from Varuṇa** - After his Ekādaśī fast, Nanda bathes in the Yamunā at an inauspicious hour of night and is carried off by a servant of Varuṇa; Krishna goes to Varuṇa's realm and brings him back (Bhāgavata 10.28.1–10).
- **Rasa-līlā (the circle dance)** - On the autumn full-moon night, summoned by Krishna's flute, the gopīs leave homes to dance the rāsa; he vanishes, they search for him, and their lament, the "Gopī-gīta" (X.31), brings him back; in the dance he multiplies himself to partner each one (Bhāgavata X.29–33). This is the theological heart of bhakti-rasa.
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa 63.15–35):* the gopīs, defying their families, seek Krishna at night and sport with him in circles (*cakravālaiḥ*) on moonlit autumn nights. There is no disappearance, no search, and no Gopī-gīta.
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.13):* a fuller rāsa with the disappearance and search, and the circle dance (*rāsa-maṇḍala*, 5.13.48ff). Like the Harivaṃśa, it names no single beloved.
  - **Rādhā's textual status (catalog all variants):**
    - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa):* Rādhā is **never named**. In the search, the gopīs find the footprints of one gopī whom Krishna led away alone and say "surely by her the Lord was worshipped" (*anayārādhito nūnam*, **X.30.28**). Commentators (Jīva Gosvāmī, Viśvanātha Cakravartī, Sanātana Gosvāmī) read Rādhā's name into the verb *ārādhitaḥ*. [scholarly: her absence here is much-discussed]
    - *Variant (Gāhā Sattasaī attributed to Hāla):* one Prakrit verse (no. 89 in Weber's edition) has Krishna blowing the dust from Rādhā's face, and it is often cited as the earliest mention of her name. Both the anthology's date (traditionally 1st–2nd c. CE through the Sātavāhana king Hāla) and this verse's place in it are contested; the verse has often been suspected as an interpolation because it is about Rādhā. Other candidates for early mentions are an Apabhraṃśa verse of Govinda quoted in Svayambhū's 9th-century *Svayambhūcchandas* and the 11th-century *Daśāvatāracarita* of Kṣemendra. [disputed]
    - *Variant (Gīta-Govinda, Jayadeva, 12th c.):* Rādhā becomes the **central heroine**, Krishna's beloved (nāyikā) through union, jealousy (māna), separation, and reunion — the work that fixed Rādhā in pan-Indian devotion. [late text relative to the Purāṇas]
    - *Variant (Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa, late):* Rādhā is Krishna's eternal śakti/consort, equal/supreme — Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa as the supreme dyad, eternally together in Goloka. On earth she even marries him: Nanda, caught in a storm in a Vraja forest with the infant Krishna, hands him to Rādhā, and Brahmā performs the wedding rites (Kṛṣṇa-janma-khaṇḍa 15). Braj tradition places the wedding at **Bhāṇḍīravana**. [late text]
    - *Variant (Padma, Brahmāṇḍa, Skanda, Devī-Bhāgavata, Garga Saṃhitā):* Rādhā named and exalted; the **Garga Saṃhitā** gives the fullest Vaiṣṇava Vraja-biography of Rādhā as eternal consort, and also tells a Bhāṇḍīravana wedding with Brahmā as priest. [late texts]
    - *Variant (Nimbārka & Gauḍīya/Caitanya theology):* Rādhā is Krishna's bliss-potency (hlādinī-śakti). Gauḍīya theology holds that Caitanya is Krishna himself, taking on Rādhā's love and complexion to taste her devotion from within (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi-līlā 1 and 4). See [Nimbārka, Vallabha & Caitanya](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/nimbarka-vallabha-chaitanya.md). [sectarian]

## Departure from Vraja & the Fall of Kaṃsa (Bhāgavata X.34–45)

- **The last Vraja demons** - A great python seizes the sleeping Nanda; at Krishna's touch it becomes the Vidyādhara **Sudarśana**, freed from a curse (Bhāgavata 10.34.5–18). **Śaṅkhacūḍa**, a follower of Kubera, drives the gopīs off toward the north, and Krishna kills him and takes his crest-jewel (10.34.25–32). Krishna then kills the bull-demon **Ariṣṭa** (X.36) and the horse-demon **Keśī**, choking him with his arm thrust into the mouth (10.37.1–8). **Vyoma**, son of Maya, disguised as a cowherd, hides the boys in a cave during a game; Krishna kills him too (10.37.28–33).
- **Akrūra's mission** - Told by Nārada that Krishna and Balarāma are Vasudeva's sons (X.36), Kaṃsa sends Akrūra to bring them to Mathurā for a "bow sacrifice" (Dhanur-yajña), intending a trap; Akrūra has a vision of Krishna as Viṣṇu in the Yamunā (Bhāgavata X.36, X.38–39).
- **The gopīs' grief / Uddhava's later consolation** - Krishna departs Vṛndāvana; the famous separation (viraha) of the gopīs, never fully resolved on earth (Bhāgavata X.39).
- **Entering Mathurā** - Krishna kills Kaṃsa's washerman, who refuses him clothes, and is garlanded by the florist **Sudāmā** (Bhāgavata X.41).
- **The hunchback Trivakrā (Kubjā)** - Krishna straightens the deformed maidservant, Kaṃsa's maker of unguents, who anoints him and Balarāma (*anulepana*) (Bhāgavata 10.42.1–12).
- **Breaking the bow & killing the elephant** - Krishna snaps Kaṃsa's great bow; slays the rogue elephant Kuvalayāpīḍa at the arena gate (Bhāgavata X.42–43).
- **Cāṇūra & Muṣṭika** - Krishna and Balarāma kill Kaṃsa's champion wrestlers (Bhāgavata X.44).
- **[Vadha of Kaṃsa](kamsa-vadha.md)** - Krishna leaps the dais, drags Kaṃsa by the hair, kills him; frees Devakī, Vasudeva, and the deposed king Ugrasena, reinstating him (Bhāgavata X.44–45; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).
- **Education under Sāndīpani** - Krishna and Balarāma learn the Vedas and the sixty-four arts in sixty-four days and nights at Avantī (Ujjain) (Bhāgavata 10.45.35–36). As guru-dakṣiṇā Sāndīpani asks for his son, drowned at Prabhāsa. The ocean says the boy was taken by the conch-shaped asura **Pañcajana**; Krishna kills him but does not find the boy in his belly, takes the conch from his body (the origin of his conch **Pāñcajanya**), and goes to **Saṃyamanī**, Yama's city, where Yama returns the boy (Bhāgavata 10.45.37–46).

## Mathurā, Jarāsandha & the Founding of Dwārakā (Bhāgavata X.46–52)

- **Uddhava's embassy to Vraja** - Krishna sends **Uddhava**, "the foremost counsellor of the Vṛṣṇis, Krishna's dear friend, a pupil of Bṛhaspati" (Bhāgavata 10.46.1), with a message to the grieving gopīs and parents; Uddhava is humbled by the gopīs' supreme love (Bhāgavata X.46–47, the "Bhramara-gīta", 10.47.12–21; see [Bhramara-gīta](bhramara-gita-uddhava-sandesha.md)).
- **Jarāsandha's invasions** - Kaṃsa's father-in-law Jarāsandha of Magadha (father of Kaṃsa's queens Asti and Prāpti) attacks Mathurā seventeen times and is repelled each time (*saptadaśa-kṛtvaḥ*, Bhāgavata 10.50.41; Harivaṃśa). Before the eighteenth attack, Kālayavana arrives (10.50.43).
- **Kālayavana & the founding of Dwārakā** - Facing the foreign (yavana) king Kālayavana, sent by Nārada, Krishna builds the fortress-city **Dwārakā** in the western sea for the Yādavas (Bhāgavata 10.50.49–57). He then runs from Kālayavana on foot and lures him into the cave of the sleeping king-sage Mucukunda, whose gaze incinerates him (Bhāgavata X.51). Fleeing Jarāsandha in turn, Krishna and Balarāma escape by leaping from the burning Pravarṣaṇa mountain (X.52). The Gujarati devotional epithet **Raṇchoḍ** ("the one who left the battlefield") commemorates this retreat. [regional]

## Marriages & the Eight Principal Queens (Aṣṭa-bhāryā) (Bhāgavata X.52–61)

- **[Rukmiṇī-haraṇa](rukmini-harana.md)** - Princess Rukmiṇī of Vidarbha, betrothed by her brother Rukmī to Śiśupāla, sends a brāhmaṇa to Krishna with a letter asking him to carry her off (Bhāgavata 10.52.37–43). On the wedding day, as she steps out of the shrine of the goddess **Ambikā**, Krishna lifts her onto his chariot in front of the assembled kings (10.53.50–55). He defeats Rukmī and is about to kill him, but at Rukmiṇī's plea spares his life and shaves him in disgrace; Balarāma then frees Rukmī and rebukes Krishna (Bhāgavata 10.54.31–37). She is regarded as an incarnation of Lakṣmī and the chief queen.
- **[The Syamantaka jewel](syamantaka-jewel.md) & Jāmbavatī / Satyabhāmā** - The sun-gem Syamantaka (given by Sūrya to Satrājit) is suspected stolen by Krishna; he tracks it to a cave and fights the bear-king **Jāmbavān**, who recognizes him as his master Rāma and gives both the jewel and his daughter **Jāmbavatī**; Satrājit, vindicated, gives Krishna his daughter **Satyabhāmā** (Bhāgavata X.56–57; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.13).
  - *Variant (length of the fight):* twenty-eight days (*aṣṭā-viṃśāham*, Bhāgavata 10.56.24); twenty-one days (*ekaviṃśati-dināni*, Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.13.46).
- **The other queens** - **Kālindī** (daughter of Sūrya, the Yamunā goddess), **Mitravindā** of Avantī, **Satyā/Nāgnajitī** of Kosala (won by taming seven bulls), **Bhadrā** of Kekaya, and **Lakṣmaṇā** of Madra complete the eight (Bhāgavata X.58). The names of the eight differ across texts:
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata):* Rukmiṇī, Satyabhāmā, Jāmbavatī, Kālindī, Mitravindā, Nāgnajitī (Satyā), Bhadrā, Lakṣmaṇā (X.58; the eight are recalled at 10.90.30).
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.28.3–5):* after Rukmiṇī it lists Kālindī, Mitravindā, Satyā Nāgnajitī, Jāmbavatī, **Rohiṇī**, the Madra princess **Suśīlā**, Satyabhāmā, and **Lakṣmaṇā** "of the lovely smile". There is no Bhadrā, and the text announces "seven" but names eight. [disputed]
- **[Naraka(asura)-vadha](narakasura-vadha.md) & the 16,100** - Krishna, riding Garuḍa with his wife Satyabhāmā, slays the demon **Narakāsura** (Bhauma, son of the Earth) of Prāgjyotiṣa (Assam). Naraka had seized Varuṇa's parasol, the earrings of Aditi and a site on the mountain of the gods (*amarādri-sthāna*, which commentators identify as Maṇi-parvata), and had imprisoned the daughters of many kings; Krishna frees the captives (Bhāgavata 10.59.2–3, 10.59.23, 10.59.33; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.29; Harivaṃśa).
  - **The 16,100 wives:** the freed women, unmarriageable in society, are accepted by Krishna as wives so they live with honor; he multiplies himself to marry and maintain each in her own palace, and the Bhāgavata later describes Nārada finding him present in every palace at once (Bhāgavata 10.59.42–43, X.69).
  - *Variant (the count):* the Bhāgavata itself gives two figures. The captive princesses number "sixteen thousand" (*ṣaṭ-sahasrādhikāyutam*, 10.59.33; *dvy-aṣṭa-sāhasram*, 10.69.2), but the closing summary gives "sixteen thousand and one hundred" queens, among whom are the eight chief ones (*ṣoḍaśa-sāhasraṃ … śatādhikam*, 10.90.29–30). The Viṣṇu Purāṇa likewise has 16,000 other wives at 5.28.5, and 16,100 freed maidens at 5.29.31 and 5.31.18. The popular total of **16,108** is 16,100 plus the eight.
- **The Pārijāta episode** - Prompted by Satyabhāmā, Krishna uproots the celestial Pārijāta tree from Indra's garden, loads it on Garuḍa, defeats Indra and the gods who oppose him, and plants it in Satyabhāmā's garden — the gods' lord bested by his own avatāra (Bhāgavata 10.59.38–40, briefly). The episode is told at length in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa (5.30–31) and the Harivaṃśa.
- **[Pradyumna](pradyumna-prabhavati-vajranabha.md)** - Krishna and Rukmiṇī's son (Kāmadeva reborn) is stolen by the demon Śambara, raised by Māyāvatī (Rati), and returns to slay him (Bhāgavata X.55).
- **Rukmī's death** - At the wedding of Krishna's grandson Aniruddha to Rukmī's granddaughter, Rukmī cheats Balarāma at dice and mocks him; Balarāma kills him with an iron bar in the assembly (Bhāgavata 10.61.26–38).
- **[Aniruddha & Uṣā / the war with Bāṇāsura](banasura-usha-aniruddha.md)** - Krishna's grandson Aniruddha weds Uṣā, daughter of the thousand-armed asura Bāṇa (devotee of Śiva); Krishna battles Bāṇa and even Śiva's forces (Bhāgavata X.62–63; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.32–33; Harivaṃśa). [major Vaiṣṇava–Śaiva interface episode]

## Friendships, Kingly Deeds & the War's Periphery (Bhāgavata X.64–90)

- **King Nṛga the chameleon** - The Yādava boys find a giant lizard (*kṛkalāsa*) in a dry well; Krishna lifts it out and it resumes the form of King **Nṛga**, cursed to that body for inadvertently giving one brāhmaṇa's strayed cow to another; released, he ascends — a royal object-lesson on the peril of misappropriating brāhmaṇa property (Bhāgavata X.64). The Mahābhārata tells the same story (Anuśāsana Parva, CE 13.69).
- **Pauṇḍraka, the false Vāsudeva, & the burning of Kāśī** - King **Pauṇḍraka** of Karūṣa (Bhāgavata 10.66.1), decked in imitation regalia (a counterfeit conch, disc, and Śrīvatsa), demands Krishna renounce the name "Vāsudeva"; Krishna beheads him and his ally the king of Kāśī. The Kāśī king's son **Sudakṣiṇa** raises, by worship of Śiva, a fire-demon (kṛtyā) against Dwārakā. Driven back by the Sudarśana cakra, the kṛtyā burns Sudakṣiṇa and his priests, and the cakra then **incinerates the city of Vārāṇasī** (Bhāgavata 10.66.27–42).
- **Dvivida the ape** - The vānara **Dvivida**, Naraka's friend, Sugrīva's minister and brother of Mainda (Bhāgavata 10.67.2), avenges Naraka by burning towns and ravaging Ānarta. He is battered to death by **Balarāma** on Raivataka hill (Bhāgavata X.67). In the Rāmāyaṇa, Mainda and Dvivida are among Rāma's monkey warriors.
- **Sāmba's capture & the dragging of Hastināpura** - Krishna's son **Sāmba** (by Jāmbavatī) abducts Duryodhana's daughter **Lakṣmaṇā** from her svayaṃvara and is captured by the Kuru champions; **Balarāma**, come as peacemaker and slighted by Kaurava arrogance, **hooks his plough into Hastināpura's ramparts and drags the city toward the Gaṅgā** until the terrified Kurus release the couple with dowry and honors (Bhāgavata X.68). [For Sāmba's later leprosy and the Sun-cult it founds, see the [Bhaviṣya Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/bhavishya-purana.md).]
- **Slaying of Jarāsandha** - Krishna, Bhīma and Arjuna, disguised as brāhmaṇa graduates (snātakas), challenge Jarāsandha to single combat; Bhīma kills him in a wrestling match, and the kings he had imprisoned are freed — clearing the way for Yudhiṣṭhira's Rājasūya (Bhāgavata X.72–73; Mahābhārata, [Sabhā Parva](mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md), CE 2.18–22, disguise at 2.19.21).
- **Death of Śiśupāla** - At the Rājasūya, Krishna is honored first (agra-pūjā); Śiśupāla insults him and is beheaded by the Sudarśana cakra, and a light rises from his body and enters Krishna (Bhāgavata 10.74.30–46; Sabhā Parva, CE 2.42). The promise to forgive **a hundred offences**, made to Śiśupāla's mother, is the Mahābhārata's (*aparādha-śatam*, CE 2.40.22); the Bhāgavata's account does not mention it. See [Śiśupāla](shishupala.md).
- **Śālva's Saubha & Dantavakra** - Śiśupāla's ally **Śālva**, wielding the aerial fortress-machine **Saubha** (built by Maya and granted through Śiva's boon), besieges Dwārakā to avenge him; Krishna shatters the Saubha and beheads Śālva with the cakra (Bhāgavata X.76–77). **Dantavakra** of Karūṣa then attacks on foot with a mace and Krishna kills him with his own mace; Dantavakra's brother **Vidūratha** is beheaded by the cakra (Bhāgavata 10.78.1–13). Śiśupāla and Dantavakra are the third and last [Jaya–Vijaya](jaya-vijaya.md) birth-pair (Bhāgavata 7.1.45–46; *śiśupāla-karūṣajau*, 7.10.38; see [Śiśupāla](shishupala.md)).
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata, Āraṇyaka Parva CE 3.15–23):* Krishna tells Yudhiṣṭhira the story of Śālva and the Saubha to explain why he was absent from the dice game: he was away fighting Śālva, who had attacked Dvārakā after Śiśupāla's death.
- **Balarāma's tīrtha-yātrā — Romaharṣaṇa & Balvala** - Refusing to take sides at Kurukṣetra, **Balarāma** tours the sacred fords; at Naimiṣāraṇya he kills the sūta **Romaharṣaṇa** (Vyāsa's disciple, who failed to rise for him) with a blade of kuśa grass, expiates the deed by granting that Romaharṣaṇa's son (**Ugraśravas**, the Sūta of the Purāṇa frames) shall be the reciter, slays the sage-harassing demon **Balvala**, and completes the pilgrimage in time to witness the Bhīma–Duryodhana mace-duel (Bhāgavata X.78–79). The Mahābhārata gives a longer, different itinerary of the same pilgrimage along the Sarasvatī, without the Romaharṣaṇa episode (Śalya Parva, CE 9.34–53).
- **[Sudāmā / Kucela](sudama-kuchela.md)** - Krishna's impoverished boyhood Brahmin friend, a fellow pupil at Sāndīpani's school, brings a humble gift of flattened rice (pṛthuka); Krishna honors him lavishly and silently grants him wealth — paradigm of devotion and divine reciprocity (Bhāgavata X.80–81).
  - *On the name:* the Bhāgavata never names him. He is "a certain friend of Krishna, a brāhmaṇa" (*kṛṣṇasyāsīt sakhā kaścid brāhmaṇaḥ*, 10.80.6) and "ragged" (*ku-cailasya*, 10.80.7). The South Indian name **Kucela** comes from that epithet. The name **Sudāmā** is a later tradition; in the Bhāgavata, Sudāmā is the garland-maker of Mathurā (10.41.43).
- **Reunion at Kurukṣetra (solar eclipse)** - On an eclipse pilgrimage to Samanta-pañcaka the Vraja folk, gopīs, Nanda, Yaśodā, and the Pāṇḍavas all reunite with Krishna (Bhāgavata 10.82.1–2 and X.82–84).
- **Devakī's six sons** - Devakī asks Krishna and Balarāma to bring back the six sons Kaṃsa killed. They go to Sutala, are welcomed by Bali, and bring the six back to her; freed from their curse, the six return to the gods' world (Bhāgavata 10.85.27–56).
- **Subhadrā's marriage** - Arjuna, on pilgrimage, hears that Balarāma means to give his sister Subhadrā to Duryodhana. He comes to Dvārakā disguised as a tridaṇḍī ascetic and carries her off with Krishna's approval; Balarāma's anger is calmed (Bhāgavata 10.86.1–12).
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva CE 1.211–213):* Krishna himself advises Arjuna to carry Subhadrā off by force during the festival on Mount Raivataka, since a woman's choice at a svayaṃvara is uncertain (CE 1.211.21–23). The ascetic disguise is not in the critical text.
- **Śrutadeva & Bahulāśva** - Visiting Mithilā (Videha) with a retinue of sages, Krishna **bilocates** — simultaneously guesting with the poor brāhmaṇa **Śrutadeva** and King **Bahulāśva** — honoring the equal devotion of pauper and monarch (Bhāgavata X.86).
- **The Śruti-gīta / Veda-stuti** - Answering Parīkṣit's question of **how the Vedas, which operate among the guṇas, can indicate the attributeless (nirguṇa) Brahman**, Śuka relays **Nārāyaṇa Ṛṣi's** teaching to Nārada at Badarikāśrama: the **personified śrutis' own hymn** to the Lord at the dawn of creation (Bhāgavata X.87; catalogued among the [lesser Gītās](lesser-gitas-catalog.md)).
- **Vṛkāsura** - The asura Vṛka, son of Śakuni, gains from Śiva the boon that anyone whose head he touches will die, and tries it on Śiva himself. Viṣṇu, appearing as a young celibate student, persuades him to test the boon on his own head, and he dies (Bhāgavata 10.88.14–36).
- **Bhṛgu tests the three gods** - The sages at a sacrifice on the Sarasvatī send Bhṛgu to find out which of the three gods is greatest. He kicks Viṣṇu in the chest, and Viṣṇu's gentle response wins the sages' verdict (Bhāgavata 10.89.1–19).
- **Recovering the dead sons of a Brahmin** - Krishna and Arjuna travel beyond the world to retrieve a Brahmin's lost children from the eight-armed Lord resting on the thousand-headed serpent Ananta in the waters beyond the darkness, revealing Krishna's supreme identity to Arjuna (Bhāgavata 10.89.21–64).
- **The Kurukṣetra war role** - As Arjuna's charioteer and counselor he speaks the [Bhagavad Gītā](bhagavad-gita.md) and steers the Pāṇḍava victory, having pledged not to bear arms. Twice in the Mahābhārata he jumps down from the chariot and rushes at Bhīṣma, once with the discus raised (CE 6.55.86–93) and once with only his whip (CE 6.102.53–54), and Arjuna holds him back (covered fully in the [Mahābhārata](./mahabharata/) deep-dive).

## Final Teachings & Departure (Bhāgavata XI; Mausala Parva)

- **Curse of the sages / Gāndhārī's curse** - Yādava boys dress Krishna's son **Sāmba** as a pregnant woman and ask visiting sages what "she" will bear. The angry sages answer: an iron club (*musala*) that will destroy the clan (Bhāgavata 11.1.13–17; Mausala Parva, CE 16.2.4–10).
  - *Which sages:* the Bhāgavata names eleven "and others" (*nāradādayaḥ*), met at the pilgrimage place Piṇḍāraka: Viśvāmitra, Asita, Kaṇva, Durvāsā, Bhṛgu, Aṅgiras, Kaśyapa, Vāmadeva, Atri, Vasiṣṭha and Nārada (11.1.11–12). The Mahābhārata names three who come to Dvārakā: Viśvāmitra, Kaṇva and Nārada (CE 16.2.4).
  - *The club:* King Ugrasena (Āhuka) has it ground to powder and thrown into the sea. The powder washes ashore and grows into **erakā** reeds; one leftover lump of iron is swallowed by a fish, and a hunter makes it into an arrowhead (Bhāgavata 11.1.21–23).
  - *Gāndhārī's curse* comes earlier, in the Strī Parva: grieving over her sons, she curses Krishna that in the **thirty-sixth year** he will kill his own kin and die alone in the forest by an ignoble means, and that the Vṛṣṇi women will weep as the Bharata women weep now (CE 11.25.38–42). Krishna recalls it before his death (Mausala Parva, CE 16.5.17). The Bhāgavata does not mention Gāndhārī's curse.
- **[The Uddhava-Gītā](uddhava-gita.md)** - Krishna's last great discourse, to his devotee **Uddhava**, on jñāna, bhakti, vairāgya, the avadhūta's 24 gurus, and yoga — the "other Gītā" (Bhāgavata XI.7–29). Uddhava is sent to Badarikāśrama before the end (Bhāgavata XI.29).
- **Yādava fratricide (Mausala)** - At Prabhāsa, drunken Yādavas quarrel and fight. When their weapons are spent they pluck the erakā reeds, which turn into clubs as hard as thunderbolts in their hands (11.30.20–21), and the clan wipes itself out (Bhāgavata 11.30.14–24).
  - *Mahābhārata details (Mausala Parva, CE 16.4.15–44):* Sātyaki taunts Kṛtavarmā over the night massacre and beheads him. Sātyaki and Pradyumna are then killed by the Bhojas and Andhakas in front of Krishna. Krishna himself takes up a fistful of erakā grass, which becomes an iron club, and kills the attackers in front of him. When Sāmba, Cārudeṣṇa, Pradyumna, Aniruddha and Gada lie dead, he kills the rest.
  - *Who survives:* Balarāma is not among the slain. In the Bhāgavata the deluded Yādavas also turn on Krishna and Balarāma, who strike back with reeds (11.30.21–23); in the Mahābhārata Balarāma is at the drinking party (CE 16.4.15) but is no longer with Krishna when the slaughter ends, and Krishna afterwards finds him sitting alone against a tree in the forest (CE 16.4.46; 16.5.1).
- **Balarāma's departure** - Balarāma (Saṅkarṣaṇa / aṃśa of Śeṣa) sits in yoga by the sea and leaves the human world (Bhāgavata 11.30.26). In the Mahābhārata, Krishna finds him seated in yoga beneath a tree and sees a great white thousand-headed serpent issue from his mouth and enter the ocean, where Varuṇa and the nāgas receive it (Mausala Parva, CE 16.5.11–15).
- **Death of Krishna** - Sitting under a pippala tree in his four-armed form, with one lotus-red foot resting on his right thigh (Bhāgavata 11.30.27–32), Krishna is shot in the foot by the hunter **Jarā**, who mistakes the foot for a deer's face. Jarā's arrow is tipped with the leftover iron of the cursed club. Krishna forgives him and departs for his own abode (Bhāgavata 11.30.27–40, arrow at 11.30.33; XI.31). In the Mahābhārata Krishna lies in yoga on the ground and Jarā pierces the **sole** of his foot (*pādatale*, Mausala Parva, CE 16.5.19–21).
  - *Why the sole (Mahābhārata):* the sage Durvāsā once had Krishna smear his body with leftover rice-pudding (pāyasa) and granted that wherever it touched he would have no fear of death. Krishna had not smeared the soles of his feet (Anuśāsana Parva, CE 13.144.38–39). Krishna remembers Durvāsā's words before his death (CE 16.5.17).
  - *Variant:* Jarā is identified as Vālī reborn — settling the karmic debt of [Rāma](./ramayana/)'s killing of Vālī in a prior avatāra. Neither the Bhāgavata (11.30) nor the Mahābhārata (16.5) says this, and no Sanskrit source for it has been located. [folk/oral tradition]
- **Submersion of Dwārakā** - After Krishna's departure, Arjuna arrives, performs the last rites for the dead, and evacuates the surviving women and children. As soon as the people have left, the sea floods Dwārakā (Mausala Parva, CE 16.8.40), and on the road the convoy is plundered by **Ābhīra** herdsmen-bandits of the Pañcanada region, against whom Arjuna's strength fails (CE 16.8.43–64; 16.9.16). See the [Mausala Parva](mahabharata/16-mausala-parva.md) deep-dive.
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata 11.31.23–24):* the sea floods the city "in a moment", except for Krishna's own palace, where he is said to remain ever present.
- **Onset of Kali Yuga** - "On the very day Krishna left for heaven, Kali-yuga set in," say the knowers of the past (Bhāgavata 12.2.33; also 12.2.29–30). The traditional date of **3102 BCE** for this epoch comes from Indian astronomical reckoning (it is implied by Āryabhaṭa's statement, c. 499 CE, of how many years of Kali had elapsed). See [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md). [scholarly: the date is an astronomical epoch, not a historical record] The Pāṇḍavas soon undertake their final journey (mahāprasthāna).

## Theological Note: Svayam Bhagavān

- The **Bhāgavata** uniquely calls Krishna not merely an avatāra but *svayam bhagavān* — "God himself" — in 1.3.28 (`kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam`), the source of all avatāras rather than one among them. This comes right after a list of the avatāras in which Balarāma and Krishna are the nineteenth and twentieth, "born among the Vṛṣṇis" (1.3.23). Gauḍīya, Nimbārka and Vallabha (Puṣṭimārga) traditions build on this. The [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) lists usually count him among Viṣṇu's ten (with Balarāma or Buddha substituting in regional lists). Jayadeva's Gīta-Govinda instead names Balarāma and the Buddha among the ten and hails Krishna as the one who takes all ten forms. [scholarly/sectarian]
