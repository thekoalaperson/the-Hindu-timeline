# The Uddhava Gītā (Haṃsa Gītā) — Kṛṣṇa's Last Teaching

> **Where this sits:** Kṛṣṇa's final philosophical discourse, delivered to his devotee-cousin **Uddhava** in the closing days of the [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga (present 7th, [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)), on the eve of the [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) (3102 BCE) — just before Kṛṣṇa winds up his avatāra. It occupies **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.6 (v.40) – 11.29**.
>
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Master index: [../README.md](../README.md) · Companions: [Krishna-līlā](./krishna-lila.md) · [Dattātreya & the 24 Gurus](./dattatreya.md) · [The Bhagavad Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md) · [The Anugītā](./anugita.md)

The **Uddhava Gītā** ("the Song [taught] to Uddhava") — also called the **Haṃsa Gītā** ("Song of the Swan") and popularly "**the Last Message of Śrī Kṛṣṇa**" or "**the second Gītā of Kṛṣṇa**" — is the climactic teaching of the [Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md). Spoken by [Kṛṣṇa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) to **Uddhava** as the Yādava dynasty is being destroyed and the Lord prepares to leave the world, it spans roughly **23 chapters (11.7–11.29) plus its frame (from 11.6.40)** and ~**1,000–1,100 verses**. Where the [Bhagavad Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md) is a battlefield call to *action through devotion*, the Uddhava Gītā is a parting, deathbed-of-an-age compendium synthesizing **bhakti, jñāna, vairāgya, yoga, varṇāśrama-dharma, Sāṃkhya, and the siddhis** — a textbook of **bhāgavata-dharma** addressed not to a warrior but to a renunciate-to-be.

---

## Narrative frame & occasion

- **The setting — the end of the avatāra** - The Yādavas, cursed by the brāhmaṇas at Pindāraka and maddened by drink, slaughter one another at Prabhāsa; Kṛṣṇa knows his līlā is ending. Before withdrawing, he gives one last full teaching to the devotee he loves and trusts most. ([Bhāgavata 11.1; 11.6](./krishna-lila.md); [Mausala Parva of the Mahābhārata](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md))
- **Brahmā's prompt** - In **11.6**, Brahmā with the gods comes to Dvārakā and asks Kṛṣṇa to return to his abode; Kṛṣṇa consents and turns to settle his devotees. The chapter is titled "Retirement on the Advice of Brahmā, and Uddhava Addressed in Private." (Bhāgavata 11.6)
- **Uddhava's plea not to be left behind** - **Uddhava** (son of Devabhāga, Kṛṣṇa's cousin, foremost of the Vṛṣṇis and his lifelong friend/minister) begs that he cannot bear separation and asks to accompany the Lord or be taught how to live without him. Kṛṣṇa instead commissions him to **renounce the world, internalize the teaching, and go to Badarikāśrama** to practice. (Bhāgavata 11.6.42–49; 11.29.41)
- **Who reports it** - Like all of the Bhāgavata, the discourse reaches us as **Śuka (Śukadeva)** narrating to King **Parīkṣit** on the bank of the Ganges in his last seven days. Thus it is a teaching-within-a-teaching: Kṛṣṇa→Uddhava, retold by Śuka→Parīkṣit. (Bhāgavata frame)
- **Theological weight** - Because it is Kṛṣṇa's *final and most systematic* instruction — and because he speaks here in his own voice as **Svayam Bhagavān** (not a borrowed itihāsa, unlike the [Anugītā](./anugita.md)) — Vaiṣṇava tradition ranks it second only to (or even alongside) the [Bhagavad Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md). (Gauḍīya & Śrī Vaiṣṇava commentary)

## The "two Gītās" — distinguishing Kṛṣṇa's discourses

Kṛṣṇa delivers several major sermons across scripture; cataloguing them prevents the common confusion:

- **Bhagavad Gītā** - To **Arjuna**, *before* the Kurukṣetra war, in the [Mahābhārata](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md) (Bhīṣma Parva); 18 chapters, 700 verses; karma-bhakti for the man of action. (See [bhagavad-gita.md](./bhagavad-gita.md))
- **Anugītā** - To **Arjuna**, *after* the war (Āśvamedhika Parva); Kṛṣṇa says he cannot repeat the Gītā verbatim and conveys the truths through old stories; heavier jñāna/Sāṃkhya. (See [anugita.md](./anugita.md))
- **Uddhava Gītā** - To **Uddhava**, *at the very end* of Kṛṣṇa's life, in the Bhāgavata Purāṇa; the grand synthesis and the literal "last word." **This file.**
  - *Variant (the "three Gītās of Kṛṣṇa")* - Tradition sometimes counts Bhagavad Gītā, Anugītā, and Uddhava Gītā as Kṛṣṇa's three Gītās; some lists swap the Anugītā for other minor discourses. [scholarly]
- **Why "Haṃsa Gītā"** - Two reasons are given: (1) Kṛṣṇa tells Uddhava to live like the **haṃsa (swan)** — dwelling in the water of the world yet never wetted by it, and able (per the folk-belief) to **separate milk from water**, i.e. essence from dross (viveka); and (2) the discourse contains the **Haṃsa-avatāra story** (11.13), in which Viṣṇu as a swan teaches Brahmā's sons. (Bhāgavata 11.13; traditional naming)

## Chapter-by-chapter map (Bhāgavata 11.6.40–11.29)

> Titles follow standard English editions (Sanskrit-commentary order; e.g. Anand Aadhar / bhagavata.org). Verse counts approximate; some editions begin the Gītā proper at **11.7**.

- **11.6 (from v.40) — Frame** - Brahmā requests Kṛṣṇa's return; Kṛṣṇa resolves to depart; Uddhava's appeal in private. (Bhāgavata 11.6)
- **11.7 — The Avadhūta & the pigeon of attachment** - Kṛṣṇa commands renunciation; to encourage Uddhava he begins the tale of the **avadhūta-brāhmaṇa** and King **Yadu**, and recounts the first gurus (earth, air, sky, water, fire, moon, sun, **pigeon**). (Bhāgavata 11.7)
- **11.8 — Lessons from nature; the story of Piṅgalā** - More gurus (python, ocean, moth, bee, beekeeper, elephant, deer, fish) and the **Piṅgalā** parable on the bliss of abandoning desire. (Bhāgavata 11.8)
- **11.9 — The remaining gurus & detachment** - Osprey, child, maiden's bangle, arrow-maker, snake, spider, wasp-grub, and the **body** as final teacher; the avadhūta departs. (Bhāgavata 11.9)
- **11.10 — The bound and the free soul** - The illusoriness of bodily identity; ritual action (karma-kāṇḍa) cannot grant liberation; the soul's apparent bondage. (Bhāgavata 11.10)
- **11.11 — Bondage & liberation explained; the sādhu** - Marks of the bound vs. liberated soul; the qualities and supreme value of the **saintly devotee (sādhu / bhakta)**. (Bhāgavata 11.11)
- **11.12 — The confidential secret: beyond renunciation & knowledge** - **Satsaṅga (association with devotees)** is exalted as the easiest, most powerful means — superior even to yoga, jñāna, and tapas; examples of beings liberated by devotion alone (gopīs, Vraja-folk). (Bhāgavata 11.12)
- **11.13 — The Haṃsa-avatāra answers Brahmā's sons** - The eponymous **Haṃsa Gītā** core: Viṣṇu as a **swan (haṃsa)** instructs the **Four Kumāras (Sanaka, Sananda, Sanātana, Sanat-kumāra)** on the three guṇas, mind, and the discrimination of Self from matter — ancient wisdom Kṛṣṇa now re-transmits. (Bhāgavata 11.13)
- **11.14 — Devotional coherence of methods; meditation on Viṣṇu** - Kṛṣṇa declares **bhakti-yoga supreme** among all paths; teaches **dhyāna** on his form and the qualities of the dear devotee. (Bhāgavata 11.14)
- **11.15 — Mystical perfections (the siddhis)** - Enumerates the **eighteen siddhis** (eight primary — aṇimā, mahimā, laghimā, prāpti, prākāmya, īśitva, vaśitva, kāmāvasāyitā — and ten secondary) and the meditations that yield them; warns they are obstacles to the true goal. (Bhāgavata 11.15)
- **11.16 — The Lord's opulences (vibhūti)** - In answer to Uddhava, Kṛṣṇa names where his glory is most manifest across the cosmos — a Bhāgavata parallel to **Bhagavad Gītā ch. 10**. (Bhāgavata 11.16)
- **11.17 — Varṇāśrama: students & householders** - The **varṇāśrama-dharma** system; the **boat of bhakti**; duties of the brahmacārī and gṛhastha. (Bhāgavata 11.17)
- **11.18 — Varṇāśrama: the withdrawn & renounced** - Duties of the **vānaprastha** and **sannyāsī**; the conduct of the perfected renunciate / paramahaṃsa. (Bhāgavata 11.18)
- **11.19 — The perfection of spiritual knowledge** - Jñāna, vijñāna, the items of knowledge; the virtues (yama-niyama), and what the realized soul attains. (Bhāgavata 11.19)
- **11.20 — Tri-kāṇḍa yoga: bhakti surpasses jñāna & karma** - The **three paths** — karma-yoga (for the attached), jñāna-yoga (for the disgusted with the world), bhakti-yoga (for the faithful in between) — with **bhakti declared the crown**. (Bhāgavata 11.20)
- **11.21 — Distinguishing good and bad** - The relativity of purity/impurity, right/wrong; scriptural injunctions are graded, contextual, and ultimately point beyond themselves. (Bhāgavata 11.21)
- **11.22 — Prakṛti & Puruṣa; enumeration of tattvas** - Sāṃkhya cosmology: the number of **tattvas** (variant counts), nature vs. enjoyer, the cycle of birth and death. (Bhāgavata 11.22)
- **11.23 — Forbearance: the Song of the Avantī Brāhmaṇa** - The parable of a miserly **brāhmaṇa of Avantī (Ujjain)** robbed of all, who learns that **the mind alone — not other people, planets, fate, or karma — is the cause of happiness and suffering**; his vow of **titikṣā (forbearance)**. (Bhāgavata 11.23)
- **11.24 — Sāṃkhya summarized** - A compact restatement of the analytic enumeration (creation, dissolution, the 24/25/26 principles). (Bhāgavata 11.24)
- **11.25 — The three modes (guṇas) and beyond** - Exhaustive analysis of **sattva, rajas, tamas** — their effects on faith, residence, diet, action, knowledge, happiness — and transcending the guṇas. (Bhāgavata 11.25)
- **11.26 — The Song of Purūravā (Aila-gītā)** - King **Purūravā**, ruined by lust for the apsaras **Urvaśī**, sings his disillusionment and renounces — a lesson in vairāgya. (Cross-link: [Purūravas & Urvaśī](./pururavas-urvashi.md)) (Bhāgavata 11.26)
- **11.27 — Kriyā-yoga: worship of the Deity (arcana)** - Detailed **deity-worship / temple ritual (arcana, pāñcarātra)** as a practical limb of bhakti for the embodied devotee. (Bhāgavata 11.27)
- **11.28 — Jñāna-yoga: the real and the apparent** - The highest non-dual instruction: transcend praise/blame, see the one Self, neither lament nor exult; the means of remaining undisturbed. (Bhāgavata 11.28)
- **11.29 — Bhakti-yoga: conquering death; Uddhava departs** - The summit: **bhakti-yoga as the most auspicious, fearless path to immortality**; Kṛṣṇa's final, intimate instruction; Uddhava, overwhelmed, takes leave and proceeds to **Badarikāśrama** to practice. (Bhāgavata 11.29)

## The 24 Gurus of the Avadhūta (11.7–11.9)

The narrative jewel of the Uddhava Gītā: to teach renunciation, Kṛṣṇa retells how a wandering **avadhūta-brāhmaṇa**, questioned by King **Yadu** about the source of his serene bliss, named **24 gurus** drawn from the natural world. Vaiṣṇava tradition identifies the avadhūta with **[Dattātreya](./dattatreya.md)**, though the Bhāgavata does not name him here. [folk/traditional attribution]

- **Full per-guru lesson list** is catalogued in detail in [Dattātreya — the 24 Gurus](./dattatreya.md#the-teaching-of-the-24-gurus-bhāgavata-117-9). In brief: **earth** (forbearance), **air/wind** (untainted engagement), **sky/ether** (the all-pervading unstained Self), **water** (purity), **fire** (austerity, stores nothing), **moon** (phases belong to body not Self), **sun** (take only to give back; one reflected as many), **pigeon** (ruin of family attachment), **python** (contentment), **ocean** (unmoved depth), **moth** (lust via sight), **bee** (take the essence from all scriptures), **beekeeper/honey-thief** (hoarders lose all), **elephant** (lust via touch), **deer** (lust via sound), **fish** (lust via taste — the hardest sense), **Piṅgalā the courtesan** (hopelessness = peace), **osprey/kuraṛa** (drop possessions, be safe), **child** (carefree, beyond honor), **maiden's single bangle** (dwell alone to avoid quarrel), **arrow-maker** (one-pointed concentration), **snake** (wander solitary, homeless, secret), **spider** (emanates and reabsorbs the web = the Lord and the cosmos), **wasp-grub** (become what you meditate on), and the **body** (field of birth/death, yet vehicle of liberation).
- **The body as 24th/capping teacher** - The avadhūta adds the **human body** itself: hard to obtain, perishable, yet — used for discrimination and devotion — the very boat across saṃsāra. (Bhāgavata 11.9.25–29)
- *Variant (count/ordering)* - Different recensions and condensations (e.g. the **Nārāyaṇīyam**) reorder or substitute gurus (hawk for osprey; bee/beekeeper split differently), but the canonical total is **24**. [scholarly]
- *Variant (attribution)* - Some streams leave the avadhūta unnamed; the **Dattātreya** identification is the dominant Vaiṣṇava reading and is also why the same teaching is treated under Datta hagiography. [folk]

## Core doctrines & distinctive teachings

- **Bhāgavata-dharma & the supremacy of bhakti** - The whole discourse argues that **loving devotion to the personal Lord** is the easiest, safest, and highest path — surpassing jñāna and vairāgya, which arise in the devotee automatically. (Bhāgavata 11.14, 11.20, 11.29)
- **Tri-kāṇḍa: three paths for three temperaments** - **Karma-yoga** for those still attached to results; **jñāna-yoga** for those repelled by the world; **bhakti-yoga** for those with faith and a taste for hearing of the Lord — the path Kṛṣṇa most recommends. (Bhāgavata 11.20)
- **Satsaṅga as the master-key** - **Association with the holy** is repeatedly named the supreme accelerant of liberation, more potent than austerity, study, or solitary yoga. (Bhāgavata 11.12)
- **The mind is the sole cause of bondage & freedom** - Dramatized by the **Avantī brāhmaṇa** (11.23): not enemies, planets, time, or karma but one's *own mind* generates joy and grief; mastery of mind through forbearance is liberation. (cf. the *adhyātma* turn of the [Anugītā](./anugita.md))
- **Be the swan (haṃsa)** - Live amid the world's "water" without being wetted; discriminate essence from dross — the paramahaṃsa ideal that names the text. (Bhāgavata 11.13–14)
- **Transcending varṇāśrama** - Though Kṛṣṇa lays out **varṇāśrama-dharma** fully (11.17–18), its telos is the **paramahaṃsa-sannyāsī / avadhūta** who has "shaken off" all social-ritual obligation — the same ideal embodied by [Dattātreya](./dattatreya.md). (Bhāgavata 11.18)
- **The siddhis as snares** - The eighteen mystic perfections (11.15) are real but are obstacles; the true yogin bypasses them for pure devotion. (Bhāgavata 11.15)
- **Kriyā-yoga (arcana)** - Practical Pāñcarātra-style **deity worship** is given as a concrete devotional discipline for the embodied (11.27), balancing the text's high jñāna with accessible ritual.
- **Sāṃkhya & the guṇas** - A thorough Sāṃkhya analysis (11.22, 11.24) and an exhaustive map of **sattva-rajas-tamas** across all of life (11.25) ground the metaphysics — making the Uddhava Gītā a near-complete philosophical manual.

## Embedded songs & stories (gītās within the Gītā)

- **The Avadhūta's account of 24 gurus** (11.7–9) — see above; the most famous block.
- **Piṅgalā's awakening** (11.8) — the courtesan who finds peace by abandoning hope/desire.
- **The Haṃsa-gītā / Haṃsa-avatāra** (11.13) — Viṣṇu as swan teaches the **Four Kumāras**; the namesake passage.
- **The Avantī (Ujjain) brāhmaṇa's song of forbearance / Bhikṣu-gītā** (11.23) — the mind as sole cause of suffering.
- **The Aila-gītā (Song of Purūravā)** (11.26) — the king's renunciation after Urvaśī; cross-link [Purūravas & Urvaśī](./pururavas-urvashi.md).

## Philosophical orientation & reception

- **Read as broadly non-dual** - Scholars note that the Gītās embedded in the Mahā-purāṇas (including the Uddhava Gītā) lean toward **monism (Advaita-leaning)** in their metaphysics, while remaining devotional in practice — a Bhāgavata synthesis of jñāna and bhakti. [scholarly]
- **Vaiṣṇava commentarial status** - Treasured across Gauḍīya, Śrī, and other Vaiṣṇava lines as Kṛṣṇa's testament; commented within the larger Bhāgavata commentaries (Śrīdhara Svāmī, Sanātana Gosvāmī, Viśvanātha Cakravartī, Vijayadhvaja, etc.). (commentarial tradition)
- **Independent circulation** - Frequently extracted and published on its own as *The Uddhava Gītā* / *The Last Message of Śrī Kṛṣṇa* — e.g. Swami Madhavananda (Ramakrishna Math), Ambikananda Saraswati (2000), and Pandit Rajmani Tigunait (2002). (publishing history)
- **Dating** - With the rest of the [Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md), the text is academically dated to roughly the **9th–10th c. CE** (South Indian provenance), far later than its internal Dvāpara/Kali-cusp setting — the two-clock distinction (scripture-internal vs. academic). [scholarly]
  - *Note (scripture clock):* By the tradition's reckoning the discourse is spoken at the very end of Dvāpara, immediately before **Kali Yuga begins in 3102 BCE**, when Kṛṣṇa departs. [traditional]
