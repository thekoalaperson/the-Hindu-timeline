# Haṃsa-Gītā — The Swan-Brahmā's Discourse to the Sādhyas

> *Where this sits: a standalone treatment of the **Haṃsa-gītā** ("Song of the Swan"), a short teacher-Gītā of the **Mokṣadharma** of the [Śānti Parva](./mahabharata/12-shanti-parva.md) (Book 12) of the [Mahābhārata](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md), in which **Brahmā in the form of a swan (haṃsa)** answers the **Sādhya** gods on the highest good. Spoken by **Vyāsa to Yudhiṣṭhira** in the closing [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga, on the eve of [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md).*
>

The **Haṃsa-gītā** (हंसगीता, "Song of the Swan") is a compact didactic inset of roughly **41 verses** within the **Mokṣadharma-parvan** of the [Śānti Parva](./mahabharata/12-shanti-parva.md) (Mbh ~12.288–299 in the Ganguli/Calcutta vulgate; "ch. 299" in some counts). Unlike most Mokṣadharma teachings — which the dying **Bhīṣma** recites to Yudhiṣṭhira from his bed of arrows — this Gītā is reported in the frame by **Vyāsa to Yudhiṣṭhira**, who relays an ancient conversation in which **Brahmā, having assumed the form of a haṃsa (swan/goose)**, answers a class of demigods, the **Sādhyas**, on *what is the highest good (śreyas)* and *the marks of the truly wise*. Its core is an ethics of **forbearance (kṣamā / titikṣā), truthfulness, restraint of speech, and the patient, silent absorption of abuse** — repaying injury not with retaliation but with composure.

---

## Narrative frame & occasion

- **The reporting frame — Vyāsa to Yudhiṣṭhira** — The Gītā is given as a *purātana itihāsa* ("ancient story"): Vyāsa recalls that in a former time the **Sādhyas** (a class of demigods born of the Dharma–Sādhyā lineage) sought instruction in the highest good and were answered by **Brahmā in swan-form**. The nesting — sage recalls a story in which a god instructs gods — is the structural signature of the [Mokṣadharma](./mokshadharma-dialogues.md). (MBh. Śānti Parva, Mokṣadharma ~12.288–299)
  - *Variant (speaker/frame):* most rosters and summaries name **Vyāsa** as the relater to Yudhiṣṭhira; because the whole Mokṣadharma is nominally Bhīṣma's discourse, some retellings fold it under **Bhīṣma's** narration. The Sādhya-questioners and swan-Brahmā teacher are constant across recensions. [disputed] [scholarly]
- **The questioners — the Sādhyas** — The **Sādhyas** (साध्याः, "those to be propitiated / the perfectible ones"), a group of celestial beings often listed among the gaṇa-devatās, approach the swan and ask after the **supreme good, the highest dharma, and the conduct of the wise**. (MBh. Śānti Parva ~12.299)
- **The teacher — Brahmā as haṃsa** — The respondent is the **creator Brahmā** himself, who has taken the form of a **swan (haṃsa)** to deliver the teaching; the bird is here the speaker of wisdom, the *paramahaṃsa* ("supreme swan") ideal made literal. (MBh. Śānti Parva ~12.299)
  - *Note (which deity is the swan):* In **this Mahābhārata Gītā the swan is Brahmā**. This must not be confused with the Bhāgavata's **[Haṃsa-avatāra](./hamsa-avatar.md)**, where the swan is **Viṣṇu** teaching the Four Kumāras — a separate text with a separate teacher. [disputed] [common error]

## The teaching — content of the swan's reply

- **The highest good is self-conquest** — The swan tells the Sādhyas that the *śreyas* they seek is not in ritual or acquisition but in **mastery of the self** — the wise man's victory is over his own anger, senses, and speech. (MBh. Śānti Parva ~12.299)
- **Repay abuse with patient silence** — The central, most-quoted teaching: **when reviled, one should not revile in return**; the man who, though struck or insulted, returns kindness — or at least equanimity — and **endures the harsh word without answering it**, conquers his abuser and himself. Anger answered by anger consumes both; anger absorbed in forbearance burns out. (MBh. Śānti Parva ~12.299)
  - *The maxim:* the wise man "**bears patiently the words of the abuser** and does not let the fire of another's wrath kindle his own" — forbearance (*kṣamā*) as the higher strength. (MBh. Śānti Parva ~12.299)
- **Control of speech (vāk-saṃyama)** — Guarding the tongue is singled out: a hard, wounding word, once loosed, "scorches" the one struck; the disciplined sage **speaks measured, true, and gentle words**, or keeps silence. Restraint of speech is named among the foremost austerities. (MBh. Śānti Parva ~12.299)
- **Truthfulness (satya)** — Truth is exalted as a pillar of the highest dharma, paired with non-injury and self-restraint as the conduct that leads to the supreme good. (MBh. Śānti Parva ~12.299)
- **The man who does not grieve, hate, or exult** — The ideal is the sage who is **unmoved by praise and blame alike**, neither elated by honour nor cast down by insult, and who therefore "sleeps and wakes at ease" — equanimity (*samatā*) as the fruit of forbearance. (MBh. Śānti Parva ~12.299)
- **Forbearance is not weakness** — The swan answers the implied objection that endurance is cowardice: the truly forbearing man is the *stronger*, for he has conquered the harder enemy — his own mind — while the wrathful man is mastered by his passion. (MBh. Śānti Parva ~12.299) [cf. the *titikṣā* teaching of the [Bhikṣu-gītā](./uddhava-gita.md#the-bhikṣu-gītā--the-avantī-brāhmaṇas-song-of-the-mendicant-1123)]

## Place among the teacher-Gītās

- **One of the "core eighteen"** — The Haṃsa-gītā is a fixture of the devotional **"eighteen Gītās" (gītā-saṅgraha)** anthologized alongside the [Bhagavad Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md) — listed there with the Anu-, Brāhmaṇa-, Bodhya-, Vyādha-, Hārīta-, Kāma-, Maṅki-, Parāśara-, Ṛṣabha-, Śampāka-, Śaṅkha-, Vāmadeva-, Vicakhnu-, Vṛtra-, and Yājñavalkya-Gītās. See the full roster and the 18/21/60 enumerations in the [Lesser Gītās Catalog](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md). [folk] [scholarly]
- **A "subject/occasion" Gītā, not a teacher-named one** — Where most lesser Gītās are named for their human teacher (Vyādha-, Parāśara-, Vāmadeva-), the Haṃsa-gītā is named for the **form of its speaker (the swan)** — structurally a peer of the Kāma-gītā (named for its subject). (MBh. Śānti Parva; gītā-saṅgraha tradition)
- **Thematic neighbours in the Mokṣadharma** — Its forbearance-and-self-mastery teaching sits beside the renunciation Gītās of the same parva — Hārīta- (saṃnyāsa), Maṅki- and Śampāka- (futility of acquisition), and Vṛtra- (equanimity in defeat) — and pairs especially with the *ahiṃsā*-over-ritual current of [Tulādhāra–Jājali](./mokshadharma-dialogues.md). (MBh. Śānti Parva, Mokṣadharma)

## The famous name-collision — three different "Haṃsa-gītās"

The single name **Haṃsa-gītā** ("Song of the Swan") attaches to **three distinct, unrelated texts**. Conflating them is one of the most common errors in Gītā-rosters; the distinction must be kept.

- **(1) This file — the Mahābhārata Haṃsa-gītā** — **Brahmā-as-swan** instructs the **Sādhya gods** on forbearance, truth, and control of speech; ~41 verses; Mokṣadharma of the [Śānti Parva](./mahabharata/12-shanti-parva.md) (~12.288–299), reported by **Vyāsa to Yudhiṣṭhira**. An *ethical* discourse. (MBh. Śānti Parva)
- **(2) The Bhāgavata's [Haṃsa-avatāra](./hamsa-avatar.md) (SB 11.13)** — **Viṣṇu-as-swan** answers the **Four Kumāras** (Sanaka, Sananda, Sanātana, Sanat-kumāra) on separating mind from sense-objects and abiding as the witnessing Self; a *metaphysical / non-dual* discourse, with a different deity, different questioners, and a different theme. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.13)
- **(3) The Bhāgavata's [Uddhava-gītā](./uddhava-gita.md), popularly "Haṃsa-gītā"** — **Kṛṣṇa's farewell teaching to Uddhava** (Bhāgavata 11.6.40–11.29, ~1,000+ verses); called *Haṃsa-gītā* because Kṛṣṇa bids Uddhava live like the swan (essence-from-water *viveka*) **and** because it *contains* text (2) at 11.13. The grand Purāṇic synthesis of bhakti-jñāna-yoga. (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11) [disputed] [common error]
- **Disambiguation rule** — In short: **swan = Brahmā → this Mahābhārata ethical Gītā; swan = Viṣṇu → the Bhāgavata avatāra-dialogue (SB 11.13); "Haṃsa-gītā" used of Kṛṣṇa's last sermon → the Uddhava-gītā.** The shared title is coincidental (the *haṃsa* as universal symbol of the discriminating, world-unwetted sage), not a sign of common authorship or content. [scholarly]

## Two clocks — scriptural vs. textual time

- **Scripture-internal** — The reported swan-Brahmā teaching is set in an unspecified former age; the *frame* (Vyāsa relating it to Yudhiṣṭhira) falls in the war's immediate aftermath, the closing [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga, traditionally just before [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) begins at 3102 BCE. [traditional]
- **Text-critical** — As part of the Mokṣadharma, the Haṃsa-gītā belongs to the **latest accreted doctrinal stratum** of the epic (mid-1st-millennium-BCE to mid-1st-millennium-CE redaction). Chapter numbers shift between the Critical Edition (BORI) and the Calcutta/Bombay/Ganguli vulgates. Keep scriptural time distinct from manuscript dating. [scholarly]
