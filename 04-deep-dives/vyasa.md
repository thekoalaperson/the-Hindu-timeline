# Vyāsa (Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana) — Arranger of the Vedas

> **Where this sits:** A deep-dive into **Vyāsa** — the island-born son of Parāśara and Satyavatī who splits the one primordial Veda into four, fathers the contending Kuru line by *niyoga*, composes the **[Mahābhārata](mahabharata/)**, the eighteen **[Purāṇas](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md)** and the **[Brahma Sūtras](../90-literature-corpus/darshanas/brahma-sutras.md)**, and walks the timeline as one of the **[chiranjīvis](the-chiranjivis.md)**. He is at once a partial **avatāra of Viṣṇu**, a **ṛṣi-redactor**, an **epic character**, and the standing office-holder *Veda-Vyāsa* of this age.
> Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Cosmic address: Śveta-Varāha Kalpa › 7th ([Vaivasvata](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/)) Manvantara › 28th Mahāyuga, at the **Dvāpara–Kali junction** of the [current Mahāyuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md), with a chiranjīvi afterlife reaching all yugas.

Vyāsa ("the Arranger / Divider," *vi-√as*, "to part out") is less a single name than a **title and an office**. The personal man is **Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana** — "the dark one of the island" — but his life's work of *editing the Veda* makes him **Veda-Vyāsa**, a recurring functionary the Purāṇas say is reborn in every Dvāpara Yuga. He is the **hinge of the whole textual cosmos**: from his pen (and Gaṇeśa's tusk) descend the longest poem on earth, the Purāṇic encyclopedia, and the seed-text of Vedānta. Catalog all variants below.

---

## Birth — Parāśara, Satyavatī, and the island

- **Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana** — born on an **island (dvīpa)** in the **Yamunā** to the ṛṣi **Parāśara** (grandson of [Vasiṣṭha](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md)) and the fisher-maiden **Satyavatī** (Matsyagandhā, "fish-scented," daughter of the apsarā-fish Adrikā). Dark of complexion (*kṛṣṇa*) and island-born (*dvaipāyana*), hence the compound name. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.3) — see the **[ṛṣi lineages & gotras](../08-beings-and-bestiary/rishi-lineages-and-gotras.md)**.
- **The boon of restored virginity** — Parāśara, smitten while crossing the river, raises a concealing mist; Satyavatī conceives and bears Vyāsa the same day, her **virginity (kanyātva) restored** and her fish-odor changed to musk (Yojanagandhā, "fragrant for a yojana"). This lets her later wed king **Śantanu** — the marriage that seeds the entire Kuru tragedy. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva)
- **Born grown, departs at once** — the infant matures instantly, vows himself to **tapas**, and tells his mother he will **come whenever she remembers him**. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva; Bhāgavata 1.4) This standing promise is the device by which he is later summoned for *niyoga*.
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.3):* the emphasis falls on Vyāsa as a **partial descent (aṃśa) of Viṣṇu / Nārāyaṇa**, born expressly to divide the Veda for the short-lived men of the coming Kali.

## The Vedavyāsa office — one per Dvāpara, the 28th in this cycle

- **A recurring cosmic post** — the Purāṇas hold that in **every Dvāpara Yuga** a *Veda-Vyāsa* is appointed to **re-arrange the single Veda** when human lifespans and intellects shrink; Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana is the **twenty-eighth (28th) Vyāsa** of the present Vaivasvata Manvantara's mahāyuga-series. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.3; Bhāgavata 1.4) Cross-yuga office, not a one-time event.
  - *Variant (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.3 list):* the text **names all twenty-eight Vyāsas**, beginning with Svayambhū/Brahmā and running through Vasiṣṭha, Sāraswata, Bṛhaspati, Śukra, and others, with **Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana** as the last; the **next (29th) Vyāsa** will be **Aśvatthāmā**, a fellow chiranjīvi. [scholarly]
- **Why divide at all** — in earlier ages the Veda was held entire by long-lived seers; for the feeble men of Dvāpara-into-Kali, the one mass must be **parted into manageable branches**. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.4; Bhāgavata 1.4)

## Dividing the one Veda into four — and the four disciples

- **One Veda made four** — Vyāsa takes the single undivided **Veda** and separates it into **Ṛg, Yajur, Sāman, and Atharva**, then arranges each into branches (*śākhā*). (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.4; Bhāgavata 1.4; Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva) This editorial act is what *earns* him the title **Vyāsa**.
- **The four disciples** — he entrusts one Veda to each pupil for transmission: **Paila** (Ṛgveda), **Vaiśampāyana** (Yajurveda), **Jaimini** (Sāmaveda), and **Sumantu** (Atharvaveda); the *itihāsa-purāṇa* (the "fifth Veda") he gives to his sūta-disciple **Romaharṣaṇa / Lomaharṣaṇa**. (Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.4; Bhāgavata 1.4)
  - *Variant (transmission of the epic):* it is the Yajurvedic disciple **Vaiśampāyana** who later recites the whole **Mahābhārata** at Janamejaya's snake-sacrifice — the frame by which the poem reaches the world. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva)

## Authorship — the Mahābhārata, the Purāṇas, the Brahma Sūtras

- **The Mahābhārata, dictated to Gaṇeśa** — Vyāsa composes the hundred-thousand-verse epic of his own descendants; needing a scribe who can keep pace, he asks **Brahmā**, who sends **Gaṇeśa**. Gaṇeśa agrees **on condition that Vyāsa never pause in dictation**; Vyāsa counters that Gaṇeśa **must understand each verse before writing it**, and so seeds the poem with deliberately **knotted verses (kūṭa-ślokas)** to win pauses for composition. When Gaṇeśa's stylus breaks, he snaps off his own **tusk** to write on. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva, in the longer recensions) [The Gaṇeśa-scribe frame is absent from the critical edition and is regarded as a later interpolation — [disputed].]
- **The eighteen Purāṇas** — tradition credits Vyāsa with compiling the **eighteen Mahā-Purāṇas** (and the eighteen Upa-Purāṇas), the great encyclopedic corpus of cosmology, genealogy and myth. (Bhāgavata 1.4; Viṣṇu Purāṇa) See the **[smṛti / Purāṇa corpus](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md)**.
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata 1.4–7):* still **dissatisfied** after dividing the Veda and writing the Mahābhārata, Vyāsa is counselled by **Nārada** that he has taught *dharma, artha, kāma, mokṣa* but not extolled the glory of the Lord; in response he composes the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** and teaches it to his son **Śuka**, who recites it to the dying king Parīkṣit.
- **The Brahma Sūtras (Vedānta-Sūtra)** — Vyāsa, here under the name **Bādarāyaṇa**, is the traditional author of the **Brahma Sūtras**, the aphoristic summation of the Upaniṣads that founds the **Vedānta** darśana. (Tradition; Bhāgavata 1.4 hints at his philosophical summa) See **[Brahma Sūtras](../90-literature-corpus/darshanas/brahma-sutras.md)**.
  - *Variant (identity question):* whether **Bādarāyaṇa** and **Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana Vyāsa** are the same person or two figures conflated under the "Vyāsa" title is a long-standing **[scholarly]** debate; the tradition treats them as one.

## Fathering the Kuru line by niyoga

- **The levirate summons** — when Satyavatī's grandsons **Citrāṅgada** and **Vicitravīrya** die childless, she remembers Vyāsa (per his birth-promise) and asks him to beget heirs upon Vicitravīrya's widows by **niyoga** (levirate). (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva)
- **Three sons by three women** — repelled by the unkempt ascetic's appearance:
  - **Ambikā shuts her eyes** → the son is born **blind: Dhṛtarāṣṭra**.
  - **Ambālikā turns pale** → the son is born pallid: **Pāṇḍu** ("the pale one").
  - A frightened **maidservant**, sent in Ambikā's place and serving without fear, bears the wise and unblemished **Vidura** (held a partial incarnation of **Dharma/Yama**). (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva) — these are the fathers/uncle of the [Chandravaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) Kuru war.
- **A son by his own austerity — Śuka** — Vyāsa also fathers **Śukadeva (Śuka)**, the supreme renunciate and reciter of the Bhāgavata, born of his tapas (variant: of the apsarā **Ghṛtācī** in the form of a parrot). (Bhāgavata 1.4; Devī Bhāgavata) [variant origin]

## Chiranjīvi and counsellor of the Kurus

- **One of the deathless (chiranjīvi)** — Vyāsa is counted among the **immortals who live across the yugas**, persisting to compile, teach, and advise; see **[The Chiranjīvis](the-chiranjivis.md)**. As the appointed Vyāsa he must survive to hand the Veda to the next age (and to his successor Aśvatthāmā).
- **The Kuru grandsire-sage** — throughout the Mahābhārata Vyāsa appears at crisis-points to **counsel and console** his descendants: he comforts the widow **Gāndhārī** and **Kuntī**, grants **Sañjaya** divine sight (*divya-cakṣus*) to narrate the war to blind Dhṛtarāṣṭra, foretells the slaughter, and after the war **raises the slain warriors from the Gaṅgā** for one night so the bereaved may see them (Āśramavāsika Parva). (Mahābhārata, Ādi & Āśramavāsika Parvas)
- **Giver of boons and birth-tales** — he grants **Gāndhārī** the boon of a hundred sons and explains the births of the Kauravas from the **maṃsa-peśī** (ball of flesh) incubated in jars. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva)

## Two clocks

- **Scriptural / cyclic:** Vyāsa is the **28th Veda-Vyāsa** of this Manvantara, active at the **Dvāpara–Kali junction** of the current (28th) Mahāyuga — the very turn of the age that his Mahābhārata war marks.
- **Academic / historical:** "Vyāsa" is best read by scholars as a **composite editorial persona** — the personification of centuries of redaction that fixed the Vedic śākhās, accreted the Mahābhārata (c. 400 BCE–400 CE), and gathered the Purāṇic corpus — rather than one historical author. [scholarly]
