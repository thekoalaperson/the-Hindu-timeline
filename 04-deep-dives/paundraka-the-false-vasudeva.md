# Pauṇḍraka the False Vāsudeva & the Burning of Kāśī

> A standalone deep-dive on **Pauṇḍraka**, the king who dressed himself in Viṣṇu's emblems and demanded that Kṛṣṇa surrender the name "Vāsudeva"; his beheading by the Sudarśana cakra along with his ally the **king of Kāśī**; the startling verse that grants the impostor **liberation through meditation-by-mimicry**; and the sequel in which the Kāśī prince **Sudakṣiṇa's** sorcery-fire (*abhicāra / kṛtyā*) rebounds and the **Sudarśana cakra incinerates Kāśī** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.66; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 5.34). Set in the Dwārakā years of the **[Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** of our 28th Mahāyuga.
> Up one level: [Deep Dives README](README.md). Related: [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md) · [Kāśī / Vārāṇasī](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md) · [Śiśupāla](shishupala.md) · [Divine Weapons (Sudarśana)](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md).

The episode is the Kṛṣṇa cycle's great satire on **imitation without interiority**: a king who *plays* God so completely that God's own discus finally answers the costume. It carries three theological stings — the assembly's laughter at the counterfeit, the impostor's unexpected *mukti* precisely because he never stopped thinking of Kṛṣṇa, and the spectacle of **Śiva's own city of liberation burned by Viṣṇu's weapon** after a Śiva-granted boon misfires. The traditions differ in detail (male fire-demon vs female *kṛtyā*, Karūṣa vs Puṇḍra as the impostor's realm) and are cataloged below, never merged.

---

## Who is Pauṇḍraka? (identity and earlier appearances)

- **Pauṇḍraka, king of Karūṣa** — in the Bhāgavata's telling he rules **Karūṣa**; puffed up by childish flatterers who hail him as "Vāsudeva, the Lord descended to earth," he comes to believe he is the true avatāra and styles himself **"Vāsudeva"** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.66.1–2).
  - *Variant (realm — Mahābhārata / Viṣṇu Purāṇa tradition):* the epic knows him as **"Pauṇḍraka-Vāsudeva," lord of the eastern peoples — the Puṇḍras, Vaṅgas and Kirātas** (roughly Bengal and the east); the very name *Pauṇḍraka* points to the **Puṇḍra** country rather than Karūṣa. The two localizations are cataloged side by side, not harmonized. (*Mahābhārata*, Sabhā Parva; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 5.34)
- **Ally of Jarāsandha** — in the Sabhā Parva, when Kṛṣṇa counsels Yudhiṣṭhira before the Rājasūya, he names **Pauṇḍraka-Vāsudeva** among the powerful kings leagued with **Jarāsandha** of Magadha against the Yādavas — placing the impostor squarely in the anti-Kṛṣṇa axis alongside Śiśupāla and Śālva (*Mahābhārata*, Sabhā Parva; see [Śiśupāla](shishupala.md)).
- **At Rukmiṇī's wedding** — Pauṇḍraka appears in the roll of hostile kings who escort Śiśupāla to Kuṇḍina for the marriage that Kṛṣṇa disrupts by carrying off Rukmiṇī (*Bhāgavata* 10.53; see [Rukmiṇī-haraṇa](rukmini-harana.md)).

## The imposture and the embassy (Bhāgavata 10.66)

- **The occasion** — the challenge arrives while **[Balarāma](characters/balarama.md)** is away visiting Nanda's Vraja, so Kṛṣṇa receives it seated in **Ugrasena's assembly** at Dwārakā (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.1, 10.66.7).
- **The counterfeit regalia** — Pauṇḍraka equips himself with imitations of Viṣṇu's insignia: a mock **conch, discus, sword and mace**, a **Śārṅga-style bow**, the **Śrīvatsa** mark, a false **Kaustubha** gem, a forest-flower garland, **yellow silk garments**, a **Garuḍa banner**, a costly crown and glittering earrings — so that, when Kṛṣṇa finally sees him on the field, he looks "like an actor dressed up as Kṛṣṇa on a stage" (*naṭa iva*), and Kṛṣṇa laughs aloud (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.12–14).
- **The embassy's demand** — Pauṇḍraka's messenger announces to the court: "I alone am Vāsudeva; there is no other. I have descended out of compassion for the world. **Give up my name and my emblems** and take shelter of me — or give me battle" (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.4–6).
- **The assembly laughs** — Ugrasena's court erupts in laughter at the message; Kṛṣṇa sends back the reply that seals the tale: he will indeed **"release the emblem"** Pauṇḍraka demands — the discus itself — upon him, and the impostor will lie on the field, his body a shelter for dogs and carrion birds (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.8–9).

## The vadha at Kāśī (Bhāgavata 10.66)

- **The shelter of the Kāśī king** — Pauṇḍraka has taken refuge with his friend the **king of Kāśī (Kāśirāja / Kāśīpati)**; Kṛṣṇa therefore marches on **[Kāśī / Vārāṇasī](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md)** itself. Pauṇḍraka comes out with **two akṣauhiṇī** divisions, the Kāśī king following in support with **three** (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.11–12).
- **The armies broken** — with discus, mace, sword and arrows Kṛṣṇa shatters chariots, elephants, horses and men, strewing the field — the text likens it to the destruction at the end of an age (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.15–17).
- **"The weapon you asked for, I now hand over"** — Kṛṣṇa tells Pauṇḍraka that the *cihnāni* (emblems) demanded through the envoy are now being "given up" exactly as requested: he cuts down the impostor's chariot and **beheads Pauṇḍraka with the Sudarśana cakra**, as Indra's thunderbolt splits a mountain peak (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.18–21).
- **The head hurled into Kāśī** — Kṛṣṇa then severs the **Kāśī king's** head with arrows and casts it flying into Kāśī city, where it lands "like a lotus flower tossed by the wind." The citizens crowd around wondering whose head has fallen; seeing the earrings they recognize their own king, and his queens and son lament (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.21–23, 10.66.27).

## The liberation of the impostor — the meditation gloss

- **Mukti by mimicry** — the Bhāgavata closes Pauṇḍraka's story with a celebrated theological turn: because he had **constantly meditated on the Lord's form** — wearing Kṛṣṇa's emblems, thinking of Kṛṣṇa without interruption, even as a rival — his bonds were destroyed and he **attained absorption in the Lord** (*tan-mayatām*, "became of His nature") (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.24).
  - *Variant (commentarial gloss):* the classical commentators read the verse as a grant of formal liberation — glossed as **sārūpya-mukti** (attaining the Lord's own form — fitting, since he had spent his life copying that form) or as **sāyujya** (merging union); traditions differ on which grade is meant. It is the standard proof-text that even **hostile or parodic remembrance** (*dveṣa-/anukaraṇa-bhakti*) binds the rememberer to God, in the family of Kaṃsa, Śiśupāla and the [Jaya–Vijaya](jaya-vijaya.md) doctrine of *vaira-bhakti*. On the graded muktis see [Mokṣa & the puruṣārthas](../05-concepts-and-dharma/purusharthas-and-karma-samsara-moksha.md). (Bhāgavata commentarial tradition) [disputed]

## Sudakṣiṇa's kṛtyā vs the Sudarśana — the burning of Kāśī (Bhāgavata 10.66.27–43)

- **Sudakṣiṇa's vow** — **Sudakṣiṇa**, son of the slain Kāśī king, performs his father's funeral rites and vows to kill his father's killer. He worships **Maheśvara (Śiva)** with great austerity in the **Avimukta** field — Kāśī itself, Śiva's own never-forsaken *kṣetra* (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.27–29).
- **Śiva's conditional boon** — pleased, Śiva offers a boon; Sudakṣiṇa asks for the means to slay his enemy. Śiva directs him to attend the **Dakṣiṇāgni** (southern sacrificial fire) with **abhicāra** (black-rite) observances, surrounded by his priests: the fire, so served, will accomplish his desire — **provided it is turned against an enemy of brāhmaṇas**, i.e. it cannot touch one who is *brahmaṇya*, devoted to the brāhmaṇas (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.30–31).
- **The fire-demon rises** — from the sorcery-altar rises the ritual fire in **personified, terrifying form**: a colossal naked figure blazing like molten copper, with hair, beard and moustache of red-hot metal, fangs bared, tongue licking the corners of its mouth, brandishing a flaming **trident**, shaking the earth as it strides toward **Dwārakā**, burning the directions before it (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.32–34).
- **Kṛṣṇa at the dice-board** — the panicked citizens of Dwārakā run to Kṛṣṇa, who is **playing dice**; he laughs, tells them not to fear, and — knowing the apparition to be **Śiva's kṛtyā-sorcery** (māheśvarī kṛtyā) — calmly **dispatches the Sudarśana cakra** at his side to deal with it (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.35–38).
- **The rebound rule of abhicāra** — frustrated by the cakra's superior fire and unable to touch Dwārakā, the *kṛtyā*-fire turns back — for sorcery that fails its target **returns upon its maker** — and burns **Sudakṣiṇa together with his officiating priests** (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.39–41).
- **Kāśī incinerated** — the Sudarśana pursues the fleeing *kṛtyā* into **Vārāṇasī** and burns the entire city: its watchtowers and gateways, assembly halls, markets and treasuries, its stores of grain, its stables of elephants, horses and chariots, its palaces — then, the city consumed, the discus returns to Kṛṣṇa's side, its energy undimmed (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.41–43; see the [Sudarśana entry](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md)).

## The Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.34 variant

- **The older telling** — the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* (Book 5, ch. 34), a principal **pre-Bhāgavata** Kṛṣṇa biography, carries the same skeleton — Pauṇḍraka's assumption of the **name and insignia of Vāsudeva**, his demand that Kṛṣṇa resign them, Kṛṣṇa's grim compliance ("the emblems you ask for I now *deliver*"), the death of impostor and Kāśī king, the severed head thrown into the city, the vengeance-rite, and the burning of Kāśī by the cakra (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 5.34; see the [Viṣṇu Purāṇa profile](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/vishnu-purana.md)).
  - *Variant (the kṛtyā is female):* in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa the being raised from the vengeance-sacrifice is explicitly a **kṛtyā — a female personification of destructive rite** — a vast, fearsome woman with hair of flame who rushes on Dwārakā; the Bhāgavata instead personifies the **Dakṣiṇāgni fire as a male figure** with trident. The two figures are kept distinct here. (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 5.34 vs *Bhāgavata* 10.66)
  - *Variant (who performs the rite):* the Viṣṇu Purāṇa gives the rite to the **son of the Kāśī king acting with his father's priest(s)**, without dwelling on the Bhāgavata's named prince **Sudakṣiṇa** and Śiva's explicit *brahmaṇya* proviso (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 5.34).
  - *Variant (scale of the destruction):* the Viṣṇu Purāṇa has the **Kāśī host march out to defend the kṛtyā**; the cakra consumes the *kṛtyā*, the army, and then the **whole city with its inhabitants**, before returning "with undiminished energy" to Viṣṇu's hand (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 5.34).
- **Harivaṃśa extension** — a late stratum of the **Harivaṃśa** (Bhaviṣya-parvan) carries an extended Pauṇḍraka narrative in which the impostor moves against **Dwārakā itself**, aided by allies — traditions associate the Niṣāda **[Ekalavya](ekalavya.md)** with his cause — before falling, as everywhere, to Kṛṣṇa. ## Motifs and tensions across traditions

- **Śiva's city burned after Śiva's boon** — the episode sits deliberately athwart Kāśī's own Śaiva theology: the *kṣetra* that calls itself **Avimukta**, resting on Śiva's triśūla and exempt from *pralaya* (see [Kāśī / Vārāṇasī](../06-sacred-geography/kashi-varanasi.md)), is here reduced to ash by Viṣṇu's discus in a **Vaiṣṇava** purāṇic frame — after a boon of Śiva fails against Kṛṣṇa, exactly as in the [Bāṇāsura war](banasura-usha-aniruddha.md). The Śaiva Kāśī-māhātmya tradition and the Vaiṣṇava burning narrative are **separate traditions preserved side by side**, not a contradiction to resolve. [scholarly]
- **The impostor motif** — Pauṇḍraka is the tradition's cautionary figure for claiming divinity by costume: the outer *cihnas* (Śrīvatsa, Kaustubha, cakra) without the inner reality invite the real cakra. Yet the same text lets the parody function as *smaraṇa* (remembrance) and save him — the episode is thus quoted on both sides of debates about imitation of God (*anukaraṇa*) versus service of God. (Bhāgavata 10.66 with commentaries) [disputed]
- **Abhicāra and its rebound** — Sudakṣiṇa's rite is the classic scriptural showcase of **abhicāra** (aggressive/black ritual) and the rule that a *kṛtyā* loosed at an invulnerable target destroys its sender — a motif shared with other purāṇic sorcery tales (*Bhāgavata* 10.66.40).

## Two clocks

- **Scriptural clock:** the episode belongs to the **Dwārakā period** of Kṛṣṇa's career, in the closing generations of the **[Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** of the 28th Mahāyuga, Vaivasvata Manvantara — after the founding of Dwārakā and Rukmiṇī's marriage, in the same span as the Śiśupāla and Śālva conflicts (*Bhāgavata*, Canto 10 sequence; see [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md)).
- **Historical clock:** [scholarly] the narrative's textual homes are dated academically — the **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** to roughly the **3rd–5th century CE** (estimates diverge widely), the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa**'s final redaction to about the **9th–10th century CE** — while Vārāṇasī itself is archaeologically attested from the **1st millennium BCE**. The burning of Kāśī is a purāṇic event, not a documented historical conflagration. [scholarly]
