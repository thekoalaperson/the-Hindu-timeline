# Southeast Asian Ramayanas — Ramakien, Kakawin, Reamker & beyond

Deep-dive: [Ramayana](../README.md) · [Deep Dives](../../README.md) · [Master Index](../../../README.md)
The Rama-cycle as it traveled, localized, and was reborn across mainland and maritime Southeast Asia — from the 9th-c. Old Javanese **Kakawin** to the Thai **Ramakien**, Khmer **Reamker**, Lao **Phra Lak Phra Lam**, Burmese **Yama Zatdaw**, Malay **Hikayat Seri Rama**, and the Maranao **Maharadia Lawana**. These are not Hindu *recensions* of [Valmiki](../README.md) but full re-tellings absorbed into Theravada-Buddhist and (in the maritime zone) Islamic literary worlds, kept here for comparison. Cross-link the parent overview's [Southeast Asian retellings](../README.md) section.

> **Two clocks.** The *story* still nominally sits at the close of the [Treta Yuga](../../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md), but these are *texts* with their own academic datings (Kakawin c. 856–930 CE; Ramakien & Reamker chiefly 18th c. CE after older lost layers). This file dates the **texts** and catalogs the **variant plots/characters**, not the cosmic events. The transmission vector is not direct Valmiki but a web of Sanskrit *kavya* (esp. the [Bhattikavya](#sources)), Tamil/Pala-era trade contact, the Pali *Dasaratha Jataka* (see [Jain & Buddhist Ramayanas](jain-and-buddhist-ramayanas.md)), and centuries of oral/performance tradition.

## Shared dynamics of localization

- **Topographic transplant** - Ayodhya, Lanka, and the forest exile are re-mapped onto local geography and royal capitals: the Thai version sets Rama's city at **Ayutthaya** (named for Ayodhya), the Lao at Lan Xang/Luang Prabang, etc. Kings adopt Rama's name (the Thai **Chakri dynasty** monarchs are titled "Rama I–X"). [folk tradition]
- **Religious reframing** - On the Theravada mainland (Thai, Lao, Khmer, Burmese), Rama is folded into Buddhist ethics and frequently treated as a *bodhisatta* / past-life of Gautama Buddha, echoing the *Dasaratha Jataka*; karma and merit displace Hindu *bhakti*/avatara theology. In the Islamized maritime zone (Malay, Maranao), the frame becomes Islamic — Allah, prophets/angels, and Sultanate court culture. [scholarly]
- **De-divinization of Rama** - Across nearly all versions Rama loses the explicit "Vishnu incarnate" status he holds in the [Bala](../1-bala-kanda.md)/[Uttara](../7-uttara-kanda.md) kandas and becomes a more human, sometimes flawed, ideal king. Several versions even let the *antagonist* (Tosakanth/Maharaja Wana) be partly sympathetic. [scholarly]
- **The Hanuman expansion** - The single most consistent SE Asian innovation: the celibate, single-minded devotee Hanuman of [Sundara Kanda](../5-sundara-kanda.md) becomes a flamboyant, amorous, scene-stealing warrior-hero with wives, sons, and comic-romantic episodes. In Thai/Khmer/Lao tellings he frequently eclipses Rama as audience favorite. [folk]
- **Performance-first transmission** - These epics live primarily as *masked dance, shadow play, and puppetry* (Thai *khon* & *nang yai*; Khmer *lkhon khol*; Javanese/Balinese *wayang kulit*; Burmese *yokthe* marionette & dance), so plot survives through stagecraft more than fixed manuscript. [folk]
- **New episodes** - A floating stock of non-Valmiki episodes recurs region-wide, esp. **Hanuman and the mermaid princess** (Suvannamaccha / Sovanna Maccha / Macchanu) who tries to sabotage the bridge to Lanka and bears Hanuman a fish-tailed son. [folk]

## Thai — Ramakien (รามเกียรติ์)

- **Ramakien ("Glory of Rama")** - The Thai national epic; *Ramakirti* = "fame/glory of Rama." Older Ayutthaya-era versions were lost in the 1767 Burmese sack of Ayutthaya; the standard text was **re-composed under King Rama I (r. 1782–1809)** of the Chakri dynasty, with notable later versions by **Rama II** (dance-drama scenes) and **Rama VI**. [late text]
- **Phra Ram** - Rama as a Thai prince; treated as an incarnation of **Phra Narai (Vishnu/Narayana)** in name but acting as a human king under a Buddhist-tinged cosmos overseen by **Phra Isuan (Shiva)** and Phra Phrom (Brahma). He is capable of rash anger — e.g. ordering Sida's death over a drawn portrait, then repenting. [folk]
- **Tosakanth / Thotsakan** - Ravana, the ten-necked king of Longka; named "Tosakanth" ("ten-necked"). Partly humanized and even pitied in some passages; his life-force is hidden externally (a soul-in-a-box / external-soul motif elaborated beyond Valmiki). [folk]
- **Nang Sida** - Sita, the abducted queen. In Rama I's text her late banishment is re-motivated: a **rakshasa (not palace gossip)** tricks her into drawing Tosakanth's portrait, triggering Rama's suspicion — softening the *agni-pariksha*/exile of [Uttara Kanda](../7-uttara-kanda.md). [folk variant]
- **Hanuman** - The most beloved figure; a **romantic, amorous white monkey warrior** who marries multiple women (incl. the mermaid **Suvannamaccha** and Benyakai); far from Valmiki's celibate devotee. Central to *khon*. [folk]
- **Khon (โขน)** - Royal masked dance-drama whose repertoire is essentially the Ramakien; UNESCO-inscribed. Also performed as *nang yai* (large shadow puppets). The Ramakien is painted in mural galleries around **Wat Phra Kaew** (the Emerald Buddha temple), Bangkok. [folk]

## Old Javanese — Kakawin Ramayana

- **Kakawin Ramayana** - The **oldest** major SE Asian Rama text and one of the finest; an Old Javanese *kakawin* (court poem in Indian meters) of **~2,774 stanzas in 26 cantos (sarga)**. [scholarly]
  - *Dating:* composed in **Central Java c. 856 – c. 930 CE** (Stuart Robson), under the **Mataram/Medang** kingdom; the final section possibly added c. 900–930. Contemporary with the great Hindu temple **Prambanan**, whose reliefs depict the Rama story. [scholarly]
  - **Sanskrit prototype** - Uniquely, its model is **not Valmiki directly** but the difficult 6th–7th-c. Sanskrit *mahakavya* **Bhattikavya** (a.k.a. *Ravanavadha*) of the poet Bhatti — a grammar-illustrating poem — for roughly its first half, after which it diverges. [scholarly]
  - **"Yogesvara" attribution** - Sometimes ascribed to a poet **Yogesvara**; authorship is effectively anonymous and the Yogesvara name is rejected by most scholars for lack of evidence. [disputed]
  - **Localization** - Indigenous Javanese deities (the **Punakawan**-type clown-servants, esp. **Semar**, in later wayang) and local nature-imagery are woven in; the text became foundational to Javanese and Balinese *wayang kulit* (shadow-puppet) Rama cycles. [folk]
- **Balinese continuation** - In Bali (which retained Hindu practice), the Rama story persists in living ritual *wayang* and dance (e.g. the **Kecak** "monkey chant," a 20th-c. development staging the Lanka battle), keeping a Hindu-frame Rama alongside the Muslim-majority Javanese telling. [folk]

## Khmer — Reamker

- **Reamker ("Glory/Honor of Rama")** - The Khmer (Cambodian) national epic; *Ream* = Rama. Surviving manuscript text is largely **17th–18th c. CE** (with older lost layers; Angkorian-era reliefs at **Angkor Wat / Banteay Srei** already depict Ramayana scenes, 10th–12th c.). [late text]
- **Buddhist reinterpretation** - Reframes the Hindu story through a **Theravada Buddhist** worldview: heavy emphasis on **karma, merit, moral accountability, and compassion** rather than divine destiny; it reinterprets rather than rejects its Hindu roots. [scholarly]
- **Preah Ream** - Rama, humanized; his divinity recedes and dynamic secondary figures take the spotlight.
- **Krong Reap** - Ravana, king of Langka, who abducts Neang Seda. [folk]
- **Neang Seda** - Sita, the devoted heroine; her capture sets the war in motion; her chastity-ordeal and estrangement from Ream are foregrounded as moral drama. [folk]
- **Hanuman** - The **white monkey general**: clever, courageous, mischievous, amorous — often stealing the show from Preah Ream. The signature Khmer-favored episode is **Hanuman and the mermaid Sovanna Maccha** (Macchanu), who obstructs the causeway to Langka and is won over by him. [folk]
- **Lkhon Khol / Robam** - The Reamker is the core of Khmer **masked dance-drama (lkhon khol)** and classical *robam* dance, and is painted along the gallery walls of the **Royal Palace / Silver Pagoda**, Phnom Penh. [folk]

## Lao — Phra Lak Phra Lam

- **Phra Lak Phra Lam ("Honored Lakshmana, Honored Rama")** - The Lao national Rama epic, named pointedly for **Lakshmana first** (a sign of Lakshmana's elevated stature). Vernacular Lao composition, roughly **16th c. onward**, blending Hindu narrative, Theravada Buddhist doctrine, and local folklore. [late text]
- **Rama as Bodhisatta** - In Theravada Laos the story is treated explicitly as a **Jataka** — Phra Ram is a **previous incarnation of Gautama Buddha**, the epitome of moral kingship; its Hindu identity is effectively dissolved. [folk variant]
- **Localization** - Set in the Mekong/Lan Xang landscape; recited by monks and performed in court/temple dance and shadow theatre; transmitted heavily through palm-leaf manuscripts and oral recitation. [folk]

## Burmese — Yama Zatdaw

- **Yama Zatdaw (ရာမဇာတ်တော်) / Yamayana** - The unofficial national epic of Myanmar; *Yama* = Rama, *zatdaw* = a (jataka-style) dramatic "great birth-story." Standardized under **Konbaung-dynasty** patronage in the **18th c.** (court contact with Thai Ayutthaya after 1767 strongly shaped it). [late text]
- **Buddhist Jataka frame** - Yama (Rama) is presented as a **past-life Buddha (bodhisatta)**; the tale carries didactic Buddhist moral weight rather than Hindu devotion. [folk variant]
- **Performance forms** - Distinctively staged through **yokthe** (Burmese marionette/string-puppet theatre), *zat pwe* dance-drama, and a highly acrobatic, ornately costumed Burmese dance idiom; episodes also appear in temple murals and lacquerware. [folk]

## Malay — Hikayat Seri Rama

- **Hikayat Seri Rama ("The Story of Sri Rama")** - The Malay prose adaptation in the **hikayat** genre (*hikayat*, from Arabic, = "story/tale"); the Rama story was Islamized in the Malay world roughly **1300–1700 CE**; one of the oldest Malay literary works (a copy reached the Bodleian Library by the early 17th c.). [late text]
- **Islamic reframing** - Opens within an **Islamic cosmological frame** — references to **Allah** and a creator-prophet hierarchy replace the Hindu pantheon at the top, while the underlying narrative remains the Rama cycle. [folk variant]
- **Moral inversion variant** - *Variant (Malaysian recensions):* **Maharaja Wana (Ravana)** is portrayed as comparatively **honorable and righteous**, while **Seri Rama** is depicted as relatively **egotistical and self-righteous** — a striking ethical reversal of the Valmiki polarity. [folk variant]
- **Wayang transmission** - Source for the Malay **wayang kulit** (shadow-puppet) Rama repertoire, esp. in Kelantan; absorbs Javanese clown-servant figures and local color. [folk]

## Maranao (Philippines) — Maharadia Lawana & the Darangen

- **Maharadia Lawana ("Maharaja Ravana")** - A **Maranao** (Mindanao, Philippines) epic-song preserving a local, indigenized Rama story; named for the antagonist. First documented/translated in the 1960s–70s (notably via **Juan R. Francis­co** and singer Ginsong-Pagiwa). [folk]
  - **Islamic & local elements** - Incorporates **Islamic features** — invocation of **Allah, angels, and royal titles like Sultan and Shah** — layered over the Rama plot; deeply hybridized through diaspora and local oral tradition. [folk variant]
  - **Indigenized cast** - Rama-figure as a Maranao prince (Radia Mangandiri / Bantugan-type heroes in related cycles); the abductor is **Maharadia Lawana**, here a (sometimes many-headed) antagonist whose external-soul/immortality must be overcome. [folk]
- **Darangen** - The vast Maranao oral **epic corpus** (UNESCO-proclaimed Masterpiece of Oral/Intangible Heritage) within which the Maharadia Lawana sits; resembles the Malay adaptations and is said by some to **predate the arrival of Islam**, implying a pre-Islamic Rama-story stratum in the southern Philippines. [disputed]

## Comparative notes & open rosters

- **Antagonist's name = title** - Across the region the epic is often named for the *villain's* glory or the *brother* (Reamker/Ramakien for Rama; Maharadia Lawana for Ravana; Phra Lak Phra Lam fronting Lakshmana) — a structural signal of how the moral center shifts in localization.
- **External-soul motif** - Tosakanth's, Lawana's, and other antagonists' lives stored outside the body (in a box, a bee, a mountain) is a pan-SE-Asian folk addition not in Valmiki. [folk]
- **Open-ended roster** - Manuscript traditions, regional sub-versions (Balinese vs. Javanese; Kelantanese Malay; multiple Lao palm-leaf redactions; Cham, Mon, and Tai-Khmun retellings), and the full inventory of interpolated Hanuman episodes are **not exhaustively cataloged here** — these are living, multi-recension oral/performance traditions. [folk]
