# Yuddha (Laṅkā) Kāṇḍa — Book of War

> Cosmic address: [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md) → 28th Mahāyuga → **[Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** → the Rāma-avatāra cycle, its climax.

> See also: [Daśāvatāra (Rāma)](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) · [Sūryavaṃśa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) · [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) · [Itihāsa-Epics corpus](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md)

The sixth and longest book of Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa, **~128 sargas / ~4,400+ ślokas** (sarga counts vary by recension; the Critical Edition is shorter than the vulgate), called *Yuddha* ("War") or *Laṅkā Kāṇḍa* ("Book of Laṅkā"). It carries the epic from Hanumān's report through the bridging of the ocean, the siege and battles of Laṅkā, the deaths of Kumbhakarṇa, Indrajit, and Rāvaṇa, Sītā's fire-ordeal, the flight north in the Puṣpaka, and Rāma's coronation. In the **Critical Edition this is the last book**; the *Uttara Kāṇḍa* (Sītā's exile, Lava–Kuśa, Rāma's departure) is widely held [scholarly] to be a later addition.

## I. Council, Embassies and the Defection of Vibhīṣaṇa (sargas 1–19)

- **Rāma honors Hanumān; the war-council** - On hearing Hanumān's report from Laṅkā, Rāma despairs of crossing the ocean but is rallied; the vānara host marches south to the shore (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 1–4).
- **Vibhīṣaṇa's counsel rejected in Laṅkā** - Rāvaṇa convenes his court; Vibhīṣaṇa (his youngest brother) and the elder minister Mālyavān urge returning Sītā, warning that righteousness is with Rāma. Rāvaṇa scorns and insults Vibhīṣaṇa (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 9–16).
- **Vibhīṣaṇa's defection (śaraṇāgati)** - Spurned, Vibhīṣaṇa flies across the sea with four companions and surrenders to Rāma. A debate erupts among the vānaras over whether to accept an enemy's brother; Rāma rules that **one who comes seeking refuge must never be turned away** — a touchstone verse of the doctrine of *śaraṇāgati* — and accepts him, pre-crowning him king of Laṅkā (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 17–19).
  - *Variant (suspicion):* Sugrīva alone counsels rejecting Vibhīṣaṇa as a spy; Hanumān argues for acceptance — the balance of counsel differs by retelling.
  - *Variant ([Rāmcaritmānas](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md)):* Tulsīdās dwells on Vibhīṣaṇa as the model devotee whom the Lord receives unconditionally; the episode is a major bhakti set-piece.
- **Rāvaṇa's spies Śuka and Sāraṇa** - Rāvaṇa sends the rākṣasas Śuka and Sāraṇa, disguised, to scout the vānara army; Vibhīṣaṇa exposes them. Caught, they are released by Rāma to carry word back — a gesture of fearlessness. They report the host's vastness; the scout Śārdūla likewise (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 20–29).
- **Rāvaṇa's deception of Sītā** - To break Sītā's will, Rāvaṇa has the sorcerer Vidyujjihva conjure an **illusory severed head of Rāma and his bow**, shown to Sītā as proof of his death; the rākṣasī Saramā (Vibhīṣaṇa's wife) consoles her and reveals the trick (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 22–24, vulgate).

## II. Bridging the Ocean — Nala-Setu / Rāma-Setu (sargas 19–22)

- **Rāma's penance before the sea** - Rāma lies in supplication for three days asking Sāgara (the ocean) to grant passage; ignored, he raises a fiery āgneyāstra/brahmāstra to dry the sea (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 21).
- **Sāgara appears and names Nala** - The ocean-god appears in terror, pleads his nature, and reveals that the vānara **Nala**, son of the divine architect Viśvakarman, is boon-blessed to build a floating causeway; Rāma diverts the unspent weapon to the northern desert/Drumakulya region (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 22).
- **The Setu built in five days** - Under Nala's direction the vānaras hurl mountains, trees, and boulders into the sea; the **100-yojana-long, 10-yojana-wide bridge** is completed in five days, and the army crosses to Laṅkā, encamping on Mount Suvela (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 22).
  - *Variant (stones float by the Name):* Devotional/folk tradition (esp. in [Rāmcaritmānas](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md) and oral retellings) holds the rocks floated because Rāma's name was written on them; in Vālmīki the floating is Nala's innate boon, not the inscribed Name.
  - *Variant (the squirrel):* A widely-told [folk tradition] adds a small squirrel carrying pebbles and rolling in sand to help; Rāma strokes its back, leaving the three white stripes seen on Indian palm squirrels today — not in Vālmīki.
  - *Variant (Śiva-liṅga at Rāmeśvaram):* Tradition holds Rāma installed/worshipped a Śiva-liṅga at the bridgehead (Rāmeśvaram), one of the [jyotirliṅgas](../../06-sacred-geography/) — a later sthala-purāṇa overlay onto the crossing.

## III. Opening of the Siege (sargas 30–45)

- **Aṅgada's embassy** - Before battle Rāma sends the prince **Aṅgada** (Vālin's son) as envoy to Laṅkā, demanding Sītā's release on pain of Rāvaṇa's death and Vibhīṣaṇa's enthronement; Rāvaṇa orders him seized, and Aṅgada shakes off the guards and departs defiantly (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 41).
  - *Variant (the planted foot):* A famous [folk tradition] / southern retelling has Aṅgada plant his foot in Rāvaṇa's court and challenge anyone to lift it; none can, humbling the rākṣasas — popular in [Rāmcaritmānas](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md) and theatre, not in Vālmīki.
- **The first assault** - The vānaras storm Laṅkā's gates; Rāvaṇa's general Prahasta and others sally. Furious single combats and mass battle fill the days (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 42–43).
- **Indrajit's first nāgāstra** - Rāvaṇa's son **Indrajit (Meghanāda)**, fighting invisibly, binds Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa with the serpent-arrows (nāga-pāśa); both fall as if slain, dismaying the army (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 44–45).
- **Garuḍa frees them** - The eagle **Garuḍa**, enemy of serpents, descends; at his approach the nāga-bonds dissolve and the brothers revive (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 50).

## IV. The Fall of the Rākṣasa Champions (sargas 46–67)

- **Dhūmrākṣa, Vajradaṃṣṭra, Akampana, Prahasta** - In successive sorties Rāvaṇa's commanders are killed: Dhūmrākṣa and Akampana by Hanumān, Vajradaṃṣṭra by Aṅgada, and the chief general **Prahasta** by Nīla (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 51–58).
- **Rāvaṇa takes the field** - Rāvaṇa himself sallies, wounds Lakṣmaṇa, and is faced by Rāma; humiliated and disarmed (Rāma spares him, telling him to return rested), he retreats (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 59).
- **The waking of Kumbhakarṇa** - Rāvaṇa orders his colossal brother **Kumbhakarṇa**, cursed (by Brahmā via Sarasvatī) to sleep six months at a time, roused with drums, elephants, and mountains of food (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 60).
  - *Variant (curse origin):* When the brothers won boons from Brahmā, Kumbhakarṇa meant to ask *Indrāsana* (Indra's seat) but Sarasvatī twisted his tongue to *nidrāsana* (a bed of sleep) [folk/Purāṇic gloss]; Vālmīki gives the long-sleep as Brahmā's curb on his appetite.
- **Kumbhakarṇa's rampage and death** - Awakened, Kumbhakarṇa first counsels Rāvaṇa to make peace, then loyally goes to war; he devours and scatters vānaras, withstands Hanumān and others, until **Rāma severs his arms, legs, and finally his head** with celestial arrows (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 61–67).

## V. Indrajit's Sorceries and the Sañjīvanī (sargas 68–91)

- **Death of Rāvaṇa's sons; Atikāya** - Narāntaka, Devāntaka, Triśiras, Mahodara, Mahāpārśva and the giant **Atikāya** fall in turn — Atikāya, armored by Brahmā's boon, is slain by Lakṣmaṇa with the brahmāstra (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 68–71).
- **Indrajit's brahmāstra fells the host** - Indrajit, fighting unseen, looses the brahmāstra and lays low the whole vānara army, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa among them (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 73).
- **The first Sañjīvanī flight** - **Jāmbavān** bids Hanumān fly to the Himālaya for healing herbs from Mount **Auṣadhi/Droṇagiri**; unable to tell the glowing herbs apart, Hanumān **carries the whole mountain** back, reviving the fallen, then returns it (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 74).
  - *Variant (Bharata's arrow):* A southern/folk addition has Bharata in Ayodhyā, mistaking the airborne Hanumān for a demon, shooting him down, then aiding him — absent from Vālmīki.
  - *Note:* Vālmīki has **two** herb-fetching episodes (here for the brahmāstra wounds, and again after Lakṣmaṇa is struck by the Śakti); popular retellings often merge them into one.
- **Māyā-Sītā slain** - To demoralize the army, Indrajit conjures an **illusory Sītā (Māyā-Sītā)** on his chariot and beheads her before Hanumān's eyes; the vānaras reel until Vibhīṣaṇa reveals it was sorcery — Rāvaṇa would never kill the woman he covets (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 80, vulgate).
  - *Variant (the "real" shadow-Sītā doctrine):* Several traditions ([Kūrma Purāṇa], [Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa], and [Rāmcaritmānas](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md)) hold that the *true* Sītā was hidden with Agni before the abduction and a **Chāyā/Māyā-Sītā** stood in her place all along — this same shadow is later "burned away" at the agni-parīkṣā to restore the real Sītā. See §VIII.
- **The Nikumbhilā yajña** - Indrajit retreats to the secret altar of **Nikumbhilā** to complete a fire-sacrifice that would render him invincible; by the boon, he can be killed only if struck **before** the rite is finished (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 84–86).
- **Indrajit slain by Lakṣmaṇa** - Guided by Vibhīṣaṇa (who knows the secret), Lakṣmaṇa, with Hanumān and Vibhīṣaṇa, reaches Nikumbhilā, disrupts the yajña, and after a tremendous duel **beheads Indrajit with the Aindrāstra** (Indra's weapon), invoked on the vow that "if Rāma is true to dharma, let this slay him" (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 88–90).
  - *Variant (Sulocanā / satī):* Later texts and theatre add **Sulocanā**, Indrajit's widow (a nāga-princess), performing satī and the severed head speaking — a popular [late text]/folk cycle (*Sati Sulochana*), not in Vālmīki.

## VI. Lakṣmaṇa, the Vāsavī Śakti, and the Second Sañjīvanī (sargas 88–101)

- **Rāvaṇa's grief and final sortie** - Devastated by Indrajit's death, Rāvaṇa storms out in full fury for the climactic battle (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 93–95).
- **Lakṣmaṇa struck by the Śakti** - In combat Rāvaṇa hurls the **Vāsavī Śakti** (Indra's infallible javelin) at Lakṣmaṇa, who falls mortally wounded, fated to die at sunrise (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 100).
- **Second Sañjīvanī flight** - The physician **Suṣeṇa** names the **Viśalyakaraṇī / Sañjīvanī** herbs; Hanumān again flies to the Himālaya and, again unable to choose, brings the **entire Droṇagiri**; Lakṣmaṇa is healed before dawn and the mountain returned (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 101).

## VII. The Slaying of Rāvaṇa (sargas 102–111)

- **Indra's chariot and Mātali** - Seeing Rāma on foot against the chariot-borne Rāvaṇa, Indra sends his own **celestial chariot driven by the charioteer Mātali**, with his bow, armor, and weapons, to equalize the fight (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 102).
- **The Āditya-Hṛdayam** - As Rāma flags, the sage **Agastya** appears and teaches him the **Āditya-Hṛdayam**, a hymn to Sūrya; chanting it thrice, Rāma is renewed with vigor for the final assault (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 105). *(A celebrated stotra recited in its own right.)*
- **The regenerating heads** - The duel rages (by tradition seven days); each head and arm Rāma severs grows back, the arrows seeming spent (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 108).
- **The Brahmāstra to the navel** - Vibhīṣaṇa (or Mātali's counsel) reveals that **amṛta lodged in Rāvaṇa's navel** sustains him; Rāma fits the **Brahmāstra given by Agastya** (the weapon of Brahmā, wind in its wings, fire in its tip) and looses it into the navel, drying the nectar and slaying Rāvaṇa at last (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 108–109).
  - *Variant (the navel secret's source):* Vālmīki's vulgate gives Mātali the prompt to use the Brahmāstra; the "amṛta-in-the-navel told by Vibhīṣaṇa" detail is strong in later/regional retellings and [Rāmcaritmānas](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md).
  - *Variant (Rāvaṇa as devotee/guru):* Some traditions have the dying Rāvaṇa, a supreme Śiva-bhakta and scholar, give a dharma-discourse; Lakṣmaṇa is sent to learn statecraft at his deathbed [folk/regional, esp. Tamil and oral].
- **Mandodarī's lament; honorable cremation** - Rāvaṇa's chief queen **Mandodarī** mourns; Rāma directs Vibhīṣaṇa to give Rāvaṇa full funeral rites, declaring enmity ends at death — "he was a mighty being, worthy of honor" (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 110–111).
- **Vibhīṣaṇa crowned king of Laṅkā** (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 112).

## VIII. The Reunion and the Fire-Ordeal (sargas 112–121)

- **Sītā summoned** - Hanumān brings word to Sītā in the Aśoka-vana; she is bathed, adorned, and brought before Rāma in a palanquin (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 113–114).
- **Rāma's harsh disavowal** - Rāma, before the assembled hosts, coldly declares he fought to redeem his honor, not for her, and that he cannot take back a wife who dwelt in another's house; he bids her go where she will. Sītā, anguished, asks Lakṣmaṇa to build a pyre (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 115–116).
- **The Agni-parīkṣā (fire-ordeal)** - Sītā, declaring her purity, enters the blazing fire. **Agni himself rises, bearing Sītā unburned**, and testifies to her chastity; the gods (Brahmā, Śiva, Indra, and Daśaratha's spirit) appear and reveal Rāma's divinity. Rāma receives her, saying he never doubted her but had to prove her purity to the world (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 117–118).
  - *Variant (the shadow-Sītā restored — Chāyā/Māyā-Sītā):* Per [Kūrma Purāṇa], [Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa], the *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa* and [Rāmcaritmānas](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md), Rāma had entrusted the **real Sītā to Agni before the abduction**, leaving an illusory double that Rāvaṇa carried off; the fire-ordeal is the device by which **Agni returns the true Sītā** — recasting the ordeal as restoration, not a test, and shielding Sītā from any taint of captivity.
  - *Variant (theology of the ordeal):* Bhakti commentators read the agni-parīkṣā as lokasaṅgraha (a public demonstration for the world's sake), Rāma never having doubted her; rationalist/[scholarly] readings treat it (and its harsher Uttara-Kāṇḍa sequel) as a later patriarchal interpolation.
- **The fallen revived** - The gods grant a boon: the **dead vānaras and ṛkṣas are restored to life** (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 119).

## IX. Return to Ayodhyā and Coronation (sargas 122–128)

- **Flight north in the Puṣpaka** - The fourteen-year exile nearly ended, Rāma (with Sītā, Lakṣmaṇa, Vibhīṣaṇa, Sugrīva, Hanumān, and the chief vānaras) boards Rāvaṇa's reclaimed flying chariot **Puṣpaka-vimāna** and flies to Ayodhyā, Rāma pointing out the bridge, Kiṣkindhā, Ṛṣyamūka, the Daṇḍaka sites, the sages' hermitages (Bharadvāja, Atri), and the rivers below (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 123).
- **Hanumān precedes them; Bharata's vow** - From Bharadvāja's hermitage Hanumān is sent ahead to Nandigrāma to forewarn **Bharata**, who had ruled as regent with Rāma's sandals on the throne and had vowed to enter fire if Rāma did not return on the appointed day (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 125).
- **The reunion at Nandigrāma** - Rāma is reunited with Bharata, Śatrughna, and the mothers; Bharata returns the kingdom (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 126–127).
- **The Rāja-abhiṣeka (coronation)** - Rāma is consecrated **King of Ayodhyā** by Vasiṣṭha and the assembled sages, the vānara and rākṣasa allies honored and dismissed with gifts; Hanumān asks the boon of living as long as Rāma's story endures (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 128).
- **Rāma-rājya** - Rāma's reign inaugurates the archetypal age of perfect dharma — timely rains, no disease or want, no untimely death — the political ideal invoked ever after as *Rāma-rājya* (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 128).
- **Phala-śruti** - The book closes with the fruits of reciting the Rāmāyaṇa (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 128). The story continues — disputedly — into the [Uttara Kāṇḍa].

## X. Major Variants Across Traditions

- **Where the epic ends** - Vālmīki's **Critical Edition closes here** (with the coronation); the *Uttara Kāṇḍa* (Sītā's later banishment, Lava–Kuśa, her descent into the earth, Rāma's departure) is [scholarly] regarded as a later book, though canonical in tradition.
- **[Rāmcaritmānas](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md) (Tulsīdās, Avadhi, 16th c.)** - *Laṅkākāṇḍa* foregrounds bhakti: the floating Name-inscribed stones, the shadow-Sītā (so no agni-parīkṣā stigma), Vibhīṣaṇa-śaraṇāgati as devotional centerpiece; ends with Rāma's homecoming and the *Uttarakāṇḍa* given over largely to devotion rather than Sītā's exile.
- **Kamban's *Irāmāvatāram* (Tamil, 12th c.)** - *Yuddha/Pōr Kāṇṭam* with distinctive Tamil amplifications, ornate battle poetry, and elevated treatment of Rāvaṇa.
- **Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa** - Advaitic/devotional retelling embedding the Chāyā-Sītā doctrine and treating the whole war as Rāma's līlā.
- **Jain & Buddhist Rāmāyaṇas** - In Jain versions (e.g., *Paümacariya*) **Lakṣmaṇa**, not Rāma (who is non-violent), kills Rāvaṇa; Rāvaṇa is a noble but flawed Vidyādhara; no monkeys-as-animals (the "Vānaras" are a human clan). The Buddhist *Dasaratha Jātaka* omits the war and the abduction entirely.
- **Southeast Asian retellings** - The Thai *Ramakien*, Khmer *Reamker*, and others recast the war with local heroes and added episodes (e.g., the mermaid Suvannamaccha obstructing the bridge-building).
