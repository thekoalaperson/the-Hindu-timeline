# Yuddha (Laṅkā) Kāṇḍa — Book of War

> Cosmic address: [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md) → 28th Mahāyuga → **[Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** → the Rāma-avatāra cycle, its climax.

> See also: [Daśāvatāra (Rāma)](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) · [Sūryavaṃśa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) · [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md) · [Itihāsa-Epics corpus](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md)

The sixth and longest book of Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa. The vulgate (Southern) text on valmikiramayan.net has **128 sargas**, and its verse-numbers come to about **5,800 ślokas** (summing the highest verse-number in each sarga). The **Baroda Critical Edition** (CE) gives the book in **116 sargas / 4,436 verses**. That is about two-fifths longer than the next-longest book, Ayodhyā (CE 111 sargas / 3,160 verses). Citations on this page use vulgate numbering unless they are marked CE. The book is called *Yuddha* ("War") or *Laṅkā Kāṇḍa* ("Book of Laṅkā"). It runs from Hanumān's report through the bridging of the ocean, the siege and battles of Laṅkā, the deaths of Kumbhakarṇa, Indrajit and Rāvaṇa, Sītā's fire-ordeal and the flight north in the Puṣpaka, and it ends with Rāma's coronation. The CE prints the *Uttara Kāṇḍa* as a seventh book (100 sargas / 2,690 verses), but most scholars [scholarly] treat it as a later addition. The war-book is therefore widely read as the epic's original close.

**Recension note.** Several vulgate passages used below are **not in the CE's constituted text**, and each is tagged *[vulgate only]* where it occurs. They are: sargas **10–15**, where CE 6.9 (Vibhīṣaṇa's first counsel) passes straight to CE 6.10 = vulgate 16 (Rāvaṇa's rebuke), so the CE lacks Kumbhakarṇa at the council and the Puñjikasthalā curse; the **Āditya-hṛdaya** (105); Rāma's lament *deśe deśe kalatrāṇi* (101.15); and the line about Mahādeva's grace at the bridgehead (123.19–20).

## I. Council, Embassies and the Defection of Vibhīṣaṇa (sargas 1–19)

- **Rāma honors Hanumān; the march south** - Rāma praises Hanumān's deed but despairs of crossing the ocean. Sugrīva rallies him (2), and Hanumān describes Laṅkā's gates, moats and garrisons (3). At an auspicious hour the vānara host marches to the shore (4) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 1–4).
- **Rāvaṇa's war-council** - Rāvaṇa asks his ministers for counsel. They boast that Indrajit alone would be enough, and Prahasta, Durmukha, Vajradaṃṣṭra, Nikumbha and Vajrahanu each offer to destroy the enemy single-handed (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 6–8).
- **Vibhīṣaṇa's counsel rejected in Laṅkā** - **Vibhīṣaṇa**, Rāvaṇa's youngest brother, restrains the champions. Force is for when conciliation, gifts and division have failed (9.8), and Sītā should be returned. The next morning he reports the omens to Rāvaṇa in private (10). In the second council he argues against Prahasta and Indrajit (14–15). Rāvaṇa finally insults him and spurns him (16) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 9–16). In the CE the sequence runs straight from the first counsel to the rebuke (CE 6.9–10).
- **Kumbhakarṇa at the first council** *[vulgate only]* - Kumbhakarṇa has just woken from his six-month sleep (12.11). He reproaches his brother: the abduction should have been debated before it was done, not after (12.29). He then pledges to kill Rāma himself regardless. The rebuke and the loyalty are one speech (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 12).
- **Why Rāvaṇa cannot force Sītā — the Puñjikasthalā curse** *[vulgate only]* - **Mahāpārśva** urges Rāvaṇa to take Sītā by force, and Rāvaṇa explains why he cannot. Long ago he had violated the apsaras **Puñjikasthalā** on her way to Brahmā's abode. **Brahmā** then cursed him: if he ever again took a woman by force, *his head would split into a hundred pieces* (*śatadhā mūrdhā phaliṣyati*, 13.11–15). It is the war-book's own answer to the question audiences ask most, but the CE does not contain it. It is distinct from the Nalakūbara curse of *Uttara* 26, whose term is **seven** pieces.
- **Vibhīṣaṇa's defection (śaraṇāgati)** - Spurned, Vibhīṣaṇa flies across the sea with four companions and asks Rāma for refuge (17.12–17, 17.26). Rāma asks his chiefs for their views. **Sugrīva, Aṅgada, Śarabha, Jāmbavān and Mainda** all distrust the enemy's brother, and **Hanumān alone** argues for accepting him (17). Rāma cites the dove that gave hospitality to the fowler who came to it for refuge (18.24). He then gives his pledge: whoever comes to him *even once* and says "I am yours" receives safety from all beings — *sakṛd eva prapannāya tavāsmīti ca yācate* (18.33). He would grant it "be it Vibhīṣaṇa or Rāvaṇa himself" (18.34). This is the touchstone verse of the doctrine of *śaraṇāgati*. Rāma accepts Vibhīṣaṇa and consecrates him in advance as king of Laṅkā (19) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 17–19; the vow is also at CE 6.12.20).
  - *Variant ([Rāmcaritmānas](recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md)):* Tulsīdās makes Vibhīṣaṇa the model devotee whom the Lord receives unconditionally, and the episode becomes a major bhakti set-piece.

## II. Bridging the Ocean — Nala-Setu / Rāma-Setu (sargas 20–23)

- **Rāvaṇa's embassy to Sugrīva** - On Śārdūla's advice Rāvaṇa sends **Śuka**, who takes the form of a bird (20.13), to detach Sugrīva from Rāma. The vānaras seize and maul him. He protests that envoys may not be killed (20.17), and Rāma stops them (20.19). Sugrīva sends back a defiant answer. Śuka is held until the army has crossed and is released on Rāma's order (24.22–24) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 20, 24).
- **Rāma's penance before the sea** - Rāma lies on kuśa grass for **three nights** (21.10–11) asking Sāgara for passage. Ignored, he looses fierce arrows that churn the sea (21.26). In sarga 22 he fixes an arrow charged with the **brahmāstra** (*brāhmeṇāstreṇa*, 22.5) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 21–22).
- **Sāgara appears and names Nala** - The ocean-god appears. He cannot change his nature, but he offers to **bear the bridge**: he will hold it up, and keep his crocodiles from the army while it crosses (22.28, 22.45). He names the builder: the vānara **Nala**, son of Viśvakarman, who is his father's equal (22.44). Nala confirms that Viśvakarman had given his mother a boon on Mandara, "a son equal to me" (22.49–50). The boon is **craft**, not buoyancy. Sāgara points the unspent weapon at **Drumakulya** to his north, where Ābhīra robbers drink his waters (22.31–33). The arrow turns that land into the **Maru desert**, and Rāma then blesses the desert with pasture, herbs and health (22.34–41) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 22).
- **The Setu built in five days** - Under Nala's direction the vānaras hurl trees, rocks and mountain-tops into the sea and stretch cords a hundred yojanas long to keep the line straight (22.60–64). They build 14, 20, 21, 22 and 23 yojanas on the five days (22.66–70). The bridge is **100 yojanas long and 10 wide** (22.74), and the army crosses (22.77, 22.85) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 22).
  - *Note (nothing floats):* Vālmīki's sarga 22 has **no floating stone and no buoyancy of any kind**. The rocks and trees go into the water, Nala's engineering holds them, and Sāgara bears the weight from below. The bridge is a feat of construction, not of levitation.
  - *Variant (the stones float — Tulsīdās):* in the [*Rāmcaritmānas*](recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md) Sāgara says that Nīla and Nala received a sage's blessing in childhood, so that at their touch even heavy mountains "will float on the sea by your glory" (*tinha keṁ parasa kieṁ giri bhāre | tarihahiṁ jaladhi pratāpa tumhāre*, Sundar 60.1). The Laṅkā Kāṇḍ repeats that by Rāma's might the sinking rocks became rafts (Laṅkā 3.4–5). The best-known image, *Rāma* **written on the stones**, is not in these verses. It belongs to oral and devotional tradition. [devotional / oral tradition]
  - *Variant (the squirrel):* A widely told [folk tradition] adds a small squirrel that carries pebbles and rolls in sand to help. Rāma strokes its back and leaves the three white stripes seen on Indian palm squirrels today. It is not in Vālmīki.
  - *Variant (Śiva at Rāmeśvaram):* The vulgate's aerial tour has Rāma show Sītā the bridgehead where "Mahādeva formerly bestowed his grace" on him (*atra pūrvaṃ mahādevaḥ prasādam akarot prabhuḥ*). The same verses name the place **Setubandha**, a tīrtha that destroys great sins (123.19–21) *[vulgate only; CE 6.111.9–10 lacks the Mahādeva line]*. Vālmīki mentions **no liṅga**. Tulsīdās has Rāma install and worship a Śiva-liṅga there before the crossing (*liṃga thāpi bidhivata kari pūjā*, *Mānas* Laṅkā 2.3) and calls it **Rāmeśvara** (3.1). The Rāmeśvaram [jyotirliṅga](../../06-sacred-geography/) tradition is carried and developed by the sthala-purāṇas; Vālmīki is not its source.
- **The portents read on the far shore** - Landed, Rāma reads the omens to Lakṣmaṇa: dust-laden winds, a trembling earth, clouds raining blood, a dark stain on the sun, a red-ringed moon, vultures flying low and jackals howling. He reads them as a coming destruction of the heroes "of bears, monkeys and rākṣasas" alike (23.3–12), and leads the army toward Laṅkā (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 23). Sarga 23 has no throbbing eye or arm.

## III. Suvela, the Spies, and the Opening of the Siege (sargas 25–50)

- **The spy-catalogue** - Rāvaṇa sends **Śuka and Sāraṇa** to scout the camp in disguise. Vibhīṣaṇa unmasks them and Rāma lets them go. Back in Laṅkā, Sāraṇa (26–27) and then Śuka (28) name the vānara host to Rāvaṇa from his palace roof, chief by chief: the roll of commanders, their fathers, their strengths and their numbers. Rāvaṇa dismisses them for praising the enemy (29). A further party of spies under **Śārdūla** is caught, released, and returns with the same answer (29–30). It is the epic's own order-of-battle, delivered by the enemy (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 25–30).
- **Rāvaṇa's deception of Sītā** - Rāvaṇa learns that the army is on Suvela (31). He has the sorcerer **Vidyujjihva** conjure an **illusory severed head of Rāma and his bow and arrows** (31.6–9) and shows them to Sītā as proof of Rāma's death. She faints and laments (32). **Saramā** comforts her and exposes the illusion (33), then at Sītā's request overhears the council for her (34) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 31–34).
- **Mālyavān's counsel** - Only now, with the enemy at the gates, does the aged **Mālyavān** argue from omens and statecraft that Sītā must be returned and peace made. The text calls him Rāvaṇa's *mātuḥ paitāmahaḥ* (35.6), usually translated "maternal grandfather". The Uttara genealogy makes him Sumālī's elder brother, and so Rāvaṇa's maternal great-uncle. Rāvaṇa abuses him and posts Prahasta and the others at the four gates (36). His counsel comes **after the crossing**, not in the earlier Vibhīṣaṇa debate (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 35–36).
- **Rāma occupies Suvela** - Vibhīṣaṇa's four counsellors spy out the defences of the gates (37). Rāma and the chiefs climb **Mount Suvela**, spend the night there and survey Laṅkā's gardens and mansions from the summit (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 38–39).
- **Sugrīva's leap at Rāvaṇa** - From the summit Rāma sees Rāvaṇa above a city gate. **Sugrīva** springs onto him, **pulls the crown from his head and flings it to the ground** (40.11), wrestles him, and escapes back through the air. Rāma rebukes him: such rashness without consultation does not become a king (41.2) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 40–41).
- **Aṅgada's embassy** - Rāma posts his chiefs at the four gates, taking the northern gate, which Rāvaṇa holds, himself. He sends the prince **Aṅgada** to demand Sītā (41.61). Rāvaṇa orders him seized. Aṅgada lets four rākṣasas take hold of him, then leaps onto the palace with them clinging to his arms, shakes them off, **breaks the palace roof**, proclaims his name and flies back (41.85–91) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 41).
  - *Variant (the planted foot):* in the [*Rāmcaritmānas*](recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md) Aṅgada plants his foot in the court and dares anyone to move it. None of the champions can, and they sit back down with heads bowed (*Mānas* Laṅkā 34.4–34ka). The scene is popular in theatre and is not in Vālmīki.
- **The first assault** - The vānaras storm Laṅkā's gates and fight paired duels (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 42–43).
- **Aṅgada breaks Indrajit's chariot** - In the night-battle **Aṅgada strikes down Indrajit's horses and charioteer** (44.28). Indrajit abandons the chariot and vanishes (44.29), furious at the defeat (44.34). The humiliation leads straight to the nāga-pāśa (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 44).
- **Indrajit's first nāgāstra** - Fighting **invisible**, Indrajit binds Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa with serpent-arrows (nāga-pāśa). Both fall as if slain, the army is dismayed, and Indrajit boasts to Rāvaṇa (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 45–46).
- **Sītā flown over the corpse-field** - Rāvaṇa has Sītā carried in the Puṣpaka to see the two brothers lying among the fallen. She laments that the soothsayers had promised her sons and no widowhood (48.2). **Trijaṭā**, once again the one rākṣasī who steadies her, reads the field correctly: the Puṣpaka "would not carry you if these two had lost their lives" (48.25), and she brings Sītā back to the grove (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 47–48).
- **Suṣeṇa proposes the herbs; Garuḍa frees them** - Before any herb is fetched, the vānara physician **Suṣeṇa** recalls how Bṛhaspati healed the gods with herbs. He proposes sending Hanumān to the Milk Ocean, to two mountains named **Candra and Droṇa** where the amṛta was churned, for the herbs *saṃjīvakaraṇī* and *viśalyā* (50.26–32; CE 6.40.26–32). Before anyone goes, **Garuḍa** arrives. The serpents flee, his touch heals the brothers' wounds, and he names himself as Garutmān, Rāma's friend, "dear as life, moving outside" (50.36–46). He tells Rāma not to ask the cause of the friendship until the war is won (50.57) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 49–50).

## IV. The Fall of the Rākṣasa Champions (sargas 51–67)

- **Dhūmrākṣa, Vajradaṃṣṭra, Akampana, Prahasta** - Rāvaṇa's commanders are killed in successive sorties: Dhūmrākṣa by Hanumān (52), Vajradaṃṣṭra by Aṅgada (54), Akampana by Hanumān with a tree (56), and the chief general **Prahasta** by Nīla (58) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 51–58).
- **Rāvaṇa takes the field** - Rāvaṇa sallies himself. He and Hanumān trade blows that make each reel, palm for palm and then Rāvaṇa's fist (59.60–62, 59.67–69). He then fells Lakṣmaṇa with a **śakti given him by Brahmā**. This is the first of the two lances that strike Lakṣmaṇa in this book; the second comes in §VI. Rāvaṇa, who could lift Himavat, Mandara and Meru, cannot lift the fallen Lakṣmaṇa, while Hanumān carries him off easily "because of friendship and devotion" (59). Hanumān then offers Rāma his back, "as Viṣṇu rides Garuḍa", and Rāma fights the chariot-borne king from it. Rāma shatters Rāvaṇa's bow, chariot and diadem, then spares him: go back to Laṅkā, rest, and return with your bow (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 59).
- **The waking of Kumbhakarṇa** - Rāvaṇa orders his colossal brother **Kumbhakarṇa** roused with drums, elephants and mountains of food (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 60).
- **The sleep — three primary accounts, kept apart** - Vālmīki explains the sleep **twice**, in two different ways, and the Mahābhārata gives a third account. The corpus keeps them separate:
  - *The war-book's account (Yuddha 61):* Vibhīṣaṇa tells Rāma that from birth Kumbhakarṇa devoured thousands of creatures. When Indra struck him with the vajra, he tore a tusk from Airāvata and wounded Indra with it. Brahmā then **cursed** him to "sleep as if dead from today" (*mṛtakalpaḥ śayiṣyase*, 61.24). Rāvaṇa pleaded that a grandson should not be cursed like this, and Brahmā fixed the term: **six months asleep, one day awake** (61.28). There is **no boon-scene, no Sarasvatī and no pun** here.
  - *The Uttara's account (Uttara 10; CE 7.10.31–41):* When the brothers take their boons, the gods ask Brahmā to confuse Kumbhakarṇa "under the guise of a boon" (*varavyājena moho 'smai dīyatām*). **Sarasvatī enters his mouth**, and he asks to "sleep for many years" (*svaptuṃ varṣāṇy anekāni*). Afterwards he wonders what words have fallen from his mouth. There is no commutation clause and no named intended word.
  - *The Mahābhārata's account (Rāmopākhyāna, BORI 3.259.28):* "his mind seized by *tamas*, he chose a great sleep" (*sa vavre mahatīṃ nidrāṃ tamasā grastacetanaḥ*). There is no goddess and no slip.
  - *Variant (the pun):* the famous wordplay, in which he meant to ask for *nirdevatvam* (the unmaking of the gods) and said *nidrāvatvam* (sleep), is **not in any of these three texts**. Modern reference works repeat it, and it belongs to later retelling. The popular form in which he means **Indra's seat** (*indrāsana*) and says **a bed of sleep** (*nidrāsana*) is a further gloss on the same idea. [later tradition / folk]
- **Kumbhakarṇa's rampage and death** - Kumbhakarṇa repeats his rebuke to Rāvaṇa (63) and rebukes the minister Mahodara for flattery (64–65). He then goes loyally to war, devouring vānaras who come back out of his nostrils and ears (67.35–36). **Hanumān breaks his iron spike over his knee** (67.63–64). Kumbhakarṇa fells Sugrīva with a mountain-peak and carries him off toward the city (67.67–72). Sugrīva comes to and **tears off his ears with his nails and his nose with his teeth** (67.87–90), then escapes. **Rāma** severs Kumbhakarṇa's arms, the second with an arrow charged with Indra's weapon (67.161), then his feet with crescent arrows (67.163–164), and finally his head. The body falls into the sea (67.157–173) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 62–67).
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata):* in the Rāmopākhyāna it is **Lakṣmaṇa** who kills Kumbhakarṇa. He cuts off arms that multiply, then burns him with the **brahmāstra** (BORI 3.271.10–17).

## V. Indrajit's Sorceries and the Sañjīvanī (sargas 68–91)

- **Death of Rāvaṇa's sons and kin** - The champions fall in a named order. **Narāntaka** dies by Aṅgada's fist (69). In sarga 70, **Devāntaka** and **Triśiras** fall to Hanumān (Triśiras's three heads cut off with his own sword), **Mahodara** to Nīla, and **Mahāpārśva** (called *Matta*) to Ṛṣabha, using the rākṣasa's own mace. The giant **Atikāya**, Rāvaṇa's son by Dhānyamālinī, is armoured by Brahmā's boon and dies by Lakṣmaṇa's brahmāstra (71). Rāvaṇa's grief closes the sequence (72) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 68–72). This Mahodara and this Mahāpārśva are sent to guard Rāvaṇa's sons and are called his **brothers** (69–70).
  - *Note (duplicated names):* a **second** Mahodara and a **second** Mahāpārśva fight and die in Rāvaṇa's final sortie at *Yuddha* 97–98. The **Virūpākṣa** killed at 96 shares his name with one of the five senāpatis Hanumān killed at *Sundara* 46. The vulgate reuses these names for distinct rākṣasas and never flags it. Do not merge the bearers.
- **Indrajit's brahmāstra fells the host** - Indrajit performs a fire-rite on the field, then fights unseen and lays low the whole vānara army with the brahmāstra, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa among them (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 73).
- **The first herb-flight** - **Jāmbavān** sends Hanumān north over the ocean to the Himālaya. Between the golden **Mount Ṛṣabha** and the peak of **Kailāsa** stands a blazing "herb-mountain" (*oṣadhiparvata*, 74.30–31), crowned by four herbs: **mṛtasañjīvanī, viśalyakaraṇī, sauvarṇakaraṇī and sandhānī** (74.33). On his arrival the herbs, knowing a seeker has come, **hide themselves** (74.64). Hanumān angrily threatens the mountain, tears off its peak and carries it back (74.65–68). The fragrance alone heals the brothers and revives the fallen vānaras (74.73–74). The rākṣasa dead do not revive, because Rāvaṇa had their bodies thrown into the sea from the start, so the enemy could not count his losses (74.75). Hanumān returns the peak to the Himālaya (74.77) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 74).
  - *Note (Droṇa and the named mountain):* the mountain of this flight is named only as "the herb-mountain". **Droṇa is a Vālmīki name**, though. It is one of the two Milk-Ocean mountains, Candra and Droṇa, where Suṣeṇa proposes to send Hanumān at 50.31, before Garuḍa makes the trip unnecessary. "**Droṇagiri**" as the name of the Himālayan peak that Hanumān actually carries comes from later tradition and the Uttarakhand sthala-legend, and it is the standard name in retellings. [later tradition]
  - *Variant (Bharata's arrow — Tulsīdās):* in the [*Rāmcaritmānas*](recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md) Bharata, keeping watch in Ayodhyā, takes the airborne Hanumān for a demon and brings him down with a headless arrow. Hanumān falls crying "Rāma". Bharata then offers to send him back to Laṅkā on that arrow (*Mānas* Laṅkā dohā 58–59). The episode is not in Vālmīki.
  - *Variant (Kālanemi):* in the *Mānas* Rāvaṇa sends the rākṣasa **Kālanemi**, disguised as a sage, to delay Hanumān on the way (*Mānas* Laṅkā 56–57). He has the same role in the [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md). The episode is not in Vālmīki.
  - *Note (two flights, one mountain):* Vālmīki has **two** herb-flights, in different sargas. One is prescribed by Jāmbavān for the brahmāstra wounds (74). The other is prescribed by Suṣeṇa for Lakṣmaṇa's śakti wound (101). In the second, Suṣeṇa sends Hanumān to "the herb-mountain **which Jāmbavān described to you before**" (101.31–32; CE 6.89.14). It is the **same** mountain both times. Popular retellings fold the two flights into one; the *Mānas* has only one. The corpus keeps them apart.
- **The night-burning of Laṅkā** - With the brothers revived, **Sugrīva** orders the attack renewed at nightfall. The vānaras go to the city with torches and set it alight, thousands of houses burning at once. This is the second burning of Laṅkā, done by the whole army rather than by one tail (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 75).
- **Kumbha and Nikumbha** - The night-fighting kills Kampana, Prajaṅgha, Śoṇitākṣa and Yūpākṣa at the hands of Aṅgada, Dvivida and Mainda (76). Kumbhakarṇa's sons come out to avenge their father. **Kumbha** is killed by Sugrīva (76). **Nikumbha**, wielding an iron club, is killed by Hanumān (77) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 76–77).
- **Makarākṣa** - **Makarākṣa**, son of Khara, the man Rāma killed at Janasthāna, comes to collect the debt. He challenges Rāma and hurls a spike **given him by Rudra** (79.32). Rāma breaks it with four arrows and kills him with the **āgneyāstra** (79.34–38) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 78–79).
- **Indrajit's second invisible attack** - Indrajit returns, protected by a brahmāstra-rite, and showers the brothers with arrows from a darkened sky. Lakṣmaṇa wants to loose the brahmāstra and kill every rākṣasa. Rāma forbids it, because the whole race should not die for one man (80.37–38). He vows instead to burn Indrajit himself wherever he hides (80.42) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 80).
- **Māyā-Sītā slain** - To break the army's spirit, Indrajit conjures an **illusory Sītā (Māyā-Sītā)** on his chariot and cuts her down before Hanumān's eyes (81). Hanumān falls back, and Indrajit goes to Nikumbhilā (82). Rāma faints at the news. Lakṣmaṇa, holding him, delivers a bitter speech on how little righteousness seems to protect the righteous (83). Vibhīṣaṇa then reveals the sorcery: Rāvaṇa would never kill the woman he covets (84) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 81–84).
  - *Variant (the "real" shadow-Sītā doctrine):* several traditions — the *Kūrma Purāṇa*, the *Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa*, the [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md) and the [*Rāmcaritmānas*](recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md) — hold that the *true* Sītā was hidden with Agni before the abduction. A **Chāyā/Māyā-Sītā** stood in her place all along and is "burned away" at the agni-parīkṣā to restore the real Sītā. See §VIII.
- **Nikumbhilā, and the yajña** - **Nikumbhilā** is the sanctuary and fire-altar inside Laṅkā. It is the city's sacrificial ground and Indrajit's private one. There, with **Uśanas (Śukra)** officiating, he had performed the **seven sacrifices** (agniṣṭoma, aśvamedha, bahusuvarṇaka, rājasūya, gomedha, vaiṣṇava, and the māheśvara that men cannot undertake). These won him the sky-going chariot, the māyā that hides him in darkness, and inexhaustible quivers (*Uttara* 25). Here he pours his oblations, and the fire flares up as it consumes **flesh and blood** (82.24–28). Vibhīṣaṇa gives the boon's terms. By his tapas Indrajit won the **Brahmaśiras** weapon and horses that go where he wills (85.12). Brahmā had told him that the enemy who strikes him **before he reaches Nikumbhilā and completes the fire-offering** will be his death (85.14). Vibhīṣaṇa grew up in the city and is the one man in Rāma's camp who knows the place (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 84–86).
- **Indrajit slain by Lakṣmaṇa** - Vibhīṣaṇa leads Lakṣmaṇa, with Hanumān, to the grove. He points out the **banyan** where Indrajit makes his offerings to the bhūtas, and tells Lakṣmaṇa to strike before Indrajit reaches it (87.3–6). Indrajit calls his uncle a traitor to his own blood, and Vibhīṣaṇa answers that Rāvaṇa's vices drove him out (87). The duel lasts until Lakṣmaṇa kills the charioteer and the vānaras kill the horses (88–89). Indrajit comes back in a second chariot. Lakṣmaṇa then charges an arrow with **Indra's weapon** on an oath: "if Rāma son of Daśaratha is righteous and true to his word… then slay this son of Rāvaṇa" (*dharmātmā satyasandhaś ca rāmo dāśarathir yadi*, 90.71). The arrow cuts off Indrajit's head (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 87–90).
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata):* in the Rāmopākhyāna Lakṣmaṇa kills Indrajit with three fiery arrows: one takes the bow-arm, one the other arm, and one the head (BORI 3.273.21–23). There is no Nikumbhilā rite.
  - *Variant (Sulocanā / satī):* later texts and theatre add **Sulocanā**, Indrajit's widow and a nāga-princess, who performs satī while the severed head speaks. It is a popular [late text]/folk cycle (*Sati Sulochana*) and not in Vālmīki. She does not appear in the *Rāmcaritmānas* either. The cycle belongs to regional Rāmāyaṇa, Marathi and Rāmlīlā material.
- **Suṣeṇa's field-surgery** - Lakṣmaṇa returns to Rāma, who embraces him. **Suṣeṇa** gives Lakṣmaṇa and the wounded chiefs a herb to inhale, and they are restored (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 91).

## VI. Lakṣmaṇa, the Maya-Śakti, and the Second Sañjīvanī (sargas 92–101)

- **Rāvaṇa's grief; Supārśva restrains him** - Devastated by Indrajit's death, Rāvaṇa takes up his sword and starts for the Aśoka-vana to kill Sītā. She sees him coming and laments. The minister **Supārśva** stops him (92.62ff). How can the younger brother of Kubera kill a woman, abandoning dharma? Let him take his anger out on Rāma in battle, and win Sītā by killing him (92.63–67). Rāvaṇa turns back (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 92).
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata):* in the Rāmopākhyāna the minister who stops him is **Avindhya**. A captive woman is as good as dead already; "kill her husband, and she is killed" (BORI 3.273.26–32).
- **The gāndharva-astra** - Rāma cuts through Rāvaṇa's massed troops with the **gāndharva-astra**. Its illusion bewilders the rākṣasas so that they cannot see where Rāma is (93.26) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 93).
- **The rākṣasīs' lament** - The women of Laṅkā mourn their husbands and kin. They blame **Śūrpaṇakhā** as the root of the war, and Rāvaṇa for ignoring Vibhīṣaṇa (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 94).
- **The last champions** - In Rāvaṇa's final sortie **Virūpākṣa** (96) and the second **Mahodara** (97) are killed by Sugrīva, and the second **Mahāpārśva** by Aṅgada's fist (98). The king is left without his last commanders (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 95–98).
- **Lakṣmaṇa struck by the śakti** - Rāma and Rāvaṇa duel with missiles (99). Lakṣmaṇa breaks Rāvaṇa's bow, and Vibhīṣaṇa kills his horses. Rāvaṇa hurls a spear at Vibhīṣaṇa, Lakṣmaṇa cuts it down, and the enraged king turns on Lakṣmaṇa. He hurls a **śakti** that was **made by Maya the dānava** through sorcery: unfailing, hung with **eight loudly clanging bells** (*aṣṭaghaṇṭāṃ mahāsvanām… mayena māyāvihitām*, 100.30–31; CE 6.88.30), and roaring like Indra's thunderbolt. It pierces Lakṣmaṇa's chest and he falls (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 100).
  - *Note (not the Vāsavī Śakti):* Vālmīki names no *Vāsavī Śakti* here. The **Vāsavī Śakti**, Indra's single-use javelin, belongs to the *Mahābhārata*. There Indra gives it to **Karṇa** in exchange for his armour, and Karṇa uses it up on Ghaṭotkaca; the corpus's own [divyāstra register](../../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/divyastras-and-divine-weapons.md) places it there. Rāvaṇa's two lances in this book are **Brahmā-given** (*Yuddha* 59) and **Maya-made** (*Yuddha* 100), and neither is Indra's.
  - *Variant (Tulsīdās):* in the *Mānas* the lance that fells Lakṣmaṇa is thrown by **Meghanāda (Indrajit)**: the *bīraghātinī sāṁgī* (*Mānas* Laṅkā 54.4). It leads to the single herb-flight, Kālanemi and Bharata's arrow (55–59). Rāvaṇa's own Brahmā-given lance strikes Lakṣmaṇa later in the Laṅkākāṇḍ (around dohā 83).
- **Second herb-flight** - Rāma laments over his brother. Wives and kinsmen can be found in any land, he says, but nowhere a brother born of the same mother (*deśe deśe kalatrāṇi deśe deśe ca bāndhavāḥ | taṃ tu deśaṃ na paśyāmi yatra bhrātā sahodaraḥ*, 101.15 *[vulgate only]*). **Suṣeṇa** finds that Lakṣmaṇa is still alive. He sends Hanumān to the **same herb-mountain Jāmbavān had described**, for the **viśalyakaraṇī** that grows on its southern peak, together with **saṃjīvakaraṇī** and **sandhānī** (101.31–33). This time Hanumān cannot recognise the herbs (101.34). He shakes the peak three times, uproots it and brings it whole (101.38–42). Suṣeṇa crushes the herb, holds it to Lakṣmaṇa's nose, and Lakṣmaṇa rises (101.43–45) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 101). The VR Sanskrit as printed names three herbs. The CE names four, adding *sauvarṇakaraṇī* (CE 6.89.15–16). The mountain-name "Mahodaya" that some translations give is a gloss: it is not in the Sanskrit of either text.

## VII. The Slaying of Rāvaṇa (sargas 102–112)

- **Indra's chariot and Mātali** - Seeing Rāma on foot against the chariot-borne Rāvaṇa, Indra sends his own **celestial chariot**, driven by his charioteer **Mātali**, with armour, arrows and a spear (102.6–18). Rāma answers Rāvaṇa's gāndharva and divine missiles in kind (102.20). He meets the serpent-missile with the **Garuḍa-missile** (102.24–25) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 102). In the next exchange Rāma upbraids Rāvaṇa for the abduction, and Rāvaṇa's charioteer drives the stunned king off the field. Rāvaṇa rebukes him, accepts his reasons, and orders him back (103–104).
- **The Āditya-Hṛdayam** *[vulgate only]* - As Rāma tires, the sage **Agastya** appears and teaches him the **Āditya-Hṛdayam**, a hymn to Sūrya, for victory (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 105). The stotra is recited in its own right. It is not in the CE's constituted text. CE book 6 never has Agastya teach the hymn: he appears as the giver of the arrow (CE 6.97.4) and is mentioned in passing at 6.103.14 and 6.111.22.
- **The regenerating heads** - After omens for both sides (106) the duel resumes. Rāma cuts down Rāvaṇa's standard. **Each head he severs grows back**, and he cuts off a hundred in all (107.58). Rāma wonders why the arrows that killed Mārīca, Khara and Dūṣaṇa, Virādha and Kabandha, pierced the sāla trees and Vālin, and shook the ocean, fail against this one (107.60). The battle goes on for **seven nights** (*saptarātram*, 107.65) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 107).
- **The Brahmā-arrow to the heart** - **Mātali** reminds Rāma that the hour of Rāvaṇa's destruction, foretold by the gods, has come, and tells him to discharge the missile presided over by Brahmā (108.1–2). Rāma takes up the arrow **that Agastya had given him** (108.3; CE 6.97.4). Agastya had handed it over in the forest years before, as a "Brahmā-given best of arrows" (CE 3.11.30). **Brahmā made it for Indra** (108.5). Wind is in its feathers, fire and sun at its point, its shaft is of ether, and it has the weight of Meru and Mandara (108.6). Rāma charges it with mantras and looses it. It strikes Rāvaṇa's chest (108.17) and **tears out his heart** (108.18). Then it sinks into the earth (108.19), and returns, blood-smeared and its work done, silently to Rāma's quiver (108.20). Rāvaṇa falls from his chariot with his bow (108.21–22). The gods rain flowers (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 108; CE 6.97).
  - *Note (no amṛta, no navel):* sarga 108 contains **no nectar and no navel**. The shot is to the chest, the killing detail is the heart, and the only counsel comes from Mātali. Nothing in Vālmīki makes Rāvaṇa's life depend on amṛta stored in his body, and nothing makes Vibhīṣaṇa reveal a bodily secret.
  - *Variant (the amṛta in the navel):* in the famous version Vibhīṣaṇa tells Rāma that **nectar lies in the pool of Rāvaṇa's navel** and keeps him alive (*nābhikuṃḍa piyūṣa basa yākeṃ*). It comes from [Tulsīdās's *Rāmcaritmānas*](recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md) (*Mānas* Laṅkā 102.3). One arrow dries the navel and the rest take his heads and arms (102–103). The regional and stage retellings that follow Tulsīdās carry the theme of the brother who knows the secret. It is the best-known telling of Rāvaṇa's death, and it is not Vālmīki's. [devotional / regional]
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata):* in the Rāmopākhyāna Rāma's brahmāstra burns Rāvaṇa with chariot, horses and charioteer. His body's elements, flesh and blood are consumed, and "not even ash was seen" (BORI 3.274.28–31).
  - *Variant (Rāvaṇa as devotee/guru):* some traditions have the dying Rāvaṇa, a supreme Śiva-bhakta and scholar, give a discourse on dharma, and Lakṣmaṇa is sent to his deathbed to learn statecraft [folk/regional, esp. Tamil and oral].
- **Laments; honorable cremation** - **Vibhīṣaṇa** mourns his brother. Rāma tells him that hostility ends with death and their purpose is achieved: "let his rites be performed; he is mine as he is yours" (*maraṇāntāni vairāṇi nirvṛttaṃ naḥ prayojanam | kriyatām asya saṃskāro mamāpy eṣa yathā tava*, 109.25; CE 6.99.39). Rāvaṇa's consorts mourn him (110). His chief queen **Mandodarī** recognises in his killer Viṣṇu himself and names the abduction as the crime that destroyed him (111). Rāvaṇa is cremated with full rites (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 109–111).
- **Vibhīṣaṇa crowned king of Laṅkā** - Mātali returns to heaven with Indra's chariot (112.4–5). At Rāma's order Lakṣmaṇa consecrates Vibhīṣaṇa with sea-water before the rākṣasas and vānaras (112.9–16). Hanumān is sent with the news to Sītā (112.24–26) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 112).

## VIII. The Reunion and the Fire-Ordeal (sargas 113–121)

- **Sītā summoned; her mercy to the rākṣasīs** - Hanumān brings Sītā word of the victory and asks leave to kill the rākṣasīs who had threatened her (113.30–38). **She forbids it.** They were servants acting on their king's orders (113.40), and her suffering was the fruit of her own past acts and fate (113.41–42). She gives him an old maxim that a **bear** spoke to a **tiger**, from a tale of a tiger, a hunter and a bear: a noble being does not repay evil with evil (*na paraḥ pāpam ādatte*, 113.44–45). It is the moral high point of her portrayal in the war-book (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 113). Vibhīṣaṇa then brings her, bathed and adorned, before Rāma (114).
- **Rāma's harsh disavowal** - Before the assembled hosts Rāma declares that he fought to wipe out the insult to himself, not for her sake (115.13–15). He cannot take back a wife who has lived in another man's house, Rāvaṇa's touch and gaze upon her (115.19–20). She may go where she likes, and may even set her mind on Lakṣmaṇa or Bharata, or on Śatrughna, Sugrīva or Vibhīṣaṇa (115.18, 115.22–23). Sītā answers that he speaks to her "like a common man to a common woman" (*prākṛtaḥ prākṛtām iva*, 116.5). Smitten by false blame, she asks Lakṣmaṇa to build a pyre, and he reads Rāma's assent in his face (116.18–21). She calls on Agni, witness of the world, to protect her if her heart has never strayed from Rāma (116.25), and enters the fire (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 115–116).
- **"I think of myself as a man"** - The gods descend and reproach Rāma, who is the Lord, for ignoring Sītā as she enters the fire. Rāma replies with the epic's most-quoted self-description: **"I think of myself as a man, Rāma the son of Daśaratha"** (*ātmānaṃ mānuṣaṃ manye rāmaṃ daśarathātmajam*, 117.11; CE 6.105.10). He asks them to say who he truly is, and Brahmā praises him as Nārāyaṇa. The avatāra is revealed to him, not claimed by him (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 117).
- **The Agni-parīkṣā (fire-ordeal)** - **Agni rises from the pyre carrying Sītā unharmed**, gives her to Rāma, and testifies that she never strayed in word, mind, thought or glance (118.5–6). Rāma accepts her. He says he knew her to be pure, but if he had taken her back untested the world would have called him a fool ruled by desire, so he let her enter the fire to convince the three worlds (118.14–20) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 118).
  - *Variant (the shadow-Sītā restored — Chāyā/Māyā-Sītā):* in the *Kūrma Purāṇa*, the *Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa*, the [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md) and the [*Rāmcaritmānas*](recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md), Rāma had entrusted the **real Sītā to Agni before the abduction**, and Rāvaṇa carried off an illusory double. The fire-ordeal is how **Agni gives back the true Sītā**. This recasts the ordeal as a restoration rather than a test, and shields Sītā from any taint of captivity.
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata):* in the BORI constituted text of the Rāmopākhyāna, Rāma rejects Sītā, and **Sītā does not enter fire**. She swears by the wind, fire, water, sky and earth. Brahmā, Indra, Agni, Vāyu, Yama, Varuṇa, Kubera, the seven ṛṣis and Daśaratha appear, and **Vāyu**, followed by the other gods, testifies to her purity (BORI 3.275.10–30). The pyre-entry is found only in a passage that the critical edition relegates to an appendix (Appendix I, no. 28, printed after 3.275.16).
  - *Variant (theology of the ordeal):* bhakti commentators read the agni-parīkṣā as lokasaṅgraha, a public demonstration for the world's sake by a Rāma who never doubted her. Rationalist and [scholarly] readings treat it, and its harsher Uttara-Kāṇḍa sequel, as a later patriarchal interpolation. The CE nonetheless keeps the ordeal in its constituted text (CE 6.104–106).
- **Daśaratha appears; Kaikeyī's repudiation withdrawn** - Śiva tells Rāma to look, and **Daśaratha** stands before him in a celestial chariot, freed from grief by his son's victory. Rāma asks him for one thing: to take back the curse with which he had renounced **Kaikeyī and her son** (*kuru prasādaṃ dharmajña kaikeyyā bharatasya ca*, 119.25). Daśaratha grants it, blesses Lakṣmaṇa and Sītā, and departs (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 119).
- **The fallen revived** - **Indra** offers a boon. Rāma asks that every vānara who died for him without counting death be restored to life and to his own people. Wherever the vānaras live, let flowers, roots, fruit and clear rivers be found even out of season (120.8–10). Indra says the boon is a great one but that he has never spoken twice, and grants it (120.12–16). The dead vānaras rise as if from sleep (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 120).
- **Rāma declines Laṅkā's hospitality** - Vibhīṣaṇa begs Rāma to stay. Rāma refuses, eager to see Bharata and his mothers, and Vibhīṣaṇa brings the Puṣpaka (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 121).

## IX. Return to Ayodhyā and Coronation (sargas 122–128)

- **Flight north in the Puṣpaka** - Vibhīṣaṇa honours the vānaras with gifts. At their request, Rāma takes them, and Vibhīṣaṇa, along to see the coronation (122). From the air Rāma shows Sītā the battlefield, the Nala-setu, the Setubandha, Kiṣkindhā, Ṛṣyamūka, Pampā, Janasthāna, Pañcavaṭī and the hermitages (123). They land at **Bharadvāja's** āśrama on the fifth day of the bright fortnight, the fourteen years now complete (124.1). The sage grants Rāma's request that every tree on the road to Ayodhyā bear fruit out of season (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 122–124).
- **Hanumān goes ahead; Bharata's joy** - Rāma sends Hanumān ahead to Guha at Śṛṅgaverapura and then to **Bharata** at Nandigrāma, telling him to watch Bharata's face for any wish to keep the throne (125). Bharata has ruled as regent with Rāma's sandals on the throne. He had vowed to enter fire if he did not see Rāma when the fourteenth year was complete (*Ayodhyā* 112, *[vulgate only]*). Overjoyed, he offers Hanumān a hundred thousand cows, a hundred villages and sixteen brides (125.43–45). Hanumān tells him the whole story of the forest years (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 125–126).
- **The reunion at Nandigrāma** - The Puṣpaka lands. Rāma is reunited with Bharata, Śatrughna and the mothers. Bharata places the sandals on Rāma's feet and gives back the kingdom he held "as a deposit", increased tenfold (127.52–55) (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 127).
- **The Rāja-abhiṣeka (coronation)** - Rāma is consecrated **King of Ayodhyā** by Vasiṣṭha and the assembled sages. Vāyu, prompted by Indra, brings him a golden garland and a pearl necklace (128.70–71). The allies are honoured with gifts (128.74–77, 128.84–87). Rāma gives Sītā a necklace bright as moonlight (128.78). She looks at Hanumān, then at her husband, and Rāma tells her to give it to the one who has pleased her. She gives it to **Hanumān**, and the text lists the virtues that live in him always: radiance, firmness, fame, skill, ability, humility, prudence, manliness, valour and intelligence (128.79–83).
  - *Note (where the chiranjīvi boon actually sits):* Hanumān asks for nothing at this coronation, and *Yuddha* 128 grants **no** deathlessness. The boon comes in the *Uttara Kāṇḍa*, in two places. At the allies' farewell soon after the coronation, Hanumān himself asks that his life stay in his body for as long as he hears Rāma's story told on earth. Rāma confirms it, saying "as long as my story goes about the worlds", and ties a necklace on him (*Uttara* 40; CE 7.39.14–21). At the Mahāprasthāna, Rāma bids Hanumān keep that pledge "as long as my stories are current in the world". In the same passage he gives Vibhīṣaṇa rule in Laṅkā "as long as the subjects endure" (*Uttara* 108; CE 7.98.22–25). Placing either grant at the coronation is a retelling's compression.
- **Rāma-rājya** - Rāma's reign begins the archetypal age of perfect dharma: timely rains, no disease or want, no untimely death. It is the political ideal invoked ever after as *Rāma-rājya* (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 128).
- **Phala-śruti** - The book closes with the fruits of reciting the Rāmāyaṇa (Vālmīki *Yuddha* 128). The story continues, disputedly, into the [Uttara Kāṇḍa](7-uttara-kanda.md).

## X. The Rākṣasa Command and the Ground of the War

Below Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa, Indrajit, Vibhīṣaṇa and Mandodarī, the war-book has a whole court and command staff who appear as bare names in most retellings. Each is listed here with kinship, office, and the sarga where his story is settled.

- **Prahasta** - Son of Sumālī, Rāvaṇa's maternal uncle, prime minister and commander-in-chief. He leads the boasting in the war-council (8) and holds the eastern gate. His son **Jambumālī** was killed by Hanumān (*Sundara* 44). The seven "sons of the minister" killed next (*Sundara* 45.1) are commonly taken to be his sons too, though the verse does not name the father [commentarial]. Prahasta takes the field when the first sorties fail and is killed by **Nīla** (*Yuddha* 57–58).
- **Mālyavān** - The eldest of the three founding rākṣasa brothers (Mālyavān, Sumālī, Mālī) and so Rāvaṇa's maternal great-uncle; *Yuddha* 35.6 calls him *mātuḥ paitāmahaḥ*. He is the senior political voice, argues from omens that a king at war with dharma cannot win, and is abused for it (*Yuddha* 35–36). He does not fight.
- **Dhūmrākṣa · Vajradaṃṣṭra · Akampana** - The three commanders sent out one after another as the siege opens. Dhūmrākṣa is killed by Hanumān (51–52), Vajradaṃṣṭra by Aṅgada (53–54), and Akampana by Hanumān with a tree (55–56). The Akampana who brings the news of Janasthāna to Laṅkā and first proposes the abduction (*Araṇya* 31) bears the same name and is generally read as the same rākṣasa.
- **Atikāya** - Rāvaṇa's son by Dhānyamālinī, given impenetrable armour and a bow by Brahmā. He is the one champion Lakṣmaṇa must use the brahmāstra on (71).
- **Narāntaka · Devāntaka · Triśiras (of Laṅkā)** - Rāvaṇa's sons. Narāntaka is killed by Aṅgada (69), Devāntaka and the three-headed Triśiras by Hanumān (70). This Triśiras is distinct from the **Triśiras of Janasthāna**, Khara's captain, whom Rāma kills in the forest (*Araṇya* 27).
- **Akṣakumāra** - Rāvaṇa's youngest son, killed by Hanumān in the grove-fighting before the war begins (*Sundara* 47). Hanumān reminds Rāvaṇa of it in battle (59.59).
- **Kumbha · Nikumbha** - Kumbhakarṇa's sons, killed by Sugrīva and Hanumān (76–77).
- **Makarākṣa** - Khara's son, come to avenge his father, armed with a spike given by Rudra. Rāma kills him with the āgneyāstra (78–79).
- **Khara · Dūṣaṇa · Triśiras — the Janasthāna command** - Rāvaṇa's standing garrison in Daṇḍaka. **Khara**, Rāvaṇa's maternal cousin (*Uttara* 24) and not his brother, is posted over fourteen thousand rākṣasas with Śūrpaṇakhā. **Dūṣaṇa** is his general and **Triśiras** his captain. Rāma alone kills all three and the fourteen thousand (*Araṇya* 26–29), and that is the provocation the whole war answers.
- **Vidyujjihva** - The sorcerer who conjures the illusory head and bow of Rāma for Sītā (31). Vālmīki does not say whether he is the Vidyujjihva who married Śūrpaṇakhā (*Uttara* 12).
- **Śuka · Sāraṇa · Śārdūla** - Rāvaṇa's intelligence service. Śuka goes to Sugrīva as an envoy in bird-form and is released on Rāma's order (20, 24). He and Sāraṇa are caught scouting the camp, exposed by Vibhīṣaṇa and released to report. Their forced recitation of the vānara order-of-battle from the palace roof is the fullest muster-roll the epic gives (25–29). Śārdūla goes next and returns with the same answer (29–30).
- **Mahodara · Mahāpārśva · Supārśva · Virūpākṣa** - Rāvaṇa's ministers and household champions. **Mahāpārśva** is the one who urges the rape of Sītā (13, *[vulgate only]*). The pair sent to guard Rāvaṇa's sons are called his **brothers** (69–70), and Mahodara flatters Rāvaṇa and urges a stratagem instead of open battle (64), for which Kumbhakarṇa rebukes him (65). **Supārśva** is the one who talks Rāvaṇa out of murdering Sītā after Indrajit's death (92). The rest die in the sorties, and the names recur on more than one bearer (see §V).
- **Laṅkā the guardian (Laṅkinī)** - The city's own tutelary guardian, felled at the gate by Hanumān. Brahmā had long before named her defeat as the sign of the rākṣasas' ruin. In Vālmīki she is "the city of Laṅkā herself" and has no personal name (*Sundara* 3). The name **Laṅkinī** is Tulsīdās's (*Mānas* Sundar 4).
- **Saramā · Trijaṭā** - The two rākṣasīs who take Sītā's side. **Saramā**, Vibhīṣaṇa's wife and daughter of the gandharva king Śailūṣa (*Uttara* 12), exposes the illusory head and spies on the council for her (33–34). **Trijaṭā** reads first the omen-dream (*Sundara* 27) and then the corpse-field (48) correctly against every appearance.

**The ground itself** — two places the war turns on, neither of them a general landscape:

- **Suvela** - The mountain overlooking Laṅkā from which Rāma's army besieges the city. The host climbs it after the crossing and camps on the summit. From Suvela Rāma first sees Rāvaṇa above the gate, and from there Sugrīva springs at him (38–40). The whole siege is fought from this peak.
- **Nikumbhilā** - The sanctuary and fire-altar inside Laṅkā, the city's sacrificial ground and Indrajit's private one. There, with Uśanas (Śukra) officiating, he performed the seven sacrifices that won him the sky-going chariot, the darkening māyā and his inexhaustible quivers (*Uttara* 25). The fire flares up as it consumes oblations of **flesh and blood** (*Yuddha* 82.25–27). The text sets the place in a grove with one **banyan** (*nyagrodha*) "dark as a rain-cloud", where Indrajit makes his offerings to the bhūtas before battle. Lakṣmaṇa is told to cut him down before he reaches it (*Yuddha* 87.3–6). Vibhīṣaṇa, who grew up in the city, is the one man in Rāma's camp who can find it. By Brahmā's terms, Indrajit can be killed only if he is struck before reaching Nikumbhilā and completing the offering (85.14), so Lakṣmaṇa's attack is aimed at the rite itself (84–90). Full treatment: [Nikumbhilā](../../06-sacred-geography/nikumbhila.md).
  - *Variant (the presiding goddess and the quarter):* later tradition gives the altar a deity of its own and the rite her name. **Nikumbhilā-devī** is identified by the commentators with **Bhadrakālī**, and in southern and tantric retellings with **Pratyaṅgirā**, so that Indrajit's interrupted sacrifice reads as a goddess-invocation rather than a fire-oblation. The same stratum places the grove in Laṅkā's **western quarter**. Vālmīki names **no deity and no quarter**, but the grove and the banyan are his own. [later tradition / regional]

## XI. Major Variants Across Traditions

- **Where the epic ends** - The war-book closes with the coronation and a phala-śruti. The *Uttara Kāṇḍa* tells of Sītā's later banishment, Lava and Kuśa, her descent into the earth and Rāma's departure. The Baroda CE prints it as book 7, but it is [scholarly] regarded as a later book, though canonical in tradition. See [Uttara Kāṇḍa](7-uttara-kanda.md).
- **Mahābhārata, *Rāmopākhyāna* (Āraṇyakaparvan 3.257–276, BORI)** - The Mahābhārata's own telling of the war, told by Mārkaṇḍeya to Yudhiṣṭhira. Kumbhakarṇa asks for sleep, "his mind seized by tamas" (3.259.28). Vibhīṣaṇa revives the bound brothers with the *prajñāstra*, Sugrīva heals them with the herb *viśalyā*, and Kubera sends water that lets them see invisible beings (3.273.5–12). There is no Garuḍa in the constituted text; only a star passage (no. 1289, after 3.273.4) brings him in. **Lakṣmaṇa** kills both Kumbhakarṇa (3.271.16) and Indrajit (3.273.21–23). Avindhya, not Supārśva, saves Sītā from Rāvaṇa's sword (3.273.28–32). Rāvaṇa is burned to nothing by the brahmāstra (3.274.31). The gods testify to Sītā's purity without a fire-entry in the constituted text (3.275).
- **[Rāmcaritmānas](recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md) (Tulsīdās, Avadhi, 16th c.)** - The *Laṅkākāṇḍ* foregrounds bhakti. Rocks float at Nala and Nīla's touch by Rāma's glory. Rāma installs the liṅga at Rāmeśvaram. Aṅgada plants his foot. Meghanāda's lance leads to a single herb-flight with Kālanemi and Bharata's arrow. Vibhīṣaṇa reveals the navel-amṛta. The shadow-Sītā removes the stigma of the fire-ordeal, and Vibhīṣaṇa's śaraṇāgati is the devotional centrepiece. The *Uttarakāṇḍ* is given over largely to devotion rather than to Sītā's exile.
- **[Kamban's *Irāmāvatāram*](recensions/kamban-iramavataram.md) (Tamil, 12th c.)** - The *Yuddha/Pōr Kāṇṭam*, with distinctive Tamil amplifications, ornate battle poetry and an elevated treatment of Rāvaṇa.
- **[Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md)** - An Advaitic, devotional retelling that embeds the Chāyā-Sītā doctrine and the Kālanemi episode and treats the whole war as Rāma's līlā.
- **[Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇ](recensions/krittivasi-ramayan.md) (Bengali, 15th c.)** - Before the final battle Rāma performs the *akāl-bodhan*, an out-of-season autumn worship of **Durgā**. When the offering comes up one lotus short of 108, he offers to pluck out his own "lotus" eye to make up the count, and the goddess grants him victory. This is the charter-legend of Bengali Durgā Pūjā. The same eastern stratum tells the **Mahīrāvaṇa** episode, in which Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa are carried off to Pātāla and rescued by Hanumān. Neither episode is in Vālmīki. [regional / late text]
- **[Jain & Buddhist Rāmāyaṇas](recensions/jain-and-buddhist-ramayanas.md)** - In Jain versions such as the *Paümacariya*, **Lakṣmaṇa**, not the non-violent Rāma, kills Rāvaṇa. Rāvaṇa is a noble but flawed Vidyādhara, and the "Vānaras" are a human clan, not animals. The Buddhist *Dasaratha Jātaka* leaves out the abduction and the war entirely.
- **[Southeast Asian retellings](recensions/southeast-asian-ramayanas.md)** - The Thai *Ramakien*, the Khmer *Reamker* and others recast the war with local heroes and added episodes, such as the mermaid Suvannamaccha obstructing the bridge-building.
