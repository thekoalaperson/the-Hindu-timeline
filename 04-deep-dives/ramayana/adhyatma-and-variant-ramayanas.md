# The Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa & Variant Rāmāyaṇas — Vedāntic and Regional Retellings

> Cosmic address: [Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) of the current 7th (Vaivasvata) Manvantara, 28th Mahāyuga — the *story* is Tretā-Yuga scripture-time; almost all of these *texts* are **Kali-Yuga** (post-3102 BCE) re-tellings, mostly 3rd c. CE → 17th c. CE. Two clocks: scripture-time vs. authorial-time.
> Up: [Rāmāyaṇa deep-dive](./README.md) · Section: [04-deep-dives](../README.md) · Master index: [The Hindu Timeline](../../README.md)
> See also: dedicated files — [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](./recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md) · [Rāmcaritmānas (Tulsīdās)](./recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md) · [Kamban *Iramāvatāram*](./recensions/kamban-iramavataram.md) · [Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇ](./recensions/krittivasi-ramayan.md) · [Jain & Buddhist Rāmāyaṇas](./recensions/jain-and-buddhist-ramayanas.md) · [Southeast-Asian Rāmāyaṇas](./recensions/southeast-asian-ramayanas.md)

This file is the **comparative deep-dive node** that sits *parallel* to the Vālmīki kāṇḍa set ([Bāla](./1-bala-kanda.md) → [Uttara](./7-uttara-kanda.md)): where those trace the base narrative book-by-book, this one traces how the **same story-skeleton is theologically re-coded** — from Vālmīki's *maryādā-puruṣottama* (ideal human king) into the supreme Brahman (Adhyātma, Tulsīdās), the Devī/Śakti (Adbhuta), a Jain *śalākāpuruṣa*, or a previous birth of the Buddha. It builds directly on the recension notes in the [Rāmāyaṇa overview](./README.md). The governing principle is A. K. Ramanujan's "**Three Hundred Rāmāyaṇas**": there is no single Rāmāyaṇa, only a field of *tellings* (Skt. *kathā*), each a complete world. **Catalog-all-variants** policy applies throughout.

## 1. The Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa — the Advaita-Vedānta keystone

*(Full treatment: [recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md](./recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md). Summary + the two doctrines that radiate outward to the bhakti tradition.)*

- **Text & embedding** - The **Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa** ("Spiritual / Self-knowledge Rāmāyaṇa") is a Sanskrit re-telling embedded in the latter (*uttara-bhāga*) portion of the [Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa](../../90-literature-corpus/puranas/brahmanda-purana.md), traditionally ascribed to **Veda-Vyāsa**; **7 kāṇḍas, 65 sargas**, ~**4,200–4,500 verses** (enumerations differ). It re-reads the whole epic as **Advaita-Vedānta allegory wrapped in intense Rāma-bhakti**. (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa)
  - *Dating variant [scholarly] [late text]:* **13th–15th c. CE** (most often **14th–15th c.**) — firmly Kali-Yuga authorial-time.
  - *Attribution variant [disputed]:* some tradition credits the saint **Rāmānanda** / his Rāmānandī circle, consistent with its saguṇa-Rāma devotionalism.
- **Outer frame — Śiva → Pārvatī** - Told as an *Umā-Maheśvara saṃvāda*: Śiva, supreme *jñānin* and arch-devotee of Rāma, reveals the **secret inner meaning** of Rāma's life to Pārvatī — replacing Vālmīki's Vālmīki-and-Lava-Kuśa frame and signalling "esoteric, knowledge-bearing" intent. (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, opening)
- **Core doctrine 1 — Rāma *is* Brahman** - Rāma is **Parabrahman, sat-cit-ānanda, the One-without-a-second**; his grief, exile, and search for Sītā are *līlā* (play) staged for the world's instruction, never real limitation. Sītā is his **Māyā-śakti / Prakṛti** — the power by which the formless One appears as the world. This is the metaphysical floor under all later Rāma-as-God bhakti. (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, passim)
- **Core doctrine 2 — the Māyā-Sītā / Chāyā-Sītā ("shadow Sītā")** - Foreknowing Rāvaṇa's plot, Rāma instructs Sītā **before the golden-deer episode** to hide her true self in the fire (Agni) for a year and put out an **illusory double (māyā-Sītā / chāyā-Sītā)** to be abducted. Thus **the real Sītā is never touched by Rāvaṇa**, and the later [agni-parīkṣā](./6-yuddha-kanda.md) (fire-ordeal) becomes the moment the **true Sītā re-emerges from Agni** and the shadow is dissolved — converting an apparent test of chastity into a reunion. (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya & Yuddha kāṇḍas)
  - *Reach of the motif:* the chāyā-Sītā doctrine is taken up by Tulsīdās and by the Kūrma & Brahmavaivarta Purāṇas; it parallels the **Chāyā/Māyā substitution of other heroines** (e.g. the shadow-Draupadī, shadow-Vedavatī traditions). See [Maya Sita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_Sita). [late text]
- **The inset *Rāma-Hṛdaya*** - An early hymn ("Heart of Rāma") in which Hanumān is taught Rāma's true Brahman-nature; scholars hold it (with the Rāma-Gītā) to have been an **independent Advaitic composition** later stitched into the frame.

### 1a. The Rāma-Gītā — the "lesser Gītā"

- **Location & form** - An inset dialogue in the **Uttara Kāṇḍa** (commonly the 5th adhyāya) in which **Rāma instructs Lakṣmaṇa** in pure Advaita — ~**62 verses** in the *Rāma-Gītā* proper (sometimes counted within a larger ~500-verse philosophical block with hymns by Brahmā, Agastya, Nārada). (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍa)
- **Why a "lesser Gītā"** - It is to the Rāmāyaṇa what the [Bhagavad-Gītā](../mahabharata/README.md) is to the Mahābhārata: God-incarnate teaching the highest wisdom to a beloved disciple **on the far side of the war** (after the coronation), not before it. Its teaching is **jñāna-yoga / ajātavāda-leaning Advaita** — *jīva = Brahman*, liberation by realizing one's true Self, with bhakti as the path that yields jñāna. Commented on by the Advaita tradition and recited as a stand-alone Vedāntic text. ([Rama Gita](http://stotrarathna.blogspot.com/2017/06/rama-gita-from-uthara-kanda-of.html))
- **Contrast with the *other* Rāma-Gītā** - Do not confuse with the *Rāma-Gītā* of the **Tattvasārāyaṇa / later compilations**, a different Vaiṣṇava text. [disputed terminology]

## 2. The vernacular bhakti Rāmāyaṇas

- **Rāmcaritmānas (Tulsīdās, 1574 CE)** - **Awadhi**, 7 kāṇḍas in *caupāī–dohā*; the most influential North-Indian *bhakti* telling and the textual basis of the **Rāmlīlā**. **Draws directly on the Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa** — inherits Rāma-as-God *and the māyā-Sītā doctrine* (in the Mānas the true Sītā likewise enters the fire before the abduction). Saguṇa-bhakti is foregrounded over Advaita allegory. *Full file:* [recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md](./recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md). [late text]
- **Adhyātmarāmāyaṇam Kiḷippāṭṭu (Ezhuthachan, 16th c.)** - **Malayalam** "parrot-song" rendering of the Sanskrit Adhyātma — carries the Vedāntic frame and chāyā-Sītā into Kerala; foundational to modern Malayalam literature.
- **Iramāvatāram / Kamba-rāmāyaṇam (Kamban, c. 12th c.)** - **Tamil**, ~10,000+ verses in **6 kāṇḍas (omits Uttara)** — the Tamil **Uttara Kāṇḍam** was later supplied by **Ottakkūttar**. A supreme literary kāvya; elevates Rāma's divinity, deepens characterization, and is famous for a **more sympathetic, tragic Rāvaṇa**. *Full file:* [recensions/kamban-iramavataram.md](./recensions/kamban-iramavataram.md). [scholarly]
- **Raṅganātha Rāmāyaṇamu (Gona Buddhā Reddy, c. 1300–1310 CE)** - **Telugu**, first complete Telugu Rāmāyaṇa, ~**17,290 couplets in *dvipada* (two-footed) meter**; rich in distinctive folk episodes. A separate **Uttara-Raṅganātha** continuation supplies the seventh book.
- **Kṛttivāsī Rāmāyaṇ / Śrīrām Pāñcālī (Kṛttibās Ojhā, 15th c.)** - **Bengali**, the most beloved Bengali telling; folk- and bhakti-saturated. Introduces the **akāl-bodhan** — Rāma's "untimely" autumn invocation of **Durgā** before the war (the mythic charter of autumn Durgā Pūjā). *Full file:* [recensions/krittivasi-ramayan.md](./recensions/krittivasi-ramayan.md). [folk]
- **Other major regional renderings** - **Saptakāṇḍa Rāmāyaṇa** (Assamese, Mādhava Kandalī, 14th c. — among the earliest complete vernacular Rāmāyaṇas); **Bhāvārtha Rāmāyaṇa** (Marathi, Ekanāth, 16th c.); **Torave Rāmāyaṇa** (Kannada, Kumāra Vālmīki/Narahari, 16th c.); **Giridhar Rāmāyaṇa** (Gujarati). [open-ended roster — many further vernacular tellings exist]

## 3. Other Sanskrit Rāmāyaṇas — Śākta, devotional, and philosophical

- **Adbhuta Rāmāyaṇa** - **Śākta** Sanskrit poem, ~**27 sargas / 1,355 verses**, ascribed pseudepigraphically to Vālmīki. Centers **Sītā as the supreme Śakti**: she is born **to Rāvaṇa's queen Mandodarī**, and — when Rāma is felled on the battlefield by a **thousand-headed elder Rāvaṇa (Sahasra-Rāvaṇa / Sahasra-mukha-Rāvaṇa)** of Puṣkara that the ten-headed Rāvaṇa of Vālmīki could not match — **Sītā assumes the form of Mahākālī and slays him**, inverting the hero/heroine roles. ([Adbhuta Rāmāyaṇa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adbhuta_Ramayana)) [late text] [Shakta variant]
- **Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa** - Pseudepigraphically "Vālmīki," ~15th c.; focuses on Rāma's **later reign and peripheral/etiological legends** and is a quarry for *vrata* (vow) stories. Distinctive back-stories: **Rāvaṇa abducts Kausalyā** (foretold that her son by Daśaratha will kill him) but fails to keep her; supplies motivations for **Kaikeyī and Mantharā** that the older epic leaves unexplained. [late text]
- **Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha (Vāsiṣṭha Rāmāyaṇa / Mahā-Rāmāyaṇa)** - A vast (~**29,000-verse**) philosophical Rāmāyaṇa-frame: a **young, world-weary Rāma** is instructed by **Vasiṣṭha** in radical Advaita — specifically *ajātavāda* (the doctrine of **non-origination**: the world never truly arose; it is mind-only appearance) — illustrated by hundreds of nested parables. Six **prakaraṇas**: (1) *Vairāgya* (dispassion), (2) *Mumukṣu-vyavahāra* (yearning for liberation), (3) *Utpatti* ("creation"), (4) *Sthiti* (sustenance), (5) *Upaśama* (dissolution/quieting), (6) *Nirvāṇa* (liberation). Syncretic — fuses Vedānta, Yoga, Sāṃkhya, Pratyabhijñā/Trika Śaivism, and Mahāyāna idealism; the narrative is a *pretext* for [Vedānta](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/atman-brahman-and-the-six-darshanas.md). ([Yoga Vasishtha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga_Vasishtha)) [late text]
- **Agastya Rāmāyaṇa** - Supplementary Sanskrit tradition (Agastya as teacher/transmitter). [late text]
- **The "core vs. frame" hinge** - The Vedāntic/Śākta theology of all the above lives in the conceptual space opened by the (later) **Bāla** and **Uttara** kāṇḍas of Vālmīki, where Rāma's Viṣṇu-divinity is made explicit — the older core (kāṇḍas 2–6) keeps Rāma chiefly human. See [Rāmāyaṇa overview → core vs. frame](./README.md). [scholarly]

## 4. Jain Rāmāyaṇas — demythologizing, ahiṃsā, and reassigned violence

*(Full treatment: [recensions/jain-and-buddhist-ramayanas.md](./recensions/jain-and-buddhist-ramayanas.md).)*

- **Paümacariya(ṃ) (Vimalasūri)** - **Prākrit (Jain Māhārāṣṭrī)**, the oldest Jain Rāma-narrative (the most popular of ~17 Jain versions); academically c. 3rd–5th c. CE, Jains claim earlier. **Rationalizes** the epic: **Rāvaṇa is a noble, learned Vidyādhara**, *vānaras* are a human clan bearing monkey-banners, and — because Rāma is the non-violent hero destined for liberation — **Lakṣmaṇa, not Rāma, kills Rāvaṇa** (in Jain *śalākāpuruṣa* cosmology the *Vāsudeva* slays the *Prati-vāsudeva*). ([Paumacariya](https://culturalsamvaad.com/rama-in-paumacariya-jaina-retelling-of-the-ramayana/)) [Jain variant]
  - *Sītā's parentage variant:* in several Jain strands **Sītā is Rāvaṇa's own (abandoned) daughter**, raised by Janaka — sharpening the moral critique of Rāvaṇa's desire. [Jain variant]
- **Padma-purāṇa (Raviṣeṇa, 7th c.)** & **Uttara-purāṇa (Guṇabhadra, 9th c.)** - Sanskrit Jain recastings continuing Vimalasūri's tradition (with internal divergences, e.g. Guṇabhadra's differing genealogies).
- **Kumudendu Rāmāyaṇa (Kumudendu Muni, 13th c.)** - **Kannada**, Jain. [Jain variant]

## 5. Buddhist Rāmāyaṇa

- **Dasaratha Jātaka (No. 461)** - **Pāli**; Rāma (**Rāma-paṇḍita**) is a previous birth of the **Buddha (bodhisatta)**. **No Rāvaṇa, no war, no abduction**: Rāma is sent into exile to spare him a stepmother's plot, returns after the term, and rules righteously — a story of *equanimity in the face of loss* (his calm on hearing of Daśaratha's death is the moral centre). In several recensions **Sītā is Rāma's sister/half-sister** who later becomes his queen — a notable inversion of the Brahmanical norm. [Buddhist variant]
- **Southeast-Asian Buddhist/Islamic-framed tellings** - Rāmakīen (Thai), Reamker (Khmer), Phra Lak Phra Lam (Lao), Yama Zatdaw (Burmese), Hikayat Seri Rama (Malay), and others — catalogued in [recensions/southeast-asian-ramayanas.md](./recensions/southeast-asian-ramayanas.md). [folk]

## 6. Comparative crib — the same skeleton, re-coded

- **Who is Rāma?** - *Ideal human king* (Vālmīki core) → *supreme Brahman* (Adhyātma, Tulsīdās, Kamban) → *vehicle of the Goddess* (Adbhuta) → *Jain hero bound for mokṣa* (Paümacariya) → *bodhisatta* (Dasaratha Jātaka).
- **Is Sītā abducted?** - Yes (Vālmīki) → **only her shadow is** (Adhyātma, Tulsīdās: chāyā-Sītā) → she is the one who **destroys** the demon as Mahākālī (Adbhuta) → no abduction at all (Dasaratha Jātaka).
- **Who kills Rāvaṇa?** - **Rāma** (Vālmīki, bhakti tellings) → **Lakṣmaṇa** (Jain) → **Sītā-as-Kālī** kills the *thousand-headed* Rāvaṇa (Adbhuta) → *no one / no Rāvaṇa* (Dasaratha Jātaka).
- **What is the agni-parīkṣā?** - A genuine test of chastity (Vālmīki) → the **re-emergence of the true Sītā from the fire** that has hidden her all along (Adhyātma/Tulsīdās chāyā-Sītā).
- **What is the genre?** - Narrative kāvya (Vālmīki, Kamban) → Vedānta *śāstra* in story-clothing (Adhyātma, Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha) → Purāṇic vrata-lore (Ānanda) → Śākta hymn-epic (Adbhuta) → didactic Jātaka / Jain *carita*.
