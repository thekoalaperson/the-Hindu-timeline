# Araṇya Kāṇḍa — Book of the Forest

> Cosmic address: [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) ← but the Rāmāyaṇa itself unfolds in the [Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara. Rāma is the 7th [Daśāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) of [Viṣṇu](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md), of the [Sūryavaṃśa / Ikṣvāku line](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
> Parent: [Rāmāyaṇa deep-dive index](README.md) · Part of [04-deep-dives](../README.md). Primary source: Vālmīki [Rāmāyaṇa](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md). Previous: [Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa](2-ayodhya-kanda.md) · Next: [Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa](4-kishkindha-kanda.md).

The **Araṇya Kāṇḍa** ("Book of the Forest") is the third of the seven kāṇḍas of Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa (~75 sargas/cantos). It covers the heart of Rāma's fourteen-year exile in the **Daṇḍaka** wilderness with Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa: their passage through the ashrams of the great forest sages, the slaying of demons that infest the woods, the settling at **Pañcavaṭī**, the **Śūrpaṇakhā** episode and the war with **Khara–Dūṣaṇa**, **Mārīca**'s golden-deer ruse, **Rāvaṇa**'s abduction of Sītā, the death of **Jaṭāyu**, and the despairing brothers' first steps toward the alliance that fills the next kāṇḍa. It is the structural hinge of the epic — the kāṇḍa in which the central catastrophe occurs.

## Entering Daṇḍaka: The Sages of the Forest (sargas 1–11)

- **Into the Daṇḍakāraṇya** - After Citrakūṭa, Rāma, Sītā, and Lakṣmaṇa cross south into the vast **Daṇḍaka** forest, a belt of ascetic hermitages plagued by rākṣasas; the sages beg Rāma's protection as a kṣatriya-in-exile (Vālmīki, Araṇya 1).
- **Slaying of Virādha** - The man-eating rākṣasa **Virādha** seizes Sītā; he is invulnerable to weapons (Brahmā's boon), so the brothers break his arms and **bury him alive** in a pit. Dying, he reverts to the **gandharva Tumburu**, cursed by Kubera, and is released by Rāma's touch (Vālmīki, Araṇya 2–4).
- **Śarabhaṅga's self-immolation** - At sage **Śarabhaṅga**'s hermitage, the sage — who has just refused Indra's offer to carry him to Brahmaloka, wishing to see Rāma first — enters the fire and ascends in a radiant body, having gained Rāma's darśana (Vālmīki, Araṇya 5).
- **Vow to the ṛṣis** - The forest sages show Rāma heaps of bones of ascetics devoured by rākṣasas; Rāma **vows to clear Daṇḍaka of demons** — the dharmic pledge that motivates the whole kāṇḍa (Vālmīki, Araṇya 6).
  - *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa / devotional reading):* the vow is framed as Viṣṇu's deliberate descent to unburden the Earth of the rākṣasa scourge, not merely a knight's promise.
- **Sutīkṣṇa's hermitage** - The trio reach the ashram of **Sutīkṣṇa** (disciple of Agastya), who has likewise awaited Rāma and offers to make over to him the worlds he has won by austerity; they pass the night there and take leave at dawn (Vālmīki, Araṇya 7–8).
- **Sītā's ahiṃsā counsel** - Setting out again, Sītā warns Rāma of the three subtle vices born of desire — falsehood, another man's wife, and **violence without enmity** — and tells the parable of the ascetic whom Indra ruined by leaving a sword in his keeping: the weapon in the hand bends the mind toward slaughter, and the quarrel of the Daṇḍaka sages is not his to fight. It is her longest ethical argument in the epic (Vālmīki, Araṇya 9).
- **Rāma's answer: the vow outranks his life** - Rāma replies that he has given the ṛṣis his word before their faces and will not take it back; he would sooner give up Lakṣmaṇa, or Sītā, or his own life than break a promise once made, and the bow exists for exactly this — the protection of the afflicted (Vālmīki, Araṇya 10).
- **Ten years' wandering** - The Kāṇḍa compresses roughly ten years of moving among hermitages before the family returns to Sutīkṣṇa and proceeds to Agastya (Vālmīki, Araṇya 11).

## Agastya, Anasūyā, and the Divine Weapons (sargas 11–14)

- **Anasūyā and Sītā** - At **Atri**'s hermitage, immediately before the forest crossing, the saintly **Anasūyā** instructs Sītā on **pativratā-dharma** (wifely devotion) and gifts her celestial unfading garments, ornaments, and cosmetics (Vālmīki, **Ayodhyā** 117–119; the trio reach Atri's ashram at Ayodhyā 116). See [Atri and Anasūyā](../atri-anasuya.md).
  - *Note:* the episode stands at the **close of the Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa** in the vulgate; the Araṇya Kāṇḍa opens after it. Anasūyā's gift is load-bearing later — these are the unfading ornaments Sītā strips off and drops to the vānaras during the abduction-flight (Araṇya 54), and that Sugrīva unwraps before Rāma (Kiṣkindhā 6).
- **Arrival at Agastya's ashram** - They reach the hermitage of the great sage **Agastya**, the "tamer of the Vindhyas" and drinker of the ocean; **Rāma himself narrates** to Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa how Agastya made the southern forests habitable by digesting the rākṣasa **Vātāpi** and burning **Ilvala** with a glance (Vālmīki, Araṇya 11–12). See [Agastya](../agastya.md).
- **The arsenal from Agastya** - Agastya hands over the deposit he has held for this hour: the great bow **of Viṣṇu**, wrought with gold and diamond by **Viśvakarmā**; **one** unfailing arrow blazing like the sun, **given by Brahmā**; **two** quivers whose shafts never run out, **given by Indra**; and a broad divine sword in a **golden sheath**. These are the arms Viṣṇu bore against the asuras, laid up with Agastya until Rāma should need them (Vālmīki, Araṇya 12).
  - *Note:* **"Brahmadatta" is not the arrow's name** — *brahma-datta* is the adjective "Brahmā-given." Vālmīki keeps Agastya's shafts distinct and the registry should too: this Brahmā-given arrow is the one Mātali later prompts Rāma to loose at Rāvaṇa ([Yuddha](6-yuddha-kanda.md) 108), while the arrow that kills Khara is a **different**, **Indra-given** shaft that reached Rāma by the same hand (Araṇya 30). Four items, three donors — not one undifferentiated gift.
- **Directed to Pañcavaṭī** - Agastya advises Rāma to settle at **Pañcavaṭī** on the **Godāvarī** river — fertile, scenic, and suited to a hermit's life (Vālmīki, Araṇya 13).
- **Meeting Jaṭāyu** - On the way the family meets the aged vulture-king **Jaṭāyu**, a friend of Daśaratha (of the lineage of Aruṇa/Garuḍa's brother); he pledges to guard Sītā when the brothers are absent — a promise that foreshadows his death (Vālmīki, Araṇya 14).

## Life at Pañcavaṭī (sargas 15–16)

- **The hermitage built** - Lakṣmaṇa constructs a leaf-and-bamboo cottage at Pañcavaṭī; the three live in idyllic forest peace through the seasons (Vālmīki, Araṇya 15–16).
- **Lakṣmaṇa's seasonal hymn** - A celebrated lyrical passage describing the **Hemanta (winter)** season at Pañcavaṭī — among the epic's finest nature poetry (Vālmīki, Araṇya 16).

## Śūrpaṇakhā Mutilated (sargas 17–18)

- **Śūrpaṇakhā's advance** - **Śūrpaṇakhā** ("she of winnowing-fan nails"), sister of **Rāvaṇa**, sees Rāma and, smitten, proposes marriage, offering to devour Sītā as a rival (Vālmīki, Araṇya 17).
- **The mocking deflection** - Rāma playfully redirects her to the "unmarried" Lakṣmaṇa, who in turn sends her back to Rāma; the byplay enrages her (Vālmīki, Araṇya 17–18).
- **The mutilation** - When she lunges at Sītā, Rāma orders Lakṣmaṇa to disfigure her; **Lakṣmaṇa cuts off her nose and ears** (Vālmīki, Araṇya 18). This single act is the spark that ignites the war.
  - *Variant (Kamban's Irāmāvatāram, Tamil):* the encounter is elaborated; Śūrpaṇakhā first appears in a beautiful disguised form (sometimes named **Kāmavallī**), her demonic nature revealed only when rebuffed. [regional]
  - *Variant (Adbhuta / folk traditions):* some versions add that only her nose is cut, or that her breasts are also wounded; the "nose" (nāsikā) becomes the iconic image — hence later folk place-names like Nāsik. [folk tradition]

## War of the Demons: Khara, Dūṣaṇa, Triśiras (sargas 19–30)

- **Khara roused** - Mutilated, Śūrpaṇakhā flees to her brother **Khara**, ruler of Janasthāna (the Daṇḍaka demon-colony) with his fourteen thousand rākṣasas (Vālmīki, Araṇya 19).
- **The fourteen and the vanguard** - Khara first dispatches **fourteen** rākṣasas with Śūrpaṇakhā; Rāma annihilates them, and she returns shrieking again (Vālmīki, Araṇya 20–21).
- **The host of fourteen thousand** - Khara marches with his full army and his generals **Dūṣaṇa** and **Triśiras** ("three-heads"); dire omens attend their advance (Vālmīki, Araṇya 22–23).
- **Rāma sends the others to a cave** - Rāma stations Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa in a hidden mountain cave and faces the army **single-handed** — one of the epic's great solo-combat set-pieces (Vālmīki, Araṇya 24).
- **Dūṣaṇa slain** - Rāma destroys Dūṣaṇa and his contingent with a storm of arrows (Vālmīki, Araṇya 26).
- **Triśiras slain** - The three-headed Triśiras is beheaded (Vālmīki, Araṇya 27).
- **Khara slain** - In the last stage of the duel, Khara's chariot and bow gone, Rāma looses the great **Indra-given** shaft that came to him from Agastya — irresistible as Brahmā's staff — and it rives Khara's chest; all **fourteen thousand** demons of Janasthāna lie dead, accomplished by Rāma alone (Vālmīki, Araṇya 28–30). The gods rain flowers.
- **Akampana's escape** - The rākṣasa **Akampana** alone gets clear of the field and races to Laṅkā to report the slaughter to Rāvaṇa (Vālmīki, Araṇya 30–31).

## Rāvaṇa Provoked (sargas 31–41)

- **Akampana's counsel and the first visit to Mārīca** - Akampana tells Rāvaṇa that Rāma cannot be beaten in open battle and that the one way to destroy him is to **carry off Sītā**, whose loss will kill him of grief. Rāvaṇa flies that night to the seaside hermitage of **Mārīca** — the demon Rāma had hurled a hundred yojanas into the sea in the [Bāla Kāṇḍa](1-bala-kanda.md) — and Mārīca, who has already felt those arrows, **talks him out of it**; Rāvaṇa turns back to Laṅkā (Vālmīki, Araṇya 31).
  - *Note:* Vālmīki's Rāvaṇa goes to Mārīca **twice**. The first approach, on Akampana's advice, is dissuaded and abandoned; only the second, after Śūrpaṇakhā's goading, produces the golden deer. Retellings that fuse the two erase Mārīca's one successful refusal — and with it the reason his second refusal must be broken by threat.
- **Śūrpaṇakhā's incitement** - Śūrpaṇakhā reaches Laṅkā, rebukes Rāvaṇa before his court for a king asleep on his throne, and goads him — describing both Rāma's lethal prowess and Sītā's incomparable beauty, planting the desire that will doom him (Vālmīki, Araṇya 32–34).
- **Rāvaṇa seeks Mārīca again** - Rāvaṇa mounts his **golden, mule-yoked** aerial chariot, crosses to the far shore, and comes to Mārīca a second time, the deer-ruse already formed in his mind (Vālmīki, Araṇya 35).
- **Mārīca's warning** - Mārīca, who has twice felt Rāma's arrows, warns Rāvaṇa that Rāma is no ordinary man and that abducting Sītā means certain destruction of himself, his city, and his kin; he urges restraint (Vālmīki, Araṇya 36–38).
- **Coerced compliance** - Rāvaṇa threatens to kill Mārīca on the spot; Mārīca, choosing "death by Rāma over death by Rāvaṇa," agrees to play the golden deer and rides back with him to Pañcavaṭī (Vālmīki, Araṇya 39–41).

## The Golden Deer and the Abduction (sargas 42–49)

- **The golden deer (māyā-mṛga)** - Mārīca becomes a dazzling **golden, silver-spotted deer** and grazes near the hermitage; Sītā, enchanted, begs Rāma to capture it (alive for a pet, or its hide) (Vālmīki, Araṇya 42–43).
- **Lakṣmaṇa's suspicion** - Lakṣmaṇa warns it is surely a rākṣasa illusion (recalling Mārīca); Sītā insists, and Rāma — half-suspecting a trick yet resolving to kill the demon if it is one — pursues, leaving Lakṣmaṇa to guard Sītā (Vālmīki, Araṇya 43).
- **The deer slain, the false cry** - Rāma chases the deer deep into the forest and pierces it with an arrow; dying, Mārīca resumes his demon form and cries out **"Hā Sītā! Hā Lakṣmaṇa!"** in a perfect imitation of Rāma's voice (Vālmīki, Araṇya 44).
- **Sītā sends Lakṣmaṇa away** - Hearing the cry, Sītā panics; despite Lakṣmaṇa's assurance that Rāma is invincible, she accuses him with harsh words (even of desiring her) until, anguished, he goes to find Rāma (Vālmīki, Araṇya 45).
  - *Variant — the Lakṣmaṇa Rekhā:* In Vālmīki there is **no protective line**; Lakṣmaṇa simply departs. The famous **Lakṣmaṇa Rekhā** (a line drawn with bow/foot that Sītā must not cross) is a **later, non-Vālmīki addition**, appearing first in medieval vernacular tellings such as the Bengali **Kṛttivāsa Rāmāyaṇa** and the Telugu **Raṅganātha Rāmāyaṇa**, and popularized thereafter; notably it is **absent even from Tulsīdās' Araṇya Kāṇḍa** in the [Rāmcaritmānas](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md). [scholarly] [folk tradition]
  - *Note (the Mānas's own seam):* the Rāmcaritmānas never stages the line in its Araṇya Kāṇḍa, yet its **Laṅkā Kāṇḍa** assumes one — Mandodarī taunts Rāvaṇa that he could not so much as cross the line Lakṣmaṇa drew. The motif was evidently current in Tulsīdās's world without being written into his own abduction scene. See [Rāmcaritmānas](recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md).
- **Rāvaṇa as the mendicant** - With both brothers gone, Rāvaṇa approaches disguised as a wandering **saṃnyāsin / brahmin ascetic** begging alms; Sītā, observing hospitality, comes out to serve him (Vālmīki, Araṇya 46).
- **Sītā's account of herself** - Taking the beggar for a guest owed courtesy, Sītā tells him who she is: Janaka's daughter, wed to Rāma, **twelve years** in her husband's house before the exile fell — and, in the epic's only plain statement of the pair's ages, **Rāma twenty-five and herself eighteen** when they left Ayodhyā (Vālmīki, Araṇya 47.4, 47.10–11).
  - *Note:* these figures do not reconcile with [Bāla](1-bala-kanda.md) 20's *ūnaṣoḍaśavarṣo me rāmo* ("my Rāma is not yet sixteen") at Viśvāmitra's request, nor with Sītā's own "twelve years in Rāma's house" restated at [Sundara](5-sundara-kanda.md) 33. Vālmīki never resolves the arithmetic; the corpus records the discrepancy rather than smoothing it. The two age-verses are also **absent from several of the older manuscripts**, though the twelve-year statement is not. [scholarly] [disputed]
- **The abduction** - Rāvaṇa reveals himself, declares his love and power, and — when Sītā scorns him — **seizes her by the hair and thigh**, sets her in his chariot, and drives up into the sky southward toward Laṅkā as she screams (Vālmīki, Araṇya 48–49).
  - *Note on the vehicle — it is **not** the Puṣpaka:* Vālmīki's abduction-car is a **golden, mule-yoked** aerial chariot that comes and goes at its master's wish (Araṇya 35, 49). Jaṭāyu smashes it to splinters and kills the mules (51), after which Rāvaṇa completes the flight **bodily**, carrying Sītā in his arms through the air (52, 54). The **Puṣpaka** — Kubera's flower-car, seized by Rāvaṇa in the [Uttara Kāṇḍa](7-uttara-kanda.md) — appears in this story only from the [Sundara Kāṇḍa](5-sundara-kanda.md) onward. The two are distinct vehicles and the abduction one is never named Puṣpaka.
  - *Variant (Rāvaṇa lifts the whole earth/hut):* in some retellings, unable to touch the chaste Sītā directly, Rāvaṇa digs up and carries the patch of earth on which she stands — a motif used to reconcile her inviolability. [folk tradition]

### The Māyā-Sītā / Shadow-Sītā Tradition [variant cycle]

A major theological variant holds that **Rāvaṇa never abducted the true Sītā at all** — only an illusory double — preserving her absolute purity and pre-explaining her later **Agni-parīkṣā** (fire-ordeal).

- *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, embedded in the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa):* Foreknowing the abduction, Rāma instructs Sītā to **enter the fire (Agni)** and leave a **Māyā-Sītā / Chāyā-Sītā** (shadow-Sītā) in her place; it is this illusory Sītā whom Rāvaṇa carries off. After Rāvaṇa's death, at the fire-ordeal the Māyā-Sītā **vanishes into the fire** and Agni restores the real Sītā — Agni declaring the duplicate was created to bring about Rāvaṇa's destruction.
- *Variant (Kūrma Purāṇa, Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa):* among the **earliest** sources of the shadow-Sītā motif, often linking her to the earlier curse-figure **Vedavatī** (whom Rāvaṇa once molested, who vowed his ruin) — the Māyā-Sītā is the agent of Vedavatī's revenge.
- *Variant (Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa):* developed Śākta version emphasizing Sītā as a form of the Goddess, untouchable by Rāvaṇa.
- *Variant (Tulsīdās' Rāmcaritmānas):* explicitly adopts the Adhyātma scheme — the "Sītā" abducted is the shadow; the real Sītā hides in fire and returns at the ordeal, a key device of the Vaiṣṇava-devotional tradition.
- *Variant (Adbhuta Rāmāyaṇa, Jagamohana / Oriya Rāmāyaṇa, Vaidehīśa Vilāsa, Bengali tellings):* further regional elaborations of the illusory-Sītā concept.
- *Contrast (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa):* the base text has **no** shadow-Sītā — the real Sītā is abducted and her fidelity is later vindicated by the fire-ordeal itself. The Māyā-Sītā is a Purāṇic/devotional overlay. [scholarly]

## Jaṭāyu's Last Stand (sargas 49–51)

- **Jaṭāyu intercepts** - Hearing Sītā's cries, the aged vulture-king **Jaṭāyu** rouses himself and challenges Rāvaṇa in mid-air, invoking Rāma and dharma, and demanding he release her (Vālmīki, Araṇya 49–50).
- **The aerial battle** - Jaṭāyu fights ferociously — tearing Rāvaṇa's bow, **smashing the chariot to pieces**, killing his charioteer and his mules, and wounding the demon — a heroic stand despite his age (Vālmīki, Araṇya 50).
- **Jaṭāyu mortally wounded** - Rāvaṇa finally **severs Jaṭāyu's wings, feet, and flanks** with his sword; the great bird falls, dying. His car destroyed under him, Rāvaṇa takes Sītā **in his arms** and rises again into the sky on his own strength (Vālmīki, Araṇya 51–52).

## Carried to Laṅkā (sargas 52–56)

- **Sītā's defiance in flight** - Held aloft in Rāvaṇa's arms she does not plead but arraigns him: a thief who struck while her protectors were drawn away, too small a creature to have faced Rāma in the field, and a fool who has swallowed his own death — she calls out to the forest, the Godāvarī, and the deer to tell Rāma which way she has gone (Vālmīki, Araṇya 52–53).
- **The tokens dropped** - Seeing **five vānaras** seated on a hilltop, Sītā silently gathers her jewels into the fringe of her upper garment and **lets the bundle fall** among them — the ornaments from her right side, ear-ring and armlet and bracelet and the anklet with its little bells, the unfading gifts Anasūyā had given her. It is the one act of her own that survives the flight, and it becomes the clue-trail Sugrīva later unwraps before Rāma ([Kiṣkindhā](4-kishkindha-kanda.md) 6) (Vālmīki, Araṇya 54).
- **The eight spies to Janasthāna** - Inside Laṅkā, Rāvaṇa first lodges Sītā in his own inner apartments under rākṣasī guard, then dispatches **eight** picked rākṣasas to re-occupy the emptied **Janasthāna**, watch Rāma's movements, and take any chance to kill him (Vālmīki, Araṇya 54).
- **The palace tour** - Rāvaṇa walks Sītā through his palace and city — the terraces, the treasuries, the aerial gardens, the retinue of women — and offers her the rank of chief queen over all of it; she rejects the whole display and bids him give her back (Vālmīki, Araṇya 55).
- **The blade of grass and the twelve-month ultimatum** - Sītā lays a **blade of grass** between herself and Rāvaṇa and answers him past it, refusing to address him directly. Rāvaṇa gives her **twelve months** to submit, threatening that if she has not yielded by then his cooks will serve her for his morning meal (Vālmīki, Araṇya 56).
  - *Note:* this clock runs across the kāṇḍas. When Hanumān reaches her in the [Sundara Kāṇḍa](5-sundara-kanda.md), Sītā says **two months remain** of Rāvaṇa's term (Sundara 22) — the deadline that makes the war urgent rather than merely inevitable.
- **Committed to the Aśoka grove** - Rāvaṇa has her taken from the palace to the **Aśoka-vana** and set under a guard of misshapen rākṣasīs, ordered to break her pride by turns of threat and flattery but on no account to let her die — the confinement in which the Sundara Kāṇḍa will find her (Vālmīki, Araṇya 56).

## The Brothers' Grief and the First Clues (sargas 57–75)

- **Return to an empty hermitage** - Rāma, having killed the deer and met the returning Lakṣmaṇa, races back to find Pañcavaṭī empty; he descends into wild grief, questioning trees, animals, and rivers about Sītā (Vālmīki, Araṇya 57–60).
- **Rāma's rage against the three worlds** - Grief turns: Rāma declares he will burn up the whole creation — earth, sky, the worlds of the gods — and empty the universe of every living thing if Sītā is not returned to him, and takes up his bow to do it. It is the epic's darkest picture of him (Vālmīki, Araṇya 64).
- **Lakṣmaṇa's restraint speech** - Lakṣmaṇa argues him down: the worlds have not wronged him, and a king's anger must fall on the guilty party alone; let them first find who took her, and then strike. The pattern of the whole epic — the younger brother holding the elder inside dharma — is set here (Vālmīki, Araṇya 65).
- **Finding Jaṭāyu** - The brothers find the dying Jaṭāyu, who names **Rāvaṇa** as the abductor and reports the southward flight (Vālmīki, Araṇya 67–68).
- **The Vinda muhūrta** - With his last breath Jaṭāyu adds the one piece of hope in the kāṇḍa: Sītā was carried off in the hour called **Vinda**, and what is lost in that muhūrta is regained. He then expires; Rāma performs his **funeral rites** with his own hands, and the bird attains the higher worlds by Rāma's grace (Vālmīki, Araṇya 68).
- **Ayomukhī** - Moving on through the Krauñca forest, the brothers are set upon by the rākṣasī **Ayomukhī**, who embraces Lakṣmaṇa and claims him for a husband; he cuts off her nose, ears, and breasts and she flees howling — a deliberate echo of Śūrpaṇakhā, and the beat immediately before Kabandha's arm closes on him (Vālmīki, Araṇya 69).
- **Kabandha slain** - The headless monster **Kabandha** — a trunk with a mouth in its belly, one eye in its chest, and **two arms a yojana long** — seizes both brothers; they hew off the arms, and, dying, he asks to be **cremated**. He names himself: a **dānava, the son of Danu**, once of surpassing beauty, who won long life from Brahmā, harassed the sages in a terrible shape at the curse of the ṛṣi **Sthūlaśiras**, and had his head and thighs driven down into his body by Indra's thunderbolt — with the release-term that he would be freed when Rāma severed his arms and burned him. From the pyre he rises in a shining form and **counsels Rāma to ally with the vānara Sugrīva** at Ṛśyamūka, naming him the one being who can find Sītā, and describes the road to Pampā (Vālmīki, Araṇya 69–73). Full treatment: [Kabandha](../characters/kabandha.md).
  - *Variant (harmonized/later stratum):* other tellings identify him instead as the **gandharva Viśvāvasu**, cursed into the rākṣasa shape. Vālmīki's own text has him give his descent from **Danu**; the gandharva identification is a later reconciliation and is cataloged, not adopted. [late text]
- **Śabarī's devotion** - At Mataṅga's hermitage Rāma meets the aged **Śabarī** — Vālmīki calls her a **śramaṇī** (a woman ascetic) of the **Śabara** people, who has kept her teachers' word and awaited him for years; she offers him forest fruit and roots, shows him Mataṅga's grove, and, blessed by his darśana, **enters the fire** and ascends. She directs Rāma to **Lake Pampā** and Sugrīva (Vālmīki, Araṇya 74). Full treatment: [Śabarī](../characters/shabari.md).
  - *Note:* the familiar "Niṣāda / Bhīl" labeling of Śabarī is later framing; the base text's word for her is *śramaṇī*, and her tribe is the Śabaras.
  - *Variant (Padma Purāṇa, elaborated in the Rāmcaritmānas & popular tradition):* the beloved episode of **Śabarī's tasted berries** (she bites each fruit first to ensure sweetness for Rāma) and the **navadhā-bhakti** (nine forms of devotion) discourse Rāma gives her — a central image of bhakti transcending caste. Neither the tasting nor the discourse is in Vālmīki. [late text / devotional]
- **Toward Pampā** - Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa proceed to the lotus-covered **Pampā** lake near **Ṛśyamūka** mountain, where Sugrīva and Hanumān dwell — the kāṇḍa ends on the threshold of the alliance that will recover Sītā (Vālmīki, Araṇya 75).
