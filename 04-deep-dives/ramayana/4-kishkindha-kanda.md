# Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa — Book of Kishkindha

> Cosmic address: [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) ← but the Rāmāyaṇa itself unfolds in the [Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara. Rāma is the 7th [Daśāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) of [Viṣṇu](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md), of the [Sūryavaṃśa / Ikṣvāku line](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
> Parent: [Rāmāyaṇa deep-dive index](README.md) · Part of [04-deep-dives](../README.md). Primary source: Vālmīki [Rāmāyaṇa](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md). Previous: [Araṇya Kāṇḍa](3-aranya-kanda.md) · Next: Sundara Kāṇḍa.

The **Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa** ("Book of Kishkindha," the vānara kingdom near modern **Hampi/Anegundi**, Karnataka) is the fourth of the seven kāṇḍas of Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa (~67 sargas/cantos). It is the kāṇḍa of **alliance, regicide, and reconnaissance**: the grieving Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, having lost Sītā at the close of the [Araṇya Kāṇḍa](3-aranya-kanda.md), forge a friendship-pact with the exiled vānara prince **Sugrīva**; Rāma slays Sugrīva's mighty brother **Vāli** with a concealed arrow (the epic's most-debated act of dharma); Sugrīva is enthroned, lapses into pleasure, is sternly recalled to his vow, and then dispatches vānara armies to the four directions to find Sītā. The southern party — under **Aṅgada, Hanumān, and Jāmbavān** — is set on the right path by the dying vulture **Sampāti**, and the Kāṇḍa ends with **Hanumān** swelling at the ocean's edge, poised for the leap that opens the Sundara Kāṇḍa.

## Arrival at Ṛṣyamūka & the Meeting with Hanumān (sargas 1–4)

- **The lament at Pampā** - Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa reach the lotus-lake **Pampā** in spring; the blossoming forest and birdsong sharpen Rāma's grief for Sītā, prompting one of the epic's great laments (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 1).
- **Sugrīva's alarm on Ṛṣyamūka** - From **Mount Ṛṣyamūka** — the one hill Vāli cannot enter (curse of sage **Mataṅga**) — the exiled Sugrīva sees two armed strangers and fears Vāli has sent them to kill him; he sends his minister Hanumān to investigate (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 2–3).
- **Hanumān as brāhmaṇa envoy** - Hanumān approaches disguised as a wandering mendicant, addresses the brothers in flawless, courteous Sanskrit; Rāma praises his speech as that of one versed in the Vedas. Reassured of their good faith, Hanumān reveals himself, **carries both princes on his shoulders** to Sugrīva (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 3–4).
  - *Note:* Hanumān's first appearance here (not the Bāla Kāṇḍa) is his entry into the main action; his divine parentage (son of Vāyu/[Marut](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md)) is recounted later, in the Sampāti and leap episodes.

## The Fire-Witnessed Friendship-Pact (sarga 5)

- **The agni-sākṣi alliance** - Hanumān kindles fire by friction; Rāma and Sugrīva circumambulate it and swear mutual aid (**sakhya**) with **Agni** as witness — Rāma to slay Vāli and restore Sugrīva's wife and kingdom, Sugrīva to mobilize the vānaras to find Sītā (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 5). This sacral oath is the dharmic ground Rāma later cites for killing Vāli.
- **The tokens of Sītā** - Sugrīva produces a bundle of ornaments Sītā had dropped from Rāvaṇa's flying chariot as it passed over Ṛṣyamūka; Rāma recognizes nothing but the **anklets/foot-ornaments** (he had only ever gazed at her feet in reverence, per the famous gloss) and weeps. This is the first physical trace of the abduction's route (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 6).

## Establishing Rāma's Prowess: The Dundubhi & Sāla Tests (sargas 8–12)

- **Vāli's strength recounted** - Sugrīva narrates Vāli's terrifying might: how Vāli slew the buffalo-demon **Dundubhi** and hurled the carcass; the bones lie like a hill. He fears Rāma cannot match such a foe (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 11).
- **Kicking Dundubhi's skeleton** - To reassure him, Rāma flicks the colossal skeleton **ten yojanas** with his big toe (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 11).
- **The seven sāla trees** - Still doubtful, Sugrīva asks for more proof; Rāma looses a single arrow that **pierces seven sāla (sal) trees** in a row, the earth, and returns to his quiver — convincing Sugrīva utterly (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 12).
- **The Māyāvī / Mataṅga backstory** - Sugrīva explains the brothers' feud: chasing the asura **Māyāvī** (Dundubhi's brother — both sons of the demon Maya) into a cave (the **Ṛkṣabila/Kiṣkindhā** cavern), Vāli told Sugrīva to wait; after a year, seeing blood flow out, Sugrīva sealed the cave-mouth with a boulder believing Vāli dead, and returned to be crowned. Vāli emerged alive, branded Sugrīva a usurper, drove him out, and **seized Sugrīva's wife Rumā** — and his mobility was confined by Mataṅga's curse barring him from Ṛṣyamūka (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 9–10).

## The First Duel & the Slaying of Vāli (sargas 13–18)

- **The first, failed challenge** - Sugrīva challenges Vāli to combat outside Kiṣkindhā. The brothers are so alike that Rāma, hidden, **cannot tell them apart** and dares not shoot; Vāli thrashes Sugrīva, who flees back to Ṛṣyamūka, bitterly reproaching Rāma (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 14–15).
- **The garland sign** - Rāma explains the look-alike problem; Lakṣmaṇa hangs a **gajapuṣpī flower-garland** around Sugrīva's neck as an identifying mark for the rematch (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 15–16).
- **Tārā's warning** - At the second challenge, the wise queen **Tārā** (Vāli's wife, daughter of the physician-monkey Suṣeṇa) begs Vāli not to fight — Sugrīva would not return so soon unless backed by the formidable Rāma, with whom alliance, not war, is wise. Vāli dismisses her counsel out of pride (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 16).
- **The concealed arrow** - During the second duel, **Rāma shoots Vāli from concealment** (behind a tree, per most readings) with an arrow to the chest; Vāli falls mortally wounded (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 16).
- **Vāli's dharma-indictment of Rāma** - Dying, Vāli delivers a celebrated rebuke (Vālmīki records it across many ślokas): Rāma struck **a stranger, unprovoked, by stealth, from hiding**, breaking the kṣatriya code of fair open combat; Vāli had no quarrel with Rāma; a king of the **Ikṣvāku** line famed for dharma has acted like a hunter snaring game; had Rāma asked, Vāli himself could have recovered Sītā in a day (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 17).
- **Rāma's tenfold justification** - Rāma answers (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 18), the core of the ethics debate:
  - The Earth belongs to the **Ikṣvākus**; as agent of emperor **Bharata**, Rāma is duty-bound to **punish adharma**, and a king/his deputy may chastise wrongdoers anywhere.
  - Vāli committed the grave sin of **taking his living younger brother's wife (Rumā)** while Sugrīva lived — and in dharmaśāstra a younger brother's wife is as a daughter; this is treated as near-incest, a capital offense.
  - As a **vānara (forest animal)**, Vāli was lawful game for a kṣatriya, who may hunt beasts **by snare, ambush, or hidden shot** whether they face him or flee — so the "unfair shot" objection does not bind.
  - Rāma was honoring a sacred **fire-witnessed pact** with Sugrīva (a friend's cause is one's own).
  - The killing **expiated Vāli's sins**, granting him a better destiny than living on in guilt.
- **Vāli's reconciliation** - Persuaded (or at least pacified), Vāli accepts the justice of his death, **commends his son Aṅgada and wife Tārā to Rāma's and Sugrīva's protection**, and begs Sugrīva not to mistreat them; he dies at peace (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 18, 22).

### Variants & the ethics debate over Vāli-vadha

- *The "from behind a tree" reading (mainstream Vālmīki tradition):* Rāma shoots from concealment; the impropriety is real and is precisely what Rāma must justify — the kāṇḍa stages a genuine dharma-dilemma, not a clean kill.
- *Variant (hunting-code defense, Vālmīki Kiṣkindhā 18):* Rāma's own primary answer — vānaras are animals, and the rules of single-combat between equals (dvandva-yuddha) simply do not apply to hunting; this is the scripturally explicit resolution.
- *Variant (the boon-of-strength reading) [folk tradition]:* a widely-told explanation absent from the core Vālmīki text — Vāli possessed a boon (from Brahmā or Indra) draining **half the strength** of any opponent who faced him in direct combat; Rāma therefore could not fight him face-to-face and had to strike from the side. Often cited to exculpate Rāma; treated as later/popular gloss, not canonical Vālmīki.
- *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa / devotional reading) [late text]:* Vāli, dying, recognizes Rāma as **Para-Brahman/Viṣṇu**; being slain by the Lord's own hand is liberation (mokṣa), reframing the killing as supreme grace rather than a juridical punishment.
- *Variant (Rāmacaritamānasa, Tulsīdās) [late text]:* Tulsīdās compresses the polemic and foregrounds Vāli's devotional realization and Rāma's saving intent over the legal argumentation; the bhakti frame softens the dharma-dilemma.
- *Variant (karmic-debt / Tāṭakā-cycle reading) [folk tradition]:* in some tellings the stealth-killing is the working-out of a debt from a prior birth (e.g. linked to the Sugrīva–Vāli rivalry being a destined balancing), used to dissolve the ethical problem cosmologically.
- *Scholarly note [scholarly]:* the **Vāli-vadha** is the Rāmāyaṇa's classic "blemish on Rāma's dharma" passage, debated from the commentators (e.g. the **govindarāja/maheśvaratīrtha** glosses) to modern Indology; many read the long apologia as Vālmīki himself flagging the act as morally hard rather than self-evidently right.

## Sugrīva Enthroned; Aṅgada Made Heir (sargas 24–26)

- **Tārā's lament and the cremation** - Tārā mourns Vāli; Rāma consoles her with teaching on the soul's immortality and the inevitability of death. Vāli's funeral rites are performed; Sugrīva, stricken with remorse, briefly wishes to die with his brother but is dissuaded (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 22–25).
- **Coronation of Sugrīva** - On Rāma's instruction (Rāma himself, in exile, will not enter the city), **Lakṣmaṇa enters Kiṣkindhā and consecrates Sugrīva** as king of the vānaras; **Aṅgada** (Vāli's son) is installed as **yuvarāja (crown prince/heir-apparent)** (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 25–26).
- **Rāma's retreat to Prasravaṇa** - Rāma withdraws with Lakṣmaṇa to a cave on **Mount Prasravaṇa/Mālyavān** to wait out the imminent monsoon, since campaigns cannot move in the rains (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 27).

## The Rainy Season, Sugrīva's Lapse & Lakṣmaṇa's Wrath (sargas 27–33)

- **The varṣā (monsoon) descriptions** - Two long lyrical cantos describe the rains — celebrated as some of Vālmīki's finest nature poetry — during which Rāma pines for Sītā and waits (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 28).
- **Sugrīva's dissipation** - With the rains ended and the harvest of action due, Sugrīva, reunited with Rumā and his new queen, sinks into pleasure and **forgets his oath**, taking no steps to search for Sītā (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 29).
- **Rāma's autumn grief & Lakṣmaṇa dispatched** - As **śarad (autumn)** opens the campaigning season, Rāma's grief turns to anger; he sends an enraged **Lakṣmaṇa** to Kiṣkindhā to remind Sugrīva of his debt — with a threat that the bow that felled Vāli could fell him too (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 30–31).
- **Tārā's diplomacy** - The drunken city alarms Lakṣmaṇa; **Hanumān** and especially the eloquent **Tārā** (now Sugrīva's counsel) pacify Lakṣmaṇa, excuse the delay, and rouse Sugrīva to action. Sugrīva apologizes and orders the vānara hosts summoned at once (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 32–33).

## Mobilizing the Vānara Hosts (sargas 37–38)

- **The world's monkeys assembled** - Sugrīva summons vānara armies from across the earth — millions strong, led by named chiefs (Gaja, Gavākṣa, Gavaya, Śarabha, Gandhamādana, Mainda, Dvivida, Suṣeṇa, Nīla, etc.) — a grand muster review before the search (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 37–39).

## The Four-Quarter Search for Sītā (sargas 39–45)

- **East** - Sugrīva sends a party under **Vinata** to the east, briefing them on the rivers, mountains, and fabled lands as far as the sunrise-mountain Udayagiri (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 40).
- **South** - The decisive party: led by **Aṅgada**, with **Hanumān, Jāmbavān (the bear-king/ṛkṣa), Nīla, Tāra, Suṣeṇa, Nala** and others, sent toward the Vindhyas, the southern ocean, and beyond. Sugrīva, who in his own exile-wanderings knew the south best, gives the most detailed geographic briefing (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 41).
- **The signet-ring** - Rāma gives **Hanumān his ring (aṅgulīya)** bearing his name as a token to convince Sītā of the bearer's authenticity — Rāma's quiet sign that he trusts Hanumān above all (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 44).
- **West** - A party under **Suṣeṇa** (Tārā's father) sent west to the lands of the setting sun and the western ocean (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 42).
- **North** - A party under **Śatabali** sent north past the Himālaya to the trans-Himalayan and uttarakuru regions (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 43).
- **The one-month deadline** - Sugrīva fixes **one month** for all parties to return with news, on pain of death — heightening the urgency that drives the southern party's desperation (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 41–43).

## The Southern Party's Ordeal: Ṛkṣabila & Svayamprabhā (sargas 49–53)

- **Wandering in the Vindhyas** - The southern party searches the Vindhya forests and caves for the deadline-month without success, growing exhausted and despairing (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 49–50).
- **The enchanted cave Ṛkṣabila** - Tormented by thirst, they follow birds into a vast magical cavern (**Ṛkṣabila**) full of golden palaces, food, and gardens, guarded by the ascetic woman **Svayamprabhā** (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 50–51).
- **Svayamprabhā's aid** - She, a disciple/guardian of the divine architect's daughter **Hemā**, feeds the famished vānaras and, by her yogic power, **transports them instantly out of the cave** to the southern seashore — but they have now overshot the one-month deadline and face death for failure (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 52–53).

## Despair, Aṅgada's Fast, and Sampāti (sargas 55–63)

- **Resolve to fast unto death (prāyopaveśa)** - On the ocean shore, with the deadline broken and Sītā unfound, **Aṅgada** and the party resolve to **fast to death** rather than return in disgrace and face Sugrīva's wrath; Aṅgada bitterly recalls Vāli's killing as he despairs (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 55–56).
- **The death of Jaṭāyu retold** - As the vānaras recount aloud the events of the quest — including the death of the vulture **Jaṭāyu** at Rāvaṇa's hands ([Araṇya Kāṇḍa](3-aranya-kanda.md)) — they are overheard (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 57).
- **Sampāti appears** - The aged vulture **Sampāti**, Jaṭāyu's elder brother — wings burned to stumps long ago when he flew too near the sun shielding Jaṭāyu — hears his brother's name and emerges from the hill, at first delighting in what seems an easy meal of starving monkeys (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 56–58).
- **Sampāti's grief and aid** - Learning of Jaṭāyu's heroic death, Sampāti mourns; in gratitude he resolves to help. With his **far-seeing vulture vision** he descries Sītā a hundred yojanas across the sea, **captive in Rāvaṇa's Aśoka grove in Laṅkā**, and tells the vānaras exactly where she is (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 58–59).
- **Sampāti's wings restored** - A sage's old prophecy is fulfilled: on rendering this aid, Sampāti's **wings regrow** and he flies away healed — a sign of dharma rewarded (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 63).
  - *Variant (regional/folk):* some retellings make the wing-restoration conditional on his service to Rāma's cause specifically, sharpening the dharmic reward motif.

## Hanumān Prepared for the Leap (sargas 64–67)

- **The crisis of the hundred-yojana sea** - The vānaras reach the southern coast and despair before the **hundred-yojana ocean** to Laṅkā; the chiefs measure their leaping powers and each falls short — Aṅgada could perhaps reach Laṅkā but might lack strength to return (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 64–65).
- **Jāmbavān awakens Hanumān** - The wise bear-king **Jāmbavān**, seeing Hanumān sitting modestly apart, **reminds Hanumān of his own forgotten divine origin and might** — son of **Vāyu**, who as a child leapt at the sun mistaking it for fruit; only Hanumān can cross the sea (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 65–66).
- **Hanumān swells for the leap** - Roused, Hanumān **grows to colossal size**, salutes the directional guardians (Sūrya, Indra, Vāyu, Brahmā), climbs **Mount Mahendra**, and crouches to spring — the mountain trembling beneath him. The Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa ends on this threshold; the leap itself opens the **Sundara Kāṇḍa** (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 66–67).

## Geography & Connections

- **Kiṣkindhā / Hampi:** tradition firmly locates Kiṣkindhā, Ṛṣyamūka, and Pampā at **Hampi–Anegundi** on the Tungabhadra in Karnataka — a major living pilgrimage and Rāmāyaṇa-memory landscape (see [06-sacred-geography](../../06-sacred-geography/) when added).
- **Forward links:** Hanumān's leap → Sundara Kāṇḍa; the southern bridgehead anticipates the building of **Rāma Setu** in the Yuddha Kāṇḍa.
- **Doctrinal threads:** the Vāli-vadha is a touchstone case for [dharma / the puruṣārthas](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/) (king's daṇḍa, the limits of fair combat, the friend's cause); Rāma's consolation of Tārā restates [karma–saṃsāra–mokṣa](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/) and the immortality of the [ātman](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/).
