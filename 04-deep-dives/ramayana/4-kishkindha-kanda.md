# Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa — Book of Kishkindha

> Cosmic address: [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) ← but the Rāmāyaṇa itself unfolds in the [Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara. Rāma is the 7th [Daśāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) of [Viṣṇu](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md), of the [Sūryavaṃśa / Ikṣvāku line](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
> Parent: [Rāmāyaṇa deep-dive index](README.md) · Part of [04-deep-dives](../README.md). Primary source: Vālmīki [Rāmāyaṇa](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md). Previous: [Araṇya Kāṇḍa](3-aranya-kanda.md) · Next: [Sundara Kāṇḍa](5-sundara-kanda.md).

The **Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa** ("Book of Kishkindha," the vānara kingdom that tradition places at **Hampi/Anegundi**, Karnataka) is the fourth of the seven kāṇḍas of Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa: **67 sargas** in the Southern vulgate, **66** in the Baroda Critical Edition. It is the kāṇḍa of **alliance, regicide, and reconnaissance**: the grieving Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, having lost Sītā at the close of the [Araṇya Kāṇḍa](3-aranya-kanda.md), forge a friendship-pact with the exiled vānara prince **Sugrīva**; Rāma slays Sugrīva's mighty brother **Vāli** from cover (the epic's most-debated act of dharma); Sugrīva is enthroned, lapses into pleasure, is sternly recalled to his vow, and then dispatches vānara armies to the four directions to find Sītā. The southern party — under **Aṅgada**, with **Hanumān** and **Jāmbavān** — is set on the right path by the aged vulture **Sampāti**, and the Kāṇḍa ends with **Hanumān** grown huge on Mount Mahendra, poised for the leap that opens the Sundara Kāṇḍa.

## Editions and numbering

- **Two numberings.** Sarga and verse numbers on this page are the Southern vulgate as printed on valmikiramayan.net (VR). Where the Baroda Critical Edition (CE, GRETIL e-text) differs in substance, its locator is given as "CE 4.x.y".
- **The one-sarga offset.** VR 1–23 = CE 1–23. **VR 24 has no counterpart in the CE**: it holds Sugrīva's remorse and wish to burn himself on Vāli's pyre, and Tārā's plea to be killed by the same arrow. From VR 25 onward, VR *n* = CE *n*−1, so VR 67 = CE 66 (Rāma's funeral-speech VR 25.2 = CE 24.2; the ocean-leap ladder VR 65.3 = CE 64.3).
- **Passages doubted inside the vulgate tradition.** The VR translator notes that sargas **59–63** (Supārśva's report, Niśākara, the regrown wings) are missing from some manuscripts and contain inconsistencies, and are kept because the old commentators commented on them (VR 59, introduction). The CE nevertheless prints them (CE 4.58–62). [disputed]

## Arrival at Ṛṣyamūka & the Meeting with Hanumān (sargas 1–4)

- **The lament at Pampā** - Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa reach the lotus-lake **Pampā** in spring; the blossoming forest and birdsong sharpen Rāma's grief for Sītā, prompting one of the epic's great laments (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 1).
- **Sugrīva's alarm on Ṛṣyamūka** - From **Mount Ṛṣyamūka** — the hill Vāli cannot enter because of sage **Mataṅga**'s curse (told at 11) — the exiled Sugrīva sees two armed strangers in ascetic dress and fears Vāli has sent them to kill him; Hanumān steadies him, and Sugrīva sends his minister to find out who they are (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 2).
- **Hanumān as mendicant envoy** - Hanumān sets aside his monkey form and approaches as a wandering ascetic (*bhikṣu*), introducing himself as Sugrīva's minister and "son of the Wind" (3.2, 3.21–23). Rāma tells Lakṣmaṇa that no one could speak so without knowing the Ṛg-, Yajur- and Sāma-veda; his grammar is perfect and not one word was wrong (3.28–29). Lakṣmaṇa tells their story (4). Hanumān resumes his own form and **carries both princes on his back** to Sugrīva (4.34), who has moved from Ṛṣyamūka to Mount **Malaya** (5.1).
  - *Note:* this is Hanumān's entry into the main action. His birth-story is told later by Jāmbavān on the ocean shore (Kiṣkindhā 66), and Vālmīki tells it again at [Uttara](7-uttara-kanda.md) 35–36; the two accounts differ in detail. See also [Hanumān](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md).

## The Fire-Witnessed Friendship-Pact (sargas 5–6)

- **The agni-sākṣi alliance** - Sugrīva offers his hand; Hanumān kindles fire with two sticks and sets it between them; Rāma and Sugrīva circumambulate it and become friends (**sakhya**) with **Agni** as witness (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 5.11–16). Rāma vows to kill Vāli, "the abductor of your wife" (5 end), and Sugrīva promises to bring Sītā back (6.5–6). Rāma later tells the dying Vāli that he gave Sugrīva this promise before the vānaras and cannot break it (18.26–27), and Vāli himself reminds Sugrīva that he made the friendship before fire (22.15).
- **The tokens of Sītā** - Sugrīva says he saw Sītā carried through the sky crying out, and fetches the upper cloth and ornaments she dropped among five monkeys on a mountaintop ([Araṇya](3-aranya-kanda.md) 54.1–3). Rāma takes them up and breaks down (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 6.7–20). This is the first physical trace of the abduction's route.
- **The anklet verse — Lakṣmaṇa's, not Rāma's** - Rāma asks Lakṣmaṇa to look at the jewels, and it is **Lakṣmaṇa** who answers: *nāhaṃ jānāmi keyūre nāhaṃ jānāmi kuṇḍale / nūpure tv abhijānāmi nityaṃ pādābhivandanāt* — "I do not know the armlets, I do not know the earrings; the anklets I know, from bowing daily at her feet" (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 6.22; the third line runs on under the number of 6.23).
  - *Note:* the VR translator's note says the couplet is "not found in ancient manuscripts or in Baroda version", and the CE's sarga 6 (23 verses) indeed has no *nūpura* verse. The vulgate keeps it, and the traditional commentators read it as the type of Lakṣmaṇa's *kaiṅkarya* (service). [disputed]

## Establishing Rāma's Prowess: The Feud, Dundubhi & the Sāla Trees (sargas 7–12)

- **The Māyāvī backstory** - Sugrīva explains the feud. The asura **Māyāvī** — elder brother of **Dundubhi** and son of **Maya** — had a long-standing enmity with Vāli "over a woman" (*strīkṛtam*, 9.4). He challenged Vāli at night and fled into a grass-covered cavern; Vāli made Sugrīva swear to wait at the mouth and went in. After more than a year, foaming blood gushed out and only the demon's cries were heard; Sugrīva sealed the cave with a boulder, offered water to his "dead" brother and went home, where the ministers made him king (9.11–21). Vāli killed Māyāvī, kicked the boulder away, returned, accused Sugrīva of trapping him, drove him out "with a single cloth" and **took his wife Rumā** (10.17–27).
  - *Note:* this cave is **not** the Ṛkṣabila. Ṛkṣabila is Svayamprabhā's enchanted cavern in the Vindhyas, reached only in sargas 50–52.
- **Vāli's strength and Dundubhi** - Sugrīva narrates Vāli's might: he strides from ocean to ocean before dawn and tosses mountain-peaks like balls (11.4–5). The buffalo-demon **Dundubhi** challenged Ocean, who sent him to Himavān, who sent him to Vāli at Kiṣkindhā's gate (11.7–24). Vāli, putting on the golden garland "given by his father Mahendra" (11.39; CE 4.11.37), killed him and hurled the carcass **one yojana** (11.47); blood-drops fell in **Mataṅga**'s hermitage.
- **Mataṅga's curse** - The sage cursed the thrower: if Vāli sets foot within **a yojana** of the hermitage he will die, and any of Vāli's monkeys found there the next day will be turned to stone for thousands of years (11.53–58). Vāli begged and was refused, and since then he will not enter or even look at Ṛṣyamūka (11.62–64). The CE's shorter curse says only that Vāli will die if he enters, with no yojana radius (CE 4.11.42). Sugrīva later tells it as Hanumān's recollection: Vāli's head would split into a hundred pieces (46.21–22).
- **The test Sugrīva sets** - Dundubhi's bones still lie like a hill, and Vāli could strip each of the seven sāla trees of leaves, **one tree at a time** (11.66–67, 11.70). Sugrīva asks Rāma to split one sāla with one arrow and to kick the skeleton **two hundred bow-lengths** (11.71–72). In the CE, Sugrīva asks for all the sālas to be pierced with one arrow (CE 4.11.49).
- **Kicking Dundubhi's skeleton** - Rāma flicks the dried skeleton with his big toe **ten yojanas** (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 11.84–85; CE 4.11.50). Sugrīva objects that Vāli threw it fresh, wet and heavy with flesh, while Rāma kicked dry bones, so the feat proves nothing (11.87–89).
- **The seven sāla trees** - Rāma looses a single golden arrow that goes through **all seven** sālas, the mountain-side and into the earth, then comes back up and re-enters his quiver; Sugrīva falls at his feet (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 12.1–9).
- **The first, failed challenge** - At Kiṣkindhā Sugrīva roars his challenge while the others hide in the thickets. The brothers look alike "in ornament, dress, build and movement", voice and glow, so Rāma, unable to tell them apart, holds his arrow; Vāli thrashes Sugrīva and chases him back to Ṛṣyamūka, where Sugrīva reproaches Rāma (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 12.13–34).
- **The garland sign** - At Rāma's word **Lakṣmaṇa** pulls up a flowering **gajapuṣpī** creeper and hangs it on Sugrīva's neck as the mark for the rematch (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 12.39–40).

## The Second Duel & the Slaying of Vāli (sargas 13–18)

- **The Saptajana hermitage** - Walking back to Kiṣkindhā, Sugrīva points out the hermitage of the **Saptajanas**, seven ascetics who lay in water head-down, ate only air once in seven days, and after seven hundred years rose bodily to heaven; their grove is unassailable "even for Indra with gods and asuras", and creatures that stray in do not come out. Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa salute it as they pass (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 13.17–28).
- **The second challenge** - The party again hides itself among trees (*vṛkṣair ātmānam āvṛtya*, 14.1; CE 4.14.1); Rāma promises that Vāli will be rolling in the dust, and Sugrīva roars (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 14).
- **Tārā's warning** - Queen **Tārā** stops Vāli at the door. A brother so recently beaten would not come back roaring unless he had a strong backer, and Aṅgada's spies have reported that the Ikṣvāku princes Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa are with Sugrīva. She urges Vāli to make peace with Rāma and to **install Sugrīva as yuvarāja** (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 15.10–24). Vālmīki has Vāli name her later as "daughter of **Suṣeṇa**" and a sure judge of omens and fine points (22.13).
- **The fatal duel** - Vāli sends Tārā back, saying he will only humble Sugrīva, not take his life (16.7). The brothers fight with fists, trees and rocks until Sugrīva weakens and looks about for Rāma. Rāma's arrow strikes Vāli in the **chest**, and he falls like Indra's banner thrown down after the festival (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 16.17–39). The arrow does not kill at once: the gem-studded golden garland **given by Śakra** keeps his life, glow and splendour in him (17.5; CE 4.17.5).
- **Vāli's dharma-indictment of Rāma** - Dying, Vāli delivers a celebrated rebuke (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 17). Rāma struck one who was turned away and engaged with another (17.16, 17.21). Vāli did him no harm in his country or city; he is a forest beast living on fruit with no dispute over land or gold (17.24–31). His skin, bones and meat are of no use to Rāma's kind (17.38–40). Had Rāma shown himself, he would have died (17.47); as it is, he has been killed "unseen", like a sleeper bitten by a snake (17.48).
  - *Vulgate:* had Rāma asked him first, he would have brought Sītā back "in a single day" and Rāvaṇa bound by the neck (17.49–50). The CE has the offer to deliver Rāvaṇa bound by the neck and to fetch Sītā even from the ocean or the underworld (CE 4.17.41–42; VR 17.51), but not "in a single day".
- **Rāma's justification** - Rāma answers point by point (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 18), the core of the ethics debate:
  - The earth with its forests and creatures belongs to the **Ikṣvākus**, and its ruler is **Bharata**; Rāma and others travel it under his order and punish those who depart from dharma (18.6–11, 18.23–25).
  - A younger brother, a son and a disciple stand as sons. Vāli has taken **Rumā**, his living brother's wife, whom Rāma calls his *snuṣā* (daughter-in-law) (18.13–14, 18.19). The remembered law prescribes death for one who goes to his own daughter, sister or younger brother's wife (18.22–23).
  - Rāma gave a promise to Sugrīva before the vānaras and cannot break it (18.26–27).
  - Two verses "sung by **Manu**": kings who punish sinners make them pure and fit for heaven, and a king who fails to punish takes on the thief's sin (18.30–32). They match Manusmṛti 8.318 and 8.316. The CE keeps the first verse without naming Manu (CE 4.18.30). His ancestor **Māndhātā** punished a śramaṇa for a like sin (18.33).
  - As a **śākhāmṛga**, a tree-branch animal, Vāli was lawful game: hunters take beasts **with nets, snares and hidden or open shots**, whether they run or stand, are wary or unwary, or have turned away, and royal sages hunt too (18.37–40; CE 4.18.34–36).
  - Kings are gods walking the earth in human form and are not to be reviled (18.42). The punishment has **cleansed Vāli of his sin** (18.61–62).
- **Vāli's reconciliation** - Vāli concedes that Rāma is right, asks pardon for his words, and **commends Aṅgada, and Tārā, to Rāma's protection**, asking that Sugrīva not hold Tārā to blame (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 18.44–66). He speaks his last words to Sugrīva and Aṅgada in sarga 22.
- **The golden garland handed on** - Reviving briefly, Vāli tells Sugrīva to take the kingdom, to care for Aṅgada as his own son, to follow Tārā's counsel, and to carry out Rāma's work, since he swore before fire (22.5–15). He gives Sugrīva the divine golden **mālā**: the noble *śrī* that dwells in it will leave it when he dies (22.16; CE 4.22.16). He counsels Aṅgada to serve Sugrīva with neither too much friendship nor too little, and dies (22.19–24). The vulgate lament of the vānaras adds that Vāli once fought the gandharva **Golabha** for fifteen years and killed him in the sixteenth (22.28–30), lines absent from the CE.

### Variants & the ethics debate over Vāli-vadha

- *The concealment (Vālmīki):* Rāma shoots from cover. The text says so in its own voice (the party hides among trees, 14.1), and Vāli charges him with it (17.16, 17.48). The impropriety is real and is exactly what Rāma has to answer; the kāṇḍa stages a dharma-dilemma, not a clean kill.
- *The hunting-code defense (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 18.37–40):* one strand of Rāma's answer is that a vānara is a forest beast, and the rules of combat between equals do not apply to hunting. It stands beside, not in place of, the royal-punishment argument (18.6–33).
- *Variant (the half-strength boon) [commentarial][folk]:* a widely told explanation absent from the verse text of both the vulgate and the CE. A verse quoted in the VR commentary to 17.48 (its source is not named there) says **Brahmā** granted Vāli a boon that anyone facing him loses half his strength to him. Rāma, the note argues, honoured the boon by not facing him; the same site's introduction to sarga 16 also speaks of "the boon and Indra's chest-plate". What the verses do give Vāli is Śakra's golden **mālā**, which holds his life after the arrow (17.5) and carries *śrī*, royal fortune (22.16). Tārā too speaks of it as the garland Indra gave him (CE 4.23.28). It is a talisman of fortune, not a drain on an opponent's strength.
- *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa) [late text]:* Vāli already tells Tārā before the fight that Rāma is **Nārāyaṇa himself**, come to lift the earth's burden, and that he will bow at his feet (Adhyātma, Kiṣkindhā 2.34–37). Rāma shoots unseen from a thicket (2.45). His answer is the incest-law argument (daughter, sister, brother's wife, daughter-in-law), not a hunting argument (2.59–62). Vāli then hails Rāma as Parameśvara and asks for Aṅgada's care. Rāma draws out the arrow and touches him, and Vāli leaves the monkey body for the highest state (2.65–71). See [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md).
- *Variant (Rāmacaritamānasa, Tulsīdās) [late text]:* Rāma watches the fight "from behind a tree" (*biṭapa oṭa*, Kiṣkindhā 8.4). Vāli asks why he, the enemy, was killed while Sugrīva is the favourite (*maiṃ bairī sugrīva piārā*), and Rāma answers that a younger brother's wife, a sister, a son's wife and a daughter are equal (*anuja badhū bhaginī suta nārī … kanyā sama e cārī*) (9.3–4). Rāma then **offers to make Vāli's body whole and let him live** (*acala karauṃ tanu rākhahu prānā*). Vāli refuses — who would cut down a wish-tree to fence a thorn-bush? — asks Rāma to take Aṅgada as his servant, and dies with his love fixed on Rāma's feet (10 and its chands). Neither the offer of life nor the refusal is in Vālmīki. See [Rāmacaritamānasa](recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md).
- *Variant (Vāli reborn as the hunter Jarā) [folk][late text]:* a popular tradition holds that the hidden shot is repaid in the Dvāpara: Vāli is reborn as the hunter **Jarā**, whose arrow kills [Kṛṣṇa](../krishna-lila.md). The corpus's [Sugrīva and Vāli](../characters/sugriva-and-vali.md) page attributes it to the *Mahānāṭaka* and *Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa*. It is not in Vālmīki, and the Mahābhārata's own account of Jarā shooting Kṛṣṇa in the heel does not name Vāli (Mausala Parva, Ganguli section 4).
- *Variant (Jain Rāmāyaṇas) [late text]:* in Vimalasūri's *Paumacariya*, Vāli voluntarily makes Sugrīva king and becomes a monk, so no Vāli-vadha takes place (Kulkarni, *Story of Rama in Jain Literature*). In Guṇabhadra's *Uttarapurāṇa*, Lakṣmaṇa kills Vāli. See [Jain and Buddhist Rāmāyaṇas](recensions/jain-and-buddhist-ramayanas.md).
- *Scholarly note [scholarly]:* the **Vāli-vadha** is the Rāmāyaṇa's classic "blemish on Rāma's dharma" passage. It has been debated from the old commentators (the VR notes on sargas 17–18 cite Maheśvaratīrtha's commentary and the *Dharmākūtam*, and the VR introduction to sarga 24 names Govindarāja among the commentators) to modern Indology, and many readers take the long apologia as Vālmīki himself flagging the act as morally hard rather than self-evidently right.

## Sugrīva Enthroned; Aṅgada Made Heir (sargas 19–27)

- **Laments and the cremation** - The vānara chiefs urge Tārā to crown Aṅgada at once or flee; she refuses and goes to the body (19). She laments over Vāli (20, 23) and refuses Hanumān's consolation, choosing to die with her husband (21). In the vulgate's sarga 24, Sugrīva in remorse asks leave to enter his brother's pyre, and Tārā begs Rāma to kill her with the same arrow; Rāma consoles her with the teaching that the Creator has ordained the world and its joys and sorrows, which no one can pass beyond (24). This sarga is absent from the CE. Rāma then tells them that grief does not help the dead and the rites must go on, and Vāli is cremated with royal honours (25; CE 4.24) (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 19–25).
- **Coronation of Sugrīva** - **Neither brother enters Kiṣkindhā.** Hanumān invites Rāma into the city for the consecration. Rāma replies that, obeying his father's order, he will enter no village or city for fourteen years (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 26.9–10; CE 4.25.9). Sugrīva goes in, brāhmaṇas offer oblations into the fire, and the **vānara chiefs — Gaja, Gavākṣa, Gavaya, Śarabha, Gandhamādana, Mainda, Dvivida, Hanumān, Jāmbavān (the CE adds Nala) — pour the consecration water** over him on a throne set facing east (26.29–36; CE 4.25.32). At Rāma's word Sugrīva embraces **Aṅgada** and installs him as **yuvarāja**, and regains Rumā (26.12–13, 26.38–42).
  - *Note:* the widely repeated line that "Lakṣmaṇa entered Kiṣkindhā and consecrated Sugrīva" is retelling-gloss. Vālmīki keeps both brothers outside the walls throughout; the abhiṣeka is performed by the vānaras' own chiefs.
- **The Kārtika covenant** - In the same scene Rāma fixes the campaign clock. This is Śrāvaṇa, the first of the four rain-months, and no time for effort; **"when Kārtika comes, strive for Rāvaṇa's death — this is our compact"** (26.14–17; CE 4.25.12–15). This is the first of the kāṇḍa's three deadlines, and the one Sugrīva will overrun.
- **Rāma's retreat to Prasravaṇa** - Rāma withdraws with Lakṣmaṇa to a cave on **Mount Prasravaṇa**, on the ridge of **Mālyavān**, to wait out the rains (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 27; CE 4.26.1, 4.27.1). The VR introduction notes that the CE has 23 verses here against the vulgate's 48.

## The Rainy Season, Sugrīva's Lapse & Lakṣmaṇa's Wrath (sargas 28–36)

- **The varṣā (monsoon) descriptions** - A long lyrical canto describes the rains — celebrated as some of Vālmīki's finest nature poetry — during which Rāma pines for Sītā and waits (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 28).
- **Hanumān's counsel and the fifteen-day order** - It is **Hanumān** who first rouses Sugrīva, before Rāma ever complains. The sky has cleared, the kingdom is won, and the friend's work remains; a friend who delays his friend's business loses the friend (29.1–15). Sugrīva orders **Nīla** to muster every vānara host: **any monkey who arrives after fifteen nights pays with his life**, with no inquiry (29.31–32; CE 4.28.31 *tripañcarātrād ūrdhvam*). This is the kāṇḍa's second deadline.
- **Sugrīva's dissipation** - Having given the order, Sugrīva goes back into his inner apartments (29.33). When Lakṣmaṇa comes, Sugrīva is still there in private with Tārā and does not heed the monkeys who report his arrival (31.22); Aṅgada later finds him there with Tārā and Rumā (31.36–38).
- **Rāma's autumn grief & Lakṣmaṇa dispatched** - As **śarad (autumn)** comes, Rāma's grief turns to anger, and he sends **Lakṣmaṇa** with a message: "the path by which slain Vāli went is not closed; keep to the compact, Sugrīva, do not follow Vāli's path" (30.81; CE 4.29.48; repeated by Lakṣmaṇa to Sugrīva at 34.18). Lakṣmaṇa wants to kill Sugrīva and let Aṅgada lead the search; Rāma restrains him and tells him to speak gently, since Sugrīva's only fault is the lapsed time (31.4–8).
- **Lakṣmaṇa at the gate** - Huge vānaras armed with trees and rocks file out under the gate to face him, which inflames him again (31.23–29). He sends **Aṅgada** in. Sugrīva, drunk, barely understands, until the uproar wakes him and his ministers **Plakṣa** and **Prabhāva** tell him to receive Lakṣmaṇa humbly (31.32–51). **Hanumān** tells Sugrīva that delay is his only fault and that he must beg Lakṣmaṇa's pardon (32). Lakṣmaṇa walks through the splendid city, and at the inner apartments, unwilling to enter among the women, he twangs his bowstring (33).
- **Tārā's diplomacy** - Sugrīva sends **Tārā**, tipsy and unabashed, to meet him (33.37–40). Lakṣmaṇa will not look at her and lowers his anger in a woman's presence (33.39). She tells him that desire makes even great sages forget time and that the summons has long since gone out (33.51–61). Inside, after Lakṣmaṇa's rebuke (34), she argues the case at length (35). **Viśvāmitra** spent ten years with the apsaras **Ghṛtācī** and thought it a day (35.7–8). Rāvaṇa's forces, as Vāli used to say, are too many to beat without allies (35.15–18). The hosts are due **this very day** (35.19–22). Lakṣmaṇa relents. Sugrīva tears off his garland, apologises, and says he owes the kingdom to Rāma alone, whose one arrow split seven sālas, and that his own help will be help in name only (36.3–11) (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 33–36).

## Mobilizing the Vānara Hosts (sargas 37–39)

- **The world's monkeys assembled** - Messengers fetch the vānaras of every mountain and forest, bringing Himālayan herbs and fruit as tribute (37). Sugrīva goes to Rāma on Prasravaṇa and falls at his feet (38), and the hosts pour in as Rāma is speaking (39). The chiefs come each with an army counted in crores. **Śatabali** brings "ten thousand crores" (39.14), and **Suṣeṇa**, Tārā's father, comes (39.15). So do **Tāra**, father of Rumā and Sugrīva's father-in-law (39.16), and **Kesarī, Hanumān's father** (39.17). Then Gavākṣa of the baboons, Dhūmra of the bears, Panasa, Nīla, Gavaya, Darīmukha, Mainda and Dvivida "sons of the Aśvins", Gaja, Jāmbavān with his bears, Rumaṇa, Gandhamādana, Aṅgada, Tāra, Indrajānu, Rambha, Durmukha, Hanumān, Nala, Dadhimukha, Śarabha, Kumuda and Vahni (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 39.14–39).

## The Four-Quarter Search for Sītā (sargas 40–46)

- **East** - Rāma asks first to know whether Sītā lives and where Rāvaṇa dwells, and leaves the orders to Sugrīva (40.11–14). The party under **Vinata** is briefed from the Bhāgīrathī, Sarayū, Kauśikī, Yamunā, Sarasvatī, Sindhu and Śoṇa rivers, past Videha, Kāśi, Kosala, Magadha, Puṇḍra and Aṅga (40.20–23), and over the sea. Then come **Yavadvīpa** "of seven kingdoms" with its gold and silver islands and Mount **Śiśira** (40.30–32), and the Śoṇa again beyond the sea. Last come the oceans of the Purāṇic world and, at its end, **Udaya**, the golden sunrise mountain. Beyond it the east is impassable, hedged by gods and without sun or moon (40.35–66; CE 4.39).
- **South** - The decisive party is led by **Aṅgada**. Sugrīva names Nīla son of Agni, Hanumān, Jāmbavān "son of Pitāmaha", Suhotra, Śarāri, Śaragulma, Gaja, Gavākṣa, Gavaya, Suṣeṇa, Ṛṣabha, Mainda, Dvivida, a second Suṣeṇa, Gandhamādana, and the fire-born Ulkāmukha and Anaṅga (41.2–4). The CE reads Vijaya for the second Suṣeṇa and Asaṅga for Anaṅga (CE 4.40.2–4). The counsellor **Tāra** is not in the list but travels with the party (48.1, 53.25). The route runs from the Vindhya, Narmadā, Godāvarī and Kṛṣṇaveṇī, through Vidarbha, Kaliṅga, the Daṇḍaka, the Āndhras, Puṇḍras, Colas, Pāṇḍyas and Keralas (41.8–12), to Mount **Malaya** with the sage **Agastya** (41.13–16). After that the Tāmraparṇī, the **golden gate of the Pāṇḍyas** (41.18; CE 4.40.19) and **Mahendra**, with a hundred-yojana island across the sea that is Rāvaṇa's seat (41.19–25). In mid-ocean waits the rākṣasī **Aṅgārakā, who seizes her prey by its shadow** (41.26; CE 4.40.26). Beyond lie the mountains Puṣpitaka, Sūryavān, Vaidyuta and Kuñjara, where Viśvakarman built Agastya's house; **Bhogavatī**, the serpent city of **Vāsuki** (41.36–37); Ṛṣabha with its gandharva-guarded sandal-woods; and finally the dark **Pitṛloka, Yama's capital**, where no one is to go (41.27–45). The east briefing, not this one, is the longest of the four.
- **West** - The party under **Suṣeṇa**, Tārā's father and Sugrīva's father-in-law (42.1–2, 42.53), goes through Surāṣṭra and Bāhlīka to the Indus mouth and on to Pāriyātra, whose fire-like gandharvas are not to be provoked (42.7–24). It passes **Cakravān**, where the Supreme Person killed **Pañcajana and the dānava Hayagrīva** and took the discus and conch (42.27–28; CE 4.41.21–22). Then Mount Varāha with **Prāgjyotiṣa**, city of the demon Naraka (42.30–31); Meghavān, where the gods anointed Indra (42.35); and the golden **Meru**, made gold by the Sun's boon. Last comes **Asta**, the sunset mountain, with Varuṇa's palace and the sage **Merusāvarṇi** (42.38–49).
- **North** - The party under **Śatabali** (43.1–3) goes through the Mlecchas, Pulindas, Śūrasenas, Kurus, Madras, Kāmbojas, Yavanas, Śakas and Daradas into the Himālaya (43.11–12). Then Mount Kāla, Sudarśana and Devasakha, a hundred-yojana wasteland, **Kailāsa** with **Kubera**'s mansion, the tunnel of **Krauñca**, and Maināka with Maya's house (43.14–30). The **Śailodā** river is crossed on *kīcaka* bamboos (43.37). **Uttarakuru**, home of the meritorious, has trees that yield clothes, ornaments and food, and river sands of pearl and gold (43.38–52). Beyond it, in the northern ocean, stands the golden **Somagiri**: sunless yet lit by its own glow, where Viṣṇu, Śambhu and Brahmā sojourn and where no one may go (43.53–58).
- **The cosmography of the briefings** - Together the four speeches are the epic's fullest world-map, a real Indian itinerary that opens into the Purāṇic world-islands. In the east lie the shadow-seizing monsters of the ocean, **Garuḍa's mansion** on Śālmali, and the **Mandehas**, demons who hang head-down from mountain-peaks and fall into the sea each sunrise, burned by the sun, only to return (40.36–43; CE 4.39.31–37). The vulgate adds that the force of the Gāyatrī fells them (40.42); the CE names only the sun. There too are the **milk ocean** with the white mountain Ṛṣabha, and the **Vaḍavāmukha**, the horse-faced fire born of a sage's wrath that feeds on the waves (40.43–49; CE 4.39.44). **Ananta** holds the earth on the golden mountain (40.50–53), and on the peak **Saumanasa** Viṣṇu set his first stride as Trivikrama (40.57–58). In the south are the **Pitṛloka** and **Bhogavatī**, in the west Meru and Asta, and in the north **Uttarakuru** and Somagiri.
- **The one-month deadline** - Each party is given **one month**: whoever stays longer will be killed, and whoever comes back within the month saying "Sītā is seen" will live like the king himself (40.69–70, 41.47–48, 42.52–53; CE 4.39.61–62, 4.40.45–46). This is the third and tightest of the kāṇḍa's clocks, and it drives the southern party to the edge of suicide.
- **The signet-ring** - Seeing that Sugrīva relies on Hanumān above all, Rāma reasons that the task will succeed through him. He gives Hanumān his **ring (aṅgulīya) engraved with his own name** as a token for Sītā, and Hanumān sets it on his head and bows (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 44.8–15).
- **The send-off** - The hosts take their leave with boasts of what each will do to Rāvaṇa, and scatter to the quarters (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 45).
- **Sugrīva's world-knowledge explained** - Rāma asks how Sugrīva knows the whole earth so well. Sugrīva answers that **Vāli chased him through every quarter** — east to Udaya and the milk ocean, south through the Vindhyas, west to Asta, north to the Himālaya, Meru and the northern ocean. At last Hanumān remembered Mataṅga's curse, and they took refuge on Ṛṣyamūka (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 46.10–22).

## The Southern Party's Ordeal: Ṛkṣabila & Svayamprabhā (sargas 47–54)

- **The empty returns** - The east, west, and north parties come back within the month with nothing; all hopes rest on Hanumān, who has gone the way Sītā was carried (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 47).
- **Kaṇḍu's dead forest and the false Rāvaṇa** - The southern party searches the Vindhyas and enters a waterless, lifeless country cursed by the sage **Kaṇḍu**, whose ten-year-old son died there (48.6–15). In another forest an asura charges them; **Aṅgada takes him for Rāvaṇa** and kills him with a blow of his palm, and they search his cave in vain (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 48.17–24).
- **Aṅgada's rally** - Much time has gone, and Sugrīva is a harsh master. Aṅgada holds them together: persistence, skill and a firm heart win results, despair loses them. They search the Vindhyas again (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 49).
- **The enchanted cave Ṛkṣabila** - Faint with thirst, they follow water-birds wet with lotus-pollen out of a vast cavern, **Ṛkṣabila**. The month set by Sugrīva has already run out while they were still searching the Vindhya (50.3), and in the narrator's and Aṅgada's later words it passes in the mountains and inside the cave (53.2, 53.8). Beyond the darkness they find golden trees, mansions and food, and an aged ascetic woman in bark and black deerskin, **Svayamprabhā** (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 50–51.1).
- **Whose cave it is** - Svayamprabhā tells them that **Maya**, the dānava craftsman, made this golden wood by his art after winning a boon from Brahmā through thousands of years of tapas. **Indra** killed him with the thunderbolt when Maya was attached to the apsaras **Hemā**. Brahmā gave the wood and the golden house to Hemā, and **Svayamprabhā, daughter of Merusāvarṇi**, guards it for her friend (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 51.10–18; CE 4.50.10–16).
  - *Note:* Hemā is **not** the architect's daughter — she is the apsaras Maya was attached to; the architect is **Maya**, whose sons Māyāvī and Dundubhi are the two demons whose deaths began the Vāli–Sugrīva feud (Kiṣkindhā 9–11).
- **Svayamprabhā's aid** - She feeds them. Hanumān explains that they have overstayed Sugrīva's month in her cave. Since no one who enters leaves alive, she has them shut their eyes and by her tapas **sets them down outside** in an instant, pointing out the Vindhya, Prasravaṇa and the southern ocean (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 52.21–31).
- **Aṅgada's despair and the cave-secession proposal** - On the shore they see trees in spring flower: the month counted from Āśvayuja is gone (53.2–9). **Aṅgada** proposes fasting to death. Sugrīva is harsh by nature and his enemy, and he, Aṅgada, was made yuvarāja by Rāma, not by Sugrīva (53.13–19). Then the counsellor **Tāra** urges them to go back into Maya's cave, where there is food and water and no fear of Indra, Rāma or Sugrīva, and the vānaras incline to it (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 53.25–27).
- **Hanumān breaks it** - **Hanumān** sees that Aṅgada is being drawn into seizing the kingdom (54.1) and breaks the plot with the four political means, chiefly ***bheda***, division. Monkeys are fickle and will not stay away from wives and sons for him, and Jāmbavān, Nīla, Suhotra and Hanumān himself will not follow (54.6–11). He adds *daṇḍa*: Indra's thunderbolt pierced that cave once, and Lakṣmaṇa's arrows would split it open (54.13–15). Then *sāma*: go back, and Sugrīva, who has no other heir and is devoted to Tārā, will keep him as heir (54.20–22). The secession collapses (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 54). It is the kāṇḍa's one open contest of political skill, and Hanumān wins it against the crown prince.

## Despair, Aṅgada's Fast, and Sampāti (sargas 55–63)

- **Resolve to fast unto death (prāyopaveśa)** - Aṅgada still will not return. He casts blame on Sugrīva and lies down on darbha grass on the shore, and the others sit with him to fast to death (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 55).
- **The death of Jaṭāyu retold** - Lying there, the vānaras talk over Rāma's exile, Daśaratha's death, Janasthāna and Jaṭāyu (55 end). **Aṅgada** laments aloud that the vulture **Jaṭāyu**, killed by Rāvaṇa ([Araṇya Kāṇḍa](3-aranya-kanda.md)), was luckier than they, and traces the whole chain back to Kaikeyī's boons (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 56.9–16).
- **Sampāti appears** - The aged vulture **Sampāti**, Jaṭāyu's elder brother, comes out of a cave of the Vindhya and is pleased at first: fate has sent him food, to be eaten one monkey a day (56.1–5). Hearing his brother's name, he asks to be brought down from the heights, since the sun burned his wings (56.18–24). The frightened vānaras bring him down (57.1–4), and Aṅgada tells him their story (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 56–57).
- **Sampāti's grief and his intelligence** - Sampāti tells how, at the time of Vṛtra's slaying, he and Jaṭāyu flew at the sun in rivalry, and he spread his wings over his fainting brother and fell burned on the Vindhya (58.4–7). He says he **himself saw** a young woman carried off by Rāvaṇa, crying "Rāma, Lakṣmaṇa" and throwing down her ornaments (58.15–18). He names Rāvaṇa, son of Viśravas and brother of Kubera, and **Laṅkā**, built by Viśvakarman on an island a hundred yojanas out. There Sītā is kept **in Rāvaṇa's inner palace** under rākṣasī guard (58.19–24). Vultures, he explains, fly in the highest of the bird-paths and see a hundred yojanas (58.26–32). Only then is he carried to the sea to offer **water to Jaṭāyu** (58.35–36).
- **Where the intelligence came from** - In the next sarga Sampāti gives a second source. His son **Supārśva**, who feeds him, once came home at sunset from Mount Mahendra with no meat. He had let pass a being dark as a heap of collyrium, carrying a woman bright as the dawn, because the stranger begged to be let through (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 59.7–16; CE 4.58.8–14). The two accounts sit side by side in the text, an inconsistency the VR translator notes for sargas 59–63.
- **Sampāti's own story** - Sampāti tells the vānaras how, after his fall, he crawled down to the hermitage of the sage **Niśākara**, whose feet he and Jaṭāyu, in human form, had often touched (60.8–20). Here the flight is a wager made before the sages on Kailāsa, to follow the sun to the sunset mountain (61.3–4). Niśākara foretold Rāma's story: Sītā would refuse Rāvaṇa's food, and **Indra** would send her divine food, which she would first offer to Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa (62.3–10). Sampāti was to wait and tell Rāma's messengers where Sītā is, and then his **wings would grow again** (62.11–14) (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 60–62).
- **Sampāti's wings restored** - As he speaks, new reddish feathers grow; he takes this as a sign that they will find Sītā, and flies off to try his wings. The vānaras set out south at an auspicious hour (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 63.8–15; CE 4.62.8–10).

## Hanumān Prepared for the Leap (sargas 64–67)

- **The crisis of the hundred-yojana sea** - The vānaras reach the southern coast and despair before the **hundred-yojana ocean** to Laṅkā; Aṅgada asks each chief how far he can leap (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 64).
- **The ladder of leaps** - The chiefs answer in ascending order (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 65.3–9). Gaja can leap 10 yojanas and Gavākṣa 20. Then come Śarabha 30 and Ṛṣabha 40 (the CE has Gavaya 30 and Śarabha 40, CE 4.64.4–5), Gandhamādana 50, Mainda 60, Dvivida 70 and Suṣeṇa 80. **Jāmbavān** says age has cut him to ninety, though once he circled **Viṣṇu as Trivikrama** (65.13–15). **Aṅgada** could cross the hundred but doubts he could come back (65.19). Jāmbavān answers that the leader is not to be sent: the one who drives the task must be kept safe (65.20–27). Aṅgada then threatens to resume the fast, and Jāmbavān turns to Hanumān, sitting apart (65.29–35).
- **Jāmbavān awakens Hanumān** - Jāmbavān **recites Hanumān to himself** (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 66). His arms are as strong as Garuḍa's wings (66.4–6). His mother **Añjanā**, wife of Kesarī, is the apsaras Puñjikasthalā, born a vānara through a curse (66.8–10). **Vāyu** embraced her unseen and promised her a son his equal in flight (66.12–20). As a child he leapt at the rising sun, taking it for a fruit, and Indra's thunderbolt broke his **left jaw** (*hanu*), hence the name "Hanumān" (66.21–24). When the angry Wind stopped blowing, **Brahmā** granted him immunity from all weapons and **Indra** death only at his own will (66.25–29). Jāmbavān adds that in his own youth he circled the earth twenty-one times during Trivikrama's strides and gathered the herbs for the Churning (66.32–33). Only Hanumān can cross.
  - *Note:* the curse that makes Hanumān forget his strength is in Vālmīki's [Uttara](7-uttara-kanda.md), not here. Sages of the **Bhṛgu and Aṅgiras** lines curse the child that "for a long time" (*dīrghakālam*) he will not know his strength, and Agastya ties this to his inaction during the Vāli–Sugrīva feud (CE 7.36.32–33, 7.36.39–40). Jāmbavān's speech in Kiṣkindhā 66 does not mention a curse. The rule that the curse lifts when someone reminds him is given in the VR translator's note to sarga 66, not in the verses. [commentarial]
- **Hanumān swells for the leap** - Roused, Hanumān **grows to colossal size**. He boasts that he could circle Meru a thousand times, race the sun from rising to setting, or bring back the nectar or Laṅkā itself (67.9–28). Jāmbavān blesses him, and the vānaras promise to stand on one foot until he returns (67.30–34). He climbs **Mount Mahendra**, which shakes, spouts water and sheds its gandharvas, sages and snakes under his tread (67.38–48), and fixes his mind on Laṅkā (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 67.49; CE 4.66). The salutation to Sūrya, Mahendra, Pavana, Svayambhū and the beings, made before the leap, opens the **[Sundara Kāṇḍa](5-sundara-kanda.md)** (CE 5.1.7).

## Geography & Connections

- **Kiṣkindhā / Hampi:** tradition locates Kiṣkindhā, Ṛṣyamūka, and Pampā at **Hampi–Anegundi** on the Tungabhadra in Karnataka, a major living pilgrimage and Rāmāyaṇa-memory landscape. The identification is traditional, not textual: Vālmīki gives no modern place-name. See [Kiṣkindhā – Hampi – Anegundi](../../06-sacred-geography/kishkindha-hampi-anegundi.md). [regional]
- **Characters:** the Vāli–Sugrīva cycle across texts is gathered at [Sugrīva and Vāli](../characters/sugriva-and-vali.md); Hanumān at [Hanumān](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/hanuman.md).
- **Forward links:** Hanumān's leap → [Sundara Kāṇḍa](5-sundara-kanda.md); the southern bridgehead anticipates the building of **Rāma Setu** in the [Yuddha Kāṇḍa](6-yuddha-kanda.md).
- **Doctrinal threads:** the Vāli-vadha is a touchstone case for [dharma / the puruṣārthas](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/) (king's daṇḍa, the limits of fair combat, the friend's cause); Rāma's consolation of Tārā restates [karma–saṃsāra–mokṣa](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/) and the immortality of the [ātman](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/).
