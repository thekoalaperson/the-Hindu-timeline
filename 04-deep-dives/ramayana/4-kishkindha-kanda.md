# Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa — Book of Kishkindha

> Cosmic address: [Kali Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) ← but the Rāmāyaṇa itself unfolds in the [Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara. Rāma is the 7th [Daśāvatāra](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) of [Viṣṇu](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/trimurti-and-tridevi.md), of the [Sūryavaṃśa / Ikṣvāku line](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).
> Parent: [Rāmāyaṇa deep-dive index](README.md) · Part of [04-deep-dives](../README.md). Primary source: Vālmīki [Rāmāyaṇa](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md). Previous: [Araṇya Kāṇḍa](3-aranya-kanda.md) · Next: Sundara Kāṇḍa.

The **Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa** ("Book of Kishkindha," the vānara kingdom near modern **Hampi/Anegundi**, Karnataka) is the fourth of the seven kāṇḍas of Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa (~67 sargas/cantos). It is the kāṇḍa of **alliance, regicide, and reconnaissance**: the grieving Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, having lost Sītā at the close of the [Araṇya Kāṇḍa](3-aranya-kanda.md), forge a friendship-pact with the exiled vānara prince **Sugrīva**; Rāma slays Sugrīva's mighty brother **Vāli** with a concealed arrow (the epic's most-debated act of dharma); Sugrīva is enthroned, lapses into pleasure, is sternly recalled to his vow, and then dispatches vānara armies to the four directions to find Sītā. The southern party — under **Aṅgada, Hanumān, and Jāmbavān** — is set on the right path by the dying vulture **Sampāti**, and the Kāṇḍa ends with **Hanumān** swelling at the ocean's edge, poised for the leap that opens the Sundara Kāṇḍa.

## Arrival at Ṛṣyamūka & the Meeting with Hanumān (sargas 1–4)

- **The lament at Pampā** - Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa reach the lotus-lake **Pampā** in spring; the blossoming forest and birdsong sharpen Rāma's grief for Sītā, prompting one of the epic's great laments (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 1).
- **Sugrīva's alarm on Ṛṣyamūka** - From **Mount Ṛṣyamūka** — the one hill Vāli cannot enter (curse of sage **Mataṅga**) — the exiled Sugrīva sees two armed strangers and fears Vāli has sent them to kill him; he sends his minister Hanumān to investigate (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 2–3).
- **Hanumān as brāhmaṇa envoy** - Hanumān approaches disguised as a wandering mendicant, addresses the brothers in flawless, courteous Sanskrit; Rāma praises his speech as that of one versed in the Vedas. Reassured of their good faith, Hanumān reveals himself, **carries both princes on his shoulders** to Sugrīva (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 3–4).
  - *Note:* Hanumān's first appearance here (not the Bāla Kāṇḍa) is his entry into the main action; his divine parentage (son of Vāyu/[Marut](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md)) is recounted later, by Jāmbavān on the ocean shore (Kiṣkindhā 66). Vālmīki tells the birth **twice**, here and again at [Uttara](7-uttara-kanda.md) 35–36, and the two accounts differ in detail.

## The Fire-Witnessed Friendship-Pact (sargas 5–6)

- **The agni-sākṣi alliance** - Hanumān kindles fire by friction; Rāma and Sugrīva circumambulate it and swear mutual aid (**sakhya**) with **Agni** as witness — Rāma to slay Vāli and restore Sugrīva's wife and kingdom, Sugrīva to mobilize the vānaras to find Sītā (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 5). This sacral oath is the dharmic ground Rāma later cites for killing Vāli.
- **The tokens of Sītā** - Sugrīva fetches the bundle of ornaments that fell from the sky over Ṛṣyamūka as Rāvaṇa carried Sītā south ([Araṇya](3-aranya-kanda.md) 54); Rāma takes them up and breaks down. This is the first physical trace of the abduction's route (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 6).
- **The anklet verse — Lakṣmaṇa's, not Rāma's** - Asked to identify the jewels, it is **Lakṣmaṇa** who answers: *nāhaṃ jānāmi keyūre nāhaṃ jānāmi kuṇḍale / nūpure tvabhijānāmi nityaṃ pādābhivandanāt* — "I do not know the armlets, I do not know the earrings; the anklets I know, from bowing daily at her feet." The verse is the single most-quoted line of the kāṇḍa and belongs to the younger brother, whose eyes never rose above his sister-in-law's feet (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 6.22–23).
  - *Note:* traditional editors themselves flag the couplet as **absent from the older manuscripts and from the Baroda Critical Edition** — a celebrated verse of contested standing, carried in the vulgate and in every commentarial tradition that reads Lakṣmaṇa's *kaiṅkarya* (servitude) as the point. ## Establishing Rāma's Prowess: The Feud, Dundubhi & the Sāla Trees (sargas 7–12)

- **The Māyāvī / Mataṅga backstory** - Sugrīva explains the brothers' feud: chasing the asura **Māyāvī** (Dundubhi's brother — both sons of the dānava **Maya**) into a mountain cavern near Kiṣkindhā, Vāli told Sugrīva to wait at the mouth; after a year, seeing blood flow out, Sugrīva sealed the cave with a boulder believing Vāli dead, and returned to be crowned. Vāli emerged alive, branded Sugrīva a usurper, drove him out, and **seized Sugrīva's wife Rumā**. Only one refuge is left to the exile: Mataṅga's curse lays it on Vāli that he will **die on the spot** if he comes within a yojana of the sage's grove, and so Ṛṣyamūka alone is barred to him (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 9–11).
  - *Note:* this cave is **not** the Ṛkṣabila. Ṛkṣabila is Svayamprabhā's enchanted cavern in the Vindhyas, hundreds of yojanas south, reached only in sargas 50–52.
- **Vāli's strength recounted** - Sugrīva narrates Vāli's terrifying might: how the buffalo-demon **Dundubhi** challenged Ocean, then Himavān, then came bellowing to Kiṣkindhā's gate; Vāli killed him and hurled the carcass **one yojana**, the blood-drops falling in Mataṅga's grove and earning the curse. The bones still lie like a hill. Vāli would also **shake the seven sāla trees bare** in a single grip, and Sugrīva fears Rāma cannot match such a foe (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 11).
- **Kicking Dundubhi's skeleton** - To reassure him, Rāma flicks the colossal skeleton **ten yojanas** with his big toe (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 12).
- **The seven sāla trees** - Still doubtful, Sugrīva asks him to pierce **one** of the sāla trees; Rāma looses a single arrow that goes through **all seven**, and the hill, and the earth, and returns to his quiver — convincing Sugrīva utterly (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 12).
- **The first, failed challenge** - They go to Kiṣkindhā and Sugrīva bellows his challenge. The brothers are so alike that Rāma, hidden, **cannot tell them apart** and dares not shoot; Vāli thrashes Sugrīva, who flees back to Ṛṣyamūka, bitterly reproaching Rāma (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 12).
- **The garland sign** - Rāma explains the look-alike problem; **Lakṣmaṇa** hangs a **gajapuṣpī flower-garland** around Sugrīva's neck as an identifying mark for the rematch (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 12).

## The Second Duel & the Slaying of Vāli (sargas 13–18)

- **The Saptajana hermitage** - Walking back to Kiṣkindhā, Sugrīva points out to Rāma the ashram of the **Saptajanas**, the seven sages who lived head-down in water, ate wind, and rose bodily to heaven after seven hundred years; no creature dares enter their grove, and Vāli himself could never subdue it. Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa salute it as they pass (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 13).
- **The second challenge** - Sugrīva, garlanded, calls Vāli out again; Vāli hears the roar and starts up in fury (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 14).
- **Tārā's warning** - The wise queen **Tārā** (Vāli's wife, daughter of the physician-vānara Suṣeṇa) catches him at the gate and begs him not to fight — Sugrīva would not return so soon, and so soon emboldened, unless backed by the formidable Rāma, with whom alliance, not war, is the sound policy; her own intelligence comes from Aṅgada's scouts. Vāli dismisses her counsel out of pride (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 15).
- **The concealed arrow** - During the second duel, **Rāma shoots Vāli from concealment** (behind a tree, per most readings) with an arrow to the chest; Vāli falls mortally wounded (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 16).
- **Vāli's dharma-indictment of Rāma** - Dying, Vāli delivers a celebrated rebuke (Vālmīki records it across many ślokas): Rāma struck **a stranger, unprovoked, by stealth, from hiding**, breaking the kṣatriya code of fair open combat; Vāli had no quarrel with Rāma; a king of the **Ikṣvāku** line famed for dharma has acted like a hunter snaring game; had Rāma asked, Vāli himself could have recovered Sītā in a day (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 17).
- **Rāma's tenfold justification** - Rāma answers (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 18), the core of the ethics debate:
  - The Earth belongs to the **Ikṣvākus**; as agent of emperor **Bharata**, Rāma is duty-bound to **punish adharma**, and a king/his deputy may chastise wrongdoers anywhere.
  - Vāli committed the grave sin of **taking his living younger brother's wife (Rumā)** while Sugrīva lived — and in dharmaśāstra a younger brother's wife is as a daughter; this is treated as near-incest, a capital offense.
  - As a **vānara (forest animal)**, Vāli was lawful game for a kṣatriya, who may hunt beasts **by snare, ambush, or hidden shot** whether they face him or flee — so the "unfair shot" objection does not bind.
  - Rāma was honoring a sacred **fire-witnessed pact** with Sugrīva (a friend's cause is one's own).
  - The killing **expiated Vāli's sins**, granting him a better destiny than living on in guilt.
- **Vāli's reconciliation** - Persuaded (or at least pacified), Vāli accepts the justice of his death, **commends his son Aṅgada and wife Tārā to Rāma's and Sugrīva's protection**, and begs Sugrīva not to mistreat them; he dies at peace (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 18, 22).
- **The golden garland handed on** - With his last strength Vāli takes from his own neck the **kāñcana-mālā**, the golden chain **Indra** gave him, and puts it on Sugrīva: the **goddess of triumph dwells in it**, he says, and she abandons a wearer at the moment of death — so it must not be burned with him. The one object in the kāṇḍa that carries kingship itself passes brother to brother on the field (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 22).

### Variants & the ethics debate over Vāli-vadha

- *The "from behind a tree" reading (mainstream Vālmīki tradition):* Rāma shoots from concealment; the impropriety is real and is precisely what Rāma must justify — the kāṇḍa stages a genuine dharma-dilemma, not a clean kill.
- *Variant (hunting-code defense, Vālmīki Kiṣkindhā 18):* Rāma's own primary answer — vānaras are animals, and the rules of single-combat between equals (dvandva-yuddha) simply do not apply to hunting; this is the scripturally explicit resolution.
- *Variant (the boon-of-strength reading) [folk tradition]:* a widely-told explanation absent from the core Vālmīki text — Vāli possessed a boon (from Brahmā or Indra) draining **half the strength** of any opponent who faced him in direct combat; Rāma therefore could not fight him face-to-face and had to strike from the side. Often cited to exculpate Rāma; treated as later/popular gloss, not canonical Vālmīki. What the folk boon is grafted onto is real and specific: Vālmīki's Vāli wears the **kāñcana-mālā**, the golden garland given him by **Indra**, in which the goddess of victory resides (Kiṣkindhā 22) — a talisman of triumph, not a drain on an opponent's strength.
- *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa / devotional reading) [late text]:* Vāli, dying, recognizes Rāma as **Para-Brahman/Viṣṇu**; being slain by the Lord's own hand is liberation (mokṣa), reframing the killing as supreme grace rather than a juridical punishment.
- *Variant (Rāmacaritamānasa, Tulsīdās) [late text]:* Tulsīdās compresses the polemic and foregrounds Vāli's devotional realization and Rāma's saving intent over the legal argumentation; the bhakti frame softens the dharma-dilemma.
- *Variant (karmic-debt / Tāṭakā-cycle reading) [folk tradition]:* in some tellings the stealth-killing is the working-out of a debt from a prior birth (e.g. linked to the Sugrīva–Vāli rivalry being a destined balancing), used to dissolve the ethical problem cosmologically.
- *Scholarly note [scholarly]:* the **Vāli-vadha** is the Rāmāyaṇa's classic "blemish on Rāma's dharma" passage, debated from the commentators (e.g. the **govindarāja/maheśvaratīrtha** glosses) to modern Indology; many read the long apologia as Vālmīki himself flagging the act as morally hard rather than self-evidently right.

## Sugrīva Enthroned; Aṅgada Made Heir (sargas 19–27)

- **Tārā's lament and the cremation** - Tārā mourns Vāli; Rāma consoles her with teaching on the soul's immortality and the inevitability of death. Vāli's funeral rites are performed; Sugrīva, stricken with remorse, briefly wishes to die with his brother but is dissuaded (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 19–25).
- **Coronation of Sugrīva** - **Neither brother enters Kiṣkindhā.** Rāma tells Sugrīva plainly that the terms of his exile bar him from town or village for fourteen years and that he will keep to the mountain cave with Lakṣmaṇa, who is held to the same vow. Sugrīva goes in with Aṅgada, and the **vānara chiefs themselves — Gaja, Gavākṣa, Gavaya, Śarabha, Gandhamādana, Mainda, Dvivida, Hanumān, Jāmbavān — pour the consecration water** over him on the throne. At Rāma's word Sugrīva then embraces **Aṅgada** and installs him as **yuvarāja** (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 26).
  - *Note:* the widely repeated line that "Lakṣmaṇa entered Kiṣkindhā and consecrated Sugrīva" is retelling-gloss. Vālmīki keeps both brothers outside the walls throughout; the abhiṣeka is performed by the vānaras' own chiefs.
- **The Kārtika covenant** - In the same scene Rāma fixes the campaign clock: Sugrīva is to enjoy his kingdom through the four months of rain, and **on the arrival of Kārtika** exert himself for Sītā's recovery. This is the first of the kāṇḍa's three deadlines and the one Sugrīva will overrun (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 26).
- **Rāma's retreat to Prasravaṇa** - Rāma withdraws with Lakṣmaṇa to a cave on **Mount Prasravaṇa/Mālyavān** to wait out the imminent monsoon, since campaigns cannot move in the rains (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 27).

## The Rainy Season, Sugrīva's Lapse & Lakṣmaṇa's Wrath (sargas 28–36)

- **The varṣā (monsoon) descriptions** - A long lyrical canto describes the rains — celebrated as some of Vālmīki's finest nature poetry — during which Rāma pines for Sītā and waits (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 28).
- **Hanumān's counsel and the fifteen-day order** - It is **Hanumān** who first rouses Sugrīva, and he does it before Rāma ever complains: the rains are over, the season for action has come, Rāma discharged your business and you have not begun his, and a friend's help delayed is help refused. Sugrīva takes the point and orders **Nīla** to have every vānara host mustered — **any who arrives after fifteen nights forfeits his life**, and there is no appeal (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 29). This is the kāṇḍa's second deadline.
- **Sugrīva's dissipation** - Having given the order, Sugrīva goes back into his inner apartments with Rumā and Tārā and does nothing further — he neither reports the muster to Rāma nor moves — and the fifteen days run out unmarked (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 29–30).
- **Rāma's autumn grief & Lakṣmaṇa dispatched** - As **śarad (autumn)** opens the campaigning season, Rāma's grief turns to anger; he sends an enraged **Lakṣmaṇa** to Kiṣkindhā to remind Sugrīva of his debt — with a threat that the bow that felled Vāli could fell him too (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 30–31).
- **Lakṣmaṇa at the gate** - Hanumān, seeing him come, warns Sugrīva to receive him properly; the drunken, unguarded city and the sound of revelry inflame Lakṣmaṇa further, and he enters bending his bow (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 32–33).
- **Tārā's diplomacy** - The eloquent **Tārā**, now Sugrīva's counsel, meets Lakṣmaṇa herself: she pleads the tyranny of long-denied desire, argues that a man newly restored to wife and throne may be forgiven a season, and reveals that the summons has in fact gone out and the armies are already on the roads. Lakṣmaṇa relents; Sugrīva apologizes and disclaims all merit, saying Rāma's arrow, not his own strength, won him the kingdom (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 33–36).

## Mobilizing the Vānara Hosts (sargas 37–39)

- **The world's monkeys assembled** - Sugrīva summons vānara armies from across the earth — millions strong, led by named chiefs (Gaja, Gavākṣa, Gavaya, Śarabha, Gandhamādana, Mainda, Dvivida, Suṣeṇa, Nīla, and Śatabali with his ten million) — a grand muster review, which Sugrīva then presents to Rāma on Prasravaṇa (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 37–39).

## The Four-Quarter Search for Sītā (sargas 40–46)

- **East** - Sugrīva sends a party under **Vinata** to the east, briefing them river by river and mountain by mountain: the Bhāgīrathī, Sarayū, Kauśikī and Yamunā, Videha and Aṅga, the Yava and Suvarṇa islands, the Śiśira and Śoṇa mountains, the milk-white sea, and at the end of the world **Udayagiri**, the sunrise mountain, beyond which nothing lives and none may pass (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 40).
- **South** - The decisive party: led by **Aṅgada**, with **Hanumān, Jāmbavān (the bear-king/ṛkṣa), Nīla, Tāra, Suṣeṇa, Nala** and others, sent toward the Vindhyas, the Narmadā and Godāvarī, Vidarbha and the Āndhra, Kaliṅga and the Kāverī, Malaya, the southern ocean, and Laṅkā beyond it. Sugrīva, who in his exile-wanderings knew the south best, gives by far the longest briefing (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 41).
- **West** - A party under **Suṣeṇa** (Tārā's father) sent west past Surāṣṭra and Bālhīka to the western ocean — the **Vaḍavāmukha**, the submarine mare's-mouth fire that drinks the sea, the golden Meru peak where the sun sets, and the guardian **Merusāvarṇi** in his hermitage (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 42).
- **North** - A party under **Śatabali** sent north past the Himālaya and the Kāla mountain to **Uttarakuru**, the land of the blessed where rivers run gold and men are born in pairs, and to the Somagiri beyond which the sun does not shine (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 43).
- **The cosmography of the briefings** - The four speeches together are the epic's fullest world-map, and they carry the corpus's Rāmāyaṇa-era geography of the marvellous: **Bhogavatī**, the serpent-city of Vāsuki guarded by the nāgas; the **Mandehas**, the demons who hang head-down from the sunrise mountain and are burned to ash each dawn by the sun's rays only to revive; the Vaḍavāmukha fire; and the trans-Himālayan Uttarakuru (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 40–43).
- **The one-month deadline** - Sugrīva fixes **one month** for all parties to return with news, on pain of death — the third and tightest of the kāṇḍa's clocks, and the one that drives the southern party to the edge of suicide (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 41–43).
- **The signet-ring** - Rāma gives **Hanumān his ring (aṅgulīya)** bearing his name as a token to convince Sītā of the bearer's authenticity — Rāma's quiet sign that he trusts Hanumān above all (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 44).
- **The send-off** - The hosts take their leave with boasts of what each will do to Rāvaṇa, and scatter to the quarters (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 45).
- **Sugrīva's world-knowledge explained** - Rāma, struck by how exactly Sugrīva knows the earth, asks how a forest king came by it; Sugrīva answers that **Vāli chased him over the whole world** when he drove him out — Laṅkā and the four oceans included — and that he learned the map running for his life (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 46).

## The Southern Party's Ordeal: Ṛkṣabila & Svayamprabhā (sargas 47–54)

- **The empty returns** - The east, west, and north parties come back inside the month with nothing; only the south is still out (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 47).
- **The false Rāvaṇa** - Searching a cave in the waterless country cursed by the sage Kaṇḍu, the southern party is charged by an asura; **Aṅgada takes him for Rāvaṇa** and kills him with a single blow of his palm, and they ransack the cave before finding it empty of Sītā (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 48).
- **Aṅgada's rally** - The month gone and the party sinking, Aṅgada holds them together with the argument that despair is the one certain defeat, and turns them back to the search (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 49).
- **The enchanted cave Ṛkṣabila** - Tormented by thirst, they follow water-birds into a vast magical cavern in the Vindhyas — **Ṛkṣabila** — full of golden mansions, orchards, and food, and find in it a single ascetic woman in black deerskin, **Svayamprabhā** (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 50–51).
- **Whose cave it is** - Svayamprabhā tells them: the cave was built by **Maya**, the dānava architect, for the apsaras **Hemā**, his beloved; Indra killed Maya with the thunderbolt for her sake, Brahmā gave the mansion to Hemā, and Hemā gave it to **Svayamprabhā**, daughter of **Merusāvarṇi** and her friend, to guard while she is away at Brahmā's court (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 51).
  - *Note:* Hemā is **not** the architect's daughter — she is the apsaras the architect built the cave for; the architect is **Maya**, whose sons Māyāvī and Dundubhi are the two demons whose deaths began the Vāli–Sugrīva feud (Kiṣkindhā 9–11).
- **Svayamprabhā's aid** - She feeds the famished vānaras, and since no one who enters Ṛkṣabila may leave it alive, she has them close their eyes and by her yogic power **sets them down outside on the southern shore** — where they find the month long overspent and death for failure waiting at home (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 52).
- **The cave-secession proposal** - On the shore the counsellor **Tāra** urges the party to go back into Ṛkṣabila and live there: it is unassailable, food and water are endless, and neither Sugrīva nor Lakṣmaṇa's arrows could reach them. The vānaras take to the idea (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 53).
- **Hanumān breaks it** - **Hanumān** dismantles the plot with the four instruments of statecraft, and chiefly with ***bheda*** — division: monkeys are fickle by nature, he tells Aṅgada, they will not stay penned from their wives and sons for your sake, and the first of them to leave will sell you. He adds *daṇḍa* — Lakṣmaṇa's arrows would break the cave open — and *sāma* and *dāna*, praising Aṅgada and Sugrīva's affection for him. The secession collapses (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 54). It is the kāṇḍa's one open contest of political skill, and Hanumān wins it against the crown prince.

## Despair, Aṅgada's Fast, and Sampāti (sargas 55–63)

- **Resolve to fast unto death (prāyopaveśa)** - On the ocean shore, with the deadline broken and Sītā unfound, **Aṅgada** and the party resolve to **fast to death** rather than return in disgrace and face Sugrīva's wrath; Aṅgada bitterly recalls Vāli's killing as he despairs (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 55).
- **The death of Jaṭāyu retold** - As the vānaras recount aloud the events of the quest — including the death of the vulture **Jaṭāyu** at Rāvaṇa's hands ([Araṇya Kāṇḍa](3-aranya-kanda.md)) — they are overheard (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 56).
- **Sampāti appears** - The aged vulture **Sampāti**, Jaṭāyu's elder brother — wings burned to stumps long ago when he flew too near the sun shielding Jaṭāyu — hears his brother's name and emerges from the hill, at first delighting in what seems an easy meal of starving monkeys (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 56–57).
- **Sampāti's grief and aid** - Learning of Jaṭāyu's heroic death, Sampāti mourns and asks to be carried down to the sea, where he performs the **water-rite (udaka-kriyā)** for his brother. In gratitude he resolves to help (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 58).
- **Where the intelligence came from** - His own **far-seeing vulture vision** descries Sītā a hundred yojanas across the sea, **captive in Rāvaṇa's Aśoka grove in Laṅkā**; and his son **Supārśva**, who had come home from Mahendra one day empty-handed, had already told him of the rākṣasa flying south with a struggling woman who called on Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa. Sampāti tells the vānaras exactly where she is and how far (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 58–59).
- **Sampāti's own story** - He recounts the flight in which he and Jaṭāyu raced the sun: Jaṭāyu faltering, Sampāti spreading his wings over his younger brother and taking the fire himself, falling burned on the Vindhya. There the sage **Niśākara**, whom he and Jaṭāyu had served long before, gave him the prophecy he has waited on ever since — that when Rāma's envoys come seeking Sītā and he tells them what he has seen, his **wings will grow again** (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 60–62).
- **Sampāti's wings restored** - Niśākara's word is fulfilled on the spot: as the vānaras watch, Sampāti's **wings regrow** and he flies away healed — dharma paid in the same coin it was earned (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 63).
  - *Variant (regional/folk):* some retellings make the wing-restoration conditional on his service to Rāma's cause specifically, sharpening the dharmic reward motif.

## Hanumān Prepared for the Leap (sargas 64–67)

- **The crisis of the hundred-yojana sea** - The vānaras reach the southern coast and despair before the **hundred-yojana ocean** to Laṅkā (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 64).
- **The ladder of leaps** - The chiefs declare their ranges in ascending order — Gaja ten yojanas, Gavākṣa twenty, Śarabha thirty, up through Mainda, Dvivida and Nīla, with **Jāmbavān** confessing that age has cut him to ninety, though in his youth he circled **Viṣṇu at the Trivikrama stride**. **Aṅgada** alone can cross the hundred, but doubts he could leap back, and as crown prince he cannot be risked (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 65).
- **Jāmbavān awakens Hanumān** - Jāmbavān, seeing Hanumān sitting modestly apart, turns on him and **recites him to himself** — son of **Vāyu** by Añjanā, the infant who sprang at the sun mistaking it for a fruit, who took Indra's thunderbolt on the jaw and was given the boons of every god. Only Hanumān can cross (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 66).
  - *Note:* Hanumān's ignorance of his own powers is not a devotional invention. Vālmīki's sages of the **Bhṛgu and Aṅgiras** lines curse the child to forget his strength until he is reminded of it, with the release built into the curse ([Uttara](7-uttara-kanda.md) 36) — which is exactly what Jāmbavān's speech performs here. The core text carries both halves; only the *placement* of the reminding differs in later retellings.
- **Hanumān swells for the leap** - Roused, Hanumān **grows to colossal size**, salutes the directional guardians (Sūrya, Indra, Vāyu, Brahmā), climbs **Mount Mahendra**, and crouches to spring — the mountain trembling beneath him. The Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa ends on this threshold; the leap itself opens the **Sundara Kāṇḍa** (Vālmīki, Kiṣkindhā 66–67).

## Geography & Connections

- **Kiṣkindhā / Hampi:** tradition firmly locates Kiṣkindhā, Ṛṣyamūka, and Pampā at **Hampi–Anegundi** on the Tungabhadra in Karnataka — a major living pilgrimage and Rāmāyaṇa-memory landscape (see [06-sacred-geography](../../06-sacred-geography/) when added).
- **Forward links:** Hanumān's leap → Sundara Kāṇḍa; the southern bridgehead anticipates the building of **Rāma Setu** in the Yuddha Kāṇḍa.
- **Doctrinal threads:** the Vāli-vadha is a touchstone case for [dharma / the puruṣārthas](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/) (king's daṇḍa, the limits of fair combat, the friend's cause); Rāma's consolation of Tārā restates [karma–saṃsāra–mokṣa](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/) and the immortality of the [ātman](../../05-concepts-and-dharma/).
