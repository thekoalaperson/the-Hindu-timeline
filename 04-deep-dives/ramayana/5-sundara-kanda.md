# Sundara Kāṇḍa — The Beautiful Book

> Cosmic address: [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md) → 28th Mahāyuga → **[Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** → the Rāma-avatāra cycle.

> See also: [Daśāvatāra (Rāma)](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) · [Sūryavaṃśa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) · [Itihāsa-Epics corpus](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md)

The fifth book of Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa, **68 sargas / ~2,885 ślokas**, named *Sundara* ("Beautiful") — by tradition because it is the most beautiful book, or because its hero Hanumān is *sundara* (handsome), or after Añjanā's pet-name *Sundara* for her son. It is the **only kāṇḍa in which Rāma is not the principal actor** — its hero is Hanumān — and is the single most independently recited/parayana'd book of the epic, held to confer success and remove obstacles. Narratively it is one continuous arc: the crossing of the ocean, the search of Laṅkā, the finding of Sītā, the burning of the city, and the return.

## I. The Crossing of the Ocean (sargas 1–3)

- **The forgetting and the reminding** - Hanumān does not know his own strength when the search-party reaches the shore. As a boy he had harried the hermitages of the **Bhṛgu and Aṅgiras sages**, who cursed him to forget his powers — with an explicit release-clause: they return the moment another reminds him of them (Vālmīki *Uttara* 36). Jāmbavān recites his birth, his parentage, and his boons back to him on the southern shore, and the strength returns; this is core Vālmīki, not devotional embroidery (Vālmīki *Kiṣkindhā* 65–66).
  - *Variant ([Tulsīdās, *Rāmcaritmānas*](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md) and oral retellings):* only the **placement** differs — the reminding is re-staged as an immediate prelude to the leap and expanded into a set-piece of rousing. The curse and its release-condition are Vālmīki's own.
- **The leap (mahā-pluti)** - On Mount Mahendra, Hanumān grows to colossal size, compresses the mountain underfoot, and launches a 100-yojana leap across the southern ocean; the recoil shakes the mountain, trees, and beasts fly up with him (Vālmīki *Sundara* 1).
- **Maināka, the golden mountain** - The ocean-god **Sāgara** — dug out by Sagara's sons and so owing the Ikṣvākus a debt — commands the submerged mountain Maināka to rise and offer Hanumān rest. Maināka has his own reason to comply: in the Kṛta Yuga, when Indra was shearing the wings from the mountains, **Vāyu** hid him beneath the sea, so the flier overhead is his rescuer's son. Hanumān touches the peak in courtesy but refuses to halt, having vowed not to pause until the task is done; **Indra, watching, grants Maināka *abhaya*** — freedom from fear, and leave to stand where he pleases (Vālmīki *Sundara* 1).
- **Surasā, mother of the nāgas** - Sent by the devas to test him, **Surasā the nāga-mātā** (*nāgamātaram*, sun-bright — a nāga-mother, not a rākṣasī by nature) takes a terrifying form for the test alone, blocks his path, and demands he enter her mouth. Hanumān swells enormously; when she widens her jaws to match, he shrinks to thumb-size, darts in and out, then bows — passing the test of strength *and* wit. She resumes her own shape and blesses him (Vālmīki *Sundara* 1).
  - *Variant (later texts):* the obstacle is sometimes doubled or renamed; some retellings make Surasā an aspect of the Mother-goddess testing his humility. [regional]
- **Siṃhikā, the shadow-seizer** - A sea-monster that catches prey by gripping its shadow drags Hanumān down toward her gullet; he shrinks, plunges into her mouth, and rends her vital organs from within, killing her, then re-emerges and flies on (Vālmīki *Sundara* 1). *(Distinct from Siṃhikā the mother of Rāhu — same name, different being.)*
- **The three obstacles are three different things** - The crossing is structured by its senders, not by escalation: Maināka is the **ocean's courtesy**, Surasā the **devas' test**, Siṃhikā a **genuine predator** met by accident. Only the third is an enemy; collapsing the first two into "demons in the way" erases the design (Vālmīki *Sundara* 1).
- **Landfall on Mount Trikūṭa / Lambā** - Hanumān alights on the wooded peak overlooking Laṅkā, shrinks to a small (cat-sized) form to scout unseen, and waits for nightfall to enter (Vālmīki *Sundara* 2).

## II. Entering and Searching Laṅkā (sargas 2–13)

- **Laṅkā beheld** - Hanumān marvels at the golden, fortified, moated city of Rāvaṇa, its ramparts, gates, and rākṣasa watch (Vālmīki *Sundara* 2–3).
- **Laṅkinī / Laṅkā-devī, the guardian goddess** - The tutelary deity of the city, in rākṣasī form, holds the gate at Rāvaṇa's orders; she challenges Hanumān and strikes him with her palm. He answers with his left fist, checking his force because she is a woman, and fells her with the one blow. Reeling, she tells him what **Brahmā** had told her long ago (*purā*): *when a vānara overpowers you by force, from that hour the rākṣasas are in danger* — she reads Sītā's abduction as its cause, names herself Laṅkā embodied, and lets him pass (Vālmīki *Sundara* 3).
- **Night search of the mansions** - Entering by night, Hanumān moves house to house through Laṅkā's palaces, gardens, and assembly halls, seeing rākṣasa revelry and sleeping women but not Sītā (Vālmīki *Sundara* 4–5).
- **Rāvaṇa's palace and the puṣpaka** - He enters Rāvaṇa's great mansion and the flying chariot Puṣpaka, describing its splendor at length (Vālmīki *Sundara* 6–8).
- **The inner apartments and Mandodarī** - In the harem he sees Rāvaṇa asleep amid his wives and a supremely beautiful queen (Mandodarī). Mistaking her momentarily for Sītā, he rejoices — then reasons that Sītā, grieving and separated from Rāma, could never be sleeping in luxury, and rebukes himself for the lapse (Vālmīki *Sundara* 9–10).
- **The mind-purity self-audit** - Having searched a harem of sleeping, half-clad women, Hanumān stops and examines his own conduct: the sense-organ that saw them is the mind, and his mind held no wrong impulse — a search for Sītā could be conducted nowhere but among women, and no disturbance arose in him. It is the epic's most explicit statement of the envoy's inward discipline (Vālmīki *Sundara* 11).
- **Despair and the consequence-cascade** - Failing to find her, Hanumān sinks into despair and reasons the failure out to its end: if he returns empty-handed Sugrīva's word fails, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa die of grief, Bharata and Śatrughna follow, the Ikṣvāku line ends, and the vānara host — Aṅgada, Tārā and the rest — perishes with it. Rather than return, he weighs fasting to death at the shore, then rejects despair as the enemy of every undertaking, prays to Rāma, Lakṣmaṇa, Sītā, Rudra, Indra, Yama and Vāyu, and turns to the one place left — the **Aśoka-vana** (Vālmīki *Sundara* 12–13).

## III. Finding Sītā in the Aśoka Grove (sargas 14–40)

- **The Aśoka-vana** - Hanumān enters the pleasure-grove and hides in a **śiṃśapā** tree — *Dalbergia sissoo*, the Indian rosewood, a distinct tree standing *within* the aśoka grove, not an aśoka itself — from which he spots a wasted, sorrowful woman in soiled garments, guarded by hideous rākṣasīs — Sītā (Vālmīki *Sundara* 14–15).
- **Rāvaṇa's wooing and threat** - At dawn Rāvaṇa comes with his women to court Sītā with promises of queenship; she rejects him utterly, placing a blade of grass between them. Enraged, he tells her the **two months still standing** of the twelve-month term he set at Janasthāna are all she has, after which she is to be killed and cooked for his morning meal; he departs, and the rākṣasīs threaten and harangue her (Vālmīki *Sundara* 18–23; the twelve-month ultimatum itself is *Araṇya* 56).
- **"I could burn you to ashes"** - Sītā tells Rāvaṇa to his face that he is fit to be reduced to ashes and that she has the power to do it — and does not, for two stated reasons: she has **no mandate from Rāma**, and she will not spend her accumulated austerity on him (Vālmīki *Sundara* 22). The restraint is chosen, not helplessness.
- **Sītā's despair** - Tormented, Sītā resolves to take her own life by hanging herself with her braid, seeing no rescue (Vālmīki *Sundara* 24–26, 28).
- **Trijaṭā's dream** - The aged rākṣasī Trijaṭā restrains the others, recounting an omen-dream: a white-toothed vānara burns Laṅkā, Rāvaṇa rides south (toward death) on a donkey clad in red, oil-smeared and garlanded, while Rāma triumphs — foretelling the rākṣasas' ruin and Sītā's deliverance (Vālmīki *Sundara* 27).
  - *Variant (Vibhīṣaṇa's lineage):* Vālmīki gives Trijaṭā no parentage. The identification of her as **Vibhīṣaṇa's daughter** (by Saramā) is later tradition — regional retellings, [*Rāmcaritmānas*](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md), and stage practice, where she becomes the lone compassionate rākṣasī. [later tradition]
- **The omens turn** - As Sītā reaches the end of her endurance, auspicious signs break over her — her left eye, arm and thigh throb, the traditional portents of a coming joy — and her despair lifts just before Hanumān speaks (Vālmīki *Sundara* 29).
- **Which language to speak** - Hanumān, still hidden, deliberates: Sanskrit as a twice-born man would speak it will make her take him for Rāvaṇa in disguise, yet the ordinary human speech of the region is the only tongue she will trust. He chooses the plain speech, spoken small and low, and begins with Rāma's genealogy rather than with himself (Vālmīki *Sundara* 30).
- **Hanumān's gentle approach** - Perched in the tree, he softly recites Rāma's story so Sītā will trust him; she still fears it is Rāvaṇa practicing illusion (Vālmīki *Sundara* 31–32).
- **The three-layer verification** - Sītā does not accept him at once, and the text makes her caution a sequence: she narrates her own story back to test his knowledge of it — including that she had lived **twelve years in Rāma's house** before the exile (*Sundara* 33.17); Hanumān then declares himself Rāma's envoy and son of the wind (34); then recites Rāma's and Lakṣmaṇa's bodily marks and his own parentage — Kesarī his father, Vāyu his begetter — until the description itself is proof (35).
  - *Note (internal chronology):* the twelve-years figure sits against *Araṇya* 47.10–11 (Rāma 25, Sītā 18 at the exile) and *Bāla* 20's "my Rāma is not yet sixteen." The three statements cannot all be squared; the epic carries the inconsistency rather than resolving it. [disputed]
- **The signet ring (aṅgulīya)** - Hanumān descends and gives Sītā **Rāma's signet ring engraved with his name** as proof; recognizing it she weeps with joy and questions him about Rāma (Vālmīki *Sundara* 36).
- **"Rāghava does not eat meat"** - Answering her questions about how Rāma lives in the forest, Hanumān tells her he takes neither meat nor liquor, but the wild fruit and roots set out for him at the fifth part of the day (*na māṃsaṃ rāghavo bhuṅkte*, Vālmīki *Sundara* 36.41) — a verse that sits in open tension with the epic's own hunting and venison passages, and is recorded here without harmonizing either away.
- **Offer to carry her back, declined** - Hanumān offers to carry Sītā across the sea on his back. She refuses on principle: she will not willingly touch another man, and Rāma's honor demands that **he himself** slay Rāvaṇa and recover her in battle (Vālmīki *Sundara* 37).
- **The cūḍāmaṇi (crest-jewel)** - As a return token Sītā unties her **cūḍāmaṇi** from the knot of her upper garment, where she had kept it hidden, and gives it to Hanumān; with it she recalls a private incident — **a crow, a son of Indra**, that tore at her breast at Citrakūṭa until Rāma loosed a blade of *darbha* grass charged as a brahmāstra and took its right eye — a sign only she and Rāma could know (Vālmīki *Sundara* 38). Its provenance is a chain, not a single gift: the jewel was **sea-born**, given by **Indra** to Janaka at a sacrifice he was pleased with, given by Janaka to Sītā as a bride, and bound on her head at the wedding — Rāma himself recites this lineage when he receives it back (Vālmīki *Sundara* 66).
  - *Note (the crow's name):* Vālmīki says only "a son of Indra." The name **Jayanta** for the Kākāsura is a later identification carried by the commentaries and the retellings. [later tradition]
- **The second private proof — the manaḥśilā tilaka** - Sītā sends a second recognition-token, and it is Vālmīki's own text, not a rival tradition: on Citrakūṭa the mark on her forehead had worn away and Rāma painted a fresh one on her cheek with **manaḥśilā** (red realgar) from the hillside. She tells Hanumān to put both memories to him — the crow and the tilaka (Vālmīki *Sundara* 40) — and he relays them when he reports (*Sundara* 67). The kāṇḍa therefore has **two** intimate proofs, not one plus a variant.

## IV. The Havoc and the Burning of Laṅkā (sargas 41–55)

- **Wrecking the Aśoka grove** - To provoke the enemy and gauge their strength, Hanumān uproots trees, smashes pavilions and ponds, and lays waste the grove "like fire in a dry forest," sparing only the śiṃśapā where Sītā sits (Vālmīki *Sundara* 41).
- **The kiṅkaras and the war-manifesto** - Rāvaṇa sends the 80,000 **kiṅkaras**; Hanumān tears an iron bar from the gateway and destroys them all, then declares himself from the archway — son of the wind-god, servant of Rāma king of Kosala, a thousand Rāvaṇas no match for him, and Laṅkā's destruction certain (Vālmīki *Sundara* 42). Questioned by the rākṣasīs about the intruder, Sītā answers only with a proverb: *a serpent knows the tracks of a serpent* (42).
- **The caitya-prāsāda and its hundred guards** - Hanumān next climbs the **caitya-prāsāda**, the city's sanctuary-tower, uproots a gold-worked pillar and whirls it until the friction fires the whole edifice; the **hundred guards** of the sanctuary come at him with every weapon and are killed (Vālmīki *Sundara* 43).
- **Jambumālī** - Sent separately and afterwards, **Jambumālī, son of Prahasta** — great-tusked, bow-wielding — is the first named champion; Hanumān drives the same iron bar through his chest and leaves neither head, arms, knees nor bow (Vālmīki *Sundara* 44). *(He is not the sanctuary's guard; that was the previous sarga's hundred.)*
- **The seven sons of the chief minister** - Prahasta's seven sons charge in chariots with picked weapons; Hanumān kills them at the gateway using nothing but his body — palms, feet, fists, nails, chest and thighs (Vālmīki *Sundara* 45).
- **The five senāpatis** - Then the five army-commanders, named in the text: **Virūpākṣa, Yūpākṣa, Durdhara, Praghasa and Bhāsakarṇa** — killed with a chariot-crash, a sāla tree, and a hurled mountain-top, and their army with them (Vālmīki *Sundara* 46.2–3).
  - *Note (a namesake collision the text does not resolve):* three of the five — **Virūpākṣa, Praghasa, Bhāsakarṇa** — bear the names of Rāvaṇa's own uncles and cousins in the *Uttara* 5 roster, and the epic uses them again on rākṣasas still alive at *Yuddha* 43.24–25 and 96.30–32. The sarga gives these five **no patronymic and no house**, only rank; no identification is asserted here. [disputed] — see [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md) and [Akṣakumāra](../characters/akshakumara.md).
- **Death of Akṣa-kumāra** - Rāvaṇa's young son **Akṣa(ya)** attacks valiantly; Hanumān, admiring his courage, seizes him by the feet and dashes him to death (Vālmīki *Sundara* 47).
- **Captured by Indrajit's Brahmāstra** - Rāvaṇa sends his eldest, **Meghanāda (Indrajit)**, who binds Hanumān with the **Brahmāstra**. Hanumān submits out of reverence for Brahmā's weapon, though he could free himself; the rākṣasas then add ordinary ropes, which (by the astra's rule) cancels its power — but he feigns captivity to enter Rāvaṇa's court (Vālmīki *Sundara* 48).
- **Hanumān's awe of Rāvaṇa** - Dragged before the throne, the envoy's first reaction is not contempt but astonishment at the rākṣasa king's splendour and force: *were this one not set against dharma, he could hold even Indra's heaven* — the epic putting its antagonist's stature in his enemy's mouth (Vālmīki *Sundara* 49).
- **In Rāvaṇa's court** - Hanumān declares himself Rāma's envoy, warns Rāvaṇa to return Sītā or face annihilation, and rebukes his arrogance. Rāvaṇa orders his death (Vālmīki *Sundara* 50–51).
- **The loophole in the boon** - The speech's cutting edge is legal: Rāvaṇa's grant from Brahmā names devas, dānavas, gandharvas, yakṣas, rākṣasas and the rest — and does **not** name men or vānaras. Hanumān tells him so in open court, and tells him the omission is the whole of his danger (Vālmīki *Sundara* 51).
- **Vibhīṣaṇa's counsel** - Rāvaṇa's brother **Vibhīṣaṇa** intervenes: an envoy may not be killed by law; let him be punished, not slain. Rāvaṇa relents to **burning Hanumān's tail** (Vālmīki *Sundara* 52–53).
- **The tail set alight** - The rākṣasas wrap his tail in oil-soaked cloth and ignite it; Sītā prays to Agni to keep it cool for Hanumān. Feeling no burn, Hanumān shrinks, slips his bonds, and grows again (Vālmīki *Sundara* 53).
- **The burning of Laṅkā** - With his blazing tail Hanumān leaps roof to roof through a named roll of the city's great houses — Prahasta, Mahāpārśva, Vajradaṃṣṭra, Śuka and Sāraṇa, Indrajit, Jambumālī, Sumālī, Raśmiketu, Sūryaśatru, Hrasvakarṇa, Daṃṣṭra, Romaśa, Yuddhonmatta, Matta, Dhvajagrīva, Vidyujjihva, Hastimukha, Karāla, Piśāca, Śoṇitākṣa, Kumbhakarṇa, Makarākṣa, Yajñaśatru, Brahmaśatru, Narāntaka, Kumbha and Nikumbha — ending at Rāvaṇa's own palace. **Vibhīṣaṇa's house he passes over deliberately** — the text states the sparing outright, and names him alone among the great houses as untouched (Vālmīki *Sundara* 54.16).
  - *Variant (quenching the tail in the sea):* the beloved image of Hanumān dipping his burning tail in the ocean is **not in the Southern text** — sarga 54 leaves him standing on the flat summit of Trikūṭa, and 55 opens on his dread for Sītā. The dousing belongs to popular and iconographic tradition. [folk / iconographic]
- **Fear for Sītā** - Seeing Laṅkā ablaze, Hanumān is struck with horror that Sītā may have burned; the cāraṇas/devas reassure him, and he returns to find her safe — fire, by her chastity, cannot harm her (Vālmīki *Sundara* 55).
  - *Variant (sequence):* some recensions and retellings place the burning of Laṅkā **after** Hanumān has already taken leave of Sītā; others (and the order of certain printed Vālmīki recensions) move the despair-over-Sītā reflection differently — the kāṇḍa's chapter order varies slightly between Northern, Southern (e.g. the *valmikiramayan.net* Southern recension), and Gauḍīya manuscript families.

## V. The Return (sargas 56–68)

- **Farewell to Sītā** - Hanumān returns to the grove, comforts Sītā, again offers to carry her (again declined), receives her final message and takes leave (Vālmīki *Sundara* 56; ordering varies by recension).
- **The return leap** - From Mount Ariṣṭa he launches the crossing back, and on the way **touches Maināka again** — the mountain having risen a second time to greet him — before landing among the waiting vānaras, shouting *"dṛṣṭā Sītā!"*, "I have seen Sītā!" (Vālmīki *Sundara* 57).
- **The recap at the shore** - To Jāmbavān, Aṅgada and the host he tells the whole crossing over again — Surasā, Siṃhikā, the search, the grove, the burning — the epic's own deliberate second telling, which is how the kāṇḍa fixes what is to be reported and what is not (Vālmīki *Sundara* 58).
- **The mandate debate** - Aṅgada argues the search-party should storm Laṅkā themselves and bring Sītā back, since they have the strength and the honour of it; **Jāmbavān rules against him** — they were sent to find her, not to fight, and a party that exceeds its mandate forfeits it. The report goes to Sugrīva and Rāma first (Vālmīki *Sundara* 59–60).
- **The Madhuvana (honey-grove) revel** - Overjoyed, the vānaras raid Sugrīva's protected honey-garden **Madhuvana**, drinking honey and fighting off its keeper **Dadhimukha**, Sugrīva's uncle, who complains to the king (Vālmīki *Sundara* 61–63).
- **Sugrīva's deduction** - Sugrīva does not punish the raiders: he reasons from the offence itself that the search must have succeeded — the party would never have dared the royal grove having failed — and sends word to bring them in (Vālmīki *Sundara* 63).
- **Reporting to Rāma** - The host returns; Hanumān narrates everything to Rāma — Sītā found, faithful, grieving, in the Aśoka-vana — and presents the **cūḍāmaṇi**, whose sea-born, Indra-to-Janaka provenance Rāma recites as he takes it (66). He then relays both private proofs, the crow and the tilaka (67), and Sītā's message with her two-month term (68). Rāma presses the jewel to his heart, weeps, and resolves on the march to Laṅkā, bridging to the [Yuddha Kāṇḍa](6-yuddha-kanda.md) (Vālmīki *Sundara* 64–68).

## VI. Major Variant Traditions

- *Variant ([Tulsīdās, *Rāmcaritmānas*], 16th c. Awadhi):* The Sundar Kāṇḍ is among the most beloved sections for recitation. It expands the **Hanumān–Vibhīṣaṇa meeting** (Vibhīṣaṇa instructs Hanumān how to find Sītā) and, distinctively, **continues past Vālmīki's fifth book** to include episodes Vālmīki places in *Yuddha*: the vānara army's march, the camp on the shore, Vibhīṣaṇa's defection and welcome by Rāma, Rāvaṇa's spies, Rāma's plea to the ocean (Samudra), and the bridge-building — so Tulsīdās's "Sundar Kāṇḍ" spans more events than Vālmīki's. [variant scope]
- *Variant ([Kamban, *Irāmāvatāram* / Kamba Rāmāyaṇam], ~12th c. Tamil):* Treasured among Śrī-Vaiṣṇava and Smārta communities of the south; emphasizes bhakti and Hanumān's devotion, with its own poetic ordering of the obstacle-tests and the Sītā meeting. [regional tradition]
- *Variant (recensional ordering):* The Northern (Devanāgarī), Southern, and Bengali (Gauḍīya) manuscript families differ in chapter count and in the placement of the burning of Laṅkā relative to Hanumān's farewell to Sītā; printed editions typically give **68 sargas**. [scholarly]
- *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, [late text]):* the allegorical/Vedāntic retelling frames Sītā as Māyā/Prakṛti and Hanumān's quest as the soul's search; Rāma is the Supreme Brahman throughout. [late text]
- *Variant (which secret sign is used):* Vālmīki sends **both** private proofs — the Kākāsura crow (*Sundara* 38) and the manaḥśilā tilaka painted at Citrakūṭa (*Sundara* 40), both relayed at *Sundara* 67. What varies by recension and retelling is which one a telling keeps: devotional and stage versions usually carry the crow alone and let the tilaka drop, and some later traditions substitute a private memory of their own. Neither of Vālmīki's two is the variant. [regional]

## VII. Why "Sundara" — Significance

- **Independent recitation (Sundara-kāṇḍa pārāyaṇa)** - Uniquely, this book is chanted on its own for success, removal of obstacles, and fulfillment of wishes — because it is the kāṇḍa of *accomplishment*: the task once thought impossible (finding Sītā) is achieved (traditional practice; widely attested).
- **The "beautiful" name** - Explained variously as: the most beautiful (sundara) of the seven books; named for Hanumān (called *Sundara*, the handsome son, by his mother Añjanā); or because it narrates the auspicious tidings (*śubha-vārtā*) of Sītā found.

The load-bearing sargas here: 1 (Maināka/Surasā/Siṃhikā), 3 (Laṅkinī), 11 (mind-purity), 12–13 (the consequence-cascade), 22 (the two-month term and "I could burn you"), 29–35 (omens, language, the three-layer verification), 36 (ring; 36.41 the no-meat verse), 38 (cūḍāmaṇi, the crow), 40 (the manaḥśilā tilaka), 42–47 (the kill-order in the havoc), 54 (the burn-list, Vibhīṣaṇa's house), 59–60 (the mandate debate), 63 (Sugrīva's deduction), 66–67 (the jewel's provenance, the tokens relayed).
- **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa**, *Kiṣkindhā* 65–66 and *Uttara* 36 — for the curse of forgetting and Jāmbavān's reminding, which are not in this kāṇḍa.
- [*Sundara Kāṇḍa*, English translation — wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/sundara-ramayana-translation-and-study/d/doc1503021.html).

**Other recensions and retellings (for the variant bullets above):**

- **Tulsīdās**, *Rāmcaritmānas*, *Sundar Kāṇḍ* (16th c. Awadhi) — for the Vibhīṣaṇa-instruction expansion, the re-staged reminding, and the extended scope.
- **Kamban**, *Irāmāvatāram* (*Kamba Rāmāyaṇam*, ~12th c. Tamil) — south-Indian regional variant.
- *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa* — allegorical/Vedāntic recension [late text].
