# Sundara Kāṇḍa — The Beautiful Book

> Cosmic address: [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md) → 28th Mahāyuga → **[Tretā Yuga](../../01-brahma-current-life/the-14-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** → the Rāma-avatāra cycle.

The fifth book of Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa, **68 sargas / ~2,885 ślokas**, named *Sundara* ("Beautiful") — by tradition because it is the most beautiful book, or because its hero Hanumān is *sundara* (handsome), or after Añjanā's pet-name *Sundara* for her son. It is the **only kāṇḍa in which Rāma is not the principal actor** — its hero is Hanumān — and is the single most independently recited/parayana'd book of the epic, held to confer success and remove obstacles. Narratively it is one continuous arc: the crossing of the ocean, the search of Laṅkā, the finding of Sītā, the burning of the city, and the return.

## I. The Crossing of the Ocean (sargas 1–3)

- **The leap (mahā-pluti)** - On Mount Mahendra, Hanumān grows to colossal size, compresses the mountain underfoot, and launches a 100-yojana leap across the southern ocean; the recoil shakes the mountain, trees, and beasts fly up with him (Vālmīki *Sundara* 1).
  - *Variant (folk / Vaiṣṇava devotional):* Hanumān has forgotten his own divine strength (a curse/boon of his childhood), and Jāmbavān must "remind" him of his powers before he can leap — this re-energizing scene is a staple of [Tulsīdās's *Rāmcaritmānas*](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md) and oral retellings; in Vālmīki the reminding (by Jāmbavān) occurs at the end of *Kiṣkindhā*.
- **Maināka, the golden mountain** - The ocean-god Sāgara, indebted to the Ikṣvākus, bids the submerged mountain Maināka rise to offer Hanumān rest. Hanumān touches it in courtesy but refuses to halt, having vowed not to pause until the task is done (Vālmīki *Sundara* 1).
- **Surasā, mother of the nāgas** - Sent by the devas to test him, the rākṣasī Surasā blocks his path and demands he enter her mouth. Hanumān swells enormously; when she widens her jaws to match, he shrinks to thumb-size, darts in and out, then bows — passing the test of strength *and* wit. She blesses him (Vālmīki *Sundara* 1).
  - *Variant (later texts):* the obstacle is sometimes named or doubled with **Devī/the test-rākṣasī**; some retellings make Surasā an aspect of the Mother-goddess testing his humility.
- **Siṃhikā, the shadow-seizer** - A sea-monster that catches prey by gripping its shadow drags Hanumān down toward her gullet; he shrinks, plunges into her mouth, and rends her vital organs from within, killing her, then re-emerges and flies on (Vālmīki *Sundara* 1).
- **Landfall on Mount Trikūṭa / Lambā** - Hanumān alights on the wooded peak overlooking Laṅkā, shrinks to a small (cat-sized) form to scout unseen, and waits for nightfall to enter (Vālmīki *Sundara* 2).

## II. Entering and Searching Laṅkā (sargas 2–13)

- **Laṅkā beheld** - Hanumān marvels at the golden, fortified, moated city of Rāvaṇa, its ramparts, gates, and rākṣasa watch (Vālmīki *Sundara* 2–3).
- **Laṅkinī / Laṅkā-devī, the guardian goddess** - The tutelary deity of the city, in rākṣasī form, challenges and strikes him at the gate; Hanumān fells her with a single blow. Reeling, she recalls a prophecy: *when a vānara overpowers her, the doom of the rākṣasas has come* — and lets him pass (Vālmīki *Sundara* 3).
- **Night search of the mansions** - Entering by night, Hanumān moves house to house through Laṅkā's palaces, gardens, and assembly halls, seeing rākṣasa revelry and sleeping women but not Sītā (Vālmīki *Sundara* 4–5).
- **Rāvaṇa's palace and the puṣpaka** - He enters Rāvaṇa's great mansion and the flying chariot Puṣpaka, describing its splendor at length (Vālmīki *Sundara* 6–8).
- **The inner apartments and Mandodarī** - In the harem he sees Rāvaṇa asleep amid his wives and a supremely beautiful queen (Mandodarī). Mistaking her momentarily for Sītā, he rejoices — then reasons that Sītā, grieving and separated from Rāma, could never be sleeping in luxury, and rebukes himself for the lapse (Vālmīki *Sundara* 9–11).
- **Despair and resolve** - Failing to find her, Hanumān sinks into despair, weighing the consequences of returning empty-handed, then rallies and decides to search the one place left — the **Aśoka-vana** (Vālmīki *Sundara* 11–13).

## III. Finding Sītā in the Aśoka Grove (sargas 14–40)

- **The Aśoka-vana** - Hanumān enters the pleasure-grove and hides in a śiṃśapā (aśoka) tree, from which he spots a wasted, sorrowful woman in soiled garments, guarded by hideous rākṣasīs — Sītā (Vālmīki *Sundara* 14–15).
- **Rāvaṇa's wooing and threat** - At dawn Rāvaṇa comes with his women to court Sītā with promises of queenship; she rejects him utterly, placing a blade of grass between them. Enraged, he gives her **two months** to relent or be killed and cooked for his meal, then departs; the rākṣasīs threaten and harangue her (Vālmīki *Sundara* 18–23).
- **Sītā's despair** - Tormented, Sītā resolves to take her own life by hanging herself with her braid, seeing no rescue (Vālmīki *Sundara* 24–28).
- **Trijaṭā's dream** - The aged rākṣasī Trijaṭā restrains the others, recounting an omen-dream: a white-toothed vānara burns Laṅkā, Rāvaṇa rides south (toward death) on a donkey clad in red, oil-smeared and garlanded, while Rāma triumphs — foretelling the rākṣasas' ruin and Sītā's deliverance (Vālmīki *Sundara* 27).
  - *Variant (Vibhīṣaṇa's lineage):* in several traditions and in [*Rāmcaritmānas*](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md), Trijaṭā is identified as **Vibhīṣaṇa's daughter**, the lone compassionate rākṣasī.
- **Hanumān's gentle approach** - To avoid frightening her, Hanumān, perched in the tree, softly recites Rāma's story and genealogy in Sanskrit so Sītā will trust him; she fears it is Rāvaṇa in disguise practicing illusion (Vālmīki *Sundara* 30–32).
- **The signet ring (aṅgulīya)** - Hanumān reveals himself, descends, and gives Sītā **Rāma's signet ring engraved with his name** as proof; recognizing it she weeps with joy and questions him about Rāma (Vālmīki *Sundara* 35–36).
- **Offer to carry her back, declined** - Hanumān offers to carry Sītā across the sea on his back. She refuses on principle: she will not willingly touch another man, and Rāma's honor demands that **he himself** slay Rāvaṇa and recover her in battle (Vālmīki *Sundara* 37).
- **The cūḍāmaṇi (crest-jewel)** - As a return token Sītā gives Hanumān her **cūḍāmaṇi** (the jewel from her hair, a wedding gift of Janaka) and recalls a private incident — the **Kākāsura (crow-demon Jayanta) episode**, when Rāma loosed a blade of grass as a brahmāstra that took the crow's eye — a sign only she and Rāma could know (Vālmīki *Sundara* 36–38).
  - *Variant (token):* devotional retellings and [*Rāmcaritmānas*](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md) emphasize the exchange of ring **for** cūḍāmaṇi as the heart of the meeting; the "secret sign" (sometimes the *tilaka*-on-cheek story, or the crow) varies by recension as the intimate proof Rāma will recognize.

## IV. The Havoc and the Burning of Laṅkā (sargas 41–55)

- **Wrecking the Aśoka grove** - To provoke the enemy and gauge their strength, Hanumān uproots trees, smashes pavilions and ponds, and lays waste the grove "like fire in a dry forest," sparing only the śiṃśapā where Sītā sits (Vālmīki *Sundara* 41).
- **Killing the kiṅkaras and warriors** - Rāvaṇa sends guards in waves; Hanumān slays the 80,000 kiṅkaras, then the temple-guard **Jambumālī** (son of Prahasta) and the sons of ministers, fighting from atop a great pillar/gateway (Vālmīki *Sundara* 42–44).
- **Death of the senāpatis and the five generals** - He kills five army-commanders and the sons of Rāvaṇa's chief ministers sent against him (Vālmīki *Sundara* 45–46).
- **Death of Akṣa-kumāra** - Rāvaṇa's young son **Akṣa(ya)** attacks valiantly; Hanumān, admiring his courage, seizes him by the feet and dashes him to death (Vālmīki *Sundara* 47).
- **Captured by Indrajit's Brahmāstra** - Rāvaṇa sends his eldest, **Meghanāda (Indrajit)**, who binds Hanumān with the **Brahmāstra**. Hanumān submits out of reverence for Brahmā's weapon, though he could free himself; the rākṣasas then add ordinary ropes, which (by the astra's rule) cancels its power — but he feigns captivity to enter Rāvaṇa's court (Vālmīki *Sundara* 48).
- **In Rāvaṇa's court** - Dragged before Rāvaṇa, Hanumān declares himself Rāma's envoy, warns Rāvaṇa to return Sītā or face annihilation, and rebukes his arrogance. Rāvaṇa orders his death (Vālmīki *Sundara* 49–51).
- **Vibhīṣaṇa's counsel** - Rāvaṇa's brother **Vibhīṣaṇa** intervenes: an envoy may not be killed by law; let him be punished, not slain. Rāvaṇa relents to **burning Hanumān's tail** (Vālmīki *Sundara* 52–53).
- **The tail set alight** - The rākṣasas wrap his tail in oil-soaked cloth and ignite it; Sītā prays to Agni to keep it cool for Hanumān. Feeling no burn, Hanumān shrinks, slips his bonds, and grows again (Vālmīki *Sundara* 53).
- **The burning of Laṅkā** - With his blazing tail Hanumān leaps roof to roof, torching mansions, gateways, and the whole golden city — sparing (by most accounts) Vibhīṣaṇa's house. He quenches the flame in the sea (Vālmīki *Sundara* 54).
- **Fear for Sītā** - Seeing Laṅkā ablaze, Hanumān is struck with horror that Sītā may have burned; the cāraṇas/devas reassure him, and he returns to find her safe — fire, by her chastity, cannot harm her (Vālmīki *Sundara* 55).
  - *Variant (sequence):* some recensions and retellings place the burning of Laṅkā **after** Hanumān has already taken leave of Sītā; others (and the order of certain printed Vālmīki recensions) move the despair-over-Sītā reflection differently — the kāṇḍa's chapter order varies slightly between Northern, Southern (e.g. the *valmikiramayan.net* Southern recension), and Gauḍīya manuscript families.

## V. The Return (sargas 56–68)

- **Farewell to Sītā** - Hanumān returns to the grove, comforts Sītā, again offers to carry her (again declined), receives her final message and the cūḍāmaṇi, and takes leave (Vālmīki *Sundara* 56–57; ordering varies by recension).
- **The return leap and report at the seashore** - From Mount Ariṣṭa he leaps back across the ocean and lands among the waiting vānaras (Aṅgada, Jāmbavān et al.), shouting *"dṛṣṭā Sītā!"* — "I have seen Sītā!" (Vālmīki *Sundara* 57).
- **The Madhuvana (honey-grove) revel** - Overjoyed, the vānaras raid Sugrīva's protected honey-garden **Madhuvana**, drinking honey and fighting off its keeper **Dadhimukha**, Sugrīva's uncle, who complains to the king — Sugrīva rightly reads the riot as proof of success and is pleased (Vālmīki *Sundara* 60–62).
- **Reporting to Rāma** - The host returns to Kiṣkindhā; Hanumān narrates everything to Rāma — Sītā found, faithful, grieving, in the Aśoka-vana — and presents the **cūḍāmaṇi**. Rāma, overwhelmed, presses it to his heart and weeps, and resolves on the march to Laṅkā, bridging to the [Yuddha Kāṇḍa](./6-yuddha-kanda.md) (Vālmīki *Sundara* 63–68).

## VI. Major Variant Traditions

- *Variant ([Tulsīdās, *Rāmcaritmānas*], 16th c. Awadhi):* The Sundar Kāṇḍ is among the most beloved sections for recitation. It expands the **Hanumān–Vibhīṣaṇa meeting** (Vibhīṣaṇa instructs Hanumān how to find Sītā) and, distinctively, **continues past Vālmīki's fifth book** to include episodes Vālmīki places in *Yuddha*: the vānara army's march, the camp on the shore, Vibhīṣaṇa's defection and welcome by Rāma, Rāvaṇa's spies, Rāma's plea to the ocean (Samudra), and the bridge-building — so Tulsīdās's "Sundar Kāṇḍ" spans more events than Vālmīki's. [variant scope]
- *Variant ([Kamban, *Irāmāvatāram* / Kamba Rāmāyaṇam], ~12th c. Tamil):* Treasured among Śrī-Vaiṣṇava and Smārta communities of the south; emphasizes bhakti and Hanumān's devotion, with its own poetic ordering of the obstacle-tests and the Sītā meeting. [regional tradition]
- *Variant (recensional ordering):* The Northern (Devanāgarī), Southern, and Bengali (Gauḍīya) manuscript families differ in chapter count and in the placement of the burning of Laṅkā relative to Hanumān's farewell to Sītā; printed editions typically give **68 sargas**. [scholarly]
- *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, [late text]):* the allegorical/Vedāntic retelling frames Sītā as Māyā/Prakṛti and Hanumān's quest as the soul's search; Rāma is the Supreme Brahman throughout. [late text]
- *Variant (the secret sign):* the intimate proof Sītā sends — variously the **Kākāsura (crow) episode**, the **tilaka/cosmetic-mark on the cheek** story, or another private memory — differs across recensions and regional retellings; all serve the same narrative function. [disputed]

## VII. Why "Sundara" — Significance

- **Independent recitation (Sundara-kāṇḍa pārāyaṇa)** - Uniquely, this book is chanted on its own for success, removal of obstacles, and fulfillment of wishes — because it is the kāṇḍa of *accomplishment*: the task once thought impossible (finding Sītā) is achieved (traditional practice; widely attested).
- **The "beautiful" name** - Explained variously as: the most beautiful (sundara) of the seven books; named for Hanumān (called *Sundara*, the handsome son, by his mother Añjanā); or because it narrates the auspicious tidings (*śubha-vārtā*) of Sītā found.
