# Sundara Kāṇḍa — The Beautiful Book

> Cosmic address: [Vaivasvata Manvantara](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md) → 28th Mahāyuga → **[Tretā Yuga](../../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** → the Rāma-avatāra cycle.
> Parent: **[Rāmāyaṇa deep-dive](README.md)** · Previous: [Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa](4-kishkindha-kanda.md) · Next: [Yuddha Kāṇḍa](6-yuddha-kanda.md)
> See also: [Daśāvatāra (Rāma)](../../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) · [Sūryavaṃśa](../../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md) · [Itihāsa-Epics corpus](../../90-literature-corpus/smriti-itihasa-epics.md)

The fifth book of Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa. In the vulgate / Southern text it has **68 sargas**; valmikiramayan.net numbers **2,809 verses** in them, and ~2,885 is the round figure most often quoted (verse totals vary with the edition). The Baroda **Critical Edition** sets the same story out in **66 sargas / 2,487 verses**. It is named *Sundara* ("Beautiful"), and tradition explains the name three ways: it is the most beautiful book; its hero Hanumān is *sundara* (handsome); or *Sundara* was Añjanā's pet-name for her son [traditional]. It is the **only kāṇḍa in which Rāma is not the principal actor**. Its hero is Hanumān, and it is the book of the epic most often recited on its own (*pārāyaṇa*), a recitation held to bring success and remove obstacles. The narrative runs as one continuous arc: the crossing of the ocean, the search of Laṅkā, the finding of Sītā, the burning of the city, and the return.

## I. The Crossing of the Ocean (sargas 1–3)

- **The forgetting and the reminding** - When the search-party reaches the shore, Hanumān does not know his own strength. As a boy he had harried the hermitages, and the sages born in the lines of **Bhṛgu and Aṅgiras** cursed him: *for a long time* he would not know the strength he relied on to trouble them (Vālmīki *Uttara* 36; Critical Edition 7.36.32–33). The same sarga says this curse is why he never used his strength while Vāli was hounding Sugrīva (CE 7.36.39–40). On the southern shore Jāmbavān tells him again of his birth, his parentage and his boons (Vālmīki *Kiṣkindhā* 65–66), and Hanumān grows to full size (*Kiṣkindhā* 67). The curse and the reminding are both core Vālmīki, not devotional additions.
  - *Note (the release-clause):* the CE verse sets only a duration ("for a long time", *dīrghakālam*). It does not state the familiar rule that his strength returns the moment someone reminds him of it. That rule is the standard reading of commentators and translators (e.g. the translator's note to *Kiṣkindhā* 66 on valmikiramayan.net). [commentarial]
  - *Variant ([Tulsīdās, *Rāmcaritmānas*](recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md)):* the rousing stays at the close of Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍ. Jāmbavān asks *kā cupa sādhi rahehu balavānā* ("why do you sit silent, mighty one?") and tells him *rāma kāja lagi tava avatārā* ("you were born for Rāma's work"), and Hanumān at once grows to a mountain's size. No curse is narrated there.
- **The leap (mahā-pluti)** - On Mount Mahendra, Hanumān grows to colossal size, compresses the mountain underfoot, and launches a 100-yojana leap across the southern ocean. The recoil shakes the mountain, and trees and beasts fly up with him (Vālmīki *Sundara* 1).
- **Maināka, the golden mountain** - The ocean-god **Sāgara** owes the Ikṣvākus a debt, because Sagara's sons dug him out. He orders the submerged mountain Maināka to rise and offer Hanumān rest (1.87–95). Maināka has his own reason to comply. In the Kṛta Yuga, when Indra was cutting the wings from the mountains with his vajra, **Vāyu** hid him beneath the sea, so the flier overhead is his rescuer's son (1.123–127). Hanumān touches the peak in courtesy but will not stop, having vowed not to pause until the task is done. **Indra, watching, grants Maināka *abhaya*** (freedom from fear) and leave to stay where he pleases (1.140).
- **Surasā, mother of the nāgas** - The devas, gandharvas, siddhas and great sages send **Surasā the nāga-mother** to test his strength (*surasāṃ nāgamātaram*, "sun-bright", 1.145; CE 1.130). She is a nāga-mother, not a rākṣasī by nature, and takes a terrifying form for the test alone. She blocks his path and demands that he enter her mouth. Hanumān swells enormously; when she widens her jaws to match, he shrinks to thumb-size, darts in and out, then bows. He has passed the test of strength *and* wit, and she resumes her own shape and blesses him (Vālmīki *Sundara* 1).
  - *Variant (Tulsīdās, *Mānas* Sundar Kāṇḍ 1–2):* keeps the same three encounters in the same order: the ocean sends Maināka, then the devas send Surasā *to know his strength and wit* (*jānaiṃ kahuṁ bala buddhi biseṣā*), then the shadow-seizer.
- **Siṃhikā, the shadow-seizer** - A rākṣasī named Siṃhikā (*siṃhikā nāma rākṣasī*) catches her prey by gripping its shadow. She drags Hanumān down toward her gullet; he shrinks, plunges into her mouth, and tears her vital organs from within. She dies, and he re-emerges and flies on (Vālmīki *Sundara* 1.183–196). *(She is distinct from Siṃhikā the mother of Rāhu; the two share a name only.)*
  - *Variant (Tulsīdās):* the *Mānas* does not name her. She is only "a night-wanderer who lived in the sea" and seized birds by their reflections (*nisicari eka sindhu mahuṁ rahaī*, Sundar 2–3). [later retelling]
- **The three obstacles are three different things** - The crossing is shaped by who sends each obstacle, not by rising danger. Maināka is the **ocean's courtesy**, Surasā is the **devas' test**, and Siṃhikā is a **genuine predator** met by chance. Only the third is an enemy; lumping the first two in as "demons in the way" loses the design (Vālmīki *Sundara* 1).
- **Landfall on Mount Trikūṭa / Lambā** - Hanumān alights on a peak of Mount **Lamba**, wooded with ketaka, uddālaka and coconut (1.208), and views Laṅkā set on the summit of **Trikūṭa** (2.1). After sunset he shrinks to the size of a cat (*vṛṣadaṃśaka-mātraḥ*, 2.49) to scout unseen (Vālmīki *Sundara* 1–2).

## II. Entering and Searching Laṅkā (sargas 2–13)

- **Laṅkā beheld** - Hanumān marvels at Rāvaṇa's golden, fortified, moated city, with its ramparts, its gates and its rākṣasa watch (Vālmīki *Sundara* 2–3).
- **Laṅkinī / Laṅkā-devī, the guardian goddess** - The city's guardian deity, in rākṣasī form, holds the gate on Rāvaṇa's orders. She challenges Hanumān and strikes him with her palm. He answers with his left fist, holding back because she is a woman, and fells her with the one blow. Reeling, she tells him what **Svayambhū (Brahmā)** had told her long ago (*purā*): *when some vānara overpowers you by force, know that danger has come upon the rākṣasas* (3.47). She traces that danger to Sītā's abduction and lets him pass. In Vālmīki she has no personal name: she calls herself *ahaṃ tu nagarī laṅkā svayam eva*, "I am the city of Laṅkā herself" (3.30, 3.45).
  - *Note (the name):* the name **Laṅkinī** comes from later retellings: *nāma laṅkinī eka nisicarī* (Tulsīdās, *Mānas* Sundar 4). In the *Mānas* she too recalls Brahmā's sign once a monkey's single blow fells her. [later tradition]
- **Night search of the mansions** - Hanumān enters the city at night, setting his **left foot** down first, a gesture aimed at his enemies (4.2). He moves from house to house through Laṅkā's palaces, gardens and assembly halls, and sees rākṣasa revelry and sleeping women, but not Sītā (Vālmīki *Sundara* 4–5).
- **Rāvaṇa's palace and the puṣpaka** - He enters Rāvaṇa's great mansion and finds the flying chariot Puṣpaka, whose splendour the text describes at length (Vālmīki *Sundara* 6–8).
- **The inner apartments and Mandodarī** - In the women's quarters he sees Rāvaṇa asleep among his wives, and apart from them a supremely beautiful queen. The vulgate names her **Mandodarī** (10.51–52; CE 8.48). For a moment he takes her for Sītā and rejoices. Then he reasons that Sītā, grieving and parted from Rāma, could never be sleeping in luxury, and he rebukes himself for the lapse (Vālmīki *Sundara* 10–11).
- **The mind-purity self-audit** - After searching a hall of sleeping, half-clad women, Hanumān stops to examine his own conduct. Looking at another man's sleeping wives is a fault in itself (11.37), but no disturbance arose in his mind. The mind is what drives every sense (*mano hi hetuḥ sarveṣām indriyāṇām pravartate*, 11.41), and Sītā could only be searched for among women. This is the epic's most explicit statement of the envoy's inner discipline (Vālmīki *Sundara* 11.37–43).
- **Despair and the consequence-cascade** - When he still cannot find her, Hanumān sinks into despair and follows the failure through to its end. If he returns empty-handed, Sugrīva's word fails; Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa die of grief, Bharata and Śatrughna follow, the Ikṣvāku line ends, and the vānara host (Aṅgada, Tārā and the rest) perishes with it. Rather than return, he weighs fasting to death at the shore. Then he rejects despair as the enemy of every undertaking and bows to Rāma with Lakṣmaṇa, to Sītā, to Rudra, Indra, Yama and Vāyu, and to the moon, the sun and the Maruts (13.59). He turns to the one place left, the **Aśoka-vana** (Vālmīki *Sundara* 12–13).

## III. Finding Sītā in the Aśoka Grove (sargas 14–40)

- **The Aśoka-vana** - Hanumān enters the pleasure-grove and hides in a **śiṃśapā** tree (14.41). The śiṃśapā is *Dalbergia sissoo*, the Indian rosewood: a separate tree standing *inside* the aśoka grove, not an aśoka. From there he sees a wasted, grieving woman in soiled garments, guarded by hideous rākṣasīs. By her bearing and her bodily marks he becomes sure that she is Sītā (Vālmīki *Sundara* 14–16).
- **Dawn in Laṅkā** - In the last watch of the night, Hanumān hears Vedic chanting from **brahma-rākṣasas** versed in the six Vedāṅgas and skilled at great sacrifices. Auspicious music then wakes Rāvaṇa (18.2–3). The text gives Laṅkā a learned, ritual life, not only its revels.
- **Rāvaṇa's wooing and threat** - At dawn Rāvaṇa comes with his women and courts Sītā with promises of queenship. She rejects him completely and places a blade of grass between them before she answers (21.2). He tells her that **two months** remain of the term he set her (22.8); if she still refuses him after that, she will be cut up in his kitchen for his morning meal (22.9). He leaves, and the rākṣasīs threaten and harangue her (Vālmīki *Sundara* 18–24; the original twelve-month ultimatum is *Araṇya* 56).
- **"I could burn you to ashes"** - Sītā tells Rāvaṇa to his face that he deserves to be burned to ashes and that she has the power to do it. She gives two reasons for not doing it: she has **no mandate from Rāma**, and she will not spend her stored-up austerity on him (*asandeśāt tu rāmasya tapasaś cānupālanāt*, Vālmīki *Sundara* 22.20). Her restraint is a choice, not helplessness.
- **Dhānyamālinī** - One of Rāvaṇa's women, the rākṣasī **Dhānyamālinī**, embraces him and draws him away: *sport with me; what is this unwilling woman to you?* He goes off laughing, and the earth seems to shake under his steps (Vālmīki *Sundara* 22.39–44).
- **The rākṣasī guards by name** - Several of the guards who threaten Sītā are named. In 23 they are **Ekajaṭā, Harijaṭā, Praghasā, Vikaṭā and Durmukhī**; in 24 they are **Vinatā, Vikaṭā, Caṇḍodarī, Praghasā, Ajāmukhī** and a guard named **Śūrpaṇakhā** (Vālmīki *Sundara* 23.5–18, 24.20–46). The text does not identify this Śūrpaṇakhā (24.46; CE 22.40) with Rāvaṇa's sister.
- **Sītā's despair** - Tormented, Sītā resolves to die. In sarga 28 she remembers Rāvaṇa's two-month limit and moves to hang herself with her own braid, seeing no rescue (Vālmīki *Sundara* 24–26, 28).
- **Trijaṭā's dream** - The aged rākṣasī Trijaṭā (*vṛddhā*) restrains the others with an omen-dream. In it, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa appear in white garlands and garments, and Sītā sits in white on a white mountain in the sea. Rāvaṇa falls from the Puṣpaka, shaven-headed and dressed in black, and is dragged off by a woman (27.23). Then, garlanded in red, drinking oil and laughing, he rides south in a donkey-chariot and falls headlong from the donkey (27.24–25). Rāvaṇa, Indrajit and Kumbhakarṇa set out southward on a boar, a porpoise and a camel (27.30). Only **Vibhīṣaṇa** stands apart, under a white umbrella in white garments, mounted on a four-tusked elephant with his four ministers (27.31–33). Laṅkā sinks into the sea and is burned by a swift vānara, *Rāma's messenger* (*dagdhā rāmasya dūtena vānareṇa tarasvinā*, 27.35–36). The dream foretells the rākṣasas' ruin and Sītā's rescue (Vālmīki *Sundara* 27).
  - *Variant (Trijaṭā's parentage):* Vālmīki gives Trijaṭā no parentage. Later texts disagree with each other. Bulcke's survey records that Kamban's *Irāmāvatāram*, Govindarāja's *Bhūṣaṇa* commentary on Vālmīki, Balarāmadāsa's Oḍia *Rāmāyaṇa*, the Javanese *Kakawin Rāmāyaṇa* and the Malay *Hikayat Seri Rama* make her **Vibhīṣaṇa's daughter**. The *Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa* makes her Vibhīṣaṇa's **wife**, and Eknāth's Marathi *Bhāvārtha Rāmāyaṇa* his **sister**. The Jain *Vasudevahiṇḍī* makes her a sister of Rāvaṇa (as cited in the Wikipedia article on Trijaṭā). See [Trijaṭā](../characters/trijata.md). [later tradition]
- **The omens turn** - As Sītā reaches the end of her endurance, auspicious signs break over her: her left eye, left arm and left thigh throb, the traditional portents of coming joy, and her garment slips a little. Her despair lifts just before Hanumān speaks (Vālmīki *Sundara* 29).
- **Which language to speak** - Hanumān, still hidden, weighs how to speak. If he uses Sanskrit as a twice-born man speaks it, she will take him for Rāvaṇa in disguise (30.18). He must instead use *mānuṣaṃ vākyam arthavat*, meaningful human speech (30.19). A verse earlier, though, he calls the speech he can use *mānuṣī saṃskṛtā*, "human and refined" (30.17), so the text does not say exactly which tongue is meant, and readings of the passage differ. He decides to speak small and low, and to begin with Rāma's lineage rather than with himself (Vālmīki *Sundara* 30.17–19; CE 28.18).
- **Hanumān's gentle approach** - Perched in the tree, he softly tells Rāma's story so that Sītā will trust him. She still wonders whether this is a dream or Rāvaṇa's illusion (Vālmīki *Sundara* 31–32).
- **The three-layer verification** - Sītā does not accept him at once, and the text lays out her caution step by step. First she tells her own story back, to test what he knows of it. This includes that she had lived **twelve years in Rāma's house** before the exile (*Sundara* 33.17; CE 31.13). Next Hanumān declares himself Rāma's envoy and the son of the wind (34). Last, he describes Rāma's and Lakṣmaṇa's bodily marks and gives his own parentage, with Kesarī as his father and Vāyu as his begetter, until the description itself is proof (35).
  - *Note (internal chronology):* the twelve-year figure is not unique to this sarga. Sītā gives it to Rāvaṇa at *Araṇya* 47.4 as well, and adds that the coronation was planned in the thirteenth year (47.5). The same speech gives Rāma's age at the exile as 25 and hers as 18 (47.10–11), and *Bāla* 20.2 has Daśaratha say "my Rāma is not yet sixteen" when Viśvāmitra asks for him. These figures cannot all be reconciled. The epic carries the inconsistency, and commentators resolve it in different ways. [disputed]
- **The signet ring (aṅgulīya)** - Hanumān gives Sītā **Rāma's ring, marked with Rāma's name** (*rāma-nāmāṅkitam*), as proof (36.2). Recognizing it she weeps with joy and asks him about Rāma (Vālmīki *Sundara* 36).
- **"Rāghava does not eat meat"** - Asked how Rāma lives in the forest, Hanumān says that he takes neither meat nor liquor, but eats the forest food set out for him at the fifth part of the day (*na māṃsaṃ rāghavo bhuṅkte na cāpi madhu sevate | vanyaṃ suvihitaṃ nityaṃ bhaktam aśnāti pañcamam*, Vālmīki *Sundara* 36.41; CE 34.39). valmikiramayan.net renders *pañcamam* "in the evening"; it is also read as the fifth part of the day. The verse is in open tension with the epic's own hunting and venison passages, and is recorded here without explaining either side away.
- **Offer to carry her back, declined** - Hanumān offers to carry Sītā across the sea on his back. She refuses on principle. Out of devotion to her husband she will not willingly touch another man's body; Rāvaṇa's touch was forced on her when she was helpless (37.60–61). Rāma's honour also requires that **he himself** kill Rāvaṇa and win her back in battle (Vālmīki *Sundara* 37).
- **The cūḍāmaṇi (crest-jewel)** - As a return token, Sītā unties her **cūḍāmaṇi** from where it was knotted in her garment and gives it to Hanumān (38.69–70). With it she recalls a private incident at Citrakūṭa. A crow, *seemingly a son of Indra* (*putraḥ kila sa śakrasya*, 38.28), tore at her between the breasts. Rāma took a blade of *darbha* from his grass bed, charged it with the brahmāstra (38.30–31), and the crow, finding no other refuge, gave up its **right eye** to save its life (38.36–37). It was a sign only she and Rāma could know. She also sets her term: *I will hold on to life for one month; beyond a month I will not live* (38.67). The jewel came to her through a chain of gifts, and Rāma himself recites it when he receives the jewel back. It was **born of the waters** and given by **Indra (Śakra)**, pleased at a sacrifice, to Janaka; Janaka, "my father-in-law", gave it to Sītā as a bride, and it was tied on her head (Vālmīki *Sundara* 66.4–5; CE 64.4–5).
  - *Note (the crow's name):* Vālmīki says only "a son of Indra". The name **Jayanta** for the Kākāsura is a later identification. Tulsīdās uses it (*nārada dekhā bikala jayaṃtā*, *Mānas* Araṇya Kāṇḍ, opening). The *Mānas* also tells the episode differently: the crow pecks Sītā's **foot** (*sītā carana coṃca hati bhāgā*), not her breast, and the scene is narrated in Araṇya Kāṇḍ rather than recalled in Sundara. [later tradition]
- **The second private proof — the manaḥśilā tilaka** - Sītā sends a second recognition-token, and it comes from Vālmīki's own text, not from a rival tradition. On Citrakūṭa, when the mark on her forehead had worn away, Rāma painted a fresh one on her cheek with **manaḥśilā** (red realgar) (Vālmīki *Sundara* 40.5; CE 38.5). She tells Hanumān to put both memories to Rāma, the crow and the tilaka, and repeats her one-month limit (40.10). Hanumān relays both at *Sundara* 65 (crow 65.19, tilaka 65.22) and tells the crow story again at length in 67. The kāṇḍa therefore has **two** intimate proofs, not one proof plus a variant.
- **Can the army cross?** - Before he leaves, Sītā doubts that Rāma, Lakṣmaṇa and the vānara host can cross the ocean at all. Hanumān reassures her about the army's strength and promises that Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa will come soon (Vālmīki *Sundara* 39). He reports the exchange to Rāma at 68.

## IV. The Havoc and the Burning of Laṅkā (sargas 41–55)

- **Wrecking the Aśoka grove** - Hanumān reasons it out as statecraft. The first three means (*upāyas*) are useless against rākṣasas, so the fourth, open force, is what the case calls for (41.2–3). Force will also let him gauge the enemy and be brought before Rāvaṇa. He uproots trees and smashes pavilions and ponds, laying the grove waste "like fire in a dry forest" (Vālmīki *Sundara* 41). The only part he spares is the śiṃśapā under which Sītā sits, and the rākṣasīs report that to Rāvaṇa (42.17–19).
- **The kiṅkaras and the war-manifesto** - Rāvaṇa sends **80,000 kiṅkaras** (*aśīti-sāhasram*, 42.25). Hanumān tears an iron bar from the gateway and destroys them all. From the archway he then declares himself: son of the wind-god and servant of Rāma, king of Kosala; a thousand Rāvaṇas would be no match for him in battle; Laṅkā's destruction is certain (Vālmīki *Sundara* 42). Asked by the rākṣasīs about the intruder, Sītā answers only with a proverb: *a serpent knows a serpent's tracks* (42.9).
- **The caitya-prāsāda and its hundred guards** - Next Hanumān climbs the **caitya-prāsāda**, the city's sanctuary-tower. He uproots a gold-worked pillar with a hundred edges and whirls it until the friction sets the whole building alight. The **guards** of the sanctuary (*caitya-pālāḥ śatam*, 43.13) come at him with every weapon, and he kills the hundred of them as Indra killed the asuras with the vajra (Vālmīki *Sundara* 43.19).
- **Jambumālī** - Sent separately and afterwards, **Jambumālī, son of Prahasta** (*prahastasya suto balī*, 44.1), great-tusked and armed with a bow, is the first named champion. Hanumān drives the same iron bar through his chest and leaves nothing of his head, arms, knees or bow (Vālmīki *Sundara* 44). *(He is not one of the sanctuary's guards; that was the previous sarga's hundred.)*
- **The seven sons of the chief minister** - The **seven sons of the minister** (*mantriṇaḥ sutāḥ*, 45.1) charge in chariots with chosen weapons. Hanumān kills them at the gateway using nothing but his body: palms, feet, fists, nails, chest and thighs (Vālmīki *Sundara* 45). The verse does not name their father. Some translators identify him as Prahasta, as valmikiramayan.net's introduction to sarga 45 does, but the text does not say so. [commentarial]
- **The five senāpatis** - Next come the five army commanders, named in the text: **Virūpākṣa, Yūpākṣa, Durdhara, Praghasa and Bhāsakarṇa** (46.2–3). Hanumān kills Durdhara by crashing onto his chariot, Virūpākṣa and Yūpākṣa with a sāla tree, and Praghasa and Bhāsakarṇa with a hurled mountain-top, and their army dies with them (Vālmīki *Sundara* 46).
  - *Note (a namesake collision the text does not resolve):* three of the five (**Virūpākṣa, Praghasa, Bhāsakarṇa**) share names with Rāvaṇa's own uncles and cousins in the *Uttara* 5 roster. The epic also uses these names for rākṣasas still alive at *Yuddha* 43.24–25 and 96.30–32. The sarga gives these five **no patronymic and no house**, only their rank, so no identification is asserted here. [disputed] See [Rākṣasas](../../08-beings-and-bestiary/rakshasas.md) and [Akṣakumāra](../characters/akshakumara.md).
- **Death of Akṣa-kumāra** - Rāvaṇa's young son **Akṣa(ya)** attacks with real courage. Hanumān admires him, then seizes him by the feet, whirls him round and dashes him to death (Vālmīki *Sundara* 47).
- **Captured by Indrajit's Brahmāstra** - Rāvaṇa sends his eldest son, **Meghanāda (Indrajit)**, who binds Hanumān with the **Brahmāstra**. Hanumān submits out of reverence for Brahmā's weapon, though he could free himself. The rākṣasas then add ordinary ropes, and by the weapon's rule that cancels its hold. Hanumān keeps up the pretence of captivity so that he can enter Rāvaṇa's court (Vālmīki *Sundara* 48).
- **Hanumān's awe of Rāvaṇa** - Dragged before the throne, the envoy reacts first with astonishment at the rākṣasa king's splendour and power, not with contempt: *had this lord of rākṣasas not been strong in adharma, he could have been the protector even of Indra's heaven* (*yady adharmo na balavān syād ayaṃ rākṣaseśvaraḥ | syād ayaṃ suralokasya saśakrasyāpi rakṣitā*, Vālmīki *Sundara* 49.18; CE 47.18). The epic puts its antagonist's greatness in his enemy's mouth.
- **In Rāvaṇa's court** - Questioned through Rāvaṇa's minister **Prahasta**, Hanumān says he wrecked the grove in order to be brought before the king (50). He declares himself Rāma's envoy and brings Sugrīva's greeting. He warns Rāvaṇa to return Sītā or face destruction, and rebukes his arrogance (51). Rāvaṇa orders his death (52.1) (Vālmīki *Sundara* 50–52).
- **The loophole in the boon** - The sharpest point of the speech is legal. Rāvaṇa trusts in the immunity his austerities won him against asuras and devas. Hanumān tells him in open court that this is exactly his weakness, because Sugrīva is none of the beings it covers: not a god, an asura, a dānava, a gandharva, a yakṣa or a serpent (Vālmīki *Sundara* 51.26–27). The Critical Edition makes the point explicit in a half-verse the vulgate lacks: *mānuṣo rāghavo rājan sugrīvaś ca harīśvaraḥ*, "Rāghava is a man, O king, and Sugrīva lord of monkeys: how will you save your life?" (CE 5.49.24–26). *(Both texts oddly list "nor a man" (na ca mānuṣaḥ) among the things Sugrīva is not: vulgate 51.27, CE 49.25. Only the CE's next half-verse names the human enemy.)*
- **Vibhīṣaṇa's counsel** - Rāvaṇa's brother **Vibhīṣaṇa** intervenes: by law an envoy may not be killed; let him be punished, but not slain. Rāvaṇa settles on **burning Hanumān's tail** (Vālmīki *Sundara* 52–53).
- **The tail set alight** - The rākṣasas wrap his tail in oil-soaked rags, set it on fire, and lead him bound through the city, proclaiming him a spy. The rākṣasīs tell Sītā, and she prays to Agni, *be cool to Hanumān* (*śīto bhava hanūmataḥ*, 53.27–31). Feeling no burn, Hanumān shrinks, slips his bonds, and grows again (Vālmīki *Sundara* 53).
- **The burning of Laṅkā** - With his blazing tail Hanumān leaps from roof to roof through a named roll of the city's great houses: Prahasta, Mahāpārśva, Vajradaṃṣṭra, Śuka and Sāraṇa, Indrajit, Jambumālī, Sumālī, Raśmiketu, Sūryaśatru, Hrasvakarṇa, Daṃṣṭra, Romaśa, Yuddhonmatta, Matta, Dhvajagrīva, Vidyujjihva, Hastimukha, Karāla, Piśāca, Śoṇitākṣa, Kumbhakarṇa, Makarākṣa, Yajñaśatru, Brahmaśatru, Narāntaka, Kumbha and Nikumbha (54.8–15). The roll ends at Rāvaṇa's own palace (54.18–20). **He passes over Vibhīṣaṇa's house deliberately.** The text states outright that he spared it (*varjayitvā … vibhīṣaṇagṛham*, Vālmīki *Sundara* 54.16), and his is the only great house named as untouched.
  - *Variant (quenching the tail in the sea):* this is **recensional, not folk**. The Critical Edition prints the verse *laṅkāṃ samastāṃ saṃdīpya lāṅgūlāgniṃ mahākapiḥ | nirvāpayām āsa tadā samudre harisattamaḥ*: having set all Laṅkā ablaze, the great monkey put out the fire of his tail in the ocean (CE 5.52.17). The valmikiramayan.net Southern text lacks it. Its sarga 54 ends with Hanumān standing on the flat summit of Trikūṭa, crowned with the flames of his tail (54.44), and the celestials rejoicing (54.46–50), and sarga 55 opens on his dread for Sītā. Tulsīdās keeps the dousing too: *kūdi parā puni sindhu majhārī* … *pūṁcha bujhāi khoi śrama* (*Mānas* Sundar 26 and dohā 26). [recensional]
- **Fear for Sītā** - Seeing Laṅkā ablaze, Hanumān is struck with horror that Sītā may have burned, and he reproaches himself for his anger. Good omens and the cāraṇas' talk of her safety reassure him: fire cannot harm her, because of her austerity and her faithfulness (Vālmīki *Sundara* 55). He resolves to see her once more before leaving (55; 56.1).
  - *Variant (sequence):* in Vālmīki, Sītā gives the cūḍāmaṇi **before** the havoc (38), and Hanumān's visit after the fire (56) is a brief second farewell. In Tulsīdās the order is reversed. Hanumān burns Laṅkā first, then goes to Sītā and asks for a token, and only then does she take off the *cūṛāmani* (*Mānas* Sundar 27: *mātu mohi dīje kachu cīnhā … cūṛāmani utāri taba dayaū*). [later retelling]

## V. The Return (sargas 56–68)

- **Farewell to Sītā** - Hanumān goes back to the śiṃśapā, glad to find Sītā unhurt (56.1). She tells him that he could finish the task alone, but that it is fitting for Rāma to fill Laṅkā with arrows and take her back himself (56.3–5). Hanumān promises that Rāma will come soon with the leaders of the vānaras and bears, and takes his leave. He does not repeat his offer to carry her (Vālmīki *Sundara* 56.1–8).
- **The return leap** - He climbs Mount **Ariṣṭa** (56.9) and launches the crossing back from it, so hard that the mountain sinks into the earth (56.26–32). On the way he **touches Maināka** again, here called *Sunābha* (57.13), and lands among the waiting vānaras. There he greets Jāmbavān and Aṅgada and tells them in brief, *dṛṣṭā devī*, "the queen has been seen" (57.35) (Vālmīki *Sundara* 56–57).
- **The recap at the shore** - Asked by Jāmbavān, Hanumān tells the whole journey again to Jāmbavān, Aṅgada and the host: Maināka, Surasā, Siṃhikā, the search, the grove and the burning. This second telling is deliberate, and it is how the kāṇḍa settles what is to be reported and what is not (Vālmīki *Sundara* 58).
- **The mandate debate** - **Hanumān himself** first urges the party to win Laṅkā and bring Sītā back before facing Rāma. He says he could do it alone, and he praises the past deeds of Jāmbavān, Aṅgada, Nīla, Panasa, Mainda and Dvivida (59). **Aṅgada** takes the proposal up with enthusiasm (60). **Jāmbavān rules against it**: they were sent to find her, not to fight, and they must not act without knowing Rāma's will. The report goes to Sugrīva and Rāma first (Vālmīki *Sundara* 59–60).
- **The Madhuvana (honey-grove) revel** - Overjoyed, the vānaras raid Sugrīva's protected honey-garden **Madhuvana** with Aṅgada's leave. They drink the honey and beat off its keeper **Dadhimukha**, Sugrīva's maternal uncle, whom Aṅgada himself throws to the ground. Dadhimukha goes to complain to the king (Vālmīki *Sundara* 61–63).
- **Sugrīva's deduction** - Sugrīva does not punish the raiders. He reasons from the offence itself that the search must have succeeded, because the party would never have dared to raid the royal grove after failing. He tells Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa so, and sends for the party (Vālmīki *Sundara* 63–64; *dṛṣṭā devī na saṃdeho*, 64.28).
- **Reporting to Rāma** - The host reaches Rāma at Mount **Prasravaṇa**. The vānaras tell of Sītā's captivity and of the deadline Rāvaṇa set her (65.3; the **two months** of 22.8), and then urge Hanumān to tell the rest. He bows his head toward where Sītā is and tells it: Sītā found, faithful and grieving among the rākṣasīs (65.10–14). He relays both private proofs, the crow (65.19) and the tilaka (65.22), hands over the **cūḍāmaṇi** (65.23), and gives Sītā's own limit: **one month** (65.24). Rāma presses the jewel to his heart and weeps. He recites its sea-born, Indra-to-Janaka provenance (66.4–5) and says that if she can last a month she will live long (66). He asks Hanumān to repeat her words: the crow story at length (67), and her doubt about the crossing with Hanumān's answer to it (68). The story then bridges to the march on Laṅkā in the [Yuddha Kāṇḍa](6-yuddha-kanda.md) (Vālmīki *Sundara* 64–68).

## VI. Major Variant Traditions

- *Variant ([Tulsīdās, *Rāmcaritmānas*](recensions/ramcharitmanas-tulsidas.md), 16th c. Awadhi):* The Sundar Kāṇḍ is among the sections most loved for recitation. It adds a **Hanumān–Vibhīṣaṇa meeting** before Sītā is found. After searching Rāvaṇa's palace, Hanumān comes upon a house marked by a Hari shrine and tulasī. Vibhīṣaṇa wakes there chanting Rāma's name, Hanumān speaks to him in a brahmin's form, and he is told how to reach Sītā (*juguti bibhīṣana sakala sunāī*). Tulsīdās also **runs past the end of Vālmīki's fifth book** into events Vālmīki places in *Yuddha*: Vibhīṣaṇa's defection and Rāma's welcome of him, the spy Śuka, Rāma's three-day plea to the ocean and his anger, and the ocean's advice that at the touch of **Nala and Nīla** even heavy mountains will float. The kāṇḍ ends there (dohā 60). The **building of the bridge itself falls in Laṅkā Kāṇḍ**, not in Sundar. [variant scope]
- *Variant ([Kamban, *Irāmāvatāram* / Kamba Rāmāyaṇam](recensions/kamban-iramavataram.md), ~12th c. Tamil):* Kamban's Sundara Kāṇṭam is recited devotionally in the south and reworks Vālmīki's material in Tamil poetic idiom, with a strong emphasis on bhakti and on Hanumān as the ideal devotee-messenger. Trijaṭā is Vibhīṣaṇa's daughter in Kamban (see the parentage note above). [regional tradition]
- *Variant (recensional numbering and text):* The **Critical Edition** (Baroda) sets the book in **66 sargas**. From the Puṣpaka sargas onward its numbers generally run **two behind the vulgate**: vulgate 11 = CE 9, 22 = 20, 36 = 34, 49 = 47, 54 = 52, 66 = 64. The two texts also differ in individual verses. The CE has the tail-dousing verse (52.17) and the "Rāghava is a man" half-verse (49.26), which are absent from the valmikiramayan.net Southern text. Both texts have the guard named Śūrpaṇakhā (vulgate 24.46 = CE 22.40). Printed vulgate editions give **68 sargas**. [scholarly]
- *Variant (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa, [late text](recensions/adhyatma-ramayana.md)):* the Vedāntic retelling makes Rāma the Supreme Brahman and Sītā his Māyā-śakti. Before the golden deer, Rāma has the true Sītā enter the fire and leave a **māyā- or chāyā-Sītā** behind (Adhyātma, Araṇya Kāṇḍa). So the captive Hanumān finds in the Aśoka grove is, on this reading, the shadow, and the real Sītā is restored only at the fire-ordeal. The shadow-Sītā device is older than the Adhyātma: the *Kūrma Purāṇa* is usually cited as its first appearance. [late text]
- *Variant (which secret sign is used):* Vālmīki sends **both** private proofs, the Kākāsura crow (*Sundara* 38) and the manaḥśilā tilaka painted at Citrakūṭa (*Sundara* 40). Both are relayed to Rāma at *Sundara* 65 (65.19, 65.22), and the crow is retold at 67. What varies between retellings is which one they keep. In the *Mānas* the crow episode is moved to Araṇya Kāṇḍ and the token in Sundar is the cūṛāmani alone. Devotional and stage versions usually carry the crow and drop the tilaka. Neither of Vālmīki's two proofs is the variant. [regional]

## VII. Why "Sundara" — Significance

- **Independent recitation (Sundara-kāṇḍa pārāyaṇa)** - This book, unusually, is chanted on its own for success, the removal of obstacles and the fulfilment of wishes. The reason given is that it is the kāṇḍa of *accomplishment*: the task once thought impossible, finding Sītā, is achieved (traditional practice; widely attested).
- **The "beautiful" name** - Explained in several ways: it is the most beautiful (sundara) of the seven books; it is named for Hanumān, whom his mother Añjanā called *Sundara*, the handsome son; or it narrates the auspicious news (*śubha-vārtā*) that Sītā has been found. [traditional]
