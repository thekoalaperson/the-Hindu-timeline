# Bhramara-Gītā — Uddhava's Embassy to the Gopīs

> *Where this sits: the aftermath of Kṛṣṇa's Vraja childhood — his cousin and minister **Uddhava** is sent from Mathurā back to Vṛndāvana to console the gopīs abandoned in love-in-separation, where their "Song to the Bee" humbles his learning. It occupies **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.46–47**, in the [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga ([Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)).*
>
> *Parent: [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · Master index: [../README.md](../README.md) · Companions: [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md) · [Rāsa-līlā](./rasa-lila.md) · [Uddhava Gītā](./uddhava-gita.md) · [Lesser Gītās Catalog](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md)*

The **Uddhava-sandeśa** ("Uddhava's message") and the **Bhramara-gītā** ("Song to the Bee") together form **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.46–47** — the supreme scriptural locus of **vipralambha-bhakti** (devotion-in-separation, *prema* tested by absence). After [Kṛṣṇa](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) has left Vṛndāvana for Mathurā and slain Kaṃsa, the gopīs — and his foster-parents Nanda and Yaśodā — are left drowning in grief. Kṛṣṇa dispatches **Uddhava**, his cousin, friend, and minister, as an envoy bearing a consoling message couched in impersonal *jñāna-yoga*. What follows inverts the embassy: the gopīs, in a fever of longing, ignore the messenger and address a passing **bumblebee (bhramara)** as Kṛṣṇa's fickle go-between — and the learned Uddhava departs declaring their wordless love higher than all his knowledge. This file gives the full episode its own treatment, as flagged "candidate for promotion" in the repo's own [Bhāgavata Tales Catalog](./bhagavata-tales-catalog.md#top-index-only-candidates-for-promotion).

## Do not confuse: Bhramara-gītā (10.47) vs. Uddhava Gītā (11.7–29)

- **This file — the Bhramara-gītā (Bhāgavata 10.46–47)** - In Vraja, *early* in Kṛṣṇa's career, **Uddhava is the learner**: he comes to teach the gopīs and instead is taught by their love. A *prema*-lament, not a philosophical discourse. **[this file]**
- **The other Gītā — the Uddhava Gītā / Haṃsa Gītā (Bhāgavata 11.7–29)** - At Dvārakā, *at the very end* of Kṛṣṇa's life, **Uddhava is again the learner** but receives Kṛṣṇa's grand farewell synthesis of bhakti, jñāna, yoga and Sāṃkhya (the 24 gurus, the Bhikṣu-gītā, etc.). A systematic discourse. See [Uddhava Gītā](./uddhava-gita.md).
- **The hinge** - Uddhava is the single thread linking **Vraja-prema** (10.46–47, where he kneels to the gopīs) and **renunciate jñāna** (11.7–29, where he is taught to become a swan). The same devotee receives two opposite lessons across Kṛṣṇa's life. [scholarly]

## Narrative — Bhāgavata 10.46 (the embassy)

- **Kṛṣṇa's commission** - In Mathurā, Kṛṣṇa observes that Nanda, Yaśodā and the gopīs of Vraja are consumed by separation from him. He chooses **Uddhava** — described as his peer in beauty, his dearest friend and trusted counsellor (a disciple of Bṛhaspati) — to carry a message of comfort to Vṛndāvana. (Bhāgavata 10.46.1–8)
- **Uddhava sent with a message** - Kṛṣṇa entrusts Uddhava with a *sandeśa* (verbal message) for the cowherds, reaching Vraja by chariot at sunset; the dust of the cows' hooves and the village's love-laden atmosphere strike him at once. (Bhāgavata 10.46.9–11)
- **Nanda and Yaśodā's grief** - Nanda receives the envoy warmly and pours out his longing, recalling Kṛṣṇa's childhood deeds; Yaśodā's breasts flow with milk at the mere mention of her son. Uddhava consoles them with the assurance that Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Self, ever present, never truly absent. (Bhāgavata 10.46.12–49)
- **Uddhava's first wonder** - Witnessing the parents' devotion through pure parental love (*vātsalya*), Uddhava begins to grasp that the people of Vraja possess something his Yādava learning lacks — a foretaste of the lesson the gopīs will complete. (Bhāgavata 10.46.50)

## Narrative — Bhāgavata 10.47 (the Bhramara-gītā proper)

- **Dawn meeting with the gopīs** - At sunrise the gopīs, who have spent the night singing of Kṛṣṇa, see Uddhava's chariot and recognise the messenger's resemblance to their beloved. They gather around him, hungry for news. (Bhāgavata 10.47.1–8)
- **Uddhava delivers Kṛṣṇa's message** - He conveys Kṛṣṇa's words: that the Lord is the **Self of all beings**, never separated from them, that their seeming separation is illusory, and that he withholds his presence only to fix their minds wholly upon him — a consolation pitched in **impersonal, philosophical (jñāna-yoga) terms**. (Bhāgavata 10.47.9–37)
- **The Song to the Bee (Bhramara-gītā)** - As Uddhava speaks, a **bumblebee (bhramara)** alights nearby. The chief gopī — overcome, half-mad with *vipralambha* — turns from the human envoy to address the bee instead, mistaking/treating it as a second messenger from the faithless Kṛṣṇa. In a virtuoso passage of *prema-vaicittya* (love's bewildered contradictions) she alternately **rebukes the bee** as the touch-tainted servant of a fickle, ungrateful lover, **mocks** Kṛṣṇa's broken promises, and yet **cannot stop praising** him and pining for the touch of his feet. The song is the canonical expression of love that scolds because it cannot cease to love. (Bhāgavata 10.47.12–21)
  - *Identification of the singer:* the Bhāgavata names only "a certain gopī" (*kācit*); the later devotional tradition — above all the Gauḍīya commentators following Jayadeva's [Gīta-Govinda](./rasa-lila.md) — reads her as **Rādhā**, making the Bhramara-gītā Rādhā's lament. The Purāṇa itself does not name Rādhā. [late text] [regional]
  - *Reading the "bee":* commentators take the bee as a figure for the inconstant lover (going flower to flower) and for the messenger who carries the master's blandishments — so that addressing the bee lets the gopī rebuke Kṛṣṇa and his envoy at once without speaking to either directly. [scholarly]
- **Madhumaṅgala / the maddened address** - Her speech wanders from anger to surrender to delirium — counting Kṛṣṇa's "betrayals" (Pūtanā, the gopīs of Mathurā, Lakṣmī herself serving his feet) yet concluding that to hear his glories is the gopīs' only life. This *unmāda* (love-madness) is presented as the highest *rasa*. (Bhāgavata 10.47.12–21)
- **Renewed message and the gopīs' reproach** - Returning to Uddhava, the gopīs reproach the absent Kṛṣṇa for abandoning them after winning their hearts, asking only whether he ever remembers Vraja. Uddhava answers again with assurance of Kṛṣṇa's promised return and presence. (Bhāgavata 10.47.22–48)
- **Uddhava's months in Vraja & his humbling** - Uddhava stays on, drinking in the gopīs' single-pointed love. He concludes — in some of the most-quoted verses of the Purāṇa — that **the gopīs' unlettered, all-consuming love is the supreme attainment**, surpassing the Vedic study, austerity, ritual and *jñāna* he had mastered; he prays to be reborn even as a **shrub, creeper or herb of Vṛndāvana** so that the dust of the gopīs' feet might fall on him. (Bhāgavata 10.47.58–63)
- **Uddhava's farewell hymn** - Taking leave, Uddhava bows to the gopīs and declares their *bhāva* the rarest thing in the three worlds, then returns to Mathurā and reports their love to Kṛṣṇa. (Bhāgavata 10.47.64–69)

## Doctrinal weight — why this episode matters

- **Vipralambha over sambhoga** - The episode establishes, against the union (*sambhoga*) of the [rāsa-līlā](./rasa-lila.md), that **love-in-separation (vipralambha)** is the *higher* devotional mood — absence intensifies and purifies *prema*. The Bhramara-gītā is its scriptural anchor. [scholarly]
- **Prema above jñāna** - Uddhava, the envoy of impersonal knowledge, is overcome by the gopīs' personal love: the Bhāgavata's verdict that **bhakti surpasses jñāna and yoga**, dramatised rather than argued. This anticipates the same Uddhava being told, at Dvārakā, that devotion crowns all paths (see [Uddhava Gītā](./uddhava-gita.md)). [scholarly]
- **The gopī as the model devotee** - The gopīs' love — desireless of liberation, indifferent to scripture, careless of reputation — becomes the paradigm of *uttama-bhakti* for the [Krishna-līlā](./krishna-lila.md) tradition and for Gauḍīya, Vallabha, and Nimbārka theology. [scholarly]
- **The "bee" topos in Sanskrit poetry** - The address-to-a-messenger is a recognised *dūta-kāvya* (messenger-poem) convention; the Bhramara-gītā gives it a bhakti turn and is itself echoed in later Vaiṣṇava lyric (Sūrdās, the *Bhramar-gīt* of the Braj poets). [scholarly] [regional]

## Variants & the wider tradition

- *Variant (Braj-bhāṣā Bhramar-gīt):* the episode generated a whole vernacular sub-genre — **Sūrdās's *Bhramar-gīt*** and others in the *Sūrsāgar* greatly expand the gopīs' rebuke of Uddhava and the bee, foregrounding **Rādhā** and turning the philosopher-envoy into a foil for *saguṇa* devotion against *nirguṇa* abstraction. [late text] [regional]
- *Variant (Rādhā as singer):* Purāṇa = anonymous "a certain gopī"; Gauḍīya/Jayadeva tradition = **Rādhā**. The repo flags this same Rādhā-centring at [Rāsa-līlā § Bhramara-gītā](./rasa-lila.md#the-aftermath-songthe-bhramara-gītā-bhāgavata-1047). [disputed]
- *Variant (placement in the gopī song-cycle):* read alongside the **Veṇu-gītā** (10.21) and **Gopī-gītā** (10.31), the Bhramara-gītā completes an arc — desire kindled, separation in the dance, separation made permanent; all three are gathered in [Rāsa-līlā](./rasa-lila.md). [scholarly]
- *Note (two clocks):* by the scripture-internal clock the embassy falls late in the Dvāpara Yuga, shortly after Kaṃsa's death (traditionally well before the 3102 BCE Kali cusp); by the academic clock it belongs to the [Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md)'s composition, dated c. **9th–10th c. CE**, South Indian provenance. [scholarly]
