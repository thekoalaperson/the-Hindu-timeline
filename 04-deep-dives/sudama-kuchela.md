# Sudāmā (Kucela)

> Where this sits: a beloved set-piece of the **Dvāraka cycle** of the [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md), narrated in the **[Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md)** (Canto 10, chs. 80–81: the visit and welcome in 10.80, the rice and the reward in 10.81) — the paradigm-tale of **devotion (bhakti) over wealth** and the Lord's lavish, unasked **reciprocity** to a poor friend's humble gift.
> Parent: [Deep Dives](README.md) · Catalog: [Bhāgavata Tales](bhagavata-tales-catalog.md) · Related: [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md), [Ajāmila](ajamila.md)

The **Sudāmā-caritra** ("the deeds of Sudāmā") — known across South India as the **Kucela-vṛttānta / Kucela-upākhyāna** — tells of an impoverished brāhmaṇa, a boyhood schoolmate of **Kṛṣṇa** at the house of guru **Sāndīpani** in Avantī (Ujjain), who, prodded by his wife, goes to **Dvārakā** carrying nothing but four fistfuls of **flattened rice** (Skt. *pṛthuka*; Hindi *poha*; Gujarati *pauā*; Tamil *aval*; Malayalam *avil*) that she has begged from other brāhmaṇas, tied in a scrap of cloth. Kṛṣṇa receives him as an equal, washes his feet, and — though Sudāmā is too abashed to ask — silently grants him untold wealth, all in return for the single fistful of rice He eats with relish. The episode is the locus classicus for the teaching that **the Lord values the love of the gift, not its value** — Kṛṣṇa speaks the Gītā's own verse *patraṃ puṣpaṃ phalaṃ toyam* (Gītā 9.26 = Bhāgavata 10.81.4) — and that He repays the devotee many times over without being asked. Like [Ajāmila](ajamila.md), it dramatises **bhakti's sufficiency**; unlike it, the saving grace flows not from the Name but from **sakhya-bhakti** (friendship as a mode of devotion).

---

## I. The Frame — Two Schoolmates of Sāndīpani

- **The poor brāhmaṇa** — "Kṛṣṇa had a certain friend (*sakhā kaścid*), a brāhmaṇa, best of the knowers of Brahman," detached from sense-objects, serene and sense-controlled; a householder (*gṛhāśramī*) living on whatever came to him unsought (*yadṛcchayopapannena*), himself ragged (*ku-caila*), with a wife likewise destitute and wasted by hunger (*kṣut-kṣāmā ca tathā-vidhā*). Kṛṣṇa later embraces him as His "dear friend" (*sakhyuḥ priyasya*). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.80.6–8, 10.80.19)
  - *On the name:* the Bhāgavata never names him: he is "a certain friend," "the brāhmaṇa," "the ragged one." The name **Kucela/Kucaila** ("badly clothed," *ku-cela*/*ku-caila*) is the Bhāgavata's own epithet turned into a name: *ku-cailasya* (10.80.7) and *ku-cailaṃ malinaṃ kṣāmam* "ragged, dirty, emaciated" (10.80.23). It became his standard name in the South. The name **Sudāmā** ("giving well, bountiful," the Monier-Williams gloss of *su-dāman*) belongs to later tradition. The one Sanskrit attestation of the name traced is the *Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa*: Kṛṣṇa "at once took away the poverty of the brāhmaṇa Sudāman" (*sadyo jahāra dāridryaṃ sudāmno brāhmaṇasya ca*) (*Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa*, Śrīkṛṣṇajanma-khaṇḍa 113.41, Wikisource text; Böhtlingk–Roth, after the *Śabdakalpadruma*, cite it as Kṛṣṇajanma-khaṇḍa 112). Monier-Williams gives the source as "BrahmaP.", evidently the same reference. No such passage appears in the GRETIL *Brahma Purāṇa* (chs. 1–246). Rūpa Gosvāmī's *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* (1541) instead calls the Dvārakā brāhmaṇa friend **Śrīdāmā** (*śrīdāma-bhūsura*, 3.3.11; §VII). The name Sudāmā is standard in the vernacular retellings (Narsinh Mehta, Narottamdās, Premānand; §VI). [late text] [scholarly]
  - *Not to be confused with:* (1) **Sudāmā the garland-maker** of Mathurā, who garlands Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma on their first walk through the city, before the bow-breaking (*sudāmno … mālā-kārasya*, *Bhāgavata* 10.41.43–52, named at vv. 43 and 49; unnamed in the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* parallel, 5.19.17–29); (2) **Sudāmā the Goloka cowherd**, cursed by Rādhā to be reborn as the asura Śaṅkhacūḍa (*Śiva Purāṇa* 2.5.27, as indexed by wisdomlib). The *Brahmavaivarta* knows the other side of the quarrel: Rādhā says she is now born Vṛṣabhānu's daughter "by Sudāmā's curse" (*ahaṃ sudāma-śāpena vṛṣabhāna-sutādhunā*, Śrīkṛṣṇajanma-khaṇḍa 111.59) [late text].
- **The shared gurukula** — In the Dvārakā reunion Kṛṣṇa recalls the two of them "living in the guru's house" (*guru-kule vāsaṃ … nau*, 10.80.31). The guru is named **Sāndīpani** (10.80.39). Kṛṣṇa's and Balarāma's schooling with "Sāndīpani of the Kāśya line, dwelling in Avantipura" is told at 10.45.31ff; the Bhāgavata does not say that Sudāmā studied there alongside Balarāma. The identification of Avantī with Ujjain is geographical tradition. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.80.27–43; 10.45.31)
- **The wife's urging** — Sudāmā's chaste (*pati-vratā*), hunger-pale wife comes to him trembling: your friend is the Lord of Śrī, *kindly to brāhmaṇas* (*brahmaṇya*) and a refuge (*śaraṇya*); he now lives in Dvāravatī as lord of the Bhojas, Vṛṣṇis and Andhakas. He gives even Himself to those who remember His feet, so surely He will give wealth to a suffering householder. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.80.8–11)
  - *Note:* the Bhāgavata leaves the wife unnamed (*pati-vratā*, *bhāryā*). Popular retellings call her **Suśīlā**. [folk]
- **Reluctance and consent** — Entreated again and again, Sudāmā resolves to go, reasoning that "the sight of Uttamaśloka (Kṛṣṇa) is itself the highest gain" (*ayaṃ hi paramo lābha uttamaḥśloka-darśanam*). He asks his wife whether there is anything in the house to take as a gift (*upāyana*). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.80.12–13)

## II. The Gift — A Cloth of Flattened Rice

- **The four fistfuls of pṛthuka** — Having nothing, the wife **begs four fistfuls of flattened rice (pṛthuka-taṇḍula) from brāhmaṇas** (*yācitvā caturo muṣṭīn viprān*; the BBT translation's "from neighboring brāhmaṇas" is a translator's gloss), ties them in a scrap of cloth, and gives the bundle to her husband as his gift. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.80.14)
  - *Note (retellings):* many retellings say simply "a handful/pinch" of poha. The smallness is the point, not the count. [folk]
- **The journey to Dvārakā** — Sudāmā sets out for Dvārakā thinking only "how shall I get to see Kṛṣṇa?" He passes three guard-posts (*gulma*) and three courtyards, goes among the houses of the Andhakas and Vṛṣṇis, and enters one of the palaces of Hari's sixteen thousand queens, "as if entering the bliss of Brahman." (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.80.15–17)

## III. The Welcome — The Lord Honours the Beggar

- **Kṛṣṇa rises and embraces him** — Seated on His **beloved consort's couch** (*priyā-paryaṅkam*), Acyuta sees his friend from afar, springs up, goes forward and embraces him with both arms. At the touch of His dear friend the lotus-eyed Lord sheds tears of joy. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.80.18–19)
  - *Gloss:* the text does not name the queen, and the BBT translation here says only "His consort's bed." The BBT names her **Rukmiṇī** at 10.81.10, and popular retellings seat Kṛṣṇa with Rukmiṇī, the consort identified with Śrī (cf. *paryaṅka-sthāṃ śriyaṃ hitvā*, "leaving Śrī on the couch," 10.80.26). [late text]
- **The washing of the feet** — Kṛṣṇa seats him on the couch and **Himself washes Sudāmā's feet**, then takes the wash-water on His own head. He anoints him with divine sandal, aloe and kuṅkuma, worships him with incense and rows of lamps, offers betel (*tāmbūla*) and a cow, and bids him welcome. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.80.20–22)
- **The goddess fans him** — The **devī** herself serves the ragged, dirty, emaciated brāhmaṇa, "his veins standing out," fanning him with a yak-tail whisk. The BBT translation renders *devī* "the goddess of fortune," commonly identified as Rukmiṇī. Sudāmā recalls it on the road home as "fanned by the queen" (*mahiṣyā vījitaḥ*). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.80.23; 10.81.17)
- **The palace women marvel** — The women of the inner palace are astonished: what merit has this unwashed mendicant (*avadhūta*), poor and despised in the world, earned, that the Abode of Śrī left Śrī on her couch to embrace him like an elder brother? (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.80.24–26)
- **Reminiscence of the gurukula** — Hand in hand, the two recall their student days (10.80.27). Kṛṣṇa asks whether his friend married a fitting wife after taking leave of the guru (28). He praises the guru as the giver of the second birth and service to the guru as what pleases Him most (31–34). Then He recalls the time the guru's wife sent "us" for firewood. In the great forest a fierce storm of wind and rain broke out, the sun set, and the boys wandered all night hand in hand, lost and buffeted (35–38). At sunrise **Sāndīpani** searched them out, praised their devotion and blessed them: "may your Vedic learning never grow stale, here and hereafter" (*chandāṃsy ayāta-yāmāni bhavantv iha paratra ca*) (39–42). Sudāmā replies that for one whose body is the Veda, living with a guru was mere play-acting (44–45). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.80.27–45)
  - *Variant (popular kathā):* the guru's wife gives Sudāmā food (usually roasted chickpeas, *canā*) for both boys. In the forest he eats it secretly while Kṛṣṇa sleeps, and when Kṛṣṇa hears him chewing he says his teeth are chattering with cold. This theft is told as the cause of his later poverty. The Bhāgavata's storm episode (10.80.35–43) has no food in it and gives no cause for his poverty. (Wikipedia "Sudama," which wrongly ascribes the cause to the Bhāgavata) [folk] [locus not verified]

## IV. The Pinch of Rice — Bhakti Over Wealth

- **"What have you brought me?"** — Smiling, the knower of all hearts asks what gift (*upāyana*) His friend has brought from home: even an atom offered with love is great to Him, while abundance offered without devotion does not please Him. He then speaks the Gītā's own verse: "a leaf, a flower, a fruit, water — whoever offers Me with devotion, that I accept" (*patraṃ puṣpaṃ phalaṃ toyam*). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.81.1–4; 10.81.4 = *Bhagavad-gītā* 9.26)
- **The hidden gift snatched** — Ashamed before the Lord of Śrī, Sudāmā keeps his face lowered and does not offer the handful of rice (10.81.5). Kṛṣṇa, seeing every heart, knows why he has come. "He never worshipped Me out of desire for wealth. He has come to please his chaste wife. I will give him riches hard even for the gods to obtain" (6–7). So He Himself **snatches the rag-tied rice from Sudāmā's garment** (*vasanāc cīra-baddhān … svayaṃ jahāra*), crying "What is this?" (8). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.81.5–8)
- **He eats a fistful** — "Friend, you have brought Me the greatest delight. These grains of flattened rice satisfy Me — and with Me the whole universe" (*tarpayanty aṅga māṃ viśvam*). He eats one fistful. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.81.9–10)
- **The second handful stayed** — As He takes up a second fistful, **Śrī** (*śrīḥ … tat-parā*, devoted to Him) catches the Supreme Lord's hand: "This much is enough, Soul of the universe, to bring a man every prosperity in this world and the next, as the cause of Your pleasure." (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.81.10–11)
  - *Gloss:* the text says *śrī*. The BBT synonyms ("the goddess of fortune (Rukmiṇī-devī)") and translation ("the devoted goddess Rukmiṇī") identify her as Rukmiṇī. [late text]
  - *Commentarial reasons (as reported in the BBT purport to 10.81.10):* **Śrīdhara Svāmī** has her mean that this much grace already assures the man vast riches, the mere play of her glance, but that one more handful would force her to give herself over to the brāhmaṇa. **Viśvanātha Cakravartī** says that if He ate it all, nothing would be left for her, her co-wives and servants, and that the hard rice would upset the Lord's tender stomach. [late text]
  - *Note (popular preaching):* some retellings split the reward: the first fistful gives wealth in *this* life, while a second, had it been eaten, would have given away abundance or liberation *beyond*, so Lakṣmī stopped the Lord giving away too much. The Bhāgavata itself says the single fistful already suffices for "this world and the next" (10.81.11). [folk]
  - *Variant (Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa):* in a brief summary, Sudāmā comes to Dvārakā "seeking refuge" (*śaraṇārthin*). Kṛṣṇa eats only **a grain** (*pṛthukānāṃ kaṇaṃ bhuktvā*) and at once removes his poverty. He grants him royal fortune, steady for seven generations (*rāja-lakṣmīṃ niścalāṃ sāpta-pauruṣīm*), a kingdom like Indra's Amarāvatī and wealth like Kubera's, then unwavering devotion, servitude (*dāsya*) and a place in Goloka. The summary passes over the hand-staying and the walk home. (*Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa*, Śrīkṛṣṇajanma-khaṇḍa 113.41–44) [late text]
- **The night in the palace** — The brāhmaṇa spends that night in Acyuta's palace, eats and drinks happily and feels himself in heaven. Next morning he sets out for home, honoured by Kṛṣṇa, who walks a little way with him along the road. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.81.12–13)
- **Sudāmā never asks** — He leaves **without having received wealth from Kṛṣṇa and without having asked for it** (*na tu yācitavān svayam*), embarrassed to ask but fulfilled by the sight of the Great One. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.81.14)

## V. The Return — Untold Wealth Unasked

- **The walk home, reflecting** — On the road Sudāmā marvels at the Lord's love for brāhmaṇas. Kṛṣṇa, who bears Lakṣmī on His chest, embraced the poorest of men, seated him on His beloved's couch like a brother, had him fanned by the queen and massaged his feet (10.81.15–18). He concludes that the compassionate Lord surely withheld wealth "lest this pauper, getting rich, grow wildly proud and forget Me" (*adhano 'yaṃ dhanaṃ prāpya mādyann uccair na māṃ smaret*). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.81.15–20)
- **The palace where his hut stood** — Arriving home, he finds his dwelling surrounded by mansions bright as sun, fire and moon, gardens and lotus-pools, and richly adorned men and women. The household greets him with music. His wife, radiant "like Śrī in bodily form," comes out among gold-necklaced maidservants. They enter a home like Indra's palace, with jewelled pillars, ivory beds, crystal walls and gem-lamps. Kṛṣṇa has **granted everything silently**, without a word. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.81.21–31; "without a word," *abruvāṇaḥ*, 10.81.34)
- **His reflection and prayer** — Sudāmā reasons that his sudden wealth can only come from the glance of the best of the Yadus. Like a rain-cloud, his friend has given abundantly without a word to one who came meaning to beg (*yāciṣṇave*). He magnifies a friend's trifling gift and makes little of His own great gifts (10.81.32–35; GRETIL marks v. 35 as a starred verse). He prays for **"loving friendship, comradeship, amity and servitude (*sauhṛda-sakhya-maitrī-dāsyam*) with Him, birth after birth,"** and for attachment to the company of His devotees (*tat-puruṣa-prasaṅgaḥ*). He reflects that the Lord withholds riches and kingdoms from devotees of immature understanding, seeing how the proud rich fall. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.81.32–37)
- **Wealth without attachment** — Thus resolved and wholly devoted to Janārdana, he enjoys the pleasures with his wife without greed, ready to renounce them. Having seen the Unconquerable conquered by His own servants, his bondage cut by the force of meditation on Him, he soon attains **His abode, the goal of the saints** (*tad-dhāma lebhe 'cirataḥ satāṃ gatim*). (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.81.38, 10.81.40)
- **Śuka's moral** — Śukadeva tells Parīkṣit that for Hari brāhmaṇas are the highest deity (10.81.39), and closes: one who hears of the brāhmaṇa-loving Lord's love for brāhmaṇas (*brahmaṇya-devasya … brahmaṇyatām*) gains devotion and is freed from the bondage of karma. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.81.39, 10.81.41)

## VI. Retellings & Regional Traditions

- **The South-Indian Kucela-vṛttam / Kucela-upākhyāna [regional]** — In Kerala and the Tamil-Telugu south the friend is **Kucela**, the Bhāgavata's own epithet (10.80.7, 23). The tale circulates as an independent devotional poem and as a *Kathakali*/*Harikathā* set-piece. The Malayalam **Kucelavṛttam Vañcippāṭṭu** ("Kucela's story, a boat-song") by **Rāmapurattu Vāriyar** (1703–1753), in the *nathonnata* metre, is the most famous. It was written in the reign of **Mārttāṇḍa Varma** of Travancore (1729–1758). K. R. Krishnapillai dates it c. 1745–1750, and 1756 has also been proposed, though that falls after the poet's reported death. Wikipedia tags this dating, and the claim that the king suggested the poem, as failing verification [disputed]. It made the episode a household devotional. A received story says it was composed and sung on one of the king's boat journeys: the poet set his own poverty before the king through Kucela's, and came home to find a new house built by royal order. This is reported via a Thiruvananthapuram Corporation artists' page cited on Wikipedia. (Malayalam bhakti literature) [regional] [folk]
  - *Note (emphasis):* the Kucela tradition dwells especially on the **ragged-cloth poverty** (the name itself) and on the wife's faith, foregrounding the brāhmaṇa's *daridra* (destitution) as the foil to Kṛṣṇa's grace.
- **Kucela Dinam [regional]** — In Kerala, the **first Wednesday of the Malayalam month Dhanu** (Dec–Jan) is kept as *Kucela Dinam*. At **Guruvāyūr** and other Kṛṣṇa temples devotees offer packets of beaten rice (*aval nivedyam*, with coconut, jaggery and ghee) in the hope of relief from poverty. (Kerala temple practice; Wikipedia "Kuchela dinam") [regional]
- **Narsinh Mehta's Sudāmā-carita [regional]** — The Gujarati *ādi-kavi* and Kṛṣṇa-bhakta **Narsinh (Narsi) Mehta** retold the tale in a *Sudāmā-carit* among his Bhāgavata-based narrative poems. His dates are usually given as 1414–1488, though scholars have no consensus on the exact dates [disputed]. Mehta was himself famously poor, and the hagiography reads his life in Sudāmā's light. The poem fused the episode into Gujarati Vaiṣṇava devotionalism. (Gujarati bhakti literature) [regional]
  - *Note:* the **ākhyāna** form (sung narrative) reached its height with **Premānand** (1636–1714), the most celebrated *ākhyānkār*, who also composed a *Sudāmā-caritra*. The episode is among the most-retold of all Gujarati ākhyānas. [scholarly]
- **Narottamdās's Sudāmā-carit [regional]** — The Braj-bhāṣā **Sudāmā-carit** of **Narottamdās** of Sitapur (Awadh) is the classic Hindi telling and his only surviving complete work. Hindi accounts place him in the 16th c.: born c. 1493 (VS 1550), recorded by the *Śivasiṃh-saroj* as living in VS 1602 (1545), though Grierson put his birth at VS 1610 [disputed] (Hindi Wikipedia "नरोत्तमदास"). It is a staple of Hindi school readers (e.g. the CBSE Class 8 *Vasant* reader). [regional]
- **Porbandar as Sudāmāpurī [folk]** — Saurashtra tradition makes **Porbandar** (old name *Sudāmāpurī*) Sudāmā's home, the starting point of his walk to Dvārakā. The **Sudāmā Mandir** there is dedicated to him. The Bhāgavata names no home town. [regional] [folk]
- **Hindi & pan-Indian retellings [folk]** — The tale is ubiquitous in Hindi *kathā*, school readers and devotional cinema. "Sudāmā's rice" (*Sudāmā ke taṇḍul*, "a poor man's present," Hindi dictionary sense; DDSA *Practical Hindi-English Dictionary* via wisdomlib) stands for a humble gift repaid beyond measure, and the **"Kṛṣṇa–Sudāmā friendship"** stands for love across the rich-poor divide.

## VII. Doctrinal & Thematic Significance

- **Bhakti over wealth** — The episode's core teaching: the Lord is won not by riches or ritual but by **love**. A pinch of poha offered with devotion outweighs any opulent sacrifice. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.81.3–4, 9; Gītā 9.26)
- **Divine reciprocity (without asking)** — Kṛṣṇa gives "like a rain-cloud" without a word (*Bhāgavata* 10.81.34), and Sudāmā has not asked (10.81.14). This exemplifies the devotee's confidence that the Lord **knows the heart's need and supplies it unbidden**: Sudāmā never begs, yet receives all. The tale is a standing proof-text for *ananya-bhakti* and *prapatti* (loving surrender). [scholarly]
- **Sakhya-bhakti** — Sudāmā's own prayer asks for *sauhṛda-sakhya-maitrī-dāsya* birth after birth (*Bhāgavata* 10.81.36), and the tale is popularly the illustration of **friendship (*sakhya*) as a devotional mode** (*rasa*): God as intimate equal and schoolmate, distinct from servitude (*dāsya*) or parental love (*vātsalya*). Rūpa Gosvāmī's *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* divides Kṛṣṇa's friends into *pura* (city) and *vraja* friends (3.3.10). The pura friends are Arjuna, Bhīmasena, Drupada's daughter and "the brāhmaṇa Śrīdāmā and others" (*śrīdāma-bhūsurādyāś ca sakhāyaḥ pura-saṃśrayāḥ*, 3.3.11), with Arjuna the best of them (3.3.13). The Vraja friends are pre-eminent among all (*sarva-vayasyeṣu pradhānatvam*, 3.3.16), and the cowherd Śrīdāmā, Sudāmā, Dāmā and Vasudāma head its *priya-sakhā* list (3.3.36). The BRS thus calls the brāhmaṇa friend in the city, evidently the Bhāgavata's, Śrīdāmā; the name "Sudāmā Vipra" found in Gauḍīya retellings is not the BRS's own. (Gauḍīya / Vaiṣṇava rasa-theology) [late text]
- **The honour of poverty** — The tale sacralises the **poor brāhmaṇa's dignity**: the name Kucela, "ragged-clad," and the palace women's scorn (*avadhūta*, *garhita*, 10.80.25) are turned into the occasion of the King of Dvārakā washing his feet, inverting worldly rank. The Bhāgavata frames it as the Lord's *brahmaṇyatā*, His love for brāhmaṇas (10.81.15, 39, 41). It is a recurring bhakti-movement theme.

## Sources

**Primary (scriptural):**
- *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (Śrīmad Bhāgavatam), **Canto 10 (Daśama Skandha), chapters 80–81**, the complete episode and its principal Sanskrit telling (no parallel found in the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*, *Harivaṃśa* or GRETIL *Brahma Purāṇa* 1–246). **10.80** covers Parīkṣit's question (1–5), the brāhmaṇa and his wife (6–13), the four fistfuls of pṛthuka (14), the journey (15–17), the welcome, foot-washing and fanning (18–23), the palace women's wonder (24–26) and the gurukula reminiscence, with the storm and Sāndīpani's blessing (27–45). **10.81** covers "what have you brought?" and the *patraṃ puṣpaṃ* verse (3–4), the snatched bundle (5–8), the single fistful and Śrī staying His hand (9–11), the night and departure (12–14), the homeward reflection (15–20), the transformed home (21–31), Sudāmā's reflection and prayer (32–37), wealth without attachment and attainment of the abode (38–40) and the phala-śruti (41). Text: GRETIL *Bhāgavatapurāṇa* (verse 10.81.35 starred). Translation and synonyms: BBT/vedabase.
- *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.45.31 (Sāndīpani of Avantipura) and 10.41.43–52 (Sudāmā the garland-maker, a different person). *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 5.19.17–29 (the same garland-maker, unnamed). The *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* and *Harivaṃśa* contain no Kucela/Sudāmā episode.
- *Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa*, Śrīkṛṣṇajanma-khaṇḍa **113.41–44** (Sudāman's poverty removed for a grain of pṛthuka; Böhtlingk–Roth's "112") and **111.59** (Rādhā and Sudāmā's curse). Text: Sanskrit Wikisource. [late text]
- Rūpa Gosvāmī, *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* 3.3.10–16, 36 (pura and vraja friends; *śrīdāma-bhūsura*). Text: GRETIL.
- *Bhagavad-gītā* 9.26, the *patraṃ puṣpaṃ phalaṃ toyam* verse, which Kṛṣṇa repeats verbatim at *Bhāgavata* 10.81.4.

**Commentary:**
- Śrīdhara Svāmī (*Bhāvārtha-dīpikā*) and Viśvanātha Cakravartī (*Sārārtha-darśinī*) on 10.81.10, as reported in the BBT purport.

**Regional retellings:**
- **Kucelavṛttam (Malayalam):** *Kucelavṛttam Vañcippāṭṭu* by **Rāmapurattu Vāriyar** (1703–1753, Travancore), the celebrated boat-song retelling.
- **Sudāmā-caritra (Gujarati):** **Narsinh Mehta** (15th c., dates disputed) and **Premānand** (1636–1714), the ākhyāna retellings central to Gujarati Vaiṣṇava devotion.
- **Sudāmā-carit (Braj/Hindi):** **Narottamdās** (16th c., Sitapur; dates disputed).
