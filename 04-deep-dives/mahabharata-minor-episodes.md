# Mahābhārata — Minor Episodes & Inset Tales

> Where this sits: an index of the dozens of *upākhyānas* (inset sub-tales) and embedded discourses woven through the epic, beyond its main Kuru-Pāṇḍava arc. Parent: [04-deep-dives](./) · Epic spine: [the 18-parva walkthrough](mahabharata/README.md) · Cosmic slot: end of the [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/), narrated forward into the [Kali Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/).

The Mahābhārata is a *frame-tale within a frame-tale* (Ugraśravas reciting Vaiśampāyana's recital of Vyāsa's composition), and into that frame it embeds scores of **upākhyānas** — self-contained legends told by one character to console, instruct, or warn another. Many are independently famous and have their own files in this section; this page is the **master roster**, giving each a one-to-two-line identity and its **source parva / sub-parva**, so the whole inset corpus can be seen at once. The roster below follows the **Critical Edition (BORI)** parva-division. Where a tale is treated in full elsewhere in this knowledge base it is cross-linked. The list of insets is **large but finite**; the count of "upākhyānas" varies by recension and by how one bounds a "tale" — see the note under Sources. [Variant counts flagged inline.]

## I. Ādi Parva insets (Book 1 — "The Beginning")

- **The story of Uttaṅka (Pauṣya sub-parva)** - The disciple Uttaṅka's quest for his guru's wife's earrings, the duel with Takṣaka the Nāga, the descent into the serpent-realm, and his urging Janamejaya to the snake-sacrifice — the narrative hinge that *triggers* the whole epic's recitation. → full file: [uttanka.md](uttanka.md). (Ādi Parva, Pauṣya Parva)
- **Pauloma & the Bhṛgus** - Origin of the Bhṛgu line: the Rākṣasa who abducts the pregnant Puloma, the premature birth of **Cyavana** (born "fallen," *cyu-*) whose radiance burns the Rākṣasa to ash; frame for Ruru, Pramadvarā and the snake-curse. (Ādi Parva, Pauloma Parva)
- **Āstika & the Snake-Sacrifice (Sarpa-satra)** - The great frame-myth: King **Janamejaya** holds a serpent-sacrifice to avenge his father Parikṣit, killed by **Takṣaka**; thousands of Nāgas burn until the young brahmin **Āstika** (son of sage Jaratkāru and the Nāga-princess Jaratkāru/Manasā's lineage) wins a boon and halts the rite — saving the serpents, and providing the occasion at which the epic is first recited. (Ādi Parva, Āstika Parva) [the frame that contains the entire Mahābhārata]
- **The Churning of the Ocean (Amṛta-manthana)** - Told within the Āstika frame to explain the gods' immortality and Garuḍa's quest. → full file: [samudra-manthana.md](samudra-manthana.md). (Ādi Parva, Āstika Parva)
- **The birth of Garuḍa & the theft of the Amṛta** - The rivalry of the sisters **Kadrū** (mother of serpents) and **Vinatā** (mother of Aruṇa and Garuḍa); Vinatā's enslavement over the colour of Uccaiḥśravas's tail; the **Vālakhilya** sages' boon producing the mighty Garuḍa; Garuḍa's raid on heaven to **steal the amṛta** to ransom his mother, his pact with Viṣṇu (becoming his mount) and with Indra (Nāgas as his food). (Ādi Parva, Āstika Parva)
- **Yayāti** - The Lunar-dynasty king cursed with premature old age who exchanges it for his son Pūru's youth, then renounces. → full file: [yayati.md](yayati.md). (Ādi Parva, Sambhava Parva 71–93)
- **Kaca & Devayānī / the Mṛtasañjīvanī** - Bṛhaspati's son Kaca infiltrates Śukrācārya's school to steal the death-reviving mantra; the mutual curses that doom Devayānī to a kṣatriya marriage (to Yayāti). (Ādi Parva, Sambhava Parva)
- **Tapatī, Vasiṣṭha & Aurva (the Gandharva's tales)** - The Gandharva **Citraratha**, defeated by Arjuna, narrates a cluster: **Tapatī** (Sun-daughter, ancestress of the Kurus, hence "Tāpatya"); **Vasiṣṭha vs. Viśvāmitra** and the devouring of King Kalmāṣapāda; and **Aurva**, who draws fire from his thigh to threaten the world in vengeance for the Bhārgavas. → see [vishwamitra-vasishtha.md](vishwamitra-vasishtha.md). (Ādi Parva, Caitraratha Parva)
- **Śakuntalā & Duṣyanta** - The hermitage romance, the lost signet-ring, and the birth of **Bharata** (eponym of Bhārata-varṣa) — the Kuru line's founding love-story. → full file: [shakuntala-bharata.md](shakuntala-bharata.md). (Ādi Parva, Sambhava Parva)
- **The birth of Vidura** - **Dharma/Yama**, cursed by sage Māṇḍavya (impaled in error), is reborn as the wise śūdra-born **Vidura** — framing his lifelong role as the epic's voice of conscience. (Ādi Parva, Sambhava Parva)
- **Māṇḍavya & the stake (Aṇī-Māṇḍavya)** - The ascetic wrongly impaled on a stake for a theft he did not commit, who curses Dharma — the etiology both of Vidura's birth and of the doctrine that sin before age fourteen is uncounted. (Ādi Parva, Sambhava Parva)
- **Vasiṣṭha & the hundred sons / Parāśara's sacrifice** - Vasiṣṭha's grief and dissuasion from suicide; grandson **Parāśara's** Rākṣasa-destroying sacrifice, halted by Pulastya — and the begetting of **Vyāsa** on Satyavatī. (Ādi Parva, Sambhava Parva)

## II. Sabhā & early Vana insets (Books 2–3)

- **The fall of the gambling hall** - Embedded *nīti* on dicing, the perils of *dyūta*, and Vidura's warnings — discursive insets rather than tales. (Sabhā Parva)
- **Kirmīra-vadha** - Bhīma slays the Rākṣasa **Kirmīra** (brother of Baka and friend of Hiḍimba) in the Kāmyaka forest as the exile begins. (Vana Parva, Kirmīra-vadha Parva)
- **Arjuna & the Kirāta (Kairāta)** - Arjuna's austerities win a duel with **Śiva disguised as a mountain-hunter (Kirāta)**, earning the **Pāśupatāstra**; followed by his ascent to Indra's heaven (Indralokāgamana) for celestial weapons. (Vana Parva, Kairāta & Indralokāgamana Parvas)
- **Naḷa & Damayantī (Nalopākhyāna)** - Told by sage **Bṛhadaśva** to console Yudhiṣṭhira over his own dice-loss: the king ruined by **Kali** at dice, his disfigurement, and reunion. → full file: [nala-damayanti.md](nala-damayanti.md). (Vana Parva, Nalopākhyāna Parva 52–79)

## III. The Tīrtha-yātrā tales (Book 3, Tīrtha-yātrā Parva 80–157)

> A pilgrimage-itinerary frame (narrated by **Pulastya** to Bhīṣma, and by **Lomaśa** / **Nārada** to the Pāṇḍavas) into which dozens of place-legends are set.

- **Agastya & Vātāpi / the drinking of the ocean** - The sage swallows the demon **Vātāpi**, digests **Ilvala**'s revival-trick, weds **Lopāmudrā**, and later **drinks the ocean dry** to expose the Kālakeya demons. → full file: [agastya.md](agastya.md). (Vana Parva, Tīrtha-yātrā / Agastya sub-parva)
- **Ṛśyaśṛṅga** - The horn-headed celibate ascetic lured to Aṅga to break a drought, told as a tīrtha-legend (of the Kauśikī). → full file: [rishyashringa.md](rishyashringa.md). (Vana Parva, Tīrtha-yātrā Parva)
- **Bhagīratha & the descent of Gaṅgā** - The king whose austerities bring the **Gaṅgā** from heaven to liberate the ashes of Sagara's 60,000 sons (burnt by **Kapila**) — etiology of the river and the Gaṅgāsāgara tīrtha. (Vana Parva, Tīrtha-yātrā Parva)
- **Sagara & the Aśvamedha horse** - The lost sacrificial horse, the digging of the ocean-bed by Sagara's sons, and their incineration by Kapila — prelude to Bhagīratha. (Vana Parva, Tīrtha-yātrā Parva)
- **Ṛṣṭiṣeṇa / Ṛṣabha-tīrtha & Śaibya (Uśīnara)** - The king **Uśīnara/Śibi** tested by Indra (hawk) and Agni (dove), offering his own flesh to ransom a dove. → full file: [shibi.md](shibi.md). (Vana Parva, Tīrtha-yātrā Parva)
- **Cyavana & Sukanyā** - The aged Bhārgava restored to youth by the Aśvins, who in return gain a share of the soma — a tīrtha-legend of the Narmadā region. → full file: [cyavana-sukanya.md](cyavana-sukanya.md). (Vana Parva, Tīrtha-yātrā Parva)
- **Maṅkaṇaka** - The sage from whose Kuśa-grass-cut wound flows **vegetable sap** instead of blood; in elation he dances, and the whole world dances with him, until **Śiva** humbles his pride by showing ash from his own thumb — etiology of the **Sapta-Sārasvata** tīrtha. (Vana Parva, Tīrtha-yātrā Parva; the dance-legend recurs in Śalya Parva ~37–38)
- **Dadhīci & the Sārasvata tīrthas** - The sage who gives his bones for Indra's **Vajra**, located among the Sarasvatī's holy fords. → full file: [dadhichi.md](dadhichi.md). (Vana Parva, Tīrtha-yātrā Parva)
- **Indra, Vṛtra & the Sarasvatī-vanishing** - Inset etiologies of why the river runs underground, and of Indra's brahminicide-guilt after slaying Vṛtra. (Vana Parva, Tīrtha-yātrā Parva)
- **The story of Kārtavīrya / Mahodaya / Vainya** and other minor royal tīrtha-legends - Place-by-place anecdotes attached to fords visited. [roster open-ended — the parva chains dozens of brief place-legends] (Vana Parva, Tīrtha-yātrā Parva)

## IV. Markaṇḍeya-samāsyā insets (Book 3, Mārkaṇḍeya-Samāsyā Parva 182–231)

> The deathless sage **Mārkaṇḍeya**, who survives the cosmic dissolution, answers Yudhiṣṭhira's questions — a vast frame for cosmological and devotional inset-tales.

- **Mārkaṇḍeya & the cosmic deluge (Pralaya)** - The sage adrift on the dissolution-ocean enters and exits the mouth of the **child Viṣṇu on the banyan leaf** — the signature vision of cyclic time. → full file: [markandeya.md](markandeya.md). (Vana Parva, Mārkaṇḍeya-Samāsyā Parva)
- **The Yuga-cycle & Kalki prophecy** - Mārkaṇḍeya's description of the four yugas' decline and the future advent of **Kalki** — one of the epic's principal eschatological passages. (Vana Parva, Mārkaṇḍeya-Samāsyā Parva)
- **Mudgala & the rejection of heaven** - The gleaner-sage **Mudgala**, so detached that when a messenger offers him bodily ascent to **svarga**, he refuses on learning heaven too is impermanent, choosing instead the imperishable **Brahman** (mokṣa) — a key renunciation parable. (Vana Parva, Mārkaṇḍeya-Samāsyā Parva)
- **The dialogue of the Brāhmaṇa, the Fowler & the Pativratā (Dharma-vyādha)** - A proud ascetic is humbled first by a devoted wife, then by a **righteous butcher (vyādha)** of Mithilā who teaches dharma from his lowly trade — the celebrated **Vyādha-gītā**. (Vana Parva, Mārkaṇḍeya-Samāsyā Parva)
- **The greatness of the Brāhmaṇa & Kauśika the crane-killer** - Frame for the Dharma-vyādha: the brahmin who incinerates a crane with an angry glance, then learns true power lies in duty, not austerity. (Vana Parva, Mārkaṇḍeya-Samāsyā Parva)
- **The Skanda / Kārttikeya birth-cycle** - Mārkaṇḍeya narrates the birth of **Skanda** from Agni and the Kṛttikās, his generalship of the divine host, and the slaying of **Tāraka**. (Vana Parva, Mārkaṇḍeya-Samāsyā Parva)
- **The greatness of Aṅgirasa / the origins of Agni** - Inset genealogies of the fire-gods. (Vana Parva, Mārkaṇḍeya-Samāsyā Parva)

## V. Late-Vana insets (Book 3, after Markaṇḍeya)

- **Draupadī–Satyabhāmā dialogue (Draupadī-Satyabhāmā-saṃvāda)** - Kṛṣṇa's queen **Satyabhāmā** asks Draupadī the secret of her mastery over five husbands; Draupadī answers with a manual of the devoted-wife's conduct — a much-cited (and much-contested) discourse on *strīdharma*. (Vana Parva, Draupadī-Satyabhāmā-saṃvāda Parva 232–234) [studied as a window on the text's gender-norms — scholarly]
- **Rāma-upākhyāna (the inset Rāmāyaṇa)** - Sage **Mārkaṇḍeya** tells Yudhiṣṭhira the **whole story of Rāma** — abduction of Sītā, the bridge, Rāvaṇa's fall — to console him that even the greatest kings suffer separation; the Mahābhārata's compressed parallel to the [Rāmāyaṇa](ramayana/README.md). (Vana Parva, Rāma-upākhyāna Parva 272–291) [relation of the two epics' Rāma-versions = scholarly debate]
- **Sāvitrī & Satyavān (Pativratā-māhātmya)** - Told by Mārkaṇḍeya right after the Rāma-tale: the wife who out-argues **Yama** to win back her husband's life. → full file: [savitri-satyavan.md](savitri-satyavan.md). (Vana Parva, Pativratā-māhātmya Parva 292–298)
- **The theft of Karṇa's earrings (Kuṇḍala-aharaṇa)** - Indra, disguised, begs away Karṇa's inborn **kavaca-kuṇḍala** (armour-and-earrings) to weaken him before the war, granting in exchange the one-use **Vasava-śakti**. → see [karna.md](karna.md). (Vana Parva, Kuṇḍala-aharaṇa Parva 299–309)
- **The Ājagara (python) dialogue** - **Nahuṣa**, fallen from heaven and cursed into a python, seizes Bhīma; **Yudhiṣṭhira** frees him by answering the serpent's questions on **dharma, caste and the soul**. → see [nahusha.md](nahusha.md). (Vana Parva, Ājagara Parva 176–181)
- **The Yakṣa-praśna (Dharma's lake / Araṇeya Parva)** - At the haunted lake the **Yakṣa (Dharma in disguise)** kills four Pāṇḍavas for drinking without answering, then revives them when **Yudhiṣṭhira** solves his 100-odd riddles on ethics — the climactic wisdom-test that ends the exile. (Vana Parva, Araṇeya / Yakṣa-praśna Parva 310–315)

## VI. Udyoga & war-book insets (Books 5–9)

- **Vidura-nīti** - On the eve of war, the sleepless Dhṛtarāṣṭra is counselled by **Vidura** with a compact treatise on statecraft, the marks of the wise and the foolish, and the duties of a king — the epic's foremost *nīti*-inset. (Udyoga Parva, Prajāgara Parva 33–40)
- **Sanat-sujātīya** - A near-Upaniṣadic dialogue in which the sage **Sanatsujāta** teaches Dhṛtarāṣṭra that death is mere ignorance, and that **brahma-vidyā** conquers it — sometimes printed as a companion to the [Bhagavad-gītā](bhagavad-gita.md). (Udyoga Parva, Sanatsujāta Parva 41–46)
- **The embassy of Mātali / the daughter-in-law search (Mātali-upākhyāna)** - Indra's charioteer **Mātali** tours the Nāga and netherworlds seeking a husband for his daughter — a cosmographical inset within Nārada's consolation of Mātali. (Udyoga Parva, Bhagavadyāna Parva)
- **Gālava & the Mādhavī tale (Gālava-carita)** - Sage **Gālava**, demanding 800 white-eared horses as guru-dakṣiṇā from Viśvāmitra, "lends out" the princess **Mādhavī**, who bears a king to each of four lords while regaining her virginity each time — told by Nārada to curb Duryodhana's pride. (Udyoga Parva, Bhagavadyāna Parva) [a noted feminist-critical passage — scholarly]
- **Vāsavī / Ambā–Śikhaṇḍin** - **Ambā**, scorned by Bhīṣma, immolates herself to be reborn as **Śikhaṇḍin**, the instrument of his fall — narrated by Bhīṣma himself. (Udyoga Parva, Ambopākhyāna Parva)
- **The story of Daṃbhodbhava / the Nara-Nārāyaṇa tale** - The arrogant king crushed by the twin sages **Nara and Nārāyaṇa** (= Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa), told to warn Duryodhana. (Udyoga Parva)
- **The Bhagavad-gītā** - The supreme inset: Kṛṣṇa's battlefield teaching to Arjuna. → full file: [bhagavad-gita.md](bhagavad-gita.md). (Bhīṣma Parva, Bhagavad-gītā Parva 23–40)
- **Maṅkaṇaka (dance-legend) & the Sapta-Sārasvata tīrthas** - Balarāma's pilgrimage along the Sarasvatī during the war reprises a chain of ford-legends, including Maṅkaṇaka's dance and the meeting of seven Sarasvatīs. (Śalya Parva, ~34–54)

## VII. Śānti & Anuśāsana insets (Books 12–13)

> Bhīṣma on the bed of arrows delivers the epic's largest reservoir of inset-fables, especially in the **Āpad-dharma** (law in distress) section.

- **The dialogue of the Vulture & the Jackal (Gṛdhra-gomāyu-saṃvāda)** - At a cremation-ground a **vulture** urges grieving kin to abandon their dead child (Time is irresistible, no one returns), while a **jackal** pleads them to stay (love forbids abandonment) — the two carrion-birds pulling the family back and forth from dusk to dawn, until **Śiva** revives the boy: a meditation on grief, Time and false consolation. (Śānti Parva, Āpad-dharma 153–154; sometimes cited near Anuśāsana) [parva-placement varies by recension]
- **The dialogue of the cat & the mouse (Mārjāra-mūṣika)** - The mouse Palita and cat Lomaśa, trapped together, strike a survival-pact — a classic fable on the politics of necessity and the limits of trust. (Śānti Parva, Āpad-dharma)
- **The fowler, the pigeons & the self-sacrificing host (Kapota / Kapotalubdhaka)** - A male pigeon immolates himself in his own fire to feast a hunter who had snared his mate — the supreme tale of *atithi-dharma* (guest-hospitality). (Śānti Parva, Āpad-dharma)
- **The ungrateful Brahmin Gautama & Rājadharman the crane** - The wandering Gautama who kills and eats his benefactor, the crane-king **Rājadharman**, and is destroyed — a fable on ingratitude (*kṛtaghnatā*). (Śānti Parva, Āpad-dharma)
- **Śambūka / the wise jackal-minister & the lion** - Court-fables on counsel and faction among beasts, mirroring royal politics. (Śānti Parva, Āpad-dharma) [animal-fable cluster, several brief tales]
- **The dialogue of the Ocean & the Rivers** - The sea explains it spares the slender reed but breaks the rigid tree — a fable on flexibility (told to illustrate the strong-yet-yielding minister). (Śānti Parva, Āpad-dharma)
- **Janaka & Sulabhā** - The mendicant woman **Sulabhā** enters King Janaka's mind to expose his incomplete liberation — a celebrated philosophical debate on whether a householder-king can be truly free. (Śānti Parva, Mokṣa-dharma) [much-discussed — scholarly]
- **The colloquy of the Brāhmaṇa & the Hunter; Piṅgalā; the bee** - A chain of **Mokṣa-dharma** parables on detachment (the courtesan Piṅgalā's awakening; learning non-attachment from the bee). (Śānti Parva, Mokṣa-dharma)
- **Nahuṣa & Agastya (the fall of Nahuṣa)** - Bhīṣma re-tells how the over-proud Nahuṣa, made temporary Indra, is kicked from heaven by **Agastya** into serpenthood — paralleling the Vana-Parva python episode. → see [nahusha.md](nahusha.md). (Anuśāsana / Śānti Parva)
- **Uñchavṛtti / the gleaner-brahmin & the measure of barley** - A poor gleaner family's gift of their last meal outweighs a king's vast sacrifice (the half-golden mongoose at Yudhiṣṭhira's Aśvamedha) — *dāna* over pomp. (Anuśāsana 13.93 / Āśvamedhika frame) [the mongoose-tale is told in Aśvamedhika Parva]
- **The greatness of the cow, the gift, and the discourse of Umā-Maheśvara** - Long catechetical insets on *dāna*, *śrāddha* and the dialogue of Śiva and Pārvatī on women's dharma. (Anuśāsana Parva)

## VIII. Āśvamedhika & later insets (Books 14–18)

- **The Anu-gītā** - After the war Arjuna asks Kṛṣṇa to repeat the Gītā; Kṛṣṇa instead gives a different, more austere teaching (the "after-song"), framed by inset tales of a **Brāhmaṇa-disciple** and the **Siddha**. (Āśvamedhika Parva, Anugītā Parva) → companion to [bhagavad-gita.md](bhagavad-gita.md).
- **The mongoose at the horse-sacrifice (Nakula-upākhyāna)** - A half-golden mongoose declares Yudhiṣṭhira's imperial Aśvamedha worth less than a starving family's gift of barley-flour — the epic's sharpest critique of grand ritual. (Āśvamedhika Parva 90)
- **Uttaṅka & the rain-cloud / the desert mirage** - The sage Uttaṅka, denied water in the desert (Kṛṣṇa testing him with amṛta disguised as a Caṇḍāla's water), is consoled with the "Uttaṅka-clouds" boon. → see [uttanka.md](uttanka.md). (Āśvamedhika Parva, Anugītā section)
- **Parikṣit revived in the womb** - Kṛṣṇa revives the still-born **Parikṣit**, struck by Aśvatthāman's Brahmāstra — closing the dynastic line that the Sarpa-satra frame will later avenge. (Āśvamedhika Parva)

## IX. How the insets relate to the frame

- **Consolation logic** - Most great upākhyānas (Naḷa, Rāma, Sāvitrī) are told *to console Yudhiṣṭhira* in exile — "others have suffered worse and endured." The frame motivates the inset.
- **Nīti / dharma logic** - The Śānti–Anuśāsana fables are told *to instruct the dying Bhīṣma's pupil-king Yudhiṣṭhira* — the animal-fables encode statecraft (*rāja-* and *āpad-dharma*).
- **Etiological logic** - The Tīrtha-yātrā and Markaṇḍeya tales explain *why a place is holy* or *how the cosmos cycles* — geography and time as narrative.
- **Frame-completion logic** - The Āstika / Sarpa-satra and Garuḍa cycles are not asides but the **outermost frame** in which the entire epic is recited.

> **Note on counts & boundaries:** scholars (e.g. the BORI Critical Edition apparatus and studies such as Hiltebeitel's on the *upākhyānas*) count roughly **67 named upākhyānas** in the Critical Edition, but the figure varies because (a) recensions (Northern/Southern/Vulgate) include or omit tales, and (b) "tale" vs. "discourse" is a soft boundary — many Śānti/Anuśāsana fables blur into *nīti*-treatises. The roster above is therefore **representative of the major and best-attested insets, not an exhaustive verse-by-verse count**, and several clusters (the Tīrtha-yātrā place-legends, the Āpad-dharma animal-fables) are flagged as open-ended above.
