# Prahlāda & Narasiṃha — the Man-Lion's Descent

> The fourth descent of the Daśāvatāra and the climax of the Satya-yuga demon-cycle: a boon-armoured tyrant, his unkillable boy-devotee son, and the **Man-Lion** who bursts from a pillar at twilight to thread every loophole of an "unbreakable" boon.
> Sits in [Satya-yuga of Mahāyuga 28](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/01-satya-yuga.md); the second discharge of the [Jaya–Vijaya three-birth curse](jaya-vijaya.md) and a key entry in the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
> Up one level: [Deep Dives README](README.md). · Sibling cycle: [Gajendra Mokṣa](gajendra-moksha.md) · index entry in the [Bhāgavata Tales Catalog](bhagavata-tales-catalog.md).

The **Prahlāda–Narasiṃha** cycle is told in the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Skandha 7, chapters 1–10** (SB 7.1–7.10), narrated by **Nārada to Yudhiṣṭhira**. It is the longest sustained avatāra-narrative in the Purāṇa's early skandhas and the doctrinal home of *dveṣa-bhakti* (liberation through total hatred of God) on the demon side and pure *bhakti* on the boy's. The episode resolves the first of the three demonic births of Viṣṇu's gatekeepers: **Hiraṇyakaśipu** is the elder of the two asura brothers (his sibling Hiraṇyākṣa already slain by Varāha), and his death by **Narasiṃha** is the fourth of the ten classical avatāras.

---

## The curse-frame and the setup (SB 7.1–7.2)

- **Jaya–Vijaya, second of three births** — Nārada explains that Hiraṇyakaśipu and Hiraṇyākṣa are Viṣṇu's Vaikuṇṭha doorkeepers **Jaya and Vijaya**, cursed by the Sanaka-kumāras to three demonic incarnations; this is birth #1 in the *Bhāgavata*'s curse-arithmetic, the asuras of Satya-yuga. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.1*) See [Jaya–Vijaya](jaya-vijaya.md).
- **Sons of Diti and Kaśyapa** — Hiraṇyakaśipu ("clad in gold / golden-robed") and Hiraṇyākṣa are the *daitya* sons of **Diti** by the sage **Kaśyapa**, born of her wrath at the twilight conception that the gods feared (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.2*; lineage echoed in *Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.17*).
- **Grief and vow of vengeance** — When **Hiraṇyākṣa** is killed by the boar **Varāha** for dragging the Earth into the cosmic ocean, Hiraṇyakaśipu swears to destroy Viṣṇu and all his devotees, and consoles Diti and his nephews with a discourse on the soul's immortality (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.2*).

---

## The boon of near-invulnerability (SB 7.3)

- **The terrible tapas at Mandara** — Hiraṇyakaśipu performs austerity so severe that ants eat his body to a skeleton and his heat scorches the three worlds, forcing **Brahmā** to come and grant a boon (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.3*).
- **The cleverly-worded boon** — He asks not for immortality (which Brahmā cannot give) but for a death hedged by impossibilities: that he die **not by any created being**, **not by deva, asura, or living creature**; **neither inside nor outside** any dwelling; **neither by day nor by night**; **neither on earth nor in the sky**; **not by any weapon (astra) or projectile (śastra)**; **not by man nor beast**; and that no rival equal him in might (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.3.35–38*).
  - *Variant (exact clause-list):* retellings enumerate the loopholes slightly differently — some add "by no animate or inanimate thing," some omit the deva/asura clause; the canonical core is the day/night, inside/outside, earth/sky, man/beast, weapon-less set that Narasiṃha later satisfies one by one. [retelling variant]
- **Conquest of the three worlds** — Armoured by the boon, Hiraṇyakaśipu seizes the realms of the devas, usurps the offerings and stations of Indra and the guardians, and rules heaven, earth, and the underworld as a universal tyrant (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.4*; *Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.17*).

---

## Prahlāda, the boy-devotee (SB 7.4–7.5)

- **A devotee born in the demon house** — His son **Prahlāda**, even in the womb (instructed by **Nārada** while Hiraṇyakaśipu was away at tapas, *Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.7*), is a perfected Viṣṇu-devotee — calm, friendly to all, seeing the Lord in everything (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.4*).
- **The schooling that fails** — Sent to the *guru*s **Ṣaṇḍa and Amarka** (sons of Śukra, the asura-preceptor) to learn statecraft and demon-policy, Prahlāda quietly rejects their teaching and instead preaches *bhakti* to his classmates (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.5*).
- **"Where is your God?" / "Everywhere"** — Examined by his enraged father on what he has learned, Prahlāda answers that the highest knowledge is devotion to **Hari**; when challenged that Viṣṇu is not present, he declares the Lord is in all things, everywhere — the exchange that seals his father's murderous resolve (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.5, 7.8*).
- **The nine limbs of devotion** — Prahlāda's reply to his father expounds **navadhā bhakti** — the ninefold path of *śravaṇa, kīrtana, smaraṇa, pāda-sevana, arcana, vandana, dāsya, sakhya, ātma-nivedana* — making this passage a foundational *bhakti* charter (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.5.23–24*).

## The failed killings (SB 7.5)

- **Every execution fails** — Hiraṇyakaśipu orders the child killed by every means and Viṣṇu silently shields him each time (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.5*):
  - **Trampled** by elephants — the tusks shatter on his body.
  - **Bitten** by venomous serpents — their fangs break, their venom fails.
  - **Hurled from a cliff / mountain-top** — he lands unhurt.
  - **Burned** in fire and **poisoned** — neither touches him.
  - **Cast into the sea** weighted with stones / boulders.
  - **Attacked with weapons**, starved, exposed to the elements, struck by the *kṛtyā* sorceries of the demon-priests.
- **Holikā and the fire** — In the widely-told fire-ordeal, the demoness **Holikā** (Hiraṇyakaśipu's sister), boon-proofed against fire, sits in a pyre holding Prahlāda; the protection transfers to the devotee and **Holikā burns** while Prahlāda survives — the etiology of the festival **Holī / Holikā Dahana**. (*Purāṇic and festival tradition; the Holikā detail is elaborated in later Purāṇas and folk sources more than in SB 7.5 itself.*) [regional] [late text]
- **The Lord within** — Each rescue is read as Viṣṇu acting from within the boy and the elements themselves, since the boon barred external killers — foreshadowing the form of the deliverer to come (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.5*).

## The pillar and the Man-Lion (SB 7.8)

- **The challenge at the pillar** — His patience exhausted, Hiraṇyakaśipu demands to know where the all-pervading Viṣṇu is; pointing to a **pillar (stambha)** of the assembly hall, he asks "Is he in this column too?" Prahlāda answers yes. The demon strikes the pillar with his mace/fist to disprove him (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.8.12–17*).
- **Narasiṃha bursts forth** — From the shattered pillar erupts **Narasiṃha** — a form **neither wholly man nor wholly beast**: a lion's head and claws on a man's torso, of terrifying radiance, roaring so that the cosmos trembles. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.8.18–22*; the man-lion form noted in *Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.20* and central to the *Narasiṃha Purāṇa*.)
- **The threshold killing — every loophole closed** — The avatāra seizes Hiraṇyakaśipu, drags him to the **doorway (the threshold, dehalī)** at **dusk (saṃdhyā / twilight)**, sets him **across his thighs/lap**, and disembowels him with his **claws (nakha)** (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.8.23–29*). Each clause of the boon is voided:
  - **Not deva/asura/man/beast** → a man-lion, no created category.
  - **Neither day nor night** → at twilight, between the two.
  - **Neither inside nor outside** → on the threshold of the doorway.
  - **Neither on earth nor in sky** → upon his own thighs/lap.
  - **By no weapon (astra/śastra)** → by bare claws.
  - **By no created being** → by the unborn Lord himself.
  - *Variant (which loopholes are enumerated):* texts and tellings stress different subsets of the boon — some foreground only the "twilight / threshold / lap / claws" tetrad; the *Bhāgavata* lap-and-claw image is the iconographic standard. [retelling variant]

---

## The pacifying of the wrath (SB 7.9–7.10)

- **The wrath unquenched** — After the kill, Narasiṃha's fury blazes on, terrifying the very gods; **Brahmā, Rudra, Indra**, the *devas* and **Lakṣmī (Śrī)** herself dare not approach to calm him (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.9.1–4*).
- **Prahlāda alone pacifies him** — At the gods' urging the child **Prahlāda** approaches fearlessly, prostrates, and offers the **Nṛsiṃha-stuti / Prahlāda-stuti** — a long hymn (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.9.8–50*) — and the Lord, melted by his devotee's love, grows gentle (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.9*).
  - *Variant (who soothes):* a popular strand has **Lakṣmī** (as the source of the later **Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha** form) approach to calm him; the Bhāgavata foregrounds **Prahlāda** as the one who succeeds, with the gods having sent him precisely because Lakṣmī could not. [regional/sectarian — basis of the Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha iconography]
- **The boon refused, then accepted** — Narasiṃha offers Prahlāda any boon; the boy, as the model devotee, asks for **nothing for himself** (refusing even liberation as a bargaining-chip), and at most requests his father's liberation and freedom from selfish desire — the scriptural template of desireless *bhakti* (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.10.4–9*).
- **Hiraṇyakaśipu liberated** — Despite his lifelong enmity, Hiraṇyakaśipu attains liberation through being slain by the Lord's own hand — the **dveṣa-bhakti** doctrine in action, discharging birth #1 of the Jaya–Vijaya curse (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.1, 7.10*). See [Jaya–Vijaya](jaya-vijaya.md).
- **Prahlāda enthroned** — Narasiṃha installs Prahlāda as the righteous king of the *daityas*; Prahlāda's line continues to his grandson **Bali**, the devotee-king of the later **Vāmana** descent — linking this cycle forward in the Daśāvatāra (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.10*; Bali at *Bhāgavata 8.15–23*). See [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).

## Place in the avatāra-sequence and the two clocks

- **Fourth of the Daśāvatāra** — Narasiṃha is the **fourth** classical avatāra (after Matsya, Kūrma, Varāha; before Vāmana), still within the **Satya-yuga** group of descents (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa*; standard *daśāvatāra* lists). See [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).
- **Scriptural clock** — Cyclically the event is placed in **Satya-yuga of the 28th Mahāyuga of the present (Vaivasvata) Manvantara** — the same age as Hiraṇyākṣa/Varāha and the demon-pair's first birth. See [Satya-yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/01-satya-yuga.md).
- **Historical clock** — Academically the narrative has **no datable historical anchor**; the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* reached its present form c. 9th–10th century CE, and the man-lion icon is attested in sculpture from the **early centuries CE** (Kuṣāṇa/Gupta and earlier terracottas), with the dedicated **Narasiṃha Purāṇa** a later compilation. [scholarly]
- **Cult and iconography** — Narasiṃha is among the most widely worshipped Viṣṇu forms in South India (Ahobilam, Yadagirigutta, Simhachalam), in fierce (*Ugra-Narasiṃha*) and benign (*Lakṣmī-Narasiṃha*, *Yoga-Narasiṃha*) modes. [regional]

## Sources

**Primary scripture**
- *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (Śrīmad Bhāgavatam), **Skandha 7, chapters 1–10** — the complete cycle: the dveṣa-bhakti frame and Jaya–Vijaya recap (7.1), Diti's sons and Hiraṇyakaśipu's vow (7.2), the tapas and the worded boon (7.3), the conquest and Prahlāda's nature (7.4), the failed killings and navadhā-bhakti (7.5), Prahlāda's teaching (7.6–7.7), the pillar and Narasiṃha's emergence and killing (7.8), the Nṛsiṃha-stuti and pacification (7.9), and Prahlāda's enthronement and boon-refusal (7.10). Narrated by Nārada to Yudhiṣṭhira.
- *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* **1.17–20** — the daitya lineage (Diti/Kaśyapa), Hiraṇyakaśipu's tyranny, Prahlāda's devotion, and the man-lion's slaying of the demon (a more compact parallel account).
- *Narasiṃha Purāṇa* — the Upapurāṇa centred on the man-lion avatāra; expands the theology, hymns, and cult of Narasiṃha.
