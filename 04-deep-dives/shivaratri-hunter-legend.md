# The Śivarātri Hunter Legend — Gurudruh / Lubdhaka and the Deer Family

> Cosmic address: a *cross-yuga vrata-charter tale* — the Purāṇas set it in no fixed yuga, but the Śiva Purāṇa's boon-strand points the hunter's rebirth into **Tretā Yuga** (as Guha, Rāma's Niṣāda friend), while the festival it charters recurs every year on Māgha/Phālguna Kṛṣṇa Caturdaśī on the [ritual calendar](../00-time-and-cosmology/hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md).
> Up one level: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Festival entry: [Festivals & Vratas — Maha Shivaratri](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md) · Deity cycle: [Śiva myth cycle](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/shiva-myth-cycle.md)

This is the **charter vrata-kathā of Mahā-Śivarātri** — the story recited to explain *why the vrata works*. A sinful hunter, treed all night above a hidden **liṅga**, unknowingly performs a perfect Śivarātri: he has fasted all day (having caught nothing), he keeps vigil all four watches (waiting for prey), and each time he shifts on his branch he showers down **bilva leaves** and drops of water onto the liṅga below. A family of talking deer pleads for reprieve, keeps its word, and returns at dawn to die; the hunter — his heart transformed by the merit he never intended to earn — spares them all, and is himself saved from **Yama**. Two major Purāṇic tellings (Śiva Purāṇa's **Gurudruh**, Skanda Purāṇa's unnamed **lubdhaka**, "the hunter") plus a Garuḍa Purāṇa parallel carry the tale; folk vrata-kathās add further frames. All versions are catalogued separately below.

---

## The charter and its texts

- **What it charters** — The tale is the *māhātmya* (glory-story) told with the Śivarātri vrata itself: it warrants every element of the observance — the day-long **fast**, the all-night **jāgaraṇa** (vigil), **worship in each of the four watches (prahara/yāma)** of the night, **bilva-leaf offering**, and **abhiṣeka** (bathing the liṅga) — by showing that even their *accidental* performance destroys a lifetime of sin. (Śiva Purāṇa, Koṭirudra-saṁhitā 38–40; Skanda Purāṇa, Māheśvara-khaṇḍa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa 33)
- **Principal sources** — [Śiva Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/shiva-purana.md), Koṭirudra-saṁhitā chapters 38–40 (vrata rules, its greatness, and the hunter Gurudruh); [Skanda Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/skanda-purana.md), Māheśvara-khaṇḍa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa chapter 33 (Śivarātri-māhātmya with the lubdhaka); a compressed parallel in the [Garuḍa Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/puranas/garuda-purana.md) (Ācāra/Pūrva-khaṇḍa, Śivarātri-vrata chapter).
- **One story, many charters** — [festivals-and-vratas.md](../05-concepts-and-dharma/festivals-and-vratas.md) records Mahā-Śivarātri's *other* charters — the **Liṅgodbhava** pillar of light (basis of the [12 Jyotirliṅgas](../06-sacred-geography/12-jyotirlingas.md)) and the **hālāhala** night of the [Samudra Manthana](samudra-manthana.md). The hunter legend is the third great charter, and the one formally recited as the vrata-kathā. Per house policy the three are catalogued, never merged.

## The Śiva Purāṇa version — Gurudruh (Koṭirudra-saṁhitā 38–40)

- **The vrata frame** — The sages ask Sūta about the rite dearest to Śiva; the Śivarātri vrata is expounded — fast on the Caturdaśī of the dark fortnight, vigil through the night, worship of the liṅga in **each of the four watches** with bathing and bilva leaves — and its greatness is then proven by the hunter's story. (Śiva Purāṇa, Koṭirudra-saṁhitā 38–39)
- **Gurudruh the sinner** — A hunter named **Gurudruh** ("injurer of elders" — the name itself brands him) lives in a forest, feeding his family by slaughtering creatures daily; the text introduces him as steeped in lifelong sin. (Śiva Purāṇa, Koṭirudra-saṁhitā 40)
- **The failed hunt = the unknowing fast** — On the day that happens to be Śivarātri he roams the whole day and catches **nothing**; hungry and desperate as night falls, he has — without intending it — kept the Śivarātri fast. (Śiva Purāṇa, Koṭirudra-saṁhitā 40)
- **The tree over the hidden liṅga** — He stations himself at a forest water-hole to ambush animals coming to drink, climbing a **bilva tree** overhanging the bank and keeping a pot of water with him; unseen beneath the tree stands a **Śiva-liṅga**. Clearing leaves for a better view, he unknowingly showers bilva leaves — and splashes water — onto the liṅga: the first watch's worship, performed by accident. (Śiva Purāṇa, Koṭirudra-saṁhitā 40)
- **First watch — the pregnant doe** — A doe comes to drink; as Gurudruh draws his bow, his movement again shakes down leaves and water onto the liṅga (first-prahara pūjā complete). The doe speaks with a human voice: she is **pregnant/has dependents**, and begs leave to go and entrust her young, **vowing to return** to be killed. Softened — the text credits the merit already silently accruing — he lets her go on her word. (Śiva Purāṇa, Koṭirudra-saṁhitā 40)
- **Second watch — the second doe** — Later in the night her sister-doe (in the text, a doe **seeking her mate**) comes to the water; the same sequence follows — drawn bow, falling bilva and water (second-prahara pūjā), a plea, a pledge to return — and again he spares her. (Śiva Purāṇa, Koṭirudra-saṁhitā 40)
- **Third watch — the stag** — The **buck**, searching for his does, arrives in the third watch; a third accidental worship falls on the liṅga; he too pleads for time to settle his family and **promises to come back**. (Śiva Purāṇa, Koṭirudra-saṁhitā 40)
  - *Variant (retellings):* popular retellings differ on the **order and number** of the deer (some give a doe with fawns first, some four separate animals across the four watches); the fourfold prahara-worship and the pledges are constant. [oral tradition]
- **Dawn — the family returns; the hunter yields** — At night's end the entire deer family — does, stag, and fawns — returns **together**, each begging to be killed first so the others may live. By now Gurudruh's sins have been burnt away by the unwitting fast, vigil, bilva-offerings, and abhiṣeka; gazing at the truthful animals he is pierced by compassion, refuses to kill, and blesses them. (Śiva Purāṇa, Koṭirudra-saṁhitā 40)
- **Śiva's grace and the Guha boon** — Śiva, pleased, grants the hunter his grace directly: his past evil is destroyed, and he receives the boon that he will be (re)born as **Guha** — the Niṣāda chief of Śṛṅgaverapura — whom **Rāma** himself will visit and befriend, granting him final liberation; see [Guha in the Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa](ramayana/2-ayodhya-kanda.md). (Śiva Purāṇa, Koṭirudra-saṁhitā 40)
- **The deer's reward** — The deer family, having kept **satya** (truth to their pledge) at the cost of their lives, is likewise freed of its animal state and attains a blessed end by Śiva's grace. (Śiva Purāṇa, Koṭirudra-saṁhitā 40)
  - *Variant (folk sky-lore):* a widespread folk coda raises the deer family to the sky as **Mṛgaśiras** (the "deer's head," in Orion) and the hunter as **Lubdhaka** — the Sanskrit name of the hunter-star **Sirius** eternally following the deer — a star-myth stitched onto the vrata-kathā. [folk] [oral tradition]

## The Skanda Purāṇa version — the Lubdhaka (Māheśvara-khaṇḍa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa 33)

- **The unnamed hunter** — The Kedāra-khaṇḍa's Śivarātri-māhātmya tells the same charter with the hunter left generic — simply the **lubdhaka** ("the greedy one," the standard word for a fowler/hunter) — a lifelong killer of creatures and great sinner. (Skanda Purāṇa, Māheśvara-khaṇḍa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa 33)
- **The same unwitting vrata** — Having caught nothing on Śivarātri day (the fast), he passes the night in a **bilva tree** above a liṅga, sleepless (the vigil), dropping bilva leaves upon it as he watches for game — the worship completed without knowledge or intent. (Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa 33)
- **Saved from Yama** — The Skanda telling drives to the afterlife: when the hunter later dies, **[Yama's](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/yama.md) messengers arrive with their nooses** to drag the sinner to the south — but **Śiva's gaṇas** bar and repel them, claiming the man whose sins Śivarātri burnt, and bear him to Śiva's world. (Skanda Purāṇa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa 33)
  - *Parallel:* structurally the Śaiva twin of the Vaiṣṇava [Ajāmila](ajamila.md) episode (Yama's dūtas turned back by Viṣṇu's pārṣadas over a technically-uttered Name) — the two stories are the classic paired proofs that even unintended devotion outweighs a ledger of sin; compare [Narakas & the afterlife](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/narakas-and-afterlife.md).
- **Duplicate by design** — House note: this file merges what circulate as two "different" stories (Gurudruh vs. Lubdhaka); they are one charter-tale in two Purāṇic recensions, with the Śiva Purāṇa elaborating the deer-dialogue and the Guha boon, and the Skanda Purāṇa elaborating the Yama rescue. (Śiva P. Koṭirudra 40; Skanda P. Kedāra-khaṇḍa 33)

## The Garuḍa Purāṇa parallel — the Niṣāda hunter

- **A compressed retelling** — The Garuḍa Purāṇa's Śivarātri-vrata chapter (Ācāra/Pūrva-khaṇḍa) proves the vrata with the same figure: a **Niṣāda** (hunter/tribal) — named **Sundarasenaka** in printed editions — who goes hunting **with his dog**, finds nothing, and passes Śivarātri night hungry and sleepless beside a liṅga under a bilva, his displaced leaves and water (in tellings, even his tears) counting as worship; his sins are annulled and he attains Śiva's realm instead of Yama's. (Garuḍa Purāṇa, Ācāra/Pūrva-khaṇḍa, Śivarātri-vrata chapter) [late text]
- **Same skeleton, thinner flesh** — The Garuḍa version generally omits the talking deer family, functioning as a bare proof-text for the vrata's power; its presence across a third Purāṇa shows the kathā had become the standard Śivarātri charter across Purāṇic strata. [scholarly]

## Folk and framing variants — catalogued, not merged

- ***Variant (Citrabhānu frame):*** a widely printed vrata-kathā frames the tale as told by King **Citrabhānu** of the Ikṣvāku line who, remembering his past life, reveals he was the hunter — named **Suswara** in this telling, of the Vārāṇasī forests — and recounts the bilva-tree night as his own biography; the frame is popularly attributed to the Mahābhārata (Bhīṣma's discourses) but is **not traceable in the critical text** — treat the attribution as unverified. [folk] [disputed]
- ***Variant (the moneylender / "Śiva Śiva"):*** the common North-Indian oral kathā adds a prologue — the hunter is **in debt**, detained by the creditor in a Śiva-maṭha on Śivarātri day, where he involuntarily hears the god's name and the vrata being observed; released at dusk on a promise of repayment, he proceeds hungry to the fateful tree. This element belongs to the oral/printed vrata-kathā tradition rather than the Purāṇic chapters. [folk] [oral tradition]
- ***Variant (which doe pleads what):*** regional tellings redistribute the pleas — pregnancy, fawns left behind, a mate to be found, aged parents — among the deer; all versions keep the point: each excuse is *true*, each pledge is *kept*. [regional] [oral tradition]

## What the story charters — the anatomy of the vrata

- **Fast (upavāsa)** — the hunter eats nothing all day, involuntarily; hence the Śivarātri fast. (Śiva P. Koṭirudra 40)
- **Vigil (jāgaraṇa) in four watches** — he stays awake the whole night, and "worships" once in each **prahara**; hence the four-watch pūjā structure of Śivarātri night. (Śiva P. Koṭirudra 38, 40)
- **Bilva and water** — leaves and drops falling on the liṅga are received as full worship; hence bilva's supremacy in Śaiva pūjā — see the [Bilvāṣṭakam](stotras/bilvashtaka.md) and [sacred flora](../09-artifacts-symbols-and-arts/sacred-flora-and-fauna.md). (Śiva P. Koṭirudra 40)
- **The efficacy of unknowing worship** — the tale's theological engine: Śivarātri's rites bear fruit even when performed *ajñāna* (unknowingly) and *akāma* (without devotional intent) — an extreme statement of the vrata's objective power, exactly parallel to the [Ekādaśī-māhātmya](ekadashi-mahatmyas-catalog.md) genre's logic. (Śiva P. Koṭirudra 40; Skanda P. Kedāra-khaṇḍa 33)
- **The ethical turn** — merit does not merely cancel sin; it *converts* the sinner: the story ends not with a successful hunt but with **ahiṁsā** — the hunter's compassion — and the deer's **satya** rewarded, folding ethics into ritual. (Śiva P. Koṭirudra 40)

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural/cyclic frame** — The Purāṇic chapters give the tale no yuga-date; it functions as a recurring charter for a festival observed **every year** on Māgha/Phālguna Kṛṣṇa Caturdaśī ([calendar](../00-time-and-cosmology/hindu-calendar-and-panchanga.md)). The Śiva Purāṇa's Guha boon, however, tethers this strand to the eve of **Tretā Yuga**: the hunter's reward matures when Rāma reaches Śṛṅgaverapura (Vālmīki, Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa 50; see [Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa](ramayana/2-ayodhya-kanda.md)). The Citrabhānu frame [folk] instead places a later Ikṣvāku king remembering the hunter as a past life.
- **Academic/historical frame [scholarly]** — The extant **Śiva Purāṇa** is dated c. **1000–1400 CE** (oldest surviving chapters c. 10th–11th c.), the Koṭirudra-saṁhitā belonging to this late compilation [late text]; the **Skanda Purāṇa** is a textually fluid corpus whose khaṇḍa material grew across the early-to-late medieval centuries; the **Garuḍa Purāṇa** survives in a heavily revised first-millennium-plus recension. The hunter-kathā is therefore, historically, a **medieval vrata-māhātmya** standardizing an all-night Śaiva observance — while the tradition itself holds the vrata and its story to be timeless.

## Sources

**Primary scripture**
- *Śiva Purāṇa*, **Koṭirudra-saṁhitā, chapters 38–40** — the Śivarātri vrata rules and greatness (38–39) and the hunter **Gurudruh**: failed hunt, bilva-tree vigil over the hidden liṅga, the four-watch accidental worship, the deer family's pleas and return, the sparing, Śiva's grace and the **Guha** boon (40).
- *Skanda Purāṇa*, **Māheśvara-khaṇḍa, Kedāra-khaṇḍa, chapter 33** — Śivarātri-māhātmya with the unnamed **lubdhaka**; the unwitting fast, vigil, and bilva-offering; Yama's messengers repelled by Śiva's gaṇas.
- *Garuḍa Purāṇa*, **Ācāra (Pūrva)-khaṇḍa, Śivarātri-vrata chapter** — compressed parallel of the Niṣāda hunter (Sundarasenaka) with his dog, saved by the unknowing observance.
- *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, **Ayodhyā-kāṇḍa 50–52** — Guha of Śṛṅgaverapura, the boon's fulfilment in the Śiva Purāṇa strand.
