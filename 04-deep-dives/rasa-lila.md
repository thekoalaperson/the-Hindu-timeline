# Rāsa-līlā — The Circle-Dance & the Heart of Bhakti-rasa

> *Where this sits: the **Rāsa-pañcādhyāya** (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.29–33), the autumn full-moon dance of [Kṛṣṇa](./krishna-lila.md) with the gopīs of Vṛndāvana, set at the junction of [Dvāpara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) and [Kali](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/04-kali-yuga/README.md) Yuga of our 28th Mahāyuga. Theological centre of the whole Vraja-līlā.*
>

The **rāsa-līlā** ("the dance of delight"; *rāsa* = a circle-dance, *līlā* = divine play) is the climax of Kṛṣṇa's Vṛndāvana career and the doctrinal core of *mādhurya-bhakti* — devotion in the mood of erotic love (*śṛṅgāra/mādhurya-rasa*). In the [Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/bhagavata-purana-skandha-map.md) it occupies the five chapters **10.29–33**, the **Rāsa-pañcādhyāya** ("five chapters of the rāsa"), traditionally read as the *prāṇa* (life-breath) of the entire Purāṇa. Around it cluster three celebrated song-insets — the **Veṇu-gītā** (10.21), the **Gopī-gītā** (10.31) and the **Bhramara-gītā** (10.47) — each flagged in the repo's own [Bhāgavata catalog](./bhagavata-tales-catalog.md) as a candidate for promotion, gathered here. This file consolidates the two duplicate "rāsa/gopī-songs" gaps in that catalog.

"Two clocks" reminder - *In-narrative* the rāsa unfolds in Kṛṣṇa's boyhood in the closing [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) (traditionally before 3102 BCE). *Text-critically* the Rāsa-pañcādhyāya is a mature first-millennium-CE Bhāgavata composition; Rādhā's name and the Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa theology that later dominate the scene are **later still** (Jayadeva, 12th c.; Brahma-vaivarta, late). Keep scriptural time distinct from textual dating. [scholarly]

## The overture — the Veṇu-gītā (Bhāgavata 10.21)

- **Veṇu-gītā (the Song of the Flute)** - As the autumn (śarad) season opens, the gopīs gather and rapturously describe Kṛṣṇa playing his flute (*veṇu*) while herding the cattle home; Vṛndāvana's creatures, rivers, hills, peacocks and cows are stilled and enraptured by the sound. Sung *by the gopīs about* the absent Kṛṣṇa, it is the lyric overture that kindles the desire culminating in the rāsa. (Bhāgavata 10.21) [the repo catalog flags this as an index-only candidate for its own file]
  - *Note (placement):* the Veṇu-gītā sits **before** the rāsa proper, separated from it by the garment-theft (vastra-haraṇa, 10.22) and the wives-of-the-brāhmaṇas episode (10.23) — see [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md). It is grouped here as the song-cycle's opening movement.

## The Rāsa-pañcādhyāya proper (Bhāgavata 10.29–33)

- **The summons of the flute (10.29)** - On the **śarat-pūrṇimā**, the clear autumn full-moon night, Kṛṣṇa, seeing the moon redden the horizon, plays his flute and the song draws the gopīs from their homes; they abandon husbands, children, milk on the fire, half-applied cosmetics, and run to the forest. Some, forcibly confined by relatives, leave their **bodies** through the intensity of meditation on him — the doctrine of *yoga* by love. (Bhāgavata 10.29)
- **The testing dialogue (10.29)** - Kṛṣṇa first *refuses* them — feigning propriety, he tells the wives to return to their husbands, praising chaste duty. The gopīs argue back: he is the Self of all, the true husband and goal; conventional dharma cannot bind those who have given him everything. Their reasoning establishes the supremacy of *prema* over social *dharma*. (Bhāgavata 10.29)
- **The first dance and Kṛṣṇa's disappearance (10.29–30)** - Kṛṣṇa dances and sports with them; but the gopīs grow **proud** (the *garva* of thinking themselves specially favoured), and to curb the pride he **vanishes** from the assembly. (Bhāgavata 10.29.48–30.1)
- **The search and Kṛṣṇa-anukaraṇa (10.30)** - Maddened by separation, the gopīs wander Vṛndāvana questioning the trees, plants and deer for news of him; in their absorption they *imitate* his deeds (the **Kṛṣṇa-anukaraṇa-līlā**), each becoming Kṛṣṇa — Pūtanā-slaying, Govardhana-lifting — a portrait of total identification with the beloved. (Bhāgavata 10.30)
- **The footprints and the favoured one (10.30)** - They find two sets of footprints — Kṛṣṇa's and a gopī's beside them — and infer he led **one** gopī away, honouring her above the rest; then her footprints too vanish (he carried her), and finally she is found abandoned, for she in turn had grown proud. (Bhāgavata 10.30.25–43) [the verse-cluster commentators read as the indirect presence of Rādhā — see below]
- **The Gopī-gītā (10.31)** - Reassembling on the Yamunā bank, the gopīs sing the **Gopī-gītā**, the 19-verse "Song of the Gopīs" of longing (*jayati te 'dhikaṃ janmanā vrajaḥ...*) — among the most beloved of all bhakti hymns, the locus classicus of *vipralambha* (love-in-separation). (Bhāgavata 10.31)
- **The return and consolation (10.32)** - Kṛṣṇa reappears; the overjoyed gopīs reproach him for abandoning them. He answers that he withdrew to deepen their love (separation intensifies devotion) and confesses he can never repay their love — *na pāraye 'haṃ* — even over an immortal lifetime. (Bhāgavata 10.32)
- **The mahā-rāsa and the multiplication (10.33)** - The great circle-dance begins: Kṛṣṇa **multiplies himself**, placing one form of himself between each pair of gopīs, so every gopī believes Kṛṣṇa dances at her side alone. The gods and their consorts shower flowers and watch from the sky; the night of the dance is said (by yoga-māyā) to last a "night of Brahmā." This is the theological summit of *mādhurya-bhakti*. (Bhāgavata 10.33)
- **Śuka's apologia (10.33.27–40)** - Foreseeing the moral objection — how can God, teacher of dharma, sport with others' wives? — the narrator **Śuka** defends the līlā to King Parīkṣit: Kṛṣṇa is the *antaryāmin* (indweller) and Lord of all, beyond the rules he gives to embodied beings; the act is utterly transcendent, not to be imitated, and those who hear it with faith are freed of lust, not inflamed by it. (Bhāgavata 10.33.27–40) [the canonical scriptural guard-rail against literal/erotic misreading] [scholarly]

## The Rādhā question — catalog all variants

The single most-discussed feature of the Bhāgavata's rāsa is that its supreme heroine is **never named.** Different traditions fill (or refuse to fill) the gap differently — never merge them.

- *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa, the base text):* **Rādhā does not appear by name** anywhere in the Rāsa-pañcādhyāya. Gauḍīya commentators (Jīva Gosvāmī, Sanātana Gosvāmī, Viśvanātha Cakravartī) read her *indirectly* into **10.30.28** — the one favoured gopī led away and traced by her footprints. [scholarly: her textual absence here is much-debated]
- *Variant (Gāhā Sattasaī of Hāla, ~1st–2nd c. CE):* the **earliest surviving mention** of the name "Rādhā" beside Kṛṣṇa — a single Prakrit verse, unconnected to the rāsa narrative. [scholarly]
- *Variant (Gīta-Govinda, Jayadeva, 12th c.):* Rādhā becomes the **central nāyikā** — Kṛṣṇa's beloved through union, jealousy (*māna*), separation and reunion. Jayadeva's lyric is what fixed Rādhā at the heart of pan-Indian Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa devotion and reframed the rāsa around the **one** beloved rather than the many gopīs. [late text relative to the Purāṇas]
- *Variant (Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa, late):* Rādhā is Kṛṣṇa's eternal *śakti* and consort, equal or supreme — the Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa dyad of **Goloka**; she is the presiding power of the rāsa-maṇḍala (the "rāsa-circle" from which Rādhā's epithet *Rāseśvarī* derives), and is even wedded to Kṛṣṇa with Brahmā officiating. [late text]
- *Variant (Gauḍīya / Nimbārka theology):* Rādhā as the **hlādinī-śakti** (bliss-potency) of Kṛṣṇa; the rāsa is the eternal play of the Lord with his own joy-power, the gopīs being her expansions (*kāya-vyūha*). [folk/sectarian]

## The aftermath-song — the Bhramara-gītā (Bhāgavata 10.47)

- **Bhramara-gītā (the Song to the Bee)** - Long after Kṛṣṇa has left Vṛndāvana for Mathurā, he sends his cousin and minister **Uddhava** to console the grieving gopīs. While Uddhava delivers Kṛṣṇa's message of impersonal *jñāna-yoga*, a **bumblebee** (*bhramara*) settles near the gopīs; the chief gopī (read by the tradition as Rādhā), in a delirium of separation, addresses the bee — alternately reproaching it as Kṛṣṇa's fickle messenger and pouring out her love. The "Song to the Bee" is the supreme expression of *vipralambha-bhakti*, and it **humbles Uddhava**, who concludes that the gopīs' wordless love surpasses all the yoga and knowledge he came to teach. (Bhāgavata 10.47) → see also the [Uddhava Gītā](./uddhava-gita.md) [Kṛṣṇa's much later, philosophical farewell to the same Uddhava] and [Kṛṣṇa-līlā § Mathurā](./krishna-lila.md).
  - *Note (the same Uddhava, two discourses):* do not confuse the **Bhramara-gītā** (10.47, Uddhava *receives* a lesson in love at Vraja) with the **[Uddhava Gītā / Haṃsa Gītā](./uddhava-gita.md)** (11.7–29, Uddhava *receives* Kṛṣṇa's final teaching at Dvārakā). Uddhava is the hinge between Vraja-bhakti and renunciate jñāna. [scholarly]

## Why these three songs are catalogued together

- The **Veṇu-**, **Gopī-** and **Bhramara-gītā** form a single arc of *mādhurya-bhakti* across the gopī cycle — desire kindled (10.21), separation in the dance (10.31), separation made permanent (10.47) — and all three are flagged "index-only — candidate for own file" in the [Bhāgavata Tales Catalog](./bhagavata-tales-catalog.md). They are distinct from the philosophical "Gītās" indexed in the [Lesser Gītās Catalog](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md): those are *jñāna*-discourses, these are *prema*-laments. [scholarly]
- The catalog also notes the **Yugala-gītā** (10.35), a paired song of the gopīs in two half-choruses praising Kṛṣṇa's flute-play, sometimes counted with this cluster; it sits *after* the rāsa and is included here for completeness. (Bhāgavata 10.35) [index-only]
