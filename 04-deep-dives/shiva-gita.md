# Śiva-Gītā

> Where this sits: an independent **Purāṇic Gītā** of ~768 verses across 16 chapters embedded in the **Uttara-khaṇḍa** of the [Padma Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md), spoken by **Śiva** to the grieving [Rāma](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) in the Daṇḍaka forest *after* Sītā's abduction — set in the [Tretā Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga. The principal **Śaiva** counterpart to the [Bhagavad Gītā](./bhagavad-gita.md).
>

The **Śiva-Gītā** ("the Song of Śiva") is a self-contained Advaita-and-Pāśupata discourse in which **Śiva**, drawn by Rāma's grief, reveals the nature of the *jīva* and *Brahman*, the discipline of *bhakti* and *jñāna-yoga*, the path to liberation, and finally his own **cosmic form (viśvarūpa)** — answering the Bhagavad Gītā's Viṣṇu-theophany with a Śaiva one. It circulates on its own as one of the "great Gītās" recited alongside the [Devī-Gītā, Gaṇeśa-Gītā, and Rāma-Gītā](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md#purāṇic--independent-gītās-the-path-to-60--pointers-only), and is the Padma Purāṇa's flagship Śaiva-Vedānta text. [scholarly]

## Textual placement & frame

- **Location** — The Śiva-Gītā sits in the **Uttara-khaṇḍa** (the long final book) of the [Padma Purāṇa](../90-literature-corpus/smriti-puranas.md), conventionally counted as **16 adhyāyas** totalling **~768 verses**. (Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa)
  - *Variant (count):* most editions give **16 chapters**; verse totals are cited around **760–770** depending on recension. [scholarly] [disputed — recension]
- **The narrative occasion** — The setting is the [Araṇya Kāṇḍa](./ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md) crisis: **Sītā has been carried off by Rāvaṇa**, and Rāma, broken by separation, wanders the forest in despair. The sage **Agastya** comes to him and prescribes the **Pāśupata vrata** (the vow of Śiva) and worship of Śiva as the means to recover Sītā and defeat the rākṣasa. (Padma Purāṇa, Śiva-Gītā 1–2; cf. [Agastya](./agastya.md))
- **The theophany** — Pleased by Rāma's austerities, **Śiva appears with Pārvatī and the gaṇas** and, in answer to Rāma's questions on suffering, the self, and release, delivers the Gītā. (Padma Purāṇa, Śiva-Gītā 3)
- **Reported frame** — Like the rest of the Purāṇa, the dialogue reaches the reader through the **Sūta (Ugraśravas / Lomaharṣaṇa) reciting to the sages of Naimiṣa forest**, who in turn report Śiva's words to Rāma. (Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa)

## The teaching — chapter movement

- **Rāma's grief and the question** — Rāma asks why a devoted, righteous man suffers, and what the **highest good (śreyas)** truly is — the Śaiva mirror of Arjuna's despondency. (Śiva-Gītā 3)
- **The greatness of bhakti** — Śiva declares that neither ritual, learning, nor lineage but **single-minded devotion (bhakti) to Śiva** opens liberating knowledge; *bhakti* is the precondition for *jñāna*, not its rival. (Śiva-Gītā 4–5)
- **The nature of the jīva** — The embodied self (*jīva*) is described through the **deha-svarūpa** (constitution of the body), the gross/subtle/causal sheaths, the entry and transmigration of the soul, and the wretchedness of repeated birth — a vivid *garbha*-meditation on embodiment and death. (Śiva-Gītā 6–9)
- **Jīva and Brahman are one** — The doctrinal core: the individual self is **not other than Brahman/Śiva**; bondage is *avidyā* (ignorance) and liberation is the direct realisation "I am That." Strong **Advaita** identity-knowledge framed in Śaiva theology. (Śiva-Gītā 10–11)
- **The path of yoga and renunciation** — Śiva expounds **jñāna-yoga**, dispassion (*vairāgya*), the futility of works done for fruit, control of the senses, and the marks of the liberated-in-life (*jīvanmukta*). (Śiva-Gītā 12–13)
- **The viśvarūpa (cosmic vision)** — The climax: Śiva grants Rāma **divine sight** and reveals his **all-pervading cosmic form**, the worlds and beings contained within his body — the Śaiva answer to Bhagavad Gītā ch. 11. Rāma, awestruck, praises him. (Śiva-Gītā 14–15)
- **Bestowal of the weapon & the boon** — Śiva confers on Rāma the **Pāśupata-astra** (Śiva's irresistible weapon) and the assurance that he will recover Sītā and slay Rāvaṇa, then departs. (Śiva-Gītā 15–16)
  - *Variant (weapon):* the **Pāśupata** grant is the Śiva-Gītā's distinctive narrative payload; in Vālmīki's [Araṇya Kāṇḍa](./ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md) it is **Agastya** who arms Rāma (later, the Āditya-hṛdaya from the Yuddha Kāṇḍa), so the Purāṇa **relocates the divine-armament motif to Śiva**. ## Doctrine & affiliations

- **Advaita-Vedānta core** — The Gītā's metaphysics is firmly **non-dualist**: one *Brahman* (= Śiva), the world as appearance, *jīva*–*Brahman* identity, liberation by knowledge. It is frequently read in Smārta-Advaita circles as a Śaiva parallel to Śaṅkara's Vedānta. [scholarly]
- **Pāśupata layer** — The framing vow (Pāśupata *vrata*) and the gift of the **Pāśupata weapon** anchor it to the **Pāśupata** Śaiva ascetic tradition; *paśu* (the bound soul), *pati* (Śiva the lord), and *pāśa* (the bonds) vocabulary surfaces in the soteriology. [scholarly]
- **Synthesis of bhakti and jñāna** — Unlike the purely *jñāna*-leaning [Anugītā](./anugita.md) or [Ṛbhu-gītā](./ribhu-gita.md), the Śiva-Gītā insists devotion and knowledge are a **single graded path** — *bhakti* purifies, *jñāna* releases. This makes it the closest structural twin of the Bhagavad Gītā among the Purāṇic Gītās. [scholarly]

## Place among the "great Gītās"

- **First-tier non-Mahābhārata Gītā** — On the standard *gītā-saṅgraha* rosters the Śiva-Gītā stands with the **Devī-Gītā** (Devī-Bhāgavata), **Gaṇeśa-Gītā** (Gaṇeśa Purāṇa), and **Rāma-Gītā** (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa) as the principal **Purāṇic** Gītās — sectarian counterparts to the Bhagavad Gītā, one per major deity-stream. (see [lesser Gītās catalog → Purāṇic Gītās](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md#purāṇic--independent-gītās-the-path-to-60--pointers-only)) [folk] [scholarly]
- **Shared protagonist with the Rāma-Gītā** — Both place **Rāma** at the centre, but invert the roles: in the Śiva-Gītā Rāma is the **student** (Śiva teaches him); in the Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa's Rāma-Gītā, Rāma is the **teacher** (instructing Lakṣmaṇa). The corpus treats them as a deliberately paired set. [scholarly]
- **Independent circulation** — Like the Devī- and Rāma-Gītās, the Śiva-Gītā is printed, commented, and recited **detached from its parent Purāṇa**, with traditional commentaries (e.g. *Abhinava-Gupta*-style and later Vedāntic ṭīkās are sometimes cited, though the best-known is the anonymous/late commentarial stratum). [late text]

## Two clocks — scriptural vs. historical

- **Scriptural time** — *In-narrative* the discourse is uttered in the **[Tretā Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/02-treta-yuga.md)** of the current Vaivasvata Manvantara, during Rāma's forest exile, at the moment of Sītā's loss — the same hinge-point as the [Araṇya Kāṇḍa](./ramayana/3-aranya-kanda.md). [scriptural]
- **Historical time** — *Text-critically* the Śiva-Gītā is a **late accretion** to the Padma Purāṇa's Uttara-khaṇḍa, a sectarian Śaiva-Advaita composition of the **later 1st / earlier 2nd millennium CE**, drawing on mature Vedānta and Bhagavad-Gītā / Devī-Gītā templates. Keep manuscript dating distinct from the Tretā-Yuga setting. [scholarly]
