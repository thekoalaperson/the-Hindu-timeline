# Krishna's Infancy Lila

> *Where this sits: the **bālya-līlā** — the infancy and toddler deeds of [Kṛṣṇa](./krishna-lila.md) in the cowherd hamlet of Gokula, in the closing [Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md) of our 28th Mahāyuga. The arc that fixes Krishna as the divine child and is the great locus of **vātsalya-rasa** (the love of God as one's own infant).*
>

This file is a dedicated deep-dive on the earliest pastoral phase of Krishna's life in **Gokula** — the demon-slayings of infancy sent by [Kaṃsa](./kamsa-vadha.md), the butter-thief (*navanīta-cora*) cycle, and the two great theophanies of the cradle: **Yaśodā seeing the universe in the child's mouth** and the **liberation of the cursed Yakṣas Nalakūbara and Maṇigrīva**. It precedes the Vṛndāvana phase (Kāliya, Govardhana, rāsa — see [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md)) and the Mathurā climax ([Kaṃsa-vadha](./kamsa-vadha.md)). The principal source is the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa X.6–11**, with the older **Harivaṃśa** (Viṣṇu Parva) and **Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Book 5** as parallels. These episodes appear in the parent file only as roster bullets; this is their full treatment.

---

## The demon-slayings of infancy

These are the *bālāriṣṭa* — the "child-perils," demons dispatched by Kaṃsa of Mathurā, who knows (in the Bhāgavata, by Nārada's warning) that his nemesis is being raised among the cowherds.

- **Pūtanā and the poison-breast** — Kaṃsa's agent **Pūtanā**, a child-killing demoness (*rākṣasī*), takes the form of a beautiful nurse, smears her breast with a deadly poison, and enters Nanda's house to suckle the infant Krishna; the babe seizes the nipple and **drains her of life itself** along with the milk, killing her so that she falls in her huge true form, crushing the trees, for twelve miles around (Bhāgavata X.6; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).
  - **Pūtanā's mokṣa** — Because she came in the *form* of a mother and gave her breast — however murderously — the Lord grants her the destiny of a true nurse: she attains **liberation (mokṣa)**, the rank of a foster-mother in his eternal realm. The Bhāgavata makes her the paradigm that even *enmity* directed at God, when it touches him intimately, redeems (Bhāgavata X.6). [scholarly: the "mokṣa of the demoness" theme recurs with Kaṃsa and Aghāsura]
  - **The protective rites** — After her death the *gopīs* and elders pass a cow's tail, cow-dust, and the divine names over the child's limbs (the *rakṣā* / *nāma-kavaca*) and burn Pūtanā's corpse, whose smoke smells of aloes — a sign her sin is consumed (Bhāgavata X.6). [folk: this *raksā*-mantra is recited over infants in Vaiṣṇava households]
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa):* the older account is terser and more martial — Pūtanā is slain but the elaborated mokṣa-theology and the fragrant-smoke detail are the Bhāgavata's later devotional overlay. [scholarly]

- **Śakaṭāsura, the cart-demon** — On the day of the child's first *utthāna* (turning over) / name-day feast, Krishna is laid beneath a heavy laden **handcart (śakaṭa)**; a demon, **Śakaṭāsura**, enters the cart to crush him, but the infant, crying for milk, flings up a tiny **foot and overturns the cart**, smashing its pots and killing the demon within. Only the small children playing nearby see that the babe's kick did it (Bhāgavata X.7; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata X.7):* the gloss makes the cart itself the demon's vehicle/possessed body; in retellings Śakaṭāsura is sometimes a cart-shaped *asura* sent by Kaṃsa, sometimes a haunting spirit. [textual]

- **Tṛṇāvarta, the whirlwind-demon** — Kaṃsa sends **Tṛṇāvarta**, who takes the form of a **whirlwind / dust-storm**, snatches the seated child up into a blinding cyclone, and carries him high into the sky while Gokula is blanketed in dust and Yaśodā despairs. Aloft, Krishna makes himself **immovably heavy**, dragging the demon down; unable to fly or shake him off, Tṛṇāvarta is **throttled** by the child gripping his throat and crashes dead onto a rock, the babe riding his chest unharmed (Bhāgavata X.7; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5).

## The vision of the universe in the mouth

- **The dirt-eating and the cosmos** — The toddler's playmates tell Yaśodā that Krishna has been **eating earth**; she scolds him and forces open his mouth to check — and within the little mouth she beholds the **entire universe**: the sky, the directions, mountains, oceans, islands, the moon and stars, the elements, the senses, *time*, *karma*, and **Vraja and herself** within it, in an infinite regress (Bhāgavata X.8).
  - **The locus of vātsalya** — Overcome, Yaśodā for a moment apprehends her son as the **Supreme Brahman**; Krishna at once draws his Yogamāyā over her so that maternal love (*sneha* / *vātsalya*) returns and she clasps him as her own infant. The Bhāgavata frames this as the supreme statement of *vātsalya-rasa* — God consenting to be *forgotten* as God so as to be loved as a child (Bhāgavata X.8). [scholarly: the verse is a touchstone of bhakti theology — the infinite contained in, and choosing, the finite]
  - *Variant:* in the Bhāgavata the same chapter (X.8) also narrates the **name-giving (nāma-karaṇa)** by the sage **Garga** — who privately names the child *Kṛṣṇa* / *Vāsudeva* and foretells he equals Nārāyaṇa — and an *earlier* dirt-eating glimpse; some retellings split the cosmos-vision into its own episode. [textual ordering]

## Dāmodara — the butter-theft and the binding

The **navanīta-cora** ("butter-thief") cycle: the toddler and his friends raid the *gopīs'* churns and hanging butter-pots, a body of līlā beloved in poetry and song.

- **The breaking of the butter-pot** — One morning Yaśodā sets the babe down mid-suckle to tend boiling milk; affronted, Krishna **breaks the butter-churn pot** with a grinding-stone, steals the fresh butter, and feeds it to the monkeys; Yaśodā finds him standing on an upturned mortar, butter on his face, and gives chase (Bhāgavata X.9).
- **Ūkhala-bandhana — bound to the mortar** — Catching the boy, the exhausted Yaśodā resolves to **tie him to the wooden grinding-mortar (ūlūkhala / ūkhala)** with a rope; but the cord falls **two finger-widths short** no matter how many ropes she knots together — a sign of his measurelessness — until, conquered by her love and labor, **Krishna lets himself be bound**. From this binding (*dāman*) about the belly (*udara*) he gains the name **Dāmodara** (Bhāgavata X.9). [scholarly: "the immeasurable God measured only by a devotee's love" is the verse's classic reading]

## The twin arjuna trees — Nalakūbara and Maṇigrīva

- **Dragging the mortar** — Left bound, the toddler **crawls dragging the heavy mortar** between a pair of tall twin **arjuna trees** standing in the yard; the mortar wedges crosswise and, as the child pulls, the two great trees are **uprooted and crash down**, and from them step **two radiant beings (Yakṣas)** who bow to him (Bhāgavata X.10).
- **The curse of Nārada** — These are **Nalakūbara and Maṇigrīva**, sons of **Kubera**, lord of the Yakṣas (see [Yakṣas & Guhyakas](../08-beings-and-bestiary/yakshas-and-guhyakas.md)). Once, sporting drunk and naked with women in the Mandākinī, they ignored the sage **Nārada**; for their arrogance he **cursed them to become trees** for a long age — but, mercifully, trees standing in *Krishna's* courtyard, so that their bondage would end at the Lord's touch (Bhāgavata X.10).
  - **Mokṣa of the Guhyakas** — Freed by the dragged mortar, the two Yakṣas praise Krishna, who confirms Nārada's grace and sends them home to Alakā, now devotees (Bhāgavata X.10–11). The episode is sometimes titled **Yamala-Arjuna-bhañjana** ("breaking of the twin arjunas"). [The Yakṣa-brothers are *guhyakas* of Kubera's court — see [Yakṣas & Guhyakas](../08-beings-and-bestiary/yakshas-and-guhyakas.md).]
  - *Variant (Harivaṃśa / Viṣṇu Purāṇa):* the falling of the twin trees and the dragging of the mortar are present, but the **Nārada-curse frame and the named Yakṣas Nalakūbara and Maṇigrīva** are most fully developed in the Bhāgavata; the older texts give the prodigy with less of the *guhyaka*-redemption theology. [scholarly]

## Departure from Gokula

- **The omens and the move to Vṛndāvana** — The repeated prodigies and demon-deaths alarm the elders of Gokula; the cowherd chief **Upananda** counsels migration, and the whole community moves with their carts and cattle to **Vṛndāvana** for fresh pasture and safety (Bhāgavata X.11). This closes the Gokula infancy and opens the boyhood Vṛndāvana arc — Vatsāsura, Bakāsura, Aghāsura, and [Kāliya](./kaliya-mardana.md) (see [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](./krishna-lila.md)).

## Two clocks

- **Scriptural time:** the bālya-līlā falls in Krishna's infancy in the closing **[Dvāpara Yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/03-dvapara-yuga.md)** of the current 28th Mahāyuga, in the years just after his birth (traditionally a generation before the war and his departure, **3102 BCE**, the onset of Kali Yuga). The internal order is fixed: Pūtanā → Śakaṭa → Tṛṇāvarta → cosmos-vision/naming → Dāmodara and the arjuna trees → move to Vṛndāvana.
- **Text-critical time:** the **oldest connected form is the Harivaṃśa** (core c. 1st–2nd c. BCE, growing into the early CE centuries), restated in the **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** (~5th c. CE), and most elaborated and devotionalised in the **Bhāgavata** (~6th–10th c. CE) — which alone fully develops Pūtanā's mokṣa, the Garga naming, the Yogamāyā-framing of the cosmos-vision, and the Nārada-curse of the two Yakṣas. The skeleton — the poison-nurse, the cart, the whirlwind, the bound mortar, the twin trees — is constant across all three. [scholarly]
