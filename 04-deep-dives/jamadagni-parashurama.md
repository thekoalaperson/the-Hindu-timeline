# Jamadagni and the Paraśurāma Cycle

> *A deep-dive into one episode-cycle of the cosmic timeline: the Bhārgava sage **Jamadagni** — counted in the current-set [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md) rosters — his wish-cow, his murder by the Haihaya clan, and the vengeance of his son **[Paraśurāma](parashurama.md)**, the sixth [avatāra of Viṣṇu](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md).*
> *⬆ Parent index: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Master index: [README](../README.md)*

**Jamadagni** (जमदग्नि, "devouring fire") is a Bhṛgu-line ṛṣi of the present **[Vaivasvata Manvantara](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/README.md)**, named in several **current-set [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md) lists** (Bṛhadāraṇyaka, Bhāgavata 8.13, Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.2). His household holds a wish-cow whose theft by the thousand-armed king **Kārtavīrya Arjuna** triggers his own beheading — and that murder is the hinge on which the entire **Paraśurāma avatāra** turns: the brahmin-son who clears the earth of kṣatriyas **twenty-one times**. This file hosts the full Jamadagni–Kārtavīrya cycle (lineage, Reṇukā, the cow, the murder, the vengeance); the cross-yuga preceptor arc and the land-reclamation legends are carried in the dedicated **[Paraśurāma avatāra](parashurama.md)** node.

**Two clocks.** Scripturally this is a single Tretā-Yuga sequence in the current Mahāyuga, climaxing in Paraśurāma the *cirañjīvi*. Attempts to read the twenty-one purges as a memory of a historical brahmin–kṣatriya conflict, or the Bhārgava–Haihaya war as dynastic history, belong to a separate historical clock and are flagged [scholarly]/[disputed] below.

## I. The Bhārgava house and the wish-cow

- **Lineage of Jamadagni** — Jamadagni is the son of the Bhṛgu sage **Ṛcīka** and **Satyavatī** (daughter of King Gādhi, hence sister to Viśvāmitra); his sons by Reṇukā are five, the youngest being **Rāma Jāmadagnya / Paraśurāma**. (*Mahābhārata* 3.115–116; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.15)
  - *Variant (the swapped oblations — Mahābhārata 3.115; Bhāgavata 9.15):* Ṛcīka prepares two *caru* (oblation-porridges), a brahminic one for Satyavatī and a kṣatriya one for her mother, to beget a sage-son and a warrior-son respectively; the two women exchange the bowls, so the *kṣatra*-charged porridge passes into Satyavatī's line. By her plea the warrior-temper is deferred a generation — making her son **Jamadagni a serene brahmin** but her **grandson Paraśurāma the axe-bearer**. This etymologizes why a brahmin house produces a warrior. [scholarly]
- **Marriage to Reṇukā** — Jamadagni weds **Reṇukā**, daughter of King **Prasenajit / Reṇu** (Ayodhyā / Solar line); she is the model chaste wife (*pativratā*) whose yogic merit lets her carry water in unbaked clay. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.15; *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*)
- **Kāmadhenu at the āśrama** — Jamadagni's hermitage holds a **wish-cow** — named variously **Kāmadhenu / Surabhi / Suśīlā / Nandinī** — who at a word furnishes endless food, hospitality, and even troops; she is the engine of the whole tragedy. (*Mahābhārata* 3.116; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.15) — see [Kāmadhenu & divine cattle](../08-beings-and-bestiary/kamadhenu-and-divine-cattle.md).
  - *Variant (which cow):* the sources do not agree whether Jamadagni's cow is "the" Kāmadhenu, her daughter, or a calf — the corpus treats *Kāmadhenu* as a class of all-yielding cows, leaving the roster open. [disputed]

## II. The beheading and restoration of Reṇukā

- **Reṇukā's lapse** — Sent to the river, Reṇukā glimpses the Gandharva king **Citraratha** (some texts **Citrasena**) sporting with apsarases and her mind strays an instant with desire; her yogic purity broken, the clay pot dissolves and she returns late. Jamadagni, by his power, perceives the lapse. (*Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*; *Padma Purāṇa*; cf. *Mahābhārata* 3.116)
- **The father's command** — Enraged, Jamadagni orders each son in turn to **slay their mother**; the four elder sons refuse and are cursed to idiocy / loss of reason. **Paraśurāma alone obeys**, beheading Reṇukā with the axe — the supreme test of obedience to the guru-father. (*Mahābhārata* 3.116; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.16)
- **The boon and restoration** — Pleased, Jamadagni grants boons; Paraśurāma asks that his **mother be revived** (with no memory of her death), that his **brothers regain their wits**, and for invincibility and long life. Jamadagni restores Reṇukā and the brothers. (*Mahābhārata* 3.116; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.16)
  - *Variant (South Indian / Reṇukā–Yellammā):* in Deccan village cult the axe falls on both Reṇukā and an outcaste woman **Mātaṅgī** who sheltered her; the hasty restoration **swaps their heads onto the wrong bodies**, generating the twin goddesses **Yellammā / Reṇukā** and **Mātaṅgī** — a major folk aetiology absent from the Sanskrit epic. [folk][regional]

## III. Kārtavīrya Arjuna and the murder of Jamadagni

- **Sahasrārjuna the thousand-armed** — **Kārtavīrya Arjuna** (Sahasrārjuna), Haihaya/Cedi king of **Māhiṣmatī** on the Narmadā, is a devotee of **[Dattātreya](dattatreya.md)**, who granted him a **thousand arms** and near-invincibility; he is the over-mighty kṣatriya whose pride the avatāra exists to humble. (*Mahābhārata* 3.115–116; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.15)
- **The hospitality and the theft** — Hunting near the hermitage, Kārtavīrya and his army are feasted to surfeit by Jamadagni's wish-cow; coveting her, he **seizes the cow (or her calf) by force** and drags her off, wrecking the āśrama, when Jamadagni refuses to give her up. (*Mahābhārata* 3.116; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.15)
- **First slaying of Kārtavīrya** — Paraśurāma pursues to Māhiṣmatī and in battle **severs the thousand arms** and beheads Kārtavīrya, recovering the cow — the opening blow of the kṣatriya war. (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.15–16; *Mahābhārata* 3.116)
- **Murder of Jamadagni** — In revenge, the **sons of Kārtavīrya** fall on the hermitage during Paraśurāma's absence and **behead the meditating, unresisting Jamadagni**, carrying off his head; the grieving Reṇukā strikes her breast **twenty-one times** (or cries out twenty-one times), fixing the number of the coming vengeance. (*Mahābhārata* 3.116–117; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.16)
  - *Variant (revival of Jamadagni):* some Purāṇic and folk tellings have Jamadagni **restored to life** (by the gods, or by Paraśurāma's tapas / *amṛta*), after which he eventually attains heaven and a place among the stars; the Mahābhārata core leaves his death as the spur to the purges. [late text]

## IV. The twenty-one-fold extermination of the kṣatriyas

- **The vow of annihilation** — Over his father's corpse Paraśurāma vows to **rid the earth of kṣatriyas twenty-one times** (*triḥsapta-kṛtvaḥ*), and circuits the world slaughtering the warrior kings and lines grown tyrannical and adharmic. (*Mahābhārata* 3.117; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.16)
- **The five blood-lakes at Samanta-pañcaka** — At **Samanta-pañcaka** (Kurukṣetra) he fills **five pools with the blood** of the slain and offers *tarpaṇa* to his ancestors; the pacified Bhṛgu forefathers (and **Ṛcīka**) appear and bid him relent — which is why the same field is later the site of the Kurukṣetra war. (*Mahābhārata* 3.117; cf. 9.53)
- **The earth-gift to Kaśyapa** — Having cleared the earth, Paraśurāma performs a vast **Aśvamedha** and **gifts the whole earth to Kaśyapa** as *dakṣiṇā*; Kaśyapa bids him quit the land he has given away, and he withdraws to **Mahendra** mountain. (*Mahābhārata* 3.117; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.16)
  - *Variant (survival of the kṣatriya order):* tradition explains the continuance of kings by holding that **kṣatriya women** bore sons to surviving brahmins, or that boy-children were hidden (with the Earth, the Sun, in mountains), so the *varṇa* regenerated between the twenty-one purges. [scholarly]
- **Retirement to Mahendra** — Paraśurāma settles on **Mahendragiri** as a deathless ascetic — the *cirañjīvi* who closes the Jamadagni cycle but lives on across the yugas. The **land-reclamation of the Konkan/Kerala coast** and his role as **guru to Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Karṇa** and **Kalki** belong to that ongoing life. → see [Paraśurāma avatāra](parashurama.md) and [The Cirañjīvis](the-chiranjivis.md).

## V. Why the Jamadagni cycle matters

- **The plot-engine cow** — Jamadagni's hermitage is the third of the great **cow-conflicts** (with Vasiṣṭha–Viśvāmitra and Vasiṣṭha–the Vasus): whenever a king covets a sage's all-yielding cow, war follows. The wish-cow is the recurring trigger that converts brahmin patience into kṣatriya catastrophe. (*Mahābhārata*; *Rāmāyaṇa*) — see [Kāmadhenu & divine cattle](../08-beings-and-bestiary/kamadhenu-and-divine-cattle.md).
- **Brahmin austerity vs. kṣatriya excess** — Jamadagni is the still, unresisting sage whose murder licenses the one avatāra whose dharma is the **violent correction of the warrior class** — a counter-weight myth to the kingly Rāma and Kṛṣṇa descents. [scholarly]
- **Saptarṣi continuity** — As a current-set Saptarṣi and Bhṛgu clan-father, Jamadagni anchors the **Jāmadagnya gotra** and (through Ṛcīka and Bhṛgu) the militant streak of the Bhārgavas. (*Mahābhārata* 13.4; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 9.15) — see [Saptarṣi](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/saptarishi.md).

## Sources

- **Mahābhārata, Vana Parva 3.115–117** — the core Jāmadagnya cycle: Ṛcīka's swapped oblations and Jamadagni's birth, the wish-cow, Kārtavīrya Arjuna's theft and first slaying, the **beheading of Jamadagni**, the twenty-one-fold extermination, the blood-lakes of Samanta-pañcaka, and the earth-gift to Kaśyapa; **3.116** for the Reṇukā beheading-and-restoration.
- **Mahābhārata, Śānti / Anuśāsana Parva (13.4 and refs)** — the Bhṛgu/Aurva background to Bhārgava militancy; Jamadagni as Saptarṣi and ancestral figure.
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.15–16** — Jamadagni's lineage (Ṛcīka & Satyavatī), Reṇukā's lapse and revival, the wish-cow, the slaying of Kārtavīrya, the murder of Jamadagni, and the twenty-one purges.
- **Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa** — the Reṇukā material: her marriage, the river-lapse, the beheading and restoration.
- **Padma Purāṇa** — variant Reṇukā narrative and Bhārgava–Haihaya conflict.
- **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.2; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.13; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.2** — current-set Saptarṣi rosters naming Jamadagni.
- **South Indian Reṇukā–Yellammā / Mātaṅgī folk cult** — the head-swap aetiology of the twin goddesses. [folk][regional]
