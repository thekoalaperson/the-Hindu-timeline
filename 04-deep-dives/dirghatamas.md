# Dīrghatamas — the Blind Seer and the Asya Vāmasya Riddle Hymn

> *Where this sits: a deep-dive into **Dīrghatamas Māmateya Aucathya** — the blind Āṅgirasa ṛṣi of the [Ṛgveda](../90-literature-corpus/vedas/rigveda.md)'s first Maṇḍala, elder half-brother-in-conception to [Bharadvāja](bharadvaja.md), niyoga-father of the eastern kings **Aṅga, Vaṅga and Kaliṅga**, father of the seer **Kakṣīvat**, and author of **RV 1.164 (Asya Vāmasya)**, the riddle hymn that gives Hinduism its most quoted line — *ekaṃ sad viprā bahudhā vadanti*. ⬆ Parent index: [04-deep-dives](README.md) · Master index: [README](../README.md)*

**Dīrghatamas** ("long darkness" — *dīrgha* + *tamas*) is the Ṛgveda's own image of the seer who sees without eyes. Blind from the womb by his uncle's curse, thrown into the river by his own household, rescued by a king who begs him for heirs, he is at once a **genealogical hinge** (the eastern kingdoms Aṅga, Vaṅga, Kaliṅga trace their eponyms to his niyoga) and the **most philosophical voice in the whole Saṃhitā**: his *Asya Vāmasya* hymn (RV 1.164) seeds the two-birds image of the [Muṇḍaka](../90-literature-corpus/upanishads/mundaka-upanishad.md) and [Śvetāśvatara](../90-literature-corpus/upanishads/svetashvatara-upanishad.md) Upaniṣads and the *ekam sat* declaration that "Truth is one; the wise call it by many names." He has until now appeared in this corpus only as scattered one-liners; this file is his canonical home.

---

## Dramatis personae & the name

- **Dīrghatamas** — son of **Utathya** (an Āṅgirasa, elder brother of Bṛhaspati) and **Mamatā**; hence his two standing patronymics: **Aucathya** ("son of Ucathya/Utathya") and **Māmateya** ("son of Mamatā"), both of which the Ṛgveda itself uses of him (RV 1.158.1, 1.158.4; cf. 1.147.3, 1.152.6). (Ṛgveda; Sarvānukramaṇī; Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva)
- **The name "long darkness"** — traditionally explained by his congenital blindness: born into darkness, he remained in it long. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~104; Bṛhaddevatā 4)
  - *Variant (womb-reading):* some retellings gloss the name from the **long time spent in the womb** — the child lingering in darkness before birth. [folk etymology / disputed]
- **Supporting cast** — **Bṛhaspati** (the uncle whose intrusion and curse blind him — see [The Gurus: Bṛhaspati & Śukra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/the-gurus-brihaspati-and-shukra.md)); **[Bharadvāja](bharadvaja.md)** (the child born of that same intrusion); **Pradveṣī** (his wife); **King Bali** (Cāndravaṃśa king of the east who rescues and employs him); **Sudeṣṇā** (Bali's queen); **Kakṣīvat** (his seer-son); **Ghoṣā** (Kakṣīvat's daughter, a brahmavādinī).
- **Lineage slot** — an Āṅgirasa of the **Utathya sub-line**, cousin-branch to the Bhāradvājas and Gautamas; via his niyoga-sons he is also ancestor of the **Aṅga–Vaṅga–Kaliṅga** kṣatriya lines. (Mahābhārata; Purāṇic genealogies) — see [Rishi lineages & gotras](../08-beings-and-bestiary/rishi-lineages-and-gotras.md).

## 1. Conception, Bṛhaspati's curse, and blindness

- **The intrusion of Bṛhaspati** — while **Mamatā** is pregnant by her husband **Utathya**, his younger brother **Bṛhaspati** forces his attentions on her; the embryo already in the womb — the future Dīrghatamas — **speaks from within and blocks the intruding seed**, protesting that there is no room for two. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~104)
- **The curse of long darkness** — enraged at being thwarted by an unborn child, **Bṛhaspati curses the embryo to be born blind** — into *dīrgha tamas*, "long darkness" — and the boy is duly born sightless, taking his name from the curse. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~104; Bṛhaddevatā 4)
- **The twin-fruit of the episode: Bharadvāja** — the spilled seed of Bṛhaspati grows into a separate child, **Bharadvāja**, whom Mamatā declines to rear (*"bhara dvā-jam"*); the same single episode thus produces both the blind seer and the [Bharadvāja](bharadvaja.md) of Maṇḍala VI — the two are womb-linked rivals-in-conception, not brothers by one father. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva; cf. Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.20) [late text / folk etymology for the name]
- **Blind, yet a master of the Veda** — despite blindness Dīrghatamas attains full Vedic learning and seerhood through tapas and the grace of the gods; tradition makes him the standing proof that *ṛṣi-hood is inner sight*. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva; Bṛhaddevatā)
  - *Variant (Bṛhaddevatā):* the Bṛhaddevatā's telling has the blind seer **gain sight through Agni/the gods' favour** after praising them — connected to the hymns RV 1.147 and 1.158 in which Māmateya calls on divine protection. (Bṛhaddevatā 4) [disputed in details across recensions]

## 2. Cast into the river; the ordeal and the rescue by King Bali

- **Expulsion by his own household** — grown old, blind and dependent, Dīrghatamas alienates his wife **Pradveṣī** and his sons (Gautama at their head): the Mahābhārata says he had adopted scandalous "**cow-practices**" (*go-dharma*, learned from Surabhi's son) that the ṛṣis and his family would not tolerate; his sons **bind him to a raft and cast him into the Gaṅgā**. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~104) [a frankly transgressive episode the epic itself reports with disapproval]
- **The Traitana attack (the seer's own testimony)** — Ṛgveda 1.158, a hymn of Dīrghatamas to the **Aśvins**, appears to reference an ordeal in the waters: the **dāsa Traitana** attempts to wound/behead him, but the blow falls on the attacker himself; the seer calls on the Aśvins as rescuers of the helpless. (Ṛgveda 1.158.4–5; elaborated in Bṛhaddevatā 4) [the ṛgvedic verses are famously obscure; the narrative is largely the Bṛhaddevatā's reconstruction — [disputed]]
- **Rescue by King Bali** — the raft floats far downstream into the eastern country, where **King Bali** (a Cāndravaṃśa monarch, in the line descending from Yayāti's son Anu) finds the blind sage, honours him, and takes him home. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~104; Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.23) — for Bali's dynastic slot see [Sūryavaṃśa & Candravaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).

## 3. The niyoga: fathering Aṅga, Vaṅga, Kaliṅga — and Kakṣīvat

- **Bali's request** — childless, King Bali begs the sage to **beget heirs upon his queen Sudeṣṇā** by the accepted custom of **niyoga** (levirate-style commissioning of a sage to continue a line). Dīrghatamas consents. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~104)
- **The substitution of the dāsī** — repelled by the blind old sage, **Sudeṣṇā sends her śūdra nurse in her own place**; on the nurse Dīrghatamas begets **eleven sons**, **Kakṣīvat** at their head — all of whom become brahmins learned in the Veda. When Bali discovers the ruse, he insists the queen herself go to the sage. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~104)
- **The five eastern kings** — upon (or by the touch/blessing of) Sudeṣṇā, Dīrghatamas fathers the five sons who become eponyms of the eastern kingdoms: **Aṅga, Vaṅga, Kaliṅga, Puṇḍra and Suhma** — the māhājanapada-names of Bengal, Odisha and the eastern Gangetic lands trace to this niyoga. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva ~104)
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.23):* the Bhāgavata gives a **six-fold list** of Bali's Dīrghatamas-born sons, the "Bāleya" kṣatra — Aṅga, Vaṅga, Kaliṅga, Suhma, Puṇḍra and a sixth eastern eponym (read variously in editions). [lists differ across Purāṇas — disputed in the sixth name]
  - *Variant (mode of begetting):* tellings differ on whether the five kings are begotten in the ordinary way or spring from the sage's **touch and mantra** after he detects the earlier deception; both circulate. [disputed]
- **Genealogical weight** — through this single episode the blind brahmin seer becomes the biological fountainhead of an entire **kṣatriya map of eastern India**, one of the epic's clearest charters of niyoga and of brahmin-seeded royal lines. (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva; Purāṇic vaṃśa lists) — see [Sūryavaṃśa & Candravaṃśa](../03-lineages-vamsha/suryavamsha-and-chandravamsha.md).

## 4. Kakṣīvat and Ghoṣā — the seer-line continues

- **Kakṣīvat the ṛṣi** — Dīrghatamas's son **Kakṣīvat** is himself a canonical Ṛgvedic seer, credited by the Anukramaṇī with **RV 1.116–126** — a run of hymns rich in praise of the **Aśvins** — and celebrated for the *dānastuti* (praise of a patron's gifts) to **King Svanaya Bhāvya** in RV 1.125–126. (Ṛgveda 1.116–126; Sarvānukramaṇī) [scholarly]
  - *Variant (the mother's name):* Vedic tradition styles Kakṣīvat **Auśija**, "son of Uśij" — Uśij being named as a **dāsī** — which dovetails strikingly with the Mahābhārata's tale of the queen's substituted nurse; the epic and the Vedic patronymic appear to preserve the same memory in two registers. (Ṛgveda; Bṛhaddevatā; Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva) [scholarly]
- **Ghoṣā the brahmavādinī** — Kakṣīvat's daughter (and thus Dīrghatamas's granddaughter) **Ghoṣā** is one of the named **women seers of the Ṛgveda**, credited with the twin Aśvin-hymns **RV 10.39–40**; the healer-twins who rescued her grandfather from the river are the family deities to the third generation. (Ṛgveda 10.39–40; Bṛhaddevatā) — full treatment in [Rigvedic women seers (brahmavādinīs)](../07-acharyas-and-sampradayas/rigvedic-women-seers-brahmavadinis.md).

## 5. The Ṛgvedic corpus of Dīrghatamas — and RV 1.164, *Asya Vāmasya*

- **Seer of RV 1.140–164** — the Sarvānukramaṇī assigns Dīrghatamas Aucathya twenty-five hymns of the first Maṇḍala, **RV 1.140–164**: Agni hymns (1.140–150), Mitrā-Varuṇa (1.151–153), **Viṣṇu's three strides (1.154–156)** — among the oldest Viṣṇu material in the Veda — Aśvin hymns (1.157–158), Dyāvā-Pṛthivī, and the great riddle hymn. (Ṛgveda Maṇḍala 1; Sarvānukramaṇī) [scholarly] — see [Rigveda](../90-literature-corpus/vedas/rigveda.md) and [Vedic deities & sūktas](../90-literature-corpus/vedic-deities-and-suktas.md).
- **RV 1.164, the *Asya Vāmasya* hymn** — a **52-verse riddle hymn (*brahmodya*)** addressed to the Viśvedevas, named from its opening words *asya vāmasya palitasya hotuḥ* ("of this beloved aged hotṛ-priest…"); it strings together enigmas of the sun, the year, speech, the sacrifice and the One, and is the most philosophically mined hymn in the Saṃhitā. (Ṛgveda 1.164)
- **The wheel of the year** — the hymn's famous time-riddles: a **twelve-spoked wheel** that never ages, on which stand **720 sons in pairs** (the days and nights of the year); seven who yoke a one-wheeled chariot drawn by a seven-named horse. (Ṛgveda 1.164.2, 1.164.11–12) — Vedic astronomy in riddle form; cf. [00-time-and-cosmology](../00-time-and-cosmology/README.md).
- **The two birds — RV 1.164.20** — *dvā suparṇā sayujā sakhāyā*: "**Two birds, companions joined together, clasp the selfsame tree; one of them eats the sweet fig; the other, not eating, looks on.**" The classic image of the experiencing self and the witnessing Self on the one tree of the body. (Ṛgveda 1.164.20)
  - *Reuse (Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 3.1.1–2):* the verse is taken over **verbatim**, read non-dualistically — the onlooking bird is the **Ātman**, and the grieving eater, beholding the other's majesty, passes beyond sorrow. ([Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad](../90-literature-corpus/upanishads/mundaka-upanishad.md) 3.1.1–2)
  - *Reuse (Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 4.6–7):* the same verse read **theistically** — the second bird is the personal **Lord (Īśa)**; two doctrinal framings of one Dīrghatamas verse, deliberately kept distinct in this corpus. ([Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad](../90-literature-corpus/upanishads/svetashvatara-upanishad.md) 4.6–7)
- **The imperishable syllable — RV 1.164.39** — *ṛco akṣare parame vyoman*: the verses rest "on the imperishable (akṣara) in the highest heaven" where all the gods abide — what has one to do with the ṛc who does not know That? An early charter for the later doctrine of **akṣara/praṇava**. (Ṛgveda 1.164.39)
- **The four quarters of Speech — RV 1.164.45** — **Vāk is measured in four quarters**; three are hidden and do not stir; humans speak only the fourth — foundational for all later Sanskrit speculation on levels of speech (parā/paśyantī/madhyamā/vaikharī in later grammarian readings [late text] of this verse). (Ṛgveda 1.164.45)
- **"Truth is One" — RV 1.164.46** — *indraṃ mitraṃ varuṇam agnim āhur… **ekaṃ sad viprā bahudhā vadanti***: "They call it Indra, Mitra, Varuṇa, Agni… **The One Being the wise speak of in many ways** — they call it Agni, Yama, Mātariśvan." The single most quoted Ṛgvedic line in modern Hindu self-description, the proof-text of theological pluralism — and it is the blind seer's. (Ṛgveda 1.164.46)
- **Afterlife of the hymn** — verses of RV 1.164 recur in the Atharvaveda (AV 9.9–10 parallels), are dissected in Yāska's **Nirukta** and the Brāhmaṇa/Āraṇyaka literature, and feed the Upaniṣads as above; Sāyaṇa's medieval commentary reads the whole as sacrificial allegory, modern scholars as a self-conscious *brahmodya* riddle-contest text. [scholarly]

## 6. The consecrator of Bharata; later memory

- **Anointing Bharata Dauḥṣanti** — the **Aitareya Brāhmaṇa** records that **Dīrghatamas Māmateya performed the *mahābhiṣeka*** (great royal consecration) of **Bharata, son of Duṣyanta** — the emperor from whom Bhārata-varṣa takes its name — by which Bharata "went round the earth conquering." (Aitareya Brāhmaṇa 8.23) — the blind seer thus crowns the eponym of India; cf. [Śakuntalā & Bharata](shakuntala-bharata.md).
- **Longevity** — tradition credits Dīrghatamas with an enormously long life spanning royal generations (from the curse in Utathya's era to Bharata's consecration); the corpus flags this as the usual trans-generational elasticity of ṛṣi chronology rather than a datable claim. [disputed as chronology]
- **Not a gotra-kāra of the first rank** — unlike his conception-twin Bharadvāja, Dīrghatamas heads no major surviving gotra; his line is remembered through **Kakṣīvat** (the Kākṣīvata seers) and through the **kṣatriya eponyms** Aṅga–Vaṅga–Kaliṅga. (Gotra-pravara tradition; Purāṇic vaṃśas) [scholarly]

## 7. The two clocks

- **Scriptural clock** — Dīrghatamas belongs to the deep genealogical past of the present **Vaivasvata Manvantara**: grandson-generation of [Aṅgiras](angiras.md), contemporary of the early **Cāndravaṃśa** kings (Bali of the Anu line), and consecrator of **Bharata Dauḥṣanti** — i.e., many generations *before* the Kuru story, in the primordial-dynastic stratum of the lunar line. (Mahābhārata; Purāṇic vaṃśas; Aitareya Brāhmaṇa)
- **Academic clock** — [scholarly] "Dīrghatamas" names the eponymous seer (or seer-family) of a block of Maṇḍala 1 hymns; Maṇḍala 1 is a **later anthology** relative to the family books (II–VII), so the hymns are conventionally placed toward the later Ṛgvedic period, **c. 1200–1000 BCE**, with RV 1.164 often judged among the most speculative-late compositions of the Saṃhitā; the biographical legends (curse, river, niyoga) are epic-Purāṇic elaborations centuries younger than the hymns. Never collapse the two frames.
