# Avadhuta-Gita — the Song of the Free Soul

> *Up to [04-deep-dives](./README.md) · master [index](../README.md). The "Song of the Free Soul" — a terse, ~289-verse, 8-chapter standalone Advaita poem ascribed to [Dattātreya](./dattatreya.md), promoted here from its one-line mention there; companion to the [lesser-Gītās catalog](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md) and rooted in the [Atri–Anasūyā](./atri-anasuya.md) household that produced its reputed author.*

The **Avadhūta-Gītā** (also **Datta-Gītā**, *Avadhūtopaniṣad* in some printings) is among the boldest standalone **Advaita** (non-dual Vedānta) poems in the Hindu corpus — a sustained, almost ecstatic declaration by the realized **avadhūta** ("the shaken-off one," the liberated wanderer beyond caste, *āśrama*, ritual, and even shame) that nothing was ever bound, nothing need be sought, and the Self alone *is*. It is traditionally a discourse of **[Dattātreya](./dattatreya.md)** — the Trimūrti-fused son of [Atri and Anasūyā](./atri-anasuya.md) and the paradigm avadhūta — and stands as a first-tier member of every "60 Gītās" roster of the wider [Gītā corpus](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md). Unlike the Mahābhārata's narrative-bound [lesser Gītās](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md), it has no story, no battlefield, no disciple's crisis: it is pure *jñāna* in the first person. [scholarly]

## The two clocks

- **Scriptural time** - In its own frame the song belongs to the **timeless milieu of [Dattātreya](./dattatreya.md)**, the avadhūta-avatāra born in the present ([7th, Vaivasvata](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md)) Manvantara. As eternal *brahma-vidyā* re-spoken, it carries no single yuga of "occurrence" and is best tagged **cross-yuga**: a primeval realized utterance with no narrative date. [scriptural]
- **Manuscript / academic time** - Text-critically the extant Avadhūta-Gītā is dated by scholars to roughly the **9th–10th century CE or later** — far post-Vedic, in the matured **post-Śaṅkara** Advaita and **Nāth**-yogic milieu. Its Dattātreya ascription is **devotional, not historical**: an attribution to the archetypal free soul rather than evidence of authorship. Keep the scriptural ascription distinct from this manuscript dating. [scholarly]

## Structure — eight chapters (~289 verses)

- **Overall shape** - The poem runs to roughly **289 ślokas across 8 adhyāyas (chapters)**; verse-totals vary slightly by recension and by whether the often-doubted Chapter 8 is counted in full. The frame, where named at all, is **Dattātreya teaching his disciple** (traditionally **Kārtika / Svāmī Kārttikeya**, or simply an unnamed seeker). (Avadhūta Gītā) [disputed — verse count edition-dependent]
- **Chapter 1 — *ātma-saṃvitti*** - The longest and most celebrated chapter: a torrent of declarations of identity of *ātman* and *Brahman*, opening with the famous claim that grace (*īśvarānugraha*) itself impels one to the conviction of all-pervading non-duality (*advaita-rūpa*). (Avadhūta Gītā 1)
- **Chapter 2** - On the worthlessness of external observance — caste, posture, pilgrimage, scripture-learning — for the one who has known the Self; the avadhūta's freedom from all rule. (Avadhūta Gītā 2)
- **Chapter 3** - Continued negation of attributes and states: the Self is neither bound nor free, neither pure nor impure, neither one nor many. (Avadhūta Gītā 3)
- **Chapter 4** - Refrains denying that there is anything to renounce, attain, meditate on, or realize — the seeker, the sought, and the seeking collapse into the one awareness. (Avadhūta Gītā 4)
- **Chapter 5** - On *oṃ* / the syllable and the inexpressibility of the Self; the inadequacy of speech and concept before that which is "not this, not this" (*neti neti*). (Avadhūta Gītā 5)
- **Chapter 6** - Further dissolution of duality — bondage and liberation, knowledge and ignorance, are alike unreal; only the partless Self abides. (Avadhūta Gītā 6)
- **Chapter 7** - The conduct (or non-conduct) of the avadhūta: he is beyond approval and blame, action and inaction, possessing nothing and lacking nothing. (Avadhūta Gītā 7)
- **Chapter 8 — the *avadhūta-lakṣaṇa*** - A description of the marks of the true avadhūta and an unfolding of the four syllables of the word *a-va-dhū-ta* (immortality/freedom from desire; freedom from change; cleansing of mind; meditation on "*tat tvam asi*"). (Avadhūta Gītā 8)
  - *Variant [disputed]:* Chapter 8 is **often regarded as a later addition** — more conventional and less radically non-dual than chapters 1–7 — and some editions print only seven chapters or treat 8 as appended. [late text]

## The uncompromising teaching

- **No creation, no dissolution** - The signature *ajāta-vāda* ("doctrine of no-origination"): there was never any creation, no bound being, no aspirant, no liberated one — these are appearances in the one undivided awareness, which alone exists. (Avadhūta Gītā 1) [scholarly]
- **No bondage, hence no liberation** - Since nothing was ever bound, *mukti* is not an achievement but the falling-away of the illusion of bondage; the avadhūta does not "become" free, he recognizes he was never otherwise. (Avadhūta Gītā 3–4, 6)
- **No seeker and no path** - The text repeatedly negates the apparatus of sādhana itself — no meditation to perform, no scripture that binds, no teacher and disciple as ultimately real; even the negations are finally let go. (Avadhūta Gītā 4–5)
- **Beyond all states and conduct** - The realized avadhūta is *guṇātīta* (beyond the three guṇas) and beyond the four states (waking, dream, deep sleep, *turīya*); he is bound by no *ācāra* (prescribed conduct), no *varṇāśrama*, no purity-rule — his very wandering, ruleless body is the teaching. (Avadhūta Gītā 2, 7)
- **The "I am Brahman" refrain** - Like the [Ribhu-Gītā](./ribhu-gita.md), much of the power is in **affirmative repetition** (*bhāvanā*): chapter on chapter the speaker reasserts "I am that partless, stainless, all-pervading awareness," dissolving the separate self by saturation rather than argument. [scholarly]
- **The four syllables of *avadhūta*** - Chapter 8's etymological meditation reads the very word as a map of liberation: *a* = freedom from the bondage of hope/the imperishable; *va* = renunciation of desire and worldly ties; *dhū* = the shaking-off / purification of the dust of the mind; *ta* = constant absorption in the truth "*tat tvam asi*" ("That thou art"). (Avadhūta Gītā 8) [the gloss varies by commentary]

## Place in the Nāth / Advaita lineage

- **A Datta-Nāth document** - The Avadhūta-Gītā is one of the core texts that link **[Dattātreya](./dattatreya.md)** — revered as Ādi-Guru of the **Nāth sampradāya** and a fountainhead of yoga — to the radical *jñāna* stream; its anti-ritual, *jīvanmukti*-now stance feeds directly into Nāth and later Sant currents. (Nāth tradition) [regional]
  - *Variant [scholarly — J. Mallinson]:* Dattātreya's full absorption into the Nāth tradition is **relatively late (c. 18th c.)**; the Avadhūta-Gītā's attribution to him reflects his standing as the avadhūta-archetype more than a fixed sectarian origin. [disputed]
- **Pure Advaita, not sectarian** - Doctrinally the poem is **non-sectarian Vedāntic non-dualism** — it names no presiding deity to worship and dissolves all forms into the formless Self, which is why it is claimed across Vaiṣṇava-Datta, Śaiva-Nāth, and pan-Advaita lines alike. (Advaita commentarial tradition) [scholarly]
- **Kin to the most radical Gītās** - It sits beside the [Aṣṭāvakra-Gītā](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md) and the [Ribhu-Gītā](./ribhu-gita.md) as one of the three most uncompromising "world-negating" Gītās — texts that deny world, bondage, and liberation alike — and is frequently anthologized and recited in modern Advaita and Ramaṇa-lineage circles. [scholarly]

## Standing among the "60 Gītās"

- **A first-tier independent Gītā** - In the wider Hindu **[Gītā corpus](./lesser-gitas-catalog.md)**, the Avadhūta-Gītā is a fixed member of the "**more than sixty Gītās**" tallies and of the Purāṇic/independent (non-Mahābhārata) Gītā lists, regularly named alongside the Devī-, Śiva-, Gaṇeśa-, Rāma-, Aṣṭāvakra-, and Ribhu-Gītās. (devotional *gītā-saṅgraha* tradition; "60 Gītās" rosters) [folk]
- **Distinct from the Bhāgavata avadhūta** - Note the name-clash: the **Avadhūta-Gītā** (this standalone poem) is *not* the **Avadhūta's 24-gurus** teaching of the Uddhava-Gītā in [Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.7–9](./dattatreya.md), though both feature Dattātreya as the *avadhūta*-teacher. The Bhāgavata episode is a narrative of learning from nature; this Gītā is a verse-by-verse declaration of the already-realized Self. [common error] [disputed]
