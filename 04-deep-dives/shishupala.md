# Śiśupāla — the Hundred Offenses

> A standalone deep-dive on the Cedi king slain at Yudhiṣṭhira's Rājasūya — the climax of the *agra-pūjā* dispute and the third-and-final birth of Viṣṇu's cursed gatekeeper.
> Sits inside the [Mahābhārata Sabhā-parva](mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md) episode; placed in the **Dvāpara-yuga** (current [Mahāyuga 28](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/README.md)).
> Up one level: [Deep Dives README](README.md).

Śiśupāla (Śiśu-pāla, "child-protected") is king of **Cedi**, son of **Damaghoṣa** and **Śrutaśravā** — the latter a sister of **Vasudeva**, making Śiśupāla **Kṛṣṇa's first cousin** (Mahābhārata Sabhā-parva 40). His life is a single arc bent toward one death: he is born monstrous and prophesied to die by his cousin's hand, accumulates a tally of insults that Kṛṣṇa has vowed to suffer, and is beheaded by the Sudarśana the instant the tally overflows. Theologically the whole arc is the payoff of the **Jaya–Vijaya** gatekeeper-curse — Śiśupāla's enmity is itself a perverse path to liberation.

## Birth & the prophecy

- **Born with three eyes and four arms** - Śiśupāla enters the world a deformed prodigy: an extra eye on his forehead and a surplus pair of arms. His parents, horrified, consider abandoning him until a disembodied voice (an aerial / *aśarīriṇī* voice) forbids it (in Dvāpara, before Kṛṣṇa's adulthood) (Sabhā-parva 40).
- **The voice's two-part prophecy** - The voice declares that the child will be both glorious and short-lived, and that the surplus eye and arms will **vanish the moment a certain person takes him on his lap** — and that same person will one day be his slayer (Sabhā-parva 40).
- **Kṛṣṇa's lap fulfils the sign** - When Kṛṣṇa (and Balarāma) visit Cedi, Śrutaśravā places the child on Kṛṣṇa's lap; the third eye disappears and the extra arms drop away. The mother now knows her son's appointed killer is her own nephew (Sabhā-parva 40). [etiological birth-prophecy]
- **The mother's plea & Kṛṣṇa's boon** - Terrified, Śrutaśravā begs Kṛṣṇa to spare her son. Kṛṣṇa promises to **pardon a full hundred offences** of Śiśupāla before exacting any penalty — the famous "100 offenses" vow that frames the entire episode (Sabhā-parva 40).
  - *Variant (count):* the canonical promise is **100** offenses; popular retellings sometimes phrase it as "100 mistakes/sins," with the killing on the **101st** — the Mahābhārata's own logic is that Kṛṣṇa forgives up to the hundredth, then strikes when they "exceed" the count.

## The Jaya–Vijaya curse — Śiśupāla's prior births

- **Third of three demonic births** - Śiśupāla is the **third and final incarnation of Jaya** (one of Viṣṇu's two Vaikuṇṭha gatekeepers, **Jaya and Vijaya**), cursed by the **Sanaka-kumāras** (Brahmā's four child-sages) to three demonic lives ending in death at the Lord's own hand — the curse's mercy being that dying by Viṣṇu shortens the exile (told fullest in *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 7.1, 10.74). See the [Daśāvatāra](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/dashavatara.md) and the gatekeeper-curse note in [Devas, Asuras & the Lokas](../02-deities-and-cosmic-hierarchy/devas-asuras-and-the-lokas.md).
  - Life #1 (Satya-yuga): **Hiraṇyakaśipu / Hiraṇyākṣa** — slain by Narasiṃha & Varāha. See [Satya-yuga](../01-manvantaras/manvantara-07-vaivasvata/mahayuga-28-current/01-satya-yuga.md).
  - Life #2 (Tretā-yuga): **Rāvaṇa / Kumbhakarṇa** — slain by Rāma. See [Rāmāyaṇa](ramayana/README.md).
  - Life #3 (Dvāpara-yuga): **Śiśupāla / Dantavakra** — slain by Kṛṣṇa (Śiśupāla at the Rājasūya; his ally/cousin **Dantavakra**, king of Karūṣa, killed by Kṛṣṇa shortly after).
- **Dveṣa-bhakti — liberation through hatred** - The Bhāgavata's theological gloss: constant, all-consuming **enmity** toward the Lord fixes the mind on him as surely as love does, so Śiśupāla's lifelong hatred ironically secures his **mokṣa** (merger into Kṛṣṇa) (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 7.1, 10.74). [scholarly: this is the locus classicus of the *dveṣa-bhakti* / "antagonistic devotion" doctrine.]
  - *Variant (Mahābhārata):* the Sabhā-parva gives the **birth-prophecy** and the radiance-merging but is **lighter on the doorkeeper frame** — it names neither the Sanaka-kumāra curse nor the full three-life sequence; the Bhāgavata supplies that scaffolding.

## Enmity with Kṛṣṇa — the grievances

- **The Rukmiṇī humiliation** - Śiśupāla was the intended groom of **Rukmiṇī**, princess of Vidarbha; her brother **Rukmī** had pledged her to him. Kṛṣṇa **abducted/eloped with Rukmiṇī** on the eve of the wedding, publicly humiliating Śiśupāla and the assembled suitors (Dvāpara) (alluded to in Sabhā-parva; narrated in full in *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.52–54). See [Kṛṣṇa-līlā](krishna-lila.md).
- **Standing grudge among the anti-Kṛṣṇa kings** - Śiśupāla is repeatedly aligned with **Jarāsandha** of Magadha (whose general/commander he is in some tellings) and **Dantavakra** — the bloc of monarchs resentful of Kṛṣṇa's rise (Sabhā-parva; *Bhāgavata*). Jarāsandha's killing by Bhīma (Sabhā-parva 20–22) clears the way for the Rājasūya at which Śiśupāla dies.

## The Rājasūya & the agra-pūjā dispute

- **The first-honour question** - At Yudhiṣṭhira's consecration the assembly must choose who receives the **arghya** (foremost guest-honour). **Bhīṣma** nominates **Kṛṣṇa** as the worthiest present, and **Sahadeva** performs the honour to him (Sabhā-parva 35–36). See [Sabhā-parva §7](mahabharata/02-sabha-parva.md).
- **Śiśupāla's eruption** - Śiśupāla rejects the choice: Kṛṣṇa is "no king, no priest, no elder — a mere **cowherd** (gopa)," unfit to be honoured before the assembled monarchs. He abuses Bhīṣma — mocking his celibacy-vow as cowardice — and rallies allied kings to walk out (Sabhā-parva 37–38).
- **The 100th offence reached** - His torrent of insults in the hall exhausts Kṛṣṇa's vow of a hundred pardons (Sabhā-parva 40).
- **The 101st & the Sudarśana** - When Śiśupāla insults beyond the hundredth, Kṛṣṇa recounts the boon to the assembly and then **releases the Sudarśana Cakra**, beheading Śiśupāla on the spot in the sabhā (Sabhā-parva 42).
  - *Variant (provocation):* some tellings have Kṛṣṇa deliberately **provoke** a final outburst (or count the insults aloud) so the tally tips past 100 — dramatising that the death is willed, not accidental. [folk / dramatic retellings]

## Death, the merging light & aftermath

- **The radiance merges into Kṛṣṇa** - A light/effulgence rises from the slain body and enters Kṛṣṇa before the whole court — the visible sign of Jaya's curse ending and the soul's absorption into the Lord (Sabhā-parva 42; *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.74).
  - *Variant (Bhāgavata emphasis):* the Bhāgavata foregrounds this as **sāyujya-mukti** (liberation by merger), the doctrinal proof of *dveṣa-bhakti*; the Mahābhārata reports the radiance more plainly as a wonder.
- **The Rājasūya concludes** - With the disturbance ended the consecration is sealed; the kings depart and Yudhiṣṭhira stands as undisputed **samrāṭ** — the high-water mark of Pāṇḍava fortune, immediately followed by Duryodhana's envy and the dice-game (Sabhā-parva 44–45).
- **His son Dhṛṣṭaketu** - Śiśupāla's son **Dhṛṣṭaketu** succeeds him as king of Cedi and — notably — fights on the **Pāṇḍava side** in the Kurukṣetra war (slain by Droṇa), his father's grudge not inherited (Mahābhārata, *Udyoga*/*Droṇa* parvas).
- **Dantavakra's revenge & death** - Śiśupāla's cousin/ally **Dantavakra** of Karūṣa attacks Kṛṣṇa to avenge him and is killed — completing the third Jaya–Vijaya birth-pair (Hiraṇyākṣa/Hiraṇyakaśipu → Kumbhakarṇa/Rāvaṇa → Dantavakra/Śiśupāla) (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.78).

## Later literary life

- **Māgha's *Śiśupāla-vadha*** - The single episode is expanded into a 20-canto ornate Sanskrit *mahākāvya* by **Māgha** (~7th c. CE), one of the *bṛhat-trayī* (the three great court epics), prized for verbal pyrotechnics. [late text / kāvya — a retelling, not an independent source for the myth]
